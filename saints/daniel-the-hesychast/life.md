# Daniel the Hesychast

doxologia.ro calls him one of the greatest saints the land of Moldavia has brought forth, a great teacher of the wilderness and a guide of monks. He was born to a poor family on the estate of the monastery of Saint Nicholas at Rădăuți, at the beginning of the fifteenth century, and was given the name Dumitru at his baptism. From infancy, the life says, he was never missing from church, did not play with the other children and did not look for rest or food, but prayed continually and obeyed his parents in everything.

At ten he was given to the monastery of Saint Nicholas at Rădăuți to learn letters, and young as he was he proved old in understanding: in a little while he had the Horologion and the Psalter by heart, and with them the spiritual labour — the hidden prayer of the heart, fasting, humility and the guarding of the mind from evil thoughts. After five years of his apprenticeship he became a monk in that monastery with the name David, and had as his teacher and spiritual father [Leontius of Rădăuți](/saints/leontius-of-radauti).

He loved stillness, fasting and prayer most of all. He tasted nothing daily until sunset, sometimes fasted three days and even five, and lived on vegetables and herbs; in obedience he was silent, gentle and subject to all; in church he stayed day and night like a lamp never put out; and in his cell he slept little, on a small stool. Of the books he loved the Psalter best, knew it by heart and repeated it daily. So labouring for some years he was counted worthy of the priesthood and of the gift of working wonders, and the sick who heard of it came running to the humble hieromonk David and were healed, while others came for counsel, for he was wise in word and saw ahead, and others to confess their sins, for he had become a noted confessor in the north of Moldavia.

Seeing himself surrounded and left no stillness for prayer, and fearing the spirit of vainglory, he took the blessing of the bishop of Rădăuți and withdrew to the monastery of Saint Laurence. There too the faithful sought him, and those troubled by unclean spirits; so by day he did his obedience and stood among men, and by night he kept vigil, prayed and wove baskets of withies for the house. Once the abbot sent him on an errand to the town of Siret, where the faithful crowded him and kept him a day, so that he could not return in time, and the abbot gave him a penance not to leave the lavra for a while; and he humbled himself and kept it gladly, giving thanks to God for everything.

After some years, feeling himself called by the Holy Spirit to the hermit's life, he took the blessing of the abbot of Saint Laurence, first put on the great and angelic habit of the schema, changing his name from David to Daniel, and then, hiding himself from the world, withdrew a little before the year 1450 into the depth of the forests on the valley of the Secu brook in the county of Neamț, where the monastery of Sihăstria later came into being. There he laboured fourteen years in hard monastic toil; and seeing himself surrounded by the world again he withdrew into the north of Moldavia.

Guided by God, he first made himself a hut of wood on the valley of the Vițeu brook near Putna. Then, finding a great rock nearby, he cut with a chisel a little cell in the face of it, just big enough to hold him, and beside it another room for a chapel of prayer, as it is still seen today; and in that rock he laboured more than twenty years. Day and night he kept vigil in unceasing prayer, fasting until sunset and sleeping very little on a little wooden stool; all week he did not come out of the cell at all; his food was crusts of bread, roots and herbs, and the work of his hands was weaving withy baskets. On Sundays he served the Liturgy and communicated, and then received those who came to him for healing and for a word of use.

For the holiness of his life, his long fasting and his all-night vigils with prayers and tears, he was tempted a long while by the demons, who sought to drive him out of the wilderness or to throw him into the sin of vainglory; but asking the help of Christ, and by the power of the holy Cross, he overcame all their snares. In a little time he was counted worthy of the gifts of tears, of foresight and of healing: he drove out unclean spirits by a word alone and healed sicknesses of every kind, knew hidden thoughts and told many the secrets of what was to come. His name became known through the whole country of Moldavia, and the people from the least to the greatest counted him among the saints from his youth, most of them calling him Saint Daniel the Old Hermit, the father and guide of all the hermits of northern Moldavia, and the monks calling him Saint Daniel the Schemamonk; and after his departure the people called him Saint Daniel the New, to tell him from other venerable men of the same name.

In 1451, when the ruler of the country Bogdan Voievod came to a sudden death, his son [Stephen](/saints/stephen-the-great) barely escaped the danger; and hearing of the labours and the wonders of Daniel, and being in hard straits, he was guided by the Holy Spirit to his cell. He stayed some days, confessed his thoughts before the venerable one, received absolution and many words of comfort; and the great hermit, having quieted his soul, blessed him, prayed for him, prophesied that he would soon be lord of Moldavia, and let him go in peace.

In the spring of 1457, coming to the throne of Moldavia, Stephen the Great was assured of the fulfilment of that prophecy and of the grace of God that was in him; and from that year the venerable one was the great prince's first counsellor, confessor and intercessor with God. The prince often stopped at his cell and confessed his sins, then asked for a word of use, and did nothing without his prayer and blessing; and Daniel encouraged him and urged him to defend the country and Christendom from the hands of the heathen, assuring him that if he built a church to the praise of Christ after each battle he would win in every war. Listening to him, the life says, Stephen defended the Church of Christ and the land of Moldavia for nearly half a century after the fall of Byzantium, winning forty-seven wars and raising forty-eight churches.

After the year 1470, seeing that he had no more stillness at Putna because of the monastery and the crowds of the faithful, he left the cell in which he had laboured more than twenty years and withdrew secretly into the old forests round the monastery of Voroneț, where some fifty monks were labouring under the guidance of the hieroschemamonk Misail, his own worthy disciple. Finding a retired place near the monastery he made himself a little cell under the rock called Șoimul. His name soon became known in all the villages of the place, so that every kind of sick person came to him, the paralysed and those held by unclean spirits, and were healed; and because the faithful could not reach his cell they waited below in the monastery, and he would come down at night, pray for them, tell them the cause of their suffering, counsel and bless them and send them home well.

In the summer of 1476, having lost the battle of Războieni to the Turks, Stephen the Great went to the cell of his good spiritual father at Voroneț. The life quotes the old account: Stephen knocked at the hermit's door for him to open, and the hermit answered that Stephen should wait outside until he had finished his prayer; and when the prayer was done he called him in, and Stephen confessed to him and asked what he should do, for he could not fight the Turks any longer — should he give the country over to them or not? And the hermit said he should not give it over, for the war was his; only, when he had won, he should build a monastery there in the name of Saint George.

When the metropolitan Theoctist died in the autumn of 1477, Stephen took counsel with the clergy and bishops of the land to choose Daniel of Voroneț shepherd and father of Moldavia; but hearing of it the venerable one prayed with tears to God and to the prince not to take him from his blessed stillness until his death, and all, won over by his humility and holiness, asked his pardon and left him in the wilderness. In the twenty years he spent as a hermit at Voroneț he made there a new home of stillness as important as the one at Putna: dozens of hermits gathered round him, labouring in the forests of Voroneț, in the mountains of Rarău and along the Eastern Carpathians, most of them keeping the prayer of Jesus, fasting and silence, others reading the Psalter daily, others making thousands of prostrations and weaving baskets, and others, being good calligraphers, writing service books for churches and monasteries.

Remembering the promise he had made to God and to Daniel, in the summer of 1488 Stephen the Great built from the foundations at the monastery of Voroneț a beautiful stone church dedicated to the great martyr George the trophy-bearer, in place of the old wooden one; and on 14 September of that same year it was consecrated by the metropolitan George in the presence of its blessed founders, Stephen the prince and the venerable Daniel, and of tens of thousands of the faithful, monks, clergy and officials of the land. On that day, by common counsel, Daniel, old as he was, was made abbot of Voroneț, and for nearly ten years he guided that brotherhood as a great spiritual father of monks, of hermits and of all Moldavia — living mostly at his cell under the rock of Șoimul, for he loved stillness, but coming down often to confess the community, heal the sick who gathered from the villages, and counsel them all.

Having become a chosen vessel of the Holy Spirit and passed the age of ninety, the great hermit of Moldavia gave up his soul into the arms of Christ at the end of the fifteenth century, in 1496. The crowd of his disciples, with the metropolitan and the prince of the land, mourned him and buried him in the pronaos of the church of Voroneț, as it is seen to this day, laying over him a stone with the inscription: this is the tomb of our father David, the schemamonk Daniel. Afterwards, seeing that wonders and healings were worked at his relics, his disciples and the faithful counted him in the company of the saints, and the brotherhood of Voroneț with the metropolitan Grigore Roșca, his own disciple, gave that lavra a second dedication in his honour beside the dedication to Saint George, and appointed him a day of celebration in the year after the memory of Daniel the Stylite. He was first painted as a saint with a halo in 1547 by that same metropolitan, on the south wall of the church at Voroneț, holding an open scroll which reads: come, brothers, and listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and knows two things about him, which it states and nothing more: that Saint Daniel was born in Moldavia at the beginning of the fifteenth century, and that he fell asleep in 1496 and was enrolled among the saints by the Orthodox Church of Romania in 1992. The year of the canonisation is the one thing in saint.gr's entry that the Romanian life behind this folder does not say; the rest of what it gives — the century, the land and the year of his falling asleep — the Romanian account gives too, and at length.
