# Sophia of Aenus

Sophia was born at Aenus, in Rhodope, and was the mother of six children. Busied though she was with the cares and the duties of this world, she kept the commandments of the Lord and lived a life full of virtue. After her children had died she became a mother to orphans and a helper of widows; she sold what she owned and gave the money to the poor.

Hers was a life of austerity, and she fed on bread and water only. The psalms of David the prophet-king were unceasingly on her lips, and tears ran without stopping from her eyes. She would rather go without what she needed herself than let a poor man leave her house with empty hands.

For her humility and her love of the poor God blessed her in this way. In her house stood a barrel of wine that she kept for the poor, and she noticed that however much was drawn out of it, it stayed full. But as soon as she had told somebody of the wonder and glorified God, the barrel emptied. Sophia was grieved, believing the wine had run out because she was unworthy, and so she increased her ascetic labours until she put her health in danger.

Feeling her end near, she received the tonsure into the monastic life. She fell asleep in the Lord at the age of fifty-three. The page gives no year, and names no place but the one she was born in.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 4 June, and saint.gr gives her the life doxologia's line only pointed at. It says she came from Aenus of Thrace and was the daughter of notable and wealthy parents; that she married and became the mother of six children, and had the grievous misfortune to lose all of them. So she found consolation in her grief by protecting orphans and widows: she gave alms without ceasing, and prayed and fasted, and did everything that gave relief to her neighbour. At the end of her life she was tonsured a nun and gave her life over to the worship of God, and she died peacefully at the age of fifty-three. The page puts no year to any of it, and its couplet says that the world was not found to be an obstacle to Sophia in reaching the perfection of virtue.
