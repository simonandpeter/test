# Artemas of Lystra

saint.gr's calendar for 30 Οκτωβρίου keeps «Άγιοι Τέρτιος, Μάρκος, Ιούστος και Αρτεμάς οι Απόστολοι από τους Εβδομήκοντα» — four of the Seventy in one entry, each with a see of his own. Artemas is the last of them, and of him the page says this and no more: he became bishop at Lystra, and as a tried servant of Christ he broke up the error of the demons in that place.

The epigram the page sets over his name turns on the Greek word for sound or whole: Artemas had a share in the glory of the minds above, having kept his own mind whole — ἄρτιον — for the Lord. The page records fragments of his relics at the monastery of Mega Spilaio at Kalavryta and at the monastery of Kykkos in Cyprus.

It gives him no year, no birthplace and no account of his death, and prints no hymn. It carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him with three other apostles of the Seventy on the Julian 30 October, and of him its one life for the four says two things: that he was bishop in the Lycaonian city of Lystra, and that he fell asleep in peace. The see is the one the Greek page gave this folder; the province and the peaceful end are new to it, and the peaceful end is worth having, because three of the four on this line died as martyrs. The name link behind the line calls him a hierarch, «Святитель Артема, епископ Листрийский, апостол от 70-ти», where the line itself calls him only an apostle. The line closes the four with «(I)», the first century, which is the only date any calendar has given him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 30 October, last of five apostles of the Seventy under one heading, and gives him one sentence: Artemas was bishop in Lystra of Lycaonia, and ended peacefully. That is the only statement about his death this folder has from any of its sources, and it is also what tells him apart from two of the four men named beside him in the same heading, whom the same paragraph has dying as martyrs.
