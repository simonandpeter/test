# Alexis of Zosimova

Alexis was born Theodore Alexeyevich Solovyov in Moscow on 17 January 1846, one of the many children of the archpriest Alexis Petrovich Solovyov, rector of the church of St Simeon the Stylite beyond the Yauza, and was baptised for the great martyr Theodore Tyro. He learned his letters from the deacon Paul Smirnov, and in 1867 married his teacher’s daughter Anna, his friend since childhood. He had finished the seminary second in his year in 1866 and, feeling no call to theological scholarship, chose to serve as a parish deacon rather than go on to the academy; Metropolitan Philaret (Drozdov) sent him to the church of St Nicholas in Tolmachi.

Their son Michael was born in 1870, and Anna died of consumption in 1872. In June 1895, after twenty-eight years at Tolmachi, he was ordained priest for the Dormition cathedral of the Kremlin, where within two years the clergy chose him as their confessor and a year later he became protopresbyter. In October 1898, his son grown and married, he left for the Smolensk Zosima hermitage north of Moscow, and on 30 November 1898 its abbot Herman (Gomzin) tonsured him a hieromonk with the name Alexis.

Herman, wary of pride in so honoured a newcomer, humbled him at first — the poorest vestments, a place below the brethren — and Alexis bore it meekly, once going at night to wake the choir director Nathanael, his own spiritual son, to ask his pardon; in time Herman came to love him and became his spiritual son too. After a grave illness in 1906 he moved into a small hut, and eldership became his chief work: when Barnabas of Gethsemane died that year many of Barnabas’s spiritual children came to him, and in his years of half-seclusion, 1908–1916, people of every station sought him out, the grand duchess Elizabeth Feodorovna among them.

In 1917, at the personal request of Tikhon, he took part in the monastic congress before the All-Russian Local Council and was elected a member of the Council, lodging in Moscow with his spiritual son Archimandrite [Seraphim (Zvezdinsky)](/saints/seraphim-of-dmitrov) at the Chudov monastery. On 5 November 1917, in the church of Christ the Saviour, it was he who drew from the casket, of the three lots, the one bearing the name of Metropolitan Tikhon. He took the schema on 28 February 1919, keeping his name. His son Michael died of typhus that October, and as a recluse he was not allowed to go to the funeral.

When the hermitage was closed in 1923 he went with his cell-attendant Macarius to Sergiev Posad, to the small house of his spiritual daughter Vera Verkhovtseva. From 1925 he could hardly walk, and in the end he only lay, still reading the day’s services and receiving a few close spiritual children and monks; Patriarch Tikhon once came to see him there. He died at Sergiev Posad on 19 September (2 October new style) 1928, was buried at the Kokuev cemetery, and was glorified by the Jubilee Council of August 2000. The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 September old style and on 24 August among the saints of Moscow.
