# Meletius of Lardos

Meletius was born at the end of the eighteenth century in the village of Lardos on the island of Rhodes, and received the name Emmanuel at his baptism. From infancy, doxologia says, he seemed a chosen vessel of the Lord, for he refused his mother's milk on the fast days, on Wednesdays and Fridays. The young Emmanuel led a deep spiritual life, loving stillness and prayer, and often withdrew to a cave in the area of the old monastery of the Most Holy Mother of God at Hypsos, which was then in ruins.

One night he was given a divine revelation: at the root of a tree he saw the icon of the Mother of God of Ypseni, and from that time he resolved to dedicate his life to God. On one of the following nights the Mother of God appeared to him in a vision and told him to raise a church in her name on the place where the icon had been found, and to rebuild the ruined monastery. So, with the blessing of the metropolitan of the region, he built the church and dedicated it to the Dormition of the Mother of God. Then he was tonsured a monk and ordained priest; the church was called a monastery, and Meletius was appointed its abbot.

A multitude of the faithful came to him to confess their sins and to hear his spiritual counsel, and he himself often visited the villages of the island to serve, to hear confessions, to strengthen the people in the true faith and in works of piety, and to steady their hearts in trials. Having grace from God, he healed many sick by his prayer and freed many possessed of unclean spirits. He was merciful, for none who came and asked his help went away without what was needed. He received into the monastery those persecuted by the Ottomans, giving them understanding and protection, and urged the Christians strongly to stand fast in the faith of their fathers and to live by the word of God, for which the Turks slandered and mocked him much.

After his falling asleep, in due time, his relics were found fragrant, a witness to his holiness, and a part of them is kept today in the church of the holy monastery of Ypseni. He received from God the gift of working wonders for those who call on him with faith and ask his protection, and he has often appeared to the sick, speaking his own name and healing them of their illnesses. His canonisation was performed by the Holy Synod of the Ecumenical Patriarchate at its session of 27 November 2013, under the Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew, and his day of celebration is 12 February. The page carries no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's long life adds what the Romanian page had not. He was born towards the end of the eighteenth century in the village of Lardos on Rhodes and named Emmanuel at baptism, of pious parents Nicholas and Stamatia; from infancy he refused his mother's milk on the fast days of Wednesday and Friday, and as a boy gave away goods from his father's storehouse to the poor without their diminishing. Praying one night near the ruined monastery of the Theotokos of Ypsos he saw a pillar of light come down and stand over an ancient olive, and at the place it marked he found an old icon of the Mother of God; she appeared to him afterwards and told him to raise a church there and rebuild the ruined house, and showed him where to dig for the money, which he found. He took the angelic habit, was renamed Meletius, was ordained deacon and presbyter and made abbot of the refounded monastery, healed the sick and freed the possessed, sheltered those the Ottomans hunted, and was particularly insistent in warning Christian women against unions with men of another religion — which is why the Ottomans looked for a chance to kill him, and, having murdered two village elders at Lardos, set out for the monastery and missed him. He died before his bishop's feet, under a slander which was uncovered when his body was prepared for burial, and was buried in the precinct of the metropolitan church; when the grave was later opened his relics were found fragrant. His head and a small part of his relics are kept today at the monastery of Ypseni, and the Holy Synod of the Ecumenical Patriarchate entered him among the saints on 27 November 2013.
