# Theodora of Sihla

Theodora was born about the middle of the seventeenth century in the village of Vânători-Neamț, in Moldavia. Her father, Ștefan Joldea Armașul, held a soldier's office as guard of the fortress of Neamț and maker of arms for those who defended it; her mother's name is not known. Of the two daughters the younger, Maghița, died young. Theodora was married against her will to a devout young man from Ismail; they had no children, and her heart being set on Christ they agreed together to take up the monastic life.

She was tonsured at the skete of Vărzărești in Vrancea, and two years later her husband became a monk at Poiana Mărului under the name Elefterie. When the Turks burnt Vărzărești the sisters scattered into the old forests, and Theodora withdrew into the mountains of Vrancea with her eldress, the schema-nun Paisia. After Paisia died she went, she said by a revelation, into the Neamț country, to the untrodden woods around the sketes of Sihăstria and Sihla. The abbot of Sihăstria, the hieroschemamonk Varsanufie, put her in the care of the spiritual father Pavel, who led her out into the wilderness near Sihla.

There she struggled many years, alone, in fasting and in prayer through the night, in tears and thousands of prostrations. Only Pavel came from time to time to hear her confession and bring her the Holy Mysteries and what she needed. When he too died she was left wholly alone, her clothes fell to pieces, and she lived on sorrel, forest berries and hazelnuts. In that school of stillness, the page says, she received the gift of the prayer of fire that works in the heart, the gift of tears, and of wonderworking, and was no longer troubled by cold or by hunger.

When the Turks came plundering as far as Sihla she hid in the cave near her cell, and at her prayer the rock at the back of it split open, as it is still seen, and she was saved. In that cave she passed her last years. Praying, her face would shine and her body rise from the ground, like [Mary of Egypt](/saints/mary-of-egypt); birds carried her crumbs of bread from the table of Sihăstria, and she drank from a hollow in a rock still called Theodora's Well.

Near her end she begged God for a priest to commune her. The abbot of Sihăstria, noticing the birds carrying crumbs towards Sihla, sent two brothers to see where they went; guided in the night, they found her at prayer with her hands raised and wrapped in a light like fire. She called them by name, asked for a garment to cover herself, and sent them down for a confessor. In the morning they brought the hieromonk Antonie and the hierodeacon Lavrentie with the Holy Mysteries; she made her confession, said the Creed, received communion, gave thanks and died. This was in the third decade of the eighteenth century, and she was buried in the cave.

Her body lay hidden there until after 1830, when the family of the Moldavian prince Mihail Sturza, who had restored the skete of Sihla, laid her relics in a rich reliquary in its church, and later moved them to a new church on their estate at Miclăușeni. In 1856 the family agreed with the abbot of the Pecersk monastery in Kyiv to give the relics in exchange for priestly and hierarchical vestments, and they are kept in the catacombs there, the reliquary inscribed in Romanian, Sfânta Teodora din Carpați, and in Slavonic, Sveti Teodora Carpatina. The Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church canonised her on 20 June 1992, with her feast on 7 August.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 7 August, and calls her one of the most important nuns who lived in the Romanian monasteries. saint.gr adds two things the Romanian line does not. The first is her forty days of prayer for a priest at the end, and how it was answered: the abbot of Sihăstria had noticed flocks of birds flying towards the mountain of Sihla with crumbs in their beaks, and wondering whether some holy hesychast lived there sent two brothers to follow them, who at nightfall saw a column of light rising to heaven and, coming near, saw Theodora shining like the sun in the face, praying with her hands raised and not touching the ground.

The abbot sent them back with the spiritual father Antonios and the deacon Laurentios; she told them her life, recited the Creed, communicated, asked the priest's blessing, said «Glory to You, O God, for all things», and in that same moment gave up her soul, and her body gave out a fragrance.

The second is where she is now. Her holy body stayed in the cave about a hundred years; between 1828 and 1834 her relics were carried to Kyiv and laid in the catacombs of the Pechersk monastery with the inscription «Η αγία Θεοδώρα των Καρπαθίων», the holy Theodora of the Carpathians, where the page says they are to this day. It tells besides the places her name still marks on the mountain — the sorrel it calls the dock of Saint Theodora, and the hollow in the rock called her well, whose rainwater, it says, never runs out — and the wall of her cave which opened for her when a band of Turks rushed in to kill her.
