# Anthony the Roman of Novgorod

saint.gr's calendar for 3 August names the venerable Anthony the wonderworker, the Roman, of Novgorod, and sets his years in its own head line, «(1067 - 1147)». Of the life behind those years it says one sentence and no more: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Ρώσου Οσίου» — we have no details of the Russian venerable one's life. It offers no deed, no place in Novgorod, no relic and no repose, and it prints no hymn to him.

What it does print is a gloss on the name: Antonios, from αντί and ωνούμαι, the one who bids higher in an offering of worth, the one who outbids, the more generous. Two other men of that name stand in this corpus from the same site and are not this one. One is the archbishop of Novgorod whom saint.gr keeps on 17 January and dates to 1231 or 1232; the other comes from its enumeration of the twelve hierarchs of Novgorod on 10 February and is dated 1231. This entry's own years are eighty years earlier than theirs, and it alone calls him the Roman.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him the third entry of its Julian 3 August, and it is the first life this folder has had: the Greek calendar gave it a line. He was born at Rome in 1086, it says, of devout and well-off parents. In that time the Roman church separated itself from the Eastern church, and all who stayed faithful to the Eastern were persecuted by the Roman clergy; and Anthony was among the persecuted.

He gave away the whole of his inherited property and took the monastic habit, and stood on a rock in the sea, struggling there for fourteen months. Then the rock broke away from its base and by some strange providence sailed over the water as far as Novgorod. At Novgorod the hierarch [Nicetas](/saints/nicetas-of-novgorod) received him kindly and helped him raise a church of the Holy Mother of God, where a monastery was afterwards. Anthony lived long as abbot of that monastery, showed a great power of grace through many wonders, and ended peacefully in 1146, passing over to the courts of the Lord.

Both of the years in this entry disagree with the ones this folder already held: it had a flat 1067 for the birth against the 1086 here, and a flat 1147 for the repose against this 1146. Both intervals are now open to hold either reading, and neither year is preferred to the other.
