# John Pettai

The Greek calendar names him on 20 Ιανουαρίου as «Άγιος Ιωάννης ο Ιερομάρτυρας», and the whole of the page behind that line is one sentence: «Ο Άγιος Ιερομάρτυς Ιωάννης Πεττάι, τιμάται υπό της Εκκλησίας της Ταλλίνης και Εσθονίας» — the holy hieromartyr John Pettai is honoured by the Church of Tallinn and Estonia. So the day gives a surname the line itself leaves out, a rank, and the Church that keeps him.

It gives no year of birth, no year of death, no place of his priesthood and no account of his end, and prints no hymn for him. This folder records the line and the sentence behind it; anything fuller would have to come from the Estonian Church's own account, which has not been read here.

The Russian calendar keeps him too, and adds the two things the Greek page withholds: a year and a rank. On 16 января ст. ст. it prints «Сщмч. Иоанна пресвитера (1919)», and the name link behind that line gives «Иоанн (Петтайя), иерей, сщмч.» — the priest John Pettaiya, the same surname the Greek writes «Πεττάι». So he died in 1919, and the rank the Greek calendar leaves as «Ιερομάρτυς» is a parish priest's.

The two calendars keep him on days that do not answer to one another: the Greek on 20 January of its own reckoning, the Russian on 16 January of the old style, which falls on the civil 29 January. The Russian day is the one recorded here, because it is the day page that listed him. Of his life, his diocese, his parish, the place of his death and the manner of it, neither calendar says anything at all.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 20 Ιανουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/01/20/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3331/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 16 января ст. ст. — [the Russian day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260116.html), read 3 October 2026, which keeps him as «Сщмч. Иоанна пресвитера (1919)» and names him «Иоанн (Петтайя)».*
