# Sarbelus of Edessa

Sarbelus was a priest of the idols at Edessa, and the Russian calendar's life for 15 October is four sentences long. It says that the holy martyrs Sarbelus and [Bebaia](/saints/bebaia-of-edessa) were brother and sister by blood, and that they suffered in the second century, under the emperor Trajan, for confessing Christianity. Sarbelus had been an idol priest at Edessa; but, turned to Christ by one bishop, he received baptism together with his sister. The pagans tore at the two of them for a long time, and then cut off their heads.

The bishop who turned him is not named on the page, and no name is supplied for him here. The day line dates the pair «ок. 98-117», about 98 to 117, which is the span of Trajan's reign, and the life itself gives no year; so their death stands as that interval rather than as a date. The line carries no Typikon sign — the day's three service icons stand in the paragraph above it, each before its own entry — and the calendar prints no troparion for the two of them.

The Greek, Romanian and Serbian calendars were read for the same menologion day, 15 October, and none of the three keeps these two martyrs at all. So everything on this page comes from the Russian calendar, and it is all of what that calendar says.

One thing is left open. The corpus already holds a «Sarbel» and a «Bebaia» out of the Russian 5 September and the Greek 4 September, and the two pairs are kept apart here because the Russian calendar keeps them apart itself. Its name index gives this martyr page 1922, «Сарвил Едесский, мч.», and the September one page 1923, «Сарвил, мч.», and each of those pages names one day and no other. The passions differ as well: the September pair are a brother hung on a tree and sawn through and a sister killed by a spear thrust to the neck, while these two are beheaded, and the calendar prints a separate life for each pair. The names have long been run together in the tradition. Nothing read here joins them, and nothing read here separates them further than the source does.
