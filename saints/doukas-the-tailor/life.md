# Doukas the Tailor

Doukas came from Mytilene and worked as a tailor in some tailor's shop at Constantinople. Going once to do his work at the house of a Turkish grandee, who was away with the army, he was set upon at once by the man's wanton wife; but like another chaste Joseph he got himself out of her house. Then she, to shake the shame off herself, went and slandered Doukas to the vizier, saying that the martyr had tried to violate her in her own house.

The eparch arrested Doukas at once and brought him before the vizier, who tried with flatteries and promises to persuade him to deny Christ and so save his life. Doukas refused stubbornly and suffered dreadful torments: the Turks flayed him alive, threw his skin into the sea, and went on with their tortures on the lifeless and shapeless body of the new martyr.

This happened on 24 April 1564 at Constantinople, the page says, and proved the martyr's divine love for the Christian faith. Its couplet turns on his trade: Doukas the tailor, flayed of his whole hide, sewed himself a garment of light. Of his parents, his age and his relics the page says nothing, and it prints no hymn.

The Serbian church keeps the two new martyrs together on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović names them both in its heading — „Св. новомуч. Лука и Никола.“ — and gives them one sentence between them: „Дивни младић Лука, кројач по занату, пострада за Христа 1564. год., а Никола 1776. год.“ — the admirable youth Luke, a tailor by trade, suffered for Christ in 1564, and Nicholas in 1776.

The tailor is this folder's man under another name, and the whole of that reading is in the attestation's own note: the trade, the youth, the rank and the year 1564 agree with saint.gr line for line, and only the name differs. **The 1564 agrees exactly, so no date was added and none changed.** saint.gr sets the death at Constantinople and dates it to this very day, «στις 24 Απριλίου 1564 μ.Χ.»; the Prologue gives no place and no manner of death, so nothing was taken from it but the commemoration, the trade and the year.
