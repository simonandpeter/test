# Victor of Thessalonica

saint.gr's calendar for 30 Μαρτίου names a holy Victor, with no epithet, and the page behind the line is one sentence: the holy Victor was martyred at Thessalonica under Maximian, together with eleven other martyrs. That is the whole of the source. It gives no year, no trade, no age, no account of the arrest or the trial, no manner of death, no relic and no hymn, and it does not name one of the eleven who died with him.

The eleven therefore have no folder here. The page counts them and stops, and a company the source enumerates nowhere is not a list this corpus can make; if a later reader finds them named, they belong with him. Maximian's reign the page does not put into years, and no century is written into this folder from it.

The city is what identifies him. This corpus already keeps Victors of Corinth, of Chalcedon, of Damascus and of Marseille, and three Russian presbyter-martyrs of the name; none of them died at Thessalonica with eleven companions, and the display name carries Thessalonica for that reason. The note the page attaches to the name — that Βίκτωρ is the Latin victor, the conqueror, and that two Byzantine iconographers bore it — is about the word.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 30 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/30/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/139/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
