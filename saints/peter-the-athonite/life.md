# Peter the Athonite

Peter was a Greek by birth, a soldier and a commander, sent with the armies to the war in Syria. The Greek force was beaten, and he was carried off with many other soldiers to the town of Samara in Arabia, beside the Euphrates, and thrown into prison in heavy irons. Sitting in his chains he searched his conscience and remembered how often he had thought of leaving the world and had not done it, and he understood that the captivity had come upon him for that; and he began to call on Saint Nicholas, to whom he had long prayed, vowing that if he were loosed he would not return home.

Nicholas came to him in a dream and said that his prayer had been heard, but that because he had himself been slow towards God's commandments he would not be loosed quickly; he was to go on asking and be patient. At a second appearing Nicholas told him to call as well upon the righteous Simeon, who received Christ in the temple at forty days, saying that the two of them praying together would be heard; and Peter did so, and was freed.

Afterwards, in a vision, he saw the Mother of God shining in glory with Nicholas standing before her and asking where this servant of hers should finish his life. She answered that his rest would be on the mountain of Athos, which her Son had given her as her own portion, and that she would defend all who struggled there in stillness. As the ship carrying him drew near Athos it stopped dead in a fair wind and deep water, and Peter asked the sailors to set him ashore there.

He lived on the mountain fifty-three years in fasting and prayer. Twice the devil came against him with the beasts and the creeping things of the mountain, and he drove them off with the sign of the Cross and the names of Christ and his Mother. In all those years he saw no human face and had no garment, the sky his roof and the ground his bed, burnt by the summer sun and frozen in winter. A hunter found him at last and heard his life from his own mouth, and was sent home for a year under a rule of abstinence, prayer and almsgiving. When the hunter came back with his brother and two monks, the saint was lying dead in the cave with his hands crossed on his breast. His brother, who was troubled by an unclean spirit, was healed at the relics; and the body was carried to a village under Macedonia, and from there by the bishop of the city to his own church.
