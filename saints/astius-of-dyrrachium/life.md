# Astius of Dyrrachium

Astius was bishop of Dyrrachium, the city that is Durrës in Albania today. Doxologia says he was arrested by Agricola, the Roman governor of the place, and tortured to death about the year 98 for refusing to worship the god Dionysus, in the persecution of Christians under the emperor Trajan; the same page opens by calling him a martyr of the second century, and the corpus records both readings rather than choose between them.

He had once had a dream, the page says, foretelling the suffering and the death that were near. He was arrested and beaten cruelly with rods of lead and with the whip, and did not give up Christ; then his body was smeared with honey, so that the stings of wasps and other insects should add to his pain, and after that he was crucified. Christians buried the martyr's body with reverence, and his feast is kept on 6 July.

In the same period, doxologia adds, many Christians fled to Albania to escape the persecution in Italy, among them seven martyrs — Peregrinus, Lucian, Pompeius, Hesychius, Papius, Saturninus and Germanus. Watching the bishop crucified, they praised his courage and his firmness, and for that they were seized, put in chains and drowned in the Adriatic. The waves carried their bodies ashore and Christians hid them in the sand; ninety years later the martyrs appeared to the bishop of Alexandria and told him to bury them and build a church over them. Their own day is 7 July.
