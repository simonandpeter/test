# Stratonicus, executioner of Paul and Juliana

saint.gr keeps five saints on one page for this day: the brother and sister [Paul](/saints/paul-of-ptolemais) and [Juliana](/saints/juliana-of-ptolemais) of Ptolemais, and with them the three men who were set to torture them. Paul and Juliana had been denounced to the emperor Aurelian as he passed through their city, and when he could not shake their faith he ordered them hard tortured; «και ενώ οι δήμιοι, Στρατόνικος, Κοδράτος και Ακάκιος, τους βασάνιζαν, θαύμασαν την ψυχική τους ανδρεία, ομολόγησαν τον Χριστό και αποκεφαλίστηκαν επί τόπου» — and while the executioners Stratonicus, [Codratus](/saints/codratus-the-executioner) and [Acacius](/saints/acacius-the-executioner) were torturing them, they marvelled at the courage of their souls, confessed Christ, and were beheaded on the spot.

That is the whole of what the page records of Stratonicus: the office he held, the two whose torture converted him, the confession and the beheading. It gives him no country and no city of his own, no year of birth and none of death — the only date on the page is the reign it gives Aurelian, 270 to 275 — no family, no relic and no hymn, and it does not say which of the three spoke first.

The distich the site prints over the three counts them and turns their trade against them: three men who had poured out the blood of many like water pour out the blood of the wise for Christ, by the sword. New executioners, the page says, finished what these three would not, burning the flesh of the brother and sister with lighted torches and then beheading them. The etymology block the page sets over the name lists an Athenian poet and lyre-player of Alexander's time and a sculptor of Cyzicus of the second century before Christ; both are notes on the word and neither is this man.

The Russian church keeps the five of them on this same day, and its line is the fuller of that calendar's two: «Мчч. Павла , сестры его Иулиании и с ними Кодрата , Акакия и Стратоника (ок. 273).» names all three of the soldiers, where the 17 августа ст. ст. line on which the corpus keeps the brother and sister has only «и прочих с ними», the rest with them. The life behind it is a single page titled for the brother and sister alone, and it calls these three воины, soldiers, where the Greek page called them δήμιοι, executioners. The type soldier is added here for that word; the display name the corpus chose off the Greek page is not this reader's to change and is left as it stands.

The Russian telling adds the occasion and takes nothing away. Aurelian came to Ptolemais in Phoenicia; the brother, standing among those who met him, signed himself with the cross, was seen doing it, and was seized and thrown into prison on the spot, and at the next day's trial confessed his faith openly and boldly. His sister, seeing his sufferings, accused the emperor before them all of injustice and cruelty and was put to the torture with him. They were beaten, their bodies torn with iron hooks and burned on heated gridirons, and nothing could break their patience; and «Трое воинов, которые истязали святых, пораженные величием духа мучеников, уверовали во Христа» — the three soldiers who were torturing the saints, struck by the greatness of the martyrs' spirit, believed in Christ. The page calls them God's new chosen and says they were executed immediately.

What the life has after that is not about these three, who were already dead, and it is fuller than the Greek summary. The emperor tried to win the sister by promising to take her to wife if she would deny Christ, and she refused him; he gave her to a brothel to be defiled, and the Lord kept her there too, for everyone who tried to lay a hand on her was struck blind.

Then he ordered the bodies of the martyrs burned again; the crowd standing about, looking at the sufferings of the innocent, began to murmur aloud, and he had the brother and sister beheaded at once. They went to the execution with glad faces, singing «Спасл бо еси нас Господи от стужающих нам и ненавидящих нас посрамил еси», which the page cites as Psalm 43:8. The year is this calendar's «ок. 273» and the reign it gives Aurelian is 270 to 275, which is the interval this folder already carried off the Greek page, so the dates do not move.
