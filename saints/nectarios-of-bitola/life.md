# Nectarios of Bitola

Nectarios was from the place now called Bitola, and his parents were right-believing and righteous before God. When the Agarenes were about to enslave that country, the mother of the venerable man, being out at their threshing floor, saw in her sleep the Most Pure Mother of God, who bade her take her husband and her sons and flee from that place at once; and as soon as she woke she ran home and told her husband the dream, and they took their children and fled and hid themselves where it seemed best to them. After the barbarians had plundered that country and all the parts about it, and the trouble had died down, they came out long afterwards, kept unharmed by the protection of the Mother of God, whom they thanked with their whole soul, with tears of grief for the barbarians' inroad and with joy for the salvation they had been granted.

His father, being old, left the world with his wife's consent and took with him the sons he had, Nectarios and another, to a monastery of the Unmercenary Saints, and there became the monk Pachomius. In his days God worked a wonder there: the Christians of the place had the custom of taking a portion of their fruits, whatever each one wished, and bringing it to that house. Nicholas, the son of the elder Pachomius, who was afterwards called Nectarios — wise from childhood and enlightened by God — seeing the wonder and thinking on the vision his mother had had, was drawn to the monastic life.

Finding the wonderful Dionysius the Younger, he clung to him and begged him earnestly not to part him from his company; and at last, cutting the hair of his head, he cut away all the cares of the world, and putting off worldly clothing he put off the passions too, and became a monk. But the envious devil, unable to bear his growth in the virtues, gave him war by his thoughts, and worked through a monk who envied him to part him from his good elder. The elder and Dionysius, hearing of it, trembled, and spoke many divine words to that monk out of the holy Scriptures and the divine fathers, that he should leave off his unjust envy.

They gave Nectarios their blessing and sent him to Daniel, who was first on the Holy Mountain, to stay with him for a time until the Lord should order what concerned him. While Nectarios was with Daniel, Philotheos, that true lover of God, died in deep old age and went to Christ whom he had desired. The blessed Dionysius also lived to a great age in a venerable and praiseworthy life, adorned with every virtue and above all with humility of mind, and went to the God he had longed for. See, brethren, how evil hatred and disobedience are: hatred and envy blind the eye of the soul, that is the mind of the man who has them, and disobedience parts a man from the friends of God.

God, seeing his longing, did not overlook him, but, willing to give him a greater crown in heaven, let him fall into many grievous bodily infirmities; and bearing them with a perfect greatness of soul and with great thanksgiving, living a truly venerable and holy life, he gave his blessed soul into the hands of God with a peaceful end, in the year of Christ one thousand five hundred, in the month of December, on the fifth day, and was numbered in heaven with those he had desired, the venerable fathers Philotheos and Dionysius.

His disciples buried him with all reverence, and after four years they translated his holy relics and found them full of an unspeakable sweet fragrance. So they made him a fine tomb on the north side of the church and laid his honoured relics there with reverence, painting his holy icon above them; and people came with much reverence and took sanctification of soul and body, and felt a spiritual fragrance from his consecrated relics.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 5 December, and names what the Romanian life leaves unnamed. saint.gr calls him the Athonite and gives his homeland as Monastiri of Macedonia, which is Bitola, and his birth in 1406. In some raid of the Agarenes, it says, his parents fled to a mountain to avoid being taken; and afterwards his father, with his wife's consent, took his two boys and became a monk under the name Pachomios at the monastery of the Holy Unmercenaries, which lay at the foot of that mountain.

Later a monk called Dionysios Iagaris, who belonged to the cell of the Archangels near Karyes of the Holy Mountain, took the boy — Nicholas was his baptismal name — and brought him to Athos. There he became a monk under the name Nectarios and attached himself to the spiritual elder [Philotheus](/saints/philotheus-of-karyes), who had the gift of discernment. From then on his ascetic life was such that his rare virtues became known over the whole Holy Mountain; and Satan tried many times, through his fellow ascetics too, to throw him into sin, but by the grace of God and his own great humility he met those trials and overcame them. He reposed in peace, the page says, on 5 December 1500.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and agrees with the other two books to the day of his death, 5 December 1500. It is the only one of the three to give his household and his cell. He was of Bitola, and he struggled first in the monastery of the Holy Unmercenaries near Bitola together with his own father, who had been tonsured under the name Pachomius; afterwards he crossed to Karyes, where he continued his labour under the elders [Philotheus](/saints/philotheus-of-karyes) and Dionysius in the cell of the Holy Archangel. When human envy, the assaults of the demons and a heavy sickness had all been overcome, he passed over to the kingdom of Christ; and his wonderworking relics, the book says, lie whole, incorrupt and fragrant in that same cell.
