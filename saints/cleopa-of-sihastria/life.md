# Cleopa of Sihăstria

Cleopa of Sihăstria was one of the great spiritual fathers of Moldavia; Patriarch Daniel called him a gift of God to the Romanian people. He was born on 10 April 1912 in the commune of Sulița in Botoșani county, to Alexandru and Ana Ilie, right-believing peasants blessed with ten children. Their house was like a church, all of them having a deep call to holy things, so that the mother and five of the children afterwards embraced the monastic life.

He was the fifth of the children and received the name Constantin at his baptism. Soon after he was born he fell gravely ill, and his mother, on the advice of an old priest, vowed him to the Mother of God and offered him before her wonderworking icon at the skete of Cozancea, not far from their village. The child was healed as by a wonder, and kept to the end of his life a particular devotion to the Most Pure Virgin who had saved him. From his early childhood he was a shepherd of sheep together with his older brothers, Vasile and Gheorghe.

Full of zeal for God and for spiritual things, the brothers often went to the skete of Cozancea, and there came to know the venerable [Paisie Olaru](/saints/paisie-of-sihastria), who afterwards guided their steps towards the monastic life. In 1929, at seventeen, Constantin entered the community of the skete of Sihăstria, under the guidance of Father Ioanichie Moroi, a tried and hard-striving abbot. He laboured there together with Vasile and Gheorghe, the latter becoming a monk with the name Gherasim; and both of them departed to the Lord, each at the age of twenty-six.

He was given a remarkable memory and held both Holy Scripture and many other spiritual books with ease, so that Father Dumitru Stăniloae, who had translated the Philokalia, said that Father Cleopa knew it better than he did. In 1937 the brother Constantin received the angelic habit at the hands of the abbot Ioanichie Moroi, and learned in a few years, from the old fathers, the craft of spiritual struggle and of humility in obedience. In 1942, the old abbot falling ill, he named Cleopa his successor in the government of the skete, so that he passed from shepherding the speechless flock to shepherding the reasoning one.

Many young men joined the community, drawn by his words and by the example of his life, until it numbered more than fifty; and so in 1947 the skete was raised to the rank of monastery and the young abbot made archimandrite. Patriarch Justinian Marina, seeing the gifts God had given him, named him in 1949 abbot of the princely monastery of Slatina, giving into his charge at the same time seven other monasteries and sketes: Sihăstria, Rarău, Moldovița, Sihla, Cămârzani, Râșca and Orata. At Slatina he turned an almost abandoned house into a monastery that was an example to the whole country.

In those years his gifts as a guide of souls and a preacher of the word drew crowds to the Church, and for that he began to be persecuted by the enemies of the faith: he was investigated and threatened again and again, and his life was in danger. Several times he withdrew into the surrounding forests so as not to be imprisoned, and there he struggled as the great hermits of old. In 1954 Patriarch Justinian called him out of the wilderness and sent him to strengthen the spiritual life of several monasteries; he returned to Sihăstria in 1956 and stayed until 1959, when a hard persecution of monks began, and he took to the mountains and forests again, living five years away from the atheist persecutors and writing there some of his most important books.

In 1964, after nearly ten years of a hermit's life, with the persecution easing and at the entreaty of the fathers of Sihăstria, he came back to the monastery, and from then to the end of his life he preached fruitfully by word, by deed and in writing. His time was divided between prayer and the service of his neighbour; he preached, counselled and heard confessions for hours together, and still found time to write books for the faithful — a Guide in the Orthodox Faith, sermons for Sundays and for the feasts of the Lord and the saints of the year, The Ascent towards the Resurrection, The Value of the Soul, On Dreams and Visions, On Holy Communion and others. His disciple Father Ioanichie Bălan printed them later, and they were sought out beyond the country's borders as well.

Knowing beforehand the time of his falling asleep, he departed to the everlasting dwellings of God in the year 1998, on the second day of December. He left many disciples who keep his memory alive, writings full of wisdom, and the example of a great spiritual father in whose heart the inward fire of faith had burned; the books about his life and teachings have been translated into ten languages. He was always a wonder for his simplicity and his depth: among his best-known sayings are "May Paradise eat you up" and "Patience, patience, patience", repeated insistently as the one thing needful for salvation beside almsgiving and prayer; and of himself he would say, "Why have you come to a rotten old man?" His grave and his cell have become a place of unbroken pilgrimage.
