# Alexis Shushania

Alexis was born on 23 September 1852 at Noqalaqevi in Georgia, to a devout Christian couple. His father died in 1868, after giving his sixteen-year-old son his blessing to take care of the family; and in the same year Alexis went on pilgrimage to Jerusalem and from there to Constantinople to visit his uncle Islam Shushania, a successful merchant and an intelligent, devout man. Taken with the trade he saw there, Alexis decided to become a merchant himself, and soon found that God's will for him was another.

One day he borrowed from his uncle a small icon of Saint John the Baptist, shut himself in his room, and there began a great inward struggle between his deep love for his mother, his sisters and brothers and his friends, and an unseen force calling him to the spiritual life. After some hours he asked at last how he could keep the charge his father had laid on him and answer God's call as well; and an inward voice answered, to his astonishment, that if he were to die now, somebody would take up his responsibilities — God would — and that he should therefore die to the world, entrust everything to God, and God would serve his family.

That turned his life. He shut himself in his room for months, reading the Scriptures and keeping a strict fast, until his uncle thought it best that they leave Constantinople and return to Georgia. His brothers and sisters were grieved when they understood that he had vowed himself to the monastic life, but his mother thanked God and blessed her son. At twenty he moved to the monastery of Teklati, took up a strict ascetic life, and went from village to village nursing the sick of tuberculosis, cholera and other grave illnesses and burying the homeless; his mother Elena, his younger sister Salome and his brother Bessarion followed him into the monastic life.

He was ordained priest at the monastery of Martvili and later tonsured into the Great Schema, and lived for a time on Mount Athos and at the Kiev Caves Lavra. Back in Georgia he gave himself again to the needy, and it is said that he kept a large cross in his cell to remind him without ceasing of the Saviour's Passion. He spent his last years with his cousins, the hieromonks Akepsimas and Pachomius, and went to the Lord on 18 January 1923; for forty days his cousins stayed shut in his humble cell for fear the communists would pull it down. He was buried at Teklati, and on 8 January 1960, with the blessing of Metropolitan Ephrem, his relics were moved to the Monastery of the Holy Archangels. He was canonised on 18 September 1995.
