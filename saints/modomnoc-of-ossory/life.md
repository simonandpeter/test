# Modomnoc of Ossory

Modomnoc is reckoned the patron of bees and of beekeepers. He lived in the first half of the sixth century and came of a noble family, the O'Neills of Ulster in the north of Ireland. From his youth he wished to serve God, and went to struggle as a monk at the monastery of Mynyw in Wales — now called Saint David's — under the guidance of the elder David.

There his obedience was the care of the monastery's bees, and he loved it. He came to have such a relation with them that he spoke to them and they swarmed about him all the time without ever stinging him. At the end of summer they gave him so much honey that he needed help to carry it to the monastery, and because of him and his faithful bees the monks of Mynyw had honey in abundance; he always thanked God for having sent him such an obedience. All the other monks were afraid of the bees and avoided walking in the corner of the garden where they lived, for they could be stung; the insects never stung Modomnoc.

After some years he decided to return to Ireland. The day before he left he took leave of his holy abbot and of the brothers, and came last into the garden to take leave of his dearest friends the bees, knowing he would miss them in Ireland. The bees, as though they felt their dear father was going, flew out of their hives — several hundred thousand of them — and began circling about him with a very loud humming, while the monks stood at a distance, amazed. Modomnoc got into the boat and set off for Ireland; and after a while he saw a little black cloud coming fast from the coast of Wales, which proved to be the bees of every hive at Mynyw, coming down eagerly and joyfully on their master's boat.

He took pity on the monks who would be left without their wonderful honey, and feared too for the bees, which might all perish on the voyage; twice he carried them back, and at last he agreed to do the abbot's will, but had to persuade the sailors a long while to take the bees with them. At his departure Saint David told him that the bees would do the sailors no harm so long as the saint stayed on board. Before setting out Modomnoc prayed that the bees, if it were God's will, might stay and live in the garden at Mynyw, since they might all die on the crossing; even so, all of them flew after their master. He made a shelter for them on the boat, and at his word they all moved into it and waited patiently for the end of the sea voyage, doing the sailors no harm at all.

On his arrival in Ireland, tradition says, he raised a church at Bremore near the town of Balbriggan, in what is now county Dublin, and beside the church he set his swarm in hives in a wonderfully beautiful garden like the garden at Mynyw. The place is known to this day as «the beekeeper's church», Llan-Beach-Aire; and from the oldest times the Irish have believed that both bees and beekeeping came into their country because of Saint Modomnoc. In Ireland he undertook missionary work and served as bishop of Ossory, and there is a tradition that he ended his days as a hermit at Tibraghny, in the south-west of the present county Kilkenny, where he was especially venerated. Our holy father Modomnoc fell asleep in the Lord about the year 550. The page carries no hymn.
