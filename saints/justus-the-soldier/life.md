# Justus the Soldier

doxologia.ro's calendar for 14 iulie gives Justus five lines and no more, and there is nothing else on the page. He was a Roman soldier in the army of the tribune Claudius, in the days of the emperor Magnentius, whose reign the page dates 350 to 353. Returning to Rome from a campaign he received holy baptism, and the tribune, who valued him, pressed him to give up his faith in Christ.

Justus would not, and was sent before the emperor to be judged. After many torments the young martyr was thrown into a red-hot furnace. The page names no place for the trial and gives no year for his death, and prints no troparion for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 14 July, and saint.gr adds what doxologia did not. He was a Roman by birth and a soldier by profession, marked out for his courage in battle and promoted after every war. On a campaign against the barbarians the army came into great danger and the self-sacrifice of the Christian soldiers saved it; Justus marvelled at their conduct and wanted to be a Christian, and was told it could only be by faith in Christ. One night, looking at the starry sky, he saw a shining cross with the word «ἀκολούθει» — follow — glowing about it, and answered at once: he left everything, was baptised, and followed Christ. When the tribune Claudius learned of it he tortured him with heated irons and then threw him into the fire, where he gave up his soul to God.
