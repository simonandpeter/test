# Terentius of Africa

Decius, the pagan emperor of Rome, sent orders through every part of his dominion that Christians be drawn by force to worship the idols and to taste what had been sacrificed to them, and that those who resisted be judged and punished. When the order reached Africa, the governor Fortunatianus sat in judgement, called the whole people to him and told them to sacrifice to the gods or be cruelly tortured and die badly; and he set the instruments of torture out in front of them. Many were frightened and fell away. Forty of the faithful stood firm and strengthened one another, remembering the Lord's word not to fear those who kill the body.

Fortunatianus told them he saw wise men, full of years, and marvelled that they should stand in such madness as to confess for God and King one whom the Jews had crucified as an evildoer. Terentius answered for them all: had the governor known the power of Christ crucified, he would have left the error of the idols and worshipped Him, for He is the Son of God, merciful and compassionate, who came down to earth by the good will of God His Father, joined His divinity to our humanity and endured the Cross willingly for our salvation.

At that the governor had Terentius, [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius), [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius) and [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius) thrown into the innermost prison under close guard, and set the other thirty-six — Zeno, Alexander and Theodore among them — before the judgement seat beside the idol's temple, telling them to sacrifice to the great god Heracles. They answered that they had told him many times that they were Christians, and he had them beaten without mercy with heavy staves and dried sinews, and put to death.

The four were brought out again and refused again, and were sent back to prison with heavy irons on their necks, their hands and feet bound, iron nails strewn on the ground beneath them and no Christian allowed in to bring them food. At midnight a great light shone in the prison and the Angel of the Lord stood before them: «Terentie, Africane, Maxime și Pompie, robii Dumnezeului Celui de sus, sculați-vă și întăriți-vă trupurile voastre» — rise and strengthen your bodies. He touched the iron chains and they broke and fell, and a table stood before them full of good things.

Then the governor had serpents, asps and vipers loosed into the prison. The guards looked in through the roof and saw the saints sitting and the Angel of the Lord standing and not letting the creatures come near; and in the morning the enchanters said their words over them and the creatures would not obey, but turned on the sorcerers and killed them and all the others there, and fled. Seeing the martyrs untouched, Fortunatianus condemned them to the sword; they went to death singing, and devout men buried their bodies with honour about two stadia from the city.

doxologia.ro's calendar line for the day names six — Terentie, Pompie, African, Maxim, [Macarie](/saints/macarius-companion-of-terentius) and [Dima](/saints/dimas-companion-of-terentius) — while the life page names only four of them, together with Zeno, Alexander and Theodore among the thirty-six; the last two names are kept here on the calendar's word alone.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and enumerates it. saint.gr's line for 10 Απριλίου is «Άγιοι Αφρικανός, Θεόδωρος, Μάξιμος, Πομπήιος, Τερέντιος και άλλοι τεσσαράκοντα Μάρτυρες», and its page dates the martyrdom and names the authorities: «μαρτύρησαν το έτος 250 μ.Χ., επί αυτοκράτορα Δεκίου (249-251 μ.Χ.) και ηγεμόνος Φουρτουνιανού, στην Καρθαγένη της Αφρικής» — in the year 250, under the emperor Decius and the governor Fortunianus, at Carthage in Africa. The torments it tells are one set of torments for them all: they were beaten until their inward parts could be seen, their hips were pierced with red-hot nails and vinegar and salt were thrown on the wounds, they prayed and pulled down the wooden images, the altars and the temples of the idolaters, and at the last they were beheaded.

The page then prints a name and a gloss for each of the rest, and the names are largely those of the Greek past: Alexander, Anaxarchus, Anaximenes, Aristides, Demaratus, Democles, Demosthenes, Dionysius, Epaminondas, Eteocles, Zeno, Elias, Heracles, Isaiah, Hephaestion, Themistocles, Theophrastus, Theseus, Thomas, Isocrates, Luke, Macarius, Miltiades, Mnesarchus, Xenophon, Homer, Parmenion, Pelopidas, Pericles, Pindar, Polybius, Polynicus, Prometheus, Sophocles, Socrates, Timothy, Titus, Philopoemen, Phocion and Chronius. That list holds forty names, while the prose above it says «μαζί με άλλους τριάντα εννέα Χριστιανούς», another thirty-nine, and the day's line says forty others; the page reconciles none of the three counts. Δημᾶς, whom the Romanian calendar keeps in this company, is not in saint.gr's list at all.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps the same company on a day of its own, the Julian 28 October, and prints it as «Мчч. Африкана, Терентия, Максима, Помпия и иных 36-ти (III)» — four men by name and thirty-six more unnamed, in the third century. Each of the four has a `/name/` page of its own behind the line, this one titled «Терентий, мч.», and each of those four pages lists this day and no other; so the Russian church has one commemoration of them where the Greek and the Romanian have theirs on 10 April. The calendar gives no place, no life page and no hymn: the forename and the rank word are the whole of what it says about this one, and the thirty-six it counts beside them it does not name.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same day, names four of them in its heading — Terentius, [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius), [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius) and [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius) — and says of the rest only that there were another thirty-six with them. It tells what the other calendars do not. They suffered for Christ and were crowned in the time of the emperor Decius: by the emperor's order the governor of Africa proclaimed to the whole people that all must sacrifice to the idols, and threatened every obstinate man with fearful torture, at which many fell away from the faith and bowed to the idols. But these forty martyrs stood unshaken, and for that were given over to torment. Terentius put heart into his companions: let us take care, brothers, not to deny Christ our God, lest He deny us before His heavenly Father and the holy angels.

The governor then divided them into two groups. The thirty-six he beheaded with the sword, after scourging them, scraping them and sprinkling salt on the open wounds; and these first four he threw into prison in heavy irons, at the neck and on the hands and on the feet. But an angel of God appeared in the prison and touched the irons of the bound men, and the irons fell off; and then the angel set them an abundant table and fed them. They were brought out again and tortured, and shut up again. The governor further ordered his sorcerers to gather as many venomous creatures as they could, snakes and scorpions, and to shut them into the same room with the martyrs; but the creatures would not touch the servants of God, and huddled into one corner and lay there three days, and when the prison was opened on the third day they rushed at the sorcerers and bit them.

At the last the governor pronounced the sentence of death on the four martyrs; and as they were led out to the beheading they sang psalms joyfully and praised God, who had counted them worthy of a martyr's death. They suffered honourably and were counted worthy of the kingdom in the year 250 — the year this folder already holds.
