# Mark the Shepherd

Mark the Shepherd, the brothers [Alexander](/saints/alexander-brother-of-alphaeus), [Alphaeus](/saints/alphaeus-brother-of-alexander) and [Zosimas](/saints/zosimas-brother-of-alexander), [Nikon](/saints/nikon-companion-of-mark-the-shepherd), [Neon](/saints/neon-companion-of-mark-the-shepherd), [Heliodorus](/saints/heliodorus-companion-of-mark-the-shepherd), and [the virgins and children](/saints/virgins-and-children-with-mark-the-shepherd) with them lived in the years of Diocletian, when Magnus was governor of Pisidia, about 290, the Greek synaxarion says.

Mark was an old man who kept sheep. Because he confessed that he was a Christian he was horribly tortured and sent to Claudiopolis, and there three brothers of the village of Katalyton, Alexander, Alphaeus and Zosimas, were called in to make bronze fetters for him; but as they began the work they felt their hands go numb, and marvelling at it they at once confessed Christ. They were martyred horribly, molten lead poured into their mouths, and then nailed to a rock; and Mark, after still more torture, was at last beheaded.

The other martyrs, Heliodorus, Nikon and Neon, died the same death with many virgins and children, all of them beheaded at a place called Moromilion. The synaxarion names none of the virgins and children.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same 28 September and keeps the epithet: «Марка пастыря», Mark the shepherd, in a line that runs his company together with the three brothers of Caluta and ends «и прочих (IV)», and the rest, fourth century. Its index of names places him and his three companions at Claudiopolis: «Марк Клавдиопольский, мч.»

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him third of the four it enumerates on this day and tells a different story, which is set down here beside the Greek one rather than reconciled with it. In the days of Diocletian a certain Magnus, governor of Antioch — not of Pisidia, as the Greek entry has him — went hunting with his soldiers, and as they drove some beast the soldiers saw it run to Mark the shepherd, who was guarding his flock there, and fawn on the man of God. Thirty soldiers who saw it were taught by Mark, believed in Christ, and were beheaded at once.

The governor bound Mark, brought him to the city, and called in three brother smiths, [Alexander](/saints/alexander-brother-of-alphaeus), [Alphaeus](/saints/alphaeus-brother-of-alexander) and [Zosimas](/saints/zosimas-brother-of-alexander), and ordered them to make instruments for Mark's torture. All three, after talking with him, received the faith of Christ and refused the order — where the Greek entry has their hands go numb over the work. The governor condemned them, and molten lead was poured into their mouths, as the Greek also says. Mark was then beheaded, and his head laid in the temple of Artemis, at which the temple fell down by the power of God.

The Prologue names neither Nikon, Neon nor Heliodorus, and says nothing of the virgins and children. Its thirty soldiers it leaves unnamed; the Greek calendar keeps on this same day a separate [Alexander and thirty martyrs with him](/saints/alexander-with-thirty-martyrs) whom it does not connect to Mark, and neither source joins the two companies.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 28 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2468/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026; the Russian church calendar's [28 September](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260928.html), read 4 October 2026; and the Serbian church's [Ohrid Prologue for the Julian 28 September](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-10-11&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
