# Demetrius of Philadelphia

saint.gr says he was born at Philadelphia in Asia Minor of a notable family; his father was a priest and was called Doukas, or Dongas. When his father died his mother went on bringing him up in the discipline and admonition of the Lord. Being modest and good-looking, he drew the envy of the Turks, who took advantage of his youth — he was thirteen — and made him a Muslim. He entered the service of a Turkish notable of Philadelphia and within a short time came into a great fortune.

For the fame of his bravery in the wars alongside the Turks he was offered, and accepted, the daughter of a Turkish official in marriage. But a little before the wedding his conscience turned on him for his denial, and he decided to come back to the Christian faith. So at twenty-five he presented himself formally before the Turkish governor and, in the presence of many Turkish officials, declared that he held the Mohammedan religion to be a deceit, that for that reason he renounced it, and that he accepted Christ. All those who were there rushed at him and beat him without mercy, and at the governor's order he was imprisoned.

During the night the governor sent teachers of the law, who tried by advice and by other means to bring Demetrius back to Islam; but he longed for martyrdom and stayed unpersuaded. The governor, who did not want his death, let him out of prison. Demetrius, to make amends for the sin of his apostasy, then went into a coffee house and began, in front of a crowd of Turks, to go through the errors of the Mohammedan religion one by one. Then he took the white turban off his head and the green Turkish coat off his back and trod them underfoot, saying: as I tread on these things that are the signs of your faith, so I tread on your faith and your law, and I deny it and turn away from it.

Enraged at his words, the Turks rushed at him, threw him to the ground and began to beat him with stones and sticks. They thought he was dead and decided to throw him on a fire; but Demetrius came round from his faint and said to his torturers, I have money to give you to buy wood to burn me with. Angrier still at that, they began to strike him with knives until the martyr gave up his spirit, on 2 June 1657. Full of rage, they tried to burn the martyr's body, and when they could not manage it they dismembered it with heavy irons.
