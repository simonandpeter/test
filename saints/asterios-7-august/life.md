# Asterios (7 August)

saint.gr's calendar for 7 August names Saint Asterios the venerable martyr and wonderworker, and then says what the synaxaria do not know: when and where he was martyred they do not report. What it has instead are two readings of him. In most of the codices, the page says, he is written down as «μάρτυς και συγκλητικός, δια ξίφους τελειωθείς» — a martyr and a senator, brought to his end by the sword. In the Synaxaristes of Saint Nikodemos he is given as «Oσιομάρτυς και θαυματουργός», a venerable martyr and a wonderworker, which is the reading the day's listing follows.

The page sets a couplet over him: Asterios, his neck cut through by the sword, shines among the choirs of the contestants as a star does. It glosses his name from the word for a star: the one who shines like a star. It prints no hymn to him, and it offers no year, no city and no judge to put with either of its two readings.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps him on the Julian 7 August under one heading with the soldier whose body he buried, and names the ranks of both at once: the first was a soldier and the second a Roman senator. In the time of the emperor Gallienus the holy [Marinus](/saints/marinus-martyr-17-march) was serving as a soldier at Caesarea in Palestine, and was cut down with the sword for the faith of Christ. Asterius the senator, who was a Christian, was present at his passion.

He took his own cloak off himself, wrapped the martyr’s body in it, took it up on his shoulder, carried it away and buried it honourably. The heathen, seeing it, cut him down with the sword too. They suffered honourably for Christ, the entry ends, about 260 — which is the first date this folder has held, saint.gr having given it no year, no city and no judge for either of its two readings of him. The same book tells this story a second time, on the Julian 17 March, where its heading names the soldier alone.
