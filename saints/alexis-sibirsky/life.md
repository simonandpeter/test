# Alexis (Sibirsky)

His life is one of two full ones the Russian calendar gives for the ten people it names on this line, and it opens not with him but with the order that killed him. In fulfilment of Stalin's order for the arrest of the clergy and laity of the Russian Church — which had in substance been defined as an anti-Soviet and therefore an anti-state organisation, and as such was to be destroyed — the whole clergy of the Novo-Karelsky district and the neighbouring Kozlovsky district of the Tver region was arrested in August and September 1937, with many wardens, members of church committees and lay people, whether they were heads of collective farms, ordinary members or still farming on their own.

Merciless torture was used on those under investigation in that time, and some of the arrested, under the weight of bodily suffering, testified against themselves and others and signed the protocols the investigators had drawn up. Not all agreed to it, and the page names him as one of the courageous pastors who did not.

He was born on 17 March 1870 in the village of Kozlovo in the province of Tver, in the family of a priest, Vasily Sibirsky. Out of the Tver seminary he was ordained priest to the church of his own village, and he served there the whole of his life until the last arrest and his martyr's end. In 1918 he saw the Bolsheviks' merciless reprisal against the peasants of the village of Nikolo-Gnezdovo who had stood up for the church of God; and he remembered how solemn the funerals had been of the first Tver martyrs of this century, two Orthodox laymen whom the page names and this corpus has no folder for.

In 1929 he was arrested for failing to meet the grain-requisition plan in time. The authorities allowed twenty-four hours for it; he did not get the grain to the collection point inside them, and for that he was sentenced to a year in a corrective labour camp and two years of exile, with all his property confiscated, personal effects included. On his return the holy archbishop [Thaddeus](/saints/thaddeus-of-tver) appointed him priest in the village of Kozlovo and made him dean.

The authorities looked for a pretext to arrest him again. During the spring sowing of 1936 somebody reported that he had prayed in church «за стахановскую весну» — for a Stakhanovite spring — which the authorities took as a breach of the law separating Church from state. Summoned at once to the district NKVD to explain, he said that he had not prayed for a Stakhanovite spring but for the abundance of the fruits of the earth, and that the words were taken from the service book. He was called to the village soviet many times besides, because the pious local people kept petitioning for leave to go round the houses with molebens and icons.

On 29 August 1937 the NKVD arrested him and shut him in the Tver prison. The interrogations went on every day for a month, with torture, and he held out and denied all the falsehoods the investigators brought against him. The record the calendar prints is a long exchange about who had come to his house: the priest Muravyev, the dean Orlov and the priest Sokolov, invited for his wife Olga Pavlovna's name-day on 4 June; a choirmaster he had known since childhood, who talked about living well in Leningrad and buying his daughter a piano; a priest looking for a parish in June 1936, to whom he recommended a vacancy. To each demand that he tell of the counter-revolutionary conversations at these visits he answered that there had been none, or that he did not remember any.

When the answers did not satisfy the investigator, he was put face to face with one of those who had agreed to sign false testimony. The witness listed a crowd of people, him among them, and explained the aim of the fascist-monarchist organisation they were all said to belong to: the overthrow of Soviet power and the restoration of capitalism with the help of foreign fascist states, by intervention and by an armed rising. Told that further obstinacy was useless, he answered that he had not been a member of any counter-revolutionary fascist-monarchist organisation, did not admit himself guilty of it, had taken no part in its gatherings and had never been at one. Pressed again with the claim that he was wholly exposed as a liar, he answered: I categorically deny my membership of the counter-revolutionary fascist-monarchist organisation and my participation in the gatherings.

On 11 November 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced the dean of the churches of the Kozlovo deanery to be shot, and he was shot a day after the decision, on 13 November 1937 — the civil date of this menologion day. Shot with him, the page says, were those who had borne false witness about him, about themselves and about others, being unable to bear the weight of the investigation. He was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops of the Russian Orthodox Church in August 2000, for veneration by the whole church.
