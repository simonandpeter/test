# Basileus of Amasea

Basileus was bishop of Amasea, the metropolis of Pontus, in the years when Licinius, having taken the whole East, fell away from Christ and returned to the idols he had been reared in. The life calls him a good man and full of spiritual gifts, and a man like the apostles; and the first thing it tells of him is that he was building a church inside the city, because until then the Christians of Amasea had only a very small one outside the walls.

Into that city came [Glaphyra](/saints/glaphyra), who had fled Nicomedia to escape Licinius. Basileus and the citizen Quintius hid her and charged her to speak to no one, lest every Christian in Amasea be endangered for her sake, and she gave for his church all the wealth the empress had given her. When a letter of hers was intercepted, the emperor ordered both of them arrested; but she died before the order came, and the governor took the bishop alone.

He went from Amasea to Nicomedia followed by two deacons, Parthenius and Theotimus, all of them ill-used on the road by the soldiers. At Nicomedia he was shut in prison, and the deacons lodged near it with a Christian named Elpidiphorus, a lover of strangers, who bribed with gold a gaoler he knew so that the three of them might come in to the bishop when they wished. They sang the daily offices with him in the prison, and the prayers of the night above all.

Before the day appointed for his trial, when the psalms were done and it was growing light, he said to the deacons that the temptations of the devil were rising against them and those of men were close at hand; that they should not fear nor faint at the troubles to come, but abide manfully and unshaken in the faith, keeping the eye of the soul awake toward Him who is able to turn grief into joy, weeping into gladness, tears into laughter and labours into rest. Then he sent them back to Amasea, and committed them to Elpidiphorus.

Brought before the emperor, he was charged first with Glaphyra — with having taken her in and hidden her and said nothing — and answered so boldly that Licinius sent him back to prison in anger. A tribune came after with the emperor's offer: pardon over Glaphyra, great gifts, and authority over the servants of the temple, if he would sacrifice. He answered that were the emperor to give him his whole empire it would never be as much as he wished to take from him, and told the tribune rather to repent and return to the Christ he had denied.

Then the emperor gave the word that he be beheaded and thrown into the sea, «ca să vedem de va putea Acel galileean să-l izbăvească» — to see whether that Galilean can deliver him. The tribune told him he stood between life and death and must choose; and Basileus rejoiced and said that he was ready not only for the sword and for drowning but for torments without number, and that while his soul was in his body he would not depart from the God who made him.

He was beaten first, and went to his death singing the psalms of David, with Elpidiphorus and the deacons and many Christians behind him. At the shore Elpidiphorus paid the soldiers to let him speak a little; he knelt on the sand, prayed that God would keep the flock over which He had set him, embraced the three of them as Paul once embraced the presbyters of Ephesus, and said to the executioner, «Săvârşeşte-ţi, prietene, porunca ta» — friend, do what you are commanded. Then he knelt again and stretched out his neck.

The soldiers would not sell Elpidiphorus the body, nor even the head, for fear the emperor would learn of it; they took a fishing boat, rowed out, and threw the head into the deep in one place and the body in another. John, presbyter of Nicomedia, stood on the shore and saw it all, and afterwards wrote down the passion.

That night an angel stood over Elpidiphorus three times in his sleep and told him the bishop had gone to Sinope and waited for them there. They took ship; and near the city an angel showed him where to cast the net. The fishermen drew up a dead man, and would have thrown him back; Elpidiphorus bought the haul from them, and found the head joined again to the body, with only the mark of the cutting upon it, and a sweet smell coming from it. He wrapped it in clean linen, hired a cart, and carried it to Amasea, where it was buried with honour in the church Basileus had built, all the Christian people weeping for him.
