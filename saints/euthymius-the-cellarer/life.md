# Euthymius the Cellarer

Euthymius was, according to the tradition the Romanian notice reports, an active disciple and fellow ascetic of [Athanasius of the Great Lavra](/saints/athanasius-the-athonite). He was born at Constantinople of distinguished and virtuous parents, and when he came to the Great Lavra he was given the obedience of steward and cellarer over the monastery's store of oil; and for that reason the house he founded later, though it was called Daphne, was better known by the name Docheiariou, that is, the cellarer's.

The building of the monastery of Daphne stood above the harbour that is called Daphne today, and was dedicated to Saint Nicholas. After a raid of the barbarians it was plundered and ruined, and Euthymius barely escaped alive — in order, the notice says, to found afterwards the new monastery of Docheiariou on the place where it stands today. There again he built a church in the name of Saint Nicholas, and cells besides, with endless toil and labour.

Later he set his nephew [Neophytos](/saints/neophytos-of-docheiariou) as abbot of his own monastery, and from then he lived in stillness, in which he gave up his spirit into the hands of God, being an old man of a hundred years, working the honey of virtue to the last. His memory is kept on 9 November. The notice, which doxologia.ro takes from Moses the Athonite's *Saints of the Holy Mountain*, gives no year and no century; and his page carries no Tropar tab, so the folder has no hymn.
