# Alphege of Canterbury

Alphege followed the monastic life from a young age, saint.gr says, and became a monk in the monastery of Bath in England. Later he was its abbot, and was marked out for the strictness of his life and for his ascetic struggles. In the year 984 he was elected bishop of Winchester, where he drew men's respect for his many virtues, and above all for his love and his charity towards the poor and the needy — so much did he care for them, the page says, that in his days no beggar was to be found in his province.

After twenty-two years as a bishop he took up, without wishing it, the throne of Canterbury, in the hard years of the Danish and Norwegian raids. He went so far as to offer himself willingly as a hostage to the invaders in order to save his flock. He endured many tortures, and at the last he was beheaded, in the year 1012.

That is the whole of the page. It names no companion and no judge, gives no year of birth, records no relic, prints no couplet and no hymn, and sets down the English form of his name beside the Greek one itself.
