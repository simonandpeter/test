# Peter (Dyakonov)

Peter Dyakonov was born on 13 January 1866 in the village of Polozovskoye in the Sarapul district of Vyatka province, the son of Theodore Dyakonov, a psalm-reader. At thirteen he entered the Perm seminary, left it after three years to sing in the bishop’s choir at Perm, and church singing shaped the rest of his life: he was given the sticharion at seventeen, and three years later Bishop Ephraim of Perm made him psalm-reader at the cathedral of the Transfiguration. In June 1887 he moved to Yekaterinburg as psalm-reader at the church of the Ascension and married Olga Ivanovna, then seventeen; they had several children, among them the daughters Faina (1888) and Olga (1890). Bishop Nathanael ordained him deacon on 1 October 1887; in 1891 Bishop Polycarp moved him to the cathedral of St Catherine, where he was ordained priest on 15 May 1891, taught singing in its parish school and led the choirs, and in 1898 taught singing at the new diocesan teachers’ courses. On 16 November 1902 he went to serve at the Ioanno-Bogoslovsky church at the Verkhnyaya Salda works in Verkhoturye district, where he lived the rest of his life as its rector, with Father [Alexis Kuznetsov](/saints/alexis-kuznetsov) serving beside him from 1909.

In 1917, about a month after the Soviet power was set up in Petrograd, a soviet of workers’, peasants’ and soldiers’ deputies was formed at Verkhnyaya Salda, and early in 1918 the ironworks was nationalised; the two priests watched many of their parishioners, the young among them, fall away from the Church. As the Red units fell back through Nizhnyaya Salda at the end of September 1918, many hostages, clergy among them, were shot near the railway station there and buried where they fell, and on 1 October 1918 Father [Alexis](/saints/alexis-kuznetsov) and Father Peter were killed there by Red Army men. When the townspeople came afterwards they found cut-off heads, mutilated bodies and blood mixed with earth. On 9 October, the feast of the apostle John, the two priests and the other villagers killed for the faith were given a Christian funeral: Alexis was buried inside the church fence, Peter in the parish cemetery. They were glorified in 2002.

The Russian calendar keeps the two on 18 September old style.
