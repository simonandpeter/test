# Gabriel of Prusa

Gabriel was bishop of Ganos and Chora, saint.gr says. He was at Constantinople in 1657 and succeeded on the patriarchal throne Parthenios III, whom the Turks had hanged. But because he was unlettered he left the ecumenical throne after twelve days and came to Prusa, where he became presiding metropolitan of Prusa and remained ten years.

There he was accused of having brought some Muslim to the Christian faith, and he was taken before the sultan and the vizier. They required the martyr to deny his faith and accept Islam; Gabriel rejected the proposal with courage, and the result was that he was tortured hard. In the end they hanged him at Prusa on the third of December 1659.

That is the whole of the account, and the page gives him no birthplace, no year of birth, no relic and no hymn. Its couplet gives him the two crowns the day's own line gives him: Gabriel the new has taken a double crown, as a hierarch and as a contender of the Lord. Beside the entry it links the synaxis of all the archbishops and patriarchs of Constantinople and the synaxis of the neomartyrs after the fall of the City; both are feasts and neither was read for this folder.
