# Clement of Ancyra

Clement was born at Ancyra, the chief city of Galatia, about two hundred and fifty years after the Nativity, in the reign of Valerian. His parents were not of one faith: his father was a Greek and a servant of the idols, his mother Sophia a Christian born of Christian parents. She had been married to the unbelieving man against her will, her own parents trusting the apostle's word that the unbelieving husband is sanctified by the believing wife; but his heart was hardened, and he laboured instead to draw her to his own religion. She prayed that they might be parted, since they were not of one spirit, and he died, leaving behind a firstborn son still in swaddling clothes.

Sophia fed that son, the life says, more on the right faith than on milk, brought him to Christ and had him baptised, and gave him the name Clement, which in Greek is a vine shoot. What follows is a passion of twenty-eight years. The page has him tortured in eleven places by eleven rulers — at Ancyra by Domitian the proconsul of Galatia, at Rome by the emperor Diocletian, at Nicomedia by the governor Agrippinus, at Ancyra again by Curichius, at Amisus by Dometius, at Tarsus by the emperor Maximian and then by Sacerdon, by Maximus and by the senator Aphrodisius, at Ancyra once more, and at last by the governor of Alexandria. Each tormentor, it says, sent him on to the next as a thing unheard of.

At Rome, when Diocletian had him beaten with sinews and hung up and torn with iron claws until the bones showed, Clement told him that the body he was tearing was not the one that came out of his mother's womb, for that one the proconsul Domitian had torn away altogether, and Christ had clothed him in this new one. It was during that stay that the faithful who came to him in prison were taken out of the city and killed, all but one young man who escaped the sword — not for fear of death, but wanting greater torments. That was [Agathangelus](/saints/agathangelus), who afterwards came to Clement, and whose coming Clement received as the coming of an angel of God; from then the two suffered together.

The end came at Ancyra. Clement was serving the divine Liturgy in the church in the cave when the pagans told the governor, who came in with soldiers and found him standing before the altar at the bloodless sacrifice. At his word a soldier struck the bishop across the neck, and his head fell on the holy table among the gifts, and his blood reddened the sacrifice and the whole sanctuary. Two deacons, Christopher and Chariton, were cut down in the sanctuary with him. Sophia took her son's body — her son by name, she says, and her spiritual father and shepherd in fact — and buried him with the two deacons near the grave of Agathangelus in the cave church, on the twenty-third day of January.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, with [Agathangelus](/saints/agathangelus), on one page. saint.gr says that the head of Saint Clement is at the holy monastery of Prousos in Eurytania, and that portions of the relic are kept elsewhere; and it prints two apolytikia for him, the first of which plays on his name — «Κλῆμα ὁσιότητος», a vine-branch of holiness — and calls him at once a fellow contender of the martyrs and a sharer of the hierarchs' throne, which is the double rank the Romanian calendar also gives him.
