# Antipas of Calapodești

Antipas was born in 1816 into a family of believing peasants in the village of Calapodești in Bacău, and was called Alexandru Luchian at his baptism. The land of Moldavia brought him forth in the nineteenth century, and at twenty he was led to the monastic struggle. He spent his first two years, 1836 and 1837, in the community of the Căldărușani monastery, and then went to Mount Athos and settled in the Romanian skete of Prodromu, where more than eighty Romanian hermits were living.

There he learned the craft of the spiritual struggle from the most chosen of the Athonite monks and became known through the skete for his fasting and his labour; there he was tonsured, and was given by God the gift of tears and of unceasing prayer. After nearly fifteen years at Prodromu he lived four more at the monastery of Esphigmenou, where he received the great and angelic habit of the schema under the name Antipas and was ordained deacon. The whole mountain honoured him as a great hermit and a worker of the Jesus Prayer, and he was granted the gifts of healing and of foresight.

Loving solitude and humility more than honour and praise, in 1860 he left the Holy Mountain and came back to Moldavia, to the monasteries around Iași. Finding himself pressed about by the faithful, for his name had gone through the whole country, after three years he went to venerate the relics of the fathers of the Kyiv Caves, and then to the monasteries of the north, where his holiness astonished everyone. Hearing of the Orthodox monastery of Valaam on an island in Lake Ladoga near the borders of Karelia, he settled there in 1865, among monks who were unequalled teachers of the Jesus Prayer.

He struggled at Valaam another seventeen years and was granted the priesthood. His greatest labour was the fiery prayer of the heart, and to it he added long fasting, all-night vigils, tears, prostrations and other unknown labours of the spirit; he served the Liturgy often, and prayed much for the world and for the country he was born in. He knew men's hidden thoughts and guided many on the way of salvation, and his name was known through Karelia and northern Russia. Among his disciples the most advanced was the hieroschemamonk Pimen. He gave up his soul in peace on 10 January 1882 and was buried in the crypt at Valaam, the only Romanian monk to have struggled in that famous hesychast house of the European north.

In 1883 the fathers of Valaam, seeing that he was venerated as a saint by his disciples and by the faithful, ordered his life to be written in short; Pimen, his cell disciple, wrote it that same year. It was printed at Saint Petersburg in 1883 and again in 1893 and spread through Russia, Finland and the Holy Mountain, and his glorification was made on Athos in 1906, twenty-four years after his death. He stands in the Russian January Menaion printed at the Panteleimon monastery on Athos, on the tenth day, at page 46. doxologia.ro marks his day with a cross.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, as «Όσιος Αντίπας ο Αθωνίτης», Antipas the Athonite, and dates him 1816 to 1882. saint.gr gives his name in the world as Alexander, his birth in the village of Calapodești in Moldavia to poor but devout parents — the deacon Constantin Luchian and Ecaterina Manase, who later became the nun Elisabeta — and his trade as a bookbinder, by which he earned what he needed to live. Burning with divine longing he left the world, stayed a short while at Neamț, and fled to Athos: four years at Esphigmenou, and then the skete of the Honourable Forerunner, the one called Romanian, where in a cupboard among the ruins of his hut he found a wonderworking icon of the Mother of God and guarded it. The page adds that his relics lie at the monastery of Valaam in Russia.
