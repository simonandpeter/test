# Didymus, companion of Theodora

saint.gr says Didymus and [Theodora](/saints/theodora-companion-of-didymus) took the martyr's crown in the fiercest persecution the Church knew, under Diocletian. Theodora had been arrested at Alexandria by the eparch Eustratius; she confessed Christ, was beaten and imprisoned twice over, and stayed unshaken, until the governor, to revenge himself on her modesty, shut her up in a brothel so that her body should be defiled.

Didymus was a man of rank at Alexandria. As soon as he heard of it he decided to take the risk of freeing her from the shame that threatened her. He put on his uniform, went to the brothel and asked to see Theodora privately; and taking the advantage of the darkness, he dressed her in his own clothes and so made her escape easy. When Eustratius learnt what had happened he boiled with rage, and ordered Didymus beheaded and his body afterwards thrown into the fire.

Theodora would not take her own safety selfishly: she presented herself to the eparch, reproached him sternly for the killing of Didymus, and was thrown into the flames after him. The page gives no year, names no relics and prints no hymn; its couplet for Didymus turns on his name: «Σὺν τῇ συνάθλῳ, Δίδυμε, τμηθεὶς φλέγῃ, / Φέρων σὺν αὐτῇ δίδυμον τιμωρίαν» — with your fellow-contender, Didymus, cut down you are burnt, bearing with her a twin punishment.
