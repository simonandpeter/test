# Bretanion of Tomis

Bretanion, the page says, seems to have been a disciple of [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great). He was made bishop of Tomis about the year 360 and led the church of Tomis until close to 381. What is known of him comes from two historians, Sozomen and Theodoret of Cyrus, and the doxologia page is careful to say so rather than to fill the gap with anything else.

Sozomen tells of the visit of the Arian emperor Valens to Tomis in 369, as he was returning from a campaign against the Goths. In his Church History he says that the emperor went into the cathedral church and asked bishop Bretanion to enter into communion with the Arians, whom he favoured, and to serve together with them. The bishop defended the right faith and, in the words the page quotes, «a vorbit împăratului cu îndrăzneală despre hotărârile celor 318 Sfinți Părinți de la Sinodul I de la Niceea (325) împotriva lui Arie, pe care nu le putea calca» — he spoke to the emperor with boldness about the decisions of the three hundred and eighteen fathers of the first council of Nicaea against Arius, which he could not transgress.

The page gives him one other piece of work. He had an important part in drawing up and sending the documents for the transfer of the relics of Sabbas the Goth to Cappadocia, at the request of Basil the Great; and it was Bretanion who wrote the martyr's act of Sabbas the Goth.
