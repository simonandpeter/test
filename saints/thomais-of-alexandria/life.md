# Thomais of Alexandria

Thomais was born in Alexandria to right-believing parents and was brought up by them in learning and in good ways. At fifteen they married her lawfully to a young Christian man, and she lived in her husband's house with honour, praised for her chastity, her gentleness and her kindness. She lived in the same house as her father-in-law, and he — by Satan's working, the life says — began to think cunningly about his son's wife, tempted by her beauty; he looked for an opportunity and found none, but coaxed her daily with kindnesses and kissed her often. Thomais, being pure in mind, took his kisses for a father's affection and was shy before him as before a father.

Her husband was a fisherman, and one night other fishermen came and took him out to fish. When the young man had left the house the old man rose and began to force his daughter-in-law to sin. She was terrified, as at an evil she had never expected, and began to fight him off: «Ce faci tată? Fă-ți semnul Crucii pe fața ta și te du, că diavolesc este lucrul acesta» — what are you doing, father? Cross yourself and go, for this thing is of the devil.

He would not leave off, and pressed her with shameless words; the more she defended herself and begged and counselled him to give over, the harder he forced her. A sword hung on the wall above the bed. The old man reached it down and drew it and began to threaten her: if you will not obey me, with this sword I will cut off your head. She answered that even if he cut her in pieces she would never do such a thing. Then, filled with rage, he struck her hard with the sword and killed her, cutting her in two; and she gave up her soul into the hands of God, choosing to die rather than anger Him by such a crime and defile her body and her husband's bed. Blindness fell on the murderer at once.

When the other fishermen came and knocked at the door, calling his son out to work, he answered from inside that his son had gone fishing, and asked them to show him the doors of his own house, for he could not find them. They opened and went in and found the old man with his hands and clothes full of blood, feeling along the walls, and the dead woman lying on the ground cut in two and rolling in her own blood. They were horrified and asked who had done the murder; the old man confessed his sin and begged them to take him before the court and give him up to the judgement of the law, to receive what his deeds deserved.

The page ends with an argument about what to call her. Though she had a husband, it says, she is counted among the martyrs without any doubt — not so much because she was very young and had brought her virginity to her marriage in purity, as because it was for purity that she suffered, and so was shown worthy to be numbered before the Lord with the choirs of the pure virgins. The Great Menaion calls her a venerable-martyr, a title that properly belongs only to nuns who suffer for Christ; but although Thomais was not a nun by rank, she showed the monastic virtue in her chastity and suffered for it to the shedding of blood, and the blessed father Daniel of Sketis did not hesitate to lay her in the monastic burial place among the venerable fathers.
