# Leander of Seville

Leander was born at Cartagena about the year 534 and died at Seville on 13 March 600 or 601, archbishop of that city. doxologia's notice makes his great contribution the spread of the catholic faith in the West, and lays to his account the conversion of the Visigoths, who then ruled Spain, from Arianism to Orthodoxy. He had a true monastic vocation, and drew his brothers after him into the monastic life — Isidore of Seville, Fulgentius and Florentina, each of them a saint of the Church of Christ in turn.

He wrote a great deal, but only two of his works survive: a rule for convents of women, *On the training of virgins and contempt for the things of the world*, and a *Homily on the victory of the Church*. Of him his brother Isidore wrote: «Leander was a monk, and as a monk he was raised to the rank of bishop of the Church of Seville, in the province of Baetica. He was a man who charmed by his speech, of extraordinary intelligence, and famous both for his manner of life and for his teaching. By his faith and his labour the nation of the Goths was converted from the Arian madness to the catholic faith.»

doxologia's notice is that short, and it cites the Romanian edition of his rule for virgins, published at Iași in 2016. The page prints no hymn for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and calls him a teacher of the Church and the enlightener of the Spaniards. saint.gr puts him in the sixth century and makes him the child of an aristocratic family: his father was a duke of Byzantine descent, his mother the firstborn daughter of the Visigoth king Leovigild, who reigned at Seville, the capital of the Visigothic kingdom. He took up the monastic life very early and was marked out for his learning and his virtues, and for those reasons the Church made him bishop in 579. He founded a theological school, meant for the spreading of Orthodoxy but also for the cultivation of the sciences and the arts generally among the people of that still barbarous kingdom. The two royal children Hermenegild and Reccared, his nephews on his mother's side, were among his pupils; Hermenegild was brought up in the streams of Orthodoxy, and his faith was strengthened further by his devout wife Ingund, daughter of Sigebert, king of the Franks. When his father moved his capital to Toledo and assigned Seville to him for his residence, a persecution of the Orthodox broke out, and the heretical Leovigild came into conflict with his Orthodox son — an account the page runs on with at length, further than this reading followed.

What the page's close says is that a council announced that the peoples of the Goths and the Suevi, now united, were returning to the unity of the Church; that Leander presided over it; and that he gave the rest of his life to teaching his flock, by his enlightened example first and by his inspired writings. He prepared his brother St Isidore, whom saint.gr keeps on 4 April, to be his successor on the throne of Seville and the glory of the Church of Spain; he helped his sister St Florentina to become the founder and abbess of forty monasteries with thousands of nuns, writing a monastic rule for her that has been called ever since «Κανὼν τοῦ Ἁγίου Λεάνδρου», the Canon of St Leander; and he organised the divine worship of the Church of Spain, the rite liturgically called Mozarabic. Having endured many adversities and trials, the page ends, he gave his soul to the Lord in 600 or 601, «πιθανώς στις 13 Μαρτίου» — probably on 13 March, which is the day doxologia's life gives too.
