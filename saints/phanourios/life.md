# Phanourios the Great Martyr

Phanourios is among the best loved saints of the Greek people, who keep his feast every year on 27 August; yet he was unknown for centuries. He became known through the finding of his icon on Rhodes in the fourteenth century, when old houses were being dug out on the south side of the old wall: an ancient church came to light, full of ruined icons, and among them one well preserved, on which Neilos II Diasporinos, metropolitan of Rhodes (1355–1369), read the name “the holy Phano…”.

The icon showed the saint as a young soldier holding a cross with a lit candle on it, and round it twelve scenes of his martyrdom: standing among soldiers and judged by the governor; struck on the mouth and head with stones; scourged stretched on the ground; seated naked and scraped with iron claws; shut in prison; tortured before the governor’s tribunal; burned on his limbs with lit candles; bound on a rack; unharmed among wild beasts; pressed under a great stone; in an idol temple holding live coals in his palms while the devil fled wailing through the air; and standing in a furnace with his hands raised in prayer. Neilos rebuilt the church in his name and, it seems, composed his service. Among his miracles the synaxarion tells of three Cretan priests captured by Hagarenes on their way home from ordination at Koroni and freed, after their spiritual father Jonas had invoked him on Neilos’s advice; Jonas carried a copy of the icon back to Crete, where the saint has been kept with festivity since.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 27 August — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/908/saint.aspx); read 23 August 2026.*
