# Sophia of Suzdal

She was Solomonia in the world, a daughter of the boyar Yuri Konstantinovich Saburov, and in 1505 the heir to the throne of Moscow chose her for his wife. The marriage was not a happy one, the reading says, because she proved to be barren; and to have an heir the grand prince resolved to marry a second time, and on 25 November 1525 ordered her to be made a nun. Tonsured by force with the name Sophia, she was sent under guard to the convent of the Protecting Veil at Suzdal.

There, the reading says, she drove the thoughts of the world out of her heart by her struggles and gave herself wholly to God. A prince who wrote of those years called her a venerable martyr; the manuscript calendars call her the holy and righteous princess Sophia the nun, who was in the maidens’ convent of the Protecting Veil, a wonderworker. She was honoured as a saint in the reign of the tsar who followed her husband’s son, and the tsaritsa of that reign sent to Suzdal a velvet pall figured with the Saviour and the saints to lie over her; the patriarch wrote to the archbishop of Suzdal about the singing of panikhidas and molebens over her.

She died in 1542. A sacristan of the city, in his description of Suzdal, set down several cases of miraculous healing at her tomb, and the calendar’s own day page keeps a troparion and a kontakion for her, which pray that Suzdal be kept from the inroads of the heathen and from civil war.
