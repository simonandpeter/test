# Agabus

Agabus, whom the page also calls Agabos, is numbered among the seventy apostles. Having a prophetic spirit, he foresaw a famine over the whole world, which came to pass in the time of Claudius Caesar; the page quotes the Acts for it: prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch, and one of them named Agabus signified by the Spirit that there would be a great famine over all the world, which happened in the days of Claudius Caesar.

He also prophesied to the apostle Paul that he would be given into the hands of the barbarians by the Jews at Jerusalem. Taking Paul's girdle and binding his own hands and feet with it, he said: thus says the Holy Spirit, so shall the Jews at Jerusalem bind the man whose girdle this is, and deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles. He preached Christ in the parts appointed to him and turned many to Christ God, and Paul mentions him in his epistles.

The Church keeps him on 8 April together with the apostles [Herodion](/saints/herodion-the-apostle), [Rufus](/saints/rufus-apostle-of-thebes), [Phlegon](/saints/phlegon-of-marathon), [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle) and [Hermes](/saints/hermes-apostle-of-dalmatia), all of whom, the page says, ended on the same day as a result of the torments they were put to. It gives no year for any of them, and no place for his own end.

The page closes with the painter's rule: the Hermeneia of Dionysius of Fourna, in the Bucharest edition of 2000, says that the apostle Agabus is to be painted as an old man with his beard parted in two.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, in the same company of four, and tells the same act out of the Acts. saint.gr says that Agabus was the one the evangelist Luke mentions, who took the Apostle Paul's girdle, bound his own hands and feet with it, and prophesied of him: «Τάδε λέγει το Πνεύμα το Άγιον. Τον άνδρα, ου εστίν ή ζώνη αυτή, ούτω δήσουσιν εις Ιερουσαλήμ οι Ιουδαίοι και παραδώσουσιν εις χείρας εθνών» (Acts 21.11). And so it came about, the page adds, for the Jews not only bound Paul but tried to kill him. Having preached the Gospel in the place where he lived, Agabus departed in peace to the Lord. The page's couplet for him calls him the prophet-apostle whose soul the Master, the saviour of souls, calls to himself.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the six together on this day and adds nothing about this one that is not already written above. Saint Agabus had the prophetic spirit, it says, and the Acts of the Apostles record two of his prophecies: first, that he foretold a great famine over the whole world, which came to pass in the time of Claudius Caesar; and second, that meeting the apostle Paul at Caesarea, as Paul was going up to Jerusalem, he took Paul's girdle and bound his own hands and feet with it, saying — thus says the Holy Spirit, the man whose girdle this is, so shall the Jews bind him at Jerusalem. It gives him no year, no see and no account of his end. It closes of all six that, like bees of Christ, they carried the honey of the Gospel about into various countries, suffering much for the love of Christ, and that all of them passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ the beloved.

**The Russian church calendar keeps the six of them together on the Julian 8 April**, which is the day the Romanian and the Serbian keep, and its line is «Апп. от 70 Иродиона, Агава, Асинкрита, Руфа, Флегонта, Ерма и иже с ними (I).» — six genitives under one rank word, a century in a Roman numeral, and a company left open at the end, «и иже с ними», *and those with them*, which is a phrase no other calendar read for this folder prints. The six name links behind the line, pages 1871, 46, 282, 1886, 2313 and 791, all lead to one life of the company; three of them give in their own titles the see the day’s line withholds, and those same three close with «сщмч.», hieromartyr, which is a rank none of these folders carries. The rank is set down here and `types` is left as it was.

That life sets them in the number of the seventy apostles chosen by Christ and sent out to preach, points to the synaxis of the Seventy on 4 января, and then disposes of four of them in a single sentence: «апостол Руф, которого приветствует святой апостол Павел в Послании к Римлянам (Рим. 16, 11), был епископом в греческом городе Фивы; апостол Асинкрит (Рим. 16, 14) епископом в Гиркании (Малая Азия); апостол Флегонт – в городе Марафоне (Фракия), апостол Ерм – в Далматии». It closes that all of them bore cruel sufferings for their fearless service to Christ and were counted worthy of a martyr’s end — which is what the Romanian page says of all six and the Greek page says of two.

**His name page carries a second reading, and it gives him both a rank and a place the other books do not.** It is titled «Священномученик Агав, апостол от 70-ти, пророк» — hieromartyr, apostle of the Seventy, prophet — where this folder carries apostle, prophet and martyr; and in four sentences it says that he foretold the great famine **«в г. Антиохии»**, at Antioch, where the company’s life and the Acts behind every other account read here put the famine over the whole world. It adds that he foretold the apostle Paul’s being put in bonds and his sufferings at Jerusalem, and that he preached Christ in various countries and turned many pagans to the faith; the binding with Paul’s girdle, which is the act all three other calendars tell, is not in it. Both readings stand and neither `types` nor `dates` is touched.

**The day prints one hymn for the company, and it is the first hymn in Russian this folder has had.** It is a kontakion in the second tone, «Явистеся Христовы ученицы и апостоли всечестнии», and it names all six of them in order before asking them to pray for the forgiveness of the sins of those who sing them. He is named second in it, «Агаве и Руфе», and the folder held no Russian hymn before.
