# Agabus

Agabus, whom the page also calls Agabos, is numbered among the seventy apostles. Having a prophetic spirit, he foresaw a famine over the whole world, which came to pass in the time of Claudius Caesar; the page quotes the Acts for it: prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch, and one of them named Agabus signified by the Spirit that there would be a great famine over all the world, which happened in the days of Claudius Caesar.

He also prophesied to the apostle Paul that he would be given into the hands of the barbarians by the Jews at Jerusalem. Taking Paul's girdle and binding his own hands and feet with it, he said: thus says the Holy Spirit, so shall the Jews at Jerusalem bind the man whose girdle this is, and deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles. He preached Christ in the parts appointed to him and turned many to Christ God, and Paul mentions him in his epistles.

The Church keeps him on 8 April together with the apostles [Herodion](/saints/herodion-the-apostle), [Rufus](/saints/rufus-apostle-of-thebes), [Phlegon](/saints/phlegon-of-marathon), [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle) and [Hermes](/saints/hermes-apostle-of-dalmatia), all of whom, the page says, ended on the same day as a result of the torments they were put to. It gives no year for any of them, and no place for his own end.

The page closes with the painter's rule: the Hermeneia of Dionysius of Fourna, in the Bucharest edition of 2000, says that the apostle Agabus is to be painted as an old man with his beard parted in two.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, in the same company of four, and tells the same act out of the Acts. saint.gr says that Agabus was the one the evangelist Luke mentions, who took the Apostle Paul's girdle, bound his own hands and feet with it, and prophesied of him: «Τάδε λέγει το Πνεύμα το Άγιον. Τον άνδρα, ου εστίν ή ζώνη αυτή, ούτω δήσουσιν εις Ιερουσαλήμ οι Ιουδαίοι και παραδώσουσιν εις χείρας εθνών» (Acts 21.11). And so it came about, the page adds, for the Jews not only bound Paul but tried to kill him. Having preached the Gospel in the place where he lived, Agabus departed in peace to the Lord. The page's couplet for him calls him the prophet-apostle whose soul the Master, the saviour of souls, calls to himself.
