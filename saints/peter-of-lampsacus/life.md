# Peter of Lampsacus

In a time of persecution of the Church of God, when the soldiers of Christ were laying down their lives for their Lord and the whole earth was reddening with the blood of martyrs, a handsome young man named Peter, strong in body and brave in spirit in the Christian faith, was seized at Lampsacus, a city of the Hellespont, and brought before the governor Optimus for examination. Asked whether he was a Christian, he answered that he truly was; and the governor set the orders of the emperors before his eyes and told him to sacrifice to the great goddess Venus.

He wondered, the young man said, that the governor should urge him to bow to a defiled and dissolute woman who had done such ugly things that it was a shame even to name them. Do you not punish men now, he asked, for daring to do the kind of things your goddess did? If you call her an open harlot, how shall I bow to her and offer her sacrifice? It falls to me to worship the living and true God, the king of all the ages, Christ my Lord, and to bring Him the sacrifice of prayer and of humility and of praise.

The governor had him stretched on the wheel and his body worked with wood and iron until his bones were broken. The more he was tormented the stronger and braver he showed himself in the power of Christ, who strengthened him; he laughed at the governor's folly, and lifting his eyes to heaven said: I thank Thee, Lord Jesus Christ, who hast given me such strength and such patience; make me worthy to overcome the ungodly enemy to the end. When the governor saw that the torments could not overcome him, he ordered him beheaded with the sword.

The same life carries the deaths of the others who suffered with him at Lampsacus in the reign of Decius: [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-of-lampsacus), Andrew and Paul were killed with stones, and the virgin Christina with the sword. Among them was one called Nicomachus, who confessed Christ under torture and then, when the crown was all but in his hands, changed from his good confession, cried out that he had never been a Christian and was ready to sacrifice — which the life sets down as plainly as the rest.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day, on one page with the seven others of his company, and it is the Greek that names the goddess. saint.gr says all eight were martyred in the years of the emperor Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251; and of Peter, that he was from Lampsacus, and that when they brought him before the governor to sacrifice to Aphrodite he confessed Christ boldly, whereupon they crushed his whole body with chains and staves, and so he gave up his spirit to God. Its couplet for him plays on the instrument: thrown into the middle of the wheels, Peter let go the wheels of this unstable life. The page adds that [Paul](/saints/paul-companion-of-andrew) and [Andrew](/saints/andrew-companion-of-paul) were Mesopotamians and soldiers of Decius who became soldiers of Christ at Athens and were stoned with [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-of-lampsacus) and Christina, and that [Heraclius](/saints/heraclius-of-athens), Paulinus and [Benedimus](/saints/benedimus-of-athens) were Athenians who fought the idols and the philosophers and were beheaded.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 18 May in an entry of five, headed for Peter, [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-of-lampsacus), [Andrew](/saints/andrew-companion-of-paul), [Paul](/saints/paul-companion-of-andrew) and [Christina](/saints/christina-martyr-18-may), and it gives each of them one word: Peter a fine young man, Dionysius a man of standing, Andrew and Paul soldiers, and Christina a virgin of sixteen years. All of them, it says, confessed Christ the Lord bravely and bore tortures and death for His name. 

It then tells the end of the one who did not. A certain Nicomachus, who was tortured with them, denied Christ in the middle of the tortures, and at once went out of his mind, and like a man possessed gnawed his own flesh and foamed at the mouth until he expired. The year, the Prologue says, was 250. The heading names him first of the five.

The Russian calendar tells the five as one trial and begins with him. He suffered first, a young man, at Lampsacus on the Hellespont, brought before the governor Опитимин, before whom he confessed his faith in Christ without fear. He was pressed to renounce the Lord and to worship the idol of the goddess Venus, and refused, declaring aloud that a Christian will not bow to the image of a harlot. He was cruelly tortured, bore it, thanking the Lord Jesus Christ who gave him help, and was beheaded with the sword. The calendar dates the whole company only by the reign of the emperor Decius.
