# Thaddeus of Tver

saint.gr's calendar for 18 December keeps Saint Thaddeus, archbishop of Tver, and the whole of what its page says of him is one sentence: Saint Thaddeus was archbishop of Tver and was martyred in the year 1937. The heading above that sentence gives the same year for his death and gives none for his birth, and there is nothing else on the page at all.

So the Greek entry is four things and no more: the fixed day, the see of Tver, the martyrdom, and the year. The page names no place of death and no court, tells nothing of how he died, lists no relic, prints no hymn, and gives nothing of his life before the see.

The Russian calendar has the life saint.gr's one sentence stands in for, in two parts behind its index page. In the world he was Ivan Vasilyevich Uspensky, born on 12 November 1872 in the village of Naruksovo in the Lukoyanov district of Nizhny Novgorod province, to the priest Vasily and his wife Lidia, who had seven sons and two daughters. His grandfather was a priest too, honoured at home as a man of deep faith and a loving, meek and forbearing heart, and of all the grandchildren he loved Ivan best and called him the bishop.

From the Nizhny Novgorod seminary he went to the Moscow Theological Academy, whose rector then was Archimandrite Antony (Khrapovitsky); the two grew close and later became friends. The page says of him what it says of a certain kind of man — that there are people appointed by God from childhood to a particular service and kept and prepared by grace for it. He kept a diary every day from his youth, and the struggle of a soul for an incorruptible beauty is in it as in a mirror.

He held the Vladimir-Volynsk see, was then deprived of liberty for nearly five years with one short break, and served afterwards at Astrakhan, at Saratov in 1927 and 1928, and at Tver. A man who knew him at Astrakhan and at Tver wrote that in both cities he was surrounded by the love of the faithful, who came to his services in uncounted numbers, and that he answered them with hour-long sermons at every service and with visits to the churches every day of the week. At Tver the same cabman drove him everywhere, and the crowds that followed the carriage irritated the authorities.

The mass arrests began in the summer of 1937: nearly the whole clergy of Tver and of the region was taken, and at Kashin many of the clergy and laity were shot with the retired bishop Gregory (Lebedev) at their head. On 20 December, at about eight in the evening, the NKVD came for him; they turned the house over until five in the morning and found neither money nor anything of value. Ten days after the arrest he was sentenced to be shot, accused of heading a church-monarchist organisation with close ties to the liquidated church-fascist one at Kashin, and the archival note the page cites has the Troika's sentence carried out on 31 December 1937.

It is told, the page says, that he was drowned in a pit of filth. The prison doctor warned the faithful that he would soon be taken out for burial, and on 2 January 1938, at about four in the afternoon, the cart came from the prison across the frozen Volga. In the spring after Pascha of 1938 women opened the grave and laid him in a plain coffin, and one of them put a Paschal egg in his hand; a cross was set over the place with an inscription on it, and the authorities destroyed it soon after. The cemetery church was pulled down, most of the monuments and crosses with it, and the exact place of the grave was forgotten.

The faithful of Tver kept the memory of it through all those years. With the blessing of Archbishop Victor of Tver and Kashin the hieromonk Damascene set out to find the remains, and in the autumn of 1990 one of the women who had searched for years, Yu. E. Toporkova, found the exact place of the burial; an examination in Moscow confirmed that the remains were his. The rehabilitation came through the Tver prosecutor's office in 1991, the archbishop wrote twice to the Synodal Commission on canonisation, in 1991 and again in 1996, and the calendar keeps the finding of the relics on 13 October 1993.
