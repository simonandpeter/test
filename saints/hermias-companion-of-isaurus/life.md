# Hermias, companion of Isaurus

All of them contended for Christ at Apollonia, saint.gr says, when Numerian was emperor, whom the page dates 283 to 284. The first three were Athenians, and they embraced the Christian faith which the Apostle Paul had preached with such warmth to the ancient Athenians. Having bound themselves as brothers in Christ with the other three as well, all six together were a pattern of the keeping of the Lord's commandments, showing thereby their great love for him — for the Lord himself said how he wishes those who follow him to love him: if you love me, keep my commandments.

The life of the six brotherly friends was denounced to the eparch Tripontius, who tried by every means to make them sacrifice to the idols, and in vain: they confessed Christ crucified. Then he put them through a series of hard tortures, out of which in a wonderful way they came unharmed, and they managed to bring many to the Christian faith. In the end they were beheaded. The page gives no year of death and no other detail, and the company it names is [Isaurus](/saints/isaurus-the-deacon), [Basil](/saints/basil-companion-of-isaurus), [Innocent](/saints/innocent-of-apollonia), Hermias, [Felix](/saints/felix-of-apollonia) and [Peregrinus](/saints/peregrinus-of-apollonia).

saint.gr prints for them one apolytikion, in the third tone, and it is a service of nine: it names Isaurus and Felix, Hermias and Peregrinus, Innocent, Manuel and Basil, Ismael and Sabel — the six of Apollonia together with the three brothers out of Persia whom the same day keeps. The page gives no kontakion and no year for any of them.
