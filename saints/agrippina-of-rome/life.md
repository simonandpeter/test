# Agrippina of Rome

Agrippina the virgin was born and brought up in old Rome. From her youth she gave herself to God and became, as the Apostle says, a sweet savour of Christ among those who are saved; for as a fragrant rose adorns a garden, so she by the example of her clean and virtuous life adorned the hearts of the faithful and filled them with that fragrance, driving off every temptation of the passions.

She made her soul beautiful with the purity of virginity and was betrothed to Christ, and for His sake ran boldly to the torments in the reign of Valerian, and was given over to many beatings for the love of her bridegroom. She was beaten with staves and suffered the breaking of her bones; she was stripped of her clothes and stretched out in bonds, and from these she was freed by an unseen hand, the angel of the Lord loosing her and strengthening her. So she broke all that impiety, and in the torments gave up her soul to God.

Other handmaids of Christ — Bassa, Paula and Agathonica — took the martyr's body secretly and carried it out of the city of Rome, and going from place to place and travelling by sea they came to the province of Sicily and buried the holy body there. When Sicily had received those honourable martyr's relics, doxologia's life says, it was at once delivered from the dark malice of the devil.

It was defended also from the Agarenes by her prayers: when they came against the town where her church stood and dared to make war on it, meaning to take it, they were suddenly given over to utter destruction. And from her honourable tomb healings of every sickness were given, and all who came in faith were cleansed, so that every infirmity was driven off by her holy prayers and by the grace of Christ our God. The life names Valerian and prints no year, so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 23 June. saint.gr says she was born and martyred at Rome; that from a young age she developed the deepest Christian mind and gave herself to the service of her Lord and Redeemer, and for that reason spent her whole fortune on the relief of the poor and the healing of the sick; and that for the love of her heavenly bridegroom she avoided marriage and chose to become a bride of Christ. As often as the church of Rome had need of her, Agrippina was the first to run and offer her valuable services, and she offered not material goods only but spiritual ones, teaching the Christian faith and leading crowds of the misled into the way of truth. This God-pleasing activity of hers could not stay hidden for long: in the year 262 she was denounced to the authorities as an overturner of the ancestral worship of the idols, and when they understood that they were not going to change her mind they put her to fresh and frightful torments, which her body could not bear, and she gave up her holy soul in a martyr's and a glorious way. Three Christian women, Bassa, Paula and Agathonike, took up her venerable body and, after wandering a good while, ended in Sicily, where they buried it.
