# Euphrosynus the Cook

Euphrosynus, an unlettered and rustic villager, left the world in manhood
and entered a coenobium, where he was tonsured. Despised by his
fellow-monks for his lack of education and his simplicity, he embraced the
humility of Christ and served them in the kitchen — serving, the Romanian
Lives say, not as to men but as to God, with great humility and submission,
labouring in obedience day and night; and as he was always blackened with
the charcoal and the ashes all laughed at him and mocked him, and he took
beatings too from the more careless, who found occasion in his silence and
forbearance. But the blessed man bore the insults and humiliations with
greatness of heart, and now bathed in sweat, now panting and cheerful, ran
the course of the virtues in secret, escaping men’s notice. In that
coenobium was a virtuous priest who for three years with fasting and
prayer begged God to show him the good things which God has prepared for
those who love him (1 Corinthians 2:9); one night as he slept his mind was
caught up into paradise, into a most delightful and fragrant garden full of
every kind of tree, sweet flowers and the clearest running waters, which
no human tongue can describe, and as he wondered whose this marvellous
paradise might be he saw the monastery’s cook, Euphrosynus, in the middle
of the garden enjoying its unspeakable good things. Astonished, he asked
how he came to be there, and whether this was the place God had prepared
for those who love him. I, honoured father, said Euphrosynus, as you know,
do not know letters — it is from you that I hear what the apostle says.
But because we have forced ourselves a very little, we see a part of what
God has prepared for those who love him, for a man wearing flesh could not
bear to see more. The priest asked whether he had been there before; by
God’s grace, he answered, I am always here, and I am the keeper of the
garden. Then the priest showed him three very fine large apples and asked
whether he had the power to give them to him, and Euphrosynus picked them
at once and put them in his cassock. At that moment the semantron sounded
for matins; the priest, leaping from his bed, thought he had dreamed, and
was amazed to find the three fruits of paradise in his cassock. In the
church he saw Euphrosynus standing as always in his stall, and falling at
his feet begged him to say where he had been that night. I was there,
father, he answered, where you found me. And what did you give me, servant
of God? tell me. You asked for three apples and I gave them to you, the
cook answered humbly. The priest made him a prostration and went to his
place, and after the dismissal brought the three apples from his cell,
showed them to the brethren and told what had happened in the night; they
marvelled and glorified God, then cut the apples in pieces and put them on
a tray, and all who received of the blessing of the Master’s garden were
healed of every sickness. But the blessed Euphrosynus, while the monks
were listening closely to the priest’s story, opened the side door of the
church and, fleeing the glory of men, left the monastery and was never seen
again. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 11 September.
