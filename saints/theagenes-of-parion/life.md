# Theagenes of Parion

Theagenes was bishop of the city of Parion, which saint.gr explains took its name from the Parians, the people of the island of Paros, who built it; the city stood between Cyzicus and Lampsacus. He was brought before the tribune Zelikinthios and there confessed Christ to be the true God.

For that he was beaten without mercy, and then bound hand and foot and thrown into the depth of the sea. So, the page says, he finished the course of his martyrdom and received from the Lord the unfading crown of eternal glory. Its epigram tells him to be cast into the midst of the sea, for though he sink there he will swim out within heaven. No year is given for him and none is supplied here.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 2 января old style and gives the year the Greek page withholds. He was bishop of the Asian city of Parion at the beginning of the fourth century, in the reign of Licinius, who ruled from 307 to 324 as the colleague of [Constantine the Great](/saints/constantine-the-great). The tribune, whom the page names Заликинтий, pressed him to lay down his holy orders, deny Christ and enter military service.

On his flat refusal he was beaten without mercy with staves and thrown into prison, where he refused food; and so he was condemned to be drowned in the sea. Before the execution he asked for time to pray, and while he prayed an extraordinary light shone about him. The sailors and some of the soldiers charged with drowning him were struck by that light and turned to Christ, but the rest made haste and threw him into the sea. He received his martyr's end, the page says, about the year 320. Christians afterwards took his body out of the water and buried it by the city wall, and many healings began to be worked in that place.
