# Zosimus of Syracuse

Zosimus came of Sicily. His devout parents had an estate near the monastery of Saint Lucy, and their small son was soon loved by the monks there for his devotion to the things of God. The abbot of the monastery took care that the boy should learn letters, and when he had grown he taught the letters of theology himself. After the abbot's death he became his successor, being reckoned by the bishop of Syracuse the worthiest of all.

So great, saint.gr says, was his devout activity that after the death of the bishop of Syracuse the voice of the people brought him to the episcopal office. From that new place he served brilliantly the teaching of the divine word and all his pastoral duties, and having lived holily he died peacefully at the age of fifty. The page gives no year of his birth and none of his repose, and prints no hymn for him.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 30 марта ст. ст. where the Greek keeps him on 21 January, and the two accounts are one man told by two hands. Both have the monastery, the abbacy and then the see of Syracuse in that order. What the Russian adds is the beginning and the end. He was born, it says, in answer to the earnest prayers of devout parents who had long been childless, and when he was seven they gave him to a monastery to be brought up; there he was tonsured as he grew, and he was superior of the house for forty years. He was ordained bishop of Syracuse by the pope the page names «Феодор», whose years it gives as 641 to 649. He was known for his mercy and for owning nothing, and he governed his flock firmly by word and by example. At the end of his life he fell gravely ill and bore the suffering with magnanimity and humility; about the year 662 he died, after thirteen years of governing the flock, and afterwards many sick people were healed at the mere touch of his tomb.

Two things in the two accounts do not fit, and neither is resolved here. The Greek page has him die at fifty; the Russian has him forty years an abbot and thirteen more a bishop, which cannot be fitted inside fifty years of life, and it is the Russian that supplies the only year either page gives. And the day line calls him «Прп.», venerable, where the name link and the title of the life both call him «Святитель», a hierarch, and the line itself gives him the see in the same breath — «Прп. Зосимы , еп. Сиракузского». The `venerable` of this folder is the day line’s word and the `bishop` and `abbot` were already here; no page was overruled to get either.
