# Luke of Emesa

Luke was a deacon at Emesa, and doxologia.ro prints one life for him and for the two taken with him. In the days of the emperor Numerian, in the year 284, it says, a persecution was raised against the Christians, and word was sent to the governor of the city of Emesa to seize [Silvanus](/saints/silvanus-of-emesa), the bishop there; and he was seized at once together with Luke the deacon and [Mocius](/saints/mocius-of-emesa) the reader.

Bound, the three stood before the governor. He questioned them closely, and hearing that they confessed Christ to be true God and put the servants of the idols under anathema, he fell into great rage and fury, put them to many and various torments, and at the last gave sentence that they should fight with beasts.

So the saints were brought out into the assembly of the people and many kinds of beasts were let loose. The saints prayed God that their life might end there, and God took the souls of his servants at once; and then the wild beasts, holding the relics of the saints in awe, drew back and did not touch their bodies at all. When night came the bodies were taken and buried with honour, with thanks given to God. The Romanian page names no persecutor beyond the emperor and gives nothing of Luke's life before that day.

The Greek calendar keeps the three together on the same twenty-ninth of January, and saint.gr gives their offices as well as their names: Silvanus the bishop, Luke the deacon and Mocius the reader. All three, it says, came from the city of the Emesenes in Coele Syria, in the years of the emperor Numerian, which it dates 284. When they were told to deny Christ they repeated their confession at the top of their voices, and so were scourged hard and shut in prison; and when after some days they were brought out exhausted and examined, to see whether their resolve had been bent, the three were found unshaken and unbending, and the same torments were repeated, again without result. Then they were brought to the amphitheatre where the beast-fights were held, to find a terrible death there from the hungry beasts. The Christians took up their relics and buried them with much reverence and love. The page prints for them no apolytikion and no kontakion.
