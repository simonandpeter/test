# Sixtus II, Pope of Rome

Sixtus, an Athenian by birth and well educated, had preached in Spain and was made bishop of Rome after the martyrdom of Pope Stephen (253–257) — a time, the life says, when whoever was elected to the Roman see was elected to a certain death. He was soon seized and imprisoned with his two deacons, [Felicissimus](/saints/felicissimus-the-deacon) and [Agapitus](/saints/agapitus-the-deacon).

His archdeacon [Lawrence](/saints/lawrence-of-rome) met him on the way to prison and begged to share his death; Sixtus told him that an easy death was for the old man, heavier sufferings for him, and that in three days he would follow — and sent him to sell the church’s treasures for the poor. Sixtus and the two deacons were beheaded on 6 August 258. The Prologue calls him first a philosopher and then a Christian, and makes the same prophecy to Lawrence.
