# Gleb

Gleb, David in holy baptism, is one of the first Russian martyrs, the passion-bearers; he suffered with his brother prince Boris, Roman in baptism. After Boris’s murder Svyatopolk the Accursed sent a messenger to the younger brother Gleb with a false report that their father, the grand prince Vladimir — already dead — was ill, meaning to kill by treachery a possible claimant to the throne of Kyiv. Deceived, Gleb hurried towards Kyiv with a small retinue; a warning from his brother Yaroslav, which reached him near Smolensk, did not stop the saint, who expected no such villainy from his brother. Not far from Smolensk the murderers overtook Gleb’s boat; he did not resist, only meekly begged them to spare his still young life, and at the murderers’ order his own cook cut his throat. The prince’s body was buried in a lonely place near Smolensk, between two logs, in a plain wooden coffin, in 1015. In 1019–1020 his brother Yaroslav sought out the grave, and the body, found incorrupt, was carried to Vyshgorod near Kyiv and buried beside Boris; later the brothers’ relics were translated to the church of Basil the Great, where many wonders were worked at their tomb, and Metropolitan John of Kyiv composed their service and set their joint feast on 24 July, kept since the first half of the eleventh century. The Russian Church has honoured the passion-bearing brothers from of old as constant helpers of their native land in its hardest trials — before the battle of the Neva in 1240 — the Sretensky calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. The Russian calendar keeps Gleb alone on 5 September (Julian), the day of his death, with a troparion and kontakion.
