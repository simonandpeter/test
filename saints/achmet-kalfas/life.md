# Achmet Kalfas

saint.gr's calendar for 24 December keeps Achmet the Kalfas, a new martyr of Constantinople. He was a Turk by birth and a Muslim at first, the page says, and worked as clerk to the chief accountant. In his house, on the authority of the Great Synaxaristes, were two Orthodox Christian slave women, Russians: the younger he kept as a wife, being unmarried, and the older as a servant, and the older he allowed to go to the church of the Orthodox on the feasts, from which she would bring back the antidoron and often the holy water for the younger woman too.

When Achmet talked with the younger woman he would smell an unutterable fragrance come out of her mouth, and he pressed her to tell him where it came from. She did not know herself at first, and answered that she ate nothing with any particular scent to it; but one day she understood that it must be the antidoron, and explained to him what the antidoron and the holy water are and who it is the Christians worship. He did not believe her and asked how such a wonder could happen, and she told him that their religion is a living one and that in Christ all things are possible.

He could not stop thinking of it, and at last dressed himself in Christian clothes and went to the church of the Patriarchate to see for himself how the Liturgy of the Christians was done. There, the page says, he saw the priest raised above the floor of the church and full of light as he went towards the holy doors, and the Patriarch sending out rays from his fingers whenever he blessed the Christians, which fell upon their heads — and what shook him was that the rays lit the heads of the Christians only and not his own. It happened so two or three times. Coming out, he thought he was not the old Achmet.

After that he wanted no further proof. He went secretly to the priest of his quarter, was catechised and baptised in the name of the Holy Trinity, and lived a virtuous life, communing of the Holy Mysteries with a particular joy; but to the day of his martyrdom he stayed a crypto-Christian, which is perhaps why the Christian name given him at his baptism has not come down. Then at a gathering of the Muslim grandees of the city he was asked what is the greatest thing in the world, and said in a loud voice: greatest of all is the faith of the Christians. His confession struck them like lightning, and he began without fear to charge the falsehood of their religion. By the order of the local ruler he was seized, tortured and beheaded, on 3 May of the year 1682, at the place called Keaphane-Bakhtse.
