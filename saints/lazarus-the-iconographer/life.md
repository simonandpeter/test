# Lazarus the Iconographer

The whole of the life doxologia prints for him is one paragraph, and this is it. From a small child he became a monk and learned the craft of the painter — of iconography, the painting of icons and of frescoes. Beside the hard life and the abstinence he practised, the blessed man pressed himself also to almsgiving, and for that he received the grace of the priesthood.

Once a priest, he made war on every heresy, and suffered such troubles — not only from the Nestorians, the Eutychians and the Dioscorians, but from the fighters against the icons — that the page says it is impossible to tell them in words. He was sent to old Rome as well, to help the fatherly and apostolic doctrines that the iconoclasts were making war on.

Returning from Rome to Constantinople, he was sent to Rome a second time for the same cause, and on that second journey, about halfway, he fell ill from the disorders of the time and gave up his soul into the hands of God. His honoured body was brought to the queen of cities and laid in the monastery called Evander's. The page gives no year, no birthplace and no other name.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 17 November, and dates his death 867. saint.gr says Lazarus embraced the monastic life from childhood, learned the painter's art and practised it as his trade, took care from his youth to harden himself and to learn self-control, and was merciful to the destitute and to all who needed his help; and that being marked out for his deep faith and the wealth of his virtues he was honoured with the office of the priesthood.

Once ordained he began hard struggles against all the heresies. He suffered many persecutions and afflictions from the followers of Eutyches, Nestorius and Dioscorus, and not from them only but from the godless iconoclasts as well. He was so ardent a supporter of the faith that he was sent to Rome to contend for the apostolic and patristic dogmas, and returned from Rome to Constantinople with great successes. Going to Rome again for the same struggles, he fell ill in the middle of the journey, and so died in peace in 867; his honoured body was translated and buried in the monastery of Euandros.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 17 November, «Прп. Лазаря иконописца (ок. 857).», and its life page makes him a presbyter of Constantinople who kept a strict ascetic life and painted holy icons. Under the iconoclast emperor Theophilus, who reigned from 829 to 842, he was seized and, after heavy tortures, thrown into prison; the empress Theodora intervened and saved him from a certain execution. He died, that page says, in 857, on his way back from Rome, where he had been sent on an embassy on church business to pope Benedict III; his remains were carried to Constantinople and buried in the church of Saint Evander. The 857 of this page and the 867 the Greek calendar gives are ten years apart, and the interval above now holds both.
