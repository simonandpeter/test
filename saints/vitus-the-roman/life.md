# Vitus the Roman

The Russian calendar keeps three martyrs together on this day and the longest of the three readings is his. He was the son of Гилас, a nobleman of Sicily and a pagan. While still a boy he came to love Christ and prayed to Him without ceasing, and the page says the gift of wonders was given him: he healed the sick and brought many pagans to the faith. The governor Валериан called the father in and told him to turn the son from it, for an edict of persecution had just gone out and the whole household stood in danger. Гилас could not, and beat him.

Brought before the governor, the boy confessed his faith and refused outright to sacrifice. He was beaten again; and when Валериан put out his hand to order the torture increased, the hand withered on the spot. The saint prayed and it was healed, and the governor broke off the trial and gave the boy back to his father with orders to turn him at any cost. Гилас tried pleasure instead — rich food, feasts and amusements, and beautiful girls brought to the house — and got nothing by it. Angels appeared and prayed beside the boy; the father, coming in and seeing them, went blind at once. He promised to renounce his idols and was healed, and then did not keep the promise, and his love for his son turned into hatred and a resolve to kill him.

[Modestus, tutor of Vitus](/saints/modestus-tutor-of-vitus) and [Crescentia, nurse of Vitus](/saints/crescentia-nurse-of-vitus), who were Christians, took the boy secretly out of his father's house to save him. At the river they found a boat; an angel went aboard with them and brought them to Lucania in Italy, where they lived hidden from their pursuers, and where the boy went on healing the sick and turning pagans to the faith until he was talked of there too.

He and his tutor were brought at last before Диоклетиан, who was struck by the boy's beauty and pressed him hard to sacrifice. He answered by convicting idolatry of madness, and healed the emperor's son of a demon. Honours, fame and riches were offered him for a renunciation; he refused and confessed Christ as before, and was shut in prison with his tutor, where Christ appeared to them and their chains fell off. Calling the wonder sorcery, Диоклетиан had him thrown into a cauldron of molten lead, and he stood in it as in cool water; a great lion was loosed on him and lay down at his feet and licked them.

The two were hung on stakes and raked with iron claws, and the nurse came out of the crowd of onlookers, confessed herself a Christian and reproached the emperor for his cruelty, and was given the same torture. Then he called on God to save them, and an earthquake began: buildings fell and killed many of the pagans, and Диоклетиан fled to his palace. An angel took the martyrs down from the stakes and carried them back to Lucania. There he prayed that God would receive their souls in peace and withhold nothing from any who should keep their memory, and a voice from heaven said that the prayer was heard; and they gave up their souls gladly, about the year 303. The page adds that his relics were taken to Prague, and that Вячеслав, the prince of Bohemia, built a church in his honour there and was afterwards buried in it.
