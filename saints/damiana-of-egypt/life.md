# Damiana of Egypt, with 40 virgins

Damiana was Egyptian, a virgin martyr who confessed Christ, the page says, at the end of the third century and the beginning of the fourth. She is one of the most loved saints of the Orthodox Church in Egypt: a convent of nuns stands near Belqas on the site where her house was, and among Egyptian women her name is one of the commonest given. She was the daughter of Marcus, governor of Borolus in the northern part of the Nile Delta, and was brought up a Christian from childhood.

At fifteen she gave her life to Christ, and asked her father to build her a place apart where she could live the life she wanted. She moved into that retired house with forty other virgins, and they kept the ascetic life and praised God together. When the emperor Diocletian ordered every governor to worship and serve the idols, Marcus obeyed to keep the emperor's favour; and when Damiana heard of it she confronted her father, brought him back to the faith, and he confessed before the emperor that he was a Christian and was beheaded for it.

Diocletian turned his anger on her next, and gave his soldiers orders to torture her unless she served the idols. She bore what was prepared for her and was thrown into a dark prison, where an angel of the Lord came and healed her wounds. At that the persecutors flayed her and threw her into a cauldron of boiling water; and when she still would not deny Christ she was condemned to death by the sword, together with the forty virgins. The page names none of the forty.
