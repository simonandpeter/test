# Callisthene, daughter of Audactus

Callisthene was the daughter of [Audactus of Ephesus](/saints/audactus-of-ephesus), the Greek synaxarion says. When her father would not give her in marriage to Maximinus, an idolater, and was exiled and beheaded for it, she cut her hair, put on men’s clothes and hid somewhere in Nicomedia. Eight years later she went to Thrace and lived near a family whose daughter had diseased eyes; she healed the girl, and the parents wanted to marry their healed daughter to her. Then she told them the truth about herself, and when they had all glorified God together she left. She came to know Constantia, the sister of Constantine the Great, and with her help not only recovered her father’s property but brought his relics from Melitene to Ephesus, built a church in his name and laid them in it. She lived the rest of her life in the manner of the apostles and died in peace. Its verse has her beholding the beauty of God, whose divine strength had sheltered her.

The Romanian synaxarion tells it the same way: when Maximinus died and Licinius took the empire, she went to his empress, a Christian and the sister of Constantine the Great, who kept her by her and made her as a mother to her son, and so she recovered all her father’s property that Maximinus had taken; she brought his relics from Mesopotamia to Asia and built a martyr’s church in his name, and after an apostolic life passed to the Lord. The Romanian calendar calls her a martyr and keeps her on 4 October on her own line; the Greek keeps her the same day with her father. Neither gives a year.
