# Demetrius (Rozanov) (12 November)

days.pravoslavie.ru names him fifth of seven on one line for 12 ноября ст. ст. and prints no life for him; the name page behind his place in the line, 13704, gives him as «Димитрий (Розанов), иерей, сщмч.» and carries no text, and the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru prints a long life. He was born on 15 August 1890 in Moscow, the son of a priest, Ioann Petrovich Rozanov, who served in one of the Moscow churches until his death in 1920.

He finished the Moscow theological seminary in 1911 and was ordained deacon in 1912 to the church in the village of Шестаково in the Glinsky district of the Ekaterinoslav province. In 1914 he was moved to one of the Moscow churches and raised protodeacon, and in 1919 he was ordained priest; he served afterwards in the church of the holy martyrs Florus and Laurus на Зацепе.

On 1 January 1933 he was arrested together with the priests, a deacon and members of the church council of that church, and all of them were shut up in the Butyrka prison. The authorities charged him with having been a member of a counter-revolutionary group of churchmen whose members held gatherings where the policy of the Soviet power was discussed in an anti-Soviet spirit and anti-Soviet jokes were told. He answered that no anti-Soviet talk had been held in his presence, and that when he visited parishioners' houses and performed the services he never afterwards talked politics.

At the next interrogation, in the hope of getting something to compromise either the priest or somebody else, he was asked whether he knew an archimandrite the page names «Филарет (Студиникин)», and he said that he did and told where the man had served. Called to a last interrogation on 10 February he said he had nothing to add to his earlier answers. On 15 March 1933 the Special Conference attached to the Collegium of the OGPU sentenced him to three years' exile in the Northern Krai.

He was sent to serve the exile in the town of Kargopol, and there he came to know [Alexander (Zverev)](/saints/alexander-zverev). When the exile was over the archpriest invited him to live in the Volokolamsk district, where he served together with another priest, [Paul (Andreyev)](/saints/paul-andreyev), in the church of the Nativity of the Mother of God in the village of Возмище. There was no vacant priest's place in the deanery at the time, so he settled at Возмище to wait for one, worked as a packer in the Volokolamsk artel «Швейник», and served in the church as he was able, concelebrating with the two archpriests.

On 8 October 1937 the authorities arrested him and shut him up in the Volokolamsk prison. The charge was that he had proposed an inscription for the ribbon of a funeral wreath for one of the artel's workers who had died, using the word «товарищ» of a man the authorities held to have been a kulak, which they counted a counter-revolutionary proposal. Questioned on 10 October he denied counter-revolutionary activity and gave the wording he had in fact proposed; asked about gatherings of priests and nuns he said he had been at none, and that he saw priests and nuns only at the services in church, where he sometimes took part in the singing. On 27 October he was asked the single question whether he admitted guilt, and said he did not.

On 14 November 1937 one NKVD troika sentenced the two archpriests and him to be shot. The two were shot on 16 November and he on 25 November 1937, and all three were buried in an unmarked common grave on the Butovo range near Moscow. One troika decision naming the three of them together is what the relation written here rests on; 25 November is 12 ноября ст. ст., which is the day the calendar keeps him on, and 16 November is 3 ноября ст. ст., which is the day it keeps the other two on.

The calendar keeps a second man of this name and this surname, an archpriest, on 26 сентября ст. ст., on his own name page, 14333. Two commemorations on one calendar are two men by the rule this corpus follows, and the two folders are told apart by day, by grade and by slug; the stored Russian form is the same bare surname for both, because neither name page gives anything beyond it. azbyka's own list of his days gives 25 ноября and two moveable synaxes, of the new martyrs who suffered at Butovo and of the saints of Moscow, which are synaxes and not second feasts. The day page carries no hymn under any of the seven names on the line.
