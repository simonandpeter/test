# Tarachus of Tarsus

In the reign of Diocletian and Maximian, when Numerius Maximus the proconsul judged at Tarsus in Cilicia, three Christians were seized in the city of Pompeiopolis — Tarachus, Probus and Andronicus — and brought before him. Looking at them, Maximus saw that Tarachus was an old and honourable man and questioned him first, as the eldest of them, asking what he was called. «Sunt creștin» — I am a Christian — he answered; and told to give his name and not his unclean faith, he said it again.

They beat him about the mouth, and under the beating he said that this name was dearer to him than the name his parents gave; then, since they wished to know it, that his parents had called him Tarah and that as a soldier he had been called Victor. He was of Roman stock, born at Claudiopolis in Syria, and had asked the commander Publion for his discharge because he was a Christian. Maximus told him to spare his old age and sacrifice, since the emperors who ruled the world worshipped the gods; he answered that they had gone astray, deceived by Satan.

For that they broke his jaws. He was stripped and beaten with rods, and said the judge had truly made him wise, for under the blows he hoped the more in God and in His Son. Charged with confessing two gods, he answered that Christ is the Son of God, equal in Godhead with the Father and the Holy Spirit, the hope of all Christians, and that suffering for Him we are saved. He had believed so, he said, for sixty-five years. Bound with great iron chains, he was thrown into prison.

At the later hearings his mouth was broken with stones and his teeth shattered; fire was laid on his outstretched hands; a red-hot iron spike was set in his jaws; his ears were cut off, the skin flayed from his head and live coals laid upon it; heated spits were set under his arms. To every torment he answered that he would not depart from the God who strengthened him, and he told the judge not to promise with words but to do quickly what he meant to do.

The rest, the page says, was written by three faithful men — Macarie, Felix and Verie — who stood hidden at the games a thousand paces outside the city. When the martyrs were carried in, for their wounds would not let them walk, the crowd turned its face away and wept and began to cry out against Maximus, and many left. The beasts loosed on him and on [Probus](/saints/probus-of-tarsus) and Andronicus would not touch them: a bear that had killed three men that day came up and licked [Andronicus](/saints/andronicus-of-tarsus)’s wounds, and a lioness sent from Antioch by Herod ran about seeking to escape, then came to the martyrs, bent her knees and lay down before Tarachus, and when he pushed her off and provoked her she stayed beside him like a gentle sheep. So, the page says, these holy martyrs made their end.

Maximus left soldiers to guard the bodies, ordering that they be mixed with the bodies of the condemned so that Christians should not know them and steal them. The three men prayed, and at once the earth shook with thunder and lightning and a storm of rain, and when they came near the fire was out and every soldier had fled. The night was very dark, and three lights like stars appeared above the relics; they took them up in secret and followed the lights over the mountain, and laid them in a cave cut in the rock, securing the entrance. Going back into the city they found the guards put to death by Maximus.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them on the same day, 12 October, and saint.gr adds where each came from and how they answered. Probus was from Pamphylia, Andronicus from Ephesus and Tarachus from Illyria; all three were soldiers, it says, truly most devout and thoroughly grounded in Holy Scripture, and they were arrested by Maxentius the eparch of Tarsus while they were in the desert, in the persecution under Diocletian and under Flavian governor of Cilicia. Brought before him, the old man Tarachus spoke first: do not look at my old age, king; my bodily strength may have given way, but the prime of the soul stays whole, and so all torments and ten thousand deaths will not put to shame even the least soldier of Christ. Struck down by the three answers, the page says, Maxentius had the skin flayed from his head and the eyes of [Probus](/saints/probus-of-tarsus) and [Andronicus](/saints/andronicus-of-tarsus) put out, and at last, unable to bear such humbling, beheaded all three.

The Russian calendar keeps the three of them on 12 October too, and gives the year the other two do not: 304, at Tarsus in Cilicia. It is the only one of the three sources to say how old he was and what that meant: «святой Тарах, старый воин», the old soldier, who answers the demand to sacrifice for all three of them by saying that he sacrifices to the one true God in a pure heart. Its life is four sentences long; the proconsul hands them to the torturers, the bodies are cut in pieces, and Christians take the remains away in secret and bury them.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the three of them on the Julian 12 October under one heading, and gives each man his own city: Tarachus was from Claudiopolis in Syria, [Probus](/saints/probus-of-tarsus) from Side in Pamphylia, and [Andronicus](/saints/andronicus-of-tarsus) the son of a notable citizen of Ephesus. All three were tried together by the proconsul Numerius Maximus in the time of the emperor Diocletian, and Tarachus, the book says, was sixty-five years old when he suffered. Asked his name three times by the proconsul, he answered all three times: a Christian.

They were beaten with staves and thrown bloodied into prison, brought out again, and tortured in several places over a long time. At last the three were thrown to the beasts in the theatre, where other condemned men had been torn apart just before them — and the beasts would not harm them, but a bear and a fierce lioness fawned about them. Many who saw it believed in Christ God and shouted against the proconsul, who, in a rage fiercer than the beasts’, sent soldiers in to cut Christ’s soldiers to pieces with their swords. Their bodies were left mixed with the other dead, and three Christians who had been present came that night to take them; in the very dark night they could not tell which bodies were the martyrs’, so they prayed — and three candles appeared at once above the holy bodies, and they took them and buried them honourably.
