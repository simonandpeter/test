# Gabriel of Białystok

Gabriel was born in the year 1684 in the village of Zvierki, which lies south of the city of Białystok in the district of Zabłudów, at a time — saint.gr says — when persecutions and discriminations were being carried on against the Orthodox Church by the Uniates in Poland. When he was six years old he was carried off and taken to the city of Białystok, and there he was martyred, in the year 1690, by the enemies of the Church.

When in the eighteenth century the Christians opened his grave, they found his holy relic incorrupt. The holy body was taken to the city of Białystok in Poland, where it remained until the Second World War, when it was carried into Russia, to the church of the Most Holy Protection at Grodno. With the blessing of the Russian metropolitan Philaret, on 21 September 1992, the holy relic was translated back with honours and reverence to the city of Białystok.

That is the whole of the page. It does not say who took the child, or before whom he was brought, or how he was put to death; it names no judge and no companion, and it prints no couplet and no hymn.

The Russian calendar gives him a birthday, parents and the whole course of his relics, and it also gives an account of his death which is not set down here. He was born on 22 March 1684 in the village of Зверки in the Białystok uyezd of the Grodno province, to the devout peasants Пётр and Анастасия, and grew up meek and without malice, with a marked bent for solitude. On 11 April 1690, with Pascha near, his mother carried her husband’s dinner out to the field, and the six-year-old was taken from the house and carried off to the city of Białystok, where he was put to death by torture over nine days. The body was thrown out in a field at the edge of the wood near Зверки; hungry dogs that found it did not tear it but kept even the birds of prey off, and the villagers who came at the barking found it and buried it near the church before a great gathering of people.

Thirty years after the burial the relics were still incorrupt. In 1746 the church in which the child was buried burned down and the relics came through the fire, with the little hand partly charred; when they were translated to a monastery the hand healed and was covered again with skin. He is reckoned a healer of children. The relics changed their resting place several times, were kept from 1944 to 1992 in the church of the Protection at Grodno, and were from there translated with solemnity to the Свято-Никольский cathedral in Białystok.

One thing in that account is deliberately left out of it. Where the Greek page this folder was first written from says only that the child was carried off and martyred by enemies of the Church, the Russian page names the man who took him as a Jew and says that the villagers judged the death a ritual murder, and the two hymns the day page prints under the child’s name repeat the charge in verse. That charge is the blood libel, and it has no historical standing. It is recorded here that the source makes it, because a reader who opens the page will meet it; it is not repeated, and neither of the two hymns was copied into this folder.
