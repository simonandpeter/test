# Pulcheria the Empress

Pulcheria the empress took part in both councils, that of Ephesus and that of Chalcedon, as a great defender of the faith; in her was the zeal of right belief and great wisdom, for in her pure soul and heart the Holy Spirit dwelt as in a holy church and enriched her with great gifts. When the Greek emperor Arcadius died he left his small son Theodosius, eight years old, and three daughters, Pulcheria, Arcadia and Marina; Pulcheria was five years older than her brother, very wise and pure, and God’s providence gave her as a gift to the Greek empire, for the help of the young Theodosius and the defence of Orthodoxy, then troubled by heretics. With an understanding beyond her years she was received into the empire with her brother and made Augusta, and was sixteen when, taking the imperial power, she began to order the empire not with a woman’s knowledge but with a man’s wisdom, so that the whole world marvelled at her wisdom, which she had from God for the purity of her life; for the love of God and for the peace of the Greeks she would not take a husband, lest there be division between husband and brother, but promised herself as bride to God and wished to remain in virginity until death — the Romanian life runs on past the point read here. The Greek synaxarion keeps her on 10 September and refers to her feast of 17 February with her husband Marcian; the Romanian calendar keeps her on 10 September.
