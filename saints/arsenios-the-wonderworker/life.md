# Arsenios the Wonderworker

The Greek synaxarion knows only that this Russian monk died in 1550 — the year of Arsenius of Komel, whom the Orthodox Church in America keeps on the same day, and whose life it prints.

Arsenius was born in Moscow of the noble Sakharusov family and tonsured young at the Trinity–St Sergius monastery, where he copied books — a Gospel in his hand survives from 1506 — and was igumen in 1525–27, though he often withdrew to the solitary Makrisch monastery; the Great Prince Basil, visiting, was surprised to find the igumen of a prosperous house in patched old clothes. He set out for the Komel forest, fifty versts from Vologda, carrying a large wooden cross on his shoulder to choose the site of a monastery, and founded it where he stumbled under the cross and a light shone on him. When local hunters killed his disciple he withdrew to the Shelegod forest; monks gathered, and then fugitives from a Tatar raid. In 1530 the Great Prince granted him land on the river Kokhtisha, where he cleared the ground with his disciple Gerasimus, tamed the wild beasts by prayer and built a church of the Placing of the Veil of the Theotokos. He died on 24 August 1550. His Life, written soon after, burned in 1596 and was restored in shortened form from the surviving manuscripts.
