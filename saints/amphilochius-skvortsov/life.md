# Amphilochius (Skvortsov)

Amphilochius, in the world Alexander Yakovlevich Skvortsov, was born in 1885 in the village of Norvashi in the Tsivilsk district of Kazan province, the son of a psalm-reader. After the Kazan seminary he took the habit with the name Amphilochius, and in 1910 was ordained hieromonk. He graduated from the Kazan Theological Academy as a candidate of theology and was kept on there as a scholar; in 1910–1911 he attended lectures in the Oriental faculty of the University of St Petersburg, and then taught at the Kazan academy in its chair of the Mongolian language and the history and refutation of Lamaism, and from 1912 to 1914 he was sent to Mongolia to study Tibetan and the Lamaist scriptures.

In 1922 he was consecrated bishop of Melekess, a vicar of the Ufa diocese, and in 1923 he was arrested. From March 1925 he was bishop of Krasnoyarsk and Yeniseisk, where he denounced the Renovationists fearlessly and refused to work with them. In the summer of 1926 he was arrested again, at Tsivilsk in Chuvashia, and sentenced to three years of camp, and until March 1928 he was held at Solovki, where he worked as a storekeeper.

In 1928 he was appointed to the see of the Don and Novocherkassk, but in 1929 he was sent back to Krasnoyarsk; soon afterwards he broke with Metropolitan Sergius, joined the Danilov opposition led by Archbishop Theodore (Pozdeevsky), withdrew into the taiga and founded a secret skete there. He was arrested in 1934 and exiled to Siberia, and in 1937 he was shot in the Mariinsk camps. He was glorified with the new martyrs and confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 18 September old style, and he is also remembered on 26 January with the new martyrs.
