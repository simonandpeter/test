# Theodora of Vasta

Theodora came from the Peloponnese, mother of saints and heroes, and so is called by some Theodora the Peloponnesian; Arcadia and Messenia seem the likeliest regions of her birth. She lived in the ninth century — the tenth, by others — in the years of the Byzantine empire, to parents poor and obscure but loving God, who handed on to their children the faith in Christ; from a small child Theodora, more than her brothers and sisters, had a particular love and bent for divine things, loving God so much that she wished to give her whole life to him, gaining day by day what the holy Fathers call the love of Christ. Growing up she resolved to live in a monastery and cultivate her love for Christ there — but, strangely, she chose not a convent but a men’s monastery: she presented herself at the monastery of the Panaitsa, on the border of Arcadia and Messenia, as a man, with the name Theodore — why, cannot be said for certain; perhaps she wanted to vanish entirely from those who knew her. In the men’s monastery she soon became an example of patience, obedience and humility — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here. Before dying, the Romanian life says, she asked God to make trees of her hair and a river of her blood, and indeed from the roof of the church raised at Vasta in Arcadia trees grow, resting paradoxically on the roof itself, a witness that where God wills, the order of nature is overcome. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 11 September.
