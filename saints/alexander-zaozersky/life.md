# Alexander (Zaozersky)

He was born on 20 July 1879 in Moscow, in the family of a priest. He finished the Заиконоспасское church school, the Moscow seminary in 1899 and the Moscow Theological Academy in 1903, and he was rector of the church of the holy Параскева-Пятница in the Охотный ряд. The Russian calendar's name page and its life page agree in calling him an archpriest, which is the office recorded here.

On 8 April 1922 he was arrested in the same case of the confiscation of church valuables. Under questioning he did not admit himself guilty; he said only that he had read the Epistle of the patriarch Тихон in church, and he refused to name through whom he had received the text of it, or the text of the protest to the Central Executive Committee that hundreds of believers had signed. At the trial, the page says, he held himself bravely and with dignity, and the judges could not tempt him with an easier sentence in exchange for evidence against the others. They found him the ideologue of the clergy, the man who had shown the greatest intransigence of them all. When he was led out onto the Lubyanka square in Moscow after the trial, he blessed the crowd that greeted him with a wide cross.

The public trial of fifty of the arrested had opened on 26 April in the hall of the Polytechnic Museum under the chairmanship of the tribunal's president Бек. On 8 May the Moscow revolutionary tribunal sentenced eleven men to be shot; on 18 May the Politburo struck six names from that list. The five who were left were [Alexander](/saints/alexander-zaozersky) himself, [Christopher (Nadezhdin)](/saints/christopher-nadezhdin), [Basil (Sokolov, 13 May)](/saints/basil-sokolov-13-may), the hieromonk [Macarius (Telegin)](/saints/macarius-telegin) and the layman [Sergius (Tikhomirov)](/saints/sergius-tikhomirov).

They were shot at the beginning of June 1922 in what the page calls the ship of the Cheka — the premises of the archive of a former insurance company, where the condemned were shot in the windowless rooms built for its safes in the half-basement — and buried at the Калитниковское cemetery in Moscow. They were canonised in 1998 as locally venerated saints of the Moscow diocese, and numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for veneration throughout the Church at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
