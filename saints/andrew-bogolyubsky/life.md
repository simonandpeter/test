# Andrew Bogolyubsky

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery gives him two readings on 4 июля ст. ст., and both say he was the son of the grand prince Yuri Dolgoruky and the grandson of Vladimir Monomakh. The longer of them opens with the years 1110 to 1174 and says he was called Боголюбский, the God-loving, while he was still a youth, for the constant attention of his prayer and his diligence at the services of the church; the shorter says that from childhood he loved those services and gave alms freely.

He was a brave soldier and went on many campaigns with his warlike father. On 8 February 1150, at the battle near Lutsk, the longer reading has him saved from a German mercenary’s spear by his prayer to «великомученику Феодору Стратилату», whose memory fell on that day. The chroniclers, it says, set his gift for making peace beside his courage, a rare thing in the princes of that hard time. With his father he built Moscow in 1147, Yuryev-Polsky in 1152 and Dmitrov in 1154, and adorned Rostov, Suzdal and Vladimir with churches, so that in 1162 he could say: «Я белую Русь городами и селами застроил и многолюдною сделал».

In the summer of 1155, obeying nothing but the will of God and without his father’s blessing, he left Vyshgorod for the Suzdal land and carried with him the wonderworking icon of the Mother of God that was afterwards called the Vladimir icon. There, the shorter reading says, the Most Pure Virgin appeared to him herself and bade him found a monastery in honour of her Nativity; he had an icon painted as she had stood before him, and both it and the monastery that rose on the place were called Боголюбская. Chosen to rule at the veche, he was the first in Rus to set up sole rule, and the Suzdal principality grew under him into a new centre of the Russian lands.

At his instance the Church established the feast of the All-Merciful Saviour and the Most Holy Mother of God on 1 августа, and soon afterwards the feast of the Protection of the Mother of God on 1 октября, which the reading calls a Russian feast unknown to the Latin West and to the Greek East alike. The first church of that feast was the Protection on the Nerl, raised in 1165 in the flood-meadow of the river where he could always see it from the windows of his hall at Боголюбово, and he had a hand himself in the writing of its service and of the Prologue account of it.

He was killed in 1174 in a plot of his courtiers. The three hymns the day page prints under his name are the Church’s own reading of that death: they call him a passion-bearer, say that he accepted death at the hands of his own for the love of Christ God, and set his blood beside the blood of Abel and of Zachariah; the calendar’s line itself grades him only as the right-believing grand prince.
