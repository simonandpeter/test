# Seraphim (Amelin)

Seraphim — Simeon in the world — was born in 1874 in a devout peasant
family in the village of Solomino in the Fatezh district of Kursk
province, and came to the Glinsk hermitage in 1893. He was made a
rasophore in 1899 and tonsured in 1904 with the name Seraphim. Sparing of
words even before his tonsure, he grew more sparing after it, having
learned from the elders that to watch every word one says is one of the
means of learning sobriety and an undistracted inner standing before God,
and as its fruit he early gained that inner peace of Christ which all true
ascetics so earnestly seek. From 1899 he worked in the icon-painting
workshop and was soon made its head; in 1913 he was ordained hierodeacon
and in 1917 hieromonk, with the obedience of sacristan; in the revolution
he secretly took the schema, and from 1919 was the treasurer. After the
hermitage was closed he lived at Kovenki, working as a joiner and
locksmith; in 1941 he served in the village’s reopened parish church, in
1942 returned to the hermitage, and from 1943 to 1958 was its superior. In
the extremely hard conditions of the time — the war and the ruin after
it, the state’s own godless ideology — he managed not only to set the
crippled monastery’s daily life in order but, what mattered most, to
restore the tradition of the strict and grace-filled life of the house
and the guidance of beginners by elders. Deeply respected and loved by the
brethren, he embodied peace and quiet, and was at the same time a keen
and watchful father from whose eye nothing escaped that needed a pastor’s
attention. He died on 18 October 1958. The Council of Bishops of 2017
entered him in the calendar of the Russian Church, which keeps him on 9
September of the old style, 22 September, with the synaxis of the Glinsk
fathers, and in the synaxis of the saints of Kursk.
