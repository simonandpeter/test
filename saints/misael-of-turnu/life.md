# Misael of Turnu

Misael was a founder of the Turnu monastery, a hermit of the Cozia mountains and the disciple of [Daniel](/saints/daniel-of-turnu), who was his confessor. From their youth both loved ascetic labour for God, left home, parents, brothers, kin and friends, and entered the monastery of Cozia as brothers, living in obedience and humility. For their labours and zeal they were soon found worthy of the monastic habit and were tonsured by their abbot.

Daniel, who knew the Scriptures and the teaching of the Fathers and was skilled in the monastic life, was ordained priest, and the fathers of the monastery chose him as their confessor and guide; among those who confessed to him was Misael, who became his spiritual disciple. After a time, with their abbot’s blessing, the two left the common life for the wilderness beyond Trajan’s Tower, in the country of Mount Cozia, where they dug cells in the rock of the mountain beside the springs of the forest, and no temptation overcame their thirst for prayer, stillness and labour.

A small community of brothers gathered round them, and they built a wooden church for its needs, dedicated to the Entry of the Most Holy Theotokos into the Temple. Daniel was confessor to all the hermits round about — by tradition the hermits [Neophytos](/saints/neophytos-of-stanisoara) and [Meletius](/saints/meletius-of-stanisoara) also came down to him on Sundays and feasts to confess and to receive communion at the liturgy in the skete — and from the old Roman tower beside it the skete was called Turnu.

Near the end of his life Daniel gave the fathers there rules for the hermit life and, knowing his end beforehand, set his disciple Misael over them in his place. Both died in the first half of the seventeenth century and were buried beside the altar of their little church; when Varlaam, metropolitan of Wallachia, built a new stone church at the skete in 1676, he laid their relics in the foundation of its altar. The Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church decided their canonisation on 25 February 2016, with their feast on 5 October.
