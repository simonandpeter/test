# Euphrosynus of Sinozero

saint.gr's calendar for 20 Μαρτίου keeps a Russian hieromartyr, Euphrosynus, surnamed Sinozersky and called Ephraim in the world. He was born in a village of Karelia near Lake Ladoga in the second half of the sixteenth century. He was a monk first at the monastery of Valaam in Finland, then in the district of Dolok in the province of Novgorod, and then near Lake Sinitse; he was ordained presbyter at the Uspensky monastery of the town of Tikhvin.

In the year 1600 he began the hermit's life in the wild marshes of Lake Sinitse. He dug out a cave, set up a cross and lived there two years, feeding on wild herbs alone. When the villagers became aware of him they began to visit him and to take his counsel on spiritual matters.

In 1612 the Polish armies were plundering Russia. Many people took refuge at the elder's hermitage, and he foresaw the attack on it and urged them to leave; and when they asked him why he did not leave himself, he answered, I came here to die for Christ. Those who took his advice were saved, and those who stayed met a terrible death. A monk named Jonas lived beside him and, frightened by the prophecy, wanted to go too; but Euphrosynus stopped him, saying that when the battle begins the two of them must show their courage, for they had promised to live and die as hermits and owed God the promise they had made him, and only death brings rest — whereas the laymen were bound by no monastic promise and had to protect themselves for their families and their children.

After that he put on the monastic schema and prayed the whole night. The next day, 20 March, the Poles fell on the hermitage. He came out wearing the schema and stood beside the cross he had set up when he first came to the place. The enemy demanded the hermitage's property, and he said, All the property of the monastery, and mine also, is in the church of the Most Holy Theotokos — meaning, the page explains, his love for God, which nobody can take out of a believer's heart. They did not understand him, and one of them struck him on the neck with his sword, and he fell dead; and when the Poles came back angry at having found nothing, his executioner split the saint's honourable head with an axe, as though the first mortal blow had not been enough.

A villager named John Souma was in the elder's cell when the enemy broke in; he survived his heavy wounds and told his son afterwards what had happened. Euphrosynus was buried with honour and reverence on 28 March, and thirty-four years later a church of the Holy Trinity was built on the place of his burial.

The Russian calendar's two readings agree with each other and add a life this folder did not hold. He was Ephraim in the world and was born in Karelia near Lake Ladoga. He lived as a novice at the Valaam monastery, moved afterwards to Novgorod the Great, and after a long time there went into the Bezhetskaya pyatina, where he served as a sacristan at the village of Dolosskoye, twenty versts from Ustyuzhna Zheleznopolskaya; he took the habit at the Dormition monastery of Tikhvin.

In 1600 he began to live alone in the wild marshes on the shore of Lake Sinichye, fifty versts from Ustyuzhna. He set up a cross, dug himself a cave, and lived two years on wild plants until the people round about found him by chance and began coming to him for counsel; some of them stayed, and the hermitage became a house with a rule.

In 1612, when Polish companies were plundering Russia, a great many people were sheltering with him. He foretold that the Poles were coming and told them all to go. Many did not believe him and asked why he did not go himself, and he answered that he had come there to die for Christ's sake; those who obeyed were saved and those who stayed were killed. He put on the great schema and spent the night in prayer. On the next day, the twentieth of March, he came out of his cell in the schema habit and stood at the cross he had set up. The raiders demanded the house's goods. He answered that all its wealth and his own was in the church of the most pure Mother of God, meaning the riches that cannot be stolen; they did not understand him, and one of them cut his neck half through with a sword and afterwards broke his head with a battle-axe. His body was buried with honour on 28 March, and thirty-four years later a new church of the Holy Trinity was raised in his house.
