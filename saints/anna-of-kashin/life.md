# Anna of Kashin

She was the daughter of Prince Dimitry Borisovich of Rostov, and in 1294 she married Michael Yaroslavich, Grand Prince of Tver, whom the Tatars put to death in the Horde in 1318 and whom the Russian calendar keeps on 22 November. After his death she went into the monastery of Holy Wisdom at Tver and was tonsured under the name Euphrosyne; later she moved to the Dormition monastery at Kashin and took the great schema, and with it the name Anna again. On 2 October 1338 she died in peace.

Her sons repeated their father's end. Dimitry Mikhailovich, called Grozniye Ochi — Terrible Eyes — was killed in the Horde on 15 September 1325; Alexander Mikhailovich, prince of Tver, was killed with his own son Theodore on 29 October 1339, the year after she died.

The wonders at her tomb began in 1611, while Lithuanian troops were besieging Kashin. She appeared to Gerasim, the sacristan of the Dormition cathedral, and told him she was praying to the Saviour and the Mother of God for the city's deliverance from the foreigners.

Her veneration was then made and unmade and made again, which is unusual enough to be the most particular thing about her. The Council of 1649 resolved to open her relics for general veneration and to number her among the saints of the Russian Church. In 1677 Patriarch Joachim put the question of abolishing that veneration to the Moscow Council, because the Old Believer schism had begun using her name for its own ends. On 12 June 1909 she was glorified a second time and commemoration of her established everywhere, and that June day is one of the three her name page still lists.
