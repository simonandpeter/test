# Peter (Pavlushkov)

Peter Ivanovich Pavlushkov was born in 1865 at Vladimir, a priest's son. He finished the Kyiv theological academy in 1892 with the title of candidate of theology and was sent to the Tula seminary, where from 26 February 1893 he taught Scripture and psychology. He sat on the consistory's audit commission for the diocese's schools and its care of the poor, became the seminary's inspector in 1907, and as a court councillor was made a knight of two orders. He was married and had five children.

On 30 September 1917 he was appointed rector of the Tula seminary. The buildings of the theological schools were confiscated after the revolution, the seminary among them, and it was closed early in 1918; for two years after that he taught in a school of the town. On 2 February 1921 he was ordained priest by the bishop of Tula and made rector of a city church, which he remained until his death. Of this stretch of his life, the page says, little survives but the court papers.

He was arrested at the height of the campaign to seize the churches' valuables, charged with anti-Soviet agitation in his sermons from the ambo, and the indictment quotes one of them: that if we do not support the Soviet power we shall not suffer for it, but if we do not keep the law of the Bible we shall be punished in the next world. The sentence of the Tula revolutionary tribunal gave him three years in a corrective labour house. **The page's own dates for this do not agree**: it puts the arrest on 26 March 1926 and the sentence on 10 April 1922, and both readings are set down here because nothing read breaks them. An amnesty halved the term and he was out after a year and eight months.

By then the renovationist diocesan administration had put a new priest into his church; the parishioners, glad of their pastor's release, drove the renovationist out, and the page says that heresy was never heard in those walls again. On 9 September 1937 he was arrested a last time, on the standard charge that a counter-revolutionary church group existed at his church and that he, with his academic education, led it. On 19 November 1937 he was condemned to death, and on 23 November 1937 he was shot in the Tesnitsky forest. 10 ноября ст. ст. is that civil day, so the calendar keeps him on the day he died.

Nothing read puts him in the same place or the same case as the archbishop of Kherson whose company the day's line gives him: his trial was at Tula, and that is where azbyka keeps him, with the Tula saints. He was glorified among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia.
