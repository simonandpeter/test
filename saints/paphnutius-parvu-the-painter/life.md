# Paphnutius Pârvu the Painter

The Romanian calendar keeps on 7 august the painter it calls Pafnutie – Pârvu Zugravul, born at Câmpulung Muscel on 12 October 1657, son of the priest Ioan Pârvescu and of a wife whose name was not kept. At his baptism he was given the name of his godfather, the great treasurer Pârvu Vlădescu. His mother died bearing her sixth child, sickness then took five of the children, and Pârvu alone was left; the priest became a monk at the monastery of Negru Vodă and took his eleven-year-old son with him.

In the monastery school he learned to read and write, and beside the monk Evghenie the first secrets of church painting: the monk took him up on the scaffolding and put into his hands the shell for mixing the colours, finding him gifted for the work. At eighteen he was sent into Moldavia and Bucovina to learn from a noted Russian master, and took as his models the many monasteries and churches painted there inside and out. After five years he came back and was for a short while court painter to the Vlădești boyars.

His first painting is in the church of Aninoasa monastery; with Evghenie, his first teacher, he then painted the church of Negru Vodă. Recommended to the princely family of the Cantacuzinos, he painted a great many of their foundations and some of those of [Constantine Brâncoveanu](/saints/constantine-brancoveanu) — the monasteries of Cotroceni, Mărgineni, Sinaia, Colțea, Berca, Mamu and the sketes of Poiana and Lespezi, the churches of Filipeștii de Pădure, Călinești, Măgureni and Fundenii Doamnei, and Sfântul Gheorghe Nou in Bucharest. He painted panel icons too, the best known of them the Holy Trinity at Sinaia.

He was surnamed Mutu, the Mute, not because he could not speak but because he painted without speaking and without eating, in fasting and prayer; if he said a word or tasted food, he broke off the work for that day. Settled at last in Bucharest, he kept his own school of painting and formed several disciples. He married Tudora and had two sons, Pârvu and Gheorghe; when she died he went on pilgrimage to Jerusalem, fasting and praying along the way.

On his return in 1718 he entered the monastery of Mărgineni with his younger son: Pârvu took the name Pafnutie and Gheorghe the name Gherasim. There, being widely read and knowing foreign languages, he also translated texts into Romanian out of the great library the Cantacuzinos had founded. In 1731 Gherasim died. Fleeing men's praise, Pafnutie took his abbot's blessing and went to the remote skete of Robaia in the heart of the forest, received the great schema, and after four more years of prayer, silence and patience died in 1735. The Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church numbered him among the saints in 2017, a year it dedicated to the makers of icons, and set his commemoration on 7 August.
