# Martinian of Caesarea

Near the city of Caesarea in Palestine is a mountain called the Place of the Ark, where many have struggled in the desert life, and on it lived the blessed Martinian, a monk full of divine grace who had trained himself in the monastic life from childhood. Loving God from his youth, he entered on a great struggle against the enemy; he was young and fair of body, eighteen years old, when he left the city and all the noise of the world and went up to that mountain to a quiet and hermit's life. There he spent twenty-five years, and was counted worthy to receive from God the gift of healing, so that many were cured by his prayers and the possessed were delivered; the report of his life went out in every direction, and those who heard came to him for profit.

The devil, who hates mankind, could not bear the sight of a young monk with such virtues. First he brought all kinds of temptations and frightened him with phantoms of many shapes: one day, as Martinian was singing the psalter, the devil took the form of a great serpent, went under the wall of his cell and began digging the earth in earnest, meaning to undermine the wall and throw the cell down upon him. Without fear and without disturbance the blessed man finished his singing, leaned out of the little window and told the serpent that it was fit only to crawl on the earth, and that its phantoms did not frighten a man who had the Lord Jesus Christ to help him. The devil fled in a dark whirlwind, saying: «Wait, wait; I know how I shall overthrow thee.»

The art he used was a woman, who for a wager came to the cell at night in poor clothes, begging shelter from the storm, and who in the morning showed herself young and beautiful and dressed for his ruin. Martinian, seeing that his own resolve was giving way, heaped up wood, lit it and stood barefoot in the fire, holding himself there while the pain lasted and calling to mind the eternal punishment, and said that if he could not bear this small fire he could not bear that one. The woman, watching him give his body to the flames for the sake of his soul, was broken, wept, and asked him to guide her to salvation.

Seven months later, when the burns were barely healed, he gave her a few dates for the road, brought her out of the cell, showed her the way to Jerusalem and told her to go in peace and save her soul in repentance, and to look back no more, for no one who puts his hand to the plough and looks back is set right in the kingdom of God. She came to Bethlehem and entered the convent of the virgin Paulina, told her everything, and stayed there; her name was Zoe. She lived twelve years in repentance, drinking no wine, tasting neither butter nor fruit but bread and water only, often every second day, and resting on the bare ground; and God gave her the gift of healing, so that a woman whose eyes were grievously painful was cured by her prayers, and she fell asleep in peace.

Martinian, seeing that the enemy would not leave him where a woman could come, prayed that God would be to him way and life and staff and scrip and bread, and went down to the sea. A boatman carried him to a bare rock standing out of the water; he climbed it, sang «I waited patiently for the Lord, and he set my feet upon a rock», and asked only for bread, water and palm-leaves to weave baskets. He would not let the man build him even a small hut, and sat on the rock burnt by the heat of the day and cold at night, the boatman bringing him bread and water two or three times a year, and he praised God day and night.

One day the devil raised a storm against a ship at sea, drove it on a rock and drowned all in it but one girl, who caught a plank, floated to his rock and cried to him to give her his hand. He saw that she had no other deliverance, smiled and said that this too was the devil's art but would not overcome his resolve; then he reasoned that if he did not pull her out he would be her murderer, and if he did he could not stay there with her. He drew her out, told her to stay and not be afraid, signed the sea with the cross, and prayed that it was better for him to die in the sea than to be joined to a woman; and he threw himself in and began to swim.

Two dolphins took him on their backs at once, by God's ordering, and carried him to dry land, the girl watching until he was out of sight. Coming ashore he praised God and said that the enemy's malice had not left him in the mountains or in the desert, nor even in the sea, and that he would now do what the Gospel teaches: when they drive you from one city, flee to another. So he began to walk from place to place and from city to city, saying to himself, «Flee, Martinian, lest temptation overtake thee», carrying no staff, no scrip, no bread, no second coat and no money; wherever he came he asked who in the place was a good man and lodged with him, and wherever night found him, in the desert or the field, there he rested a little.

Going about in that manner for two years he passed through a hundred and sixty-four cities, and at last came to Athens, where God revealed everything about him to the bishop of the Athenians. Knowing the time of his end and falling ill, he went into the church, lay down on the ground and asked those there to call the bishop quickly; they took him for a madman, and when they told the bishop he said: «You are the mad ones; that man is better than I am.» The bishop came in haste, and Martinian, unable to rise, stretched out his hands to him; the bishop honoured him the more, saying that God had long promised to show him his servant and had now kept the promise, and asked to be remembered when he should be in the heavenly Kingdom.

Then he lifted his eyes to heaven and said: «Lord Jesus Christ, into thy hands I give my spirit»; and making the sign of the cross he said to the bishop, «Father, give me to God.» His face began to smile, and his soul, rejoicing, parted from the much-tormented body; and the bishop buried his holy body in the church with great honour. The girl left on the rock lived there six years, taking bread and water from the boatman as Martinian had ordered, and asking him at last to bring wool with the bread, that she might not eat their bread for nothing; she prayed twelve times by day and twenty-four by night, and ate a piece of bread every second day. She was twenty-five when she went up on the rock, and she died there; her name was Photinia, and the boatman found her two months later with her hands set in the sign of the cross, her eyes closed and her face shining as though she slept, and carried her to Caesarea, where the bishop buried her with psalms and singing.
