# Hagi-George the Athonite

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 17 decembrie reads «Sfântul Cuvios Hagi-Gheorghe Athonitul», and the page the calendar links under that name carries neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab: the heading, the day, and nothing further. What the line says is that he was a venerable father, that he is called Hagi-Gheorghe, that he belonged to the Holy Mountain, and that the Romanian church keeps him on this day.

No year, no monastery, no country and no account of his life has been taken from anywhere else, and none has been supplied from memory. The English form of the name here keeps the Romanian spelling of the first element, because no other form of it was read. If a life for him is printed elsewhere, it has not been read.

Where doxologia gave only the name, the Greek calendar gives the life, and says where it got it from. saint.gr calls Hagi-George one of the most important ascetic figures of the Holy Mountain in the nineteenth century, and sets out what [Paisios the Athonite](/saints/paisios-the-athonite) gathered in his book on him, from the testimonies of Hagi-George's spiritual grandchildren, Cappadocians and Russian Athonites. He was born in 1809 at Kermira in Caesarea of Cappadocia and was called Gabriel in the world; his parents, Iordanes and Maria, were devout and charitable people, and his mother had a particular love for the ascetic life and brought the little Gabriel into contact with stories of ascetics and monks, so that the longing for the monastic life was born in him as a child.

Young, he left his homeland and came to the Holy Mountain, and followed the monastic road there with great severity: fasting and vigil became the groundwork of his life, and the old Athonites remembered him as a great ascetic and a very great faster, so that his name became proverbial for a man who fasted much — this one is a Hagi-George! But his ascesis, the page says, was not its own end: for him fasting and vigil and prayer were the road to humility, to purity of heart and to love for God and for man. Over the years many monks, Greek and Russian, gathered round him and a great spiritual family grew up; he was strict in ascesis and especially tender as a spiritual father, careful of the younger monks and of the children who came to him, and the Cappadocians among them called him Bizim Baba, our father.

His life was not free of trials. He met with oppositions, slanders and persecutions, and Paisios counted him among the wronged men of the Holy Mountain and spoke of what he went through as the example of a man who endured without returning evil. In 1882 he was removed from the Holy Mountain and settled at Constantinople; and there, instead of shutting himself up in the pain of his exile, he went on with the same life of prayer. His last years were full of bodily trial and he was bedridden at the end, but he still cared for the people who came to him. Once, when people who needed money came to ask his help and the elder had nothing left to give, he pointed to heaven and said: there is my bank; here I have no money.

He fell asleep at Constantinople on 17 December 1886 and was buried at Balikli, in the church of the Life-giving Spring, and his memory stayed alive on the Holy Mountain and in the communities that had known him. The honour generations of Athonite conscience paid him took its official church form in 2026: on 11 February the Holy and Sacred Synod of the Ecumenical Patriarchate decided unanimously to enroll the monk George, called Hagi-George, of Cappadocia and fallen asleep at Constantinople, in the calendar of the saints; on 29 August the ceremony of enrolment was held at the Ecumenical Patriarchate and the Patriarchal and Synodal Act was signed; and his memory was appointed for 17 December, the day of his falling asleep.
