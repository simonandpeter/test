# Michael (Novitsky)

The Сретенский monastery's calendar prints him eighth of the nine entries it puts on the Julian 17 April, in one line — «Св. Михаила исп., пресвитера (1935).», the holy Michael the confessor, a presbyter, and the year 1935 — but behind the name link there is a life, which is unusual for a twentieth-century entry. The link gives the fuller form, «Михаил (Новицкий), протоиерей, исп.», with the surname the line leaves out and the grade of archpriest; and the page it leads to is a long one of the new martyrs of the Belarusian land, twenty-three hieromartyrs of Minsk with a paragraph apiece.

He was born in 1889 into a large priestly family, that paragraph says, and received the priesthood on the eve of the revolutionary events, being already a man of wide education. From 1920 he was rector of the church of the holy apostles Peter and Paul in the town of Uzda in the Minsk district. Several times the authorities offered him good work if he would give up his orders, and he refused. In 1933 his church was closed, and he went on serving in a small watchman's hut in the churchyard.

There, in the night between Great Friday and Great Saturday in 1935, a man came to him and demanded the church's valuables. When the priest refused, telling him to go away because he had to prepare for the service, the man beat him so badly that he could not move again. He served his last Liturgy on the day of the bright Resurrection of Christ lying down, and on the third day of Pascha he departed to the Lord.

The page identifies the man who beat him, and the crowd it says appeared at the funeral, by their religion, and goes on to describe what that crowd did. That characterisation is the page's and is not repeated here: it is an accusation against a people and not a fact about this priest, and nothing in the page supports it beyond the telling. What the page attests of him is the demand, the refusal, the beating, the Liturgy served lying down and the death three days after Pascha. The calendar's own line and his name page both grade him a confessor; the page that tells his story calls him a hieromartyr.
