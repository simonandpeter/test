# Dius of Antioch

Dius's homeland was Antioch of Syria. He was born of Christian parents and, growing up in the right faith, entered from his youth on the ascetic struggle of the desert, guided by men inspired by God into the better monastic way. He had much fighting with the devil, the unseen enemy, and with the adversary of his own household, his body, which desires against the spirit; and he overcame the devil with unceasing prayer and subdued the body with fasting, with sleeplessness and with many other labours, putting his earthly members to death in every way and making the body a slave to the spirit.

He took very little food, and not every day but on the second or the third day, and often passed a whole week without food and without sleep. By such abstinence he put himself to death and overcame the adversary, and rose to the height of the life without passions and became a pure dwelling of the Holy Spirit, for the grace of God settled in him; and he became a man powerful in deed and in word, working wonders and guiding many to salvation, so that the heavenly Father was glorified through him.

When he had lived many years at Antioch, a command came to him from God in a vision, that he should go to Constantinople for the profit of many. At first he would not believe the vision, fearing some contrivance of the adversary, since the devil knows how at times to make himself an angel of light. But when the same vision and command of the Lord came to him a second time — and it was even shown him what that city was like, which he had never seen, for in the vision he saw the whole of Constantinople — he submitted to the will of God, who orders all things to our profit; and leaving Antioch he went to the imperial city.

The Romanian page's account of his years there is long and the middle of it is left here unsummarised. At the end, having been told the hour of his death by a man who appeared to him, he bowed to that man and thanked God for such a warning, called the brethren, spoke them his last word of teaching, and gave orders about his burial: that they should not let the people of the city take his body, but should bury it in his own monastery.

Then, setting in order for himself all that belongs to a good Christian end, praying at length for them all and for himself and giving everyone the last kiss, he saw the angel of God standing before him, and gave up his honoured soul to him with joy. The emperor and the patriarch wanted his honoured body brought into the city for the city's defence, but the monks told them his ordinance, that his body should not be buried anywhere but in the monastery's tomb; and, not daring to go against the elder's ordinance, they did as he had commanded and buried him in his own house with an honourable burial.
