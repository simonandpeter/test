# Yaropolk Izyaslavich

Yaropolk was a son of the Grand Prince Izyaslav of Kyiv, a grandson of Yaroslav the Wise and a great-grandson of Vladimir equal-to-the-apostles; at his baptism he was given the name the calendar prints as «Петр». The exact time of his birth is not known, the shorter of the two lives says, and it is supposed to have been «не позже 50-х гг. XI столетия», not later than the 1050s. He shared his father’s unhappy lot when Izyaslav was driven out of Kyiv by his brothers, and he travelled on his father’s errands to the king of Poland, to the German emperor, and to the Roman bishop Gregory VII, whose years the page gives as 1073–1085.

After the death of the Grand Prince Svyatoslav in 1078 his father had the grand-princely throne back and Yaropolk received Vyshgorod; after his father’s death the city of Vladimir-Volhynia was given him as his portion, and from it the Rostislavichi tried to drive him out. On the road from Vladimir to Zvenigorod of Galicia he was killed treacherously by Neryadets, a man of his own retinue, in 1086; the murderer, the page says, was probably bribed by the Rostislavichi. His body was carried to Kyiv and buried on 5 December in «церкви святого Петра», which he had himself begun to build.

He was marked, the shorter life says, by humility, meekness, love of the poor and piety, and it is that page which closes with the honour: many church records, beginning with the chronicle of [Nestor the Chronicler](/saints/nestor-the-chronicler), witness that the murdered right-believing prince was honoured among the saints of God. The troparion the calendar prints for him sets his death beside the deaths of [Boris, son of Vladimir](/saints/boris-son-of-vladimir) and [Gleb](/saints/gleb-prince-and-passion-bearer), his own kinsmen and fellow passion-bearers, and puts in his mouth a prayer to be given an end like theirs, that he too might wash away his sins with his own blood.
