# Alexander (Tetyuev)

Alexander Pavlovich Tetyuev was born on 10 March 1879 in the village of Saltanovo in the Cherdyn district of Perm province, into a family whose forebears had all served the Church in that province; his father Paul was a priest at Solikamsk until his death in 1916. As a schoolboy at Solikamsk he and his friends swapped nearly all the town’s shop signs in one night, and his father took him out of school and sent him to the Trinity monastery at Solikamsk to be corrected.

He spent two years there as a novice, latterly under Archimandrite Chrysanthus (Klementyev), passed the school’s examinations externally, and from 1901 taught in church schools at Moshevo and then Ust-Nyrob, where he married Claudia Alexandrovna. Archimandrite Chrysanthus counselled him to give up teaching and blessed him to become a priest; he was ordained in 1903 for the village of Lekmartovo and soon moved to the Resurrection cathedral at Cherdyn.

When the Bolsheviks came to Cherdyn many of its clergy were killed, some by being doused with icy water in the frost. Father Alexander was arrested in 1920 for refusing to work with the Cheka and spent about a year in the Cherdyn prison. In 1929 the Renovationists took the Resurrection cathedral by force; he refused to serve with them and moved to the Dormition church, and his parishioners followed him, so he was arrested again, charged with sowing discord among believers, and held at Usolye, in a building that had once belonged to the Church and where all his brothers and sisters had been born.

Freed, he went back to serving at Cherdyn, and sent his wife and children to another town to spare them. In 1936 his family urged him to leave the priesthood for civil work, even buying him a suit, and he refused, saying he believed that Orthodoxy, which the Russian people confessed, would not be destroyed despite all the persecution. He was arrested on 5 August 1937 and shot soon afterwards.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 20 September old style, on one line with the priest [Theoctistus (Smelnitsky)](/saints/theoctistus-smelnitsky); azbyka.ru keeps him also with the saints of the Perm metropolis. Its life is Abbot Damascene (Orlovsky)’s, from the second book of his martyrs, confessors and ascetics of the Russian Church of the twentieth century.
