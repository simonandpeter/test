# Bartholomew the Apostle

The Greek calendar keeps 25 August for the return of his relics, and the synaxarion’s note for the day tells that story rather than his life.

The apostle Bartholomew died by crucifixion in Armenia. The Christians laid his body in a stone coffin and hid it at Urbanopolis; because the coffin healed many illnesses, crowds streamed to it, and the pagans, when they found their chance, threw it into the sea together with four other coffins of martyrs. Then, the synaxarion says, something wonderful happened: the coffin with Bartholomew’s body, the four others in its train, passed the Black Sea, the straits of the Hellespont, the Aegean and the Adriatic and came to the island of Lipari, to the left of Sicily, while the four companions went each to a different place in Italy. The saint revealed himself to Agathon, bishop of Lipari, who came down to the shore, saw the coffin and stood amazed; it was carried with reverence to the spot the apostle himself miraculously pointed out, and a magnificent church was built there.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep the translation of his relics the same day, 25 August (Julian). The Prologue tells it: when the great apostle was crucified at Albanopolis in Armenia the Christians took down his body and buried it honourably in a leaden coffin, and because many wonders, especially healings of the sick, happened at the grave, so that the number of Christians grew, the pagans took the coffin and threw it into the sea, and with it four more coffins with the relics of the martyrs Papian, Lucian, Gregory and Acacius; by God’s providence the coffins did not sink but floated — Acacius’s to the city of Ascalus, Gregory’s to Calabria, Lucian’s to Messina, Papian’s to Sicily and Bartholomew’s to the Lipari islands, where by some secret revelation the bishop Agathon learned that the apostle’s relics were approaching, and went out to the shore with clergy and people and received the coffin with great joy, many sick being healed. The relics lay in the church of Bartholomew there until the time of Theophilus the iconoclast, about 839, when the Muslims threatened Lipari and they were carried to Benevento. The Sretensky calendar’s life of the apostle begins with his lot, after Pentecost, to preach with Philip in Syria and Asia Minor, and the Russian calendar prints for the day the troparion of the apostles Bartholomew and Titus and a kontakion for each.
