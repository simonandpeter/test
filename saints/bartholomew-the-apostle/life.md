# Bartholomew

The Greek calendar keeps 25 August for the return of his relics, and the synaxarion’s note for the day tells that story rather than his life.

The apostle Bartholomew died by crucifixion in Armenia. The Christians laid his body in a stone coffin and hid it at Urbanopolis; because the coffin healed many illnesses, crowds streamed to it, and the pagans, when they found their chance, threw it into the sea together with four other coffins of martyrs. Then, the synaxarion says, something wonderful happened: the coffin with Bartholomew’s body, the four others in its train, passed the Black Sea, the straits of the Hellespont, the Aegean and the Adriatic and came to the island of Lipari, to the left of Sicily, while the four companions went each to a different place in Italy. The saint revealed himself to Agathon, bishop of Lipari, who came down to the shore, saw the coffin and stood amazed; it was carried with reverence to the spot the apostle himself miraculously pointed out, and a magnificent church was built there.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep the translation of his relics the same day, 25 August (Julian). The Prologue tells it: when the great apostle was crucified at Albanopolis in Armenia the Christians took down his body and buried it honourably in a leaden coffin, and because many wonders, especially healings of the sick, happened at the grave, so that the number of Christians grew, the pagans took the coffin and threw it into the sea, and with it four more coffins with the relics of the martyrs Papian, Lucian, Gregory and Acacius; by God’s providence the coffins did not sink but floated — Acacius’s to the city of Ascalus, Gregory’s to Calabria, Lucian’s to Messina, Papian’s to Sicily and Bartholomew’s to the Lipari islands, where by some secret revelation the bishop Agathon learned that the apostle’s relics were approaching, and went out to the shore with clergy and people and received the coffin with great joy, many sick being healed.

The relics lay in the church of Bartholomew there until the time of Theophilus the iconoclast, about 839, when the Muslims threatened Lipari and they were carried to Benevento. The Sretensky calendar’s life of the apostle begins with his lot, after Pentecost, to preach with Philip in Syria and Asia Minor, and the Russian calendar prints for the day the troparion of the apostles Bartholomew and Titus and a kontakion for each.

The Romanian calendar keeps his principal feast on 11 iunie, and doxologia.ro's life for that day adds that after the tongues of fire came at Pentecost the lot fell to him, together with the apostle Philip, to go into Syria and upper Asia for the preaching of the word of God; that the two went sometimes together and sometimes apart through the cities, that Philip's sister, the virgin Mariamne, travelled with them, and that they came through Lydia and Mysia — beaten, imprisoned and stoned — as far as Asia, to [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), and then into Phrygia, where at Hierapolis they overcame by prayer the viper the city honoured as a goddess. The same life adds that some hold his true name to have been Nathanael, since the Gospels that name Bartholomew do not name Nathanael and the Gospel that names Nathanael does not name Bartholomew, while others do not agree; and that the city of Alban where he suffered is not the country of Albania in Europe but a city of that name in Greater Armenia.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 11 June, which the other three calendars name as his principal feast while all four of this folder’s rows stand on the 25 August translation of his relics; one row to a church is the schema, so this day is recorded as a note on that row and he does not render on it. **The entry is much the fullest account on this page.** By all appearances, it says, Bartholomew and Nathanael are one and the same person. He preached with the apostle [Philip](/saints/philip-the-apostle) and with Philip’s sister the virgin [Mariamne](/saints/mariamne-sister-of-philip), and for a time with [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), first through Asia, then in India, and last in Armenia, where he ended as a martyr.

At Hierapolis the apostles killed by prayer a great serpent which the heathen kept and worshipped in a temple, and in the same city gave sight by prayer to a man who had been forty years blind. The crowd rose against them for it, and both Philip and Bartholomew were crucified, Bartholomew head downwards. Then an earthquake came, in which the wicked judges and many of the people perished; and many, taking it for a judgement of God, ran to take the apostles down from their crosses, but Philip had already given up his spirit while Bartholomew was still alive.

After that he went into India, where he preached and translated the Gospel of Matthew into the Indian tongue, and then into Armenia, where he healed the king’s daughter of madness. The king’s envious brother Astyages seized him, crucified him, flayed off his skin and at last beheaded him at Albanopolis in Armenia. Christians buried the body honourably in a lead coffin; and because many wonders happened at the relics the heathen took the coffin and threw it into the sea, and the water carried it to the Lipari islands, where the bishop met it on a revelation in a dream and buried it in a church.

Two appearances close the entry. He appeared in church to [Joseph the Hymnographer](/saints/joseph-the-hymnographer), clothed in white robes, and blessed him with the Gospel so that he might be able to sing spiritual songs: let the waters of heavenly wisdom flow from thy tongue. And he appeared to the emperor Anastasius and told him he would guard his newly built city of Dara. Later, the entry ends, the relics of this great apostle were carried to Benevento and then to Rome, and great and fearful wonders happened over them.

The Russian calendar keeps him on four days and one of them, 22 апреля ст. ст., heads a line of three apostles — «Апп. Нафанаила , Луки и Климента (I)» — in which the first name is Нафанаил and the link behind it goes straight to this apostle’s own page. Both of the lives that page indexes print «Нафанаил апостол» under the title, and the fuller of them ends by naming the authority for it: «Святители Иоанн Златоуст, Кирилл Александрийский, Епифаний Кипрской и некоторые другие учители Церкви считают апостола Варфоломея за одно лицо с Нафанаилом (Ин. 1, 45-51; Ин. 21, 2)» — three hierarchs, and certain other teachers of the Church besides, reckon the Apostle Варфоломей one person with Нафанаил, on John 1:45–51 and John 21:2. The identification is therefore the calendar’s own, held as the reading of those Fathers. Their names are left in the calendar’s script because this corpus keeps a folder for each of the three, and being cited as an authority for somebody’s identity is not a relation to him; and Нафанаил is left in it because the corpus keeps a different Нафанаил, a hermit of Nitria of the sixth century, whom nothing joins to this apostle.

That page adds the detail of the journeying and of the relics. He was of Cana of Galilee and of the Twelve; the lot of Syria and Asia Minor fell to him and to [Philip](/saints/philip-the-apostle) after Pentecost, and [Mariamne](/saints/mariamne-sister-of-philip) went with them. At Hierapolis they destroyed by prayer a huge viper the pagans worshipped, healed a man named Stachys blind for forty years, and were condemned to crucifixion; the earth opened and swallowed the governor and the priests, and Bartholomew, hung low, was taken down alive while Philip died. He set Stachys bishop there and went on into India, where he translated the Gospel of Matthew into the local tongue out of Hebrew, and then into Great Armenia, where he healed the daughter of King Polymius and refused the gifts sent him, saying he sought only the salvation of men’s souls. The king’s brother Astyages seized him at Albanopolis, crucified him head downwards, and when he would not stop preaching from the cross had him flayed and beheaded.

Of the relics the calendar gives a route. They were laid in a tin reliquary, carried about 508 to Dara in Mesopotamia, and when the Persians took that city in 574 the Christians fled with them towards the Black Sea and, overtaken, let the ark down into the water. It floated to the island of Lipari, where the bishop Agathon met it with his clergy; myrrh flowed from the relics there until the ninth century, when the island fell and merchants carried them to Benevento, and in the tenth a part went on to Rome. The editors print beside this a letter from the chancery of the bishop of Baku disputing the life’s phrase «Албан Армянская» and setting out three readings of the place of his death, of which the Orthodox tradition holds Baku; it is noted here because a reader who opens the cited page will meet it.
