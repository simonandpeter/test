# Solochon

The holy martyrs Solochon, [Pamphamer](/saints/pamphamer) and [Pamphylon](/saints/pamphylon) — the last name also written Pamphalon or Pamphalion — came from Egypt and contended in the years of the emperor Maximian, who reigned from 286 to 305. They were serving in the army, and while they were at Chalcedon, the emperor having ordered that the Christian soldiers be sought out, they were commanded by the tribune Campanus to sacrifice, all of them, to the idols. All complied except these three, who refused and confessed their faith in the Trinity, and were at once arrested and put to frightful tortures.

Pamphamer and Pamphylon met a martyr's death under them. Solochon endured, and rebuked Campanus for his inhumanity, and was pierced with an iron instrument from the ear through to the brain; from that torture he was left without his senses and paralysed. Christians took him up and carried him to the house of a devout widow, and there he gave up his spirit to God. The page's couplet for him plays on his name: having fled Satan's ambushes, Solochon has come unwounded to the highest Word. One of its illustration captions gives his name in a second form, Solon.
