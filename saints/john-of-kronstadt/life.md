# John of Kronstadt

He was born on 19 October 1829 in the village of Sura in Archangel province, and named for John of Rila. His parents were very poor and very devoted to the Church, and the boy learned early to feel for others in their misfortune; his neighbours took to asking him to pray for them, having noticed the gift in him.

When he was ten his parents raised enough money to send him to the school attached to the church, and there he foundered — he worked days on end and could not keep up, read poorly and remembered nothing. He described one night afterwards: unable to sleep and understanding nothing he had been taught, he grew so low that he fell on his knees and prayed, and did not know how long he had knelt when something shook his whole being, as though a veil had fallen from his eyes and his mind had been opened. He remembered the day's teacher and lesson clearly, and the topic and the examples, and felt light and joyful.

After that he did so well that he was among the first of his class chosen for the seminary, and from there went to the Theological Academy at Saint Petersburg. He reposed in 1908.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 20 December too, and prints for his life the Romanian Synaxarion of the hieromonk Macarie of Simonos Petra. That account adds what the Russian one leaves out: that his father was a sacristan; that he took a post as secretary alongside his studies when his father died; that after a dream he agreed to marry the daughter of the protopresbyter of the Kronstadt cathedral, giving up his hope of mission in China for mission at home, and told her on their wedding day that there were enough happy families and that the two of them should serve the Lord — they lived in purity to the end, calling each other brother and sister.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr fills in the dates round it. It gives his birth at the village of Sura in the Archangel country on 18 October 1829, the feast of [John of Rila](/saints/john-of-rila), whose name he was given; his ordination as deacon on 11 November 1855 and as presbyter the next day, at the hands of the bishop Christopher Vinnitsky; and his repose at Kronstadt at twenty minutes to eight in the morning of 20 December 1908, in his eightieth year, with more than twenty thousand following the funeral. It dates the Russian Synod's act of recognition to 8 June 1990, and it puts aside as an urban myth the story that he held Rasputin in high esteem, saying the confusion is with another priest of the time, the protopresbyter Ioann Ianyshev.
