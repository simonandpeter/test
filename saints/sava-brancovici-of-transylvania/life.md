# Sava Brancovici of Transylvania

He was born at Inău of God-fearing parents, Ioan and Maria, and was given the name Simeon at his baptism. He learned his letters and the order of the services at Comana monastery, and became protopope and a servant of the Church of Christ in his native village, performing the holy things with great zeal and fear of God. Then he was widowed, and his mother took the monastic habit; and in 1656 the blessed priest Simeon was chosen metropolitan and spiritual shepherd of Ardeal.

He was first tonsured at the metropolia of Târgoviște under the name Sava; then, ordained bishop, he was set with honour on the metropolitan throne at Alba Iulia, in the place of the reposed hierarch [Simeon Ștefan](/saints/simeon-stefan-of-transylvania). As shepherd and spiritual father of the Romanians of Transylvania he proved a great defender of the Orthodox faith. In the twenty-four years he was metropolitan he confessed the right faith with much courage, contested the Calvinist teachings, and strengthened the unity of the Romanians around the Orthodox Church.

He raised many places of worship through the villages and the towns, set devoted priests everywhere, comforted the Transylvanian peasants and strengthened them in faith and hope, and renewed the metropolia and the churches that had been plundered and damaged. Seeing that he could not turn the people from the Orthodox faith while Sava stood there, the prince of Ardeal sowed enmity and revenge against the good shepherd.

Unjust testimonies were brought against him, and in 1680 he was put out of his throne. Then he was thrown into prison, and for three years was persecuted, beaten with cudgels, mocked and tormented as a martyr for the right faith, being pressed to go over to Calvinism; but he confessed Christ firmly and defended the dogmas and the tradition of the Orthodox Church. In 1683 he was brought out of prison, and weakened by the torments he gave up his soul at once. The faithful counted him among the saints while he was still alive, and the Romanian Orthodox Church canonised him in 1955 as a confessor of the right faith; his memory is kept on 24 April, and the day's troparion is one hymn for him and for [Iorest](/saints/iorest-of-transylvania) together.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, under the name Sabbas the Confessor of Romania, and its four sentences agree with the Romanian account and add a year of birth. Sabbas, Simeon in the world, lived in the seventeenth century in Transylvania. Having spent some time at the monastery of Comana, where he was taught the sacred letters, he was appointed a priest in the country of Arad; and in the year 1656 he was elected metropolitan of Transylvania, and shepherded his flock with holy zeal and love.

For that reason, in the year 1680, he was deposed from his throne and imprisoned. For three whole years he suffered dreadful torments in the prison for the love of Christ. He was released in 1683, and a little while afterwards, because of the hardships and the torments, he gave up his holy soul to God. The Greek page heads him «(1600 - 1683)», where the Romanian life gives only the year of his death.
