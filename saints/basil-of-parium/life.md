# Basil of Parium

Parium, the old city of Lesser Mysia, was honoured after it received the faith of Christ with the rank of an episcopal see, set under the metropolitan of Cyzicus. Basil was made bishop of that city, the life says, for his surpassing virtue and for a life pleasing to God; and that is nearly all the page tells of his beginnings, which it does not date and whose place of birth it does not name.

When the heresy of the icon-fighters broke out in the reign of Leo the Isaurian, Basil showed himself a confessor of Christ. He would not join the heretics nor agree with them, and though he was pressed to it he would not sign for the casting down of the holy icons. For that he was persecuted and ill-used by them, and he passed his life, the page says, as the Apostle Paul did — in troubles, in dangers and in straits, moving from place to place, keeping the fathers' teachings unchanged, loving the right faith and hating the assemblies of those who believed wrongly.

Pleasing God in all things, he fell asleep in peace in the Lord. The life gives no year, no account of his exile and no account of his death beyond that one sentence, and the Romanian calendar keeps him on 12 April with the troparion of a hierarch.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and gives him four sentences, which is more than the Romanian calendar prints. In the time of the iconoclasm, it says, this virtuous man was bishop in the city of Parium in Asia Minor. He refused to sign the imperial paper against the veneration of the icons, and for that was much persecuted and much tortured; but he stayed firm in Orthodoxy like a diamond. He ended in the first half of the eighth century and went over to the Lord — the only dating any source read for this folder gives him, and it is recorded here as the interval the phrase states and not as a chosen year.
