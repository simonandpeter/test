# Jonah of Yashezero

Jonah was born in the village of Shoksha, sixteen versts from the monastery he later founded. The monastery began in the 1580s, when a wooden church of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos was built and eight monks joined him for the ascetic life in the wilderness. He laboured hard over its building: to make fishing easier he dug a channel with his own hands from Lake Yashozero into the neighbouring Lake Sennoye, often rode on horseback along the forest’s remote paths in search of what the monastery needed, and turned wooden vessels for use in the services himself.

The fame of his holy life spread far beyond the monastery; pilgrims brought gifts, among them service books, and the monastery and its churches grew. Isidore, metropolitan of Novgorod, and the abbots of Solovki, Jacob and Irenarchus, held him in deep love and respect. He died at the end of the sixteenth century, the Sretensky life says, and was buried in the Annunciation monastery he had founded.

The fuller life on azbyka.ru sets the founding at the end of the reign of Ivan the Terrible, gives the first church a side chapel of Saint Nicholas, and says that two leather saddlebags of his and a vessel he turned from Karelian birch are still kept there, and that the nun Martha Ioannovna and Tsar Basil Shuisky (1606–1610) gave the monastery books, and the tsar lands as well.

It dates Abbot Jacob of Solovki 1581–1597, Irenarchus 1613–1626 and Metropolitan Isidore 1603–1619, notes a second church, of the Transfiguration, by 1628, and says that the time of Jonah’s death is not known exactly, probably at the end of the sixteenth century or the beginning of the seventeenth; the Sretensky day prints 1589–1592 and azbyka.ru’s day “after 1592”. His relics lie hidden in the chapel beneath the monastery’s bell tower. The Russian calendar keeps him on 22 September old style and prints no hymn for him.
