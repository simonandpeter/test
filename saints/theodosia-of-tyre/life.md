# Theodosia of Tyre

Doxologia's life for Theodosia is a quotation. About the virgin of Tyre, it says, Eusebius, bishop of Caesarea in Palestine, who saw it himself, wrote thus: the persecution by the idol-worshippers had run over them for five years when, on the second day of April, at the very feast of the Lord's Resurrection, in the city of Caesarea in Palestine, a faithful and well-born virgin from Tyre, not yet eighteen, came up to those shut in prison and bound for Christ, and spoke to them boldly about the kingdom of God.

She wished them well and asked them to remember her before the Lord when they should stand before Him, after the end of their martyr's contest. The soldiers, seeing her talking with men bound for Christ, seized her as though she had done some great wrong and brought her for questioning to the governor Urban. He, full of anger and of a beast's savagery, tormented her cruelly, tearing her ribs and breasts to the bone with iron claws; but her face was bright and she bore it all with courage. He then ordered her drowned in the depth of the sea.

This, doxologia says, is what Eusebius relates, who was an eyewitness to all the torments. To it the page adds what it calls the Romans' account: that after her drowning Theodosia was brought up alive out of the deep to dry land by angels, and walked carrying in her hands the stone that had been tied to her neck; that, taken again and brought to judgement, she was given to the beasts and they did not harm her; and that the torturer then ordered her beheaded.

When her head was struck off, a dove was seen flying out of her mouth, shining brighter than gold, and it went up towards heaven. That night she appeared to her parents in the midst of the holy virgins, in a garment whiter than snow, a golden cross in her hands and a crown on her head, saying: see how great is the glory and the gift of my Christ, of which you wished to deprive me. Her parents had seen her longing for the martyr's contest and had held her back from it; hiding from them, she ran to those bound for Christ and gave herself up. The page prints no year.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the same day, gives the same saying in the same words, and is the first book read for her to put a year to it. In the days of the emperor Maximian, it says, many Christians stood bound one day before the praetorium at Caesarea in Palestine; and this pious virgin Theodosia came up to them and blessed them and encouraged them for a martyr’s death. The soldiers, hearing what she said, led her too before the judge. In his rage he had a stone hung at her neck and had her thrown into the depth of the sea; but the angels of God brought her up alive to the shore, and when she appeared before the judge again he ordered her beheaded.

The following night Theodosia appeared to her parents, all in an exceeding heavenly brightness and surrounded by many other maidens who had been saved, and said: do you see how great is the glory and the grace of my Christ, of which you wished to deprive me? She said it to her parents because they had been turning her away from the confession of Christ and from martyrdom. She suffered honourably and was glorified, the Prologue says, in the year 308. It names the emperor where doxologia names the governor, and it has neither the iron claws, nor the beasts, nor the dove.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps her on 3 апреля ст. ст. — «Мц. Феодосии девы (307-308).» — and that is her death, where the Romanian and Serbian books this folder was written from both keep her on 29 May. This book keeps both days and says what each is for: 3 April is her martyrdom, and on 29 May it celebrates the translation of her relics to Constantinople and afterwards to Venice. A relic-translation is not a death, so the year is read off the April line and the May day is recorded here and not in the feast.

Its life makes her seventeen years old and sets the scene in the fifth year of a persecution. She came up to condemned Christian prisoners in the praetorium on the day of Holy Pascha, where they were speaking of the Kingdom of God, and asked them to remember her before the Lord when they stood before Him. Soldiers saw her bow to the prisoners, took her and brought her to the governor Урван, the same governor another of this calendar’s April days sets over Caesarea. He pressed her to sacrifice to the idols and she refused, confessing her faith in Christ.

Her body was torn with iron claws until the bones showed, and she bore it silently and with a glad face; asked a second time to sacrifice, she answered «Безумный, ведь я сподобилась присоединиться к мученикам!» — madman, I have been counted worthy to join the martyrs. They threw her into the sea with a stone about her neck and angels brought her out of the deep; they gave her to the beasts and the beasts would not touch her; and at the last they cut off her head. That night she appeared to her parents, who had tried to talk their daughter out of going to her sufferings, in bright clothing with a crown on her head and a shining golden cross in her hand, and said «Смотрите, вот какой великой славы вы хотели меня лишить!» — see what great glory you wanted to deprive me of.

The name link behind her line carries the title «Мученица Феодосия Тирская, Кесарийская», of Tyre and of Caesarea, and the second toponym is the city she died in rather than a second woman. The corpus does hold another Theodosia under a Caesarea of its own, whom two other books keep on 25 March beside Pelagia and date to 361; she is not this woman, her folder is named in the note on the name form below, and nothing here is moved onto it. The day page prints no hymn under this name.
