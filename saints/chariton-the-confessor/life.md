# Chariton the Confessor

Chariton was of the eparchy of Lycaonia, a citizen of Iconium, and in the days of the impious emperor Aurelian he showed himself a confessor of the name of Christ: when the decree went out that Christians must sacrifice or die, he - shining among the first of the city's believers - was seized, told the judge to his face that the idols of the pagans are demons, and was tortured for it. Released, he set out for Jerusalem, and near the city fell into the hands of robbers, who finding nothing on him dragged him to their cave to kill at leisure and went back to the road. As the bound man prayed, a serpent drank from a vessel of wine in the cave and spat its venom back; the robbers returned thirsty, drank, and died to the last man.

The robbers' hoard of many years Chariton put to good use - on the poor, on churches and monasteries - and with what remained he turned the robbers' cave into a church and built around it the dwelling called Pharan, gathering brothers. Fleeing the fame that followed, he found another cave a day's walk off, in the borders of Jericho, and lived there like an angel, fed on the wild greens of the place and more on the word of God, founding communities as men gathered around each of his flights; and to all the monasteries he had built they came running to him for guidance to his deep old age. The desert fathers mourned him with one voice, the life says, and buried his relics with honour - the confessor, martyr in will, and good ascetic of Christ.
