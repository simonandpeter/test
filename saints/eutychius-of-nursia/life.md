# Eutychius of Nursia

Eutychius and [Florentius](/saints/florentius-of-nursia) were monks who laboured in Italy in the sixth century, in the region of Nursia. Eutychius turned many to God by his teaching; when the abbot of the neighbouring monastery died he was begged to take charge and agreed, but went on caring for the old place of his labours, where his companion Florentius remained. Florentius worked wonders in his lifetime — he tamed a bear, which served him, pastured the sheep, carried water and did the elder’s other bidding; four monks, envying his fame, killed the bear, and the saint foretold God’s punishment on the killers, which came as he said, for they were struck with sickness — and seeing it Florentius grieved bitterly, calling himself the murderer of those monks. Eutychius worked no wonders in his life, but after his death healings were given from the garments he left, and in drought they were carried over the fields and God sent rain, as in 1492. Eutychius died on 23 May 540 and Florentius on 1 June 547.
