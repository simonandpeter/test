# Antonius, son of Melasippus

Antonius was the son of Melasippus and Cassine of Ancyra in Asia Minor, and saint.gr puts the three of them in the years of the emperor Julian the Apostate, which the page dates to 361. His parents, true Christians, brought him up with great care and he became a worthy member of society and a tried labourer of the Gospel. When the duke Agrippinus learned of the household's faith he had them arrested, and he pressed the boy by telling him that it lay with him to save his parents, as he told the parents that their son's life lay with them.

None of the three gave way. The parents were hanged on a wooden frame and killed; Antonius was horribly tortured and then put inside a lighted oven, and by the help of divine grace he came out of it unharmed. Seeing that wonder, forty young children came to the faith of Christ, and the king's servants beheaded them at once. Then they threw Antonius into a white-hot furnace, and again by God's intervention he was not burned; and after that wonder many more came over to the faith. In the end the executioners beheaded him.

His own distich bids him take the stroke with joy: «Στὰς Ἀντώνιε, τὴν τομὴν χαίρων δέχου, / Καὶ δέξεταί σε τῆς χαρᾶς θεῖος τόπος» — stand, Antonius, and receive the cutting with joy, and the divine place of joy will receive you. Kept with him are [Melasippus](/saints/melasippus), [Cassine](/saints/cassine) and [the Forty Children](/saints/forty-children). The page prints no hymn.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps the three of them together on the same day the Greek calendar does. Its line for 7 ноября ст. ст. is «Мчч. Меласиппа и Касинии и сына их Антонина (363).», and the name link behind «сына их Антонина» gives the calendar's own nominative, «Антонин Анкирский, мч.».

The life behind the three name links is four sentences and is the same for all three of them. They suffered in the reign of the emperor Julian the Apostate, in the city of Ancyra, in 363. Melasippus and Cassine, torn with iron hooks and disfigured, died under the torture. Their son, the boy Antoninus, whom the persecutor made stand and watch his parents' torments, spat in the face of the God-forsaking emperor; for that he was put to the cruellest tortures of all, in which he stayed unhurt, and was then beheaded.

Forty young men who saw that the Lord was keeping his confessor Antoninus and that the tortures did him no harm believed in Christ, confessed their faith openly and received a martyr's end — [the Forty Children](/saints/forty-children) this corpus keeps from the Greek line for this same day. **The Russian line does not name them**: it stops at the three, printing «Мчч. Меласиппа и Касинии и сына их Антонина (363).» and nothing more, so no Russian row was written on that folder and the forty are reported instead.

**The page puts Ancyra in Phrygia, and the two calendars do not give the same year.** Its words are «в городе Анкире во Фригии», where the pages this folder was written from put Ancyra in Galatia; the page does not reconcile the two and nothing is chosen here. The year is the second disagreement: saint.gr gave 361, the year of Julian's accession, and this page gives 363 on its line and in its life, so the death above now reads 361 or 363.
