# John of Suzdal

John entered one of the monasteries of the city of Suzdal from his youth, and the Russian calendar's life for 15 October says that for his virtuous and humble life he was made, in 1350, the first bishop of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod. It gives him one vision: during the divine Liturgy the prince of Suzdal, Boris Konstantinovich, saw an angel of God concelebrating with the hierarch. An article the same calendar prints beside the life carries the prince's own question and the bishop's answer — if God has shown you this vision, shall I dare hide it from you? That was an angel of the Lord.

He is remembered on that page for his love of the poor and the sick. For the poor he petitioned the princes to reduce their taxes; for the sick he set up almshouses and hospitals. He took much care, it adds, for the enlightening of the pagan Mordva with the Christian faith. When Suzdal was joined to the metropolia of Moscow he took the schema and withdrew to the Bogolyubsky monastery, where he lived in solitude and died in peace; and many healings were witnessed at his tomb.

The calendar gives no year for that death on the life page. The year 1373 is on the day line alone, and the only other year the page prints is 1350, for his consecration. His own name page keeps him on two days — 20 August, for the translation of his relics, and 15 October — and only the October day was read here.

His hymns know something the prose does not. The kontakion sings that he was counted worthy of divine defence when the eyes of his malicious slanderers were struck blind, and that, having made them see again by his prayer, he returned to his city and shepherded his flock well; the life as the calendar prints it has no slander and no blinding in it. The second of the two troparia on the same page, which is not drafted here, sets him beside Leontius the teacher of Rostov and says that he first bound and then blessed again the ill-minded churchmen who had opposed him.

The page also prints hymns for him together with Theodore of Suzdal, a troparion and a kontakion to the two hierarchs at once, and a prayer to the pair. Those belong to the joint commemoration rather than to this day's entry, and no claim is made here about which Theodore of the corpus they name. The Greek, Romanian and Serbian calendars were read for the same menologion day and none of the three keeps him; the Suzdal hierarch the Greek does keep on 15 October is Dionysius the archbishop, who is a different man.
