# Neophytos the Recluse

Neophytos was born in 1134 at Lefkara near Larnaka in Cyprus. At eighteen, betrothed by his parents, he fled the house and the wedding for the monastery of St John Chrysostom at Koutsoventis under the Pentadaktylos - was fetched home after two months, and would not be turned; his parents gave way. He came to the monastery illiterate, taught himself to read and write, learned the whole Psalter by heart, and served in the vineyards and as sacristan, until he found the cave near Paphos that became his Enkleistra: he enlarged it with his own hands into church, refectory and cell, and painted it with frescoes.

He never left his cave again. When Cyprus came running to it, he moved to a higher cave and counselled the faithful from there; and enclosed though he was, he had the whole picture of his age, and wrote it - his writings describe the island's events and its little-known saints, a hagiographic source in their own right, with services and catecheses for monks besides. Before his death he ordered the Enkleistra and laid the foundations of its church of the Dormition, which stands to this day; he departed this life in deep old age on 12 April 1219. On 28 September 1750 his forgotten tomb was found in a wondrous manner - which is the finding this feast day keeps.
