# Sosthenes of Chalcedon

[Victor](/saints/victor-of-chalcedon) and Sosthenes were soldiers at Chalcedon, and the Russian calendar tells their story inside the life of the great martyr [Euphemia](/saints/euphemia-the-great-martyr), about 304. When the governor Priscus ordered the two to throw Euphemia into a burning furnace, they saw two terrible angels in the flame, refused the order, and came to believe in the God she worshipped. Declaring boldly that they too were Christians, they went to their sufferings and were given to the beasts. As they were put to death they called on God’s mercy to receive them too into the Kingdom of Heaven, and hearing a heavenly voice calling them they departed to eternal life; the beasts did not so much as touch their bodies.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, tells it more briefly under Euphemia’s entry: seeing her kept unharmed in the fiery furnace, the two soldiers believed in Christ and were thrown to the beasts. It gives them no entry of their own.
