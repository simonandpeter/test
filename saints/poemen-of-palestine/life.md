# Poemen of Palestine

Poemen of Palestine lived in the sixth century in a cave in the desert of Rouba, under the emperor Maurice; Sophronius and John tell of him in the hundred and sixty-seventh chapter of the Spiritual Meadow. One winter the monk Agathonicus came to him for instruction and stayed the night in a neighbouring cave, and in the morning said he had suffered greatly from the cold; Poemen answered that he had been naked and felt no cold, because a lion had come and lain beside him and warmed him — but know, the ascetic added, that I shall be eaten by beasts, because when I lived in the world and kept sheep a man passed my flock and my dogs fell on him and tore him, and I could have saved him and did not — out of mischief, the Prologue says; it was revealed to me that I too shall die such a death. So it happened: three years later it became known that the holy hermit Poemen of Palestine had been torn by beasts, at the end of the sixth century.
