# Sosthenes of Colophon

saint.gr's calendar for 8 December keeps six apostles of the Seventy in one entry and names them all: Sosthenes, [Cephas](/saints/cephas-the-apostle), [Apollos](/saints/apollos-the-apostle), [Tychicus](/saints/tychicus-the-apostle), [Caesar](/saints/caesar-the-apostle) and [Epaphroditus](/saints/epaphroditus-the-apostle). Of Sosthenes it says that he was shown forth as bishop of the city of Colophon in Ionia, and that he was marked out for his gift of teaching and for his self-denial.

He was a fellow worker of the apostle Paul, the page says, and a mark of the love Paul bore him is that in the first letter to the Corinthians he calls him his fellow soldier. The couplet set over his name turns on his own: Sosthenes, bearing your
strength, my Christ, saved many by clothing them in strength. The page adds that
Epaphroditus took the see of Colophon after him.

The same calendar keeps a «Άγιος Κρίσπος» on 4 December, an apostle who is said
to have founded the Church of the island of Aegina and who was besides the ruler
of the synagogue at Corinth; and on that page it quotes Chrysostom from the
thirty-ninth homily on the Acts: «Oίμαι δε τούτον και Σωσθένη λέγεσθαι. Oς
τοσούτον ανήρ πιστός ην, ώστε και τύπτεσθαι, και παρείναι αεί τω Παύλω» — I
think that this man is also called Sosthenes, who was so faithful a man as to be
beaten, and to be always with Paul. The 8 December page quotes the same sentence
back.

The Crispus page notes one thing besides that points the same way without
Chrysostom: that some hagiologies keep Crispus on 8 December as well, together
with others of the Seventy, Sosthenes among them. This corpus held the two days
as two folders; the author ruled on 3 October 2026 that they are one man, and
this record is both. The identification is still Chrysostom's, reported by the
calendar and settled by neither of its pages, and it is left standing as that
rather than turned into a fact. Beyond the apostolate, Aegina and the synagogue,
the 4 December entry gives no century, no manner of death, no relic, no couplet
and no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, in a company of seven where the Greek calendar printed six — it adds Onesiphorus to the list — and it gives each of them a see. His, it says, was Caesarea: «Свети Состен би епископ у Кесарији», Sosthenes was bishop in Caesarea. That is not the city this folder is named for, and it is the city this corpus gives to another of the same seven; nothing is renamed on it here and neither reading is chosen. The book adds that [Tychicus](/saints/tychicus-the-apostle) was his successor in the same place.

It says of all of them together that they preached the Gospel of Christ with a burning love, that they suffered torments for his holy name, and that they passed over into the kingdom of everlasting joy. It also records its own other days for them: all seven are celebrated on 4 January with the rest of the lesser apostles, and it gives separate days to [Apollos](/saints/apollos-the-apostle) on 10 September and to [Cephas](/saints/cephas-the-apostle) and [Caesar](/saints/caesar-the-apostle) on 30 March. The day recorded here is the one whose heading names him.

The Russian church calendar keeps the five of them together on 30 марта ст. ст. and prints two readings behind the line, which do not agree with each other about where they sat. Of Sosthenes both agree: before he was a Christian he was the ruler of the Jewish synagogue at Corinth, he was beaten in the rising against the apostle Paul, and he was afterwards converted by Paul himself and made bishop in Colophon. The longer reading adds that Paul greets the church of Corinth in both their names — «Павел, волею Божиею призванный Апостол Иисуса Христа, и Сосфен брат» — and that Tychicus took the see of Colophon after him, where the Greek page gives that succession to Epaphroditus. The reading that heads the company closes it «Все эти апостолы мирно преставились к Богу (вторая память 30 марта)»: they all fell asleep in peace, and this day is their second memory.
