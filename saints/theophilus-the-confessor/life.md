# Theophilus the Confessor

Theophilus lived when Leo the Isaurian, the iconoclast, was emperor. The Greek synaxarion says he went about Constantinople warming the endurance of the Orthodox and exposing the error of the persecutors of the icons, and that his virtue and the power of his word made him one of the strongest champions of piety. He was imprisoned and then exiled, and ended his life in exile, having refused a passing freedom bought with the betrayal of the truth and the loss of eternal life. Its verses have God tell his friend Theophilus that his death is precious in his sight.

The Romanian life calls him a devout monk who went to the emperor and rebuked him to his face as lawless and a forerunner of Antichrist; Leo, in his anger, had him cruelly beaten and thrown into prison, where he suffered hunger, and, when he would not submit, sent him into distant exile, where he gave up his soul to God with thanksgiving. Neither calendar gives a year.
