# Gregory of Nyssa

Gregory was brother in the flesh to [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great), and from his youth he went through the whole of learning to its end: a famous rhetorician and a chosen philosopher, as Basil his brother was. He married the blessed Theosebia, whom [Gregory the Theologian](/saints/gregory-the-theologian) honoured afterwards with many praises, calling her the ornament of the Church, the beauty of Christ, in truth a holy wife of one consecrated, and worthy of the great mysteries.

For while her husband was ordained presbyter, she lived in holiness, in purity and soundness of mind, serving the sick, caring for the poor, and shining with good works; and she was counted worthy of the service of the diaconate, being consecrated deaconess, which is why the Theologian says she is worthy of great mysteries. Having pleased the Lord, she fell asleep and was numbered among the saints.

Her husband was raised to the rank of the episcopate at Nyssa, and shepherded the Church of Christ well. In the reign of Valens the Arians grew strong, having on their side an emperor blinded by the same heresy, and Gregory was driven from his throne; he went from place to place for eight years, until the emperor's death, travelling through cities and countries and strengthening the right-believing.

In the reign of the great Theodosius, when the second council of the whole world met at Constantinople in 381 against Macedonius, who fought against the Holy Spirit, Gregory was there with the holy Fathers as a helper of the right faith, confronting and shaming its adversaries with the force of the truth shown out of the divine Scripture. Sitting on his throne many years afterwards, he came to a deep old age and fell asleep in the Lord, leaving behind him many writings useful to the holy Church. In the body, the life says, he was like his brother Basil, but grey, and a little more cheerful.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day. saint.gr gives his birth at Neocaesarea in Pontus in the year 332, brother of [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great); he was schooled as his great brother was, and was marked out in his turn for his cleverness, his diligence and a most philosophical genius, and had been ordained reader. He married [Theosebia](/saints/theosebia-the-deaconess), whom the page calls a truly holy woman and whom the same day keeps, and death took her quickly. Strong of character as he was, the page says, he did not despair, and in his fortieth year he became bishop of Nyssa — Nevşehir today.
