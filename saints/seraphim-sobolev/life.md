# Seraphim (Sobolev)

Seraphim (Sobolev), archbishop of Boguchar, was born Nicholas Borisovich Sobolev on 1 December 1881 at Ryazan in Russia, in the family of the clerk Boris Matveyevich Sobolev; his mother, Maria Nikolaevna, was marked by a deep faith and an upright life. The life quotes the psalm — *from my mother's womb thou art my protector* — and says that it was made good in him from his birth, for his mother could not bring him forth, and the doctors held that the child must be cut out to save her life. She came to herself, learned what they had decided, and made her husband swear not to allow the child to be killed.

After a night of terrible pains, at the first stroke of the church bell, at five in the morning, the child was born by himself, with no help from outside. His mother asked to see him — «show me the child I nearly died for» — and when he was brought said: «What a serious *muhtar* has been born.» In his childhood his relations sometimes called little Nicholas «muhtar», which he did not like at all; years later, in Bulgaria, reading the memoirs of bishop Nestor of Manchuria, he learned that in Arabic *muhtar* means a bishop. So from his birth, the page says, his mother knew the honour of her child.

From early childhood the sadness of one chosen by God could be read in his face. He was quiet, sensitive, attentive to those about him, unusually serious for a boy of his age, and his humility was remarkable; the life illustrates it with an incident from the parish school, where at twelve he alone had understood the master's arithmetic and would not say so. Between that beginning and his end the page prints a very long account of his years in Russia and then in Bulgaria — more than can be read at one sitting — and this paragraph says so rather than summarising what has not been read here.

God called his faithful servant to himself on the Sunday of Orthodoxy, 13/26 February 1950. In the days before his death the smile was on his lips, and when his nearest spiritual children lamented, asking who would comfort them now, the bishop told them that if he found boldness before the Saviour he would intercede for them at the throne of the Holy Trinity. God was pleased to reveal to him the day of his own end: five days before his falling asleep, feeling beyond measure weak and hardly to be heard, he whispered to those about him, «Five days more»; the next day he said, «Four days more»; and after four days he gave up his spirit.

His departure was a great loss to his spiritual children, but their sorrow was quickly driven off, for the bishop appeared in a dream to one of them, a monk who wept at night after his repose, and said: «Why dost thou weep? I have not died, but am alive.» His grave, in the Russian church of Saint Nicholas in Sofia, is an unfailing spring of wonders; as in his life, so after his death, he goes on helping, full of love, those who run to him for help, not only from every corner of Bulgaria but from abroad. The page gives no hymn.
