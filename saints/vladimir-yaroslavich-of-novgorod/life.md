# Vladimir Yaroslavich of Novgorod

He was the eldest son of Yaroslav the Wise, born in 1020, and at fourteen his father made him his lieutenant in Novgorod. The voivode Vyshata and the holy bishop Luke Zhidyata helped him govern it. Grown, he became a brave defender of the country and a devout Christian: he had a stone fortress built at Novgorod, and he studied in the law of the Lord — the prophetic books with their commentaries were copied out for him in 1047, which the calendar records as a fact about him.

What he is chiefly remembered for is the cathedral of Holy Wisdom at Novgorod. It was founded in 1045 and consecrated on 14 September 1052 by Bishop Luke. He died twenty days after that consecration, on 4 October 1052, aged thirty-two, and his relics were laid in the church he had built. The Novgorod Synodicon mentions his wife, the princess Alexandra.

His commemoration was not immediate. It was established in 1439, nearly four centuries after his death, by [Euthymius of Novgorod](/saints/euthymius-of-novgorod), who is kept on 11 March. The Russian calendar is the only one of the four read here that keeps him at all.
