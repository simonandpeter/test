# Titus the Wonderworker

Titus loved Christ from his youth, and leaving the world he went into a community. Taking the angelic habit upon himself, he walked the narrow and hard road of the monastic life with much patience for God's sake; and he had such humility and such obedience that in those virtues he surpassed not only the brothers of his house but all men whom the life knows of.

Afterwards he was set as shepherd over the rational sheep of Christ, and he had so much gentleness, love and mercy towards everyone that in that time there was no other man of his kind. Having kept himself clean in body and in soul from his young years, he was like an angel of the Lord; and so he was given to work miracles, and he showed himself a living and unmoving pillar to the holy Church at a time when it was troubled by the heresy of the war against the icons. Leaving his disciples the pattern of a hermit's life, he departed to the Lord.

That is the whole of what doxologia prints. It gives no year and no century, names no country, no city and no monastery, and names no emperor; the iconoclast trouble is the only thing in it that places him at all, and the page does not say on which side of it he stood beyond calling him a pillar of the Church. It does carry a troparion for him, in the eighth tone.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's life is a portrait rather than a history: it says nothing of iconoclasm and gives him no year, no country and no monastery. It sets over him the Lord's word to the disciples, «ἐμὸν βρῶμά ἐστιν ἵνα ποιῶ τὸ θέλημα τοῦ πέμψαντός με» — my food is to do the will of him who sent me — and says that this was Titus's food too. When he became a monk he shone by his kindness to his brothers, his meekness and his forbearance: a character, the page says, who knew how to overlook, to be long-suffering, to bear with, to talk a misunderstanding apart, to win trust quickly and to take other men's hearts.

So he became a spiritual leader whom laymen and monks in numbers sought out for their profit, and God repaid the purity of his soul and his life with the gift of working wonders. He stayed firm in the faith to the end of his life and departed to the Lord, leaving many imitators behind him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, tells the same portrait in the same order, and gives him the one thing neither other page does: a century. From his young years, it says, he loved Christ the Lord and hated the vain world, and for that he left the world, went to a monastery and received the angelic habit; sparing himself nothing, he gave himself to the sorrowful and narrow monastic way, and through great patience gained the two root virtues, humility and obedience, in which he surpassed not only the brethren but all men. Purity of soul and body he had kept from his youth. In the time of the iconoclast heresy he showed himself an unshakeable pillar of the Church of God. For his great humility and purity the gift of wonderworking was given him by God both in his life and after his death, which is more than the other two pages say; and when he passed over to the Lord he left many disciples behind him. He reposed peacefully, the entry ends, in the ninth century — the first and only placing of him in time that any source read for this folder gives.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the Julian 2 April as well — «Прп. Тита чудотворца (IX).» — and its life gives him the two things the other three pages withhold: a house and a rank. It has him give himself to the monastic life from his young years and sets his struggle in the ninth century in the Студийский monastery near Constantinople, the house of the Studion, which neither doxologia nor saint.gr nor the Prologue names. By his fasting, the purity of his life and his meek temper, it says, he earned the common love of the brethren, and at their own asking he was ordained presbyter.

A warm faith moved him to stand manfully for the Orthodox honouring of the icons through the iconoclast persecutions — the same trouble the other lives place him in, here stated as a side taken and not only as a time endured — and for his life of virtue he was granted the gift of wonderworking by God. He departed to the Lord in deep old age. The year the calendar prints, the ninth century, is the one this folder already holds from the Prologue, and nothing here narrows it. The day page carries a kontakion for him in глас 4, his own and naming him, and no other hymn under his name.
