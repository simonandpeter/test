# Paul of Crete

saint.gr's calendar for 17 Μαρτίου keeps a venerable-martyr Paul, and what it knows of him it knows at second hand. He came from Crete, the page says, and was martyred under Theophanes Lardotyros, the arch-satrap of the island of Crete; and the source it names for this is the life of [Stephen the New](/saints/stephen-the-new), whose memory the Church honours on 28 November.

Stephen was a monk on Mount Auxentius in Bithynia, seized as a friend of the icons and shut up at Constantinople under the emperor Constantine V Copronymus (740–775). In the prison he found three hundred and forty-two other monks held for the same cause, and among them a monk from Crete named Antonius, who began to tell the other prisoners of the ferocity of the persecutions in Crete and of the martyrdom of Saint Paul.

Theophanes had Paul brought to the praetorium of Heraklion and pressed him either to trample on the icon of the Lord, who was crucified for man, or to take the road of the tortures. Paul not only refused: he knelt down and kissed the icon. The arch-satrap, enraged, ordered him stripped and bound with irons, and then hung him up and burned him alive. The page sets his martyrdom at about the year 765 or 766.

Over him saint.gr prints a couplet, «Ζῆλος διεξέκαυσε Παῦλον Εἰκόνων, ᾯ φλὸξ δι' αὐτὰς ἐξεκαύθη καμίνου» — zeal for the icons set Paul alight, and for their sake the flame of the furnace was kindled for him. No hymn is printed under his name and no relics are named, and nothing else about him has been read anywhere else or supplied from memory.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 17 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/17/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3910/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
