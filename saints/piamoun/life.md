# Piamoun

saint.gr says the venerable Piamoun came of Egypt, where she lived in the fourth century. She was left fatherless young, but her Christian mother brought her up in the instruction and admonition of the Lord; and when she was grown she gave herself, together with her mother, to works of almsgiving and compassion. They were not rich, the page says, but out of their poor income they came to the help of the poor and the weak among their brethren.

When her mother fell asleep, Piamoun, now alone, gave herself to the service of the suffering. She visited them in their houses, consoled them, and steadied them in the faith. Such was the name she won by it that when enemies were besieging her birthplace, they heard her name and lifted the siege and went away. She fell asleep in a holy manner and was buried amid a general mourning.

That is the whole of the notice. It gives no city in Egypt, no year of birth or of repose, no monastery, no relic and no hymn, and it does not say who the enemies were. The distich over her is the only other thing there, and it sets her in the black habit against the golden fringes of the psalm: Piamoun the black-robed goes away clothed in her virtues.

The Serbian church keeps her on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells a different five sentences about her. For Christ's sake she would not marry, it says, but gave herself to the ascetic life in her mother's house. She took only a little food, and that every second day, and spent her time in prayer and in contemplation. She had the gift of foresight. She fell asleep in peace, having betrothed her soul to the Lord, about the year 377.

That year is the only one any source read for her gives, and it is now recorded as her death beside the fourth century saint.gr states for her life. The two accounts otherwise share almost no incident: where saint.gr has her orphaned young, giving alms together with her mother out of a poor income, and winning such a name that besiegers heard it and went away, the Prologue has the refused marriage, the food every second day and the foresight. Both keep her on the third of March and both make her a virgin of Egypt; nothing was read that would reconcile the rest, and nothing was dropped.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps her on the Julian 3 March, «Прп. Пиамы девы (337).», and its life is the fullest of the three read for her. She struggled not far from Alexandria. In her mother’s house she lived as though in enclosure: she saw nobody, took food every other day, and after her prayers spun flax. She was counted worthy of the gift of foresight.

When the people of a neighbouring and more populous village, blinded by greed, were ready to destroy the villagers of her own in order to turn the whole of the Nile’s flood onto their own fields, she knew of the wicked intention in spirit and told the elders of her village. They fell at her feet in terror, begging her to go to the neighbours and hold them back from the crime; she would not go to meet them, having long given up the company of people, and spent the whole night in prayer instead.

In the morning the armed men of the neighbouring community, on their way to her village, stopped suddenly and could not move further, and the Lord made known to the wretched men that it was the prayer of the virgin Piamoun that held them. They came to themselves and repented of the plot, and sent envoys to the village asking for peace, and said: thank God, who by the prayers of the virgin Piamoun has delivered you. She departed to the Lord in peace.

The year is where this calendar and the Greek part, and both readings are now carried. saint.gr’s life, from which this folder was written, has her die about 377; this calendar gives 337 both on its line and in its own last sentence, «Святая мирно отошла ко Господу в 337 году». Forty years apart, and the two years are each other’s digits reversed, which is a thing a reader may notice and not a thing a reader may decide: no third source has been read, neither is chosen, and the interval carries both. The fourth century already recorded as her floruit holds either. The day prints no hymn under her name.
