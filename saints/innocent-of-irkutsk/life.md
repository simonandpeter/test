# Innocent of Irkutsk

saint.gr's calendar for 26 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Ιννοκεντίος ο Θαυματουργός πρώτος επίσκοπος Ιρκούτας» — Innocent the wonderworker, first bishop of Irkutsk — heads his page with the year 1731, and then says outright that it has nothing more: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Ρώσου Αγίου», we have no details of the life of the Russian saint.

That one word, Russian, is all it says of where he came from, and the see and the epithet are all it says of what he did. The page gives him no year of birth, no manner of death, no companions, no relics of its own and no hymn, and it sets no couplet over him. This folder records the line, the year and that sentence, because there is nothing else on the page.

The Russian calendar has the dossier the Greek page said it did not have. days.pravoslavie.ru carries two lives for him on 26 ноября ст. ст., and the fuller of them opens where saint.gr stops: he was born towards the end of the seventeenth century in Little Russia, in the province of Chernigov, by tradition into a priest's family of the name Кольчицкий or Кульчицкий, an old Polish line whose surname and nobility the page traces to a grant of Bolesław the Brave. At baptism he was called John. He was schooled at home and then at the Kyiv theological academy, where he worked at every subject and at rhetoric hardest of all, meaning to spend his life preaching; and before he left it he was tonsured, in the cave of Antony outside Kyiv, under the name Innocent.

About 1706 to 1708 he was called to Moscow as teacher and prefect of the Slavonic-Greek-Latin academy, and from there to St Petersburg and the Nevsky monastery that was then only being founded, to be a pattern of monastic life in it. The emperor noticed him. A Russian mission was to be planted at Peking under a bishop, and on the recommendation of the metropolitan of Siberia he was chosen for it: on the third week of the Great Fast, Sunday 5 March 1721, he was consecrated bishop in the Trinity cathedral of that same Nevsky house, with the emperor himself standing by.

He never reached Peking. Titled bishop of Bielsk, he left Petersburg in Bright Week with two hieromonks, a hierodeacon, five singers and three servants, took nearly a year to reach Irkutsk, went on beyond Baikal and halted at Selenginsk on the Chinese frontier to wait for leave to enter. The Jesuits then strong at Peking saw that the leave was refused, and the pretext was a word in his own letter of request: he had been styled «богдо», the great, which the Chinese used of their emperor alone, and two great persons could not be in China at once.

He sat at Selenginsk three years without stirring and without his salary, kept by alms, fishing and day labour, mending his own worn clothes and painting icons for the monastery's church so as not to eat its bread for nothing; and he wrote to the Synod asking plainly where he was to lay his head. Those three idle years, the page says, were not idle: he had a bishop's right to ordain, and he used it for the country beyond Baikal, which until then had to send its candidates as far as Tobolsk.

In March 1725 he was told to move to the Вознесенский monastery at Irkutsk. The embassy of 1726 was to have carried him into China after all, but an archimandrite who wanted the mission for himself worked on the ambassador, the ambassador wrote to Petersburg that the Chinese would never receive him, the letter was believed unchecked, and the mission went to the archimandrite. What came instead was a new see: he was made bishop of Irkutsk and Nerchinsk in his own right, the first of them, and the independent line of Irkutsk bishops begins with him.

The salary never came; the consistory held that it had been granted for China and not for Irkutsk. He said that if it ever came he would spend the first thousand roubles on a stone church. He served his last liturgy on the Sunday of 3 October, lay ill through November, and died at the seventh hour of Saturday 27 November 1731 — one day past the day on which this calendar keeps him. He was buried on 5 December in a stone vault under the altar of the wooden Тихвинская church.

The page then follows his relics for two centuries and a half, which is the part of his story the Greek calendar does not know at all. They were taken up out of the vault and carried in procession on 9 February to the Вознесенский cathedral at Irkutsk, where they lay fifty-five years; moved for twelve more while the church was rebuilt; returned to it on 15 October 1872. In January 1921 they were opened for a «medical examination», stripped and left uncovered, and then carried away under guard to an unknown place. They were held lost until 1990, when unidentified relics were found in a back room of the church of Никола Надеин at Yaroslavl and the forensic department of the Yaroslavl medical institute matched their description to the act drawn up at Irkutsk in 1921.

The Охридски пролог gives him two sentences and no life at all: he ended in the year 1731, and his wonderworking relics were uncovered in 1804. The year of his repose is the one this folder already held from the Greek calendar, which has no narrative for him either; the uncovering of the relics is new here, and it is recorded in this paragraph rather than in the folder’s dates, which hold the years of the man and not of his relics. The book heads him Иркутски, of Irkutsk, and чудотворац, wonderworker, which are the see and the grade this folder already carries.
