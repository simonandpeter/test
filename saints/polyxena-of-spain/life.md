# Polyxena

Polyxena and her elder sister [Xanthippe](/saints/xanthippe-of-spain) lived
in Spain in the days of the apostles — under the emperor Claudius, the
Romanian synaxarion says — and first heard the divine teaching of Christ the
Saviour from the apostle Paul when he preached in their country. Xanthippe
and her husband Probus, the governor of Spain, received Christianity;
Polyxena was still a pagan when a man taken with her extraordinary beauty
carried her off by force to Greece, and the Lord kept her unharmed: on the
ship she heard the preaching of the apostle Peter and believed, and reaching
Greece she put herself under the protection of the Christians, who hid her
at Patras in Achaea, where she was baptised by the apostle Andrew the
First-called himself. She saw his wonders, and the patience and meekness
with which he bore his tortures, and stood by the cross on which he was
crucified; after his martyrdom she returned to Spain, where with her sister
she turned many pagans to Christ — for about forty years, the Russian church
calendar says, she preached the Gospel in Spain and confirmed multitudes in
the faith, while Xanthippe shared her labours and preached in the populous
city of Toledo. The Romanian synaxarion tells it a little differently:
Polyxena was baptised by Andrew with her friend Raveca, and the two came
home in the company of the apostle Onesimus, at which Xanthippe too was
baptised, and with her she preached our Lord Jesus Christ to the end of her
life. Polyxena died about 109, a virgin to the end. The Russian calendar
keeps the two sisters on 23 September of the old style, 6 October, and the
Romanian calendar on 23 September.

The Greek synaxarion keeps [Xanthippe](/saints/xanthippe-of-spain) and Polyxena together on the same day, «Οσίες Ξανθίππη και Πολυξένη», and sets them in the middle of the first century, when Claudius I (41–54) was Caesar. Its telling has Polyxena, on her wanderings, hear the apostle Philip preach in Greece after Peter, before Andrew baptized her; and both sisters, it says, died peacefully in advanced age, strengthening weak souls in the Christian hope to their last breath. The Greek calendar keeps the pair with two apolytikia and two kontakia, of which the first apolytikion and the second kontakion are Polyxena’s alone.
