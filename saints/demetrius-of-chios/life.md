# Demetrius of Chios

Demetrius was born in 1780 in the village of Paliokastro on Chios, saint.gr says, of virtuous parents, Apostolis and Maroulou. Young still, he came to Constantinople and worked beside his brother Zannis, who was a merchant. When he became engaged to a girl without his brother's leave, Zannis turned him out of the shop.

Finding himself in great poverty, he remembered that the Sheikh-ul-Imalil owed his brother a sum for purchases of cloth, and went to the man's house to collect it and use it himself. There he met a young Muslim woman who fell in love with him and told him that to marry her he would have to change his religion. Demetrius accepted the offer and stayed in the Turks' house for two months.

But he soon came to himself, escaped, and hid for a time with a Christian family in the quarter of Stavrodromi. His brother Zannis found him there. Demetrius confessed to his brother's spiritual father, and at the same time wrote a letter to his own father setting out everything that had happened to him and his longing to bear witness for Christ. Having received communion of the immaculate mysteries he presented himself to the Turkish governor and confessed his faith boldly.

The Chiots of Constantinople gathered money for his release, but Demetrius stayed unshaken in his faith; and so, after fearful tortures which he bore without a murmur, he was beheaded on 29 January 1802. The Christians took up his honoured relic and buried it in a monastery on the island of Proti. The page prints for him an apolytikion and a kontakion, both copied here, and a megalynarion, an oikos and a kathisma besides, which this corpus has no fields for.
