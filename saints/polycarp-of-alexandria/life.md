# Polycarp of Alexandria

saint.gr says the martyr Polycarp lived in the reign of the impious Maximian — the page sets that reign down as 286 to 305 — and came from the city of Alexandria. He was a Christian with a great zeal for God, and day after day, watching the prisoners confess Christ and be tried with one torment after another, he could not bear to hold still and endure it.

One day he saw the governor sitting at his work and the blood of men being poured out like water; and he stood in front of him and reproached him: «Γιατί τόσο πολύ λησμόνησες την ανθρώπινη φύση, ακόρεστε σκύλε, και κομματιάζεις τους συγγενείς και ομοεθνείς ανθρώπους με τα ξίφη σαν ξύλα, επειδή κηρύττουν τον αληθινό Θεό και ελέγχουν την πλάνη των ειδωλολατρών, όπως και εγώ που είμαι δούλος του Χριστού;» — why have you so far forgotten human nature, insatiable dog, and cut your own kinsmen and countrymen to pieces with swords like so much wood, because they preach the true God and reprove the error of the idolaters, as I do, who am a servant of Christ?

For this, and because he had enraged the governor, he was seized and tortured; and having the name of Christ on his lips to the end, he was beheaded. The page names no governor, gives him no year of birth and no year of death beyond the reign, names no relics and prints no hymn under his name. It does print a couplet on him: «Ὡς κλῆμα τμηθεὶς Πολύκαρπος Κυρίου, / Τὸν καρπὸν οὕτω πλείονα Χριστῷ φέρει» — cut like a vine-branch of the Lord, Polycarp bears the more fruit for Christ. He is not the bishop of Smyrna of that name, whom the same calendar keeps on 23 February.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the Julian 2 April too, in a line of four words and a century: «Мч. Поликарпа (IV).» Its life is nearly as short. He suffered, it says, for boldly reproaching the emperor Maximian for the shedding of innocent Christian blood in the city of Alexandria; he confessed himself a Christian openly and went to the torments of his own will; and after heavy sufferings he was beheaded. The ruler saint.gr leaves unnamed is here the emperor himself, and the two pages do not agree on when that was: saint.gr’s Maximian reigns 286 to 305, this one’s 305 to 313, and the day line says only the fourth century. The folder’s date is widened to hold both readings and narrows neither. The day page prints no hymn under his name.
