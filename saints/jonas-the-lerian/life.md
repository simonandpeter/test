# Jonas the Lerian

saint.gr says that from the «ΒΡΑΒΕΙΟΝ» of the holy and royal monastery of St John the Theologian on Patmos it can be established that many men of Leros left the island over the five preceding centuries and went to the monastery of Patmos to be monks, and that one of them was the monk Jonas, of whom the Bravelon records: «αφξα (1561) Φεβρουαρίου κη (28) εφονεύθη εις την Λειψόν ο δούλος του Θεού Ιωνάς μοναχός ο Λέριος» — on 28 February 1561 the servant of God Jonas, monk, the Lerian, was killed on Leipsoi.

Of this venerable-martyr of the Church individually, the page says plainly, we have no specific information; what follows on it comes from the book of Archimandrite Nikephoros Koumoundouros, parish priest of Leipsoi, on the blessed elders and five venerable-martyrs of the island of Leipsoi in the Dodecanese. These holy souls, it says, were proclaimed venerable-martyrs by the Ecumenical Patriarchate, and all five are celebrated together on the first Sunday after 10 July — a feast this corpus has no field for, being neither a fixed day nor an offset from Pascha, so only the fixed 28 February of his own killing is recorded here.

The page's account of the other four runs alongside his: the monk Neophytos the Amorgian had been killed in 1558, before him; in 1609 the monk Neophytos Fazos was killed by the Hagarenes with an adze; in 1635 Pekir Pasha killed the monk Jonas the Nisyrian by flogging; and in 1696 the anchorite monk Parthenios was killed with a harpoon that pierced his throat. All five of the wrongly killed blessed elders, it ends, were buried with tears by their fellow ascetics and lamented by the devout people of Leipsoi and by their own countrymen, and all of us keep them in our memory with gratitude and reverence, counting them worthy venerable-martyrs. The other four are not lines on this day's calendar and are not kept here.
