# Mark of the Lavra of Pskov

saint.gr's calendar for 29 Μαρτίου names three venerable fathers on one line, «Όσιοι Ιωνάς, Μάρκος και Βάσσος», and the page behind the line is a single sentence about all three: the venerable fathers Mark, Jonah and Bassos lived and practised ascesis in the Lavra of Pskov in Russia during the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, and fell asleep in holiness and peace. The narrative puts Mark first where the calendar line puts Jonah first, and that is the only thing on the page that distinguishes one of them from another.

There is nothing else. No year of birth and none of death, no office, no abbacy, no teacher, no disciple, no wonder, no relic, no hymn, and no word of what any of the three did in the two centuries the sentence spans. The gloss the page prints on his name — «ΜΑΡΚΟΣ (από το λατινικό marceo) = ο αδιάφορος προς τα υλικά επίγεια», the man indifferent to material earthly things and therefore a lover of spiritual ones — is about the word and not about him.

He is kept here with [Jonah](/saints/jonah-of-the-lavra-of-pskov) and [Bassos](/saints/bassos-of-the-lavra-of-pskov), which is how the source keeps him, and the monastery is recorded in the only form the source gives it, saint.gr's Greek «Λαύρα του Πσκωφ». A Russian calendar would say more of all three and would spell the house at home; none was read for this folder.

The Russian church calendar keeps this house’s first fathers on 29 марта ст. ст., and the life it links is one life for three of them. Nobody knows when the first hermits settled by the brook Каменец in the natural caves of the hill the country people called the holy hill; the monastery chronicle keeps the account of hunters from Изборск who came to the edge of the hill with their father and heard what seemed to be church singing, sung in order and with reverence, with no singers to be seen and the air full of the fragrance of incense.

In 1472 a peasant named Иван Дементьев was felling timber on the brow of the hill; one of the trees rolled down, tearing up the earth and the other trees with it, and the landslip opened the mouth of a cave over which was written «Богом зданная пещера», the cave built by God. To that place came a priest called Иоанн, surnamed «Шестник», the settler: a man of the Moscow country who had served the Russians of Юрьев, which is Tartu now, and who fled with his family to Pskov in 1470 from the persecution of the German Catholics. Hearing that his fellow priest «Исидор» had died a martyr, he resolved to withdraw into the newly found cave and to found a monastery there, on the very border with Livonia, as a bulwark of Orthodoxy.

Of the first elders of that house only one is known by name, and it is his. The chronicle says of him that an elder lived at the brook Каменец by the cave, and that certain hunters used to see him by three stones lying above the cave church of the Mother of God, but that nothing certain could be found of what manner of elder he was, or of what family, or how and whence he came to that place, or how long he lived there, or how he died. The second abbot entered his name in the monastery’s synodikon; a later abbot doubted the entry and ordered it struck out, fell suddenly and gravely ill, was given to know that this was why, and restored the name after asking forgiveness with tears at the elder’s grave. When the cave church of the Dormition was dug out and the burial caves widened, the abbot found his coffin rotted away and the relics and the clothing untouched. The day’s line gives his century as «XV».
