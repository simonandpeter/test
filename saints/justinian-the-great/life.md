# Justinian the Great

Justinian the Great, emperor from 527 to 565, was born in the year 483 of believing parents at Tauresium, a village south of Uskub, near Skopje, the capital of today's Macedonia. His parents, whom the Romanian life names Sabbatius and Vigilantia, were Christians and gave him a choice education. In 518 his uncle Justin became emperor; and four months before his own death, in the month of April in the year 527, it appears that he adopted Justinian and anointed him caesar in his place.

He seems to have been near his uncle from his youth, and so had the chance of an uncommon education, becoming an exceptional student of the law and of the history of the Roman Empire and a very good theologian besides. As emperor he was a firm supporter of the Church. After the emperor Theodosius I had proclaimed Christianity the one religion accepted in the state, Justinian's legislation came with fresh reinforcements of it, taking from non-Christians the right of holding public office.

In his reign a great many churches were raised, the most impressive of them the cathedral of Hagia Sophia; and in the year 553 the fifth ecumenical council was called at Constantinople, where the monophysite heresy was condemned. His legislative work, the Justinian Code, and his religious work are together, the life says, a valuable inheritance left to those who came after him. He passed to eternal things in November 565. The site prints no hymn of his and no more of his life than this.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the Julian 14 November, not on 2 august, and in one line with his consort: «Правоверного царя Иустиниана (565) и царицы Феодоры (548).» Its life makes him a Slav, born in Bulgaria — a thing neither of the other calendars says — and credits his reign of 527 to 565 with victories in Persia, Africa and Italy by which paganism was rooted out among the Vandals and the Visigoths. He closed the pagan schools of Athens by decree. To spread Christianity in Asia Minor he sent John, bishop of Ephesus, who baptised more than seventy thousand, and he ordered ninety churches built for the newly baptised.

The best buildings of his time, that life says, are the monastery on Sinai and the church of the Holy Wisdom at Constantinople, and under him more churches were raised to the Mother of God than to any other. He had the clergy and the monks taught rhetoric, philosophy and theology. Against the Origenists, whom it reads as reviving the Nestorian heresy, he composed the hymn «Единородный Сыне и Слове Божий», whose singing before the little entrance at the Liturgy has not lapsed since; and in 553 he called the Fifth Council, which this calendar says condemned the teaching of Origen and confirmed Chalcedon, where doxologia had said it condemned the monophysites. His «Новеллы», it adds, stand in every version of the Russian Kormchaya. He fasted much, and died in 565.

The other half of the day's line is his consort [Theodora](/saints/theodora-wife-of-justinian), of one mind with him, numbered among the saints with him and dead in 548. The calendar's life gives her a single sentence at its close, and that sentence is the whole of what the Russian calendar says about her.

The Ohrid Prologue makes a claim about his origin that none of this folder's other calendars does, and it hedges it in its own words: a Slav by descent, probably a Serb, from the neighbourhood of Skopje, whose Slavonic name was Upravda. He succeeded his uncle Justin on the throne. The whole greatness of this emperor, it says, is bound inseparably to his deep Orthodox belief: he believed and he lived by the faith. Through the Great Fast he ate no bread and drank no wine but fed on herbs and drank water, and that only in the daytime.

He went to war against the barbarians of the Danube for one reason only, that they were castrating their captives — which the entry offers as a mark of his high feeling for men. He was fortunate and successful in his wars and in his works alike. He built a great number of beautiful churches, among them the most beautiful of the beautiful, Holy Wisdom in Constantinople. He gathered and published the Roman laws, and issued many strict laws of his own against immorality and dissipation.

He composed the church hymn beginning «Only-begotten Son and Word of God», which began to be sung at the Liturgy from the year 536; and he called the Fifth Ecumenical Council in 553. He died in peace on 14 November 565, in the eightieth year of his life — the year this folder already records, with the day of the month added, which is the day it is kept on.
