# Glycerius the Farmer

Glycerius is a poor farmer in the synaxarion of [George the Trophy-bearer](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer), and saint.gr says so in as many words: «Ο Άγιος Μάρτυς Γλυκέριος αναφέρεται στο Συναξάρι του Μεγαλομάρτυρα Γεωργίου». When George was shut in prison, it says, the fame of his wonders had reached the whole city and the country round, so that every night a crowd filled the prison, giving the gaolers great gifts to see the saint and receive a spirit of power, of joy, of faith and of love.

Among them was the poor Glycerius. He had one ox and no more, and it dropped dead while he was ploughing his field. So he fell at George's knees and begged him for help; and at his sincere confession that he believed in God the saint sent him off, telling him that his ox was alive. When Glycerius found that it was, he came back to thank him, crying out: «Μέγας ο Θεός του Γεωργίου» — great is the God of George.

For that he was arrested, and suffered death by the sword. The page's couplet plays on his trade: thy neck as the earth and the blade as the ploughshare, Glycerius the farmer, I judge fittingly. It gives him no year, no country and no judge's name, and prints no hymn.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the great martyr alone — „Св. вел. муч. Ђорђе.“ — and names the rest in its body. [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) was born in Cappadocia, the son of rich and devout parents; his father suffered for Christ and his mother moved to Palestine. Grown, he went into the army, reached the rank of tribune in his twentieth year, and in that rank was in the service of the emperor Diocletian. When the emperor began his terrible persecution of the Christians, George stepped before him and confessed boldly that he was a Christian too.

The emperor threw him into prison, ordered his feet set in the stocks and a heavy stone laid on his chest; then had him bound to a wheel with boards of great nails set under it and turned on it until his whole body was one wound; then buried him in a ditch with only his head above the earth and left him there three days and three nights; and then had a magician give him a deadly poison. Through all of it George prayed without ceasing, and God healed him on the instant and saved him from death, to the great wonder of the people. And when by his prayer he raised a dead man, many received the faith of Christ.

Among them, the entry says, were the emperor's wife [Alexandra](/saints/alexandra-the-empress), the chief priest of the idols [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-once-a-magician), the farmer Glycerius, and [Valerius](/saints/valerius), [Donatus](/saints/donatus-23-april) and [Therinus](/saints/therinus). At the last the emperor condemned George and his own wife to be beheaded with the sword; the blessed Alexandra gave up her spirit at the place of execution before the sword came to her, and George was beheaded in 303. The wonders at his grave are without number, the entry says, and so are his appearances, in dreams and waking, to those who have remembered him and asked his help from then to this day.

What the entry adds to this folder is nothing but the confirmation of its own English name: he is „земљоделац Гликерије“, Glycerius the farmer, named third among those who received the faith at the wonder of the raising. The entry gives this folder no year of its own. It dates only the great martyr's beheading, „св. Ђорђе би посечен 303. год.“, and the empress's death just before it; of the others it says only that they received the faith, and nothing at all about how or when they died. **No date was added here**, and the folder stands as it was.
