# Glycerius the Farmer

Glycerius is a poor farmer in the synaxarion of [George the Trophy-bearer](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer), and saint.gr says so in as many words: «Ο Άγιος Μάρτυς Γλυκέριος αναφέρεται στο Συναξάρι του Μεγαλομάρτυρα Γεωργίου». When George was shut in prison, it says, the fame of his wonders had reached the whole city and the country round, so that every night a crowd filled the prison, giving the gaolers great gifts to see the saint and receive a spirit of power, of joy, of faith and of love.

Among them was the poor Glycerius. He had one ox and no more, and it dropped dead while he was ploughing his field. So he fell at George's knees and begged him for help; and at his sincere confession that he believed in God the saint sent him off, telling him that his ox was alive. When Glycerius found that it was, he came back to thank him, crying out: «Μέγας ο Θεός του Γεωργίου» — great is the God of George.

For that he was arrested, and suffered death by the sword. The page's couplet plays on his trade: thy neck as the earth and the blade as the ploughshare, Glycerius the farmer, I judge fittingly. It gives him no year, no country and no judge's name, and prints no hymn.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 23 Απριλίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/04/23/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1361/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
