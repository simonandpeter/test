# Andrew Argentis

saint.gr's calendar for 29 Μαΐου names him and gives three sentences. The new martyr Andrew Argentis, it says, came from Chios and suffered a martyr's death at Constantinople in the year 1465, and so is counted among the first of the new martyrs, those put to death by the Turks after the fall of the City. His name is a Chiot family name and not a title.

The account of his martyrdom, the page adds, was written by George of Trebizond. Nothing else is on the page: no age, no trade, no manner of death, no hymn. What it does do is set him under the synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered after the fall of Constantinople, which the same calendar keeps as a heading and which names nobody on his page.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and gives him one sentence: he suffered for the faith at the hands of the Turks in the year 1465, at Constantinople. Both the year and the city are already on this folder from the Greek page, so nothing is changed by it. What is worth noting is the heading: it calls him Andrew of Chios, by his island, where the Greek calendar calls him Argentis, by what that page itself says is a Chiot family name and not a title. The two are the same man said two ways.

The same day’s last entry in this book is the fall of Constantinople, on 29 May 1453, twelve years before his death — which is the event the Greek calendar’s synaxis of the new martyrs after the fall of the City is named for, and under which it sets him.
