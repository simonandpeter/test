# John the Stranger of Siva

John, called the Stranger, is not in the synaxaristae; his life survives in a
manuscript at the village of Siva in the province of Pyrgiotissa in Crete, where he was born. He
was born of devout parents in the days of Constantine VII Porphyrogenitus and came of age at Siva
after Nicephorus Phocas freed Crete in 961. From boyhood he leaned to the monastic life and
became a hermit in the caves of Crete. He went to Mount Raxos, built a church to Saints Eutychius
and Eutychianus and was tonsured there by a devout elder, then withdrew to Myriokephalo, where he
built the monastery of the Theotokos Antiphonitria that stands yet. He travelled much of Crete
building churches and monasteries, and settled at last in the west of the province of Kissamos,
at the village of Akti, where he raised a quiet dwelling and then the great coenobium of Saint
Eustathius. There he made his testament and died in peace. A faithful copy of that testament is
in a codex of the Bodleian Library at Oxford. The Greek calendar keeps him on 20 September.
