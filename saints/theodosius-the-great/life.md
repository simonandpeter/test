# Theodosius the Great

Theodosius was born in 346, the son of the general Theodosius, Count of Africa, who had been distinguished under the emperor Valentinian and was then put to death unjustly on a slander. The son, who was himself noted for his piety and his generalship, withdrew to the family estates in Spain and kept away from every office. When Gratian, emperor of the West, inherited the East as well, he took him on as a colleague; a victory over the Sarmatians won him the highest rank, and Gratian crowned him Augustus of the East at Sirmium on 19 January 379. He was then thirty-three years old.

By an edict of 27 February 380 he fixed the meaning of Orthodoxy in law, allowing the name of Christian only to those who received the First Ecumenical Council, and by a further law he required that all the churches be restored to the Orthodox. He drove the Arian Demophilus from the throne of Constantinople and gave the place to [Gregory the Theologian](/saints/gregory-the-theologian), and in 381 the Second Ecumenical Council met by his care and completed the Creed with its article on the Holy Spirit.

Two things are told of him for his repentance. In 387 he had resolved to punish Antioch with blood, because the city had thrown down the statues raised in his honour and in that of his wife [Placilla](/saints/placilla-the-empress); the empress herself and the bishop of the city, with the monks of the region, begged him to relent, and at Pascha he granted an amnesty instead. In 390, after a riot at Thessalonica in which the commander of the garrison was killed, he ordered the spectators in the hippodrome cut down, and seven thousand were killed before his recall arrived.

For that [Ambrose](/saints/ambrose-of-milan) stood in the door of the cathedral of Milan and shut the emperor out. Theodosius obeyed him humbly, asked pardon with tears, kept the penance the bishop laid on him, and after eight months came into the church like any other man, in a plain tunic with no mark of his office, and communicated at the Nativity. The fruit of that repentance was a law that no one condemned to death should be executed until thirty days had passed from the sentence. He had copied out the whole Gospel in his own hand and read in it daily.

He reigned sixteen years, and the hardships of them broke his health. He fell asleep in peace in 395, and on the fortieth day Ambrose of Milan spoke the funeral oration that set him up as the pattern of an Orthodox ruler. He was buried in the church of the Holy Apostles, beside the monument of [Constantine the Great](/saints/constantine-the-great) and his successors.
