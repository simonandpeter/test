# Sergius (Zverev)

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 7 ноября ст. ст. heads a line of three with his name: «Сщмчч. Сергия , архиеп. Елецкого, Николая пресвитера и мч. Георгия (1937).» — one class, «Сщмчч.», for all three, and one year. The name link behind «Сергия» supplies the calendar's own nominative with the surname and both of his sees, «Святитель Сергий (Зверев), архиепископ Елецкий, Мелитопольский, сщмч.», and he is the only one of the three with a life page behind his place.

In the world he was Зверев Александр Михайлович, born on 4 February 1870 at Petropavlovsk in a priest's family. He finished the seminary, then St Petersburg University, then the Court Singing Chapel with the title of precentor, and in 1899 the Moscow Theological Academy with the degree of candidate of theology. He was ordained priest and served as inspector of a women's diocesan school of the Taurida diocese, then took the monastic habit with the name Sergius and was raised archimandrite.

In 1922 he was consecrated bishop of Sevastopol. **The page then records something against him, and hedges it**: «Есть данные об уклонении ненадолго в обновленческий раскол (в 1924 году); по покаянии принят в лоно Православной Русской Церкви» — there is evidence that he went over for a short time to the Renovationist schism in 1924, and that on repenting he was received back into the Russian Orthodox Church. It is recorded here as the page gives it, hedge and all.

In 1925 he became bishop of Melitopol and entered the temporary Holy Synod that «св. Патриарх Тихон» established in that year; in 1925 and 1926 he administered the Samara diocese for a time, because its own bishop, «Анатолий (Грисюк)», was in prison. In 1926 he was arrested and sentenced to two years of exile, and in 1927 he was raised archbishop. The page adds that there is evidence he stood in hidden opposition to «митрополит Сергий» after the Declaration and did not commemorate his name at the services.

From 1929 to 1935 he was archbishop of Yelets. In 1935 he was arrested «за антисоветскую пропаганду», for anti-Soviet propaganda, and sentenced to five years in the camps. **He was shot on 31 October (13 November new style) 1937 in the Karaganda camp.** He was glorified in 1999 by the Holy Synod of the Ukrainian Orthodox Church, at Melitopol, and numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of August 2000.

**The day the calendar keeps him on is not the day his own life gives for his death.** The line is 7 ноября ст. ст., which is the civil 20 November; the life says he was shot on 31 October old style, the civil 13 November. The difference is a week and not the thirteen days of the style conversion, so it is neither a second feast nor an arithmetic slip that could be read away. The attestation's day is the day page that listed him, as the brief requires, and the death above is the date the life gives; the disagreement is recorded here and nothing is chosen. His name page lists «26 января (Новомуч.)» besides, a synaxis and not a feast.

**Two sees, and only one of them is in the office above.** The line calls him «архиеп. Елецкого» and the name page «архиепископ Елецкий, Мелитопольский»; the life has him bishop of Sevastopol from 1922, of Melitopol from 1925, archbishop from 1927 and archbishop of Yelets from 1929 to 1935. The office records the see the line names him by and the rest are in this life.

He is given no relation to the two others the line names. The line's «Сщмчч.» is plural and sets the three of them together, and the archbishop's own life — the only life the entry has — says he was shot in the Karaganda camp and names neither the presbyter nor the layman, nor anybody else who died with him. A shared line, a shared class and a shared year are not a company unless a page says they died together, so no `related` row is written here in either direction.
