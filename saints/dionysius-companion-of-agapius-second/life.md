# Dionysius, companion of Agapius (the second of the name)

Dionysius was one of eight martyrs whom saint.gr keeps together on 15 Μαρτίου, in a line that names them all: «Άγιος Αγάπιος και των συν αυτώ μάρτυρες: Πλήσιος, Ρωμύλος, Τιμόλαος, Αλέξανδρος, Αλέξανδρος (έτερος), Διονύσιος καί Διονύσιος (έτερος)». He was the second of the two Dionysii of the company, both of them from Tripoli of Phoenicia. The page puts their contest in the persecution of Diocletian against the Church and gives no year of its own.

The eight came from different countries — [Agapius](/saints/agapius-of-caesarea-in-palestine) from Gaza of Palestine, [Timolaus](/saints/timolaus) from the Black Sea, the two Dionysii from Tripoli of Phoenicia, Romylus from Lydda, and [Plesius](/saints/plesius) with the two Alexanders from Egypt — and they were accused of being Christians and brought before Urbanus, the eparch of Caesarea, where they confessed Christ with boldness of speech.

The eparch made vain attempts to entice them and to frighten them, for, as the page has it, the word of God prevailed in the mind of them all: do not fear their fear, neither be troubled. They all died as martyrs by beheading, giving to all Christians who struggle a message of courage and of hope. The distichs the page keeps for them turn on that one end — for the two Alexanders and the two Dionysii it says that the calling, the cutting off and the crown were common to both — and its apolytikion names Agapius as the one who gathered the choir.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same menologion day and tells it differently again. Its heading is „Свети мученик Агапије и седморица с њим“ — Agapius and seven with him — and it says they all suffered at Caesarea in Palestine under the prince Urban, in the time of the emperor Diocletian. All seven except Agapius, it says, were quite young men, and were not yet Christians, nor had they ever been baptised with water; but their baptism was by blood.

One day the seven youths watched Christians being tortured — some on the fire, some on the gallows, some before the beasts — and seeing with what patience they bore it all they took fire with zeal for Christ, bound their own hands behind them, and came so bound before Urban, saying: we too are Christians. His flatteries and his threats were wasted on them. [Agapius](/saints/agapius-of-caesarea-in-palestine), a man of standing in that city who had already suffered much for Christ, joined himself to the youths and kindled in them the faith and the love of the Lord still further. All were beheaded with the sword in 303, which falls inside the reign this folder already records and is not written over it, and passed over to the courts of the King of Heaven.
