# Nikon of the Kyiv Caves

saint.gr's calendar for 23 Μαρτίου keeps the abbot of the Lavra of the Caves at Kyiv. Nikon lived in Russia in the eleventh century, the page says, and was a disciple of [Anthony](/saints/anthony-of-the-caves), whose memory it puts on 10 Ιουλίου; he was a monk of the Lavra of Kyiv and had been honoured by God with the priestly rank. With Anthony's blessing he tonsured, after a canonical testing and a fitting spiritual preparation, those who sought the angelic habit with longing; and he was counted worthy of the great honour of tonsuring with his own hands [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves), the great founder of the monastic life in Russia, whom the page keeps on 3 Μαΐου. About the year 1062 he also tonsured two great figures of the Lavra, the eminent boyar Varlaam and the eunuch governor Ephraim.

For those tonsures he bore a great affliction. The prince Iziaslav was enraged at losing two important men and two useful colleagues from his side, and ordered the one who had dared tonsure them brought before him. He looked at Nikon with great anger and shouted, you, monk, dared make my boyar and my eunuch such as yourself? Yes, I, by the command of my venerable father and the blessing of the heavenly King Jesus Christ, who called them to this road of asceticism — so the saint answered, calmly and boldly. The prince in his fury ordered the new monks back to their former state, and said that otherwise he would destroy the cave.

After that the brethren had to leave the cave and go far off, to a place the prince's anger would not reach. Nikon went to Tmutarakan and found a deserted place near the town, where he settled and gave himself at once to hard ascetic struggles, living in silence, «μόνος μόνῳ τῷ Θεῷ προσευχόμενος», alone praying to God alone. His presence soon became known and his name spread through the whole region. The Orthodox faith was not yet firm there and the monastic life was wholly unknown; little by little the inhabitants began to come near him and to be struck by a life so strange to them, and then, for the Lord's glory, he broke his silence and spoke to them of the true God in Trinity and of the Orthodox faith. All were won by his wisdom, his knowledge and his holiness, repented and glorified the Lord in their life and their works, and some asked him in time to make them monks; and after testing and admonishing them he tonsured them. So were laid the foundations of the later monastery of the Most Holy Theotokos there, which became a great monastic centre, the equal of the monastery of the Caves.

After the death of Rostislav Vladimirovich, prince of Tmutarakan, the inhabitants of the country asked Nikon, in whom they had unbounded trust and devotion, to go to Svyatoslav Yaroslavich, prince of Chernigov, and ask for his son Gleb as their prince. He carried out the errand his flock had laid on him, and coming back with prince Gleb he passed through Kyiv and visited the monastery of the Caves. When the blessed abbot Theodosius saw his old fellow-ascetic after so many years he leapt for joy; the two fell to the ground and made a deep prostration to each other, then embraced in tears and sat down to talk. When Nikon made ready to go, Theodosius broke into sobs again and begged him to stay in the Caves so that they might finish their earthly ascetic journey together. I must go, Nikon said, and set in order what concerns my monastery; then, if it be God's will, I will come back.

And he did. He went to Tmutarakan with prince Gleb, who took the rule of the country, settled his monastery's affairs carefully and then, as he had promised, returned to the Lavra beside Theodosius, submitting to the holy abbot in humility and carrying out all his commands exactly, putting his own will to death; and Theodosius, when he had to be away from the monastery, would leave Nikon as his deputy in the shepherding of the brotherhood. Nikon knew the craft of bookbinding, and often sewed and bound books, while Theodosius sat humbly beside him, abbot though he was, and prepared the sponges he needed for his handiwork; and at those times and at others the two would gather the brethren about them and admonish them with spiritual teachings and ascetic words.

Later, when the known quarrel broke out between the three brother-princes — Iziaslav of Kyiv, Svyatoslav of Chernigov and Vsevolod of Pereyaslavl — Nikon went back to Tmutarakan and lived some years there with the same ascetic zeal. When he heard that the country of Kyiv was at peace he took the road to the Lavra again, and arriving found that Theodosius was no longer alive and that the abbot now was the God-loving Stephen. He resolved to end his earthly life in the house of his repentance, and so he stayed there, visiting Theodosius's grave often and washing it each time with tears of joyful sorrow. The page heads him «(; - 1088)» and says his relic lies incorrupt in the Lavra of the Caves.
