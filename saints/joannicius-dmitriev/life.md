# Joannicius (Dmitriev)

Joannicius was born Ivan Alexeyevich Dmitriev in 1875, a peasant's son and one of five children — at Редкие Дворы in the Moscow province by the calendar's life page, at Заводские Хутора in the Tula province by azbyka's, which hangs a footnote of its own on the province. For three years he walked to a teacher's house in the winter to learn to read and herded cattle in the summer. At twelve he went round the villages with the village tailors as an apprentice and learned the trade for three years; at seventeen he went to Moscow to a tailor's workshop, and when his mother died he took his father and his brothers and sisters onto his own keeping. He was a pious child and as a youth went to church almost daily and read many spiritual books.

Two years he lived with the thought of a monastery, and settled at last on the skete of Optina hermitage. He came there in 1908, lived a fortnight and talked with the superior, who saw the young man's bent and took him in as a novice; he was tonsured with the name Joannicius and ordained hierodeacon in 1915. From 1917 he was steward in the bishop's house at Kaluga. Mobilised into the rear levy in 1918, he served two years, and in 1921 was ordained hieromonk and sent to a village near Kaluga. In 1928 he was made igumen and superior of a monastery at Meshchovsk, with his bishop's blessing to help every aged monk and nun living by it.

The monastery was closed in 1929 and a commune set up in its place; he was made rector of the town's cathedral, and the monks of the closed house gathered round it into something like a community, keeping the full order of services though they now lodged in private rooms. He kept his blessing and helped them, and especially those too old or ill to keep themselves. That a number of monastics had gathered round a town cathedral drew the authorities' attention; they were in the middle of collectivisation, feared both the passive resistance of peasants who would not join a collective farm and the active kind, and counted clergy and monks their first enemies and, being the most educated people, their enemies twice over.

In October 1932 nineteen people were arrested at Meshchovsk, eleven of them monks and nuns. He was taken on 31 October and held in the Bryansk prison, and at his interrogation on 16 November he answered that he did not admit the charge of forming a counter-revolutionary group; that the services were long, that some monastic prayers had been shortened, that the akathists had been read and then sung as they had been before his time, that services were held daily and that he did this out of care for the true Orthodox faith as every priest should; and that there had been no underground monastery at the cathedral, the monastics owning him their confessor but not their head. When the investigators read him the evidence of their witnesses he said he categorically denied the alleged instances and had never anywhere spoken against the authorities' measures, nor heard any of those arrested with him do so.

On 15 March 1933 a troika of the OGPU sent him to the Northern Territory for five years. Back from exile he was made archimandrite and sent to a church at Kaluga, and in the autumn of 1937 he was arrested with the archbishop and a group of the city's clergy. At his interrogation on 28 October he said plainly that he had more than once voiced his discontent with the Soviet power among clergy and faithful, blaming it that churches had closed all over the Union and that it was unjust in its repressions — and then refused to admit counter-revolutionary activity or to name anyone else.

On 19 November 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced him with the archbishop [Augustine (Belyaev)](/saints/augustine-belyaev), the archpriest [John (Speransky)](/saints/john-speransky), the psalmists [Alexis (Gorbachev)](/saints/alexis-gorbachev) and [Apollon (Babichev)](/saints/apollon-babichev), and [Michael (Arefyev)](/saints/michael-arefyev) of a church council, to be shot; they were shot on 23 November 1937 and buried in one unmarked grave. The hieromonk [Seraphim (Gushchin)](/saints/seraphim-gushchin) of the same hermitage, with whom azbyka prints his troparion, was shot that day too. 10 ноября ст. ст. is that civil day. He was glorified in August 2000 by the jubilee council of bishops.
