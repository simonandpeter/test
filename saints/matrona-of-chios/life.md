# Matrona of Chios

Matrona was born about the fourteenth century in the village of Volissos on Chios, to a well-off family and to pious parents named Leon and Anna. From her youth, the Romanian page says, she was drawn to the monastic life. One day she left her parents and went to live in a deserted place, where she found a small convent of nuns; other nuns soon joined her there, and they struggled together for their salvation.

She worked wonders in her lifetime and after it, and was glorified on Chios for the purity and holiness of her life; she was merciful to the poor and had the power to heal the sick. Her akathist was composed by Niketas, metropolitan of Rhodes, and was found in a codex of the year 1455, which records that she had died before that date.

doxologia.ro says she is commemorated on 20 October and also on 15 July, the day on which her head was found, and it is on 15 iulie that the Romanian calendar prints her among the day's saints. The page says no more than this.

The Greek calendar keeps her on 20 October rather than on the Romanian 15 July, and saint.gr tells her story from the beginning. She was named Maria at first, it says, and was born in the village of Volissos on Chios to pious and wealthy parents, Leon and Anna; her six elder sisters married one after another, sought-after brides for their beauty, their upbringing and their dowries, while the youngest gave herself to the study of divine things and to works of charity. Her regular visits to the nuns of the island's women's monasteries made her long for the monastic life, but love of her parents held her at home until they died. She then tried that life beside a pious widow who was living as an ascetic with her two daughters, and afterwards entered the monastic ranks, was tonsured and renamed Matrona. The money from the sale of her property built a most beautiful church at the monastery; and when the woman who had trained her died, the nuns chose Matrona abbess against her will, and under her the sisterhood lived in great abstinence, obedience and piety. Because abbesses were called κυρά, the page adds, she is called Saint Kyra to this day, and the names Κυράτσω and Κερασιά keep her feast.
