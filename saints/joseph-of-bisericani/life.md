# Joseph of Bisericani

The Romanian life calls Joseph the first known hermit of the mountain of Bisericani and the founder of the monastery of that name. Born in a village of the Neamț country, he entered the community of Bistrița monastery young, was tonsured there, and learned fasting and unceasing prayer from the old fathers. Finding too little stillness at Bistrița, he went with his abbot’s blessing to worship at the Lord’s tomb in Jerusalem, and then withdrew into the wilderness of the Jordan valley, where he lived in a cave, first alone and then with disciples, and within a few years was a famous hermit; seventeen hermits gathered round him, the first known Romanian community in the Holy Land.

When the Arabs overran the Holy Places he brought his disciples back to Moldavia, to Bistrița, and so his hermitage moved from the Jordan to the mountain of Bisericani. As the disciples grew in number he built a small church of the Annunciation with cells around it, and gave them the sleepless rule on the model of the Studite monastery at Constantinople: unceasing prayer in church and in the cells, fasting, prostrations, the singing of the psalms, obedience and love.

When the Turks burned their church they set out for Mount Athos, but on the way the Theotokos appeared to them in an oak and asked where they were going; to your garden, they answered, and she told them to turn back, for here too was her garden, and they set up an icon of her in memory of it. The hermitage was later called Bisericani, the life says, meaning of the devout, because the monks prayed there with tears and many were healed by Joseph’s prayer and blessing. The life gives no year. The Romanian calendar keeps him with [Cyriacus of Bisericani](/saints/cyriacus-of-bisericani) in one troparion and kontakion.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 24 April, where the Romanian keeps him on 1 October, and saint.gr's account is the Romanian one told again with two dates that do not fit it. Joseph is the first hesychast of the mountain of Bisericani in Moldavia, it says, and the founder of the monastery there; born in a village of the country of Neamț and a monk from a young age, tonsured at the monastery of Bistrița, and, wanting more stillness, gone with the blessing of the abbot Dometian to the Holy Places — where he venerated the All-Holy Tomb and then withdrew into the wilderness of the Jordan.

His strict ascetic life, his vigils, his prayers and his fasting began to gather a crowd of monks and disciples about him. The new brotherhood was made up of fifteen Romanians and two Greeks, and it followed the elder's ascetic example faithfully: through the week each lived alone in his own cell and ate very little once a day, until sunset, and on Sunday, the day of the Resurrection, they all gathered at the monastery of Gerasimos of the Jordan to be at church and to communicate. On that day they ate together, and the time went by with psalmody and spiritual conversation; and in the evening each went back to his cell for another week of asceticism and silence.

But the Arab raids on the Holy Places forced Joseph and his disciples to take refuge in Moldavia, at the monastery of Bistrița and then on the mountain of Bisericani. Here he built a wooden church and a cell, while his disciples Simon, Methodius, Barnabas, Averkios, Germanos and Pyrrhos settled on the hills round about — and the local names bear witness to their presence to this day: the mountain of Simon, the mountain of Methodius, the mountain of Barnabas. The rules of the ascetic life in the new wrestling-ground were the same as those they had kept on the Jordan: unceasing prayer, silence, confession, vigils, holy communion; and Joseph tried to put into practice the sleepless life of the fathers of the inner watch, after the tradition of the monastery of Studios at Constantinople. He lived on his ascetic mountain more than thirty years, the page says, and fell asleep in peace — in the year 1711, it adds, which does not agree with the fifteenth century it has just given for his birth, and the page does not reconcile the two.
