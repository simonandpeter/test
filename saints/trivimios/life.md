# Trivimios, companion of Nestor

saint.gr names the holy martyrs [Nestor](/saints/nestor-the-martyr-2-march) and Trivimios together on this day, gives his name in two forms — Trivimios, or Trivíminos — and tells the two of them one story. They came of the city of Perge in Pamphylia, the country of the Kibyrrhaiotai, and lived in the years of the impious emperor Decius; and because they were Christians and preached Christ with a spiritual courage they were denounced to the ruler of Perge.

He sent a crowd of soldiers at once, had them seized and brought bound into court, and set all the instruments of torture out in front of them. But the martyrs despised the threats and confessed Christ with a noble bearing, and for that they were stripped and beaten with hard ropes made of oxen's sinews, then hung up and their flesh torn away, and at the last beheaded; and so, the page says, their life being finished, the Lord who pays the wage received their souls with gladness.

The page gives neither of them a year of birth, a relic or a hymn. Its one further remark is about his companion and not about him — that the Nestor of this day is very probably the same as the one it keeps on the twenty-eighth of February, because their biographies coincide — and it makes no such suggestion about Trivimios, whose name stands nowhere else in the corpus. The distich over the pair turns on it: the man who struck Nestor's neck with the sword rubs a like wound into Trivimios.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him with the man who suffered beside him, and keeps the two of them a day earlier than the Greek and the Romanian do: its line for 1 марта ст. ст. is «Мчч. Нестора и Тривимия (III).», and the life page behind it is headed for the pair and repeats that day. What it says agrees with saint.gr on every point this folder already records. The two were of the region of Pamphylia in Asia Minor; during the persecution of the impious emperor Decius, whose reign it gives as 249 to 251, they preached Christ without fear.

When they stood before the pagan court the governor had every kind of instrument of torture laid out in front of them, to frighten them into denying the faith, and to all the threats they answered that nobody would be able to part them from Christ. The judge in his anger had them racked: they were beaten with dry ox sinews, hung on a tree and had their bodies scraped, and they did not stop glorifying the Lord, and were beheaded. The day prints no hymn under either name, and the century on its line, «(III)», is the same third century the reign it names falls in, so the folder’s dates are left as they stood. He suffered with [Nestor](/saints/nestor-the-martyr-2-march).
