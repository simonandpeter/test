# Eucles of Perge

saint.gr keeps nine martyrs together on 1 Αυγούστου and names them all: [Leontius](/saints/leontius-of-perge), [Attus](/saints/attus-of-perge), [Alexander](/saints/alexander-of-perge), [Cindeus](/saints/cindeus-of-perge), [Mnesitheus](/saints/mnesitheus-of-perge), [Cyriacus](/saints/cyriacus-of-perge), [Menaeus](/saints/menaeus-of-perge), [Catunus](/saints/catunus-of-perge) and Eucles. They lived, the page says, in the time of the emperor Diocletian and of the governor Flavian, at Perge in Pamphylia. All of them were farmers by trade except Menaeus, who was a joiner. Christianity was in them from a very early age, and it was kept so after they had grown up.

One evening they decided that they wished to be martyred for Christ, and so they went to the idol temple of Artemis and destroyed all the statues that were there. Straight after that act they were seized by the idolaters, who questioned them and then put them to the tortures. First they burned their sides; then, using iron claws, they tore their flesh; then they took lighted torches and pierced their eyes. After that they shut them in an inhuman prison, without water and without food.

After a little while they brought them out and threw them into a cage with wild beasts, to be torn apart. The beasts, for all their hunger, sat quietly and would not come near the saints. Those who saw that marvellous thing were truly astounded and cried out with one voice, «Μέγας ο Θεός των Χριστιανών» — great is the God of the Christians. And then the wonder happened: at once thunders were heard and lightnings began to fall, and rain, while at the same time a voice was heard calling the saints to heaven; and as soon as they heard it they rejoiced exceedingly.

At that the tyrant was so enraged that he ordered them to be beheaded; and so the martyrdom of the nine saints was completed, and they passed over into the eternal Kingdom. The couplet of the synaxarion says it in two lines: «Ἐκ Παμφυλίας ἐννὰς ἐτμήθη ξίφει, / Μίαν φυλὴν ζητοῦσα, τὴν τῶν Μαρτύρων» — a nine out of Pamphylia was cut down by the sword, seeking one tribe only, the tribe of the martyrs. The page gives no year and prints no hymn.

Of Ευκλής himself the page says nothing beyond his name, his place in the nine and his trade: all of them were farmers, it says, except Menaeus, who was a joiner.
