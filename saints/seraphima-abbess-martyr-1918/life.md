# Seraphima the Abbess

Seraphima — Elizabeth Nikolaevna Sulimova in the world — was born in 1858 at
Ustyuzhna in the Cherepovets district of Novgorod province. In 1874 she
entered the Leushino convent and ten years later was made a novice; on 14
February 1901 she was tonsured with the name Seraphima, in 1902 was made the
convent’s treasurer, and on 2 July 1906 abbess of the Ferapontov monastery —
founded by Ferapont, the friend of Cyril of Belozersk, twenty kilometres
from his monastery, suppressed under Catherine II and made a parish church,
and reopened as a convent only in the new century, when the abbess Taisia
(Solopova) of Leushino invited any of her sisters who wished to go there,
Seraphima among them. She was known for good judgement, gentle with the
sisters and strict when need was, and ordered the house by the monastic
rule. At the end of January 1918 the decree separating the Church from the
state made all church property the state’s; the parish council took an
inventory with the local executive committee on 27 February and resolved on
8 April that no one, private or official, should be admitted to the convent
without its own representatives. When in May the Kirillov executive
committee ordered a fresh inventory of every monastery’s sacristy, the
abbess asked the convent’s priest [John
Ivanov](/saints/john-presbyter-martyr-1918-2-september) to announce the
commission’s coming after the Liturgy on Sunday 6 May; the four
commissioners arrived during the vigil that evening and demanded her — I
have nothing against the inventory, she answered, but I must inform the
chairman of the parish council and its delegates — and agreed the work
should begin in the morning; but before the chairman came the peasants
gathered at the gate, she went out to calm them (the commission will only
copy our own inventory and leave tomorrow), they would not hear of it, and
drove the commissioners out with fists and logs, firing after three of them
and seizing the fourth, whom the priest saved from lynching. On 12 May she
was summoned to Kirillov for questioning and held there, charged with
inciting the peasants; the day before her departure some forty peasants of
two villages had demanded the keys of the stores, gone through them with a
commissar and taken sixteen sacks of oats and fifteen of rye, and the next
day a great crowd looted the convent for two days, going through the cells,
taking flour and rusks, breaking chests, stealing money and utensils and
insulting the sisters with threats to drive them out; on 29 May it was
looted again, a hundred poods of flour taken and the sisters ordered out, so
that eight were left to mind the gardens, the cattle and the guesthouse,
with no bread at all. In the Kirillov prison she met [Barsanuphius
(Lebedev)](/saints/barsanuphius-of-kirillov), brought in on 14 September,
who told the prisoners he did not fear a violent death but did not dare
think the Lord would find him worthy of a martyr’s end.

On Sunday 15 September 1918, about five in the morning, the bishop, the
abbess and four laymen — Nicholas Burlakov, Anatoly Barashkov, Michael
Trubnikov and Philip Maryshev — were led out of the prison toward Goritsy by
a squad of twenty executioners. The early Liturgy was beginning in the
monastery, and the bishop’s request to go in and communicate was refused;
passing the metochion of the Filippo-Irapsky hermitage he wished to cross
himself before the icon of Philip over its door and a guard struck his hand
with a rifle-butt, and when he quickened his pace they mocked him: do not
hurry, you will get to the kingdom of heaven in time. They went by the old
road of Cyril’s day along the shore of Lake Siverskoye, the bishop first in
his klobuk with his staff, the abbess almost beside him, the laymen behind —
he firm in the knowledge that the decisive hour had come, the hour of
release from earthly bonds to be with Christ, she not yet believing they
would be killed without trial and thinking they were being taken to Goritsy
for the steamer; a prayerful quiet and the peace of Christ came down on
their souls, until at the verst-post that read five versts to Goritsy and
two to Kirillov the executioners turned them off the road to the right, and
there was no more doubt. Here is our Golgotha, said the bishop; the abbess
swayed and he held her up — mother, take heart; you are a person of the
Church; we must go to death without fear, as to a wedding feast, with
gladness; the time will come when we shall be envied — and her weakness
passed; one of the condemned began to speak sharply of those who had
condemned them, and the bishop stopped him: after the Saviour’s example we
must forgive everyone everything; we must pass into the other life at peace
with all. They were stood facing Zolotukha hill with their backs to the
monastery, the bishop between Seraphima on his right and Trubnikov on his
left. Every evening after compline the abbess had bowed to the ground before
her sisters and said, forgive me, accursed; now she turned to the killers
and said quietly, forgive me, accursed — they took it that she called them
accursed, and fired and killed her; then five volleys one after another, and
all were dead but the bishop, who stood praying with his hands raised to
heaven, reading the prayer for the departing, and when he had said amen
heard an executioner shout, put your hands down! — I have finished, he said;
you finish too — and turned to the monastery, blessed it, and lowered his
hands, and a shot at point-blank range felled him. Merchants were made to
dig the grave; the bodies were laid in with great care, the bishop nearest
the monastery, then Burlakov, then the abbess, with Barashkov and Maryshev
at their feet. Hearing the shots at dawn, many ran to the place, and the Red
Guards they met mocked them: run, run! yours has risen! in three years he
will turn up as relics! Twice the brethren got leave to dig up the bodies at
night and twice were stopped, once by Red Guards firing in the air — we will
shoot all the Volkovs; what is the executive committee to us! — and once by
a written ban; on the evening of 17 September, behind closed gates, the
retired bishop Misail (Krylov) sang the funeral for the murdered in their
absence, and Bishop Alexis (Simansky) of Tikhvin ordered the bishop’s name
into every synodicon of the Novgorod diocese. The Cherepovets chairman’s
answer to the diocese’s envoy was: allow you to carry the body to the
monastery? never! you will want to make relics of him next! The place was
honoured for many years by the Orthodox who came to pray there; in the 1960s
the authorities, seeing that the veneration did not wane, destroyed every
sign of the grave and built farm buildings on it, and on 17 December 1998 a
cross was raised on the place of execution. With the bishop, the priest
[John Ivanov](/saints/john-presbyter-martyr-1918-2-september) and the
martyrs [John Ivanov](/saints/john-presbyter-martyr-1918-2-september),
[Nicholas Burlakov](/saints/nicholas-martyr-1918-2-september), [Anatoly
Barashkov](/saints/anatoly-martyr-1918), [Michael
Trubnikov](/saints/michael-martyr-1918-2-september), [Philip
Maryshev](/saints/philip-martyr-1918) she was numbered among the new martyrs
and confessors of Russia by the jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000,
for veneration by the whole church; the Russian calendar keeps the seven
together on 2 September of the old style, 15 September. The life is by
Igumen Damascene (Orlovsky).
