# Lawrence of Rome

Lawrence, archdeacon of Rome, suffered in 258 under the emperor Valerian, together with Pope Sixtus, the deacons Felicissimus and Agapitus and the soldier Romanus. [Sixtus](/saints/sixtus-ii-of-rome), an Athenian by birth and well educated, had preached in Spain and been made bishop of Rome after the martyrdom of Pope Stephen, in a time when the bishop of Rome was elected to a certain death; he was soon seized and imprisoned with his two deacons, [Felicissimus](/saints/felicissimus-the-deacon) and [Agapitus](/saints/agapitus-the-deacon).

Lawrence met him as he was led to prison and wept: where are you going, father, without your archdeacon, with whom you always offered the bloodless sacrifice — take your son with you, that I may share with you the shedding of blood for Christ. I am not leaving you, my son, Sixtus answered; I am old and go to an easy death, but heavier sufferings await you; three days after our death you will follow me. Now go, sell the church’s treasures and give to the persecuted and needy Christians. Lawrence did so with zeal.

When Sixtus and the deacons were led to judgement Lawrence came to tell him that the treasure was distributed, and the soldiers, hearing of a treasure, took him into custody; the martyrs were beheaded on 6 August. The emperor shut Lawrence in prison under its keeper, [Hippolytus](/saints/hippolytus-of-rome). There he healed by prayer the sick who gathered to him and baptised many, and Hippolytus, astonished, believed and was baptised with his whole house.

Brought again before the emperor and ordered to show the hidden treasures, Lawrence asked for three days, gathered a multitude of the poor and the sick who lived on the Church’s alms, and said: these are the vessels in which the treasures are laid; whoever puts his treasure in these vessels receives it with increase in the kingdom of heaven. He was beaten with scorpions — thin iron chains with sharp spikes — his wounds scorched with fire, and struck with leaden rods; the soldier [Romanus](/saints/romanus-of-rome), seeing a shining youth beside the martyr wiping his wounds, believed, was baptised by him in prison and beheaded on 9 August.

Led to the last trial, Lawrence told Hippolytus, who wanted to declare himself a Christian and die with him, to keep his confession in his heart a little longer. He was laid on an iron grill over hot coals, and said to the rulers: you have roasted one side of my body, turn me onto the other and eat; and died with the words, I thank you, Lord Jesus Christ, that you have made me worthy to enter your gates. Hippolytus took the body by night, wrapped it in linen with spices, and a vigil and the Divine Liturgy were held over it in the house of the widow Kyriake; it was buried in a cave on 10 August 258. Hippolytus and the others suffered three days later, as Lawrence had foretold.

The Ohrid Prologue tells the same story for the Serbian calendar, adding the healings of Kyriake’s headache and of the blind Crescentio and Lucillus, and closing with verses of the martyr’s exchange with the emperor.

The Greek calendar keeps the three on the same day, 10 August, and saint.gr's one page for them turns on a single scene. It says the archdeacon Lawrence was among the best known clergy of the Roman Church and that the management of its property had been entrusted to him; that when the persecution came, Sixtus the Second, pope of Rome, who came from Athens, offered himself first and was beheaded confessing Christ; and that they then arrested Lawrence and, because he was the Church's steward, ordered him to hand over its treasures.

Lawrence agreed, the page says, and came back a little later with a long column of wagons full of poor people, orphans and the crippled, and answered the governor that the treasures of the Church were laid up in all these sufferers, «ὅπου οὔτε σῆς οὔτε βρῶσις ἀφανίζει, καὶ ὅπου κλέπται οὐ διορύσσουσιν οὐδὲ κλέπτουσιν» — where neither moth nor rust destroys, and where thieves do not break through nor steal, the words of Matthew's sixth chapter. Enraged at it, the idolaters roasted him alive on an iron grid.

saint.gr puts the martyrdom in 253 rather than the 258 the Russian calendar prints above, and gives no year of birth for any of the three. Its own distich for him plays on his name: «Τὸν Λαυρέντιον λαύρακα Χριστοῦ λέγω, / Ἐπ' ἐσχάρας ἄνθραξιν ἐξωπτημένον» — Lawrence I call the sea bass of Christ, grilled through on the coals of the gridiron. A further line fixes the day: «Ὤπτησαν δεκάτῃ Λαυρέντιον ἠΰτε ἰχθύν» — on the tenth they roasted Lawrence like a fish. The page records the greater part of his relics at the Roman church of his name outside the walls, with portions at Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain and at Kykkos in Cyprus.
