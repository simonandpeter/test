# Damian of Asia

The brothers Cosmas and Damian were of the country of Asia, with a pagan father and a Christian mother named [Theodotia](/saints/theodotia-of-asia). After her husband's death she lived as a widow, free and unhindered, and served Christ diligently, giving her whole life to God; she became, the life says, like the widow the Apostle praised, who hopes in God alone and continues in prayers and supplications night and day.

As Theodotia lived, so she taught her beloved sons, feeding them on good teaching in the Christian faith and on the sweetness of the divine Scriptures and guiding them to every good work. Coming to full age, living in the law of the Lord and practised in a blameless life, they became like two lights shining on earth with good works; and for this they received from God the gift of healing, giving health to souls and bodies, curing every kind of illness, healing all infirmities and driving out cunning spirits.

They helped not men only but animals too, and took nothing from anybody for it. They did it not for property, to grow rich with gold and silver, but for God, to show their love for him by their love for their neighbour; nor did they want human glory from these healings, but the glory of God. They delivered men from illness not with herbs but with the name of the Lord, without payment and without waiting for thanks, fulfilling the commandment of him who said: freely you have received, freely give. For this the faithful called them the physicians without payment, the unmercenaries. So their life passed in right belief, and they ended it in peace; and not in their life only but after their death they were glorified by various wonders, for which the Church honours them with a commemoration in the yearly round, as warm helpers and physicians and defenders of our souls and bodies.

Of their good life and their healing without payment the page tells this story. A woman named Palladia, who had lain in pain for many years and had no help from physicians, heard of these saints that they healed every illness and sent asking them to come to her, for she was near death. They listened to the request and came to her house, and at once, according to her faith, she received healing by the coming of the holy physicians and was made whole, glorifying God who had given his servants such a gift. Grateful for that mercy, she wished to repay them with gifts, and they would not take them, because they never took anything from anybody and did not sell the gift they had from God.

So she thought to press at least one of them by entreaty to accept some small return; and taking three eggs she came secretly to Damian and begged him to take them from her in the name of the Holy Trinity. Damian, hearing the name of God in Trinity, took the little gift from her, for the great oath by which she had asked him. When Cosmas learned of it he was much grieved, and when his own departure from this life came near he gave orders that Damian should not be laid beside him when he died, because he had broken the Lord's commandment and taken payment from a woman for a healing.

Cosmas reposed in the Lord, and after some time the hour of Damian's end came also. The people were at a loss where to bury him, for they knew Cosmas's order and did not dare lay Damian beside his brother; and while they stood in perplexity a camel ran up, one that had been possessed before and that the saints had healed, and it spoke with a human voice and said that they should without doubt lay Damian near Cosmas, since he had not taken the three eggs from the woman for payment but for the name of God. So their honourable relics were laid together, in the place called Pheremman.

The life doxologia prints under Damian's name and the life it prints under [Cosmas](/saints/cosmas-of-asia)'s are one text, told of the two brothers together; the three eggs and the camel's word are the only part of it in which they are told apart.

Two of the wonders the page tells after their death belong to the same place. A man of those parts went out at harvest time to reap his field, and weakened by the heat of the sun went under an oak to rest and fell into a heavy sleep with his mouth open; a snake came and went into his mouth and down into his belly. He woke knowing nothing of what had happened, reaped until evening, came home, and after supper lay down; and as he rested the snake began to bite his inward parts. He cried out with the pain, and all ran to him and felt him over and could not tell where the pain came from, until he cried aloud: "Holy physicians Cosmas and Damian, help me!" The saints came at once and put him to sleep so that the snake might go out the way it had come in; and as he slept again with his mouth open the snake was drawn out of him by their prayers and the power of God, and all who were there were terrified at the dreadful wonder, and the man woke perfectly well.

In the same place was another man, named Malchus, who lived near the church of the holy physicians at Pheremman. Meaning to go on a long journey, he brought his wife to the church and said: I am going far off, and I leave you in the keeping of Cosmas and Damian; sit in your house until I send you a sign from me that you will know to be mine, and when God wills I will send that sign and fetch you to me. So he entrusted his wife to the saints and set out. A few days later the devil took the shape of a man she knew, came to her and showed her such a sign as her husband had spoken of, and bade her come to him, saying that her husband had sent him to bring her.

She said that she knew the sign but would not go, being entrusted to the unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian; and if he wished her to come with him to her husband, let him come with her into the saints' church and swear to her before the altar that he would do her no harm on the road. The devil promised, went with her into the church, and swore before the altar: so I swear by the power of Cosmas and Damian, that I will do you no harm on the road but will bring you to your husband. Hearing the oath she believed the lying devil, who had appeared in the shape of a man she knew, and set out with him.

The deceiver took her into a desert and untrodden place, meaning to harm her and to kill her. Seeing herself in the last extremity she lifted her eyes to heaven and cried out of her heart to God: God, help me by the prayers of the holy Cosmas and Damian, and make haste to deliver me out of the hands of this murderer. At once the swift helpers appeared, the unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian, crying out against the devil; and he, seeing them, left the woman and fled, and running to a high ravine fell into the gulf and perished.

The saints took the woman and brought her to her house; and she bowed to them and thanked them for delivering her from a terrible destruction, and asked who they were, that she might know whom to thank to the end of her life. They answered: we are Cosmas and Damian, the servants of Christ, to whom your husband entrusted you when he went on his journey; and therefore we hastened to your help and delivered you from the devil by the grace of God. Hearing this she fell to the ground for fear and joy, and they became invisible. Then she cried out, praising and thanking God and his servants, and ran weeping to the church and fell before their icon and told everybody what had happened; and she prayed, thanking the God of Abraham, of Isaac and of Jacob, who quenched the fiery furnace for the three young men and helped his servant Thecla in the arena, for delivering her too, a sinner, from the chains of the devil by his servants Cosmas and Damian.

The Greek calendar keeps the two brothers on the same day, 1 November, and tells no story at all. saint.gr's page for them is relics, two epigrams and five hymns, and not one line of life: it is the hymns that say who they are, and one of them says it plainly — «Ἐκ τῆς Ἀσίας ὥσπερ δύο ἀστέρες, ἐξανατείλαντες Ἀνάργυροι θεῖοι», as two stars risen out of Asia, divine unmercenaries. The epigram set over them turns their absence round: though the two unmercenaries have left the earth, they fill the earth with wonders again, as before. Of his relics in particular the page says that fragments are at the monasteries of Dionysiou and Pantokrator on the Holy Mountain and in the Roman Catholic church of his name at Rome. It gives neither brother a year.

The Russian church calendar keeps the two brothers and their mother on 1 November, under the typikon's six-stichera sign, and the long life it serves tells more of them than either of the other calendars does. It opens by refusing to date them: neither the time of their birth nor the time of their death is known, and only this is certain, that they lived no later than the fourth century — argued from the churches raised in their name in the first half of the fifth century, and from the Copts, who separated after Chalcedon and who hold the brothers saints while receiving no saint of later date.

Their father was a Greek and a pagan and died while they were small. The brothers studied Scripture and medicine together, and healed for nothing, asking only that those they cured should believe firmly in Christ and live holily in him, and working to turn to the faith any who were not yet Christians. The life says they did not forget the beasts either, but went through houses, deserts and woods looking for sick animals, which afterwards followed them in herds; and that once in a deserted place they found a camel half dead and healed it and let it go.

They never parted and never travelled alone, because the one vow they kept all their lives was to take nothing from anyone, and each wished to be sure the other could not be given something in secret. It is in this calendar's life that the vow is broken, and he is the one who breaks it: a woman they had cured of a long illness bound Damian by the name of God to accept three eggs in the name of the Trinity, and he yielded to the oath.

Cosmas grieved when he heard of it and left a testament that his brother's body should not be laid with his own, and the Lord appeared to him that night and said the eggs had been taken not for payment but for the oath sworn in his name. Cosmas was comforted and told nobody, and died first, in peace; Damian followed him some time after, both in middle age. When the people stood over Damian's body not knowing where to lay it, the camel the brothers had healed came up and spoke, and asked them not to separate the two, and they were buried in one reliquary at a place called Phereman, since destroyed.

The day page carries their troparion and their kontakion in Slavonic, and this folder had neither: the troparion is the one it already held in Romanian, and the kontakion is not the Greek one.
