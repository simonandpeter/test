# Arsenie of Prislop

Arsenie was born on 29 September 1910 at Vața de Sus in Hunedoara. He finished the Avram Iancu Orthodox national lyceum at Brad at the head of his year in 1929, and in the same year entered the theological academy at Sibiu, which he completed in 1933. On the recommendation of his professor Nicolae Popovici he was given a scholarship by Nicolae Bălan, Metropolitan of Ardeal, to study at the Institute of Fine Arts in Bucharest; alongside it he sat in on the medical lectures of Francisc Rainer and on Nichifor Crainic's lectures in Christian mysticism.

Taken with the Ladder of Divine Ascent of Saint [John of the Ladder](/saints/john-of-the-ladder), he translated it into Romanian in five months. In 1935 he was set reader and subdeacon, and on 11 September 1936 Metropolitan Nicolae Bălan ordained him a celibate deacon. In 1939 he spent three months on Mount Athos, where he was formed in the monastic life he had chosen while still a student at Sibiu, and from which he brought back the manuscripts that lie behind the Romanian Philokalia.

He was received into the monastery of Sâmbăta de Sus in Brașov county and tonsured a monk in 1940, on the Friday of the Life-giving Spring; in 1942 he was ordained priest and made abbot of the Brâncoveanu monastery, which he rebuilt. After the Soviet army occupied the country he was arrested for the first time at Râmnicu Vâlcea on 17 July 1945 and released on the 30th, found innocent. On 14 May 1948 he was arrested again for having helped the anti-communist fighters of the Făgăraș mountains with food, and was tortured for six weeks and made to give statement after statement before being let go.

In November 1948 Metropolitan Nicolae Bălan moved him from Sâmbăta to the monastery of Prislop, where he was abbot and, once the house became a convent, its spiritual father, with breaks for the periods of arrest and interrogation. On 15 January 1950 he was arrested a third time and held in "administrative" detention at the Canal until 23 March 1951; his release was owed to Patriarch Justinian Marina, who pointed out to the minister Teohari Georgescu the danger of a revolt among the people of Făgăraș.

He was questioned again at Pentecost 1953, and held at Timișoara, Jilava and Oradea for six months from 5 October 1955 until April 1956. In 1959 financial irregularities were staged against him so that he could be put out of monasticism and forbidden the altar; the decision was reversed in 1998, after his death. There followed the years of wandering in Bucharest, where he was kept always at the margin; he worked as a church painter until he retired in 1968, and remained under the watch of the Securitate.

After 1984 he withdrew to the monastery at Sinaia, and on 28 November 1989 he departed to the Lord, seventy-nine years old. He was buried, as he had wished, at the monastery of Prislop on 4 December 1989, and his grave there is today one of the great places of pilgrimage in the country.
