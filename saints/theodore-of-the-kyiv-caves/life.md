# Theodore of the Kyiv Caves

Theodore and [Basil](/saints/basil-of-the-kyiv-caves), monk-martyrs of the Kyiv Caves, lived in the eleventh century in the Near Caves. Theodore gave his wealth to the poor, withdrew to the monastery and settled in the Varangian cave next to the caves of Theodosius, and spent many years there in strict abstinence. When the enemy sowed in him grief for the riches he had given away, Basil comforted him: I beg you, brother Theodore, do not lose your reward; if you want possessions, take all that I have. Theodore repented and came to love Basil with all his heart, and they lived in one cell.

Once, while Basil was three months on an obedience outside the monastery, a demon in his likeness came to Theodore and showed him treasure that robbers had long ago hidden in the cave; the monk was ready to leave the monastery, buy an estate and live in the world, but when Basil came back the deception was unmasked. From then on Theodore watched himself more closely, set up a millstone and ground grain at night so that idleness should not feed his thoughts, and by long labour freed himself from the passion of avarice.

Rumour reached Prince Mstislav Svyatopolkovich that the monk Theodore had found great treasure in the cave. He summoned him and ordered him to show where it was; Theodore answered that he had indeed seen much gold and vessels, but for fear of temptation he and Basil had buried them, and God had taken from him the memory of the place. The prince did not believe him and had him tortured to death — beaten until his hair shirt was soaked with blood, then hung head downwards over a fire.

Drunk, the prince ordered Basil tortured too, and killed him himself with an arrow; dying, Basil threw the arrow at the prince’s feet and foretold that he would soon be mortally wounded by it — and on 15 July 1099, on the wall of the fortress of Volodymyr, Mstislav was struck in the chest through a gap in the planking, and recognising his own arrow said, I die for the monk-martyrs Basil and Theodore. The Prologue dates their death to 1098, from the violence of the avaricious prince, and finds Theodore’s story especially instructive for the covetous.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them on the same day, 11 August, and adds nothing to the life. Its line is «Άγιοι Βασίλειος και Θεόδωρος οι Οσιομάρτυρες εν τω Σπηλαίω» — the holy Basil and Theodore, venerable martyrs, in the Cave — and the one page behind it gives a head line to each of them and then a single sentence: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο των Ρώσων Αγίων.» We have no details of the life of the Russian saints. The same Greek day carries a second and separate entry for a Θεοδόσιος of the Cave, who was a prince before his tonsure; that is a different man with a different page.
