# Musa of Rome

The Russian calendar's whole notice of her is a single paragraph, and the paragraph is a dream. She lived in the fifth century, and what the page says is known of her is that she was of good conduct. The story is not told at first hand: Григорий Двоеслов, the pope of Rome, told it to his archdeacon Пётр as he had heard it from Пров, who was her brother. The calendar gives her no year of birth, no year of death and no place beyond the epithet Римляныня in the name it prints for her.

In a dream the Mother of God appeared to the girl, surrounded by young maidens, and asked her whether she wished to follow her and to live with them. I do, the child answered. Then do nothing unseemly, she was told; keep away from laughter and from children's games, and on the thirtieth day I shall come for you and you shall be with us. From that time the girl grew grave and prayed steadily and with her mind collected; and when her parents, astonished at the change in her, questioned her, she told them the vision.

On the twenty-fifth day she fell ill, and on the thirtieth she saw the Mother of God again. «Иду, иду, Госпожа моя» — I am coming, I am coming, my Lady — and with those words, the page says, the blessed girl departed to God.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 16 мая ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260516.html) and [the life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life1080.htm); read 8 October 2026.*
