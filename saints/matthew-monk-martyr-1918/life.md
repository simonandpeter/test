# Matthew

Mikhail Vladimirovich Pomerantsev was born about 1881 in the village of Tolmachevo in the Bryansk district of Orel province; he finished the second Orel church school in 1896 and the Orel seminary in 1902, both in the first rank, and in 1906 the Kyiv Academy as candidate of theology. He taught at the Orel seminary and the diocesan women’s school, was assistant superintendent of the Kamyshin school in Saratov province, taught at the Olonets seminary, and in 1911 was tonsured and ordained hieromonk in Kyiv with the name Matthew, taking charge that December of the parish schools at the Kyiv Caves Lavra; then Ostashkov in Tver province, assistant superintendent from 1914 and superintendent from 1916; and on 8 February 1917 he was made rector of the Perm seminary and archimandrite.

Elected to the Church Council of 1917–18 by the monastics, he was called, the members said, the Council’s favourite: he went to Kyiv to investigate the murder of Metropolitan Vladimir and reported on it, sat on the commission on the persecution of the Church, and was the author and speaker of its inquiry into “so-called Bolshevism in the Church”.

In August 1918 he asked leave for Perm and offered to join the commission investigating the arrest of Archbishop Andronik if that were thought useful; Patriarch Tikhon said that Archbishop [Basil](/saints/basil-of-chernigov), who had already gone, thought his presence valuable. The Soviet government had consented to the inquiry and even given the commission its own carriage.

On 27 August 1918, on the way back between Perm and Vyatka, beyond the bridge over the Kama, Red Army men burst into the carriage where the Council’s delegation travelled under Archbishop Basil, took its members off the train and shot them; the materials of the inquiry and the money collected for the Council were stolen. The local peasants found the bodies and buried them; when a pilgrimage began, the authorities dug them up and burned them. He was glorified at the Jubilee Council of 2000. The Sretensky calendar prints no life of his own; this one follows azbyka.ru.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 4 June, with the archbishop he died beside, and not on the Julian 14 August the Russian entry gives. saint.gr's line for him reads «Άγιος Ματθαίος ο Αρχιμανδρίτης Διευθυντής της Σχολής της Περμίας», with the date «(; - 1919)» — a year later than the Russian source's 1918 — and it names his office, which the Russian entry does not: he was the archimandrite in charge of the school of Perm.

Of the killing it says that in 1919, between the cities of Perm and Vyatka, the archbishop Basil, the archimandrite Matthew, director of the school of Perm, and one layman were murdered by Bolsheviks and their bodies thrown into the water from the Kama bridge; that Christians took their honourable relics and buried them secretly; that the place of their burial soon became a pilgrimage and a place of sanctification and of wonders; and that for that reason the revolutionaries destroyed the monument over the grave and burned the relics.
