# John Arnaoutogiannis

John was a soldier of Albanian descent — saint.gr explains the by-name it gives him, «Αρναουτογιάννης», by saying that the Albanians are called Arnaut in Turkish — and he was baptised a Christian with the name John. He made his home in the village of Agios Ioannis of Phaistos in Crete, and lived a pious and reverent life, keeping himself by the trade of a δραγάτης, a guard of the fields.

Some rebels killed two Turkish Gypsies. The Ottomans of the district, who hated John because he had renounced their religion, took the chance to be revenged on him: they denounced him to the civil authority as the murderer of the two, and charged him besides with having conceived, since his change of religion, a wish to destroy Muslims. He was arrested and sent to Heraklion for trial, and there his innocence was proved.

The judge Rechit Efendi then asked him to change his religion back, so that it might appear that he had not killed out of religious enmity, and said that otherwise he would condemn him to death. John confessed Christ with courage. For three days he was tortured horribly; among other things they heated an iron vessel red and set it on his head. When he gave up his soul to God the Pasha ordered that his body be given to the Christians for burial, and they buried him at the Spitalia in Heraklion.

saint.gr adds that his death spread grief and anger through the Christians of Heraklion — Stephanos Nikolaidis is quoted for «φοβερά στάσις των Χριστιανών εναντίον της Κυβερνήσεως», a terrible rising of the Christians against the government — and that to satisfy the people the president of the court was exiled and replaced. Some time later his relics were taken up and found hallowed; the Russian consul took them and sent them to Kiev, where they are kept. The page credits the finding of the saint to the long research of Chrysostomos Papadakis of the Metropolis of Gortyna, and the writing of his service to the hymnographer Charalampis Bousias.
