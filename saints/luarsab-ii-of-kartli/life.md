# Luarsab II of Kartli

days.pravoslavie.ru’s calendar for 21 июня ст. ст. names two Georgian kings on one line, «Мчч. Арчила II, царя Иверского (744), и Луарсаба II, царя Карталинского (1622) (Груз.).», marks the entry «(Груз.)», and gives each of them a life page of his own. He was born in 1587, his page says, the son of a king of Georgia poisoned by the Persian shah. After his father’s death he was left with two sisters, Хорешан and Елена; he was still a boy, but was marked by sense and piety, and young as he was he was crowned king of Kartli.

In 1609 a Turkish army invaded under «Дели-Мамад-хан», and the young king gave it battle at a village between Гори and Сурами. On the eve of the fight the fourteen thousand of the Georgian levy spent the whole night in unsleeping prayer, and in the morning, after the divine liturgy and the communion of all of them, they put sixty thousand of the enemy to flight.

The shah, alarmed at that victory and eaten with envy, looked everywhere for a way to destroy him. To save central Georgia from ruin the king was forced to give his sister Елена in marriage to the shah, a Muslim, at his demand; and even that did not stop him. The shah invaded with a great army, and through the treachery of several of the nobles the king and the king of Kakheti had to withdraw at the end of 1615 into western Georgia. The shah laid Kakheti waste, threatened Kartli with the same, and demanded that the king come to him, promising peace if he did; and the king went, trying to keep the churches of Kartli from destruction, with the words that he would put all his hope in Christ, and whatever awaited him there, life or death, blessed be the Lord God.

He was received peaceably and the shah seemed ready to keep his promises. But after a hunt together the shah invited him to Мазандаран, and at dinner the king refused to eat fish, because it was the Great Fast, for all the shah’s urging and demanding. The shah, angered, began to press him to accept Islam, promising to send him back to Kartli with great treasures if he did and threatening a death of torments if he did not; and the king, who had kept strict fasts from childhood and prayed continually, rejected it without hesitation.

He was then bound and shut up in an unassailable fortress near Shiraz. A bishop whose account the page cites relates that he was seven years in that prison in irons, bearing dreadful oppression and frequent beatings, and pressed all the while to become a Muslim; but he stayed faithful to the holy Church of Christ and died a martyr in 1622, in the thirty-fifth year of his life. Two faithful servants were put to death with him. The bodies were thrown down in the prison by night without burial, and the next day the Christians laid them in the ground in a common grave.
