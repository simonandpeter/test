# Michael (Bukrinsky)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him last of five presbyters on the one line it gives to fourteen people of 1938 for the Julian 1 March. Michael Alexeyevich Bukrinsky was born on 19 November 1869 in the village of Bulygino in the Zaraysk district of the province of Ryazan, the son of the psalmist Alexy Bukrinsky. He finished the Ryazan theological seminary in 1890 and in 1894 was ordained priest to the Trinity church in the village of Zimenki in the Zaraysk district.

He served in that one church for forty-four years, and his whole life was bound up with that parish. He had seen out of this passing life the older people among whom his service had once begun, and a new generation of his parishioners had been born and grown up under his eyes. In 1929 he was set an agricultural delivery to the state that he could not possibly meet, and sentenced for not meeting it to three years’ imprisonment; he protested the sentence in court and it was commuted to a fine. He was fined again in 1930 and in 1931, and then sentenced again to three years’ exile, and that sentence was quashed.

Gathering material against him in February 1938, the officers of the НКВД questioned the chairman of the village soviet and the head of the village school. They said that in 1934 he had called on the people to stand together in the district town against the closing of the church — «давайте действовать дружней, чтобы нам не лишиться храма Божьего» — and that in July 1936 he had called on them to leave the field work and go out to the field and serve a moleben for rain. Some of the collective farmers did not go to work but gathered by the church for it.

The chairman summoned him and warned him not to do it again, and he answered, by the chairman’s own account, that the draft of the new constitution had a clause in it on the free and unhindered profession of faith by every citizen, and that «вы на местах искажаете законы и обманываете массу» — you here twist the laws and deceive the people; he would tell the faithful that they were not being allowed to keep the rites of their religion, he would not obey the soviet, and he would do as a pastor ought.

He was arrested on 2 March 1938 and questioned the same day. He denied conducting any activity against the Soviet power and denied ever having spoken his mind, and then said what he thought: that he held the Stalin constitution to be a docked thing, because one thing was written in it and another done — freedom of religion written down, and the Bolsheviks closing the churches, forbidding prayer, arresting and exiling the priests, and so forbidding religion by force against the will of the people. He said he had never called on anyone to demand the church be opened, having thought such demands pointless, and that when the faithful asked him to petition he had refused and told them to petition themselves.

On 7 March 1938 the troika of the НКВД sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on 14 March 1938 and buried in a common unmarked grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow. azbyka.ru keeps him on 14 марта and on two moveable days besides, the synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered at Butovo and the synaxis of the saints of Moscow, and neither is a fixed day nor an offset from Pascha this corpus can hold. The day’s line calls the five of them «пресвитеров» together and azbyka.ru heads him «пресвитер», while the name link behind his name reads «иерей», a priest, which is what the office here records.
