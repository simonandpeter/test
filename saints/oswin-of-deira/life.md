# Oswin of Deira

saint.gr says that Oswin was king of the region of Deira in northern England, and that he was martyred at Gilling in Yorkshire on 20 August in the year 651. In 633, when his father, king Osric, was killed by the pagan Welsh king Cadwallon, he took refuge in Wessex, and there he was baptised and educated by [Aidan](/saints/aidan-of-lindisfarne).

[Bede](/saints/bede-of-jarrow) informs us, the page says, that Oswin was handsome in appearance, pleasant in speech, gentle in his manners and generous, and that he soon won the love of the people. He reigned successfully for about nine years, until king Oswy declared war on him.

Rather than provoke a bloody battle of doubtful outcome, since his army was far the smaller, Oswin decided to escape, and hid in the house of his best friend, the earl Hunwald, at Gilling. But Hunwald betrayed him, and so Oswin was murdered. He was buried at Tynemouth, where he was venerated as a martyr.

King Oswy, when he realised his crime, built a monastery at Gilling to make amends, but Oswin's relics stayed at Tynemouth. When the barbarian raids of the Vikings began, the page ends, the saint's grave was forgotten, until it was found again in the year 1065.
