# Elias (3 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 3 Νοεμβρίου names «Όσιος Ηλίας», and the page behind that line is four words long: «Ο Όσιος Ηλίας απεβίωσε ειρηνικά» — the venerable Elias died peacefully. There is no city, no monastery, no century, no account of anything he did, no relics and no hymn. The Greek calendar keeps him, calls him venerable, says he died in peace, and stops.

The only other thing printed under his name is the epigram, which leans on the prophet he is named after rather than on him: having gone up into the chariot of the virtues, Elias came to the vault of heaven, like that other Elias. The page does not say he was a monk, where he lived, or whom he was taught by.

This folder takes its name from the day, because the calendar gives it nothing else to take a name from, and because the corpus already keeps fifteen men of this name on other days. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands, bare as it is.

The Ohrid Prologue gives him the life the Greek page does not, and it is seven sentences long. He struggled near Antinoe, the chief city of the Thebaid — the book adds its own modern gloss, that this is Sheikh Ibada today, near the town of Mallawi in Egypt. He lived seventy years in harsh and inaccessible rocks of the desert. He fed on nothing but bread and dates, and in his youth he fasted whole weeks together. He healed every torment and every infirmity in men. He trembled all over from old age. He reposed in the hundred and tenth year of his life and passed over into the joy of his Lord.

The entry closes on a saying of his own rather than on a hymn, and the Prologue prints no hymn for him at all: guard your mind from evil thoughts about your neighbours, knowing that the demons throw them in on purpose, to draw the mind away from the sight of its own sins and from its striving towards God. Neither this book nor the Greek page gives any year, any emperor or any century, so this folder stands undated still; the two numbers it does give are the seventy years in the desert and the hundred and ten of his life.
