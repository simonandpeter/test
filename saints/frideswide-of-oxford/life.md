# Frideswide of Oxford

saint.gr's calendar for 19 October keeps «Αγία Φριδεσγουΐδη», and prints her name in Latin letters beside the Greek: Frideswide, an English princess and abbess, whom the head line dates 650 to 727. She was the daughter of Dida of Eynsham, under-king of Mercia, and with his help she built a community of nuns.

Algar, a king of Mercia, asked for her in marriage; Frideswide refused, and Algar tried to carry her off. To escape him she hid in a wood outside Oxford. The king followed her, but outside the gates of the city of Oxford he fell from his horse and broke his neck. Another time the nuns of the community at Binsey complained that they had to carry water from the Thames and that the distance was great; Frideswide prayed, and a spring welled up. The spring has healing properties, the page says, and is there still at the church of Saint Margaret at Binsey, a few miles from Oxford.

She remained abbess at her monastery at Oxford until her repose in 727 — or in 735, according to some other sources, as the page allows — and was buried there. Frideswide is the patron saint of Oxford and of its University, and the calendar records that her relics are in the church of Christ Church at Oxford in Britain. It prints no hymn for her.
