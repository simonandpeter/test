# Callinicus of Caesarea

doxologia.ro prints one life for the martyrs of 14 decembrie, and Callinicus comes into it near its end. He was a servant of the idols at Caesarea in Bithynia, a man of understanding and of good family whom the Greeks honoured as they would a god; and he had watched from the first the wonders done through [Thyrsus](/saints/thyrsus-of-caesarea), until he began little by little to know the weakness of his own gods, and prayed inwardly to be received as a newly chosen soldier and strengthened against those who fought the truth.

The governor was lying sick. Callinicus came to him and mocked him with a straight face: this man, he said, badly beaten as he is, has thrown great Zeus to the ground and broken him, and has now shattered Apollo the sun-bearer three times over, and has laid Heracles flat — not with hands, not with weapons, not with a sword, but with a word and the calling on Christ. So let us go, he said, and raise up the god Heracles, who helps others in their troubles, and beg him to remember his old courage and come to the help of his father Zeus and of Apollo, for those two seem to me to be held fast in a heavy sleep.

The governor took none of it and told him to go alone and pray the gods against the sorcerer Thyrsus. Callinicus answered that the power of the God who had thrown them down looked great to him, and that he was afraid they could not help even themselves. At that the governor saw through him; and Callinicus, not willing to keep the right faith hidden any longer, ran to his own house, shaved the hair of his head and his beard, stripped off his clothes, carried them to the governor and threw them at his feet: take my hair and my clothes, which the sacrifices and the smoke and the spilt blood and the devils' mysteries have defiled, and know from now on that I am a Christian.

Asked in astonishment how the signs of Thyrsus could have turned so well-born a servant of the gods, who had been honoured by them with many gifts, he said that the cause of his change was Heracles himself, who after all the victories told of him could not stand before the word of one man. Then he offered to go with the governor to great Asclepius and pray with him, that his pains might be eased and he made well, so that it should be seen he was under no spell. In the temple he prayed to Christ instead, and reproached the idol, and it fell at his feet as though thrown down by a strong hand.

The governor had him shut in prison, and the next day gave sentence on both men: Callinicus to be destroyed by the sword, for falling from the service of the idols and joining what he called the Christian deceit; Thyrsus, who boasted in many signs and had deceived Callinicus too, to be laid in a wooden coffin and sawn through. Callinicus was led out of the town at once by the soldiers, asked for time to pray, prayed at length, and was made perfect by the sword. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mucenic Calinic» and gives no year; the life sets him under Decius, in the persecution that had begun at Caesarea with the beheading of [Leucius](/saints/leucius-of-caesarea).

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them on 14 December as well, and saint.gr adds the magistrate's name and the order of the deaths. It has them living in the middle of the third century, of Bithynian stock but living at Caesarea, all of them of distinguished families, humble in character and loving God «ἐν ἀγάπῃ ἀνυποκρίτῳ» — with a love that is real and free of hypocrisy — so that they kept clear of the noise of publicity and were nonetheless known to everybody for a life full of almsgiving.

The eparch Koumbrikios, a fanatical idolater, seeing the Christians prosper, tried every way to hold them down; and it was this that moved [Leucius](/saints/leucius-of-caesarea) to go to him and beg him to be more moderate towards the Christians. Koumbrikios had him arrested the moment he heard it, condemned him to death, and in the morning beheaded him outside the city. The thing grieved the Christians, but it lit the flame of their faith in Christ the more; and two days later another Christian, [Thyrsus](/saints/thyrsus-of-caesarea), went to the eparch and told him boldly that idolatry is a delusion and that the final victor will be Christ.

Among those who heard Thyrsus's wise words was a priest of the idols, Callinicus. His soul was lit, and he declared that he was a Christian; and the eparch beheaded Callinicus on the spot and had Thyrsus sawn apart. The Greek page gives this man a second name as it opens, calling him «κατ' άλλους Κορωνάτος», Coronatus by others — the name under which [Coronatus](/saints/cornutus-of-iconium) stands in this corpus as a fourth member of the same company.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the three of them on the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars already keep him on, and it is the only one of them to say what he was before his conversion: the idol-priest of the three. Where [Thyrsus](/saints/thyrsus-of-caesarea) and [Leucius](/saints/leucius-of-caesarea) were honourable citizens of Caesarea in Bithynia, he served the idols.

What turned him was a collapse. Thyrsus, taken a second time and tortured, bore the torments as though in sleep and not awake, and at the power of his prayer many of the idols fell; and seeing that, the idol-priest turned to the faith of Christ. The two of them were condemned to death together. He was beheaded with the sword, while the saw sent against Thyrsus in his wooden chest could not so much as scratch the wood.
