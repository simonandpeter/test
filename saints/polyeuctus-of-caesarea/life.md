# Polyeuctus of Caesarea

saint.gr's calendar for 19 December keeps two martyrs together, Timothy and Polyeuctus, and says that they died after being thrown into the fire, and why: because they were catechising and baptising idolaters. Polyeuctus died at Caesarea, the page says, and [Timothy](/saints/timothy-19-december) in Africa; its epigram for him asks what his end was and answers in his own voice, I went under the fire and found the end I had prayed for.

Although their own people were present at the execution and were weeping, the page says, the two of them were undaunted by divine grace and comforted them; and they went into the flames keeping their good courage and chanting hymns to the Lord. The page then corrects other books in its own voice: certain synaxaristes wrongly put a second Saint Polyeuctus on this day, who is however the same man as the one above.

That is the whole of the entry. It gives no year and no reign, names no judge, lists no relic and prints no hymn for either of them.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 декабря ст. ст., the same menologion day as the other calendars here, and its line is «Мчч. Полиевкта и Тимофея диакона (IV).» — the martyrs Polyeuctus and Timothy the deacon, fourth century. That century is the only date any source in this folder gives, and it is set down here and not written into the folder’s dates, a bare century put after two names in a calendar line being an era marker rather than a year this corpus mints from. The calendar’s own name page says which Caesarea it means: «Полиевкт Кесарийский (Каппадокийский)», Caesarea in Cappadocia. No life page stands behind the entry for either man, and the day page prints no hymn under either name.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 19 Δεκεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/12/19/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3304/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 19 декабря ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261219.html), read 13 October 2026.*
