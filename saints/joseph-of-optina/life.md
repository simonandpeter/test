# Joseph of Optina

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 9 mai reads «Sfântul Iosif de la Optina», and his page on the site carries no Viață tab: what it holds are icons, photographs, places of pilgrimage, articles, prayers and akathists of the Optina elders as a body — the Akathist of the Elders of Optina, the daily prayer of the holy Elders of Optina, a prayer to the holy Elders of the monastery of Optina — and an icon of the synaxis of the elders. No year of his birth or of his death is printed, and nothing is said there about his life.

What the page does print in his own name is one saying, set under his picture: «Dacă nu vom răbda necazurile, şi chiar dacă le vom răbda, dar cu cârtire, cu mânie, cu supărare şi cu ceartă, ce răbdare este aceasta? Este numai înrădăcinarea în noi a răutăţii, a mâniei, iar de aici vine mândria.» — if we do not bear our troubles, and even if we bear them but with murmuring, with anger, with vexation and with quarrelling, what patience is that? It is only the rooting of malice and of anger in us, and from there comes pride.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and its page tells his life at length. In the world he was Ivan Evfimovich Litovkin, born on the second of November 1837 in the village of Gorodishche in the district of Starobelsk in the province of Kharkov, of pious and God-loving parents, Euthymius Emilianovich and Maria Vasilievna Litovkin. They had six children — Ivan, Peter, Simeon, Alexandra, Anna and one daughter more — and his sister Alexandra, who later became a nun with the name Leonida, used to tell how the little Ivan was a very affectionate and sensitive child, sharing in the griefs of others with his tender soul, though his inborn modesty and shyness often would not let him speak. When he was four he lost his father, and a little after that his mother, in the cholera that fell on the village.

His love for the monastic life led his steps to Optina. After three years, on the fifteenth of April 1872, he was given the monastic habit with the name John, and on the sixteenth of July of the same year he was tonsured and received the name Joseph; in 1877 he was ordained deacon, quite unexpectedly and in a way that showed plainly that the hand of God was guiding him, and in October 1884 he received the grace of the priesthood from Vladimir, Bishop of Kaluga. From the first day of his ordination he served willingly, reverently and without haste, and felt great spiritual joy in it; the other fathers revered him and loved him particularly, because they held him to be a true monk. The elder of the monastery, [Ambrose](/saints/ambrose-of-optina), saw in him his future successor and gave him his blessing to hear the confessions of some of the monastery's visitors, preparing him little by little and teaching him by word and by example alike.

When the elder Ambrose fell asleep everyone turned to Joseph, and he took up the spiritual guidance of the brethren with prayer and humility. His prayer had great power, and God counted him worthy of the gift of foresight. He never allowed a monk to refuse an obedience, saying that whoever carries through the obedience laid on him is counted worthy of a blessed end; and when people complained to him that obedience was hard and caused much sorrow, his face would light up with joy, his eyes fill with tender fatherly love, and he would say in a particular spiritual tone: well, and what of it? By this obedience you will become martyrs. A lover and a worker of unceasing prayer, he tried to encourage others to that divine work too, taught prayer very well and believed it the most important occupation for everyone; but he sternly kept the inexperienced and the novices from the prayer of the mind, teaching them to go forward by degrees, beginning with the Jesus prayer said aloud over a few knots of the rope, because, he said, that protects a man from pride. Every day the poor gathered outside his cell, and the monk on duty gave alms to them all.

He served the Liturgy for the last time at the feast of the Dormition of the Mother of God in 1905. From the next day he was ill and everyone expected the end, but God prolonged his life, which was precious to them all, until 1911. A little before his repose he communed of the immaculate Mysteries and begged the brethren to pray God to free his soul from his body. He asked them to set an icon near him; the monks brought the icon of the Mother of God with which the monk Isaakios had blessed him, placed it opposite him on the little table by his pillow and lit a lamp before it. He held in his hands a small white wooden cross, and on his breast they had laid the relic of the holy Great Martyr Barbara. So he fell asleep in peace.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 9 мая ст. ст. and gives him a life the Romanian and Greek pages did not. He was born Иван on 2 November 1837, the son of the village elder Ефим Литовкин and his wife Мария. He lost his father at four and his mother at eleven, and the childhood that followed was hard: work in a tavern and in a grocer’s shop, five-pood sacks to carry, carts of goods to escort, hunger, wandering, and beatings from a cruel master. The life says plainly that none of it made him bitter.

He was not thinking of a monastery until a letter came from his sister Александра, by then a nun called Леонида at Borisovka, telling him to go to Optina and the elders — and this just as a good place had opened with a Taganrog merchant who wanted to give him his daughter. The elder Макарий was already dead, but Амвросий was shining in his stead, and to the young man’s «Батюшка, благословите в Киев» he answered «Зачем тебе в Киев, оставайся здесь». That was 1 March 1861, and it began half a century in the monastery.

He served first in the refectory, where the life says his obedience, his industry, his silence and his freedom from malice were made plain, and then as cell-attendant to Амвросий himself. He twice set out in secret for Kiev and Athos, wearied by the crowds at the elder’s door, and was twice stopped. For decades he had no corner of his own, sleeping in the reception room among visitors and rising at one in the morning for matins. He was tonsured to the mantle in 1872, ordained hierodeacon in 1877, and made hieromonk on 1 October 1884 at the liturgy that opened the convent at Shamordino; in 1888 a mortal illness took him to the infirmary, where he was clothed in the schema on 14 February and from which, the life says, the prayers of Амвросий raised him.

After Амвросий died in 1891 the skete, the brotherhood and the Shamordino sisters fell to him, and he kept them for twelve years — a strict faster in poor old clothes, sleeping very little, never long in speech, answering in a few words and sending the sick away well. One woman he received put his prayer-rope in her hands, told her to wait, went into his bedroom, and when he came out she had forgotten her illness; a peasant woman whose neck had twice been operated on and only swelled the more he told not to be cut a third time but to have a moleben sung to the great-martyr Пантелеимон, and she was healed. He began to fail about 1905 and bore a long illness, and fell asleep on 9 мая 1911, 22 May in the new style. He was buried on the twelfth, and the life records that the dead man’s hand stayed soft and warm as a living one’s.
