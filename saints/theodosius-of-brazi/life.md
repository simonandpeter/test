# Theodosius of Brazi

Theodosius was born in the first half of the seventeenth century near the Brazi monastery, of free peasant stock that traced itself to the men of Stephen the Great. He entered Brazi as a novice at eighteen and was tonsured at Bogdana. A devout monk with a sharp mind and a grounding in Scripture and the Fathers, he was made Bishop of Rădăuți in 1669 - from where he would go often to Stephen's tomb at Putna, where a lamp burned without ceasing - then of Roman in 1671, and in 1674 Metropolitan of Moldavia.

The times were merciless to him. He was imprisoned and driven from his see in the political storms of the Moldavian court, and retired at last to the Brazi monastery he had known as a boy, restoring churches and monasteries and leaving what the faithful had given him to his disciple, the deacon Laurence, for the building of two more. The chronicler Ion Neculce writes that in those years the Tatars came like locusts; in 1694 a band of them broke into the Brazi monastery and tortured the old metropolitan to make him give up the treasures and the money, and when he would not, they cut off his head. It was just after the Feast of the Cross.

The Romanian Church solemnly glorified him on 5 October 2003, and set his memory on 22 September.
