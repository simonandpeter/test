# Sergius (Tikhomirov)

He was a layman, and the only layman of the five the Russian calendar keeps on this day. Сергей Федорович Тихомиров was born in 1866 — the page prints 1867 after it with a question mark, and both years are kept here — in the family of a Moscow merchant of the second guild, who had stood in the merchantry since 1867 and traded in the Охотный ряд. From 1895 he was himself the owner of a meat shop on the Arbat; a second shop came to him in 1902 in Дорогомилово, and in 1910 he moved there.

His shop stood opposite the church, and when the alarm bell was struck he was among the first to run to defend the holy things of that church. He was arrested in April 1922 in the case of the confiscation of church valuables. The verdict of 8 May named him with the hieromonk of the five and others as having taken part in public gatherings, stirred the people to resist those carrying out the confiscation, and spread what it called knowingly false information. At the trial he held himself bravely, asked for no mercy, and refused his last word.

The public trial of fifty of the arrested had opened on 26 April in the hall of the Polytechnic Museum under the chairmanship of the tribunal's president Бек. On 8 May the Moscow revolutionary tribunal sentenced eleven men to be shot; on 18 May the Politburo struck six names from that list, and five were left: [Christopher (Nadezhdin)](/saints/christopher-nadezhdin), [Alexander (Zaozersky)](/saints/alexander-zaozersky), [Basil (Sokolov, 13 May)](/saints/basil-sokolov-13-may), [Macarius (Telegin)](/saints/macarius-telegin) and this man.

They were shot at the beginning of June 1922 in what the page calls the ship of the Cheka — the premises of the archive of a former insurance company, where the condemned were shot in the windowless rooms built for its safes in the half-basement — and buried at the Калитниковское cemetery in Moscow. They were canonised in 1998 as locally venerated saints of the Moscow diocese, and numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
