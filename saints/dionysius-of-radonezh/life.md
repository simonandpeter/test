# Dionysius of Radonezh

The venerable Dionysius of Radonezh, David Zobninovsky in the world, was born about 1570 in the town of Rzhev. He was a monk and then abbot of the Uspensky monastery at Staritsa, and through the Time of Troubles he was the most faithful helper of [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-moscow), patriarch of Moscow. From 1610 he was archimandrite of the Troitse-Sergiev Lavra, and by his care hospitals were founded in the monastery for the sick, the wounded and the homeless of the war that followed the Polish-Lithuanian invasion.

In a time of famine, at his insistence, the brotherhood of the Lavra lived on bread of oats and on water, so that the wheat and rye bread could go to the sick. In 1611 and 1612, together with the Lavra's monk Abraham Palitsyn, who died in 1625, he wrote letters begging that soldiers and money be sent for the freeing of Moscow from the Poles, and he wrote also to prince Dmitry Pozharsky and to his soldiers to hasten their march on the city. His unceasing prayer and his daily spiritual labours brought him the gift of wonderworking.

A thing apart is his share in the correcting of the theological books. From 1616 he led the work of correcting the Synopsis of the holy Mysteries, comparing the old Slavonic against various Greek editions, and the correctors found many grave differences in other books published between 1612 and 1619 as well. The men responsible for those errors accused him of heresy at the synod of 1618. He was stripped of his priestly rights, cut off from the Church and shut in the Novospassky monastery, where he suffered hunger; the intervention of Theophanes IV, patriarch of Jerusalem, and of the patriarch Philaret, returned from Polish captivity, ended his imprisonment and he was acquitted. He fell asleep in peace in 1633 and was buried in the Lavra.
