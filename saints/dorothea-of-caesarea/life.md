# Dorothea of Caesarea

The Russian calendar's life for 6 февраля ст. ст. tells one story of four. Dorothea lived at Caesarea in Cappadocia and suffered under the emperor Diocletian, in 288 or in 300, together with the martyrs [Christina](/saints/christina-companion-of-dorothea) and [Callista](/saints/callista-companion-of-dorothea) and the martyr [Theophilus](/saints/theophilus-companion-of-dorothea). She was a pious Christian maiden, marked out by great meekness, humility and chastity, and by a wisdom given her of God that astonished many.

Seized at the order of the governor Sapricius, she confessed her faith in Christ firmly and was put to torture. Unable to break her will, the governor handed her over to two women, sisters, Christina and Callista, who had been Christians before but had denied Christ in fear of torment and were living an impious life; he ordered them to persuade Dorothea to sacrifice to the pagan gods. The opposite happened. Convinced by Dorothea that God's mercy gives salvation to all who repent, the women repented and turned back to Christ, and for that they were bound back to back and burned in a barrel of pitch.

Dorothea was tortured again, bore it with great joy, and received the sentence of death as joyfully. As she was being led out, a learned man, a scholastic named Theophilus, said to her mockingly: bride of Christ, send me roses and apples out of thy bridegroom's garden. The martyr nodded to him. Before her death she asked time to pray, and when her prayer was finished an angel stood before her in the form of a beautiful youth and held out, on a clean cloth, three apples and three roses. She asked that they be carried to Theophilus, and then she was beheaded by the sword.

Receiving the gifts of grace, the lately persecutor of Christians was astounded, believed in the Saviour and confessed himself a Christian; he was cruelly tortured for it and met his martyr's end by the sword. The life adds that Dorothea's relics are at Rome, in a church of her name, and her head also at Rome, in the church of the Mother of God in Trastevere.

The Serbian church keeps her on the Julian 6 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue tells the same four lives and parts from the Russian one in four details. She is an honoured and beautiful maiden of Caesarea in Cappadocia, and Sapricius the governor of the region hands her to two sisters, Christina and Callista, to turn her from Christ; but here the two are pagans, where the Russian life makes them Christians who had denied Christ in fear and were living an impious life. The opposite happened, and Sapricius in his anger had the two bound back to back and thrown into a vat of pitch and set alight.

Then Dorothea was condemned, and heard the sentence with joy: "I thank thee, Christ who lovest souls, that thou callest me into thy Paradise and bringest me into thy most holy courts." A nobleman standing by named Theophilus — a nobleman here, not the Russian life's scholastic — laughed at the words and called after her, "Listen, bride of Christ, send me some apples and pomegranate flowers out of thy bridegroom's Paradise." "Truly I will do it," she answered. At the place of execution a beautiful boy appeared all at once with three fine apples and three red pomegranate flowers; it was an angel of God, and the season was winter. She told him to carry them to Theophilus and say, "Here is what thou didst wish for." When Theophilus took the message and saw the gift he was utterly terrified, everything in him turned over, and that hardened pagan became a Christian; and he too was tortured and killed for Christ, and went in his soul to the Paradise of the Lord Jesus soon after her.
