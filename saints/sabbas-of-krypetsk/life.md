# Sabbas of Krypetsk

Sabbas of Krypetsk was tonsured on Athos — a Serb by birth, the Prologue says — and came from there to Pskov. He began his labours on Snetnaya hill at the monastery of the Mother of God near Pskov, then moved to a more secluded place on the little river Tolva, to the monastery of Euphrosynus, and at last withdrew to complete silence in the Krypetsk wilderness, fifteen versts from Tolva, settling alone by a small lake in impassable forest; the hermit’s food was bread and water, and on Wednesday and Friday he ate nothing, and he suffered much from unclean spirits but always overcame them by prayer. After some years lovers of the desert life gathered to him and begged him to found a monastery and build a church of John the Theologian; he refused to be abbot and gave the government to the monk Cassian, and many came to the strict elder from Pskov, whom he healed and admonished, taking gifts from none. Once the prince of Pskov, Yaroslav Vasilyevich Obolensky, who often visited the monastery, came with his sick wife; Sabbas sent to say, the sinful elder Sabbas tells you, prince, do not enter the monastery with the princess — our rule here is that women do not enter; if you break the fathers’ commandment your princess will not be healed — the prince asked pardon, and the saint blessed her and prayed for her outside the monastery and healed her of her sickness — the Sretensky calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. He reposed in 1495, and his relics were and remain wonderworking; of his successors at Krypetsk the abbot Dositheus is remembered. The Russian calendar keeps him with two troparia and a kontakion — a new bright sun, the kontakion says, rose from the desert over the glorious city of Pskov.
