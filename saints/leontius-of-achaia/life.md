# Leontius of Achaia

saint.gr's calendar for 11 December keeps the venerable Leontius who lived as a monk in Achaia. He was born at Monemvasia in the Peloponnese, the page says, of Andreas and Theodora; his father was a rich and considerable man, to whom the emperor Andronikos II Palaiologos, whose reign it dates 1283 to 1328, gave an important place in the central administration of the Morea. Leon, for that was his name before, had much care from his parents and studied foreign languages, philosophy and theology at Constantinople.

When his father died he went back to Monemvasia and looked after his mother. Afterwards his mother withdrew to a monastery, and Leon with her blessing married; and he was a pattern of a husband, of a householder and of a public benefactor. But then the great trials came. His wife died, and then his children, and so he decided himself to become a monk.

He became a monk, then, with the name Leontius, and went to an experienced ascetic called Menides, with whom he stayed a considerable time. After that he went to the Holy Mountain, where he got a fairly great ascetic experience, and returned again to the Peloponnese. There he chose for his dwelling the mountain near Aigio, and from there he used to go into the cities of Achaia and preach the divine word; and others came to him who were zealous for the angelic life, many of whom afterwards distinguished themselves in the holy clergy.

Leontius reposed in peace, and the fame of his virtue remained after his death; and the Palaiologos brothers Thomas and Theodore, in the venerable man's honour, built a monastery on the place of his asceticism in the name of the archangel Michael. The greater part of his relics, the page says, is at the monastery of the Archangels of Aigialeia.
