# Luke the Evangelist

Luke was of Antioch in Syria, and from his youth learned the Greek wisdom and the physician’s craft and became a skilled doctor; he was also a chosen painter. He knew Egyptian and Greek well, and having mastered the Hebrew learning as well he went to Jerusalem. At that time the Lord was walking on earth among men and sowing the seed of the word of salvation, which grew in Luke’s heart as in good ground and brought forth a hundredfold, for he learned to know the true God, to believe in Him and to teach the faith to others.

He was one of the seventy apostles, whom he himself recalls in his Gospel — „the Lord appointed other seventy also, and sent them two and two before His face into every city and place” — and being of that apostolic company he went before the face of the Lord by his holy preaching, preparing His way and assuring the peoples that the Messiah who was awaited had come into the world.

In the time of the saving Passion, when the shepherd was struck and the sheep of the flock were scattered, Luke went about mourning and weeping for his Lord, who had willingly consented to suffer; and he who sowed in tears reaped his reward with joy. For when Christ was risen, and Luke and Cleopas were going to Emmaus and speaking sorrowfully between them of the sufferings of their beloved teacher, the Lord Himself comforted them by His appearing and wiped the tears from their eyes, coming near and asking what manner of communications they had one to another as they walked and why they were sad.

The fire of love for God that was hidden in his heart came out in those words, „did not our heart burn within us while He talked with us by the way, and while He opened to us the Scriptures?” He knew the Son of God in the breaking of bread, whom Judas at the Mystical Supper had not wished to know. And that the memory of the Lord whom he loved with his whole heart should not be lost, fifteen years after the Ascension he wrote the Gospel with every assurance — not only what he had seen and heard himself, but what he had written in his heart, the page says, not with a pen but with love.

He shared the pains and labours of Paul in the good tidings of Christ, following him in preaching Christ to the Jews and to the nations; he was with him at Rome, as the Acts of the Apostles show, which he also wrote, and he was greatly loved by Paul, who calls him in the letter to the Colossians the beloved physician, and praises him to the Corinthians as one chosen of the churches to be their companion in travel.

Leaving Rome he went eastward, preaching Christ and suffering pains and labours for His holy name. He went through all Libya, came into Egypt and enlightened the Thebaid by his preaching, and in the cities of Boeotia he set the churches in order, ordaining priests and deacons; he healed the sick in body and in soul, and having suffered much he rested in the Lord at more than eighty years of age.

On the place where his holy body was laid, God, glorifying His servant, rained down a clear water that heals the pain of the eyes, in token of his physician’s craft; and by that the faithful knew his grave, for they were healed there of various sicknesses. Constantius, the son of Constantine the Great, hearing of the healing relics, sent Artemius, the governor of Egypt — who was himself afterwards tormented for Christ by Julian the Apostate — and he brought the relics of the apostle with great honour into the imperial city.

As they were carried in with singing and praise, a eunuch of the imperial palace named Anatolius, long bedridden and having spent much money on physicians without finding healing anywhere, prayed earnestly to the saint, rose as far as he could and had himself carried to the apostle’s healing reliquary; touching it in faith and venerating the relics, he was healed at once, and in perfect health and strength carried the reliquary on his own shoulders with the others into the church of the Holy Apostles, where it was laid beneath the holy table.

It is said of him that he painted wonderfully the likeness of the most holy Mother of God bearing in her arms the pre-eternal Child; and that he painted two other icons of her and brought them to her to see whether they pleased her, and that seeing those likenesses of herself she said, «Darul Celui ce S-a născut din mine și al meu să fie cu icoanele acestea» — may the grace of Him who was born of me, and mine, be with these icons. He painted on wood also the likenesses of the great apostles Peter and Paul; and from him, the page says, began in all the world the good and honourable work of painting the holy icons.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 18 October. saint.gr says Luke was of Antioch in Syria, Greek by nationality, a physician who also knew the painter's art well, and that to him are ascribed the first icons of the Mother of God with the infant Christ — one of them, it says, at the Monastery of Mega Spileo to this day — and the icons of the Apostles Peter and Paul. He was catechised by the Apostle Paul, whom he met at Thebes, and then preached in Dalmatia, Italy, Gaul, Achaia and Boeotia, writing the third Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles. He is said to have died a martyr's death, or by other accounts peacefully at eighty; in 357 his body was taken to the church of the Holy Apostles in Constantinople. The page names him patron of Lamia.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 18 October and gives his whole course in one paragraph. He was from Antioch by birth, and in his youth had learnt Greek philosophy well, and medicine, and painting. While the Lord Jesus was still at work on earth Luke came to Jerusalem, where he saw the Saviour face to face, heard his saving teaching and was a witness of his wonderful deeds; and believing in the Lord he was numbered among the Seventy apostles and sent out to preach. He was the one who, with Cleopas, saw the risen Lord on the road to Emmaus.

After the descent of the Holy Spirit he went back to Antioch and became there the fellow-worker of the apostle Paul, with whom he travelled as far as Rome, turning Jews and pagans to the faith of Christ — Luke the beloved physician greets you, as Paul writes to the Colossians. At the request of the Christians he wrote the Gospel, about the year sixty. After Paul’s martyrdom Luke preached through Italy, Dalmatia, Macedonia and other parts.

He painted an icon of the most holy Theotokos — and not one but three — and icons of the apostles Peter and Paul as well, and from this, the entry says, Luke is held to be the founder of Christian iconography. In his old age he visited Libya and Upper Egypt, and came back from Egypt to Greece, where he went on preaching and turning men to Christ with great zeal, in spite of his deep old age. He wrote both the Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles, and dedicated both to Theophilus, prince of Achaia.

He was eighty-four years old when the malicious idolaters put him to torture for Christ’s sake and hanged him on an olive tree in the city of Thebes in Boeotia. The wonderworking relics of this marvellous saint, the entry ends, were translated to Constantinople in the time of the emperor Constantius, the son of Constantine.
