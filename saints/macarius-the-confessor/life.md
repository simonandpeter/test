# Macarius the Confessor

Doxologia's calendar names Macarius for 19 February and gives no life. His page carries the line «Sfântul Cuvios Mărturisitor Macarie», the date, and the readings appointed for his day — the Apostle from Galatians on the fruit of the Spirit, and the Gospel from Matthew, Come unto me, all ye that labour — and there is no life tab, no troparion tab and no notice of any kind.

What the line itself says is that he is kept as a venerable one and as a confessor: a monk, that is, who suffered for the faith and was not killed for it. Where he lived, under whom he confessed and what was done to him, this source does not print, and this entry does not supply it. Doxologia keeps another confessor, Eugenius, on the same day and prints as little for him.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them on one line and on the same day, and tells what they confessed and where they were sent. The venerable fathers [Eugenius](/saints/eugenius-the-confessor) and Macarius were seized under the emperor Julian the Apostate, saint.gr says, whose reign it gives as 361 to 363, and before him they declared with spiritual manliness that Jesus Christ is the true God and reproved the idolatrous emperor for his opinions. Julian at once ordered them tortured: they were hung up and laid on a red-hot gridiron, and by the grace of God they were kept whole and unhurt. After that Julian exiled them to Mauretania.

There they came to a place where they lived as ascetics with prayer and fasting; but the people of that country advised them to leave it, because there was a serpent there. They were not afraid, but prayed fervently to God, and the wonder happened at once: a thunderbolt fell from heaven and burnt the serpent up. After that they went into the cave the serpent had lived in and began to pray, without eating or drinking anything. On the thirtieth day they heard a voice from heaven commanding them to go to a rock that was near by; and when they went, they saw a strong light coming out of the rock, and at once, by a wonder, the rock was split in two and abundant water came out of it. Having drunk and quenched their thirst they prayed another eight days, and fell asleep in peace, giving up their holy souls to the Lord. Of its two couplets on them, one has Eugenius dwelling with Macarius like well-born shoots in the land of the Blessed — a play on the second name — and the other that before dying the two of them suffer ten thousand dreadful things together. The page gives them no homeland, no year and no hymn.
