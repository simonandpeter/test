# George of Diipion

George lived in the tenth century. saint.gr says the Paris codex has him flourishing under John I Tzimiskes and the codex of Vienna under Romanos and Constantine the Porphyrogennetoi, and that his memory stands in the Lavriotic codex as well. He had a wife and children, and left them, and went about from place to place, out of the cities into the desert, in hardship and ill usage, which is the whole of what the page tells of his ascesis while he lived.

In the last seven days of his life he came to Constantinople and took refuge in the church of St John the Theologian at Diipion, where he fell asleep in peace. Then those who were laying him out for burial saw with astonishment that very heavy irons were bound on his body, by which his whole body had been worn away; and understanding from that that he was a man of ascesis, they made a stone coffin and buried him in the narthex of the church. From that time the place gave out myrrh, which healed various sicknesses and did many miracles — and that, with the two lines the page keeps for him, is why the calendar calls him ο Νεοφανής, the newly-revealed.
