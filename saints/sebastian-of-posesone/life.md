# Sebastian of Posesone

saint.gr's calendar for 26 Φεβρουαρίου names him and gives no life. Its line reads «Όσιος Σεβαστιανός του Ποσεσόνε», and the whole of the page standing under it is a referral: «Για τον Όσιο Σεβαστιανό του Ποσεσόνε βλέπε στις 18 Δεκεμβρίου» — for the venerable Sebastian of Posesone, see 18 December. Above the referral stands only saint.gr's standing gloss on the name Sebastian, that it comes from σεβαστός, the reverend.

So the Greek calendar keeps him twice, and the fuller account stands on its December day, which is not this day's reading. The place the calendar calls «Ποσεσόνε» is not explained here, and the form of it in this folder's name is no more than a transliteration of what the page prints; the entry gives no century, no year, no office, no relics, no couplet and no hymn.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day, gives the life saint.gr's page withholds, and in doing so says what «Ποσεσόνε» is. Its line reads «Прп. Севастиана Пошехонского (ок. 1500).» — Sebastian of Poshekhonye — and the page behind it heads «Дни памяти: 26 февраля, 23 мая (Ростов.), 18 декабря»: the same 26 February on which the Greek calendar names him, and the same 18 December to which the Greek page refers its reader for his life. The Greek «του Ποσεσόνε» and the Russian «Пошехонский» are read here as one epithet and these two entries as one man; the English name on this record still carries the Greek form of it.

He founded a monastery in honour of the Lord's Transfiguration on the river Sokhot, some ninety versts from the town of Romanov — Tutayev now — in the Yaroslavl country. The monks of the house tilled the ground themselves and lived by the work of their hands, which their founder taught them by his example as much as by his words; and he died about the year 1500.

The Transfiguration house on the Sokhot was afterwards made a dependency of the Resurrection monastery at Cherepovets, and in 1764 it was closed. In the middle of the nineteenth century a stone church was built over his relics, where they lie still.
