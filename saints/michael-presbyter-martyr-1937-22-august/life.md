# Michael the Presbyter (22 August)

Michael Lyubertsev was born on 21 October 1883 in the town of Ostashkov,
the son of a tailor. He finished the church school and from 1901 to 1907
taught at the fourth parish school of his town; choosing a wholly churchly
path, he became psalmist at the Transfiguration church of Ostashkov in
1907, and in 1912 was ordained deacon to a church of Staritsa, where he
served until 1918, when, returning to Ostashkov, he served at the Znamensky
monastery. The Bolsheviks’ assault on the Nilov hermitage and the seizure of
the valuables from the churches of Ostashkov passed before his eyes. At the
end of the twenties the persecution hardened, and in 1931 the OGPU arrested
Bishop Tikhon (Rozhdestvensky) of Velikiye Luki and with him the clergy of
Velikiye Luki, Toropets and Ostashkov, the monks of the Nilov hermitage and
many nuns and novices — a hundred and ninety-one people, ninety-nine
priest-monks and monks, twenty-five secular priests and deacons and
sixty-seven lay people, peasants, former officers and gentry. Deacon
Michael was arrested on 19 March 1931. They were accused of running an
illegal monastery in defiance of the closing of every monastery in the
region, one that drew the laity by the high ideal of the Christian
struggle, of running circles to teach schoolchildren the law of God, and of
founding sisterhoods at Velikiye Luki, Toropets and Ostashkov that spread
theological books; to give it a political colour the authorities said this
religious activity had broken up the collective farms of the districts.
He was sentenced to three years in the Northern region. Prison and exile
did not break him but strengthened his faith and his trust in God, and
though he had a large family — three sons and two daughters, the youngest
six and ten — he came back in 1934 resolved to serve God and the church to
the end, and was ordained priest to the Dormition church at Shchuchye in the
Ostashkov district, where he served until his death.

The authorities tried to shut the church’s grace inside its walls, forbidding
him to go round the far villages of the parish with molebens and to hear
the confessions of those who could not reach the church; he went all the
same, walked the villages in procession, served in houses and in the fields,
and was a true pastor and father to his people — and, not wanting to be
accused of acting without asking, went each time to the executive committee
for permission, and was almost always refused. On 18 June the faithful of
one village asked the village soviet for molebens in their houses and a
procession with icons at Trinity, and were refused; on 22 July he was
summoned to the collective farm’s board, where the chairman, before the
farmers, pressed him to subscribe to the state loan. Subscribing to the loan
is a good thing, he answered, but they will not sell me bread at the
co-operative, they will not let me go round the parish with molebens, and I
already pay a very heavy tax; still, I will subscribe, on condition that you
let me hold services in the houses — on 2 August, St Elias’s day, at
Lukyanovo, say. Let those who want it apply, said the chairman, and the
soviet will consider it; that evening he told a member of the parish
twenty to gather the signatures and tell the soviet that if the molebens
were allowed all the faithful would subscribe. The application went in on
23 July and was refused; the next day the NKVD man drew up the papers for
his arrest — he had, they said, agitated against the loan by making the
farmers’ subscription conditional on the soviet allowing services with
icons in the villages. He was arrested that day and put in the Ostashkov
prison, the material evidence taken from his house being a volume of the
works of John of Kronstadt and twelve illustrations to sacred history. In
prison and under interrogation he was fearless and free, and did not admit
his guilt in any way. The witnesses said he told the faithful to stand firm
for the faith and make the soviet reckon with believers; that he had asked,
in the drought of June, for permission to walk the villages with icons and
pray for rain, and when refused had said there was no rain because God was
angered, and that trampling the faith would be punished; that he had said
there would soon be a war and many would die in it. On 31 August the NKVD
troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 4 September 1937 —
22 August of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him, with the
bishops John and Alexius and their company. The Jubilee Council of Bishops
of August 2000 numbered him among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia.
