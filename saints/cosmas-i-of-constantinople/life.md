# Cosmas I of Constantinople

Cosmas came from Antioch and was surnamed the Jerusalemite because he stayed a long while in the holy City; from there he came and lived as a monk in Constantinople. saint.gr says he fell short in grammatical learning, but that a guileless piety adorned him, and a deep and sincere virtue, and that men loved him for his straightness, his character and the simplicity of his manners.

When the patriarch John VIII died on 2 August 1075 the emperor Michael Doukas brought Cosmas to the throne, old though he already was. Under his patriarchate the archbishopric of Patras was raised to a metropolis with three bishoprics under it, and he himself ordained and sent, in 1080, the Greek John as metropolitan of Rus, a man of great learning and many virtues.

The difficulties of governing the patriarchate made his simple nature long for the quiet of his former monastic life. So on 8 May 1081, after serving in the church of Saint John the Theologian, he left with his servant and withdrew to the monastery of Kallias in Constantinople. They begged him in vain to return; he stayed unpersuaded, and died in that monastery.
