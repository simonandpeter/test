# Maria Brâncoveanu

Marica Brâncoveanu, whom the Romanian church keeps as Sfânta Doamnă Maria, came of an influential boyar family — her father a postelnic, her grandfather a ruling prince — and was married in 1674, at thirteen or fourteen, to [Constantine Brâncoveanu](/saints/constantine-brancoveanu). Doxologia.ro's life says she took up the work of a wife and householder with a maturity beyond her years, bore eleven children and raised them with care, and became her husband's dependable partner in the management of the family's estates and its wide network of lands and investments.

Through her husband's reign Wallachia knew peace and a flowering of culture, and the life says she was present and active in it: she visited the estates herself, rewarded good service and punished abuses, and was respected and feared. In 1706 she addressed the sultan directly to protect the family's property in Istanbul, and carried her point. She supported the building and restoring of churches — among them Sfântul Nicolae Dintr-o zi and the monastery of Surpatele — and encouraged schools, printing houses and libraries, and the printing of books in several languages.

The year 1714 was the hardest of her life. After her husband's deposition the family was carried into exile, and the prince and his four sons — [Constantin](/saints/constantin-son-of-brancoveanu), [Ștefan](/saints/stefan-brancoveanu), [Radu](/saints/radu-brancoveanu) and [Matei](/saints/matei-brancoveanu) — were put to death at Constantinople for refusing to give up the Orthodox faith. She was a witness of it. Left alive, she was imprisoned, robbed and banished, and the life says she held firm, understanding the suffering as a cross appointed her by God.

After three years of exile she returned and set herself to recover her husband's body. In 1720 she brought the relics of Saint Constantine secretly into the church of Sfântul Gheorghe Nou in Bucharest and laid them under an uninscribed slab, which kept its anonymity for two centuries; a discreetly given lamp with a plain inscription was the only sign of him there. To the end of her life she went on rebuilding what was left of the Brâncoveanu inheritance and providing for her grandson Constantin, and she spent her last years withdrawn at Surpatele in prayer.

In its working session of 1 July 2025 the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church approved her canonisation under the title Sfânta Doamnă Maria Brâncoveanu, with 16 August for her commemoration; doxologia.ro's troparion page records that her troparion was sung by the Tronos psaltic group on 6 February 2026, at the general proclamation of the canonisation of sixteen Romanian women saints. Doxologia.ro gives no year for her birth or her death.
