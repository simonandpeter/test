# Gregory V of Constantinople

Gregory was George Angelopoulos in the world, born in 1745 at Dimitsana in the Peloponnese to devout parents named John and Asimina. He began his schooling in the village, his father not being able to afford better, and in 1765 went to Athens to study under the well-known teacher Demetrios Vodas. In 1767 he moved to Smyrna, to his uncle the sacristan Meletios, and attended the Evangelical School there; afterwards he studied philosophy on Patmos under Daniel Kerameas.

He was tonsured at the monastery of the Transfiguration on the Strophades islands and took the name Gregory. Procopius, metropolitan of Smyrna, called him back and ordained him archdeacon, and after his ordination to the priesthood he returned to Dimitsana and gave fifteen hundred groschen for the lodging of poor pupils. In 1785 he was consecrated bishop and chosen metropolitan of Smyrna, and on 19 August 1785 he was elected Ecumenical Patriarch, holding the throne until December 1798.

He was an ascetic who kept the fasts and gave himself to the unceasing prayer of the heart, and his labours, the life says, drew the envy of many bishops who slandered him, so that he was removed and exiled to the Holy Mountain from 1798 to 1806. In 1818 he was elected to the ecumenical throne for the third time and held it until the day of his martyrdom. He set up a fund for the poor of Constantinople and reorganised the patriarchal press, publishing many spiritual books.

In that same year, 1818, he joined the Filiki Eteria, which was preparing a rising against the Turkish occupation, and his links with the Greek patriots came out only after Alexander Ypsilantis crossed the Prut with his army against Sultan Mahmud. On 10 April 1821, the night of Pascha, having served the Liturgy of the Resurrection with eight other hierarchs, Gregory was arrested and imprisoned; at three that afternoon, vested as a bishop, he was hanged from the gate in front of the Ecumenical Patriarchate. His body hung there three days, and the gate has stood shut ever since.

It was then taken and carried about the city and thrown into the Bosphorus. A Greek sailor, Nicholas Sklavos, recovered it from the sea; it was taken secretly to Odessa and buried in the Greek church of the Holy Trinity on 19 June 1821. The Holy Synod of the Church of Greece proclaimed him a saint on 10 April 1921, a hundred years to the day, and his relics now lie in the Metropolitan Cathedral of Athens. The page carries no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and fills in the years before the patriarchate. saint.gr says that George Angelopoulos, as he was in the world, was born at Demetsana in 1745 of pious and virtuous parents, John and Asimina; that in 1767 he went to Smyrna, to his uncle the ecclesiarch Meletios, and studied at the Evangelical School there, and then read philosophy at Patmos under Daniel Kerameus. After his studies he came to the imperial monastery of the Transfiguration on the Strophades islands, where he was tonsured and took the name Gregory; from there Prokopios, metropolitan of Smyrna, called him and ordained him his archdeacon, and when he was later ordained presbyter he went back to Demetsana and gave fifteen hundred piastres for the housing of poor students.

saint.gr puts his election as ecumenical patriarch on 19 August 1785 and his holding of the throne until December 1798, when the Porte deposed him as incapable of keeping the Christian peoples in subjection under the Turkish yoke and exiled him to the Holy Mountain; in 1818 he was called to the throne a third time, and stayed on it until the day of his martyr's death. In the middle of that same year 1818, on Athos, he was initiated into the Philiki Etaireia by John Pharmakis, and the page quotes the record: «Ἔδειξεν εὐθὺς ζωηρότατον ἐνθουσιασμὸν ὑπὲρ τοῦ πνεύματος αὐτῆς», he showed at once the liveliest enthusiasm for its spirit, and prayed from the heart for the success of its purpose.

The page is at pains about the excommunication of the revolutionaries. To save the Greek population from slaughter and at the same time to mislead the Sultan and give the fighters room to work, it says, the national martyr was forced to anathematise them; and it prints Alexander Ypsilantis's instructions to the captains of the Peloponnese as the answer to Gregory's accusers — the patriarch, compelled by the Porte, sends you an excommunication and exarchs, urging you to join with the Porte; but you are to hold these things void, as being done by violence and coercion and without the patriarch's will. It also prints the letter he sent on 26 December 1820 to Isaiah, bishop of Salona, which it values because it shows how closely he was following the preparations in Greece, and which warns him that the times are evil and that the greatest need is for secrecy in every step.

Its couplets say that though he died on the gallows he lives for ever in the divine Eden, and that on the tenth the patriarch became a sacrifice for the sake of the nation.
