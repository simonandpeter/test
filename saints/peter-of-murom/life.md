# Peter of Murom

Peter was the second son of Yuri Vladimirovich, prince of Murom, and came to the throne of Murom in 1203. Some years before, he had fallen ill with leprosy, which no one could cure; in a dream it was revealed to him that he could be healed by the devout maiden [Fevronia](/saints/fevronia-of-murom), a beekeeper’s daughter, a peasant of the village of Laskovaya in the Ryazan land. He sent his men to the village, and when he saw her he so loved her for her piety, wisdom and goodness that he vowed to marry her when he was healed; she healed him and married him, and the holy couple carried their love for each other through every trial.

The proud boyars would not have a peasant woman as princess — the Sretensky calendar’s life, read as far as that, tells the rest of the well-known story: exile together, return, the monastic tonsure of both as David and Euphrosyne, and their death on the same day. The Russian calendar keeps on 31 August (Julian) the translation of their relics in 1992; their principal feast is 25 June.

The Romanian calendar keeps him with his wife on 25 June, and doxologia.ro's life adds two dates the Russian one did not give here: that he became prince of the land of Murom in 1203, and that the pair were glorified by a council of the Russian Church in 1552. It tells the story in the same order — the sickness at the beginning of his reign, the beekeeper's daughter famous for healing with herbs, the marriage, the boyars' demand that he put her away, the exile by boat, the misfortunes that fell on Murom and the people's repentance, the return and the years of almsgiving, the tonsure as David and Euphrosyne, the prayer to die on one day and the single coffin with a partition between the bodies, and their death together on 25 June 1228. They are held, it ends, to be the protectors of the newly married, having suffered for the holiness and the inviolability of marriage.
