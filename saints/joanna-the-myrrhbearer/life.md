# Joanna, wife of Chuza

Joanna was one of the company of myrrhbearing women who followed the Saviour Jesus Christ. By tradition, doxologia's life says, she was the one who recovered the head of [John the Baptist](/saints/john-the-baptist) after Herod had given the order for it to be cut off and thrown away.

She was the wife of Chuza, the steward of King Herod Antipas, and she is named in the Gospel of Luke, at the third verse of the eighth chapter, as one of the women who had followed Christ from Galilee and who helped the Lord's disciples, together with Susanna, Mary Magdalene and others.

In Luke, from the fifty-fifth verse of the twenty-third chapter to the eleventh of the twenty-fourth, it is told how these women went to the tomb to finish the anointing with spices that Joseph and Nicodemus had begun in haste. When they came there they were astonished to find the tomb empty, nothing left in it but the linen cloths; and an angel appeared to them and told them of the Lord's resurrection. They believed, and so became the first preachers of the resurrection of Christ.

The Church keeps her on 27 June, and also on the Sunday of the Myrrhbearers. The calendar prints no year and no hymn for her, and none is recorded here.
