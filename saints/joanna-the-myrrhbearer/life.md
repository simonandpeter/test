# Joanna, wife of Chuza

Joanna was one of the company of myrrhbearing women who followed the Saviour Jesus Christ. By tradition, doxologia's life says, she was the one who recovered the head of [John the Baptist](/saints/john-the-baptist) after Herod had given the order for it to be cut off and thrown away.

She was the wife of Chuza, the steward of King Herod Antipas, and she is named in the Gospel of Luke, at the third verse of the eighth chapter, as one of the women who had followed Christ from Galilee and who helped the Lord's disciples, together with Susanna, Mary Magdalene and others.

In Luke, from the fifty-fifth verse of the twenty-third chapter to the eleventh of the twenty-fourth, it is told how these women went to the tomb to finish the anointing with spices that Joseph and Nicodemus had begun in haste. When they came there they were astonished to find the tomb empty, nothing left in it but the linen cloths; and an angel appeared to them and told them of the Lord's resurrection. They believed, and so became the first preachers of the resurrection of Christ.

The Church keeps her on 27 June, and also on the Sunday of the Myrrhbearers. The calendar prints no year and no hymn for her, and none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 27 June, and says of her what the Gospel says and little more. saint.gr's entry is «Αγία Ιωάννα η Μυροφόρος», and the page behind it holds a single sentence: that the holy Joanna the myrrhbearer died in peace, that she was the wife of Chuza, Herod's steward, and that she ministered to the Lord together with the other women, for which it cites Luke 8:3 and 24:10. Above the sentence stands a couplet of the synaxarion: «Oύπερ παρέστης Iωάννα τω τάφω, Tούτου παρέστης έσχατον και τω θρόνω» — you who stood by his tomb, Joanna, stood at the last by his throne as well. The page gives no year, no country and no hymn, and it does not repeat the tradition the Romanian life carries about the head of the Forerunner.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 27 June, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep her on, and gives her six short sentences of which four are about one act. She was the wife of Chuza, the courtier of Herod. When Herod beheaded the Forerunner he threw the head into an unclean place; and Joanna took the [Forerunner](/saints/john-the-baptist)’s head and buried it honourably on the Mount of Olives, on an estate of Herod’s own. Only long afterwards, the entry says, in the time of the emperor whose name is recorded on this folder’s Serbian attestation rather than here, was that head found.

Saint Joanna is remembered as present both at the Lord’s passion and at His resurrection, it ends, and she reposed peacefully. The book gives no year of any kind, no age and no reign for her own life, so this folder still records none; and the finding of the head, which it does date by a reign, is neither her repose nor a date of hers.
