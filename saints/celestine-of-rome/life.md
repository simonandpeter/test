# Celestine of Rome

Doxologia.ro's calendar for 8 aprilie names him and gives no life. The line is «Sfântul Ierarh Celestin, episcopul Romei» — the holy hierarch Celestine, bishop of Rome — and his own page on the site carries no life tab and no troparion: an icon, the readings appointed for the day, and articles that treat him together with the apostles Rufus and Agabus, who share the day.

The line asserts two things and no more: that he was a hierarch, and that his see was Rome. It gives no year, no council, no emperor and no account of how he governed or how he died, and this entry adds none.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives the years and the acts of his pontificate. saint.gr says Celestine was born at Rome and that his father was called Priscus; that early, while still a deacon, he was marked out for his learning, his piety and his warm zeal, and that for this reason, after the death of Pope Boniface in 422, he succeeded him on the pontifical throne. He intervened at once in Africa, in Gaul and in Calabria to enforce the holy canons in the election of bishops, and was most energetic in putting down various heresies.

The page credits him with working for the disappearance of Pelagianism from Great Britain and, by a mission sent for the purpose, with bringing Ireland to Christianity; and it says that at the Third Ecumenical Council the pope, through a delegation of his, condemned the error of Nestorius. In general, it adds, he took care of the affairs of the clergy and of divine worship, adorning the churches with icons and with various sacred vessels. He died under the emperor Theodosius II in 432, on 26 or 27 July by what the page calls the most probable opinion — a day it gives for his death and not as a feast, the Greek calendar keeping him on 8 April as the Romanian does.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and gives him three sentences, which are more than the Romanian calendar prints and less than the Greek. He was a great zealot of the Orthodox faith, it says; in the time of the Third Ecumenical Council he wrote an epistle against the heretic Nestorius; and he reposed in peace in 432, the year already written above. The epistle is this entry's own contribution: the Greek page has the pope condemn Nestorius's error at that council through a delegation of his and says nothing of a letter, and the two readings stand together rather than against each other. The entry gives him no birthplace, no father and no see beyond Rome.

**The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 8 April**, the day all three other calendars keep, and its line is «Свт. Келестина, папы Римского (432).» — the rank, the forename, the see and the year 432 this folder already holds. The name link, page 1192, is titled «Святитель Келестин I Римский, папа» and adds the ordinal no other book read here gives him; it lists this one day and nothing else. The life opens by giving a second spelling in brackets, «Святитель Келестин (Целестин), папы Римского (422 - 432)», and the two dates of the pontificate the Greek page also gives.

**A third date for one death, and `dates` are left alone.** This life closes «мирно скончался 6 апреля 432 года», he died in peace on 6 April 432 — where the Greek page, recorded above, gives 26 or 27 July 432 as what it calls the most probable opinion, and the Ohrid Prologue gives the year alone. So two books now name a day and the two days are three and a half months apart, with the year the only thing all three agree on. The stored `death` keeps its flat 432 on a `traditional` basis and its display stays the bare year; **a day neither this reader nor the corpus can choose between is not a narrowing.** And the feast is on neither of the two days: the Julian 8 April is two days after the one and a season from the other, so this is **a commemoration that is not the day of the death by any book that gives one**.

What the Russian adds to the acts is the shape of the Nestorian business from the Roman end. He had a good education, it says, knew philosophy excellently, and studied the Scriptures and thought over theological questions harder than anything else; his virtuous life and his standing as a theologian won him the respect and the love of clergy and laity alike, and after the death of «святого папы Бонифация I (418 - 422)» he was elected to the see of Rome. The reign it sets him in is that of «святого царя Феодосия Младшего (408 - 450)», the same emperor the Greek page has him die under.

**Then a local council, a letter and a term of ten days.** At the local council at Rome in 430 he exposed the heresy and the heretic «Несторий» was condemned; after the council he wrote to «святителю Кириллу, архиепископу Александрийскому», whose memory it places on 18 января, a letter saying that if the heresiarch did not renounce his false teaching **within ten days** he would be deposed and excommunicated. He sent further letters to other Churches, naming Constantinople and Antioch. The Greek page has him condemn the error at the Third Ecumenical Council through a delegation; the Ohrid Prologue has him write an epistle against the heretic at the time of that council; and the Russian has a Roman council of its own the year before, with a named year and a named term. The three readings stand together.

An article the name page carries adds that in ten unquiet years of his episcopate he had chiefly to stand up for the true faith, that he fought Pelagianism actively, and that it was news **from** «святителя Кирилла Александрийского» that set the Roman condemnation going — which is the direction of the correspondence the other books leave open.

The last two years it gives him are the two years after the council, in which it says he preached unceasingly the true teaching of Christ as God and man. The Russian prints no hymn for him, as none of the other three calendars did, and the folder stays without one. **No `related` row is written for the archbishop of Alexandria** although the folder would carry it well: a row has to be reciprocal and the other folder is outside this reader’s day, so the name stands in Cyrillic and the pair is left to the author.
