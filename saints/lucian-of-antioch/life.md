# Lucian of Antioch

Lucian was from the city of Samosata in Syria, born of right-believing parents. At twelve he was left an orphan, and gave away to the poor what had come to him from his parents; choosing for himself the narrow and hard life, he trained himself in the study of the divine books. From his youth he drank no wine but only water, took food once a day and sometimes tasted nothing all week, and his food was bread and water. Most of his life he spent in prayer and tears, seeking the blessedness of those who mourn.

Loving silence, he comforted himself always with the thought of God, and his face was always sad; when he had to speak, the divine Scriptures flowed from his mouth. Coming by such a life to the depth of wisdom and outdoing others in learning, he was counted worthy of the rank of priest at Antioch. Wishing to see many others in the right faith and in wisdom like his own, and above all to teach the young, he made a school and gathered a multitude of disciples, teaching both the written and the spiritual wisdom.

He was skilled at writing too, writing beautifully and fast, and by the work of his hand he fed himself and the poor. The Old and New Testaments, written in Greek and corrupted by the cunning of heretics, he set right again out of the Hebrew. And such was the grace of God in him, the page says, that as he walked through the city he was seen by some and not seen by others.

The unbelieving emperor Maximian, called also Galerius, who was then in the countries of the East and meant to root out the holy faith, was killing the wise and famous Christian teachers above all — he had lately killed [Anthimus of Nicomedia](/saints/anthimus-of-nicomedia) and Peter of Alexandria — reckoning that if he threw down the pillars the Church of Christ would fall easily. Hearing of Lucian, he ordered him sought out; and Lucian, fearing as any man might that he would weaken under torture, hid from the searchers.

A presbyter living at Antioch named Pancratius, who held the bad faith of Sabellius and had long envied Lucian his good name, told the emperor’s men where he was hidden, and, like Judas, sold to death a man not guilty of death. They took him and brought him to Nicomedia. On the road, in Cappadocia, seeing soldiers deny Christ for fear of the judges, he rebuked them: they staked their health and their very souls daily for an earthly and passing emperor, and for the heavenly and worthy King would not stand firm.

At Nicomedia he found many Christians and disciples of his own who were in great fear of the torments, some hiding, some ready to deny their Christianity; and he strengthened them all by his powerful word not to fear those who kill the body, since they cannot kill the soul. One of his disciples, a virgin named Pelagia, being sought for the torments and fearing for her virginity, leapt from a high window and died, and the faithful honoured her as a martyr.

Maximian had heard that Lucian’s face was so beautiful and his words so sweet that nobody could see him and hear him without becoming a Christian; so, afraid of becoming one himself, he spoke with him from a distance and through a curtain. He offered him many honourable gifts first, and when Lucian laughed at his deceitful words began to threaten him with torments; but he was not frightened by the threat, and was put in prison, where he bore every kind of torture, bound in chains and stretched on potsherds.

When it was time for the Liturgy and the faithful with him asked where the table was, he said, lying face upward on the sherds, «Pe pieptul meu să o puneți și va fi pristol viu al Dumnezeului celui viu» — lay it on my breast and it will be a living altar of the living God; and so the divine Liturgy was served in the prison upon the breast of the saint, with all its proper prayers, and all communicated of the holy Mysteries.

In the morning the emperor sent servants to see whether Lucian was still alive, and when they came in at the prison doors he cried out three times, «Sunt creștin!» — I am a Christian — and with those words gave up his spirit. Maximian ordered the body thrown into the sea, and they tied it to a stone and threw it into the deep, where it stayed thirty days. Then he appeared to one of his disciples, Glycerius, and told him to go to the shore and take his body and bury it; and Glycerius and other Christians came and saw dolphins bringing the martyr’s incorrupt body to land and laying it on the dry ground. Long afterwards the empress Helen, mother of Constantine, raised a church over his grave.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 15 October, and gives him a birthplace and a work. saint.gr says Lucian came from Samosata in Syria, of a devout family; that he received the education he needed, and after his parents died, at twelve years old, divided his property among the poor. He gave himself to the study of Holy Scripture, and knowing Hebrew he corrected and completed the versions of the Seventy, of Aquila and of Symmachus against the original, giving his own translation to the church of Nicomedia. He was ordained presbyter at Antioch and was marked out for the heat of his preaching, by which he urged the faithful towards martyrdom; and when he learned that Diocletian was persecuting the Christians at Nicomedia he left Antioch and went there to stand by them. He was arrested by Diocletian, imprisoned, and died of hunger.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same menologion day, 15 October, and sends him to a city the other accounts leave out. Its life says that, left an orphan at twelve, he gave his property away to the poor and went to Edessa, to the confessor Macarius, under whose guidance he read the Holy Scriptures diligently and learned the ascetic life; and that for his piety and his zeal in spreading Christianity among the Jews and the pagans he was made presbyter. At Antioch he opened a school, gathered a multitude of disciples and taught them the wisdom of books.

The same page adds what became of the Bible he mended. He laboured at learned works and corrected the text of Holy Scripture where copyists and heretics had spoilt it; and the complete corrected Greek text he made was hidden in a wall during his time of confession, and was found in the days of Saint [Constantine the Great](/saints/constantine-the-great). In Diocletian's persecution he was seized on the denunciation of heretics and sent to the prison at Nicomedia, where for nine years he strengthened the Christians who were with him in their confession, persuading them not to fear torments or death.

He died in the prison of many fearful tortures and of hunger. Before his death, wishing to commune of the Mysteries of Christ at the feast of Theophany, the martyr-presbyter — chained to his bed — performed the bloodless sacrifice upon his own breast, and all the Christians in the prison communicated. His body was thrown into the sea; but after thirty days dolphins carried it out to the shore, and the faithful buried the much-suffering body with honour. The Russian calendar dates his death 312, where the Serbian dates it 7 January 311.

The Serbian calendar keeps him on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue, which tells the passion much as the Romanian life does, adds three things to it. In the persecution not even small children were spared: two boys who would not eat of the idol sacrifices were thrown into a scalding bath and gave up their holy souls to God in the torment. On the road to Nicomedia he turned forty soldiers to the faith of Christ by his counsels, and all of them ended as martyrs. And it keeps his own words at the Liturgy in prison — «Окружите мене, и будите црква», surround me and be the church, and then «На прси моје положите, нека буде живи престо живоме Богу!», lay it on my breast, let it be a living altar to the living God.

The Prologue also quotes Saint John Chrysostom on him: that Lucian despised hunger, and that we should despise luxury in the same way and destroy the rule of the belly, so that when a time comes requiring such courage of us we may be prepared beforehand by lesser struggles and appear glorious in the hour of the fight. It names Pancratius the envious heretical priest who gave him away, and it says that his name went on the emperor Maximian's list of those to be killed at the same time as [Anthimus of Nicomedia](/saints/anthimus-of-nicomedia) and [Peter of Alexandria](/saints/peter-of-alexandria) were being tortured. He suffered, it ends, on 7 January 311.
