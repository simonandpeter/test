# Joseph the New of Partoș, Metropolitan of Timișoara

Joseph the New was born at Ragusa in Dalmatia — Dubrovnik — in 1568 to well-off parents; his father John, a Venetian shipowner, drowned in a shipwreck when the saint was twelve, and his mother Catherine, from Lemnos, was marked by her piety. Jacob at baptism, after his father’s death his mother took him to Ohrid, where her brother was a rich merchant; he had a good education and stayed five years with a hieromonk cousin of his mother who was a monk at the monastery of the Theotokos at Ohrid, continuing his studies there. In 1588 he came to the Holy Mountain and settled at the monastery of Pantokrator, where he was tonsured and named Joseph in the great schema, and after two years ordained priest; he trained himself carefully in obedience, prayer and study, with calligraphy for his handiwork, in which he became excellent, and lived afterwards at the monasteries of the Great Lavra, Chilandar, Xeropotamou and Vatopedi, where he stayed a good while and, by his biography, worked there his first — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 15 September, the Romanian with a troparion and kontakion that call him the unconquered defender of the land of Banat.
