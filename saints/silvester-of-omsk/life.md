# Silvester of Omsk

doxologia.ro's page for Silvester has no Viață tab, and what it prints of him is one sentence. This is that sentence, and it is the whole of what the source gives: in the last two months of his life he was subjected to terrible torments, at the end of which a red-hot dagger was driven into his heart, and so he received a martyr's end, in the year 1920; and he was numbered with the saints by the Russian Orthodox Church in the year 1998.

The calendar's own line adds his rank and his see — the holy hieromartyr and new martyr Silvester, archbishop of Omsk — and nothing more. The page names no birthplace, no family name, no year of birth, nothing of his years as a bishop and nothing of who put him to death. It carries no hymn. A page of its own on the site records that a portion of his relics is kept for veneration at the monastery of Frumoasa, and that is the only other thing it says of him.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this day and gives the life doxologia's page has not got. In the world he was Justin Lvovich Olshevsky, born on 31 May 1860 in the village of Kosovno in the Skvira district of the Kiev province, in the family of a church clerk. He finished the Kiev seminary in 1883 and the Kiev theological academy in 1887; from 1889 he was diocesan missionary of Kiev and from 1890 of Poltava, where he also taught in the seminary, and in 1892 he was ordained, unmarried, in celibacy.

In 1910 father Justin was tonsured a monk with the name Silvester and was soon made archimandrite. On 16 January 1911 he was consecrated bishop of Priluki, vicar of Poltava; in 1914 he became bishop of Chelyabinsk, vicar of the Orenburg diocese; and from 1915 to 1920 he held the see of Omsk and Pavlodar, being raised to archbishop in 1918. In the lands held by the White Army, which had no communication with the patriarchate, a Siberian church council met at Tomsk in November 1918 — thirteen bishops and twenty-six members of the All-Russian Council — and set up a Provisional Church Administration with Silvester at its head.

When the Reds took the city he was arrested in the night, after a procession with the cross, and his steward, who would not let drunken sailors into the bishop's cell, was killed. He died a martyr's death on 13 февраля — 26 February by the new style — in 1920, a prisoner at Omsk, and was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia for veneration throughout the church at the Jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000.
