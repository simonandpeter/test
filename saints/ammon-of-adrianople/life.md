# Ammon of Adrianople

saint.gr's calendar for 2 Σεπτεμβρίου names two martyrs together, Aithalas and Ammon, and the page behind the line gives them one short passion. Both came from Adrianople in Thrace, and both worked with the hottest zeal — «με θερμότατο ζήλο» — for the spreading of the faith of Jesus Christ. Their love of their fellow men was proverbial, the page says, and by it they did good to Christians and to idolaters alike.

That last was what told. The page says the kindness they showed the heathen contributed much to turning idolaters towards the truth of the Gospel. It was this that reached the eparch Vavdos; and when he learned what Aithalas and Ammon were doing, he ordered the two of them seized. Because they refused to sacrifice to the idols they were put to death with the cruellest blows, scourged with whips made of the sinews of oxen.

The page gives no year, no emperor and no place of death, and prints no hymn for them. The two verses set above the life are all the verse there is: «Aειθαλάς Aμών τε πικραίς βασάνοις, / Aιωνίως θάλλουσιν άμφω εν πόλω» — Aithalas and Ammon, by bitter torments, both flourish for ever in heaven. The Greek calendar keeps the two together on 2 September.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 2 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/09/02/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/914/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026.*
