# Amos

Amos was born in the city of Tekoa, in the land of Judea not far from Bethlehem. He was of humble and poor stock and passed his days herding flocks; and the Lord, who looks not to the rich and glorious but to the lowly and the poor, called him from the sheep to prophecy, as he had called Moses and David, and sent him out of Judea into the land of Israel, to call its people back from idols. Since the days of Jeroboam, who broke away from Solomon's son and made a separate kingdom over ten tribes, the people there had fallen from the right worship of God.

Jeroboam, afraid that his subjects would attach themselves again to the temple at Jerusalem if they went up there for the feasts, cast two heifers of gold and set one in Dan and the other in Bethel, telling the people: "Behold thy gods, O Israel, that brought thee out of Egypt." Amos began to prophesy in the days of Uzziah, king of Judah in Jerusalem, two years before the great earthquake that the old writers connect with Uzziah's presumption in censing in the Holy of Holies; the temple split at its top, walls fell throughout Palestine, and the sunlight through the crack fell on the king's face.

Amos went above all to Bethel, where the golden heifer stood on a high hill, and cried aloud to the people at their festivals, pleading with them and warning them of the punishments to come; and few listened. A servant of that heifer named Amaziah denounced him to Jeroboam, but the king, idolater though he was, honoured the prophet and would not harm him. So Amaziah took it on himself: again and again he seized Amos, beat him savagely and drove him out of Bethel.

At last Amaziah told him to go and prophesy in his own country of Judea and not to come back to Bethel, since it was the king's sanctuary and the house of his kingdom. Amos answered that he had been no prophet nor the son of a prophet but a herdsman, poor and fed on the fruits of the wilderness, until the Lord took him from the flock and told him to prophesy to his people Israel; and he foretold what would come when the Assyrians entered the land. At that Amaziah had him beaten without mercy, and Amaziah's son, named Uzziah, struck him so hard across the brows that the wound was mortal. He did not die at once, but was carried barely alive back to Tekoa, and after some days gave up his soul and was buried with his fathers.

The page adds a note distinguishing him from the other Amos, the father of Isaiah, who was of the royal line of Judah, was born and lived in Jerusalem, was not numbered among the prophets, was sent nowhere by God, and did not die a martyr's death.
