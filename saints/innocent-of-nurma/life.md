# Innocent of Nurma

saint.gr's calendar for 19 Μαρτίου keeps a Russian abbot it calls Innocent the Wonderworker. He was born at Moscow, the page says, of the noble family of the Okhlyabinin, and became a monk in the monastery of St Cyril of the White Lake, whose memory it puts on 9 June; there he was placed under the guidance of [Nilus of Sora](/saints/nilus-of-sora), kept on 7 May.

The two of them travelled through the East as it was then known, visiting Palestine and Constantinople and spending many years in the monasteries of the Holy Mountain. When they came back to Russia they preferred the stillness of the desert to a monastery and withdrew into an impassable forest near the river Sora, where they built a church and separate cells and lived under a strict rule.

Foreseeing his own repose, Nilus urged Innocent to go to the river Nurma and prophesied that he would build a coenobitic monastery there. After Nilus's death Innocent withdrew to a tributary of the Nurma called the Eda and built there a monastery marked by a very severe ascetic life; following his teacher's commands he would accept no donation towards the building and worked alone at it for thirty whole years until it was finished.

He charged the monks who settled there to keep the strict ascetic life Nilus had taught, to avoid quarrels, to love Christ and to hold spiritual peace; he forbade the tonsure of very young novices and forbade women to enter the monastery. Last of all he asked that a church be built dedicated to St John the Forerunner, in memory of the third finding of his honourable head, because John is the patron saint of monks — and later, the page adds, the monastery was dedicated to the Transfiguration of the Saviour. Innocent fell asleep on 19 March 1521, and by his last wish was buried in a corner of the monastery near a marsh, where a carved stone on his grave gives the year, the month and the day of his repose.

The Russian calendar's two readings add the years and the rule. [Nilus of Sora](/saints/nilus-of-sora), foreseeing his own end, sent him away to the river Nurma with a word that God would glorify him there and that his would be a coenobitic house where the Sora hermitage would stay as it was, each brother in his own cell; and in 1491, after his teacher's repose, he set a cell on the little river Eda that falls into the Nurma, took no man's endowment, and in a short time had disciples about him.

He laboured thirty years at the building of the monastery and left the brethren a rule drawn from the fathers and above all from his teacher's own writings: before everything, to avoid quarrels and disputes, and to keep love in Christ and peace of spirit. He forbade the tonsuring of the young and beardless in that desert and the bringing in of women; a monk who left it lost his right to his cell, and could take it again only with the consent of the superior and the brethren. The church to come he asked to have consecrated to the Forerunner, in memory of the third finding of his head, because the Forerunner is the teacher of all monks and hermits. He died on 19 March 1521 and was buried, as he had asked, in a corner of the monastery by the marsh, under a stone cut with the year, the month and the day.
