# Agathon of the Desert

saint.gr says Agathon was one of the learned and much-experienced ascetics of the desert whose wise sayings are in the Gerontikon, and that he died in peace. It gives him no year, no country and no monastery, and instead tells two of the sayings, which is all the page has of his life.

The first: some men who had heard that he had great discernment wanted to test whether he would grow angry, and said to him, "Are you Agathon? We hear that you are a fornicator and a proud man." He said, "Yes, that is the truth." Again they said, "Are you Agathon the chatterer and the slanderer?" He answered, "Yes, I am." Again they said, "Are you Agathon the heretic?" and he answered, "I am not a heretic." They begged him to tell them why he had accepted the other insults and not borne this one; and the elder answered that he had accepted those because they were a profit to his soul, but that heretic means separation from God, and for that reason he would not accept it. Hearing this they marvelled at his discernment and went away the better for it.

The second the page tells more briefly. One of the fathers of the desert called out one day to Agathon, who was still a young monk, "Abba." Another who heard it asked him: have you made him an abba already? I did not make him one, he answered, but the manner of his life did.

The Serbian church keeps him on the second of March, and the Ohrid Prologue is where everything this folder knows of his life outside the sayings comes from. It calls him a great Egyptian ascetic of the fifth century and a disciple of [Lot](/saints/lot-22-october), and says that he took care to fulfil all the commandments of God. One of the brethren praised a little knife of his, with which he cut the withies for his baskets; hearing the praise, the saint handed that brother the knife as a gift, and did it with joy.

The Prologue gives him one saying of his own and then asks its own question about it. Agathon said that it would be a satisfaction to him if he could take to himself the body of some leper and give the leper his own body in its place; and the entry ends by asking whether that is not perfect love. It names no monastery and no city, and gives no year — only the century, the country and the name of his teacher.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 2 March, the day the Охридски пролог gives him and not the 8 January of the Greek, and its life is far the fullest of the three: «Прп. Агафона Египетского (V).» He struggled in the desert of Scetis in Egypt, a contemporary of the great Macarius whom the same calendar keeps on 19 января, and was marked by an extraordinary humility, holding himself the most sinful of men.

Monks once came a long way to him for a spiritual conversation and asked, Are you father Agathon? You see before you a sinful servant of God, he answered. There is a rumour, they went on, that you are a proud man and intemperate. Perfectly true, he agreed. We have heard too that you are a liar and like to sit in judgement on others. That is true as well, he confirmed. Besides that, they said, they say you are a heretic — and met a contradiction at once: No, wrongly; I am not a heretic.

Asked why, having taken the other vices on himself, he refused the last, he explained that those vices one cannot but ascribe to oneself, since it is proper to every man to err and we are all, by our spoilt nature, drawn unwillingly into them, whereas heresy is apostasy from God, a wilful renunciation of the truth of God. Asked which labours matter more in the business of salvation, the outward or the inward, he said that a man is like a tree: the outward or bodily occupation brings leaves, and the work of the soul grows fruit; and since Scripture says that every tree that does not bring forth good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire, the fruit is plainly what wants the more attention.

But the tree has need of its leaves too, he added, to keep the sap alive by them and to have their shade defend the tree and its fruit from the withering heat. He departed about the year 435. Three days before the end he sat silent and gathered in, as though looking at something, and when the monks asked he said he saw himself at the Judgement before Christ. And do even you, father, fear the judgement? they asked. I have kept the Lord’s laws as I could, he said, but as a man can I be sure that my work pleases God? Do you not hope in the good works you have done to please God? I do not hope, until I see God: the judgement of men is one thing and the judgement of God another. And saying it he departed to the Lord.

The year is the gain here, and it is this calendar’s alone: the day’s line closes «(V)», the fifth century, which is the century the Охридски пролог gave and the folder already records as a floruit, and the life closes «Преподобный Агафон преставился около 435 года». That is entered as an approximate death inside the century already held, and the floruit is left as it stood. The day prints no hymn under his name.
