# Simeon Metaphrastes

Simeon was born at Constantinople in the time of the emperor Leo the Wise, the year 886, as the Romanian life puts it in one clause. For his good works and his wisdom he was raised to the rank of magistros and logothete, and stood in great honour with the emperor.

When the Arabs went into Crete and plundered many towns and villages, the emperor sent the general Himerios together with Simeon as envoys to them, giving them full power either to subject them willingly or by arms — as, the life notes, Metaphrastes himself says in the life of [Saint Theoctista](/saints/theoctista-of-lesbos), who is kept on the same day.

Though the emperor loved and honoured him for his wisdom, his virtue and his courage, Simeon had no inclination to war, but thought to leave the affairs of the world and become a monk. So he asked the emperor that if he came back victorious from the war he should be given a gift, and the emperor promised it. In Crete they persuaded the Arabs to submit and to pay tribute to the emperor; and returning to Constantinople he asked that his desire be granted according to the promise. The emperor was grieved, unable to part with so wise and brave a man, but would not break his given word, and embraced and kissed him with tears, saying: go, my son, with the mercy of God, and pray to Him for my sins too.

After he had become a monk he wrote the Lives of the Saints, as many as could be found. Then, being rich and having power and standing, he sent men into different places and they brought him all the lives of saints they could find; and he set them out in sweeter words, for which he is called the Metaphrast. That is where the Romanian notice ends: it gives no year of his death, and his page carries no Tropar tab, so the folder has no hymn.
