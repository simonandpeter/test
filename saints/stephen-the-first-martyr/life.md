# Stephen the First Martyr

After our Lord Jesus Christ had ascended into heaven and had sent the Holy Spirit in tongues of fire, and the first Church began to multiply, the Greek-speaking Jews of the dispersion complained against the Jews of Jerusalem that their widows were overlooked in the daily service — the poorest work being ordered for them, and the smaller and last part of the food and the clothing given them. Then the twelve holy apostles gathered the whole Church of that time and said: it is not fitting for us to leave the word of God and to serve tables; look out therefore, brethren, seven men of good report among you, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom, whom we shall set over this service, while we continue in prayer and in the preaching of the word.

This word of the apostles pleased the whole body of the faithful, and so they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit, and Philip, [Prochorus](/saints/prochorus-the-deacon), [Nicanor](/saints/nicanor-the-deacon), [Timon](/saints/timon-the-deacon), [Parmenas](/saints/parmenas-the-deacon) and Nicolae of Antioch, whose names show that they were not of the race of the Jews of Jerusalem but of those who lived in the Greek parts, since their names are not Hebrew but Greek. Stephen himself was a kinsman of Saul, who was called later to the faith and to the apostolate and named [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), and who was from the city of Tarsus. These were brought before the apostles, who prayed and laid their hands on them and made them deacons.

Being full of faith and of power, Stephen was the first among them, and for that reason was called archdeacon; and he did great signs and wonders among the people, which Scripture does not recount — as it does not recount the deeds of Christ the Lord himself, for if they had been written one by one the books would not have been contained in the whole world. But we shall say without doubt that Saint Stephen, being like the apostles, laid his hands on the sick and made them whole; and he was a man mighty in deed and in word.

There was in Jerusalem the assembly of the Cilicians, of the Alexandrians and of the Cyrenians, and the assembly of the Libertines — a people among the Jews who traced their descent from those Jews once taken captive by Pompey of Rome and afterwards given their freedom. Unable to say anything against the truth, which was brighter than the sun, these were kindled with anger, and being seized by envy they hired men who loved a lie to say in the great Jewish synagogue that they had heard Stephen speak words of blasphemy.

Stephen stood in the midst of that murderous assembly like an angel of God, shining with the light of the divine grace, as Moses was once glorified with the shining of his face; and all who sat in the assembly, looking at him, saw his face as the face of an angel. Overthrowing the false witness that he had blasphemed against the holy place, he said that Solomon had built him a house — as though to say that he was not ignorant of the holy place appointed by the good pleasure of God through the wise king Solomon.

Then, filled with divine zeal as Elijah was once, he brought a word of rebuke against them: you stiff-necked, uncircumcised in heart and in ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit; as your fathers were, so are you. These words of the saint moved the high priest, the scribes and all that hardened people of the Jews to unspeakable anger, so that hearing them they were furious in their hearts and gnashed their teeth at him; but he took no notice of their anger, for he was full of the Holy Spirit.

He did not hide what he saw. It is the way of the saints not to show others the revelations made to them, for their humility's sake; but Stephen made that most glorious revelation plain, and he did it to strengthen the faithful in the faith. So the first martyr does not keep silence but tells what he sees, as though calling others after him to the same martyr's crown. And the envious Jews, who had learned to kill the prophets and had dared even against the Lord, the fulfiller of the law and the prophets, dragged him out to die.

He was killed in the valley of Josaphat, which lies between Olivet and Jerusalem by the brook of the Cedars, where there was a multitude of stones along the bank; and the most pure Virgin stood far off upon a rock with Saint [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), looking down from above and praying to the Lord, her Son, to strengthen him in his endurance and to receive his soul into his hands. The Lord looked on his sufferings from the heights of heaven, and his most sweet Mother from the earthly hills, together with the beloved disciple.

A great rain of stones flew at him, and he was reddened all over with his own blood; and as his body weakened and he was loosed from the bonds of nature, his heart ached for those who were killing him, and he prayed more earnestly for them than for himself. For himself he said, «Lord Jesus, receive my soul»; and for them, bending his knees and crying with a loud voice, «Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge». And saying this he fell asleep.

So the good contender was finished, and the first martyr was crowned with red roses by the bleeding of the stones, and went into the heaven he had seen opened, to the Lord and King of glory, to reign with him in the kingdom that has no end. He had been chosen archdeacon by the holy apostles immediately after the descent of the Holy Spirit, and suffered in the same year after the Ascension of the Lord, on the twenty-seventh day of the month of December, being more than thirty years old from his birth. He was beautiful in face, and more beautiful in soul.

His holy body was thrown out to be eaten by the beasts and the birds and lay unburied a day and a night. On the second night Gamaliel, that renowned teacher of the Jewish law in Jerusalem who afterwards believed in Christ together with his son Avelvie, sent honourable and faithful men, who took the relics of the saint secretly and carried them to a village of his some twenty stadia from Jerusalem, called Cafargamala, and buried them there with honour, making a great lamentation over him. Many years afterwards the pious Eudokia, wife of the emperor Theodosius the Younger, coming to Jerusalem, built a most beautiful church at the place where Stephen had been stoned and the earth reddened with his honoured blood.

doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Apostol, Întâiul Mucenic și Arhidiacon Ștefan» and gives no year. The life it links is long, and its middle — his disputes in the synagogues and the whole of his speech before the council — was read in summary only.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 27 December. saint.gr calls him one of the most distinguished among the seven deacons whom the first Christians chose to oversee the common tables of the brethren, so that no mistakes should be made, and whom the Apostles ordained; and though the charge of so many brethren was wearying, it says, Stephen still found the time and the strength to preach the Gospel of Christ.

For his service and his virtues he was honoured with the gift of wonderworking, and with his deep theological grounding he easily overturned what the Jews taught falsely about the work of Christ, which is what drew their anger and their envy upon him. His defence before the Sanhedrin the page calls a pattern of daring and courage: from a man on trial he rose up a stormy accuser, and they dragged him outside the city and killed him with stones. There his great readiness to forgive his enemies appeared in the words «Κύριε, μὴ στήσης αὐτοῖς τὴν ἁμαρτίαν ταύτην» — Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge. The town of Skydra holds him patron.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps the finding and translation of his relics on the Julian 2 August, and tells it at length. When he had been stoned, the body was left in the open below the city to be eaten by dogs; but the Providence of God willed otherwise. It lay there a night and two days, and on the second night Gamaliel, the teacher of Paul and a secret disciple of Christ, came and took it away to his own estate at Kaphargamala and buried it honourably in a cave. In the same cave Gamaliel afterwards buried his friend Nicodemus, who died weeping over that grave; and his own baptised son Abibus; and by his own will he was buried there himself.

Centuries passed, the entry says, and no living man knew where the body lay. Then in the year 415, in the time of the patriarch of Jerusalem, John, Gamaliel appeared three times in a dream to Lucian, the priest of Kaphargamala, told him everything about the burials and pointed out the exact place of the forgotten graves. Shaken by the dream, Lucian told the patriarch, and with his blessing went out with men and dug open four graves; Gamaliel had already told him in the dream which grave was whose. A strong sweet fragrance from the holy relics filled the whole cave.

The relics were then carried solemnly to Sion and buried there honourably, while the others were taken out to a hill above the cave and laid in a church there. Many healings of the sick happened that day from the relics, and later they were carried to Constantinople. So, the entry closes, the Lord crowned with much glory the one who first shed his blood for His name. The 415 is the year of the dream and the digging, and the book gives no year for anything else here.

The same Охридски пролог heads 27. децембар ст. ст. with him — „Свети првомученик Стефан, архиђакон.“ — and gives him the fullest life on the page, which is his passion, where its 2. август entry keeps the finding and the translation of his relics. He was a kinsman of the apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), it says, and a Jew of those Jews who lived in the Greek regions. Stephen was the first of the seven deacons whom the holy apostles ordained and set to the service of helping the poor in Jerusalem, and that is why he was called archdeacon.

By the power of his faith he worked great wonders among men. The malicious Jews disputed with him and were always beaten by his wisdom and by the power of the Spirit acting through him; so, put to shame and used to slanders, they stirred up the people and the elders of the people against the innocent Stephen, accusing him of blaspheming God and Moses, and quickly found false witnesses to confirm it. Then Stephen stood before the people, and all saw his face „као лице ангела“ — as the face of an angel, which the book cites from Acts 6:15: lit up with the light of grace as Moses’ face once was when he had spoken with God.

He opened his mouth and set out the many benefits and wonders God had done for the people of Israel in time past, and the many crimes and resistings of God on that people’s part; and he reproved them above all for the killing of Christ the Lord, calling them „издајницима и крвницима“, betrayers and murderers. And while they ground their teeth, Stephen looked and saw heaven opened and the glory of God, and told the Jews what he saw: „Ево видим небеса отворена и Сина Човечјега гдје стоји с десне стране Бога“ — behold, I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God.

Then the spiteful men led him outside the city and killed him with stones. Among his tormentors, the book says, was his own kinsman Saul, afterwards the apostle Paul. And at that time the Most Holy Mother of God was standing at a distance upon a rock with Saint [John](/saints/john-the-theologian) the Theologian, watching the martyrdom of this first martyr for the truth of her Son and God, and praying to God for Stephen. It happened, the entry says, a year after the descent of the Holy Spirit upon the apostles — a distance from an undated event rather than a year, and it is recorded here and written in no field. The body was taken secretly and buried on his own estate by Гамалил, a Jewish prince and a secret Christian; so gloriously did this firstborn among the Christian martyrs end, and pass over into the kingdom of Christ God.
