# Ignatius of Constantinople

Ignatius, patriarch of Constantinople, was the son of the emperor Michael Rangabe and the empress Procopia, the brother of Theophilus and the nephew of the emperor Nicephorus. The whole of what doxologia.ro prints about him is a short notice, and it is almost entirely about how he was put into his see and taken out of it again.

Being a eunuch, he became a monk, and rose to be abbot of the monastery of the Archangel, which was then called Anatelon and is now called Satyrus. Afterwards he was made patriarch of Constantinople, and guided the Church eleven years and five months.

Then he was put out by the emperor Michael, the son of Theophilus, who set [Photius](/saints/photius-of-constantinople) the protospatharios and protasekretis in his place, having first had him made a monk. Photius in turn was put out by the emperor Basil the Macedonian, who restored Ignatius and kept him ten years; and then he was put out again, and Stephen, the son of the emperor Basil, was made patriarch. Ignatius went to the monastery of Satyrus and died there in peace, having lived seventy-nine years in all.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 23 October, and saint.gr tells his story as a story of a steady character. He was the youngest son of the emperor Michael I Rangabe and of Prokopia, daughter of the emperor Nikephoros I, and was born in 799; when Leo V took the throne he shut Ignatius up in a monastery, and the king's son took gladly to the life of piety and virtue, was freed of the life of courts and emperors, and was made a monk, distinguishing himself as abbot of the monastery of the Archangel which was then called τοῦ Ἀνατέλλοντος and is now called τοῦ Σατύρου. In 846, under the empress Theodora, he succeeded the dead patriarch Methodius I. He showed that character twice over: when Theodora's brother Bardas led her son the emperor Michael III to force his mother and sisters to become nuns, Ignatius refused to perform so compulsory a tonsure, knowing well what he risked; and he also refused Bardas himself holy communion, because he was living with his own daughter-in-law. On 23 November 857 Bardas took his revenge and denounced the patriarch for conspiring against the emperor, so that he was exiled to the island of Terebinthos. When Basil the Macedonian came to power Ignatius returned to the patriarchal throne, on 13 November 867; he died in 878, and was succeeded by [Photius](/saints/photius-of-constantinople) the Great.
