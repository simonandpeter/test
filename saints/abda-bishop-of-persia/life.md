# Abda (Abdias)

Abda — Abdias or Abidas, as the calendars variously spell him — suffered in
Persia in the reign of the shah Yazdegerd I for refusing to renounce Christ
and worship the sun and fire instead. He died after many tortures, and the
life the Orthodox Church in America prints for the day says that to his last
breath he gave thanks to God for letting his chosen one die for his holy name.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 5 September (Julian), a martyr in Persia of
the fifth century, and prints no life of its own. The Greek synaxarion keeps
Abdaios, bishop of Persia, on the same day with a verse — the martyr Abdaios
bears the thorns of the rods, honouring the Master crowned with thorns — and
sends the reader on to 31 March, where his memory is repeated as Saint Abdas.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 31 March, the day the Greek synaxarion sends its reader to, and prints a fuller account of the occasion. In the reign of Theodosius the Younger, Yazdegerd, emperor of the Persians, made war on the churches, and took his occasion from this: Abda, a bishop adorned with many kinds of virtue and driven by divine zeal, destroyed the Pyreion, the temple in which the Persians worshipped fire. Learning of it from the magi, the emperor had him brought, asked him gently why he had done it and ordered him to build the temple again; and when Abda answered that he could by no means do such a thing, Yazdegerd threatened to destroy all the churches and began his threats by ordering Abda killed first. Hearing it, the saint rejoiced greatly, and so, rejoicing and glad, received his end.

The same notice says that Abda the bishop, [Benjamin the deacon](/saints/benjamin-the-deacon), nine martyrs with them and many other saints who confessed in Persia were thrown into prison and eaten alive by the mice and the cats shut in with them; and that thirty years afterwards the magi loosed the storm of persecution on the faithful again.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and gives him a see the other calendars do not: it heads him bishop of the city of Susa. Its whole account of him is one sentence — he was beheaded for Christ in the year 418, in Persia, by the king Izdegerd — and then it turns to his deacon, Saint [Benjamin](/saints/benjamin-the-deacon), who was let go by the torturers on the condition that he preach the Gospel no more. Benjamin agreed to that condition at first, but could not hold his heart to it, and went on spreading the truth of Christ among the people; for which he was seized and killed three years after Saint Abda, in the year 421. The year 418 falls inside the fifth century this folder already holds and is narrower than it, so the interval was not narrowed; and the see stands recorded here rather than written into this folder's office, which the other calendars gave. The nine leading Christians the Greek calendar puts to death with him are not mentioned on this page at all.
