# Ananias the Persian

Ananias came of the city of Arbela in Persia, saint.gr says, and he was one among those saints who glorified the soil of Persia by their virtues and their heroism for the faith. He was arrested because he was trying to enlighten his own countrymen in the true faith of Christ. He was tortured hard, but he did not bend.

At the moment when he was giving up his soul to the Lord he said these words: I see a ladder stretching as far as heaven, and young men with light-bearing faces standing upon it, who say to me, come with us, come and we will carry you to a city full of light and of unspeakable gladness. As soon as he had uttered them, the page says, the martyr breathed his last.

That is the whole of it. No year, no shah, no judge, no manner of death beyond the torture, and no burial; and no hymn. The page gives his couplet, which says that Ananias was flesh, and yet took not the least sensation of the torments of the flesh, as flesh would; and it reproduces a miniature of his martyrdom from the Menologion of the emperor Basil II.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the Julian 1 December and prints one line and no life: «Мч. Анании Персянина.», with no year beside it where it gives years to the two saints above him on the same day. Its name link calls him Ananias the Persian and nothing further. So this calendar adds the day and the Slavonic form of his name and not one fact about him, and the Greek page remains the whole of what is known here. The day page carries hymns for two of the saints it heads and none under his name.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 1 Δεκεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/12/01/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3005/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 1 декабря ст. ст. — [the Russian day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261201.html), read 7 October 2026.*
