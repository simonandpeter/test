# Euelpistus

saint.gr's calendar for 1 Ιουνίου prints eight names in one line — [Justus](/saints/justus-1-june), [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), [Charito](/saints/charito) the virgin, Euelpistus, [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june), [Paion](/saints/paion) and [Liberianus](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin), whom the page says others call Valerianus — and the life behind the line is one paragraph. These, it says, received the martyrs' crowns together with [Justin the philosopher and apologist](/saints/justin-the-philosopher), whom the same calendar keeps on the same day, in the persecution against the Christians under Marcus Aurelius.

They belonged to the brave phalanx of the Christians of Rome, where only genuine Christian courage could keep a man from being dismayed by the power of the idol-worship that the palace and the army upheld. Having confessed Christ repeatedly, they were condemned to die by beheading; and all of them underwent that martyrdom untroubled and joyful, in the conviction that the immortal goods of heaven awaited them, which the heavenly Father gives to all who have lived on earth in faith and love.

That paragraph is the whole of the account, and it is shared by every folder in the company. Beside it the page prints a short epigram over each name, and the one over Euelpistus turns on his name, which means good hope: Euelpistus is fittingly of good hope, since beheaded he will have God for his inheritance. Nothing else about him is on the page — no age, no trade, no parentage and no year — and it prints no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the whole company in one heading of eight names: [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), [Charito](/saints/charito), Euelpistus, [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june), [Paeon](/saints/paion), [Valerian](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin) and [Justus](/saints/justus-1-june). All of them suffered at Rome, it says, in the time of Marcus Aurelius and of Rusticus the eparch. When Rusticus asked whether they thought that if they died for Christ they would receive a reward in heaven, Justin answered for them all: we do not think, we know. After that they were all beheaded, in the year 163, and passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ God.

He is named fourth. This book has nothing of the slave of the emperor set free by Christ, nor of the parents in Cappadocia, which the Romanian life gives — it narrates no trial beyond the one question and the one answer. What it does is keep him at all, where the Romanian calendar prints no line for him, and give him a year: the 163 it states of the whole company is recorded here as the death.
