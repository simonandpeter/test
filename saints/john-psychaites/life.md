# John Psychaites

saint.gr sets him in the years of the hard struggle for the holy icons, and says nothing narrower than that. He was most austere in his life, it says, and had a formidable strength of soul and a great boldness; his very appearance shook his opponents and steadied his friends and those who held the same faith. The iconoclast emperors hunted him down, so that he passed through many hardships, and he overcame them and wore them out; after each persecution he came back more impetuous than before.

He was gifted, the page adds, with the grace of working wonders. Death found him upright in the stadium of the sacred contests, and carried him to the immortal dwellings of the righteous. That is the whole of the notice: no country, no monastery, no emperor named, no year, no relics and no hymn. His epithet is all that distinguishes him, and the page's couplet turns on it — bearing the relation of the soul alone, it says, father, you are justly called Psychaites.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 26 May, not on the 7 May the Greek calendar keeps, and it supplies three of the things that page says it does not have. In his early youth, it says, he withdrew from the world and drew back into the Psychaite lavra at Constantinople — the house his epithet is made of, which the Greek notice never names — and there he struggled many years for the love of Christ.

And in the eighth century, it says, he suffered exile for the veneration of the icons. That is the first date any calendar read for him gives, and it is the date of the exile and not of his death; it is held as a floruit for that reason, and no year of his birth or his repose is stated by anybody. The Prologue names no emperor either.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 26 мая ст. ст. and gives him one short page, which disagrees with itself about when he lived. Its first sentence sets him «в конце VIII - начале IX века», at the end of the eighth century and the beginning of the ninth, while the day’s own line prints «(IX)» and this folder holds the eighth century from the Ohrid Prologue. From his young years he left the world and took monastic vows in the Psychaite lavra, in a suburb of Constantinople, and for his holy life and his saving labours he received from God the gift of driving out demons and of healing sicknesses.

In that time the heresy of the iconoclasts was raging and those who honoured the holy icons were persecuted. He was brought to an interrogation and pressed to renounce the veneration of the holy icons and to write out his renunciation; and instead of a renunciation he rebuked his persecutors, calling the emperor a heretic. For that he was sent into exile, and there he died, having borne much hardship from the iconoclasts.

The emperor the page names is «Льва Исавра (717 - 741)», Leo the Isaurian, and that reign falls in the first half of the eighth century and cannot contain a man the same page says lived at the end of it. No year, no century and no interval has been altered in this folder on the strength of a page that holds three readings at once.
