# Nicholas of Lesvos

Nicholas was a young Greek studying at a university in France when [Raphael](/saints/raphael-of-lesvos) came there on one of his missionary journeys. The life says he was taken by the preaching of the future saint, left the life of the world and followed him, and was ordained deacon not long afterwards. When Constantinople fell in 1453 and the Turks invaded Thrace the year after, the two of them sailed on 14 March 1454 from Alexandroupolis to the island of Lesvos, to the village of Thermi, and settled in the monastery of the Nativity of the Theotokos.

In 1463 the Christians of Lesvos rose against the Turks; the rising was crushed, the monastery at Thermi was suspected of a hand in it and destroyed, and the community was arrested on Great Friday during the service of the Lamentations. Nicholas was beaten and cruelly tortured, and being the frailer of the two he suffered a heart attack and died under it, on the same days as his abbot and [Irene](/saints/irene-of-lesvos) the child.

His relics were found on 13 June 1960 on the hill of Karyes above Thermi, a year to the day before the spring of holy water was uncovered there; they now lie in the lower church of the monastery built on the site, beside Irene's. The troparion the Romanian calendar sings calls him «cinstea preoților... odorul diaconiei» — the honour of priests, the jewel of the diaconate.
