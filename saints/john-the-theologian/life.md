# John the Theologian

John - the God-worded, the son of Zebedee and of Salome the daughter of Joseph the Betrothed - was called from his fishing nets to the preaching of the Gospel: Christ, walking by the sea of Galilee, saw James and John in the boat with their father mending their nets, and they left the boat and their father at once and followed Him. He named him a son of thunder, for like thunder he would be heard through the whole world; and He loved him for his perfect guilelessness and the purity of his virginity, taking him - with Peter and James - to the raising of Jairus's daughter, to Tabor, and into Gethsemane. At the mystical supper John lay on the breast of his Teacher to ask who the betrayer was; at the Passion, when all fled, he alone stood by the Cross, and heard the Lord give His Mother into his care - and he served her, the life says, until her own honoured falling-asleep, carrying before her bier the shining branch Gabriel had brought her.

Then he went with his disciple Prochorus into Asia. The life the Romanian synaxarion keeps is a whole book of what followed: the shipwreck from which the sea returned him alive after fourteen days, the bath-house at Ephesus and the tyrannous mistress it belonged to, idols falling to dust in houses that received him, poison drunk without harm, and the exile to Patmos - where he received and wrote down the unspeakable revelations of God, the Apocalypse, and where his Gospel's thunder began. Released, he returned to Ephesus and the house of Domnos.

When all his years were a hundred and some, he went out with seven disciples, had them dig a grave in the shape of the Cross to the measure of his own height, kissed them - "draw the earth, my mother, cover me" - and was covered by degrees, to the knees, to the neck, and wholly. The brethren of the city came and dug the grave open, and found nothing. Each year, on the eighth of May, a fine dust would rise from the tomb, and healings were given at his prayer. How many years he lived, the life says plainly, is not known exactly - some write a hundred and twenty, some a hundred and five and seven months; more than a hundred is certain.
