# John the Theologian

John - the God-worded, the son of Zebedee and of Salome the daughter of Joseph the Betrothed - was called from his fishing nets to the preaching of the Gospel: Christ, walking by the sea of Galilee, saw James and John in the boat with their father mending their nets, and they left the boat and their father at once and followed Him. He named him a son of thunder, for like thunder he would be heard through the whole world; and He loved him for his perfect guilelessness and the purity of his virginity, taking him - with Peter and James - to the raising of Jairus's daughter, to Tabor, and into Gethsemane.

At the mystical supper John lay on the breast of his Teacher to ask who the betrayer was; at the Passion, when all fled, he alone stood by the Cross, and heard the Lord give His Mother into his care - and he served her, the life says, until her own honoured falling-asleep, carrying before her bier the shining branch Gabriel had brought her.

Then he went with his disciple Prochorus into Asia. The life the Romanian synaxarion keeps is a whole book of what followed: the shipwreck from which the sea returned him alive after fourteen days, the bath-house at Ephesus and the tyrannous mistress it belonged to, idols falling to dust in houses that received him, poison drunk without harm, and the exile to Patmos - where he received and wrote down the unspeakable revelations of God, the Apocalypse, and where his Gospel's thunder began. Released, he returned to Ephesus and the house of Domnos.

When all his years were a hundred and some, he went out with seven disciples, had them dig a grave in the shape of the Cross to the measure of his own height, kissed them - "draw the earth, my mother, cover me" - and was covered by degrees, to the knees, to the neck, and wholly. The brethren of the city came and dug the grave open, and found nothing. Each year, on the eighth of May, a fine dust would rise from the tomb, and healings were given at his prayer. How many years he lived, the life says plainly, is not known exactly - some write a hundred and twenty, some a hundred and five and seven months; more than a hundred is certain.

The Greek synaxarion keeps his translation on 26 September. Many, it says, held that John did not die but was taken into the other life as Enoch and Elijah were, because of the Gospel’s words in John 21:22, though the next verse makes the matter plain. The tradition the Church has received is this: John died in deep old age at Ephesus and was buried outside the city, and when his disciples visited the grave some days later they found it empty. So the Church holds that what happened to the Theotokos happened to the beloved disciple too: he died and was buried, and after three days rose and passed into eternal life. The Greek calendar keeps him with an apolytikion and a kontakion.

doxologia.ro's Romanian calendar keeps him a second time, on 8 mai, and says plainly that this day is not his repose — that is written at length under 26 septembrie — but the commemoration of the healing dust which by a wonder comes out of his tomb every year on the eighth day of May, and which the Christians there call manna. Living more than a hundred years and labouring in the preaching of Christ, the account says, he went out of the city of Ephesus with seven of his disciples and ordered them to dig him a grave in the shape of a cross and to bury him in it alive; when the brothers in the city heard of it they came and opened the grave and did not find the apostle's body, and returned weeping for him. Afterwards they came often to the grave to make their prayers, and each year in this month and on this day a fine wonder-working dust began to come out of it, which the faithful took and healed with it the infirmities and the sufferings among the people.

The Russian calendar keeps the same 26 September and calls it his «Преставление» — his falling asleep — and dates it «начало II», the beginning of the second century, where it dates nothing else on the page so loosely.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him first of the two it enumerates on this day, and it is the only one of the four calendars here to say how he came to Patmos. He laboured most of all at Ephesus, and his inspired preaching and his wonders shook the old worship from its foundations; the pagans, embittered, bound him and sent him to Rome to the emperor Domitian. There he was tortured and beaten, and when neither the fiercest poison they gave him to drink nor the boiling oil they threw him into harmed him, the emperor took fright, reckoned him immortal, and sent him into exile on Patmos.

On the island, the Prologue says, he brought many over to Christ by his words and his miracles and set the church of God firmly there, and it was there that he wrote his Gospel and the Revelation. In the time of the emperor Nerva, who gave liberty to all prisoners, he made his way back to Ephesus and spent some time confirming the work he had begun there. He was over a hundred years old when he departed to the Lord; when his disciples afterwards opened the grave they did not find his body, but every year on the eighth of May a fine dust, fragrant and healing, came out of it.

The day's reflection in the Prologue repeats a story of him that it takes from Clement of Alexandria. In some place in Asia Minor he had baptised a pagan youth and left him in the care of the local bishop while he went further on to preach. In his absence the young man went to ruin — drink, then theft, and at last a band of robbers who attacked travellers out of the forest and plundered them.

When John returned and heard from the bishop what had become of him he lost no time, found a horse and a guide, rode to that forest and came out before the chief of the robbers. The youth recognised him and fled; the old man ran after him and, for all his years, overtook him. He fell at the apostle's feet, too ashamed to look him in the face, and John embraced and kissed him as a shepherd does a lost sheep that has been found, and brought him back to the city and confirmed him again in the faith and in a life of virtue.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps him on 26 September, as this corpus does, and gives the Julian 8 May to **the wonder shown from his grave**, which it tells in four sentences. When John was more than a hundred years old, it says, he took seven of his disciples, went out of Ephesus, and ordered them to dig a grave in the form of a cross. Then the old man went down living into that grave, and was buried.

When the faithful later opened John's grave they did not find the body in it. And on the eighth of May — the twenty-first by the new reckoning, the book writes it as 21/8 — a dust used to rise every year from his grave, and from that dust the sick were healed of various diseases. **Neither the grave in the shape of a cross, nor the seven disciples, nor the yearly dust is in the Russian, Romanian or Greek account this folder was built from.**

**No date was taken and no row was moved.** The entry prints no year; the book's own first sentence sends the reader back to 26 September for his memory, so the Serbian feast stays on that day and the May wonder is recorded as a note on it, which is the one place this schema has for a second day.
