# Montanus of Singidunum

The edicts of persecution at the beginning of the fourth century ordered the Christians' places of worship pulled down, their assemblies forbidden, their holy books burnt and their archives destroyed, the loss of the goods and of the public offices the faithful held, exile, and hard punishment for clergy who would not apostatise. The fourth edict, of January–February 304, wrung from Diocletian by Maximian Galerius, was harsher still than the three before it, for it copied the edict of the emperor Decius of 249, which had declared general war on Christianity.

These measures were applied most strictly in the eastern parts and in the Danubian provinces of the Roman Empire, where Diocletian reigned from Nicomedia in Bithynia and his son-in-law Galerius from Sirmium in Pannonia Inferior; and so they were applied also in the city of Singidunum, today Belgrade, on the Danube. There, on 26 March 304, the Daco-Roman priest Montanus and his wife [Maxima](/saints/maxima-wife-of-montanus) suffered for the faith in Christ.

Seeing the head of Singidunum begin the persecution of Christ's servants, Montanus remembered the word «if they persecute you in this city, flee into the other», and went from Singidunum to Sirmium, the seat of the prefecture in which he lived. There the persecutors seized him and brought him before Probus, governor of Pannonia Inferior, who asked him his name, whether he was a Christian, and what his occupation was; and he answered boldly that he was a Christian and a priest of the Church of Christ in Singidunum.

Probus urged him to obey the emperors and sacrifice to the gods. Montanus answered that whoever honours the gods and not God will be torn up by the root; and when the governor said the emperors had commanded that he either sacrifice or be put to the torments, he said he had been taught to endure torments rather than deny God and sacrifice to demons, and that he would rejoice to be made a sharer in the Passion of his Lord. Under the torture Probus asked him whether he would sacrifice now, and he answered serenely that by these torments he was offering himself to the God to whom he had always sacrificed.

Seeing his endurance, Probus called in his wife, thinking that a woman, being weaker by nature, would be softened by her husband's torments and would urge him to sacrifice; but to the pagan's astonishment Maxima asked to be taken to the torments herself, to be made a sharer in the Saviour's Passion. «You Christians are mad, you set no value on life», said the governor; and Montanus answered with the Apostle Paul that God chose the foolish things of the world to shame the wise.

Probus pressed him again to sacrifice and be spared worse, and Montanus told him to do as he wished and see what patience the Lord Jesus Christ would give him against his snares. Then the governor gave sentence that Montanus and Maxima be thrown into the river, as having shown themselves disobedient to the imperial commands; and the two received the sentence of death as though it were a priceless gift, and gave thanks to God for finding them worthy of the everlasting glory.

When the servants of the demons had brought them to the bank of the Sava at its mouth on the Danube and had tied a stone to each of their necks, Montanus lifted his hands and prayed more fervently still: that Christ, who offered Himself for the salvation of the world, receive the souls of His servants Montanus and Maxima; that He increase and defend the faithful flock of His Church in Singidunum and through all the valleys of the Danube; and that with the eyes of his mind he saw a new people rising in that quarter of the earth, calling on His holy name in the churches, in the Roman tongue.

So they drowned the two fearless children of the Church in the Sava. The waves drew their honoured relics to the banks, and the faithful took them up in secret and kept their bodies carefully, as treasures of great price. Their memory is kept, after the Christian order, on the day of their passing into the world of the righteous. doxologia.ro's life cites for them the studies of Fr Nicolae M. Popescu (1942) and Fr I. Rămureanu (1982), who made him out a Daco-Roman priest.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and adds the frame of the trial. saint.gr puts the martyrdom in 304 under the emperor Diocletian: when the persecution against the Christians began, the presbyter Montanus left Singidunum for Sirmium, and there he was seized and brought before Probus, the governor of Lower Pannonia, who ordered him to sacrifice to the idols. The priest of the Most High God answered him, «Κάνε ότι θέλεις και θα δεις τι υπομονή θα μου χαρίσει ο Κύριος και Θεός μου» — do what you will, and you will see what patience my Lord and God will give me. Seeing the steadfastness of Montanus and of [Maxima](/saints/maxima-wife-of-montanus), the governor ordered them thrown into the river Savus; the executioners tied a stone to the neck of each of them, and the priest asked leave to pray a little first, and lifting his hands to heaven begged his Lord to receive their souls and to protect his people. Christians afterwards took the holy relics from the river bank and buried them with honour and reverence.
