# Olga of Alaska

Olga, whose Yup'ik name was Arrsamquq, was born on 3 February 1916 in the village of Kwethluk in Alaska, into a Yup'ik family. Russians lived in the community as well, doxologia's life says, and their faith spread among the native people and many were converted to Orthodoxy; Arrsamquq was among the first, and took the baptismal name Olga. She loved God from childhood, and from childhood prayed not only for her own family but for everybody in the village on the bank of the Kuskokwim.

When the time came she married a man of the village chosen by her parents. He was enterprising — he opened a general store in Kwethluk and the first post office the place had ever had — but he was not close to the Church, and things between husband and wife were very strained. Olga did not lose heart. She prayed a great deal, and her husband became Orthodox, was baptised with the name Nicolai, studied theology, and from 1963 served as the priest of their village.

He had twelve other villages in his care, scattered across the polar waste along the Kuskokwim, and reaching them by boat, by car or by dog sled took him a long time. Meanwhile Olga kept the house, raised her children, wove priestly vestments, baked prosphora and helped the people of her own community and of the villages where Father Nicolai served. She bore thirteen children, of whom eight lived, and the work of bringing them up fell chiefly on her.

In a sense, the life says, everybody was her child: she knitted stockings and heavy clothes and gave them away, and helped her neighbours with their work — a villager remembered that there was hardly a friend or a neighbour who did not have something Presbytera Olga had made for them. She sent clothes and gifts to parishes hundreds of miles off. She was a midwife and helped many women give birth, and helped others recover from the harm done them by domestic or sexual abuse, or come through hard times.

At the last she fell ill of cancer, and it was found only at its final stage. She carried the suffering with dignity and refused to lie down: weak as she was, she went on working about the house and carrying full pails of water from the village well. She left this world on 8 November 1979, having prayed, confessed and received communion. On 9 November 2023 the Orthodox Church in America canonised Saint Olga (Arrsamquq) Michael, the first woman saint of America.

The Greek calendar keeps her on 8 November, the day she died, where the Romanian keeps her on 27 October. saint.gr calls her Matushka — little mother — Olga, a native of Alaska of the Yup'ik people, born on 3 February 1916, and its account adds a few things the Romanian life does not. Her husband Nikolai Michael was the village postman and manager of the general store, and was later ordained priest and then archpriest, so that she served her community as a priest's wife as well as a midwife. She bore thirteen children of her own, many times without the help of a midwife or of her own people, and eight of them survived. She was known for her sympathy and care towards those who had suffered abuse of every kind, and especially sexual abuse; and though her family was poor she gave open-handedly to those worse off than herself, often giving away her own children's clothes to the destitute. She was known too for being able to tell when a woman was pregnant, even before the woman had missed her period. Many from the surrounding country wanted to come to her funeral, the page says, and because it was November the weather seemed to make it impossible; but from the day of the funeral a wind from the south brought warm weather, thawed the ice and the snow and made the journey to Kwethluk possible, and when the mourners left the church to carry the body to the cemetery a flock of birds followed. Those who dug her grave found that the ground had thawed as well; and on the evening after the funeral the ordinary hard winter came back.
