# Macarius of Zhabyn

Macarius, the wonderworker of Belev, was born in 1539. As a young man he was tonsured with the name Onuphrius, and in 1585 he founded the Zhabyn hermitage of the Presentation near the river Oka, not far from the town of Belev. In 1615 Polish troops under Lisowski laid it waste entirely; he came back to the ashes, gathered the brethren again and restored the monastery, building a stone church of the Presentation of the Most Holy Theotokos with a bell tower over the gate, and the monastery’s synodicon recorded the frost, heat, hunger and thirst he bore. He often went alone into the forest to pray.

Once on a forest path he heard a faint groan and found a Polish soldier who had lost his company slumped against a pine, his sabre beside him, perhaps one of those who had plundered the monastery, begging for water; with a prayer Onuphrius struck the ground with his staff, a cool spring broke out, and he gave the dying man to drink. When the monastery was strong again he gave the brethren into the care of one of his disciples, took the great schema with the name Macarius, and withdrew to the head of the Zhabynka stream, the well called Zhabynets, a verst from the Oka. He died there aged 84 in 1623, at cockcrow, and was buried on 22 January, the feast of the apostle Timothy, opposite the monastery gates. A second, shorter life on the same calendar has him become abbot of the monastery he rebuilt.

His veneration took hold at the end of the seventeenth century and the beginning of the eighteenth, and icons of him were painted; the relics, tradition says, lay open, but were hidden in 1721. The monastery fell desolate in the eighteenth century, and he was so far forgotten that when his incorrupt relics were found in 1816, during the building of a church of Saint Nicholas, a requiem was served for him.

His memory was restored through Abbot Jonah, who was born on 22 January, began his monastic life at Optina and became superior of Zhabyn in 1875; at his petition, backed by the people of Belev, the feast was solemnly kept again on 22 January 1888, and in 1889 a church in Macarius’s name was begun over his grave. When Jonah and the contractor made ready to open the relics on their own authority, the saint appeared to both in a dream and sternly forbade it. The Russian calendar keeps him on 22 January and on 22 September old style, and prints his troparion and kontakion on the second day.
