# Anthony of Leokhnovo

Anthony came of the Tver boyar family of the Veniaminovs, and the Russian calendar's life for 17 October is the only account of him any of the four calendars gives. He lived as a hermit not far from Novgorod, in the Rublevo hermitage on the river Perekhod. About the year 1556 he moved to the hermit Tarasius, who was living beyond Lake Ilmen at Leokhnovo, not far from Staraya Russa, and received the tonsure from him; and so there was built up there a desert house in honour of the Transfiguration of the Lord, afterwards called the Leokhnovsky, or the Ivetsky-Antonievsky, monastery. He lived to a deep old age and acquired the gift of foresight.

In 1611, when the Swedes laid waste the country round Novgorod, he moved into Novgorod at the summons of the metropolitan Isidore. He died on 14 September 1611 at the age of eighty-five, and was buried near the church of the holy evangelist [Luke](/saints/luke-the-evangelist) on the Sophia side of the city. Before his death, in the presence of many, he had said that his body would rest in his own desert house.

His disciple Gregory, having come back to the place of the house the Swedes had ruined and burnt, set up a cell with a chapel and stayed living there. Three times Anthony appeared to him in sleep and said: brother Gregory, go to Novgorod, tell the metropolitan Cyprian and the elders of the city to lay me in the place of my own house. When Gregory had reported it the metropolitan made a procession with the cross to Anthony's grave; the relics were found incorrupt and were taken to the Leokhnovsky house on 13 July 1620, and at the finding a blind man named Joseph received his sight, and many other wonders were done.

The round of commemorations the churches of his name kept is peculiar to him, and the page sets it out. At the church at Leokhnovo, near Staraya Russa, and at the Rublevo hermitage, the second Friday after the feast of the chief apostles [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle) and [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) remembered the finding of his relics and their carrying from Novgorod to the Leokhnovsky monastery; the Ascension of the Lord remembered his coming from the Rublevo hermitage to Leokhnovo; and 17 October is the commemoration of the repose of the saint, who died on the feast of the Exaltation of the honoured Cross at the ninth hour of the evening. At the Rublevo hermitage the consecration of the church of his name was kept as well, on 30 August, in 1873.

So the day the calendar keeps his repose on is not the day it says he died on, and nothing on the page reconciles the two; both stand here as the page gives them. It gives him no year of birth either: the 1525 or 1526 on this folder is arithmetic from the death and the age at it, and is marked as inferred for that reason. His name page keeps him on three days, 11 July, 13 July for the translation of the relics, and 17 October; only the October day was read.
