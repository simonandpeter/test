# Jacob Tsalikis

Jacob Tsalikis was born on 5 November 1920 in the village of Livisi in Asia Minor, to devout Christian parents, Stavros and Theodora, who brought him up in the way of God. In 1922, at the exchange of populations between Greece and Turkey, his family and kindred had to leave, and they were settled in the village of Farakla on the island of Evia, where he lived until he entered monasticism. From his earliest childhood he leaned to holy things, wanted to imitate what the priests did at the services, cared for the little churches round about, and prayed fervently to the saints.

Those who knew him called him *the monk*. The gift of healing came to him while he was still small: by the age of nine the simple and poor people of the place, and most of all the refugees, had made him their refuge, and he read prayers and exorcisms over them, so that they began to call for him whenever something went wrong. Between 1947 and 1949 he did his military service, and coming home he took up the family's burdens, his parents having already departed to the Lord.

In 1951, once he had seen his sister married, he entered the monastery of the Venerable David on Evia, where, doxologia's life says, the saint himself met him. The monastery was in a wretched state and had only three inhabitants; they and the people of the district did everything they could to drive him out, and even threatened to kill him, without success, for he was resolved to stay whatever happened to him, trusting in the providence of God.

In November 1952 he was tonsured a monk, and the month after he was ordained hieromonk. Hard fasting, much labour, the long services and the daily vigil brought him into great bodily weakness, but he would not lessen his struggle in any way; and on top of that the demons came and beat him cruelly and gave him heavy bodily sufferings. After 1970 his spiritual growth went in step with his pains: he felt endless suffering, and what came out of him was inexhaustible. He said himself, *my heart is a garden of paradise*.

He became abbot in 1975, and in the same year was granted at the proskomide to see and to touch the Most Holy Blood of the Saviour. People came to him from all over Evia and from Athens to see him, to hear him and to confess, although he was a very simple man who had only finished primary school. Father Porphyrios said of him that he was one of the greatest seers of the age, and hid himself carefully so as not to be glorified; and [Paisios the Athonite](/saints/paisios-the-athonite) said that he had the gifts of Saint Nectarios.

With the years his bodily strength grew less and less, so that after 1987 he might have died at any moment. He suffered from several diseases of the heart and from thromboses, but his great love for people left him no room to think of his own troubles, and he went on hearing confessions and counselling without a pause. In his last year his body was a ruin, only skin and bone; his face alone stayed clear and lit, seeming almost immaterial, and a divine peace spread about him.

On 21 November 1991, the day of the feast of the Entry of the Mother of God into the Temple, having just heard the confession of a spiritual child, he departed to the heavenly dwellings. Doxologia records two of the many miracles after his repose. On the night of 4–5 April the priest in charge of the church of [John the Russian](/saints/john-the-russian) saw the elder in the church, and the elder asked that John's reliquary be brought so that he might venerate it; it was brought, and he venerated it with gladness, saying prayers whose words gave great joy to those about him.

At length he finished and said: *Take the venerable one.* The priest and those with him bowed to venerate the relics and to lift the reliquary — and saw to their amazement that it was the elder Jacob lying in it, and not the venerable John. And he appeared to a believing woman and told her: *Before, I used to ask the Venerable David to go to God and intercede for people; but now I go myself.*
