# Jonah the Sabaite

Jonah was a presbyter in the year 829, and the father of the confessors Theodore and Theophanes, called the Graptoi, whose faces the iconoclast Theophilus had cut with writing. He went to the Lavra of Saint Sabbas and became a monk, grew in reverence towards God and in every Christian virtue, and reached a great age before he died in peace. The verse says he left the place of an unsteady life and found a place that stands firm.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 22 September old style, a day after the Greek, as the presbyter Jonah, father of Theophanes the maker of canons and Theodore, the Graptoi; its life and the Greek entry name the same father of the same two sons at the same lavra, so this folder takes them for one man. It says he lived in Palestine at the end of the eighth century and the beginning of the ninth, virtuously and pleasing to God, with the two sons whom the Church later glorified for confessing Orthodoxy under the iconoclasts; after his wife’s death he withdrew to the Lavra of Sabbas the Sanctified, where both his sons had already been tonsured, and laboured there until his death in the ninth century, and the Lord gave him the grace of healing.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps him on the same day as the presbyter Jonah, father of Theophanes the writer of canons and Theodore the Graptos, a wonderworker who died in the monastery of Sabbas the Sanctified in the ninth century.
