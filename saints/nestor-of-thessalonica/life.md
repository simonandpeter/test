# Nestor of Thessalonica

The Romanian life doxologia.ro prints belongs to the account of the great martyr Demetrius of Thessalonica. The emperor Maximian, called also Herculius, the friend of Diocletian, came to Thessalonica, shut Demetrius the proconsul in prison for confessing Christ, and gave himself to public games, boasting of his own fighter, a Vandal named Lyaeus whom nobody could beat. Lyaeus was another Goliath: bigger of body than other men, terrible to look at, with the face and the temper of a beast and a voice like a roaring lion, and unclean spirits lodged in him.

The emperor built him a high, wide platform on pillars in the middle of the city, and under it set a mass of spears point upward, so that whoever Lyaeus beat was thrown down on them. Many brave men died there, and Christians were dragged up by force to fight him, while the emperor and his armies looked on with delight. In the city was a young man named Nestor, brave and fair of face, barely bearded, who knew Demetrius and had learned the faith from him; seeing Christians killed without mercy, he burned with anger and resolved to fight.

He ran to Demetrius in the prison, told him how many Lyaeus had killed, and asked his prayers, that he might beat this adversary and lift the reproach from the Christians. Demetrius signed his forehead and his breast with the cross, blessed him, and told him: you will beat Lyaeus, and you will confess Christ. Nestor ran to the place of combat, threw off his clothes before everybody and cried out that he would fight. The emperor, pitying his youth, asked whether he had not seen how many stronger men Lyaeus had beaten; Nestor answered that he was small and weak, but great and unconquerable was the power of his Christ.

Hearing the name of Christ the emperor knew him for a Christian and sent him to the platform in anger, thinking he would die like the rest. Nestor crossed himself and cried aloud, "God of Demetrius, help me!" — and God, who had once strengthened David against Goliath, strengthened him: he caught Lyaeus up like a bird and threw him from the high platform onto the sharpened spears, and the whole people of Thessalonica cried out that the God of Demetrius is great. The emperor went back to his palace in shame and grief for his fighter.

He ordered Nestor seized, and learning that it was Demetrius who had sent him to the fight and foretold his victory, he ordered them both killed. Demetrius was run through with spears; Nestor was beheaded; and the life ends by saying that both received the crowns of victory from Christ.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 27 October, and saint.gr tells the fight itself. Nestor was very young in years, it says, beautiful to look at, and an acquaintance of the holy and glorious [Demetrius](/saints/demetrius-the-myrrhstreamer), whom it cross-refers to 26 October. Seeing that the emperor Diocletian took pleasure in the victories of a hulking barbarian called Lyaios, he hated the man's arrogance; and seeing the wonders of Demetrius he took courage. So he went to the prison where the great martyr was shut up and fell at his feet, and said: servant of God, Demetrius, I am ready to fight Lyaios in single combat, so pray for me in the name of Christ. The saint sealed him with the sign of the precious Cross and told him that he would both beat Lyaios and be martyred for Christ. Then Nestor went into the stadium without fear and cried out, God of Demetrius, help me; and having fought Lyaios he dealt him a strong blow with his knife to the heart and killed him. Diocletian, enraged at that, gave orders and they killed Nestor with a spear, and Demetrius with him; and by this act, the page says, Nestor taught that at every human challenge we should cry out, the Lord is my helper and I shall not fear what man shall do to me.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day and adds nothing to the story except its hymns, which are three and are the most particular thing any of the four calendars gives him. The troparion is the common of a martyr with his name in it. The kontakion is his own, and it says what the Greek and Romanian pages only imply: that he inherited undying glory as an excellent soldier of the Master «молитвами Димитрия мученика», by the prayers of the martyr [Demetrius](/saints/demetrius-the-myrrhstreamer), and it asks the two of them together to go on praying. A second kontakion calls him the unconquerable soldier of piety and the fellow and the armour-bearer of the truth, and bids the Church praise «Нестора великаго» today.

The day page carries no life for him. No life page follows his name on it, and his name page prints four icons, the one day, and these three hymns and nothing else.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 27 October, the day after [Demetrius](/saints/demetrius-the-myrrhstreamer), and tells the same scene from Nestor’s side. He was a youth at Thessalonica in the time of Demetrius’s suffering, and he learned the faith of Christ from Demetrius himself. The emperor Maximian, who fought against Christ, was putting on games and amusements for the people, and his favourite was a Vandal named Lyaeus, of a giant’s height and strength, who called men out to fight him every day and killed them.

This book describes the apparatus. The emperor had a special theatre built for those fights, like a threshing floor raised on pillars, and underneath it spears were planted with their points upward; when Lyaeus beat a man in the wrestling he pushed him off the top of it down onto that whole forest of spear points, and the heathen crowd stood round about with their emperor and enjoyed watching some wretched human being writhe in agony until he died. Many Christians were among Lyaeus’s innocent victims — for whenever nobody came forward of his own will, Christians were dragged to the fight by force, on the emperor’s order.

Nestor’s heart was torn with pain at that heathen entertainment, and he resolved to go out against the giant himself; but first he went to Demetrius in the prison and asked his blessing for it. Demetrius blessed him, signed him with the cross on the forehead and on the breast, and foretold him both halves of what was coming: thou shalt overcome Lyaeus, but thou shalt suffer for Christ. So the young man went out. The emperor was there with a multitude, and they all pitied him and tried to turn him from the fight; Nestor crossed himself and said, God of Demetrius, help me. He overmastered Lyaeus, threw him down and pushed him onto the sharp spears, where the heavy giant quickly found his death, and the whole people shouted that the God of Demetrius is great.

The emperor was shamed before the people and grieved for his favourite, and in his fury against both of them he gave one order for the two: Nestor to be cut down with the sword, Demetrius to be run through with spears. So this glorious champion of Christ ended his young earthly life, the entry says, in the year 306 — which is the year this folder already recorded, with the difference that the Greek page gave it in a bracket and this book gives it in a sentence.
