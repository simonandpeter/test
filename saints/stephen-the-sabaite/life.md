# Stephen the Sabaite

doxologia.ro's life of Stephen is three sentences long, and this is what they say. Stephen, the hymnographer of the monastery of Saint Sabbas, lived the ascetic life at the Lavra of Saint Sabbas in Palestine. He and Andrew the Blind were among the first to compose hymns for the season between the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee and Palm Sunday. He seems not to be the same man as Saint Stephen the Sinaite, whose feast is on 13 July.

The page prints nothing further of him — no place of birth, no year, no account of his death — and nothing here is added to it. The troparion doxologia prints under his name is a common of a teaching father, with his own name set into it, not a proper composed for him.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the same day and gives the century the Romanian page did not: «Прп. Стефана Савваита, творца канонов (IX)», a maker of canons of the ninth century. Its life for him is a single sentence, and all it adds is where he laboured — the lavra of Saint Sabbas in Palestine. His name page lists this day and no other. The day page prints a troparion and a kontakion for him, both his own and both naming him: one praises the abstinence by which he put the passions to death, the other the paradise of virtues he planted and watered with his tears.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 28 October and gives him four sentences, two of which are new to this folder. He was the writer of many beautiful canons. He practised asceticism in the house of the Sanctified — the lavra in Palestine the Russian calendar also names, and the house whose founder gives him the by-name Саваит. Later, this book says, he was a bishop; it gives no see for him, so none is recorded here.

And he reposed peacefully, it ends, in 807. That is the first year of his death this folder has had: the Russian calendar gave only the ninth century, which 807 lies inside, and the century is kept beside the year because this book offers no span of its own. The year is also the point at which this entry touches a question the corpus cannot settle from a calendar, and the question is noted rather than answered here.
