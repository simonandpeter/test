# Ignatius (Dalanov)

Ignatius was born on 12 September 1885 in the village of Kozhemyakino in
the Sebezh district of Vitebsk province to the peasant Michael Dalanov,
and named John at baptism; he had his schooling at the village school. On
6 June 1911 he entered Optina, on 11 September was blessed to wear the
cassock, and from 1914 bore his obedience in the skete of John the
Forerunner; in July 1914 part of the brethren — rasophores and novices —
were called to the front in the war with Germany, among them the novice
John, sent to the 5th Siberian reserve infantry regiment, and on Great
Friday 1917 he was tonsured rasophore. After Optina was closed by the
godless power he was tonsured a monk with the name Ignatius and became
watchman of the Tikhvin church of the village of Strelna near Kozelsk; in
1929 he was ordained hieromonk, and from 1934 served at the Saviour church
of Kurynichi in the Peremyshl district, until the mass persecutions of
1937. In November 1937 the NKVD questioned the usual witnesses — the
secretary of the village soviet, a collective-farm brigadier and a farmer
— who testified as required that the hieromonk had said the communists
held the census only to learn how many had died under Soviet power and how
many believers were left, so as to decide whether to close or reopen the
churches; that preparing for the elections the communists were putting
people in prison for nothing; and that they called the Soviet land the
most democratic in the world, where the people could elect whom they
liked, while in truth it was the other way — they named the candidates
they wanted, and anyone who objected would be jailed and written down a
counter-revolutionary. He was arrested on 23 November 1937 and questioned
on the 27th: you are arrested and charged with counter-revolutionary
activity; the inquiry proposes you testify — there was no
counter-revolutionary activity on my part; you testify falsely, the
inquiry has enough material — I give the inquiry sincere testimony that I
carried on no counter-revolutionary activity of any kind; in November you
agitated over the elections to the Supreme Soviet — I carried on no
agitation over the elections in November or at any other time; in April
you slandered the Soviet power — I say again that neither in April nor at
any other time did I agitate against the existing order, and I hold the
witnesses’ testimony false. Whence had a monk of a Saratov monastery come
to him, and why did he live with him? — he had served a sentence in
Siberia and at Oryol for counter-revolutionary activity, come to Kaluga,
and been sent to us by the bishop; he did not serve in church but worked
in the garden. Who else lived in the church lodge? — a nun, the
watchwoman, the churchwarden’s wife and her sister. Had he organised a
monastery there? — two monks, a nun and three church women lived in the
lodge; we organised no monastery, we gathered there because we had nowhere
to live. Denying the charges, said the investigator, you have taken the
path of deceiving the inquiry, which has enough material to convict you —
I do not admit myself guilty of the charges, and I declare once more that
no counter-revolutionary activity was ever carried on by me. The inquiry
closed that day, the indictment was written the next and sent to the
troika, and on 2 December 1937 the NKVD troika sentenced him to ten years
in a labour camp; after various prisons he reached the Temnikov camp in
Mordovia on 25 July 1940, fell gravely ill on 24 August 1942 and was put
in the camp infirmary, and died on 3 September 1942 — 21 August of the old
style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him, among the saints of
Optina — and was buried in an unknown grave in the camp cemetery. The
life was compiled by the igumen Damascene (Orlovsky) for the lives of the
new martyrs of Optina.
