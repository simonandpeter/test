# Reginus of Skopelos

saint.gr says Reginus was born at Levadia in Boeotia at the beginning of the fourth century, of devout and virtuous parents who saw to it that he had both a secular education and an Orthodox upbringing; and that his love for the Lord and his spiritual progress made him a chosen vessel and a temple of the Holy Trinity. He lived, the page goes on, in the reigns of the two sons of Constantine the Great — Constantius at Constantinople in the East, Constans at Rome in the West — both brought up in the principles of the Christian faith, but Constantius a deliberate adherent of Arianism and Constans faithful to the dogmatic decisions of the First Ecumenical Council.

The page makes their church policy the setting of his life: both fought the old national religion and both defended the unity of the Church, and their continual interventions in ecclesiastical affairs, arbitrary or not, widened rather than healed the split between the friends and the enemies of Nicaea, and were a source of tension in the Arian quarrels of the fourth century. It was into that that Reginus was sent to the island of Skopelos by his uncle [Achilleus](/saints/achillius-of-larissa), whom saint.gr keeps on 15 May as the patron of the city of Larissa, to strengthen the exiles there and settle them in the Orthodox faith.

According to some notices in the synaxarion of St Achilleus, the page adds, Reginus attended the workings of the First Ecumenical Council in 325 together with Achilleus. But although the fathers condemned Arianism unanimously, Arius's followers did not die out and went on spreading their errors, so that great unrest, crisis and a parting into two camps came back into the Church, which alarmed both emperors; and they agreed at last on a new council at Sardica, which is Sofia. It met in 343, and Reginus took part in it and refuted all the heresies by his word and by the boldness of his judgement. Afterwards he went back to Skopelos.

Then the Church was shaken again by Julian the Apostate, who reigned at Constantinople from 361 to 363 and wanted the religion of the ancient Greeks brought back. In the persecutions he ordered, the eparch of Greece and of the Sporades reached Skopelos, called for the island's shepherd at once, and told him to change his faith and embrace idolatry; Reginus despised the suggestion and held to the piety of his fathers with spiritual manliness and steadiness. On 25 February 362 he was brought before the eparch for the last time, and to the urgings to deny Christ he gave no answer at all. He was taken to the island's stadium and endured further frightful tortures there, and then to the place called the Old Bridge, where the executioner cut off his head; that night the Christians took up his body and buried it in the wood of the hill above, where his tomb still is. His right arm is on Skopelos, the page says, and a fragment of his body at the monastery of Kykkos in Cyprus, and he is the island's patron.
