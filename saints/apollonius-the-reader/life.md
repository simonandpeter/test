# Apollonius the Reader

doxologia.ro prints one life for the martyrs of 14 decembrie, and Apollonius belongs to its Egyptian half. Under Diocletian, when [Arianus](/saints/arianus-of-the-thebaid), the governor in the parts of the Thebaid, had tortured and killed two leading Christians named Asclas and Leonidas at the city of the Antinoites, he had every Christian in the place arrested and the instruments of torture set before them, and offered them the choice of sacrifice or death. Thirty-seven men came out into the middle of the assembly rather than obey, and Apollonius, a reader of the Church, was one of them.

Under the long questioning, seeing the many and various torments, he faltered, and fear fell on him. He trembled at what was coming and at the same time was afraid of falling from faith in Christ and losing his soul; and he turned over how he might escape both the torments and the devils' sacrifices, saving his soul out of the devil's hands and his body out of the tormentors'. Standing near him was a man of the Greek faith named [Philemon](/saints/philemon-the-flute-player), a comedian by trade who entertained the governor at his festivities.

Apollonius called him over and promised him four gold pieces if he would sacrifice to the idols in his place, covered in his cloak so as not to be known. Philemon agreed at once and went towards the altar in the borrowed cloak — and there the divine light shone in his heart, and he confessed Christ instead. The life says God meant by this exchange to draw Philemon to himself through Apollonius, and Apollonius through Philemon; and when fire came down and burnt the pipes and trumpets, which were Apollonius's and had gone with the change of clothes, it burnt them in front of him.

Then Theon, Philemon's brother, came to the governor and told him in detail what Apollonius had done: how he had dressed Philemon in his own cloak and made him enter the contest in his stead. Apollonius was brought in, and Arianus looked at him with angry eyes and a terrible face and said he was the worst of men, who out of fear of the torments had despised the laws and the gods, changed clothes with this wretch, overturned his heart with sorceries and robbed the whole city of a great comfort; he should rather have come and opened his soul to the governor, who would have let him go free.

Apollonius answered that the reproach was well and justly made and that he did not resist it, for he accused himself too — not because he had been the cause of so many good things to Philemon, and not because Philemon had put on his cloak, but because he himself had put on Philemon's. Since both of them, by God's judgements, had now been clothed in the garment of salvation, the governor should know that neither Philemon nor Apollonius would ever sacrifice to his gods; and where he had feared the torments before, now, with God's help, he would show great courage.

He was kept in chains for worse torments while Philemon was beaten about the face by three soldiers; and when the governor saw the two of them unchanged he had the ankles of both bored through and had them dragged by ropes through the whole city. After the arrow that came down out of the air and blinded the governor's right eye, both were beheaded by his order, and their honoured bodies were laid beside the martyrs Asclas and Leonidas. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mucenic Apolonie» and gives no year.
