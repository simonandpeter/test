# Nicander of Egypt (15 March)

Nicander lived in the years of Diocletian. A warm zealot for the faith, saint.gr says, he admired with his whole soul those who poured out their blood for it; and as often as he learned of the death of martyrs he would go to the place, take up their holy relics and bury them, watering them with his tears. In the doing of that sacred work, in the year 305, he was arrested and tried.

Because he stayed faithful to his confession of Christ he was condemned to the harshest death. By the tyrant's order they flayed off his skin and then burned it with lighted torches. The two lines the page keeps for him say the same: the cooks of error laid hands on Nicander and skinned him like a lamb. There is no hymn, no relic and no place in Egypt named beyond the epithet of the line itself.

The corpus keeps another Nicander of Egypt, whom the Romanian calendar has on 5 June among ten martyrs tormented by the ruler of Egypt with hunger, thirst and cold; that is a different account on a different day, and this folder is the man the Greek calendar sets on 15 March, for burying the martyrs of Diocletian's last year.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and says the whole of it in three sentences. His skin was flayed off him and then he was beheaded for the faith of Christ. His guilt was this: that as a physician he helped the Christian martyrs and buried the bodies of the executed honourably. He suffered honourably in 302 — three years earlier than the year saint.gr gives, which is the year this folder keeps.

The Russian church keeps him on the same menologion day the Serbian does, and gives the work he died for in plainer terms. The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery says he suffered in Egypt under Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305; that he was a physician; and that during the persecution he visited the Christians held in the prisons, helped them, brought them food, and buried those who died.

Once, it says, he came to a place where the bodies of martyrs had been thrown out to be eaten by the beasts. Afraid to bury them in the daylight he waited for night and buried them under cover of the dark; he was seen, and was put to dreadful torments — the skin flayed from him while he was alive, and then beheading. The page closes the year as about 302, three years earlier than the Greek reading, so the interval now holds both.
