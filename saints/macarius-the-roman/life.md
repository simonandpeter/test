# Macarius the Roman

Macarius was born at Rome, the son of a Christian senator named John and of his believing wife, about the middle of the fourth century. He was brought up in the knowledge of the Scriptures, and out of them came his love for Christ and his wish to serve the Lord all his life as a monk. Against his will his parents betrothed him to a girl of good family; but on the night of the wedding he slipped out of his father's house.

A believing widow took him in, and went to his parents' house and brought him word of how his kinsfolk were searching for him. After seven days, on a Sunday night, he thanked her, prayed long with tears, and set out. God sent the Archangel Raphael in the shape of an old man, who took him with him; they travelled from Rome for three years and came beyond the borders of Persia, and there the old man vanished and appeared again surrounded with light, saying: "I am Raphael the Archangel, who appeared to thee in the shape of an old monk and brought thee here at the command of the Most High. Fear not, but give glory to God, rise and go on thy way."

Led then by wild beasts, he came to a cave where he found a dead lioness and her two cubs whimpering with hunger. He buried the lioness and reared the cubs, and they served him; so the troparion calls him the man who struggled in a cave and was served by two lions. Three pilgrim monks, Sergius, Hyginus and Theophilus, found him at last in the depth of the wilderness, and he told them that when he had once tried to go further than his place the Lord had said to him: do not try these things, thou canst not pass beyond this place, but return to thy cave — and that he had understood that a man in the body cannot come to the knowledge of them.

After the Vespers they had read together they asked him about the labours of his life, and he told it to them. A great raven came bringing a loaf and a half, so that all of them ate, and they glorified God. The three monks asked his prayers and went, accompanied by the lions for three days, and came again wonderfully to Jerusalem, where they venerated the places of our salvation before returning to their monastery and making the life of Macarius known. He died in peace in the wilderness, and is remembered on 23 octombrie.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 23 October, and saint.gr tells the same beginning and a stranger middle, warning first that the life of Macarius in the Synaxaristes is very mythical and strange. As his surname shows, it says, he was from Rome, the son of a senatorial lord named John; his parents forced him into marriage, and he left his wife on the wedding night. Wandering for some days he found a cave, and lived inside it together with two lions in a wild state. He was discovered there at length by three monks, Sergius, Hyginus and Theophilus, who lived in the monastery of Saint Asklepios in Mesopotamia of Syria, and from then on Macarius became known for the severity of his asceticism and for the great virtues he gained by it. According to the Synaxaristes' account, the page ends, he died in peace.
