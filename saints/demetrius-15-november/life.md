# Demetrius (15 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 15 November keeps a martyr Demetrius of whom it gives three sentences and no more. He came, it says, from the village of Davoudio, which was near the city of Amapasos. He was arrested in the years of the king Maximian and of the ruler Publius, in the year 298. And after suffering many tortures for Christ, at the last they beheaded him.

The calendar gives him no rank in the Church, no companion and no hymn, and the year it prints belongs to the reign and the governor rather than to him, so no date is recorded here beyond the words of the page. The verse over the entry says that Demetrius, cut down by the executioner's hand, now dances in the companies of the Lord's contestants.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 15 November in a line of four words, «Мч. Димитрия (ок. 307).», and carries no life page for him at all. The name link behind the line gives what the line itself leaves out: «Димитрий Дабудский», Demetrius of Daboudia. That toponymic is what identifies him, for the Greek calendar’s martyr of this day is the man who came from the village of Davoudio; the Russian calendar gives the place and no story, and the Greek the story and no name for the man beyond Demetrius.

The two calendars do not give the same year. The Russian line sets about 307 against his name; the Greek page gave 298 together with the reign of Maximian and the ruler Publius, which is why no year was recorded here when the folder was written. About 307 is set down as his death, and 298 is left where it stood. The day prints no hymn under his name, and the troparia and kontakia it carries belong to other saints it heads.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 15 Νοεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/11/15/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2918/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 15 ноября ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261115.html), which prints no life for him, read 5 October 2026.*
