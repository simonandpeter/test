# Moses the Hungarian

Moses was one of three Hungarian brothers in the service of Prince Boris of Kyiv.
When Boris was killed in 1015, Moses escaped, but was taken prisoner three years
later when Bolesław of Poland sacked the city, and carried off with the rest of
the captives.

The *Paterikon* of the Kyiv Caves tells the rest as a story of sustained refusal.
A wealthy Polish widow bought him, and — the account is unusually direct about
this — wanted him as a husband rather than a servant. He refused; she tried
persuasion, then confinement, then starvation, then beating. He was secretly
tonsured a monk by a visiting Athonite, and was mutilated on the widow's orders.
He is said to have endured five years of this before political upheaval freed
him.

He walked to the Monastery of the Caves at Kyiv and lived there roughly ten
years more, dying in 1043. He is venerated as a confessor rather than a martyr,
having survived what was done to him.
