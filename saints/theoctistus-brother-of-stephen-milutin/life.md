# Theoctistus, brother of Stephen Milutin

saint.gr's calendar for 30 Οκτωβρίου keeps three of one family together: «Όσιοι Στέφανος Μιλιούτιν, Θεόκτιστος ο αδελφός του και Ελένη η μητέρα τους». Theoctistus and his brother [Stephen Milutin](/saints/stephen-milutin) were sons of Stephen I, king of the Serbs, the page says, and grandsons of the Saint Stephen whom the same calendar keeps on 24 September.

Of Theoctistus the notice says this much: he ruled for a short time, then resigned the throne of Serbia and lived as an ascetic at Srem until his repose in 1316. He worked hard to bring the heretical Bogomils back to the right faith. The page gives him no birth year, no account of his tonsure or of the name he bore as king, and no relics.

His mother [Helen](/saints/helen-mother-of-stephen-milutin) is kept on the same day, and his brother ruled Serbia after him. saint.gr prints no hymn for any of the three, and carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him with his brother and his mother on one line and one page, and it calls him by the name he reigned under, Dragutin, with the name he took as a monk in brackets after it. It says why the throne changed hands. He was a faithful Christian, and after a short reign he handed the power over to his younger brother; and he himself, having come to love the hermit's life, shut himself up at Srem, where he performed secret ascetic labours in a grave-pit he had dug with his own hands.

In that righteous life, the page says, he laboured much at the turning of the Bogomil heretics to the true faith — which is the one thing his kontakion on the same day also says of him, that he rooted out their soul-destroying heresy. His end followed on 2 March 1316: the Greek page had the year and the Russian adds the day. The calendar prints a troparion and a kontakion for him, both his own and neither a common, and the troparion has him cleansed by repentance and adorned with meekness and humility.
