# Dorotheus of Gaza

doxologia.ro's calendar names him on 13 august and prints three sentences. Our venerable and God-bearing father Dorotheus of Gaza lived the ascetic life first in the community of Abba [Seridos](/saints/seridos-of-gaza), near Gaza; afterwards, when Abba [John](/saints/john-the-prophet-of-gaza), who was called the Prophet, had died and the perfect silence of the great [Barsanuphius](/saints/barsanuphius-the-great) was complete, he founded his own monastery; and he ended his days in peace.

There, the page says, his disciples gathered the Spiritual Instructions, which became in time one of the fundamental works for Orthodox monastic life. His writings are part of the Philokalia, and they are the one concrete witness left of him across the centuries.

Nothing further is set down here because nothing further was read: the page gives him no year, and the Greek, Russian and Serbian calendars were not consulted for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr tells much more of him than the Romanian page does. It calls abba Dorotheos a great ascetic figure of the sixth century who to most of the world remains unknown. Young, he settled in a cenobitic monastery whose abbot was abba [Seridos](/saints/seridos-of-gaza), and nearby lived the two great ascetics [Barsanuphius](/saints/barsanuphius-the-great) and [John](/saints/john-the-prophet-of-gaza), known from the Book of Barsanuphius and John, which holds the wise answers they gave to questions of the spiritual life. Because they were enclosed they received the questions in writing and answered in writing, and Dorotheos, who revered them greatly, consulted them with his abbot's agreement about every serious thing and did nothing without their assent.

One example the page gives: his thoughts once urged him to leave the monastery for the desert, since the infirmary he served in left him many distractions and little quiet or time for prayer, and he feared he would lose his soul that way. He disclosed the thought to abba John, as was his habit, and then obeyed him. Quiet, John told him, is for a man to guard his heart from giving and taking and pleasing men and the rest of such doings; to gather the fruits of quiet he must first put out the leaves of ascetic practice, that is, obedience and patience. Barsanuphius, asked as well, answered the same. So Dorotheos stayed in obedience, service and patience, and advanced greatly.

Later, grown mature, he did withdraw to the desert, meaning to end his life a hesychast; but a great number of monks gathered about him and compelled him to found a monastery, and he died in peace. The page adds that words of his are in the Catecheses of Theodore the Studite, that a number of his writings survive, and that the discourses collected as the Ascetic Works of Abba Dorotheos — on renunciation, on humility, on conscience, on the building and jointing of the soul's virtues, on cutting the passions early, on the holy fasts — are rather oral homilies written down by his hearers and afterwards developed under his own supervision.
