# Zeno, companion of Eudoxius

Zeno was one of the martyrs Eudoxius, Zeno and Macarius, who with their
company died for Christ under Maximian Galerius, the successor of
Diocletian — the Greek synaxarion says under Diocletian, the Prologue too.
Eudoxius held the high rank of commander in the imperial armies — a count,
the Greek calls him — and was a Christian, as were his friend Zeno and his
steward Macarius. When Diocletian’s edict ordered the death of Christians
who refused to sacrifice, many, noblemen and officials among them, fled to
other lands with their families, and Eudoxius with his wife Basilissa and
all his household left his high post, abandoned his property and hid in the
region of Melitene in Armenia. The governor of Melitene sent soldiers to
find him; meeting Eudoxius himself in poor clothes and not knowing him,
they asked where the commander Eudoxius was, and without revealing himself
he invited them into his house, fed them and gave them a bed for the night
— taking the meeting as the Lord’s call to martyrdom. In the morning he
told his guests that he was the man they sought; grateful for his
hospitality they offered to hide from their superiors that they had found
him, but he would not have it, and having set his house in order told his
wife not to mourn him but to keep the day of his martyr’s death as a feast,
put on his soldier’s dress and went with the soldiers to the governor,
Basilissa and his friends Zeno and Macarius following him. The governor
tried to talk him into sacrificing to the idols and so keeping his life,
his high place and his property; he refused everything outright, exposing
the madness of those who worship soulless idols, and the military belt,
the sign of his command, he took off himself and threw in the governor’s
face — and the soldiers standing by, secret Christians, more than a
thousand of them, did the same. The confounded governor asked the emperor
what to do and was told: torture the officers and let the rest go. After
long torments Eudoxius was led out to execution; Basilissa followed her
husband weeping, and Zeno wept too, and Eudoxius again urged his wife not
to weep but to rejoice that he had been found worthy of the martyr’s
crown, asking her to bury his body in the place called Amimna, and told the
weeping Zeno that they would stand together in the Kingdom of Heaven at
one time. Heartened by the words, Zeno confessed himself a Christian aloud
and was at once condemned to death. Basilissa took up her husband’s body
unhindered and buried it where he had bidden her, and for that was seized
and brought before the governor; longing to share her husband’s lot she
fearlessly denounced him and his false gods, but he saw what she wanted,
did not torture her and drove her away — and going, she told him that God
saw her wish to suffer for her faith and would take the wish for the deed
done. Seven days later Eudoxius appeared to his wife in a vision and bade
her tell his friend and steward Macarius that he and Zeno were waiting for
him to come to them; Macarius went straight to the governor, confessed
himself a Christian, was condemned and beheaded, and many Christians
suffered a martyr’s death at the same time, in 311 or 312. The Prologue
counts the soldiers Eudoxius had brought to Christianity at 1,104 — 1,134,
by others — and says that after his death he appeared to his wife, who kept
the faith of Christ to the end and reposed in peace.
