# Zeno, companion of Eudoxius

Zeno was one of the martyrs Eudoxius, Zeno and Macarius, who with their company died for Christ under Maximian Galerius, the successor of Diocletian — the Greek synaxarion says under Diocletian, the Prologue too. Eudoxius held the high rank of commander in the imperial armies — a count, the Greek calls him — and was a Christian, as were his friend Zeno and his steward Macarius. When Diocletian’s edict ordered the death of Christians who refused to sacrifice, many, noblemen and officials among them, fled to other lands with their families, and Eudoxius with his wife Basilissa and all his household left his high post, abandoned his property and hid in the region of Melitene in Armenia. The governor of Melitene sent soldiers to find him; meeting Eudoxius himself in poor clothes and not knowing him, they asked where the commander Eudoxius was, and without revealing himself he invited them into his house, fed them and gave them a bed for the night — taking the meeting as the Lord’s call to martyrdom. In the morning he told his guests that he was the man they sought; grateful for his hospitality they offered to hide from their superiors that they had found him, but he would not have it, and having set his house in order told his wife not to mourn him but to keep the day of his martyr’s death as a feast, put on his soldier’s dress and went with the soldiers to the governor — the Sretensky calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. Tried and tortured by the governor of Melitene, the Prologue says, he suffered with his friends Zeno and Macarius and 1,104 soldiers whom he had brought to Christianity — 1,134, by others — and after death appeared to his wife, who kept the faith of Christ to the end and reposed in peace.
