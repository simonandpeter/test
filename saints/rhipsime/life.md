# Rhipsime

The Greek synaxarion keeps Rhipsime, Gaiane and thirty-two other virgin martyrs together. Rhipsime was martyred in the years of Diocletian (284–304), in 292. She was beautiful in body and modest in her ways, and some say a nun. Because she would not accept Diocletian’s offer to make her his wife, she fled to Armenia with [the elder Gaiane](/saints/gaiane-companion-of-rhipsime), who is said to have been her abbess.

But Tiridates, king of Armenia, heard of her beauty and wanted her for his wife as well, and when she refused, he ordered her found and seized. His men found her hiding near Ararat, and there they put out her eyes, cut out her tongue, and at last cut her body into small pieces. The elder Gaiane and [thirty-two virgin martyrs](/saints/thirty-two-virgin-martyrs) were martyred with her in terrible tortures, and [seventy men](/saints/seventy-martyrs-with-rhipsime) who were hiding in those parts died with them.

The Romanian life, which serves Rhipsime and Gaiane alike, calls them two noble women of Diocletian’s time, virgins of great beauty and nuns. Tiridates had Rhipsime brought to him by force, but by the power of Christ could not overcome her and lay as though dead; she fled, and his soldiers caught her, tore off her clothes, bound her hands behind her, cut out her tongue, burned her body and put out her eyes. Seventy Christian men and thirty-two women were killed with her. The brave Gaiane was pierced through the throat and blown up like a bladder, flayed to the breast, crushed with stones and beheaded.

In the Romanian life of [Gregory the Illuminator](/saints/gregory-the-illuminator) the virgins, thirty-seven in all, lived under the abbess Gaiane and fled Diocletian’s envoys to Armenia, where they camped before the city and earned their bread; Gaiane counselled Rhipsime in Latin before the king, and had her teeth broken with stones for it; Rhipsime was burned with torches, cut open and cut to pieces; thirty-three of the others were put to the sword; and Gaiane and two virgins with her were hung head down, flayed alive and beheaded. Nine days later Gregory, out of the pit, gathered and buried their bodies and laid them in the church the king built.

The Russian church calendar keeps her on the Julian 30 September, in one line with Gaiane and their companions: «Мцц. Рипсимии, Гаиании и с ними 35-ти святых дев (начало IV)». Its index of names heads her «Рипсимия Армянская, дева, прмц.» It dates the martyrdom to the beginning of the fourth century where saint.gr gives 292 — a vaguer reading rather than a rival year, so her date here is still the Greek one and the Russian wording is recorded in the Russian row.
