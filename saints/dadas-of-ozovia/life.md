# Dadas of Ozovia

Dadas is the third of the men of Ozovia whom doxologia.ro's calendar keeps on 28 April, and his name is the one the site is not consistent about: the calendar line and the title of the life call him «Dadas», while the closing sentence of the life itself names the three as «Sfinţii Mucenici Maxim, Dada şi Cvintilian». Nothing on the page chooses between the two spellings.

The passion is one account for all three. Diocletian and Maximian, in the second year of the proconsuls Tarquinius and Gabinius, issued the edict the page quotes: that all under Roman rule be brought by every means to the honour and love of the gods, and that whoever anywhere should be found naming the name of Christ would draw their anger on himself. The peoples gathered and sacrificed; the next day an idolater denounced three men who had refused and who worshipped One God in the heavens.

The servants sent after them found them in their village of Ozovia at prayer, bound them in iron chains, and brought them to the city of Dorostolon, in the south of what the page calls today's Dobruja. The three spent the night in prayer for strength to overcome their adversaries and for the crowns of victory, and in the morning stood before the two proconsuls, where [Maximus](/saints/maximus-of-ozovia) answered first for them all.

They were thrown down and beaten, and answered together that, strengthened by their God, they cared nothing for the torments and would not sacrifice to demons. Brought out again after the seventh hour they refused once more; Gabinius threatened them with beheading in barbarian places, and they told him to do in deed what he had said in word.

So, glorifying God and asking Him to receive them into His rest, they were taken back to the place the life here spells Ozevia, and there beheaded with Maximus and [Quintilian](/saints/quintilian-of-ozovia) on the twenty-eighth day of April. The page gives no year, only the reign of Diocletian and Maximian and the proconsulate of Tarquinius and Gabinius.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them a fortnight earlier than the Romanian, on 13 April, and adds what they were. saint.gr says these saints lived in the years of the emperor Maximian, whose reign it dates 285 to 305, and of Diocletian, 284 to 305, and under the consuls Gabibius and Tarquinius; that they came from Ozovia, and that they were readers of the Church. When the persecution against the Christians came they were imprisoned; afterwards they were beaten hard and thrown back into prison, and there the idolaters made many and various attempts to draw them into denying Christ.

They stayed faithful to the end, the page says, and were counted worthy of the martyr's crown, being beheaded for their faith. The Christians gathered their holy relics up with reverence and carried them to Constantinople, to the church of the Most Holy Mother of God «ἐν τοὶς Βιγλεντίου», where their synaxis used to be kept. Its couplet asks who these are that lie here without their heads, and answers: Quintillianus, Maximus and Dadas.

The Serbian church keeps the three of them on this day, with the Romanian calendar and not with the Greek, which keeps them a fortnight earlier. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the whole of them in three sentences: they suffered in the time of Diocletian; they were judged and tortured by the voivode Tarquinius; and after imprisonment and torments they were beheaded with the sword. **That reign is the only time it names**, and because the book prints no bounds for it no year was recorded here on its account, so this folder stands undated as the other two calendars left it.

The Russian calendar's reading sets the three in the reign of Diocletian, when the order went out that sacrifice was to be offered to the gods at the public festivals and that Christians were to be put to death. The emperor's governors Tarquinius and Gavinius held a great festival at Durostorum which the people of the surrounding villages attended as well as the townsmen, and when it was over somebody informed against the three brothers for not obeying and for having gone home to their village of Ozovia. Soldiers found them at prayer there and brought them to trial.
