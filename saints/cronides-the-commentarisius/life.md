# Cronides the Commentarisius

Cronides was a commentarisius, the officer who kept a governor's records and had charge of his prisoners, and saint.gr's calendar for 27 Μαρτίου keeps him among the martyrs of the household he was set to guard. In a time of persecution in the year 125, under the emperor Hadrian, [Philetus](/saints/philetus-the-senator) the senator, his wife [Lydia](/saints/lydia-wife-of-philetus) and their sons [Theoprepius](/saints/theoprepius-son-of-philetus) and [Macedon](/saints/macedon-son-of-philetus) were handed over to [Amphilochius](/saints/amphilochius-the-duke), duke in Slavonia, who hung the parents on a tree and flayed them.

It was at that sight, the page says, that Cronides believed in Christ; and he was shut up in prison together with the rest of the saints. In the night, while they sang and prayed, angels came and gave them courage for the struggle before them. The next day they were brought out to the duke, who ordered them thrown into a bronze cauldron heated and full of oil and resin; the saints made the sign of the Cross, and it went cold. Amphilochius then believed too, and threw himself in.

The couplet the page prints for the pair of them is «Δοὺξ συντελευτᾷ τῷ Κομενταρησίῳ, Ἐξουσιάζων ἐξυπηρετουμένῳ» — the duke ends his life together with the commentarisius, the man in authority with the man who served him. Of Cronides himself the page gives no birthplace, no years, no relic and no hymn, and his one act is his confession in the prison yard. He is a different man from the Cronides, deacon of Alexandria, whom the corpus keeps in September.

The Russian calendar keeps the company four days earlier in the menologion than the Greek, on the twenty-third of March, old style, and tells the whole of it in one reading. Philetus was a man of rank at the court of the emperor Hadrian, and for confessing Christ openly he came before the court with his wife and their two sons. By Hadrian’s own order the family was sent into Illyria, to the commander there, to be put to the torture; and he had them hung on a tree and their bodies scraped with knives.

They were then shut up in prison together with the governor of the prisons, who had come to believe. In the night an angel stood before them and strengthened them for what was coming. The next day the martyrs were lowered into a cauldron of boiling oil, and the oil went cold in an instant and they were unhurt. The commander was so struck by it that he believed in Christ and stepped into the boiling oil himself, praying, Lord Jesus Christ, help me — and lived.

The same torture was tried again when Hadrian himself came into Illyria. The holy martyrs were thrown into the boiling oil again and again and by the power of God stayed alive, and the emperor went back to Rome shamed, while they gave thanks and glorified God and gave up their souls to him in prayer. The calendar sets the whole of it inside Hadrian’s reign and gives no closer year than that.

The life makes him the governor of the prisons, «начальник тюрем», who had already come to believe when the family was shut up with him — which is the office the corpus’s own by-name for him states, and not a child of theirs as the day’s line would have it. The folder had no date before this.
