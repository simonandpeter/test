# Ignatius of Methymna

Ignatius was born in 1492 in the village of Faranga on Lesbos, near Kalloni. His father, Emmanuel Agallianos, was a priest in Mytilene and came of a great family of Constantinople. The son, named Ioan in the world, married and was ordained priest at Sakellionos. In that time a heavy epidemic came upon the island, and his wife and his children died, except one child, who was afterwards tonsured with the name Methodios and carried on his father’s work.

After the epidemic Ignatius withdrew to the place of his birth, and in 1526 founded the monastery of Limonos. In the years of the Turkish occupation of the island he gave the Christians tireless support in those hard times. He served as abbot of the monastery until 1530, for in 1531, while he was at Constantinople, he was chosen metropolitan of Methymna.

At the end of 1563 he asked to retire from the office of metropolitan, so as to spend the rest of his life as a monk in his monastery. He fell asleep in the Lord on 14 October 1566. That is the whole of what doxologia.ro prints of him, and the page has no Tropar tab, so no hymn is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 14 October. saint.gr says Ignatius was born in 1492 in the village of Pharanga on Mytilene, near Kalloni, and was surnamed Agallianos; his father Emmanuel Agallianos was a priest on the island. John — that was his first name — married young and became a priest himself; and when some epidemic took his wife and his children, all but one, Methodios, he withdrew to the Taxiarch of Leimon and gave himself to study, to prayer and to the working of his father's land. In the years of the Turkish occupation of the island he rendered great and considerable services to the monasteries there and to the Christians under that yoke; and for those, and for the holiness of his life, he was made metropolitan of Methymna in 1531, under Jeremiah II. In his new post, the page says, he showed himself an excellent shepherd, and having pastured the flock of Christ in a way pleasing to God he died peacefully in 1566.
