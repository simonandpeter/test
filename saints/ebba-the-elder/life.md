# Ebba the Elder

Ebba — Æbbe, Tabbs in Butler’s heading — was the sister of Oswald and Oswiu,
kings of the Northumbrians, and with the liberality of Oswiu founded a
nunnery on the Derwent in the bishopric of Durham, called after her
Ebchester, and then a double monastery, the men’s house and the women’s
apart, at Coldingham in the marches below Berwick, now in Scotland. The
house of nuns there she governed herself until she was called to eternal
bliss in 683. That is the whole of Butler’s entry, which rests on Bede’s
history, book IV, chapters 19 and 25, and on Harpsfield. The Greek
synaxarion keeps her on 25 August among the saints of Britain with no
details of her life, and Butler on the same day.

*After Alban Butler’s Lives of the Saints (1866), volume VIII, August — [St. Ebba, or Tabbs, Virgin and Abbess](https://www.bartleby.com/lit-hub/lives-of-the-saints/volume-viii-august/st-ebba-or-tabbs-virgin-and-abbess), read 6 September 2026; the Greek synaxarion’s line (saint.gr, 25 August) was all this entry had until then.*
