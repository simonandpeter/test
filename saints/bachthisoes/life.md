# Bachthisoes

saint.gr names him last of three and gives two sentences for all of them together. Its line for 16 Μαΐου reads «Άγιοι Ισαάκιος, Συμεών και Βαχθισόης», and its page says that Saints [Isaac the Persian](/saints/isaac-the-persian-of-metsovo), [Simeon](/saints/simeon-martyr-16-may) and Bachthisoes were perfected by fire, and that the Synaxarion of Constantinople records their contest in the years of the emperor Constantine the Great, whose reign it dates 306 to 337, and of the Persian king Shapur II, whose reign it dates 309 to 379, after they had been brought before the king and had confessed their faith in Christ.

The synaxarion verse says only that the three of them breathed out their lives with one mind into a single fire. Of Bachthisoes in particular the page says nothing at all: no city, no year, no office, no parentage, no relics and no hymn. He has a name, a day, a manner of death and two companions, and that is the whole of what is set down here.

The Russian calendar names him third of three and, unlike the Greek, says something of him alone. Its own index heads him a hierarch and a bishop and a hieromartyr, of Бет-Никтор — Beth-Nictor — and calls him the Persian: a rank, a see and a grade where the Greek page had given him nothing at all but a name, a day, two companions and a manner of death.

The notice the three share says they were Christians living in the third century in Persia under king Шапур, a cruel persecutor of Christians; that they were pressed to renounce Christ and turn to the superstition of the fire-worshippers and answered that they would not renounce the Creator of creation and would not bow to the sun and to the fire; that they were cruelly tortured and then shut in prison and given no food for seven days; and that at the last they were beheaded with the sword. **The sword is not the fire.** The Greek page had the three of them perfected by fire and its synaxarion verse had them breathe out their lives with one mind into a single flame, which is the reading already set down in this folder. The two books give the same three men two different ends, and this folder now carries both and chooses neither; the calendar's day line also prints the fourth century where its own notice says the third.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 16 Μαΐου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/05/16/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/415/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 18 мая ст. ст. — [the Russian day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260518.html), [the Russian notice of the three](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life1095.htm) and [his Russian name page](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/name/401.html), read 8 October 2026.*
