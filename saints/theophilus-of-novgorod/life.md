# Theophilus of Novgorod

saint.gr's calendar for 26 Οκτωβρίου names Theophilus, archbishop of Novgorod, and then says in one sentence that it has nothing to tell of him: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Οσίου» — we have no details of the life of the venerable one. The see in the line is the whole of what the entry carries beyond the name.

There is no century, no year of his election or his repose, no monastery, no companions and no relics; the page prints no couplet for him and no hymn. Of the many Theophilus folders the corpus holds, none is an archbishop of Novgorod.

The Russian calendar is the first of the four to give him more than a line, and it keeps him on 26 октября ст. ст. with a second entry among the fathers of the Far caves on 28 августа. He was the last archbishop of Novgorod chosen at the people's assembly — by lot, after the death of his predecessor [Jonah](/saints/jonah-of-novgorod) — and he was consecrated at Moscow on 15 December 1472, having lived until then as sacristan in the Otenskaya hermitage. The years that followed were the hardest a Novgorod bishop had.

The posadnitsa Marfa Boretskaya and her party were arming the city against the grand prince of Moscow, Ivan III, and a monk of her faction worked the people's hatred up against the archbishop. Some of the Novgorodians were ready to go over to Lithuania, and with that to apostasy. He stood in their way and said so plainly: «Не изменяйте Православию или не буду пастырем отступников, уйду назад в смиренную келью, откуда извлекли вы меня на позорище мятежа» — do not betray Orthodoxy, or I will be no shepherd of apostates. His letter of resignation, written in 1479, survives.

The city would not hear him, the war came, and the beaten Novgorodians had to beg for quarter; many of them owed their lives to his pleading, and he had got boyars out of Lithuanian prisons by it before. In 1480 Ivan III sent him to be held in the Chudov monastery at Moscow, where he «сидел полтретья лето, ту и преставися», sat two and a half summers and there died; the calendar's line gives the year as 1482.

Lying ill there he saw in a dream [Niphon of Novgorod](/saints/niphon-of-novgorod), buried in the caves at Kyiv three centuries before, who reminded him of a promise to venerate the wonderworkers of those caves. He set out, and had nearly reached the Dnieper when the illness took hold and it was revealed to him that he would not come to the caves alive but would rest in them, which is what happened: his relics lie in the Far caves. The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion for him, the kontakion playing on his name — that Christ the Saviour appeared to him who had truly kept to what his name means, God-loving.

The calendar's line calls him «Прп.», venerable, where the name page behind it heads him «Святитель», a hierarch. Both words are kept and neither is chosen over the other, beside the archbishop's see this folder already held.
