# Sophronius of Soumela

Sophronius was the nephew of [Barnabas](/saints/barnabas-of-soumela), and in the world the two were called Sotirihos and Vasile. Both were born at Athens in the tenth century, and when they were tonsured monks and ordained priests they took the names Sophronius and Barnabas. After a sign from God they set out together on a long journey into Pontus, to find the wonderworking icon of the Most Holy Mother of God called Soumela and to build a monastery for it.

On the way, the account says, they venerated the relics of Saint Luke at Steiris, of Barbarus, of Achillius of Larissa and of Demetrius the Myrrh-streamer; they met bishops and ascetics; and they came to the Holy Mountain, where they venerated at the Great Lavra of Athanasius the Athonite and visited Vatopedi. There the monk Lazarus, who had been told of them by the Mother of God, received them, and the abbot wished to keep them; but their purpose lay elsewhere.

From Maroneia they were led to Mount Papikion, and so through cities and monastic centres to the cave on Mount Mela, where they found the icon of the Mother of God, the Hodegetria of Athens, and began to build the monastery, the water they needed having sprung up first by a miracle and by prayer. With fasting, prayer, vigils and many labours they finished their godly life, and both rested in the Lord on the same day, 18 August, which is also the day of their commemoration.
