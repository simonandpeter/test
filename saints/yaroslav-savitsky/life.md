# Yaroslav (Savitsky)

The Сретенский monastery's calendar names him ninth in its clause of fifteen for 25 ноября ст. ст. and carries no life for him; azbyka.ru prints one out of fond.ru. Ярослав Исаакович Савицкий was born on 28 March 1882 at Пухлое in the Бельск district of the province of Grodno, the son of a deacon. He went into the seminary as his father had, finished it in 1903, worked first as a teacher and then as a psalm-reader, married Ольга Федоровна, and was ordained priest in 1905; they had two sons and a daughter.

Where he served in those first years is not known, but there are grounds, the page says, for thinking it was the Krasnostok convent of the Nativity of the Theotokos near Grodno, founded in 1901. In the First World War, before the German advance, the clergy and the sisters of that house had to leave Grodno and move to the Catherine hermitage in the province of Moscow. The men's monastery there was closed in 1919, but the Krasnostok sisters set up an agricultural labour commune and the house survived in that shape until 1929. A minute of the commune's general meeting of 17 February 1921 survives, and it resolved to keep him and one deacon at the parish church; the other four priests were taken into the levy.

He was raised archpriest in 1919, and in November 1923 the abbess asked that he be given the next award, writing that he had borne all the hardship of the war and the flight and had not left the hermitage since 1918. On that petition stands the resolution of the Patriarch, [Tikhon of Moscow](/saints/tikhon-of-moscow): «Удостоить» — let him be granted it. In the twenties he and his family were stripped of the vote. When the artel was broken up in 1929 and the church closed, a hundred and seventeen nuns and novices were still there, and he was made rector of the church of the martyrs Florus and Laurus at the village of Ям.

There the collectivisation fell on his people, and he did not stand aside from it: in the words of his accusers, on 18 March 1930 he organised in the church a demonstration of up to a hundred and seventy people which went to the village soviet singing hymns, and during the liquidation of the kulaks he served services for the health of those deported. The parish house had been taken, so a parishioner gave his family a small room, and they lived in it until his arrest with nothing but a few personal things. The villagers remembered him as a very kind man, learned, clever and strong in character, whom all the parish loved. In 1933 a court sentenced him to five years for buying candles, on a case so crudely got up that the regional court quashed it.

He was arrested at about two in the morning on 27 November 1937 and held in the Серпухов prison; he did not admit the charge of anti-Soviet agitation. The judicial troika sentenced him to be shot on 1 December, and he was shot on 8 December 1937 and buried on the Butovo range outside Moscow.
