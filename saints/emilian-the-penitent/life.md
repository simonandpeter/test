# Emilian the Penitent

The Ohrid Prologue says Emilian was born at Rome and committed many grave sins in his youth; but when he sobered from his sinning and came to himself, he trembled at the very thought of the Judgment of God. He entered a monastery, and by fasting, by vigil and by obedience he tamed his body and dried it out. In every good struggle he was a model to his brethren, and he often went out of the monastery at night to a cave nearby, to pray.

The abbot of that monastery did not know where he went, and one night followed him secretly. He saw Emilian standing at prayer in fear and weeping. All at once a heavenly light, stronger than the sun, lit up the whole of that mountain, and the cave and Emilian in particular; and a voice was heard from heaven: Emilian, thy sins are forgiven thee. The abbot was struck with awe and fled back to the monastery.

The next day he told the brethren everything he had seen and heard that night, and from then on Emilian was held in great honour among them. He lived long, the entry ends, and fell asleep in the Lord. It gives no year and no century, names no monastery and no mountain, does not give the abbot a name, and prints no hymn.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day and gives him the only life any of the four calendars read has put behind his name: «Прп. Емилиана Италийского (VI).» He was a Roman by birth, the page says, and his name in the world was Викторин — «до старости вел греховную жизнь», he led a sinful life into old age. Then he repented, withdrew into a monastery, took the monastic habit with the name Емилиан, and served God humbly for the rest of his days, astonishing the brotherhood by an obedience without a murmur in it and by a severe fasting. The monks noticed that at night he was secretly visiting a cave not far from the monastery. One night the abbot followed him and found him there praying in broken tears, lit by an unearthly light, and heard a voice from above: «Емилиан, отпускаются тебе грехи твои» — Emilian, thy sins are forgiven thee.

Deeply moved, the abbot asked the old man after the morning service to tell the brotherhood his secret, and the saint told them all of the great mercy of God towards him. Then the abbot explained it to the brethren: «Господь и в молчании мог бы простить ему грехи, но ради всех нас Он открыл Свою милость явлением света и голоса, чтобы все мы видели Его благость и милость к искренне кающимся грешникам» — the Lord could have forgiven his sins in silence, but for the sake of all of us he disclosed his mercy by the appearing of the light and the voice, so that we might all see his goodness and his mercy towards sinners who repent sincerely. He passed the rest of his life in spiritual joy and departed peacefully to the Lord.

That is the whole of it, and it is the whole of what this folder knew of him before as well: the Serbian book that made it gave him the bare «Емилијан» under a rank word and no century. **The (VI) at the end of the Russian line is therefore the first date this folder has ever carried**, and it is a century and not a year. The page gives him no monastery by name, no city but Rome, no companion, no relic and no hymn — the day prints four hymns and none is his.

**Two other men of this name stand close to him on this very day and are not him, and the Russian line's by-name is what separates them.** The Greek calendar keeps on 7 March a martyr of Rome of about 259 who died with two companions, and this corpus keeps him and both companions as three folders; the Russian page keeps instead a Roman penitent of the sixth century who died in his bed, and the by-name it gives him, Италийский, is the only word in either calendar that tells the two apart — both men being Romans, and both called by the same name. Nothing is asserted between them, and this folder takes no `related` row from the resemblance.
