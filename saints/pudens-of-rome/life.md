# Pudens of Rome

Pudens is named by the apostle Paul in the second letter to Timothy, where he writes «Închină-se ție și Pud» — Pudens greets you. He was a just and faithful man, doxologia's notice says, among the nobles of Rome, and he received into his own house the apostles Peter and Paul and the gathering of the other faithful. That house was afterwards turned into a church and was called the pastoral church, and in it, they say, Peter himself performed the holy things.

With [Trophimus](/saints/trophimus-companion-of-paul) and [Aristarchus](/saints/aristarchus-of-apamea) he followed Paul through all his persecutions, and at the end, when Paul was beheaded by Nero at Rome, these three apostles of the Lord were beheaded with him.

The page is a single short notice shared by the three, and it gives no year, no parentage and nothing of Pudens's life beyond the house he opened and the death he shared.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them a day earlier than the Romanian, on 14 April, and says where it reads them out of. All three were of the Seventy, saint.gr says, and devoted fellow workers of the apostle Paul. Of Aristarchus the Acts of the Apostles report that he was a Macedonian from Thessalonica and in all likelihood a Jew; Paul names him in the letter to the Colossians as his fellow prisoner at Rome, and in the letter to Philemon as his fellow worker. Of Pudens he speaks in the second letter to Timothy, from which the page gathers that Pudens was among those who worked for the Gospel at Rome.

Trophimus was brought to the Christian faith by Paul himself when he came to Ephesus, and from then on he followed him to Jerusalem and to Rome, to work and to suffer ill together with his teacher; Paul names him in the second letter to Timothy. In the terrible persecution of Nero, which saint.gr dates 54 to 68, all three of these fellow workers of Paul were counted worthy to bear witness by death, being beheaded. The page's couplets play on each name in turn, and one line of the synaxarion says simply that the disciples departed on the fourteenth.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the three of them on the Romanian calendar's day, 15 April, and is a third witness that agrees with the other two: all three were of the number of the Seventy, and all three were beheaded in the persecution under Nero at the time the apostle Paul was beheaded. Of Pudens it says that he was a man of standing among the citizens of Rome, that the apostle mentions him once — and here, where the other pages give only the letter, it gives the place, the second letter to Timothy at chapter four, verse twenty-one — and that his house was first a refuge for the chief apostles and was afterwards turned into a church, which was called the Pastoral. The entry prints no year of any kind, no parentage and no hymn, so this folder gains nothing beyond that chapter and verse and the word for the rank he held among his townsmen.

**The Russian calendar keeps the three of them on the Romanian and Serbian calendars' day, the 15 April, and not on the Greek one, and it prints them a kontakion.** Its life for the three together tells the same short story the other three sources tell, and adds one thing none of them has: the year. All three were of the Seventy whom the Lord sent out before him, it says; all three were put to death at Rome under Nero, whose reign it dates 54 to 68, at the same time as the apostle Paul, about the year 67.

Of Pudens it says what the others say — a member of the Roman Senate who received the chief apostles into his own house, which was turned into a church and called the Pastoral — and then the two Russian readings disagree about who served in it. The life the day serves for the three together says that the apostle Peter performed the holy things there, which is what the Romanian page says; his own life page, served from the same index, says it was the apostle Paul. The source prints both and settles neither, and neither is chosen here.
