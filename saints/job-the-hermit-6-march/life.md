# Job the Hermit

saint.gr gives the venerable Job three sentences, and the life says no more than they do. His name in the world was John, and he was born at Moscow in the year 1635. On the third of April 1701 he was tonsured a monk under the name Job at the monastery the page calls Solofski, and began his spiritual struggles under the guidance of the starets Jonah. He fell asleep in peace in the year 1720.

The day's own line calls him the hermit, and that is the only word the site gives to the way of life the name points at: no desert is named, no cell, no wilderness and no years in it. Nor is anything said of the sixty-six years he lived before the tonsure — no family, no trade, no reason for coming so late to the habit — and nothing of the monastery beyond its name, nothing of the starets Jonah beyond his.

The page names no relic, prints no distich and gives no hymn; the head of it leaves his year of birth a question mark although its own body supplies it. The corpus keeps three other men of this name — of Pochaev, of Zographou, and the Long-suffering — and none of them stands on this day or is named on this page.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović fills in two of the things saint.gr left bare: why he came to the church, and why he came to Solovetsky. He was born at Moscow in 1635, it says, and it was the chanting and the service of the church that drew him to the church. He became the spiritual father of the Tsar Peter the Great; but on account of some slander he withdrew to the monastery of Solovetsky, where he struggled hard.

He fell asleep in the Lord in 1720, in his eighty-fifth year, which agrees with the two years the other calendar gives. Before his death, the entry ends, he cried out: Blessed is the God of our fathers! and since it is so, I am not afraid, but go out of this world with joy.

The Russian church keeps him on this day, and it is the calendar that knows who he was: «Прп. Иова, в схиме Иисуса, Анзерского (1720).» The Сретенский monastery's index puts two separate lives behind that one line, and between them they give a dossier the Greek and Serbian entries have nothing of. He was born at Moscow in 1635 and called Иоанн at his baptism, most likely out of a clerical family, and was made priest of one of the Moscow parish churches; the first life says he served with such attention and love that his name soon became known across the city, and that his true consolation was in the Liturgy, which he served so that everyone about him prayed harder than usual without meaning to. After prayer his favourite work was almsgiving. He set out meals for the poor of his parish, used his standing among influential acquaintances to lighten the lot of the wretched, kept the gates of his house always open, and sent each visitor away with whatever he could give. Word of him reached Peter I, who made him first a court priest and then confessor to the whole reigning house; the rank changed nothing in his way of living except to widen the giving, and from then on it was the almshouses and the prisons he visited most. The second life adds that he strengthened the innocently condemned in patience and paid off the debts of men imprisoned for them.

Then «дело вора Гришки Талицына» — the affair of Grishka the thief — undid him, and both lives say the same thing about it: Grishka had opened his plot to him under confession, and he kept the seal of confession and did not inform the authorities. Under interrogation Grishka announced that he had confessed to his spiritual father. The denunciation went to the sovereign, who held the charge against his own confessor to be founded and ordered him sent to bishop Афанасий of Kholmogory to be tonsured a monk at Solovki. He went to the far north without a murmur in 1701, was received with love by the bishop and by the archimandrite Фирс, and was tonsured with the name Иов in the monastery's chief cathedral of the Transfiguration; the former court priest was put under the elder Иона and worked at the harshest obediences with the rest, eating nothing but bread and water. When the brotherhood saw what he was they released him from the obediences so that he could give the whole of his time to prayer, fasting and the word of God. The report of him grew again and reached the tsar, who had by then satisfied himself that his confessor had been slandered and called him back; the old man refused and asked leave to withdraw into the Anzer skete for silence instead, and was made its builder.

The first life gives him eight years in that office and says the brethren grew to thirty under him, and that the particular labour he took on himself was the nursing of the sick — he washed them and bound up their sores, and not seldom healed them by prayer. He liked to withdraw for silence, and sometimes visited the hermit Паисий, one of the younger disciples of Eleazar of Anzersky, who lived on Секирная гора. In 1710, at his own wish, he was tonsured into the great schema with the name Иисус, after the righteous Joshua the son of Nun. Afterwards, on the mountain, the Mother of God appeared to him in a light sleep together with Eleazar himself, and he started up and bowed before his visitors in trembling; «Эта гора отныне назовётся второю Голгофою», she said — this mountain shall from now on be called the second Golgotha, a stone church shall be built on it in the name of my Son and Lord, a skete shall be established for your dwelling with your disciples, and I myself will visit the mountain and will remain with you for ever — and a voice came at once from heaven, «Освяти гору Голгофу и поставь на ней крест». He and his friend Паисий went up and consecrated the mountain without delay. The building was blessed in 1713 by the bishop Варнава; a church in honour of the Crucifixion of the Lord was put up and consecrated in 1715, cells were made and twenty brethren gathered, and the reigning house gave generously towards it. He laid the same foundations for the common life that Eleazar had laid in the Anzer skete, carried water up the mountain himself and cut the firewood, and by his prayer, after a second appearing of the Mother of God, a spring opened at the summit.

He dug his own grave and prayed beside it. Before the end he fell into a fever and lay three days unconscious, then recovered so far that he could serve the Liturgy; after it he lay down again, named his disciple Макарий to the governance of the skete after him, and in the night of 6 March 1720 — the Sunday of Orthodoxy — died. His face shone, and he said «Благословен Бог отец наших! Если так есть, я уже не боюсь, но в радости отхожу от мира сего!», blessed be the God of our fathers; if it is so, then I am no longer afraid, but go out of this world in joy. That same minute the cell was lit by a light from heaven. He was buried in the grave he had dug, and a church was built over it. He was numbered among the venerable of the Russian Church for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000, which is the latest canonisation date any of the four calendars gives for him.

**The two Russian lives disagree about when he came to Anzer and about whom he succeeded, and this corpus can date the disagreement.** The first says he withdrew after being freed from his obediences and was appointed builder when the previous builder died, and gives no year; the second says he moved in 1702 for greater silence and was appointed superior «вскоре по смерти Анзерского строителя Елеазара», soon after the death of Eleazar the builder of Anzer. But the same calendar keeps Eleazar on 13 января and this corpus records his death in 1656, forty-six years before 1702 — and the second life itself prints Eleazar's memory day in brackets where it names him in the vision. So the two accounts are one man's dossier written twice, and the second has telescoped two builders into one; nothing is changed in the dates on that account, because neither life dates the appointment and the one year either of them gives, 1702, is for the move and not for the office.

Three other things the Russian pages carry and this folder does not take. **The display name calls him a hermit and the calendar does not.** Every Russian page grades him прп., venerable, and the office recorded here is the one both lives say in words — священноинок and then иеросхимонах, a hieroschemamonk — while the English display name, made off the Greek «Όσιος Ιώβ ο Ερημίτης», keeps the hermit; he lived in a skete of twenty and thirty men and governed it, which is not what the word usually means, and the name is left as it stands because renaming a folder is the author's. **The relic-finding is not entered.** Both life pages head themselves «Дни памяти: 6 марта, 29 мая (обретение мощей)», and the finding of his relics on 29 мая is a second feast this folder deliberately does not carry a row for. **And the hymns are not taken here.** days.pravoslavie.ru prints no hymn of his on the day page at all — the four it prints are the two of the forty-two of Amorion and the two of the icon «Благодатное Небо» — while azbyka.ru's page for the same civil day prints him a troparion in the fifth tone beginning «Безмо́лвия и́стинный рачи́тель быв», a second troparion in the same tone, a kontakion in the eighth beginning «Боже́ственная восхожде́ния в се́рдце стяжа́в» and a second kontakion beside it. The incipits are reported and the texts are left, because the day page of the source of record is where this wave takes a Russian hymn from and it gives him none.
