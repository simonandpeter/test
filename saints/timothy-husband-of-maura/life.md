# Timothy, husband of Maura

In the time of the persecutions Christians were hunted out and brought for examination before Arian, governor of the Thebaid, set there by Diocletian. Among those brought to him was a young man named Timothy, a reader of the Church, from the village of Pirapei, who not long before had been joined to a maiden named [Maura](/saints/maura-wife-of-timothy); twenty days after the wedding he was seized as a Christian. Asked who he was and of what rank, he answered: «Sunt creștin, citeț al Bisericii lui Dumnezeu» — I am a Christian, a reader of the Church of God.

The governor demanded his books, to understand their power. Timothy refused him: what man gives his own sons over to death? The books he had written were his sons, and when he read them the angels of God stood about him. Two irons sharpened like stakes and heated red were then driven into both his ears, and from that pain the sight ran out of both his eyes. He answered that the eyes of his body, which had seen many unseemly things, had now suffered this blindness, while with the eyes of salvation his Lord lit his soul.

He was hung head downward with his hands bound behind him, a piece of wood forced between his teeth and a heavy stone tied to him. Maura was sent to him, scented and adorned, to turn him back; smelling the perfume on her clothes he called to his father, the priest Picolpos, who stood watching in the crowd, and asked him to cover his face against it. She answered that she had been his wife barely twenty days, that she knew neither his ways nor the rooms of his house, and that she wept to see him in torment.

He told her to leave the short and empty things of this world and come with him to that beautiful struggle, for which they would be given crowns. She said that she had come to him with her heart full of worldly attachment, but that once he began to speak the Spirit of God entered into her, and that she now desired what he loved. She feared she could not endure the sight of the torments, being only seventeen; he prayed aloud that God, who had helped the three youths in the fire and Daniel among the lions, would not let them be parted in this contest.

She went and stood before Arian and rebuked him to his face, saying that she had put on the armour of her Lord Jesus Christ. He offered to marry her to one of his richest centurions; she answered that she was joined to the heavenly Bridegroom. He had the hair torn from her head and her fingers cut off and thrown away, and she thanked him for taking the fingers with which she had put on her deceiving ornaments. Thrown into a great cauldron boiling like thunder, she stood in the middle of it unhurt, and when the governor came to test the water she poured it over his hands and scalded them, so that he cried out that blessed is the God of Maura.

He threatened to fill her mouth with live coals, and she answered with Isaiah and the seraph's coal, asking that not her mouth only but her face and head and whole body be covered with coals, that she might be a sweet savour of Christ. The crowd cried out to him to stop inventing new torments for so young a girl, and she turned and told them each to mind their own work. **There doxologia's account stops.** The page prints no more: not the death of either of them, and no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's notice gives the place and the office. He and [Maura](/saints/maura-wife-of-timothy) were born in the little town of Panapea in the Thebaid of Egypt, it says, and Timothy was a reader and a preacher of the Gospel in the church of the Panapeans, and carried out his duties with the warmest zeal and with wonderful results. That was what stirred the envy of the idolaters, who in the end denounced him to the ruthless pagan prefect Arrianos, barely twenty days after his marriage to Maura.

Arrianos called on him to give his sacred books to the fire. Timothy refused outright to destroy them, telling the prefect that he held them for his sacred spiritual weapons and supplies; and the prefect, enraged, put him to a succession of tortures which he bore with a proverbial patience. First they put red-hot irons into the martyr's ears, so that the pupils of his eyes melted and fell to the ground; then they bound his ankles to a wheel and sealed his mouth with a gag; then they tied a heavy stone to his neck and hung him from a tree.

To break his strength the prefect turned to his wife, flattering her at first so that she would sacrifice to the idols and not follow her husband's deadly road; but at Timothy's urging Maura confessed her own faith in God, and was tortured in her turn. Arrianos then had the two of them crucified together, and saint.gr says that nine days passed on the cross before they both gave up their souls to the Lord. The page gives no year for any of it.

The Serbian church keeps the two on this same menologion day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović is the only one of the three accounts that reaches their death and gives it a year. Strange, it begins, is the fate of these wonderful martyrs, the bridegroom and the bride: twenty days after their wedding they were brought to trial for the Christian faith before Arian, governor of the Thebaid, in the time of the emperor Diocletian. Timothy was a reader of the church in his own place. Asked who he was, he answered that he was a Christian and a reader of the Church of God.

The governor asked whether he did not see the instruments of torture made ready about him, and Timothy answered: „И ти не видиш ангеле Божје који ме крепе“ — and you do not see the angels of God who strengthen me. Then the governor had his ears pierced through with an iron rod, so that from the pain the pupils of his eyes started out. Maura was frightened by the torments at first; but when her husband gave her courage she too confessed her unshakable faith before the governor, who had all her hair torn out and then the fingers of her hands cut off.

After many other torments, under which they would soon have sunk had the grace of God not held them up, the two of them were crucified **facing each other**. So hanging on the cross they stayed alive nine full days, counselling and encouraging one another to endure; and on the tenth day they gave up their spirits to the Lord, for whom they had borne the death of the cross, and so were counted worthy of His Kingdom. **They suffered honourably for Christ in the year 286** — the first year either of these two folders has carried, where doxologia's account stops before the death and the Greek notice gives none.
