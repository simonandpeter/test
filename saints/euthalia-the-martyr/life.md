# Euthalia

Euthalia lived in Sicily. Her mother, also Euthalia, suffered from a flow of blood and saw in a dream the martyrs Alphaeus, Philadelphus and Cyprinus, kept on 10 May, who told her that if she believed in Christ and was baptised she would be healed and saved; waking, she believed their word, and with her daughter received holy baptism and was healed.

Her son Sermilianus, hearing that his mother had become a Christian, tried to strangle her, and she escaped with the help of a maidservant. Then Euthalia rebuked her brother boldly; he beat her and gave her to his servant to be dishonoured, but at her prayer the man was struck blind. Seeing the miracle, Sermilianus — another Cain — cut off his sister’s head, and the blessed Euthalia received the unfading crown of martyrdom.

The Greek calendar keeps her twice over, and saint.gr is what ties the two days together. Its day page for the second of March prints «Αγία Ευθαλία η παρθενομάρτυς» and the notice behind that line tells this same life, in the same order and with the same names: the mother Euthalia with the flow of blood, the dream of the martyrs Alphaeus, Philadelphus and Cyprinus whose memory the page puts on the tenth of May, the son Sermilianus who tried to strangle her, the servant struck blind at Euthalia's prayer, and the brother who, like another Cain, beheaded his sister. Then it adds the one thing doxologia did not have: that in a codex numbered 73 of the monastery of the Panagia on Halki her memory is carried on the twenty-seventh of August — which is the day the Romanian calendar keeps her on and the day this folder was first written from. The corpus keeps one feast to a church, so the Greek row records the day page that was read and the codex's reading stands as a note on it.
