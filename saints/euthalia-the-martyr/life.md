# Euthalia

Euthalia lived in Sicily. Her mother, also Euthalia, suffered from a flow of blood and saw in a dream the martyrs Alphaeus, Philadelphus and Cyprinus, kept on 10 May, who told her that if she believed in Christ and was baptised she would be healed and saved; waking, she believed their word, and with her daughter received holy baptism and was healed.

Her son Sermilianus, hearing that his mother had become a Christian, tried to strangle her, and she escaped with the help of a maidservant. Then Euthalia rebuked her brother boldly; he beat her and gave her to his servant to be dishonoured, but at her prayer the man was struck blind. Seeing the miracle, Sermilianus — another Cain — cut off his sister’s head, and the blessed Euthalia received the unfading crown of martyrdom.

The Greek calendar keeps her twice over, and saint.gr is what ties the two days together. Its day page for the second of March prints «Αγία Ευθαλία η παρθενομάρτυς» and the notice behind that line tells this same life, in the same order and with the same names: the mother Euthalia with the flow of blood, the dream of the martyrs Alphaeus, Philadelphus and Cyprinus whose memory the page puts on the tenth of May, the son Sermilianus who tried to strangle her, the servant struck blind at Euthalia's prayer, and the brother who, like another Cain, beheaded his sister. Then it adds the one thing doxologia did not have: that in a codex numbered 73 of the monastery of the Panagia on Halki her memory is carried on the twenty-seventh of August — which is the day the Romanian calendar keeps her on and the day this folder was first written from. The corpus keeps one feast to a church, so the Greek row records the day page that was read and the codex's reading stands as a note on it.

The Serbian church keeps her on this day. The Ohrid Prologue tells the same life in the same order and differs in two small things. It says plainly that all three of them — Euthalia, her mother of the same name, and her brother Sermilianus — were unbaptised and heathen before the dream came; and it has the brother begin by mocking his mother and his sister for the faith of Christ and go on to threaten them, at which the mother took fright and fled the house, and only then did he turn on his sister.

She was not frightened, the Prologue says, because Christ was dearer to her than her brother, and she told Sermilianus that she was a Christian and was not afraid to die. The servant he sent to defile her lost the sight of his eyes when he attacked her; the brother saw the wonder and stayed hard of heart, pursued her, caught her and cut off her head, like Cain. The entry closes by reading her death against the Lord's own word that He had brought a sword among men, which divides those who are kin by blood and not by faith.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps her on the Julian 2 March and is the first of the four sources read for her to give her a year: «Мц. Евфалии (257).» Its life is the one this folder already carries, told in a few sentences. She lived with her mother and her brother in the town of Leontini on the island of Sicily. Her mother, a pagan, was long and grievously ill, and one night the martyrs [Alphaeus](/saints/alphaeus-martyr-10-may), [Philadelphus](/saints/philadelphus-martyr-10-may) and [Cyprian](/saints/cyprian-martyr-10-may), whose memory it puts on 10 May, appeared to her in sleep and told her she would be healed if she believed in Christ.

The mother was baptised with her daughter and was healed of her sickness. But the brother, a pagan, learning that his own people had been baptised, fell into a violent rage; the mother managed to escape, and Euthalia confessed herself a Christian before him and took a martyr’s death. After a cruel beating she was beheaded with the sword. The day prints no hymn under her name, and the calendar gives her two toponymics on its name link, Leontini and Sicily, where the Romanian, the Greek and the Serbian gave her none.
