# Arsenios the Hagiopharangite

saint.gr's calendar for 14 July names Arsenios the Hagiopharangite — of the Agiofarango, the holy gorge on the south coast of Crete — and what the page knows of him it knows through another man's life. It says that Arsenios initiated [Gregory of Sinai](/saints/gregory-of-sinai) into the inward, watchful work of the prayer of the mind, as published Greek and Slavonic manuscripts report, and that the prayer of the mind passed from Arsenios, through Gregory, to the Holy Mountain.

The long extract the page prints, from the Life of Gregory in the Moscow and Lavra codices, tells how Gregory came by sea to Crete after venerating the holy places of Jerusalem, stopped at the harbour called Kaloi Limenes because of the swell, and settled in caves he and his disciple found there. He kept to one meal a day and a little water, and still looked for someone who could lead him further than he had come. Arsenios was the man in that place who embraced stillness more than the rest.

Gregory knocked at his cell and was taken in gladly, and the two of them prayed and sat down. The page describes Arsenios as a ripe, white-haired and grave man, who opened a divine and holy book and spoke of the guarding of the mind, of sincere watchfulness and of pure prayer — how, by the working of the commandments, the mind is cleansed, and the man who so cares and meditates in the love of God becomes wholly light.

When Gregory had told his own story, his withdrawal from the world, his love of the desert and the labours he had chosen to bear, Arsenios smiled slightly and said that all of it is what the God-bearing fathers call practice, and never contemplation. Gregory fell at his feet and begged to be taught what prayer and stillness are; he was taught without reserve or delay, and then rose, boarded a ship, and went to the mountain of Athos.

The page gives no year for Arsenios, no birthplace, nothing of his end, and no hymn. The Agiofarango and that one encounter are all it holds.
