# Raphael of Agapia Veche

Raphael is the second of the two hermits the Romanian calendar keeps on 21 iulie at Agapia Veche, beside [Parthenius](/saints/parthenius-of-agapia-veche), and doxologia.ro prints one life for the pair of them. The greater part of that page is left here unsummarised; what it says of Raphael comes at the end, and it gives him no year of birth or death and no century of his own.

By long struggle, the page says, he came to the gaining of high gifts, which can hardly be set out in words. God who loves mankind and wills the salvation of all appointed him to be the guide of the brethren of the community, for by his angelic life and by a wisdom above nature he was a wonder to everyone. In the Holy Register and in the old commemoration lists he is called the Blessed Elder Raphael, and all looked on him as an icon of the virtues.

He was a lamp of light to the hermits, and also a loving father to the Christians of the villages of Moldavia, who came to him as to a harbour, for prayers, for healings and for help in every kind of need, in times of peace and in times of danger for the country.

After he had passed from this world his disciples, knowing his life and his struggles — it is said that wonders were worked at his grave — dug up his body and found it full of divine glory and sweet-smelling, and laid it in the church for veneration. It was a treasure of great price for Moldavia, for the faithful came to venerate his holy relics, above all at the patronal feast of the monastery of Neamț, when the pilgrimage to the monasteries of Neamț, Agapia and Secu took place. At Agapia Veche that pilgrimage continues today, although the whole bodies of the saints are hidden; parts of those relics, it seems, are to be found in some of the monasteries of Moldavia.
