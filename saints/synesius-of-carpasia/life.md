# Synesius of Carpasia

saint.gr says at the outset that the material for this Cypriot saint is unfortunately very poor, and that his synaxarion and his service between them offer very little. What they offer is this: Synesius was born at Carpasia; he was a man meek and free of resentment; and he succeeded Saint Philon on the episcopal throne, whose sacred work he took up with zeal and completed.

Against the heresies, which by Saint Epiphanius's count were then more than eighty in number, he moved with fire and Christian prudence. With the missionary's staff and the sword of the Spirit the page has him take up the hard struggle to meet them and break them up; and his meekness, the sweetness of his words and his persuasive arguments, strengthened by his many miracles, brought their results everywhere, so that in a few years the holy island found its peace again.

Even when he had fallen asleep, the page says, he did not stop granting cures and gifts to those who take refuge in his grace, and the people of Carpasia tell many of his miracles with reverence and gratitude. He is honoured particularly as a healer of difficulty in passing water — and as one who punishes with that same illness anybody who dares to try to make away with his property. Among the people, saint.gr adds, it is held that this kindly bishop grants his gifts without asking dear or precious things in return: he is very easily satisfied, and one onion is enough to content him and have his healing. There is one thing only he will not have, and that is his own things meddled with or taken away.
