# Aphrodisius

Aphrodisius was from the country of Cilicia. He believed from childhood and worshipped Christ, and he was taken and brought before the ruler Dionysius; and when he confessed Christ to be the true God, he was burned across the back with heated irons, put into a cauldron full of lead boiling and bubbling, and hung up head downwards.

He was delivered from all of it by a wonder, and by that wonder drew many of the pagans to faith in Christ. They confessed Christ with boldness, and they too were beheaded. Seeing it, the tyrant had a great stone cut in two, and the saint stretched out, and that cut stone laid over him; fifty-five soldiers had lifted it. At once the saint gave up his soul and took the crown of martyrdom.

That is the whole of what doxologia prints for him: the country, the ruler's name, the torments and the end, with no year, no bishopric and no place of burial. The calendar prints no hymn for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 21 June. saint.gr says that Aphrodisius glorified the land of Cilicia by his martyrdom, that he was full of divine zeal and worked for the enlightening of the unbelieving, exposing the falsehood of idolatry and teaching the truth of the Gospel, and that in the year 65 the ruler Dionysius, learning of it, condemned him to death. It tells the same tortures this life already carries, and adds one wonder before them: at first he was thrown as food to a lioness in the amphitheatre, and the beast's rush stopped a few paces from him, and the hungry animal passed him by without touching him. Then they burned him with red-hot iron, and at the last he met a martyr's death between two great stones, where they had put him and crushed him; his body was literally broken to pieces, the page says, but the strength of his soul and his faith were kept whole.
