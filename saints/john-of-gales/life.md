# John of Galeș

About the middle of the eighteenth century, the Romanian life says, many defenders of the right faith rose from among the Orthodox Romanian priests and people of Transylvania against the attempts of the Habsburg authorities to draw them away from the "ancestral law". Among them were the priests [Moses Măcinic](/saints/moses-macinic-of-sibiel) of Sibiel and John of Galeș, villages that then belonged to the seat of Săliște.

John was ordained at Bucharest or at Râmnic, there being no Orthodox bishop in Transylvania, and he was among the boldest defenders of the Orthodox faith against every attempt to impose another. In 1756 he was arrested and taken in chains to Sibiu. The empress Maria Theresa ordered him held in the prison of the fortress of Deva until his death; but towards the end of the next year he was taken under heavy guard to the prison at Graz in Austria.

A chronicler of Brașov, Radu Duma, wrote that in 1776 some merchants of that city visited him in prison, and that he told them he would sooner die there than give up the faith of his fathers. Later he was moved to Kufstein, where other confessors and martyrs of Transylvanian Orthodoxy had ended their lives.

In 1780 another prisoner there, the Serb Ghenadie Vasici, got a letter out to the empress Catherine II and the Synod of the Russian Church asking them to intervene for his release, and wrote in it: "Here in the fortress there is also a Romanian priest from Transylvania, by the name of John, who has suffered in captivity for twenty-four years for the Orthodox faith". That was John of Galeș, imprisoned in 1756. For their Orthodox witness and their martyr's death he and Moses are honoured as saints, above all in Transylvania, out of whose midst they came. [Oprea](/saints/oprea-of-saliste) of Săliște, who went with Moses to Vienna, died in the same prison.
