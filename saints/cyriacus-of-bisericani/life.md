# Cyriacus of Bisericani

The Romanian life says Cyriacus struggled at the beginning of the seventeenth century at the monastery of Bisericani, which then had more than a hundred monks. He withdrew into the wilderness, to a cave on Simon’s mountain, and lived there alone in hard labour for sixty years, summer and winter on the mountain with his body bare, in pure prayer, overcoming by the power of Christ the weakness of nature and the temptations of the devil.

Dosoftei, metropolitan of Moldavia, knew him, and after his death kissed his relics; he is remembered as one of the great hermits of the Carpathians. His relics were laid in the cave where he had lived, which can still be seen and where a chapel was later made in his honour; later still, because of the troubles in the land, they were divided. The Romanian calendar keeps him with [Joseph of Bisericani](/saints/joseph-of-bisericani), the first known hermit of that mountain, in one troparion and kontakion.
