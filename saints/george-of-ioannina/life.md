# George of Ioannina

George is held to be the spiritual protector of Ioannina. He came from the place called Tzourhli, which now carries his name, and was left an orphan of both parents at eight years old; he was poor and unlettered, and in time took service as a groom to a Turkish commander, whose people would not call him by his Christian name but called him «ghiaur Hassan». When his master moved to Ioannina in 1836 he became engaged to a poor Christian girl named Elena.

A Turk who knew him was astonished at the engagement and charged him with being a Muslim marrying a Christian. Before the judge George said he was a Christian and was not believed, and was sent on to the vizier; there he confessed the Orthodox faith again, and the vizier asked his master, who declared that his servant was a Christian, so that the marriage went forward. He followed his master for a while to Prousa in Asia Minor, then returned to Ioannina, took service with another commander, and stayed behind with his wife and their son John when that master left.

Going into the market one day to look for work he met the same accuser, who questioned him closely about his faith; the quarrel drew a crowd, and he was brought before a judge who knew from the record that he was a Christian and used the occasion to imprison him and press him to deny the faith. After many torments he was condemned on the feast of Saint Anthony, and went to the gallows giving glory to God with his face shining. At the city gate he said once more that he was a Christian and worshipped Christ and the Most Holy Mother of God, asked the executioners to loose his hands, crossed himself, and called out to the Christians to forgive him. He was hanged, doxologia.ro's life says, on 27 January 1838, at thirty years old.

His body hung three days and three nights, and each night a heavenly light came down and wrapped it; after three days the Christians were allowed to bury him, in the courtyard on the north side of the metropolitan church of Saint Athanasius, where a chapel was later built over the grave because of the many miracles. The relics were taken up in 1975 and are now in the new church raised in his honour at Ioannina. In the icons he wears the short white pleated kilt, the fustanella, of the Greek and Albanian national dress, and he is the official patron of the presidential guard in Athens.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr fills out the account. He was born in 1808 at Tzourchli in the district of Grevena, of poor farming parents, Constantine and Vasilo; he was orphaned young, went to Ioannina, and served eight years as groom to an officer of Emin Pasha. Slandered in October 1836 as an apostate from Islam, he answered the judge boldly and was let go. He married a wife named Helen, and their son, born on 30 December 1837, was baptised John on 7 January. On 12 January 1838 a second man laid the same charge; he was imprisoned, refused to escape when clergy and people urged it, confessed Christ before the judge three times, and was hanged in the market place on Monday 17 January 1838. His body hung three days, a light shining over his head each night, and healings followed in the city. saint.gr names him patron of Ioannina, and its account of the martyrdom is dated 17 January where doxologia.ro's gives 27 ianuarie.
