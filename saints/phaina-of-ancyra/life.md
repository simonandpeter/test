# Phaina of Ancyra

Phaina was one of the seven virgins of Ancyra whom the Romanian calendar keeps on 18 mai with Christina, and whose names the calendar itself does not print. They are read out of the life of [Theodotus of Ancyra](/saints/theodotus-of-ancyra) that doxologia prints for the same day, which names them [Thecusa](/saints/thecusa-of-ancyra), [Alexandra](/saints/alexandra-of-ancyra), [Claudia](/saints/claudia-of-ancyra), Phaina, [Euphrasia](/saints/euphrasia-of-ancyra), [Matrona](/saints/matrona-of-ancyra) and [Julia](/saints/julia-of-ancyra), and says that these holy virgins suffered on the eighteenth day of the month of May. The life is signed by Saint Nilus, who says he lived with Theodotus and was with him in prison, and writes only what he saw.

In the city, he writes, were seven virgins who from their youth had been strong in sober wisdom and in the fear of God, and had betrothed themselves in maiden purity to Christ, the incorruptible and deathless Bridegroom; and by the time of the persecution they had grown old. The eldest of them was the blessed Thecusa, the aunt of Theodotus. When the governor Theotecnus came to the borders of Ancyra the churches emptied at once, and the hills were full of people who had fled.

He seized the seven and put them to many torments and could not force them to the idolatry. Then he gave them over to some debauched young men to be defiled, mocking the Christian faith. The virgins sighed from the bottom of their hearts and lifted their eyes and prayed: Lord Jesus Christ, as long as it was in our power to keep our virginity clean, Thou knowest with what watching and what care we have kept it unstained until today. Thecusa caught the boldest of the youths by the feet, threw the covering from her head and showed him her grey hairs, and asked him to be ashamed before them and to think of his own mother; and the young men were pricked to the heart, left off their desire, wept, and went away without harming them.

They were drowned afterwards in the lake, with stones at their necks, for refusing to serve the idols at the washing of them. Theodotus, with Polychronius and Theocharides, went out at night to take up their bodies; Thecusa appeared to him in a vision and reproached his sleeping; the soldiers set to watch the lake fled at the sight of an armed man shining like fire, whom the life names as the martyr Sosander, and the wind drove the water to the far bank so that the bodies were seen. They cut the ropes from their necks with sickles, carried them away in carts and buried them beside the church of the holy patriarchs.

In the morning the theft was known, Christians were seized and examined, and Polychronius betrayed the burial. The pagans dug up the relics of the seven and burned them, and Theodotus gave himself up and was beheaded. The life records no year for any of them, and none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same civil day, in the same company, and adds what they were doing when they were denounced. saint.gr says they came from Ancyra in Galatia, gave themselves wholeheartedly to the service of the Gospel, and were among the greatest servants and contenders of the Christian faith; always most ready in works of mercy and charity, they worked together at drawing idolatrous women into the bosom of the Church. They were denounced for that work and refused to sacrifice to the idols, and Theoteknos, the governor of Ancyra, handed them over to the soldiers to be corrupted; because by God's grace they were kept pure, all of them were drowned in the depths of the lake there. saint.gr spells her name Phaeine, which is the form recorded here.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 18 May, in an entry headed for [Theodotus](/saints/theodotus-of-ancyra) and the seven maiden martyrs, whose names it prints in its heading: Thecusa, Alexandra, Claudia, Phaina, Euphrasia, Matrona and Julia — the same seven the Greek and Romanian books name, and not the list the Russian calendar gives, which puts Polactia and Athanasia among them.

In the body of the entry the seven are spoken of only together. At that time, it says, seven maidens were brought to trial and tortured for Christ; they were tortured and mocked, and at the last drowned in the lake. One of them, the holy Thecusa, appeared in a dream to Theodotus and told him to take their bodies out of the lake and bury them. The Prologue gives the maidens no year of their own. The heading names her fourth of the seven, in the place where the Russian line prints Polactia.
