# Hermogenes

Hermogenes was born at Kazan about the year 1530. By his own testimony he served as a priest in a church of Saint Nicholas near the market there. Later he took the monastic habit, and from 1582 was archimandrite of the monastery of the Transfiguration at Kazan; on 13 May 1589 he was consecrated bishop and became the first metropolitan of Kazan. It was while he was serving in the church of Saint Nicholas that the icon of the Mother of God of Kazan was found.

With the blessing of Archbishop Jeremiah of Kazan he carried that icon in his own arms from the place where it was found to the church where he served, and, having the gift of writing, he set down in 1594 the account of its finding and of the miracles worked by its grace. In 1591 he gathered the newly baptised Tatars into the church and taught them the right faith for several days together. In 1592 the relics of Germanus, archbishop of Kazan, who had died at Moscow on 6 November 1567 in the time of the plague, were brought to that same church of Saint Nicholas, and later, with the blessing of Patriarch Job, Hermogenes moved them to the monastery of the Dormition at Sviyazhsk.

On 9 January 1592 he wrote to Patriarch Job asking his blessing for a commemoration at Kazan of the soldiers who had given their lives for the right faith and for their country in the fighting against the Tatars, and he named three martyrs who had suffered at Kazan for faith in Christ: John, of Nizhny Novgorod, who had been taken by the Tatars and is kept on 24 January, and Stephen and Peter, Tatars of the right faith, who are kept on 24 March.

He was patriarch of Moscow in the years of the invasion, and from his imprisonment he would not give the order the enemy wanted. I fear God alone, he answered them: if our enemies leave Moscow I will bless the Russian militia to withdraw from it, and if they stay I bless it to stand unshaken and give its life for the true faith. His last letter to the Russian people, written from prison, blessed the army's victory over the invaders.

The Russian commanders could not agree on taking the Kremlin and freeing him, and so he suffered nine months of dreadful confinement, until on 17 February 1612 hunger took him and he gave up his zealous soul into the hands of the Lord. The land was freed afterwards, as he had blessed it to be. His body was buried with great honour in the Chudov monastery, and in 1654 was moved to the cathedral of the Dormition in Moscow, where many miracles were worked. The Church glorified him with the saints on 12 May 1913, and keeps him on 17 February, on 12 May, and on 5 October with the synaxis of the hierarchs of Moscow. Doxologia prints no troparion for him.
