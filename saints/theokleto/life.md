# Theokleto

saint.gr says the venerable Theokleto lived in the ninth century, in the years of the emperor Theophilos the iconoclast, and that she came «ἐκ τοῦ θέματος τῶν Ὀπτιμάτων» — out of the theme of the Optimatoi, which the page glosses as the Opsikion. Her parents were called Constantine and Anastasia, and they were rich enough and, above all things, pious Christians. They gave their daughter an upbringing to match, so that she was faithful, reverent and modest, and studied the divine word without ceasing.

When she married — Zacharias, a man pious and merciful — she was, the page says, a pattern of a Christian wife. Her house stood open without a break to the wretched, to widows, to orphans and to poor heads of families, and she would often go herself to the houses of the poor, giving alms with an open hand and tending the sick. Death found her at work such as that.

Her relic, the page goes on, was granted to become an instrument of many healings and wonders, and of the many it tells one. Her kinsfolk had the custom, every year, of raising her holy relic, which is whole and entire, and changing her clothes for new ones. They straighten too the hair of her head, which is white; then they cut the nails of her hands and of her feet, which have grown as though she were alive; and at the last they sing the Trisagion and lay her body back in its case.

The page closes with a note of its own: that in codex 73 of the monastery of the Panagia of Chalki she is called Theoklete, and that her memory is put there on 21 August. It prints no hymn to her.
