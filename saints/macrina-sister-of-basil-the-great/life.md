# Macrina, sister of Basil the Great

The life doxologia.ro prints for Macrina is not a compiler's summary but a letter: it is the letter [Gregory of Nyssa](/saints/gregory-of-nyssa), her own brother, wrote to Olympius the monk — a letter, as the page's opening says, whose matter overran the bounds of a letter and spread into a narrative and the writing of a life.

Gregory begins by reminding Olympius of a conversation they had when he met him at Antioch, as Olympius was setting out for Jerusalem to venerate the holy places. Among many things they spoke of, the memory of the blessed Macrina's manner of life came up — a story, Gregory says, that he did not have at second hand from others but from testing and knowing it himself, since she was his sister, the first born of the same parents. Since Olympius judged it a gain to the soul that her life should not be forgotten and hidden in silence, Gregory agreed to tell it in few and short words, plainly and without art.

This virgin, he writes, was called Macrina by their parents: that was the name she bore openly, but she had another name in secret, given her by a divine vision before she was born. Their mother [Emilia](/saints/emilia-mother-of-basil-the-great) was a woman of great virtue who wished to keep her virginity and pass her life unblemished, but she was left an orphan of father and mother and was very beautiful, so that many pressed to take her as a wife, and had she not married a man of her own will she stood in danger of being carried off by force by one of those who were wounded with desire for her.

The letter as doxologia prints it is very long — the greater part of it is left here unsummarised — and it closes with Gregory explaining why he stops. He says he will not tell the glorious provision the blessed one made in the time of famine, when the wheat she took out of the barn and shared to the poor was not diminished at all but stayed whole, because those who measure such things by their own experience take them for a lie and do not believe them.

She worked other wonders still more glorious than that, he writes — healings of the sick, cures of the possessed, and prophecies of things to come — which those who have true and whole knowledge believe to be all of them true, however incredible they seem, since they are above nature, while the dull-minded and carnal reckon them impossible; for they do not know that the divine gifts are given by the measure of a man's faith, small to the little-believing and great to those whose faith is great and ample. And so, he says, he has left the most glorious of the wonders God worked through Macrina unwritten, lest the little-believing be harmed, reckoning what he has written enough for a comprehensive account of her life.

The Romanian calendar names her as the sister of [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great), and the page gives her no year of birth or death.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 19 July, and saint.gr tells in a page what Gregory's letter tells at length, with a few things the letter as doxologia prints it does not say outright. God had endowed her with many gifts of soul and of body, it says, and she was the elder sister of [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great) and of [Gregory of Nyssa](/saints/gregory-of-nyssa); she was brought up according to the commands of the Gospel by her most pious mother [Emilia](/saints/emilia-mother-of-basil-the-great). When she had grown she devoted herself to good works and to the rearing of her brothers, which played a decisive part in the spiritual course they afterwards took. Macrina had been betrothed, but her betrothed died; then, together with her mother, she withdrew to a women's monastery in Pontus, near the river Iris, where her brother Basil was living as a monk nearby. In the monastery she passed her life in the study of the Scriptures, in prayer, and above all in works of charity, having always in her mind the word of God that bids men do good, be rich in good works, be ready to share, and so lay up for themselves a good foundation for the time to come. The page gives her years as 324 to 379, and lists parts of her relics at the monasteries of Iviron and Stavronikita on Athos, at Panachrantou on Andros, and at Kykkos in Cyprus.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 19 July, calls her the eldest sister of [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great) and of [Gregory of Nyssa](/saints/gregory-of-nyssa), and is the only one of this folder’s three books to give her the words of her own vow. As a virgin she was betrothed early to a young man of good birth; but when her betrothed died she vowed never to enter into marriage, saying that it is not right for a girl betrothed to one bridegroom to go after another, and that by the law of nature there should be one marriage as there is one birth and one death.

She justified it further by faith in the resurrection, reckoning her betrothed not dead but alive in God. It is a sin and a shame, she said, for a wife not to keep faith when her husband has gone away into some far country. Then, together with her mother [Emilia](/saints/emilia-mother-of-basil-the-great), she received the monastic habit in a convent of virgins, where the two of them struggled with the other nuns; and they lived by the labour of their own hands, giving the greater part of their time to the thought of God, to prayer and to the unceasing lifting up of their minds to him.

In time her mother died, and after her mother her brother Basil. In the ninth month after Basil’s death Gregory came to visit his sister, and found her on her deathbed. Just before her death she raised this prayer to the Lord: thou, Lord, layest our bodies to rest in the sleep of death for a time, and thou wilt wake them again with the last trumpet; forgive me, and grant that when the soul is stripped of the body’s clothing it may stand before thee undefiled and without sin, and be as incense before thee.

Then she made the sign of the cross with her hand on her brow, on her eyes, on her face, on her heart — and breathed out. She reposed in the Lord in the year 379, which is the year saint.gr gives for her death as well.
