# Anastasia the Patrician

Anastasia came of noble and wealthy parents and was, saint.gr says, one of the most God-fearing young women of Byzantium in the years of Justinian the Great. She was the first lady in waiting of the empress Theodora, and Justinian, for her surpassing worth, gave her the title of patrician, by which the calendar still names her. Her virtues drew the envy of the empress; and Anastasia, to put out every occasion of that envy, took a part of her property and withdrew to Alexandria.

There, at a place called Pempton, she built a monastery which came to be called the monastery of the Patrician, and lived an ascetic life in it. But when she learned that Justinian was searching for her she left the monastery and went to the Skete of abba Daniel and told him what had happened. He dressed her in man's clothes and renamed her Anastasios, and settled her in a cave beside the Skete, where two monks brought her what she needed; and he told her never to come out of the cave and never to receive anybody. She stayed shut up there twenty-eight years.

When she felt her end coming she called for abba Daniel, and having communicated of the immaculate mysteries she gave up her righteous soul. The page keeps for her one distich, «H Πατρικία πάντα λιπούσα τάδε, / Πάντων κατέστη κυρία εν τω πόλω» — the patrician, having left all these things behind, became lady of all in heaven — and it prints no hymn and names no relic.

The Serbian church keeps her on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives her five sentences and the reason she left the city. She was a patrician and a lady at the court of the emperor [Justinian](/saints/justinian-the-great); and when she was widowed, and saw that the empress Theodora could not bear her, she disappeared all at once out of Constantinople and was found in the Egyptian desert.

The famous spiritual father Abba Daniel tonsured her and gave her out as the monk Anastasius the eunuch, at her own wish, so that as a woman under a man's appearance she might be saved the more easily and might also be hidden from the emperor's searches for her. She shut herself in a narrow cell, where she passed twenty-eight years and where she reposed in the year 563. Before her death, the entry ends, the elder Daniel saw her face shining like the sun.

The Russian church keeps her on this same day, and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery tells the story in the same order and adds the years. She lived at Constantinople, of an aristocratic family, was a pattern of virtue to many and stood high in the esteem of the emperor Justinian, whose reign it gives as 527 to 565; widowed early, she resolved to leave the world, left the capital in secret for Alexandria, and founded a small monastery near the city. When Justinian was widowed in his turn and set about finding her to marry her, she went at once to a far-off skete for help, and the elder there dressed her in a man's monastic habit, called her the eunuch Anastasius and shut her in one of the most distant caves with a rule of prayer and an order never to leave it and to receive nobody.

One monk only knew the place, whose obedience was to bring a small loaf and a jug of water to the cave once a week and leave them at the entrance; and in that strict enclosure she lived twenty-eight years, everyone believing a eunuch named Anastasius was labouring there. The Lord made her day of death known to her, and she wrote a few words on a shard and laid it at the mouth of the cave; the elder came at once with what was needed for her burial, found her already dying, confessed her and gave her the holy Mysteries, blessed her at her own asking, and heard her say «Господи, в руки Твои предаю дух мой» before she quietly died about the year 567 or 568.

The page keeps the discovery for the end. When the grave was ready the elder gave his disciple his own cassock and told him to dress the departed brother, and the monk, dressing the body, understood that it was a woman and dared say nothing; and only on the way back to their own house, when he asked whether the elder had known, did the elder tell him her story, which was written down afterwards and became widely known. Her relics, it adds, were carried to Constantinople in the year 1200 and laid near the church of Holy Wisdom. The day page prints no hymn for her.
