# Anastasia the Patrician

Anastasia came of noble and wealthy parents and was, saint.gr says, one of the most God-fearing young women of Byzantium in the years of Justinian the Great. She was the first lady in waiting of the empress Theodora, and Justinian, for her surpassing worth, gave her the title of patrician, by which the calendar still names her. Her virtues drew the envy of the empress; and Anastasia, to put out every occasion of that envy, took a part of her property and withdrew to Alexandria.

There, at a place called Pempton, she built a monastery which came to be called the monastery of the Patrician, and lived an ascetic life in it. But when she learned that Justinian was searching for her she left the monastery and went to the Skete of abba Daniel and told him what had happened. He dressed her in man's clothes and renamed her Anastasios, and settled her in a cave beside the Skete, where two monks brought her what she needed; and he told her never to come out of the cave and never to receive anybody. She stayed shut up there twenty-eight years.

When she felt her end coming she called for abba Daniel, and having communicated of the immaculate mysteries she gave up her righteous soul. The page keeps for her one distich, «H Πατρικία πάντα λιπούσα τάδε, / Πάντων κατέστη κυρία εν τω πόλω» — the patrician, having left all these things behind, became lady of all in heaven — and it prints no hymn and names no relic.
