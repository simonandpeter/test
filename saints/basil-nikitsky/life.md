# Basil (Nikitsky)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him first of five presbyters on the one line it gives to fourteen people of 1938 for the Julian 1 March, and prints no life for any of them. Basil Petrovich Nikitsky was born on 3 January 1889 in the village of Aleksandrovskoye in the Volokolamsk district of the province of Moscow, the son of the psalmist Peter Nikitsky and of his wife Ekaterina, who had nine children. The family lived poorly: a house, three desyatinas of land and a cow were the whole of what they had. In 1905 his father went to Moscow to visit his brother and vanished, and every attempt to find him came to nothing.

The whole family was then on the mother and the eldest brother, and their position was desperate; to keep the small children from starving Ekaterina put them into an orphanage and went to work there herself as a cook. A woman of deep faith, spending her time in labour and prayer, she was able with God’s help to bring the children up and give them an education. He finished the Vifany theological seminary in 1913 and went as a teacher to the school attached to the factory at Pavlovsky Posad in the Bogorodsk district, and soon married Ekaterina, a teacher at the same school and the daughter of the priest Michael Nechaev; they had three children.

In December 1915 he was ordained priest to the church of the Nativity of the Mother of God in the village of Porechye in the Mozhaysk district, a church built at the expense of the local landowners, counts Razumovsky and Uvarov. In 1920 the authorities took him into the rear militia, where he spent half a year before illness got him released, and he came back to serve at Porechye. He stood high with his parishioners and many came to his house for advice; he had a large library of spiritual books and lent them to anyone who wanted them. When the father of the Kapaev family died and the widow was left with five children and nothing to live on, he and his wife came to her help at once.

In 1929 the authorities tried to close the church and he opposed them. A report of 30 August said that the priest, «выступая на собраниях, “обрабатывал” общественное мнение против закрытия церкви», that up to a thousand signatures had been gathered and the mood of the faithful greatly stirred. He was arrested on 4 September 1929 and held in the Butyrka prison in Moscow; the witnesses called were the secretary of the local Komsomol cell, a member of the church council and the parishioner in whose house the meeting had been held, and that last man, signing his statement, wrote under it that he had not himself heard the words put into his mouth.

On 18 November 1929 he was sentenced to three years’ exile in the Northern Krai and sent to the timber felling in the Vologda region. He came back to Porechye, was moved to Ilyinskoye in the Volokolamsk district, in 1934 to a church in the Taldom district, in 1937 to Borisovo in the Mozhaysk district for half a year, and then, when the authorities told him that church would be closed for unpaid taxes, to Teryaevo in the Volokolamsk district, where he began to serve on 18 January 1938.

The case against him was built out of two statements and a queue. The chairman of the village soviet wrote on 12 February 1938 that the priest was spreading word that the Soviet power would not let him serve or bury the dead in the cemetery, making people bury them like dogs, and that standing in the shop queue for galoshes he had said the Soviet power had nothing to sell — a hundred and fifty people in the line and twenty pairs of galoshes brought in. The witnesses heard on 14 February said he preached counter-revolutionary sermons but could not say what was in them.

He was arrested on 26 February 1938 and held in the prison at Volokolamsk. At his interrogation on 2 March he allowed that he had stood in the queue for galoshes and denied every word of the rest, and named the people who had come to his lodging: a deacon of the Spirovo church whose surname he did not know, a man from the village of Valuyki, and the former church warden Maria Boldina, whom he had met in Moscow at the Patriarchate and who had called him to serve at Teryaevo. On 4 March 1938 the troika of the НКВД sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on 14 March 1938 and buried in a common unmarked grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow.

azbyka.ru keeps him on 14 марта and on two moveable days besides — the synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered at Butovo and the synaxis of the saints of Moscow. Neither is a fixed day nor an offset from Pascha this corpus can hold, so neither is recorded as a feast. The grade is the one place the two hosts read differently and the reading is the calendar’s own index: the day’s line calls the five of them «пресвитеров» together and azbyka.ru heads him «пресвитер», while the name link behind his own name reads «иерей», a priest, which is what the office here records.
