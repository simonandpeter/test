# Helen (Chernova)

Helen Ivanovna Chernova was born in 1874 in the village of Alexandrovka
in the Bogimovo district of the Tarusa county of Kaluga province, to a
peasant family, and in time married; of her life, the short notice says,
we know very little. She was arrested on 28 September 1937 and sent to the
Butyrka prison, charged as a member of an illegal counter-revolutionary
monarchist organisation of churchmen, keeping the leaders of the
organisation in touch with the outside world — for her dealings with
priests and fools-for-Christ. A month after her arrest she was asked what
clergy, monks and churchmen she knew and what her relations with them
were: I am a believer, she answered, and as such I went for a time to the
church at the Semyonovskoye cemetery, where Fr Michael served — where he
is now I do not know; after it was closed I went to the church of the
Prophet Elijah at Cherkizovo, where the deacon Nicholas and the fathers
Simeon, Paul and Theodore served, and to the Yelokhovo church, where the
priests Nicholas, Demetrius and Basil served; I knew them as serving in
church and never spoke with them on any public subject. Which of the
so-called blessed ones did she know? A blind woman of about ninety-four
named Seraphima in the fourth Donskoy lane, whom she had known some five
years, sent to her by a paralysed woman, Maria, since dead, and whom she
visited two or three times a year before the feasts to clean her flat and
wash the floors — last about a year ago; Seraphima had advised her to go
to the church of the Placing of the Robe behind the Donskoy monastery,
where Fr Alexander Khotovitsky served, whose service she liked so much
that she went there on the feasts — where he is now I do not know. By the
inquiry’s information she carried on anti-Soviet and counter-revolutionary
agitation among believers — did she admit it? I carried on no anti-Soviet
or counter-revolutionary agitation among believers and do not admit myself
guilty of it. That was not enough, and she refused to say more or to
incriminate herself, and no witnesses were found against her, until on
16 November 1937 a priest, Paul Tsvetkov, testified that Chernova was a
person hostile to the Soviet power, as she had said more than once in
talk with believers — the Soviet power has set up a persecution of
religion, the clergy are condemned and exiled innocent, the churches are
closed — a great admirer of various blessed ones, a visitor of the
archpriest Khotovitsky, since exiled for counter-revolutionary activity,
and of the blessed Seraphima, and that after visiting such
counter-revolutionary elements she spread rumours of war, famine and the
imminent fall of the Soviet power. On 29 November 1937 the NKVD troika
sentenced her to eight years in the camps; on 27 December she reached the
distribution point of the Siblag, and on 1 May 1942 was sent to the
Mariinsk camps, the Baim section of the Siblag in the Kemerovo region,
where she died of hunger on 17 September 1943 — 4 September of the old
style, the day the Russian calendar keeps her — and was buried in the
section’s cemetery in grave no. 18. By decision of the Holy Synod of 26
December 2006 she was numbered among the saints, and the Russian calendar
keeps her also in the synaxis of the saints of Moscow.
