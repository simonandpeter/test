# Gregory of Akragas

In the parts of Sicily there is a village called Pretoria, near the city of Akragas, and there Gregory was born of Orthodox and virtuous parents. His father was called Chariton and his mother Theodotia, and they were very merciful to the poor, having much wealth out of which they gave no small alms to all. The child was received from holy baptism by the blessed Potamion, bishop of the Church of Akragas.

In his eighth year he was given to a skilled teacher named Damian, and in two years learned to read all the books well, and to write, and to sing the hymns of the church; in his twelfth year the bishop Potamion brought him to the church and entrusted him to Donatus the archdeacon, to guide him in spiritual labours and a life of good works. Day and night the boy sat over the books, reading the divine Scriptures and the lives of the holy fathers and burning at heart to follow them.

At twenty-two he wished to go to Jerusalem to venerate the Holy Sepulchre, and prayed that God would direct his way. One night, sleeping in the archdeacon's house, he was called three times by name; twice he ran to his elder, who had not called him, and the archdeacon, understanding that the calling was from God, told him how to answer. The third time he answered as he had been taught, and the angel said that his prayer was heard, and that he should go down to the shore and find those who were calling him to the Holy Places.

He rose at once, told nobody, and at the seashore found a ship bound for Carthage, whose master took him aboard; in three days they came to Carthage, where he often went to the church of the Martyr Julian. There three grave monks came in to pray — Abba Mark, with Serapion and Leontius — and they took him with them; and at Pascha they went up to the Holy City of Jerusalem, venerated the Holy Places, and came in to the patriarch Macarius for his blessing, who kept the young man with him.

Abba Mark and his companions, going on to Rome, passed by Akragas and lodged with the bishop Potamion for the feast of the Apostles Peter and Paul, and there Gregory's parents Chariton and Theodotia learned at last where their son was, and fell to the ground as though dead for joy. Gregory himself, after a year more in Jerusalem, went to Antioch and then to Constantinople, where he lived in a monastery and became so honoured by the emperor and so widely famous that he fled from human glory.

In that time Theodore, bishop of Akragas after Potamion, departed to the Lord, and there was great division and uproar in the city: some wanted the priest Sabinus for the bishopric, some Crescentius, some Euplus. Neither of the first two was counted worthy of it; Gregory was chosen instead, a stranger to the city, and when the people heard that a new bishop was coming they asked one another which of the three it was. He came, and went out to see the city and to visit the sick and the poor, and the citizens laid their sick along the road he was to pass, and every kind of illness was healed.

After some years the devil raised war against him through the two priests he had passed over. They hired a harlot with much gold to consent to their cunning plan, promising that she would come to no harm, and watched for their moment; and one night they broke into the bishop's room, brought the woman out before him and before all the people who had gathered, and cried: *See, men, what our bishop does.* He was put in prison, and a crowd of the faithful who did not believe the slander sat by it weeping, while he gave thanks to God that he was counted worthy to suffer.

The exarch came, sat in judgement, and could do nothing against him; so he resolved to send him to the patriarch of Rome, prepared a ship, wrote a letter setting out the charge, and had him taken away by night. When the people found their shepherd gone they raised an outcry against the unjust judgement, and the exarch fled the city in fear; the people burnt the houses of Sabinus and Crescentius, who took refuge in the church, and Euplus had to hide until Gregory's return.

At Rome the patriarch read the letters from the exarch and the slanderers, was filled with anger against Gregory, would not so much as see his face, and shut him up. There he healed the jailer's son, who was tormented and had broken his fetters, so that the jailer fell at his feet; and a woman brought him her daughter, bent double, and she too was healed, and told her neighbours that a foreign bishop from Sicily had done it. A whole year passed with him lying in prison.

Then the patriarch remembered him and sent for Abba Mark, who counselled him to gather a synod and to call bishops not from the West only but from the East as well. The emperor and the patriarch of Constantinople, reading the letters, marvelled at the slander, did not believe it, and grieved that Gregory had sat so long in prison though innocent. They sent bishops quickly; and Marcian, coming within about eighteen stadia of Rome, fell into a grievous illness, and a woman led his servants to the prison, saying that the physician who heals with a word sat there — and he was healed.

The patriarch of Rome had the synod gathered in the church of Saint Hippolytus, near the prison, and a hundred and fifty bishops came, and monks from the monasteries, so that the church could not hold them. Asked what they charged their bishop with, the slanderers said they had found him sinning with a woman; asked whether they had seen it with their own eyes, they had to send for the woman — and she was brought in out of her mind, held up by two men. The patriarch said that the possessed and the witless are not questioned at a trial.

When she came to herself and stood before them all she was asked her name, and said: *Eudokia.* She said she knew her bishop well, having often seen him walking in the city, and she threw herself at Gregory's feet weeping and asking his pardon; and when the soldiers began to take the slanderers to prison they all cried out to him for mercy, and he pleaded for them. The patriarch told them they had made themselves like their father the devil by bringing a lying accusation against this man.

While he was still in Rome the patriarch asked him to move certain trees which lay in the river and which nobody could drag out, being held by an unseen power. Gregory took burning coals from a kindled furnace into the skirt of his mantle and carried them there, laid incense on them and censed with the mantle as with a censer, and it was not burnt, at which all marvelled, as at the bush of old that burned and was not consumed; and at the end of his prayer the trees moved and were drawn to the bank without effort, and on them was found written: *Five trees are the Apostle Peter's, and five trees the Apostle Paul's.*

After that the patriarch of Rome gathered the bishops again and judged the heretic Eleusius, to send him into exile in Spain, and released Gregory with honour to his own throne. He went first to Constantinople, where the emperor and the patriarch received him with honour, and then returned to Sicily. The citizens of Akragas came out to meet their shepherd with psalms and hymns and received him with great honour; but he would not enter the church the heretics had profaned, nor the bishop's house, and quickly built another very beautiful church in the name of the Apostles Peter and Paul, and other houses for his dwelling. He lived many years more, shepherding the flock of Christ well, wrote many words of teaching and worked miracles without number, and came to deep old age before he left this earthly life for the heavenly and eternal one.
