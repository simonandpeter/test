# Romanus of Tarnovo

saint.gr names him and gives him one sentence. Its calendar for 17 February prints «Όσιος Ρωμανός ο εκ Τυρνόβου», the venerable Romanus who was from Tarnovo, and the page behind that line says that the venerable Romanus the hesychast came from Tarnovo in Bulgaria, lived in the fourteenth century after Christ, and fell asleep in peace about the year 1370.

That, with the reading of the name, is the whole of the entry: the page glosses Ρωμανός either from ῥώμη, strength, as the robust and brave man, or as the Byzantine word for a Roman, the name of many emperors. It gives him no monastery, no teacher, no disciple, no relics, no couplet and no hymn; what it calls him is a hesychast, and where it puts him is Tarnovo.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him the last sentence of an entry that is otherwise his master's: the heading names him as the disciple of [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-tarnovo), and the body says that when his master had ended his earthly life at Constantinople in 1362 the disciple went on struggling in Theodosius's own house near Tarnovo until his own death. The Prologue says nothing else of him and gives him no year.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 17 Φεβρουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/02/17/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2389/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026; and the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 17. фебруар ст. ст. — [the Serbian day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-03-02&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
