# Agathon of Rome

Agathon — our venerable father and a worker of miracles, as doxologia's life opens — was of Italy, the son of Christian parents, devout and of good honour, who took pains to teach him the whole of the Scripture that is inspired of God and profitable. He profited by it so much, and was so humbled by it, that when his parents died he gathered up the whole of their wealth, called the poor together once, and shared it out among them to the last of it; and then he went off to a monastery and became a monk.

Clothed in the angelic habit, he served God day and night and made prayers for the world; and he laboured so hard at virtue — and virtue does not stay hidden — that he came to be pope of Rome. Having adorned that dignity well, he departed in peace to the Lord.

That is the whole of the life the page prints. It gives no year and no century, names no council and no city but Rome, and says nothing of how he governed; and the page carries no troparion.
