# Anthimus

Anthimus was born at Nicomedia at the end of the third century and raised from childhood as a true Christian; his body was mortified, the Prologue quotes, his spirit humble, envy rooted out, anger tamed, sloth banished — love towards all, peace with all, and a zeal for the glory of God plain to everyone. Rich in the treasure of divine truth, his warm teaching found its way to the faithful, and the Christians of Nicomedia pressed him to become their priest and later their bishop. He was bishop in the years of the fierce persecution under Diocletian and Maximian, when the blood of Christians ran in Nicomedia in streams — in one year twenty thousand were burned in a church at the Nativity — and the persecution went on, many Christians thrown into prison to await torture and death. Anthimus withdrew to a village, Omana, not to escape death but to hearten his flock from there for the contest of martyrdom, so that no one should fall away for fear; a letter of his to the Christians in prison was intercepted and given to Maximian, who sent twenty soldiers to bring him. The grey, far-seeing old man met them, took them in and fed them, and only then told them that he was the Anthimus they sought; astonished at his goodness they offered to hide him and tell the emperor they had not found him, and he answered that he would not allow God’s commandment to be broken by a lie to save his life, and went with them — and on the way the soldiers all came to believe in Christ and were baptised by him. Before the emperor he was long and cruelly tortured, and at last beheaded with an axe, at the beginning of the fourth century — 302, the calendars print. The Russian calendar keeps with him the deacon Theophilus and the martyrs Dorotheus, Mardonius, Migdonius, Peter, Indes, Gorgonius, Zeno, the virgin Domna and Euthymius.
