# Frumentius of Ethiopia

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 30 noiembrie reads «Sfântul Ierarh Frumențiu, Episcopul Etiopiei» — the holy hierarch Frumentius, bishop of Ethiopia — and the page that stands under his name carries neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab. The line itself gives his rank and his see, and those, with the day, are all that is entered here.

Nothing is said there of how he came to Ethiopia, of who consecrated him or of when he lived, and nothing of the kind is written here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 30 November, and saint.gr answers all three of the questions this folder said the Romanian line left open. In the years of Constantine the Great, about 330, a philosopher from Tyre named Frumentius went to Abyssinia — Ethiopia — in order to collect historical material about the country. He became known at the royal court for his learning and was appointed to a high administrative post, and he used that place and that influence to begin the spread of Christianity.

Afterwards he returned to Alexandria, where he told [Athanasius the Great](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria), then archbishop, that more systematic Christian work in that country would bear fruit. Athanasius agreed and gave him that mission, having first consecrated him bishop, in the year 341, with the title of Axum; and the mission, with Aedesius for Frumentius's helper, did indeed bring a good deal of fruit. The page prints no hymn for him, and his couplets play on the name: guarded by the grace of the holy Trinity, Frumentius showed error to be fled away.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day, gives him a year the other two did not, and names his country twice over. Its line is «Свт. Фрументия , архиеп. Индийского (Ефиопского) (ок. 380).» — archbishop of India, in brackets of Ethiopia, about 380 — and its name page heads him the same way, «Святитель Фрументий Индийский (Эфиопский), архиепископ». Where saint.gr made him archbishop of Abyssinia, this calendar prints two countries and chooses neither, setting India first and Ethiopia in brackets after it; what the brackets mean it does not say. It carries no life page for him, and the day page's two hymns stand under the Apostle's name.

The Охридски пролог begins the story in the time of Constantine the Great, with a learned man of Tyre named Meropius travelling to India, who had taken with him two young Christians, the brothers Edesije and Frumentius. On that journey their ship was broken up by a storm on the coast of Abyssinia, and the wild Abyssinians killed everyone from the ship except those two brothers.

They lived some years in Abyssinia and managed to get into the court service of the Abyssinian king. Frumentius began to preach the Christian faith — cautiously enough, the book says — and satisfied himself that the ground for such preaching was fit and fruitful. Then the two brothers took ship and parted: the one for Tyre and his parents, the other for Alexandria, to [Athanasius the Great](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria), the patriarch.

He laid the state of Abyssinia before the patriarch and asked for pastors for those newly brought to the faith, and the patriarch consecrated him bishop. He went back to Abyssinia, where by his zeal and by wonders he succeeded in bringing the whole of Abyssinia to the Christian faith within his own lifetime. This great pastor of Christ’s flock and enlightener of Abyssinia, the entry closes, ended peacefully in the year 370 and passed over into the kingdom of his Lord.

That year is the corpus’s first year of death for him: this folder held an episcopate of 330 to 341 from the Greek calendar and no death at all, and the two agree rather than compete, because the episcopate was never his whole life. His brother has no folder in this corpus, and neither has Meropius.
