# Julian, companion of Pamphilus

saint.gr's calendar for 16 February prints one line for the whole company, «Άγιος Πάμφιλος και οι συν αυτώ Μάρτυρες» — Pamphilus and the martyrs with him — and the page behind it names the twelve of them one by one: [Pamphilus](/saints/pamphilus-of-caesarea), Daniel, Elias, Isaiah, Theodulus, Jeremiah, Julian, [Valens](/saints/valens-the-deacon), [Paul](/saints/paul-of-jamnia), Porphyrius, Samuel and [Seleucus](/saints/seleucus-of-cappadocia). They were martyred under the emperor Diocletian, whose reign the page gives as 284 to 305; they came from various places, but the love and the faith of Christ joined them. Working at Caesarea in Palestine they confessed Christ before the eparch Firmilianus, who spent every effort on persuading them to deny their faith in Christ, and they stayed fixed immovably in the piety of their fathers; so Firmilianus gave the order that they should be put to death after being tortured first.

Julian did not die by the sword with the nine. He was burned alive together with [Porphyrius](/saints/porphyrius-servant-of-pamphilus), Pamphilus's servant, who had been taken while he was searching for his master's body; and over the two of them the page prints one couplet: two divine martyrs leapt towards the fire, the two of them feeding one fire of divine longing. Of Julian himself it says nothing else at all — no homeland, no age, no office, no hymn — and the name it glosses as the glorious one, after Julius Caesar.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day, in one entry for the whole company: the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads it „Светих дванаест мученика.“ — the holy twelve martyrs who suffered in the time of the emperor Diocletian — and then names all twelve in its body. And last Julian, who kissed the martyrs' dead bodies and praised them. So they gave a little for much, the Prologue closes, and the cheap for the precious, and the mortal for the immortal.
