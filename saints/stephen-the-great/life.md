# Stephen the Great

Stephen the Great of Moldavia was the son of the right-believing Christians Voievod Bogdan II and the Lady Maria-Marina Oltea. From childhood he showed a particular love for his country and for the faith of his fathers. His coming to the throne of Moldavia followed troubled years of fighting over the rule. The people called him honest, good, great and holy: good for his works of mercy and his forgiveness of those who wronged him; great for the skill with which he governed the country justly; holy for his battles in defence of the whole of Christendom and for the many churches and monasteries he built and endowed.

On the Plain of Justice he was met by the multitude of the people, with Metropolitan Theoctist at their head, on 12 April in the year of salvation 1457. Asking the gathered people whether it was the will of all that he should be their prince, he was answered with one voice: May God let you reign for many years. And he reigned, the life says, forty-seven years, two months and three weeks, fighting for the borders of the country and for the faith of his fathers, building fortresses of war and fortresses of the soul, which is to say many churches and monasteries.

In everything he did he showed love, justice and generosity, never for a moment forgetting the brave soldiers, the poor and the suffering, rewarding and helping all with a boundless fatherly love. He was tried by many sufferings — the wound in his leg taken at the battle of Chilia, the death of four of his children and of two wives, the betrayal of some of his counsellors, and many wars — and he never lost his hope in God, but carried the cross of his life with Christian patience.

A lamp has been kept burning at his tomb at Putna from his death until today, a sign of the honour the whole people has given him down the centuries, venerating him as a saint and a defender of Christendom; so he is painted in the church of the monastery of Dobrovăț in the county of Iași, his last foundation, painted not long after his death. His numbering among the saints by the decision of the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church on 20 June in the year of salvation 1992 was, the life says, the fulfilment of a work pleasing to God and fitting to the piety of the people.

The Greek calendar keeps the Moldavian voivode on the same 2 July, and opens by saying that his person is cut most deeply of all into the consciousness of the Romanians of every age. saint.gr follows the familiar course — born 1439; made co-ruler by his father the voivode Bogdan II in 1450; Bogdan murdered at Deuseni by Petru Aron in 1451, over whose place of execution Stephen later built a church; proclaimed ruler in 1457 by the clergy, part of the boyars and the heads of the army, and anointed by the metropolitan Theoctist.

It counts more than thirty-four victorious battles against Turks, Poles, Tatars and Hungarians, and more than forty monasteries and churches built after them; it quotes Pope Sixtus IV calling him «αληθή αθλητή της χριστιανικής πίστεως», a true athlete of the Christian faith, and promising help — and adds, with an exclamation mark of its own, that the help never came. It notes his habit of keeping a long strict fast after a victory, he and his subjects together, and of marrying off twenty-four boys and as many girls before a campaign, dowering them and giving them honours and offices.

What the Greek page adds to the Romanian life is Athos. It calls him one of the greatest benefactors of the Holy Mountain after the fall of the City: the monastery of Osiou Gregoriou he renovated entire, of Zographou he is the second great founder, and at Vatopedi he built the arsanas, where a marble plaque survives showing him giving the harbour to the Mother of God. He gave manuscripts too — menaia, gospel books, epistle books — and gold-embroidered stoles and cloths to houses on Athos and in Moldavia alike.
