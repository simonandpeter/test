# James of Chamatoura

saint.gr's calendar for 13 October keeps «Άγιος Ιάκωβος o Χαματουρίτης», and begins with his monastery. The monastery hewn into the rock on Mount Hamatoura at Kousba in Lebanon is a Greek Orthodox house of the Patriarchate of Antioch and one of the oldest in the country; it is dedicated to the Dormition of the Theotokos but is more widely known as the Holy Monastery of Hamatoura, which is the name of the mountain it is built on. Towards the end of the thirteenth century, the page says, James began his ascetic life there.

Afterwards, when the monastery was destroyed by the Mamluks, he established the monastic life again along the edge of its ruins, and in time rebuilt the house and gave the monasticism of that country new strength. His spiritual vigour, his energy and his standing among the faithful drew the attention of the Mamluks, who settled on stopping his zeal and converting him to Islam. He refused their relentless pressure stubbornly; and when the attempts at compulsion failed they dragged him, with some monks and laymen from the monastery of Saint George above Mount Hamatoura, to the city of Tripoli and handed him over to the wali, the governor.

For nearly a year, the page says, he endured enormous tortures without giving way or renouncing his faith, under flatteries and threats and intimidation alike. At last, on 13 October, James was beheaded, and the Mamluks burnt his body as well, to make sure the Orthodox Church would not give him the honourable burial of a martyr. It was in that period, the page says, that the Church declared his sanctity and added him to the list of her honoured martyrs.

He was then almost forgotten in the course of history, which the page lays to the sufferings of the Orthodox Church under the various Muslim sultanates, and to manuscripts and records lost, destroyed or never translated. What kept him was the witness of pilgrims to the monastery who reported visions of him. A clear mention of him has lately been found in a Gerontikon kept at the monastery of the Panagia of Balamand, manuscript 149 of its archive, which shows plainly that the Church honours his memory on 13 October; and the monastery at Kousba kept his memory for the first time on 13 October 2002, at an all-night vigil, where those present chanted a troparion and an office composed for him by the monks of the house. The page does not print either text.
