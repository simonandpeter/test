# Festus, one of the twelve tribunes

saint.gr says they were martyred in the years of the emperor Elagabalus, who reigned from 218 to 222. [Meletius](/saints/meletius-the-stratelates), the leader, as the page puts it, of this whole line of martyrs, was serving as a soldier together with two other saints, [Stephen](/saints/stephen-companion-of-meletius) and [John](/saints/john-companion-of-meletius). They were denounced for believing in Christ and ordered to deny him; and because they did not do it they were put to dreadful torments. In the end Meletius they hanged, and Stephen and John they beheaded.

But [Serapion the Egyptian](/saints/serapion-martyr-24-may) the idolater, when he saw the holy Meletius break the idols of the temple by his prayer, believed; and [Callinicus](/saints/callinicus-the-magus) the magus, when he too saw that Meletius had suffered nothing from the deadly poison they had given him to drink, believed as well. Both of them, having confessed Christ steadfastly, were beheaded.

At the sight of their martyrdom others believed and gave their lives for Christ: twelve komites and tribunes, whom the day's line names as Festus, [Faustus](/saints/faustus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Marcellus](/saints/marcellus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Meletius](/saints/meletius-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Sergius](/saints/sergius-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Marcellinus](/saints/marcellinus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Felix](/saints/felix-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Photinus](/saints/photinus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Theodoriscus](/saints/theodoriscus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Mercurius](/saints/mercurius-of-the-twelve-tribunes) and [Didymus](/saints/didymus-of-the-twelve-tribunes); three women, [Marciane](/saints/marciana-martyr-24-may), [Palladia](/saints/palladia-companion-of-meletius) and [Susanna](/saints/susanna-companion-of-meletius); and two infants, [Cyriacus](/saints/cyriacus-the-infant) and [Christian](/saints/christian-the-infant). After them followed a multitude of martyrs whose number, the page says, reaches eleven thousand two hundred and eight. Not one of that multitude is named, so not one of them is a folder here.

The manner of each death comes from the couplets the synaxarion sets over them, which the page prints one by one. Of Meletius: they call the pine bitter, but to him who was hung upon it it was a kind of Hymettian honey. Of Callinicus: beheaded, you exchange the magus for a contender, and you were not called Fair-Victor for nothing. Of Stephen and John: with John, Stephen bows his head to the sword for longing after the unfading crowns. Of Serapion: the martyr, not sacrificing to carved things, has his neck cut through by a worshipper of carved things. Of the twelve tribunes: perishable were the ranks of tribune and count, and out of the fire they found the rank that does not perish. Of the two infants: the sword took away Cyriacus and Christian, Christian servants of the Lord. Of the three women: Marciane, Susanna and Palladia are killed with wood for not sacrificing to Pallas.

The Russian church calendar keeps the whole of this company on 24 мая ст. ст. Its line names seven and then «и с ними 1218 воинов с женами и детьми (ок. 218)», and the life page behind that line enumerates part of the 1218 by name. This is one of the twelve tribunes of the regiment that list names: «Известны имена некоторых из воинов, 12-ти трибунов: святых мучеников Фавста, Фиста, Маркелла, Феодора, Мелетия, Сергия, Маркеллина, Филикса, Фотина, Феодориска, Меркурия, Дидима.» The calendar says nothing else of him at all — no page of his own, no epithet, no year, and no sentence of narrative.

What it does give is the company. Their commander was the military governor of the province of Galatia under the emperor the calendar dates 218 to 222. He was a Christian and prayed that the Lord would put an end to the pagan error; the demons that lived in the shrines, terrified by his prayer, went into dogs whose howling put the province in fear; and he destroyed the maddened dogs and pulled down the shrines with his soldiers, was taken before the governor Максимиан, was tortured for refusing to sacrifice and died confessing Christ. The tribunes Стефан and Иоанн were beheaded. The rest of the regiment, who also declared themselves Christians, were put to the sword with their wives and children, 1218 of them, or by some sources, the calendar says, eleven thousand; and Феодор and Фавст were burned with many others.
