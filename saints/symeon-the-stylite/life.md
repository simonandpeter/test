# Symeon the Stylite

Symeon was born in the village of Sisan, in Cappadocia by the Russian life and Cilicia by the Greek, to the Christians Susotion and Martha, and from thirteen kept his father’s sheep, taking to this first obedience conscientiously and with love. Hearing the Beatitudes read in church one day he was shaken by their depth; not trusting his own unripe judgement he asked an experienced elder, who gladly explained them and confirmed him in his resolve to follow the Gospel way — and without going home he went to the nearest monastery and after a week of tearful asking was received among the brethren. At eighteen, the Prologue says, he left his parents and was tonsured, giving himself to the hardest labours, fasting sometimes forty days; then he took up a feat unknown before him, to stand day and night on a pillar in unceasing prayer. His first pillar was six cubits high; they raised him others of twelve, twenty-two, thirty-six and at last forty cubits. His mother [Martha](/saints/martha-mother-of-symeon) came twice to see him and he would not receive her, saying from the pillar: do not trouble me now, mother; if we are found worthy we shall see each other in the other world. He bore numberless assaults of demons and overcame them all by prayer, worked great and many miracles, healing many sick by word and prayer, and people streamed to his pillar from every side, rich and poor, emperors and slaves — to some he gave back bodily health, to others comfort and teaching, others he rebuked for heresy, and the empress Eudocia he turned from the heresy of Eutyches back to Orthodoxy. He lived under Theodosius the Younger, Marcian and Leo the Great, the first stylite of Christendom and a great wonderworker, and reposed in the Lord on 1 September 459, aged a hundred and three; his relics were taken to Antioch, to the church of his name. The Romanian life tells how he built the pillar so that those who wished to touch him could not, with a narrow cell of two cubits on it, and so became the first stylite.
