# Paulinus of Athens

The Romanian calendar names Paulinus for 18 mai and sends the reader to the life it prints for the martyrs of this day, where he appears in the closing paragraph and nowhere else. The martyrs Heraclius, Paulinus and Benedimus, it says, were citizens of Athens, and there they preached Christ with much boldness, urging all the pagans to leave off the empty service of idols and to be baptised in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

For this they were seized and handed over to the ruler of Athens. Under examination they confessed the true God, the Maker of every creature, and called the idols insensible stones and wood and things made by men's hands. They were tormented with many kinds of torment; and at the last they were thrown into a heated furnace, and so died and received the incorruptible crowns from Christ our God.

That is the whole of what the page says about him. It gives no year; the reign of Decius which it names belongs to the martyrs of Lampsacus earlier in the same life, and is not stated of the three Athenians, so no date is recorded here. Heraclius and Benedimus, who died with him, are not named in the Romanian calendar's list for the day and so have no folder of their own here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day, on one page with the seven others of his company, and it confirms the city. saint.gr says all eight were martyred in the years of the emperor Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251, and that [Heraclius](/saints/heraclius-of-athens), Paulinus and [Benedimus](/saints/benedimus-of-athens) were from Athens, where they gave a hard fight against the error of the idols and against the philosophers who made war on the Christian religion; they were seized, tortured and at the last beheaded. The couplet printed for the two of them makes Paulinus speak: seeing me, Benedimus, your own Paulinus cut down, he says, be cut down with me. Of the rest the page says that Peter of Lampsacus was crushed with chains and staves for refusing to sacrifice to Aphrodite, and that Paul, Andrew, Dionysius and Christina were stoned.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 18 May in an entry of three sentences, headed for [Heraclius](/saints/heraclius-of-athens), Paulinus and [Benedimus](/saints/benedimus-of-athens). They were Athenians, it says; they suffered for the faith in the time of Decius; and for the name of Christ they were burned in a fiery furnace. That last is not what the Greek page says, which has the three of them seized, tortured and at the last beheaded; both readings stand here as their books print them. The Prologue names the reign and gives it no bounds, so no year is taken from it. The heading names him second of the three. He is the one of the three for whom no book read here has yet given a year.

The Russian calendar names him second of the three and gives the three one notice of four sentences. They suffered for Christ at Athens, where they preached Him to the pagans and pressed them to give up the worship of senseless idols. They were tried together with their own disciples, who had received the true faith from them and whom the page does not name. After many tortures all of them were thrown into a heated furnace and gave up their souls to God there. The calendar names no judge, no emperor and no year in its text; the year on its day line is inside the bracket this folder already carried.
