# Philo of Carpasia

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. His page for 24 ianuarie carries the line «Sfântul Ierarh Filon, episcopul Carpasiei» — the holy hierarch Philo, bishop of Carpasia — the readings appointed for a bishop's feast, and nothing else: no account, no year, no troparion, no kontakion, not one sentence of narrative. What the calendar asserts is therefore only this, and it is set down here as the whole of what was read: that the Romanian church keeps on the twenty-fourth of January a hierarch named Philo who was bishop of Carpasia.

The corpus holds nothing further about him, because nothing further was read. Anyone who comes to finish this entry should begin with the Greek and Cypriot calendars for the same day, which were not consulted.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and where the Romanian page printed no life saint.gr gives one. [Epiphanius of Cyprus](/saints/epiphanius-of-cyprus) thought much of Philo while he was still only a deacon, it says, and raised him to be bishop of Carpasia in Cyprus in the year 401; and according to Suidas this bishop wrote commentaries on the Pentateuch and on the Song of Songs. The page also keeps a prayer of his out of the synaxarist, touching in its plainness, in which he asks the Lord who came down to earth for men's salvation, wore the flesh of his servant, suffered and was crucified, buried and risen, to dispense the like now, that these deluded men whom the devil has led astray may be saved too.

*After doxologia.ro's calendar for 24 ianuarie — [the day](https://doxologia.ro/24-ianuarie) and [his page](https://doxologia.ro/sfantul-ierarh-filon-episcopul-carpasiei), which prints no life; read 19 September 2026; and saint.gr's calendar for 24 Ιανουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/01/24/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3520/saint.aspx), read 30 September 2026.*
