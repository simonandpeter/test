# Jonas of Pergamos in Cyprus

saint.gr's calendar for 11 Οκτωβρίου names Jonas, the saint of Pergamos in Cyprus, and calls him a great hermit, ascetic and wonderworker. The page says that when he learned that the men of the Pope of Rome were going round the countries of Europe calling the faithful to a campaign for the liberation of the Holy Places from the Muslims, he was the first to enlist, together with three hundred other Orthodox Greeks who were working in Germany. That army, which we know as the Second Crusade of 1147 to 1149, broke up before it reached its destination.

So the three hundred, who are known as the Alamanoi, went on to fulfil a holy longing of their own and worshipped at Jerusalem, which European Christians still held; and afterwards they decided not to go back to their work but to stay and live the monastic and ascetic life in the desert of the Jordan. Persecuted there by Saracens and Latins, they gathered and came to Cyprus about 1150 or later, with Auxentios as their leader, whose feast the page gives as 28 Σεπτεμβρίου. Their ship reached Paphos and was broken on the rocks of the harbour, and the soldiers, coming ashore with great hardship, decided to scatter and live as ascetics in Cyprus.

His friend [Kendeas](/saints/kendeas) settled first in a hut on a rock near the shore at Paphos, and Jonas, taking leave of him, went on through various places and settled in a cave in the Mandres district of Trachiada in the province of Famagusta, «εις χωρίον λεγόμενον Πέργαμον». There he set up his hermitage. The page tells how the devil, for some days, took the saint's own form outside the cave and poured away the water his disciple was bringing, until the saint understood the trick and told him never to hand it over except inside his dwelling; and how the saint then struck the rock of his cave with his staff, like another Moses, and a spring of crystal water opened.

That spring was there until 1912 and was known as the holy water of the venerable one, until it and the cave were destroyed; the saint's old icon, half ruined, is kept at Xylotymbou, with the inscription «Όστις προσφέρει δώρον εις την εμήν ανάμνησιν, καμέ προς Θεόν ευρήσει προστάτην».

The page tells of a father who brought his dead child to the cave, and of Jonas praying over it and taking it by the hand with the words «In the name of Jesus Christ our Redeemer and God I say to you: rise, my child», and of the child opening its eyes as out of a deep sleep. He left the cave only to visit Kendeas, who had moved from Paphos to another cave near Avgorou, and the two would go out from time to time to strengthen one another. The faithful found him dead in his cave, his body giving off a fragrance, and buried him where he had lived. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.
