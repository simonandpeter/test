# Nicander of Pskov

Nicander, baptised Nikon, was born on 24 July 1507 to the peasants Philip and Anastasia in the village of Videlebye near Pskov. As a child he longed to follow his fellow villager Euphrosynus, the head of the Pskov hermits; his elder brother Arsenius became a monk first, and after their father’s death the seventeen-year-old Nikon persuaded his mother to give away the estate and enter a convent, where she lived out her days.

He went round the monasteries of the Pskov land and venerated the relics of Euphrosynus and of his disciple [Sabbas of Krypetsk](/saints/sabbas-of-krypetsk), and was set on the hermit life. Hired as a labourer by a Pskov man, Philip, who had him taught to read, he heard while praying in a Pskov church a voice from the altar sending him to the wilderness the Lord would show him through his servant Theodore, and the peasant Theodore led him to the little river Demyanka between Pskov and Porkhov; both men later became monks at Krypetsk.

After some years of silence and hard labours he came to Sabbas’s monastery, where the abbot, seeing how weak he was, would not take him until he prayed at Sabbas’s shrine as to a living man, and he was tonsured Nicander. The blessed Nicholas had foretold his trials in the wilderness while he was still in Pskov, and [Alexander of Svir](/saints/alexander-of-svir) twice appeared to him to teach and strengthen him. Robbers once nearly killed him and took his books and icons; when one of them died suddenly, the other two repented and he forgave them. He lived at Krypetsk twice, as keeper of the order of services and as cellarer, and each time went back to his wilderness; every Great Lent he went to the Damian monastery to confess and receive communion, and eight years before his death took the great schema there. Many came to him for help, and he built them a poor guesthouse by an oak.

He foresaw that he would die when enemies attacked the land and that they would soon be defeated. On 24 September 1581, during the invasion of Stephen Báthory’s Polish army, a peasant found him dead on his mat with his hands crossed on his breast, and clergy and people came from Pskov to bury him. In 1584 a monastery, the Nicander hermitage, was founded on the place of his labours by the monk Isaiah, healed through his prayers; in 1686, under Patriarch Joachim, he was glorified, with his feast on the day of his death, and his relics, found hidden in the wall when the cathedral was rebuilt, are also remembered on 29 June.
