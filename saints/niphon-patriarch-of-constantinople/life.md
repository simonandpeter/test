# Niphon

A Greek by birth, Niphon became a monk in his youth and lived the ascetic life first outside the Holy Mountain and then on Athos itself, in several monasteries, longest at Vatopedi and Dionysiou, where he was loved by all for a rare wisdom and an unusual meekness.

Against his will he was made bishop of Thessalonica. After two years he went on church business to Constantinople, and there he was elected to the vacant patriarchal throne. The sultan banished him to Adrianople, where he lived as an exile until the Wallachian prince Radu obtained his release and made him archbishop of Wallachia. When Radu transgressed, Niphon left Wallachia for Athos and the monastery of Dionysiou, and there he lived out his days: the Prologue says he reached his ninetieth year, and gives 1460 as the year he passed into the kingdom of God. He composed the prayer of absolution that is read at the funeral service.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 11 august, and the long life it prints adds his beginnings and his end. He was from the Morea, the son of Manoil and Maria, and was called Nicolae at his baptism; sent to school for the Scriptures, he kept no childish company but went to wise teachers to gather the honey of virtue, ate little bread and drank little water, and outran his fellows at learning. A hieromonk named Iosif took him away in secret to teach him philosophy, and near Epidauros they found an old ascetic named Antonie. Iosif went on; Nicolae fell at the elder's feet and begged with tears to stay, and would not be put off by the hardness of the life or his own youth.

At the last, after his death, his relics worked such healings in Wallachia — the lame straightened, the blind saw, fevers left — that the prince gathered a synod, appointed his feast on 11 august, the day of his repose, and composed his service. Neagoe had a reliquary of gold made for him, ornamented with precious stones and pearls, with the saint painted on the lid and himself painted kneeling before him; he kept back only the head and one hand, and in exchange sent to the monastery of Dionysiou the head of John the Forerunner in a golden casket, with much else, and raised buildings there from the foundations, so that he is remembered without ceasing as its founder. The head and the hand he carried with him while he lived, and left them to the church he built at Curtea de Argeș; they are now at Craiova, in the church of Saint Demetrius.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 11 August, and sets out the course of his life. saint.gr says this is the patriarch Niphon the Second, who held the throne three times — 1486 to 1488, 1497 to 1498, and 1502. He came from the Peloponnese and was born to Manuel and Maria, and his name in the world was Nikolaos. He attached himself to a monk called Antonios and was tonsured at Epidaurus with the name Niphon.

After his elder's death he went to the castle of Narda, where he met the virtuous Athonite Zacharias, and settled with him at the monastery of the Theotokos at Ochrid. When Zacharias was elected archbishop of Ochrid, Niphon withdrew to the Holy Mountain and was ordained deacon and priest at the monastery of Dionysiou. From there he was called to be Metropolitan of Thessalonica, and from there, after the death of Symeon, he took up the ecumenical throne.

Twice he was removed from it, the page says, and took refuge in Wallachia and then on the Holy Mountain at Dionysiou again. The third time he was called to the throne he did not go to Constantinople at all; he died at the monastery, having lived an ascetic life. The page's own distich puts it the same way: «Λιπὼν ὁ Νήφων τὴν κάτω προεδρίαν / Χριστῷ παρέστῃ τῷ προέδρῳ τῶν ὅλων» — Niphon, having left the presidency below, stood before Christ, the president of all.
