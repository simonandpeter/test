# Theodore of Antioch

**A boy of fifteen whom the sources will not call dead.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him sixth of the twelve of the Julian 23 November — «Мч. Феодора Антиохийского (после 363).» — calls him «мученик» in the heading of its life page and «исповедник», a confessor, on the name link, and hangs behind him a notice of five sentences. He was fifteen years old, and for confessing Christ was given over to cruel tortures by the emperor Юлиан Отступник, whose reign the page brackets «(361 - 363)». The torturers were astonished that the saint rejoiced while he was being tormented and felt the pain only when they stopped; they reported this wonder to the emperor and he ordered the saint released. Afterwards the confessor told how, while they tortured him, an angel appeared and lightened his sufferings, and when the tortures were over the angel went away and he began to feel the pain. The notice closes «Святой мученик Феодор дожил до старости и с миром отошел ко Господу» — he lived to old age and departed to the Lord in peace.

**He is not this corpus’s `theodore-martyred-at-antioch`, and the refusal is written here rather than acted on.** That folder was made off saint.gr’s 24 Νοεμβρίου, «Άγιος Θεόδωρος που μαρτύρησε στην Αντιόχεια», and its Greek notice is a different dossier with a different end: that Theodore suffered under the same emperor and the eparch Σαλούστιος, was arrested a second time and beheaded, and the folder dates his death 361. This one is a boy of fifteen who was let go and died of old age, and the day is the 23rd and not the 24th. An emperor and a city are all the two accounts share, and the two calendars keep them on two days — so a second folder is made and that folder’s Russian row is left `undocumented`, as it was. **Whether they are one man is the author’s**, and if the author merges them the row written here is the one that moves.
