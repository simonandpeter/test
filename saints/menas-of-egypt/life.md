# Menas of Egypt

Menas was an Egyptian by race, a Christian by faith and a soldier by calling, serving under the tribune Firmilian in the metropolis of Cotyaeum. Diocletian and Maximian then reigned, pagan emperors who had given order everywhere that Christians who would not bow to the idols should be tormented and killed, so that the faithful were forced on all sides to the idol sacrifices.

Not bearing to see that violence, and unwilling to look on while soulless idols were honoured, Menas left his soldiering and went up into the mountain, into the desert, thinking it better to live with the beasts than with a people who do not know God. He went from place to place through the mountains and the wildernesses, learning the law of the Lord, cleansing his soul with fasting and prayer, and serving the true God day and night.

After a long time there was a great pagan feast in the city of Cotyaeum, at which a crowd gathered and games and shows were held, horse races and wrestling in honour of the unclean gods, and the whole city watched from the high seats. Menas, foreseeing that feast in the spirit, was kindled with zeal for God, and leaving the mountains he came into the city, stood in the middle of the show on a high place where all could see him, and cried out with a loud voice: *I came to those who did not seek me; I was made manifest to those who did not ask after me.* All the eyes in the theatre turned to him, and they wondered in silence at his boldness.

Pyrrhus, the governor of the city, was there, and had him seized and asked him who he was; and Menas cried out in the hearing of all the people that he was the servant of Jesus Christ, who reigns in heaven and on earth. Asked whether he was a stranger or of that place, and how he dared cry out in the middle of the show, he did not answer; but one of the soldiers standing by knew him and said that this was truly Menas the soldier, who had been under the tribune Firmilian. Asked whether he had been a soldier, as they said of him, he answered that he had, and that he had lived in that city, but that seeing the lawlessness of the people he had left it.

The governor, growing angrier, had hooks and many iron nails strewn on the ground and had him dragged bound over them; and he, as though he were being drawn over flowers, reviled the gods the more boldly and the madness of a people deceived by devils. Then he was beaten a long while with rods of lead. One of the soldiers there, named Heliodorus, said to the tormentor that it was no secret to him that the Christian race is without understanding and takes no notice of torments, like a stone or a soulless piece of wood, and counts death a sweet drink; so he should not weary himself further, but order this hardened Christian to be finished quickly.

The governor then gave sentence: that Menas the bad soldier, who had fallen into the Christian faith and would not obey the imperial order or sacrifice to the gods, should be cut down with the sword and his body burnt in the fire before everyone. The soldiers took him outside the city and cut off his head, and lit a great fire and threw the martyr's body into it. When the fire had died some of the faithful came and took the parts of his relics that the fire had left, wrapped them in clean linen and anointed them with spices; and a little later they carried them to his own country and laid them in an honoured place, where a church was afterwards built in his name, and many wonders were worked in it by his prayers. The Romanian life gives no year; it places him by the reign of Diocletian and Maximian and by nothing else.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 11 November, under the epithet «ὁ ἐν τῷ Κοτυαείῳ», he of Kotyaeion. saint.gr has him born in Egypt about the middle of the third century of idolatrous parents; the idolatrous surroundings he grew up in could not harden his heart, which leapt when the time came at the voice of the God who tries hearts and reins, and so Menas became a Christian while he was still a youth. Growing up, he chose a career in the Roman army, in the Rutalic squadron of cavalry under the command of Argyriskos. Its head line gives the year of his death as 304, and the page names the cities that hold him as their patron: Kastoria, Heraklion in Crete and Salamina. He is one of three martyrs this day whom one apolytikion honours together, with Victor, Vincent and Stephanida beside him.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 11 ноября ст. ст. as «Вмч. Мины (304).» and its name link gives him two by-names, «Мина Котуанский (Фригийский)» — of Kotyaeion, of Phrygia. Its life agrees with the Greek in the year and fills in the names around him: an Egyptian by birth, he was a soldier serving in the city of Kotuan under the centurion Фирмилиан in the reign of Diocletian and Maximian, which it dates 284–305.

When the co-emperors began the harshest persecution in history he would not serve persecutors; he left the army and withdrew into the mountains to fast and pray. Coming back to the city he had served in during a pagan festival, in the thick of the games that the whole town had gathered to watch, he raised his voice and preached faith in Christ the Saviour of the world. Before the judgement seat of the governor Пирр he confessed his faith and said that he had come there to convict them all of impiety.

He refused to sacrifice, bore the cruellest torments and was beheaded in 304. His body was ordered burnt; Christians came by night and gathered what had survived in the dead fire, and laid it in a church of his name built, after the persecutions ended, on the place of his sufferings and his death. The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion to him, and they are now in this folder with the English typed here.
