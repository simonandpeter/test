# Severian (20 April)

Severian is one of nine martyrs saint.gr prints together on 20 April, and the page makes the cause of all nine the same: «αίτια του ενδόξου μαρτυρίου τους, ο μεγαλομάρτυς και τροπαιοφόρος Γεώργιος» — the cause of their glorious martyrdom was the great martyr and trophy-bearer [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer). They all suffered, it says, in the years of the king Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305.

Severian was one of five — Victor, Acindynus, Zoticus, Zeno and Severian — who, when they saw George remain untouched upon the torturing instrument of the wheel, with one voice all five confessed Christ true God. Their beheading was immediate, and so they took the crown of martyrdom.

The couplet the page sets over them says that the sword kills four together with Acindynus, men who had escaped the danger that comes of error. The other four of the nine were guards of the king — Christopher, Theonas, Caesarius and Antoninus — who confessed when they saw George raise a dead man by prayer, and who were hung up, torn, burned with torches and at the last cast into the furnace. It was the year 303.

The page gives Severian no country, no city, no rank beyond that of the guards, and no relic, and it prints no hymn. Its whole account is the shared one, and the nine stand in it as they stand in the calendar's line: [Victor](/saints/victor-20-april), [Acindynus](/saints/acindynus-20-april), [Zoticus](/saints/zoticus-20-april), [Zeno](/saints/zeno-20-april), [Christopher](/saints/christopher-20-april), [Theonas](/saints/theonas-20-april), [Caesarius](/saints/caesarius-20-april) and [Antoninus](/saints/antoninus-20-april).
