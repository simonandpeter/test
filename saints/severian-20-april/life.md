# Severian (20 April)

Severian is one of nine martyrs saint.gr prints together on 20 April, and the page makes the cause of all nine the same: «αίτια του ενδόξου μαρτυρίου τους, ο μεγαλομάρτυς και τροπαιοφόρος Γεώργιος» — the cause of their glorious martyrdom was the great martyr and trophy-bearer [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer). They all suffered, it says, in the years of the king Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305.

Severian was one of five — Victor, Acindynus, Zoticus, Zeno and Severian — who, when they saw George remain untouched upon the torturing instrument of the wheel, with one voice all five confessed Christ true God. Their beheading was immediate, and so they took the crown of martyrdom.

The couplet the page sets over them says that the sword kills four together with Acindynus, men who had escaped the danger that comes of error. The other four of the nine were guards of the king — Christopher, Theonas, Caesarius and Antoninus — who confessed when they saw George raise a dead man by prayer, and who were hung up, torn, burned with torches and at the last cast into the furnace. It was the year 303.

The page gives Severian no country, no city, no rank beyond that of the guards, and no relic, and it prints no hymn. Its whole account is the shared one, and the nine stand in it as they stand in the calendar's line: [Victor](/saints/victor-20-april), [Acindynus](/saints/acindynus-20-april), [Zoticus](/saints/zoticus-20-april), [Zeno](/saints/zeno-20-april), [Christopher](/saints/christopher-20-april), [Theonas](/saints/theonas-20-april), [Caesarius](/saints/caesarius-20-april) and [Antoninus](/saints/antoninus-20-april).

The Ohrid Prologue keeps five of this company on the Julian 18 April, two days before the day the Greek calendar keeps all nine on, and tells their conversion in three sentences. All of them were tortured in the time of the emperor Diocletian, it says. They were pagans until they saw the torments of [George the Great Martyr](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer); but seeing those torments, and the courage of that glorious martyr, and the many wonders that appeared then, they received the Christian faith, and for it they suffered soon afterwards and were crowned with glory.

Its heading names Severian among five — Victor, Zoticus, Zeno, Acindynus and Severian — and not the other four the Greek page counts in the company, so this folder's Serbian row rests on a heading of five and the four unnamed took none. The entry gives no year, naming only Diocletian, so the 303 this folder already held off saint.gr stands untouched; and it gives no city, no trade and no manner of death for any of the five.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 18 April, two days before the Greek day and on the same day as the Ohrid Prologue, and its line names the same five men the Prologue names: «Мчч. Виктора , Зотика , Зинона , Акиндина и Севериана (303)». Its reading gives the reign as that of Diocletian, 284 to 305, and says that among the first to suffer when the persecution began was the great martyr and trophy-bearer [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer), whose unshaken faith and courage under torture brought many pagans to Christ; and that these saints, struck that he took no hurt from the wheel of torment, declared aloud in the hearing of all that they too believed in Christ. By the judge’s order their heads were struck off at Nicomedia, about 303.

Two things in that reading are new to this folder. The life page the day links is headed for **six** men — «Мученики Виктор, Зотик, Акиндин, Зинон, Севериан и Кесарий» — adding to the five of the line the [Caesarius](/saints/caesarius-20-april) whom the Greek page counts instead among the four guards of the king; the day’s own line names five, so this row rests on a heading of five and that folder took nothing from this day. And the Russian is the first of the three calendars to name the place of the beheading, Nicomedia, which the Greek page and the Prologue both leave out.

That place has to be said carefully here, because the same calendar keeps a different martyr of the same name at the same city on 22 августа, among the companions of Agathonicus, and the corpus holds him in a folder of his own. The two are told apart by the name pages: this man’s gives the one day, 18 апреля, and the other’s gives 22 августа; the years differ, about 303 against 305 to 311; and the stories have nothing in common but the city. Nothing was merged, and the Russian page prints no hymn for either company.
