# Adrian of Megara

The time and the manner of the martyrdom of Adrian and of his three companions, [Polyeuctus](/saints/polyeuctus-of-megara), [Plato](/saints/plato-of-megara) and [George](/saints/george-of-megara), are unknown, saint.gr says. What is known is how they came to light: their graves and their holy relics were discovered, by the grace of God, at Megara.

In the years of the Turkish rule, between 1600 and 1670, a man of Megara called Oikonomou wished to raise a building on the spot where the church of the holy martyrs now stands. While the workmen were digging, one of them felt such heat under his feet that he could not work in that place; he told Oikonomou, who was nearby, and Oikonomou, digging deeper with his own hands, found a marble slab with the saints' names cut into it, and beneath it the honoured relics of the victors.

But some sacrilegious men, as soon as the relics were uncovered, stole them and went off secretly to the Peloponnese; they were looked for and not found, partly because the Turkish authorities did not care. After that Oikonomou went to Constantinople, taking with him the slab on which the martyrs' names were cut, and reported to the patriarch of Constantinople of that time what had happened in the town of Megara.

As to how the saints came to be at Megara, the page says, two things may be supposed: either that they suffered persecution from enemies of Christians at Serres and, driven out from there, took refuge at Megara, where they were made perfect as martyrs; or that they bore witness at Megara while serving there as soldiers. Both are the page's own guesses, and neither is a fact about the man. It gives no year, no reign and no apolytikion or kontakion.
