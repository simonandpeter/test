# Veronica of Caesarea Philippi

Doxologia's notice of her is one paragraph, and this folder holds no more than it says. Veronica came from Caesarea Philippi — Paneas, near Mount Hermon in the north of the Holy Land. According to an old tradition, the page says, she is the woman whom the Saviour healed of a grievous sickness, and it quotes Matthew for the healing: a woman with an issue of blood twelve years came behind Him and touched the hem of His garment, saying within herself that if she might only touch His garment she would be made whole; and Jesus, turning and seeing her, said, Daughter, be of good cheer, your faith has made you whole — and the woman was made whole from that hour.

It also seems, the page goes on, that Veronica was the woman who wiped the Saviour's face during His passion, on the road to Golgotha, and that the image of His face was imprinted on that cloth, which is known in history as the Holy Veil, the first icon of the Saviour, one not made by a human hand. Both identifications are given as tradition and as what seems to be so, and the corpus keeps them in that form.

There is no year on the page, nothing of her life before or after, and no account of her death; the troparion the page offers is a recording in Greek and no Romanian text of it is printed.
