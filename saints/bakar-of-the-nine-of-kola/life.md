# Bakar, one of the Nine of Kola

The martyrdom of the nine child martyrs happened in Georgia in the sixth century. In a large village of south-western Georgia, on the plain of Kola where the sources of the river Kyros are, very few Christians were living, and most of the inhabitants were still idolaters. The Christians' children played with the idolaters' children, and as soon as the priest struck the bell for vespers they would leave the game and run to the church.

Nine of the idolaters' children always followed them: [Guaram](/saints/guaram-of-the-nine-of-kola), [Atarnerse](/saints/atarnerse-of-the-nine-of-kola), Bakar, [Vache](/saints/vache-of-the-nine-of-kola), [Bajimi](/saints/bajimi-of-the-nine-of-kola), [Tatsi](/saints/tatsi-of-the-nine-of-kola), [Juansheri](/saints/juansheri-of-the-nine-of-kola), [Ramazi](/saints/ramazi-of-the-nine-of-kola) and [Parsman](/saints/parsman-of-the-nine-of-kola). But when they reached the gate of the church the Christians would not let them go in. This happened several times; the children kept at it, and decided to be baptised Christians.

The village priest, a reverend and holy Levite, catechised them and taught them the commandments of the Gospel. He did not dare to baptise them by day, for fear of the idolaters; so one night he took them with him and led them down to the river, and many Christians followed him. At that season the river was frozen — but as soon as the children stepped into it to be baptised the water, by the power of God, became warm. And this wonder was followed by another marvel: angels came down from heaven and clothed them in white tunics.

The next day the idolatrous parents went looking for their children. They found them in the Christians' houses and learned what had happened, and were beside themselves with rage. They began to beat them and to try to make them deny Christ; but the children confessed their faith in the Lord with innocence and courage. So the parents decided to kill them, as an example to the rest of their fellow villagers: headed by the ruler they went down beside the river, dug a deep pit, and threw the small saints into it. They were all between seven and nine years old.

Bakar is the third named in saint.gr's list of the nine, and the page tells the nine of them one story and gives him nothing of his own.
