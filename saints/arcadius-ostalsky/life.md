# Arcadius (Ostalsky)

He was born in April 1888 at Yakovitsy in the Zhitomir province, a son of the priest Joseph Ostalsky, and the family moved into Zhitomir when his father was transferred there. He finished the Volhynian theological seminary, served from September 1911 as a priest of the cathedral church at Staro-Konstantinov, then as rector of a church at Poltava, and during the war as a regimental priest. From 1917 to 1922 he was rector of a church in Zhitomir, and he founded an Orthodox brotherhood at it whose work through the civil war was practical: it fed and nursed the poor and the sick, taught children, buried the dead.

The reading says he knew every sick person the brotherhood looked after by name, and would ask who was sitting with which of them that day and who was carrying whose dinner. He urged nobody to a poverty he had not taken himself, and those close to him, knowing that he kept nothing, learned not to give him anything he could give away.

He was consecrated bishop, and the two records of his see disagree: the calendar’s line and its name page call him bishop of Bezhetsk, and the title of its own reading calls him bishop of Lubny and vicar of Poltava. Prison and camp took the rest of his life. Questioned at the last, he said that the Church was shaken by the moral fall of its own people and that it was by their moral amendment that it would be strengthened, and that when he was released he would seek not to govern a diocese but only to be able to serve in a church, or failing that to be the watchman of any church at all, hiding his episcopal rank from the faithful.

He would not admit the charge of counter-revolutionary activity, and he confirmed none of it. He was shot on 16/29 December 1937 at the NKVD range at Butovo outside Moscow and buried in a common grave. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
