# Maximus the Soldier

[Juventinus](/saints/juventinus-the-soldier) and Maximus were soldiers — bodyguards of the emperor Julian the Apostate, under whom they suffered. Once at Antioch Julian decided to defile the Christians by sprinkling all the food sold in the markets with the blood of idol-sacrifices; the two openly condemned the emperor’s act and boldly rebuked him for his apostasy from the Christian faith — at a soldiers’ feast, the Prologue says, which Chrysostom tells in a homily in their praise, and when men of the emperor came to the prison to turn them from the true faith, saying how many of their comrades had denied Christ, they answered like knights: that is just why we must stand bravely and offer ourselves as a sacrifice for their apostasy. Both, after merciless beatings, were put to death in prison at the impious emperor’s order, about 361–363 — beheaded in the dark of night, the Prologue says; their relics were found and proved wonderworking.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them on 12 October rather than on the September day of the Russian and Serbian books, and saint.gr tells the same story in fewer words, calling him Maximus or Maximinus. It says they lived under Julian the Apostate, that Saint John Chrysostom wove a particular encomium for these two martyrs, and that they happened to be at a banquet of soldiers — perhaps, it adds, because they were soldiers themselves — where in the course of the talk they protested at the evils the emperor was committing against the Christians. Someone reported it; Julian confiscated what they had and shut them in prison. Because the prison had become a church from the frequent visits of Christians who came to hear the two and be taught, he gave the order, and soldiers went secretly by night and beheaded them.

The Russian calendar keeps the two soldiers twice, on its 5 September and again on its 9 October, printing the same line both times with the same years. The October page's index of names adds the epithet the day line leaves out, «Мученик Максим Антиохийский», Maximus of Antioch. This folder renders on the September day, which is the one its Russian row prints, and the October day is recorded on that row.
