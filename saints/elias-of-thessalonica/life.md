# Elias of Thessalonica

saint.gr says the archbishop of Thessalonica Elias played an active part in the church affairs that went before the Fifth Ecumenical Council. On 6 January 553 he put his name, with Apollinarius of Alexandria and Domninus of Antioch, to the letter that the patriarch of Constantinople [Eutychius](/saints/eutychius-of-constantinople) sent to Pope Vigilius; Eutychius, the page says, moved vigorously and took the initiative for calling the council. The letter set out the pressing need to keep the unity of the Church, and set beside it a confession of faith in the four Ecumenical Councils that had gone before and in the papal letters.

For reasons unknown Elias was absent from the work of the Fifth Ecumenical Council, which met in the same year, from 5 May to 2 June 553. He was therefore represented by his titular bishop of Heraclea in Macedonia, Benignus, who signs the acts of the council — surviving in a Latin translation — as Benignus, by the mercy of God bishop of the city of Heraclea which is of the first Macedonia, acting in the place of Elias the most holy archbishop of the city of the Thessalonians, both for him and also for myself. It is worth noting, the page adds, that in the episcopal lists the locum tenens of the absent archbishop of Thessalonica holds the seventh place, immediately after the patriarchs.

Finally, the page says, the name of the archbishop Elias is written down among those who took part in the Fifth Ecumenical Council in the work of the patriarch of Constantinople Germanus I, On the Holy Councils: and a council was assembled here of the hundred and sixty-five holy fathers, Eutychius the president of our city being in it, Apollinarius of Alexandria, and Elias of Thessalonica, and very many others. If this is not a historical misreading, the page adds, it is meant to stress the leading part the archbishop of Thessalonica Elias played in the preparatory steps towards the calling of the council. The notice stops there: no death, no relics, no hymn.
