# Joseph of Aleppo

Joseph came from Aleppo, and the Turks, because he was a godly man, brought a slander against him: that he had said he would become a Turk. Before the judge's promises and flatteries he stayed unmoved in his faith, and with courage he called the Muslim religion to account. Having proved unshakeable and impossible to talk round in the Turks' attempts to make him change his faith, he received the crown of martyrdom by beheading on 4 February 1686.

The page adds one thing about the day itself, and it is worth recording: codex number 2142 (129) of the eighteenth century in the monastery of Esphigmenou on the Holy Mountain, section 23, puts the saint's martyrdom on 17 February. The day page and the page's own head both keep him on 4 February, and that is the day quoted here. saint.gr prints no hymn for him, and over him sets a couplet bidding him be cut manfully by the sword and receive an incorruptible crown from God.

The Serbian church keeps him on the Julian 4 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue gives him three sentences that agree with the Greek page on his city, his end and his year, and part from it on the occasion. There is no slander in this telling. He was pressed by the Turks to turn Turk, and not only refused but began to expose the Mohammedan lies and to praise the faith of Christ; and for that he was tortured and beheaded in 1686. The Prologue keeps him on the 4th, so it does not corroborate the 17 February that the Esphigmenou codex gives on saint.gr's own page.
