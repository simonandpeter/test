# Modestus of Jerusalem

doxologia.ro prints a short life of him, and this is read out of it. Modestus was born of Orthodox parents in the city of Sebastia. His father was called Eusebius and his mother Theodula, and she was barren; at the prayer of the two of them God gave them a son, this great father, after forty years of their life together. After his birth the father was accused to Maximian of being a Christian, and was bound and shut in prison.

When Theodula heard of it she went to the prison too with the child, who was only five months old; and there, praying together to the Lord, the two gave up their spirits in peace into the hands of the angels, and were martyrs of their own will. The guards found them dead and the child alive between them, and took him to Maximian, who, seeing that he was beautiful and pleasing, gave him to a senator to be brought up well, so that he should be worthy to serve Zeus. While he was being reared by that man, the blessed Modestus learned who his parents had been and that they had died for Christ; and at thirteen he came upon a Christian teacher, who taught him the right faith, to which he clung with his whole soul, though he had one grief, that he lived among the heathen.

Once the emperor Maximian ordered everyone to sacrifice to the idols. Then the blessed one went to his parents' grave and prayed to them with all his heart to deliver him out of the heathens' hand, that he might also be counted worthy of holy Baptism. A silversmith from Athens found him, took him and carried him away with him, and on the road the saint worked various wonders. When they came to Athens the silversmith took him to the bishop to be taught the mysteries of the faith and baptised; and while the Baptism was being performed the silversmith saw a pillar of fire come down from heaven and rest on the head of the one being baptised. After his baptism he healed the silversmith's brother of a deadly illness by prayer and the touch of his hand alone, and healed a man possessed as well.

When the silversmith and his wife died they had written Modestus into their will as an heir together with their sons; but he gave his share of the inheritance to the silversmith's sons and went away into altogether waste places and lived there in stillness. The sons, seeing him honoured by everyone, could not bear it for envy; and when they set out on a trading journey they persuaded the blessed one to go with them, and in Egypt sold him as a slave to an unbeliever, at whose hands he had much to suffer for seven years. By his persistent prayer to God he delivered his master from the heathen error and persuaded him to be baptised, and then healed him of a grave illness; and after his master's death he went to venerate the life-giving Tomb of the Lord, and after that to Mount Sinai, where, serving God, he worked many wonders.

When the bishop of Jerusalem died, the blessed Modestus was ordained bishop of Jerusalem by a divine revelation, in the fifty-ninth year of his life, and worked many wonders there also. The silversmith's sons, who had sold him in Egypt, came to Jerusalem on a trading journey and did not know that he was the bishop of the city; and not only did he not avenge himself for what they had done, but he received them with love, lodged them kindly and did them good generously. So this great father, living in holiness as bishop for thirty-eight years, and ninety-seven years in all, passed to the everlasting dwellings. doxologia.ro gives no year for any of it, and this folder gives none.
