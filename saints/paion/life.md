# Paion

saint.gr's calendar for 1 Ιουνίου prints eight names in one line — [Justus](/saints/justus-1-june), [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), [Charito](/saints/charito) the virgin, [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june), Paion and [Liberianus](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin), whom the page says others call Valerianus — and the life behind the line is one paragraph. These, it says, received the martyrs' crowns together with [Justin the philosopher and apologist](/saints/justin-the-philosopher), whom the same calendar keeps on the same day, in the persecution against the Christians under Marcus Aurelius.

They belonged to the brave phalanx of the Christians of Rome, where only genuine Christian courage could keep a man from being dismayed by the power of the idol-worship that the palace and the army upheld. Having confessed Christ repeatedly, they were condemned to die by beheading; and all of them underwent that martyrdom untroubled and joyful, in the conviction that the immortal goods of heaven awaited them, which the heavenly Father gives to all who have lived on earth in faith and love.

That paragraph is the whole of the account, and it is shared by every folder in the company. Beside it the page prints a short epigram over each name, and the one over Paion turns on the verb that sounds like it, to strike: Paion, striking the impious faith with words, is struck on the neck by a sword-bearing hand. Nothing else about him is on the page, and it prints no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the whole company in one heading of eight names: [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), [Charito](/saints/charito), [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june), Paeon, [Valerian](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin) and [Justus](/saints/justus-1-june). All of them suffered at Rome, it says, in the time of Marcus Aurelius and of Rusticus the eparch. When Rusticus asked whether they thought that if they died for Christ they would receive a reward in heaven, Justin answered for them all: we do not think, we know. After that they were all beheaded, in the year 163, and passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ God.

He is named sixth. The Greek calendar had kept him in a line of eight names with one shared paragraph and an epigram on his name; this book is the second to keep him and the first to give a year, and the 163 it states of the whole company is recorded here as the death. Of the confession he had from his parents, which the Romanian life gives, this book says nothing.
