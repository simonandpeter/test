# Zosimas of Palestine

Zosimas was a monk of the monastery by the Jordan in the reign of Theodosius the Younger. He is the Zosimas who found [Mary of Egypt](/saints/mary-of-egypt) struggling beyond the Jordan, who carried the holy Communion to her once and who buried her the following year; and he departed to the Lord at the age of a hundred.

That is the whole of what doxologia prints for him, and the page names its own source for it: Saint Nicholas Velimirović's Ohrid Prologue, in the Romanian translation of Mihaela Grosu, volume one, page 406. It gives no year of birth and no year of death, and no account of his own monastery beyond its standing by the Jordan; the long account of him belongs to her life, told there by Sophronius, and is printed on this same site under her name for 1 aprilie.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and names its authority: Sophronius, archbishop of Jerusalem, who wrote his life. saint.gr has him dedicated to God from childhood and trained in every kind of virtue; he went round about a thousand ascetics famous for their virtue, to be taught by their virtue and their wisdom, and then settled in a monastery of Palestine. His longing was to subject the flesh to the spirit. He kept obedience to the elders of the house and took on with great gladness whatever rules and kinds of asceticism they gave him; his food was plain and he earned it with handwork, and his chief occupation was the chanting of psalms and the study of the divine Scriptures.

Many times he was counted worthy to see visions, according to the Lord's word that the pure in heart shall see God, and the report of his holiness went everywhere, so that monks from monasteries near and far came to consult him.

He passed his ascetic life in that monastery with all his strength until the fifty-third year of his age; but he loved the desert and the life of stillness, and wanting to contend further he decided to settle at the monastery of the Forerunner on the river Jordan. There he gave himself to strict asceticism and fasting. The gate of that monastery stayed always shut, so that the monks could pray and labour undistracted, and it was opened only when one of them had great need to go out, which was rare, for the house was in the desert and the country round it unknown and hard to cross. In the season of Great Lent he would go off into the desert, and there, by God's good pleasure, he met [Mary of Egypt](/saints/mary-of-egypt), whom the same calendar keeps on 1 April; he gave her the immaculate mysteries and buried her. He fell asleep in peace in deep old age.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and identifies him not by his name but by what he did: he was a monk of the Jordan community, and he was the man who found Saint [Mary of Egypt](/saints/mary-of-egypt), gave her communion and buried her. On the question this folder's sources disagree about, the Prologue takes the Romanian side: it puts him in the time of the emperor Theodosius the Younger, where saint.gr sets him in the reign of Justinian. Nothing was changed on the strength of it — the years this folder holds, 460 and 560, are saint.gr's and a reign is not a year — but it is now two sources to one on the reign. Its own two numbers fit those years exactly: he reposed in the Lord, it says, in his hundredth year, in the sixth century.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 4 апреля ст. ст. and says in the line itself what he is remembered for: «Прп. Зосимы , обретшего прп. Марию Египетскую в пустыне Иорданской (ок. 560).» — the Zosimas who found [Mary of Egypt](/saints/mary-of-egypt) in the desert of the Jordan. The life behind the line is hers, told almost throughout from his side, and it is the fullest account of him any book read for this folder has given.

He was given to a monastery in the neighbourhood of Caesarea in Palestine as a small child and laboured there until he was fifty-three, when a thought troubled him: whether anywhere, even in the furthest desert, there was a holy man who had gone beyond him in watchfulness and in work. An angel came to him at once and said that by the human measure he had not laboured badly, but that of men there is none righteous; and told him to leave that house, as Abraham left his father’s, and go to the monastery by the Jordan. He went, and found there elders truly shining in their labours, and set himself to imitate them.

That house had a custom. On the first Sunday of the Great Fast the brethren communicated, ate a little, asked forgiveness of one another, took the abbot’s blessing, and went out through the opened gates into the desert singing the psalm «Господь просвещение мое и Спаситель мой»; each took what food he needed and some took none at all and lived on roots, and they crossed the Jordan and scattered as far apart as they could so as not to see one another fast. They came back on Palm Sunday, and nobody asked anybody how he had laboured.

In his twentieth day beyond the Jordan, while he was singing the psalms of the sixth hour, something like the shadow of a human body appeared to his right. He crossed himself, put away the fear that it was a demon, and saw a naked figure walking in the desert, the body burnt black by the sun and the short hair bleached white as a lamb’s fleece. It fled from him; he forgot his age and his tiredness and ran, and stopped spent at a dried-up watercourse, begging for a blessing — and the voice that answered him without turning round called him by his name and said she was a woman and had nothing to cover her with, and asked for his cloak. Knowing that she could not have known his name without the gift of discernment, he threw it to her.

What she told him then is her own story and is in her folder. He saw her again on the Jordan a year later, gave her the Holy Mysteries, and the year after that found her dead at the dried-up watercourse with her hands folded and her face to the east, and an inscription written at her head telling him her name and bidding him bury her. The ground was too dry for the piece of fallen wood he had found; and when he straightened up he saw a great lion licking her feet, which at his word dug the grave out with its paws. He went back and told the brethren and the abbot what he had seen and heard, and the memory of her was established there.

The page ends his part of it by saying that he lived on in that same monastery, pleasing God, and ended his life there a little short of a hundred years old. That is an age and not a year, and no date is written off it: the folder keeps the 460 and the 560 it already held, and this book’s own «ок. 560» in the day’s line agrees with the second of them without narrowing it. The day page prints no hymn under his name, and the first writer of the life it serves is named at the foot of it as the archbishop of Jerusalem whose memory the page puts on 11 March.
