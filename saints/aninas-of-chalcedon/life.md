# Aninas of Chalcedon

Aninas came of believing parents at Chalcedon, and was small of body — like Zacchaeus of old, the life says — but a great vessel of the Holy Spirit. He was gentle and silent from his youth, and had no teacher in the virtues but himself. Orphaned in his fifteenth year, he renounced the world at once, entered one of the holy monasteries and took the angelic habit; and then, wanting a life without noise, went off into the furthest desert, where the Euphrates divides Syria from Persia.

There he found a monk named Maiumas living in a cave, and stayed with him, matching him in fasting and in prayer through the night. The two ate nothing at all through the forty days before Pascha and were as cheerful of face as if they had feasted daily at a king's table; and the disciple outran his elder, often going without food outside the fast as well. There was no water near, the river being five stadia off, and Aninas fetched it in a small vessel so as to labour the more by going often. Once, a stadium from the cave, an angel filled the vessel for him; and Maiumas, knowing then what grace his disciple had, went away rather than be served by such a man, and told the brethren of the monastery on the Euphrates about him.

From that hour he was known, and monks began to come to him, and then men and women bringing their sick, whom he healed by the grace of God that was in him and sent away whole. He stopped going out into the inner desert and sat in his cell for the sake of those who came, so that none should find it empty and go away without comfort. He dug a small cistern for rain-water; and in the seventeenth year of his ascesis, when thirsty men came in the heat and the cistern was dry, he told his disciple to draw, and the disciple found it full to the brim.

When that water was spent he would not ask such a wonder a second time, but carried water himself from the Euphrates all night, from evening until morning, with the praise of God in his mouth and the Gospel's promise in his mind, that whoever gives a cup of cold water in Christ's name shall not lose his reward. Once, when brethren came thirsty from the monastery and there was none at all, he took the vessels and was back almost at once; those who ran to meet him, thinking he had turned back from weakness, found the vessels heavy and full, and living cold water sprang from the saint's own shoulders, and he told them it had been given for their thirst and not for him.

Patricius, bishop of Neocaesarea, loved him, ordained him priest against his will, and sent him a mule so that he need not carry water himself; Aninas gave the mule to a poor man to sell and pay his debt. The bishop sent another, lent and not given, and he gave that one away too to a beggar; so the bishop came, had a great well dug, and had it filled and refilled with water brought by his own animals. When a stylite named Pionius in the region of Apamea was struck on the head with a stone by robbers and thought of coming down from his pillar to go to law, Aninas knew it from afar, fed the lion that served him, tied a letter about its neck and sent it to Apamea; the lion scratched at the gate, laid the paper before the stylite, and the stylite, reading the counsel not to lose an eternal reward through impatience, gave up the thought.

A believing woman coming to him for healing was set upon by a barbarian who planted his spear in the ground and tried to force her; she cried out «Sfinte Anine, ajută-mi!» and he was at once made mild and began to tremble, and could not pull his spear from the earth, and was seized by a demon. She was healed, and the barbarian afterwards ran to the saint repenting, and was healed, taught the faith, baptised, and in time clothed in the angelic habit; and out of the spear that had rooted itself a great oak grew.

He lived to a hundred and ten years. Foreseeing the day of his departure he gathered the brethren he had gathered about him, taught them at length, pointed to a virtuous priest among them named Veronikianos and named him abbot in his place. After seven days' illness he grew afraid at the last hour and tried to rise from his bed, and bowing his head said, «Peace to you, my lords» — and then, «Who are you, my lords?» — and then, «The will of my Lord be done; I too am going at the Lord's command.» Asked what he had seen, he said he had seen the Jerusalem above opened and a shining cloud come out of it, and three men bearing light who said to him, We are Moses, Aaron and Hur, and the Lord calls thee. He asked the brethren to pray that he be not ashamed before the dread Judge, said «Receive my spirit, Lord», and departed on the eighteenth day of March.

The Greek calendar keeps him two days earlier than the Romanian, on 16 March, and saint.gr's page for that day is the same life. It has him love stillness and ascesis from a young age, lose his parents at fifteen and withdraw into the desert of Mesopotamia, where he stayed beside a monk named Maioumas, distinguished for the holiness of his life; their ascesis was so severe that they often ate only every fortieth day. When Maioumas left the place Aninas stayed on as a hermit and a hesychast, and because he had subdued the passions of the flesh by his struggle he received from God the gift of the subjection of wild beasts.

His fame passed the bounds of his cell and crowds came for his blessing, for spiritual counsel and for healing, and he had the gift of wonderworking and healed the suffering and the sick by his prayer. He carried the water he needed to his cell from the Euphrates himself, and built a small cistern for the pilgrims; once, when many had come and his attendant said the water would not be enough, he looked up to heaven, prayed in faith and told him to go and draw for the thirsty, and the cistern was found full of cool clean water. Hearing that he carried his own water, Patrikios, bishop of Caesarea, gave him a pack animal to spare him the labour; and when a poor man pressed by his creditor came to the cell, he would not let him go away empty and gave him the animal to sell and pay the debt.

He did many other miracles and passed his life doing good to men, and above all to the suffering and the poor. He lived in his hermitage ninety-five years without moving from the place, and gathered a brotherhood of many ascetics; a little before his blessed end he called the members of it together, appointed his successor and blessed the monks, and a few days later fell asleep in peace at the age of a hundred and ten.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day as the Romanian page and tells a life neither of the others gave. He was of Chalcedon by birth, small in stature as the Zacchaeus of old but great in spirit and in faith. At fifteen he renounced the world and settled near the river Euphrates in a hut, where he did penance and prayed to God — first with his teacher Maiumas and, after that man's death, alone. By the power of his prayer he filled a dry well with water; he healed the sick of various torments and tamed wild beasts, and one tamed lion was constantly in his service.

He saw what was far off. When robbers not far from him attacked a stylite named Pionius and beat him so that the man thought of coming down from his pillar and going to complain to the judges, Saint Aninas saw into that stylite's soul and his intentions, and sent him a letter by his lion, telling him to give up what he intended, to forgive his attackers and to carry on his struggle. He was merciful beyond telling. The bishop of Neocaesarea gave him a donkey to make the carrying of water from the river easier, and he gave the donkey away to a poor man who had complained to him of his poverty; the bishop gave him a second, and he gave that one away too; so the bishop gave him a third, but only on loan, as a water-carrier, on condition that he keep it and give it back.

Before his death he saw [Moses](/saints/moses-the-prophet), [Aaron](/saints/aaron) and Hur coming to him and calling him: Aninas, the Lord calls you, rise and come with us. He told his disciples of it and gave up his spirit to the Lord whom he had served faithfully. He was a hundred and ten years old when his earthly life ended.
