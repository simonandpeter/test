# Meletius the Confessor

Meletius came from the country of the Black Sea, about the year 1209, the son of a famous general. After a vision he left his homeland, his goods and his parents and went on pilgrimage to the Holy Places on foot and in the depth of winter. He became a monk on Mount Sinai, where he would pass whole weeks without eating and whole nights without sleeping; and when these labours began to draw the admiration of the other monks he decided to flee, to escape vainglory. Counted worthy to see with his own eyes the holy fire that comes each year from the Holy Sepulchre at the paschal vigil, he then lived as a wanderer, gathering the virtues of one and another like a zealous bee, and settled for a time in Egypt and in Syria.

One day Christ appeared to him in a blinding light and told him to go to Constantinople and defend the right faith, then threatened by the political compromises of the emperor Michael VIII with the Latins. In the capital many were drawn to him; unable to bear the fame, he withdrew to the mountains nearby and lived again as a hermit, and there too the faithful found him out, so that he became the intercessor of all and a giver of grace through his miracles. When Michael VIII and Patriarch John Bekkos began to persecute those who would not unite with the Latins, Meletius left his retreat at once and went through the whole of Bithynia strengthening the true faith and refuting the heresy; passing by the mountain of Saint Auxentius he founded a gathering of hermitages on the little island of Saint Andrew, and had a vision of Saint Auxentius, who foretold that he would soon receive the crown of the confessors of the right faith.

He went then to the court with Galaktion, one of his fellow ascetics from Mount Galesion, and together they rebuked the emperor manfully for betraying the Orthodox faith and the apostolic traditions. For this they were thrown into prison, tortured, and exiled to the island of Skyros; from there Meletius was sent to Rome in 1279, where he stood up to the Latin theologians and was kept prisoner two whole years, then sent back to Skyros and shut in a dungeon. Because the fame of the confessors remained a support and a hope to all the Orthodox, the emperor had them brought before him; and when they stood unmoved and called the tyrant a new Julian the Apostate, he gave them over to torments. Meletius was hanged on a tree, which blossomed the moment it received him, and then his tongue was cut out, and Galaktion's eyes were put out — and, a wonder, the torments did not stop the one from preaching the true faith nor the other from celebrating the Divine Liturgy.

When Andronicus came to the throne in 1282 and brought back Patriarch Joseph at once, Meletius settled in the city monastery of Saint Lazarus and had an important part in the restoration of Orthodoxy. Grown old, he fell ill, and even so did not leave off his accustomed labour: for three years he ate nothing but a few vegetables. When at last he gave up his spirit to the Lord, his face shone brighter than the sun; the year was 1286. His body remained incorrupt and countless miracles were worked through it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and adds what the Romanian page left unnamed. saint.gr says that his first name was Michael, that he was born in 1206 in the town of Theodotos in Pontus, that his parents were called George and Maria, and that his father was a soldier. From childhood he loved the monastic life, and having left the world he became a monk on Mount Sinai and then visited the Holy Places and Egypt. He wrote much against the Latins and suffered much for his Orthodox faith, and for that he is called the Confessor. He fell asleep in peace in 1283 at the age of seventy-seven and was buried at the monastery of Saint Lazarus. The chief of his works, the page says, is the «Αλφαβηταλφάβητος», a great poem of some thirteen thousand lines setting out theological thoughts, and he wrote also on the teaching of the Holy Trinity.
