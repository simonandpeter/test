# Hypatius of Gangra

Hypatius, the holy bishop of the city of Gangra in the country of Paphlagonia, was one of the three hundred and eighteen fathers who took part in the first council of the whole world, gathered at Nicaea in the year 325, in the reign of Constantine the Great; and there, with the other fathers, helping the right faith and full of the Holy Spirit, he rebuked Arius for his heresy and gave him over to anathema. He had great power from God in the working of miracles, healing every infirmity and driving out devils.

In one village he made sweet the bitter waters that ran down from the mountains; in another place he brought out by prayer a spring of warm water for the healing of men. Walking one dark night near a river, he saw lights kindled out of the water lighting his way, so that he should not slip and fall into the rapids. And he killed a terrible dragon: for after the death of Constantine the Great, when his son Constantius was reigning, a very great dragon crept into the imperial palace, no one knew from where.

The emperor sent for Hypatius, whose name was glorious for the miracles he worked by the grace of Christ; and when he came the emperor met him, bowed to the ground before him and received him with all honour, begging him to drive that dragon out of the imperial treasuries by prayer — for no human power could drive it out, and many men of the spiritual order who had armed themselves with prayer and attempted it had gained nothing, and some had even suffered from the beast.

Hypatius said: «Our prayer cannot do it, O emperor; but your faith towards God can.» He bowed to the ground and prayed a long while, and then rose and told the emperor to have a furnace made ready in the middle of the hippodrome, where his father's image stood, and heated very hot, and to wait until he came. When it was ready and burning, the wonderworker took his bishop's staff, went to the imperial chambers, opened the doors of the treasuries and beat the dragon hard with the staff.

All the people with the emperor watched from far off, held by fear and trembling, for the dragon did not come out and the day was ending, and they all waited for the saint to be killed by it as other priests had been. But the bishop lifted his eyes to heaven, called on the Lord for help, put his staff into the dragon's mouth and said: «In the name of my Lord Jesus Christ, follow me.» And the dragon took the staff in its teeth and followed him like a bound slave, for the great wonderworker had truly bound it with the power of God.

Coming out of the treasuries he went round the whole course of the hippodrome and the market leading the dragon; and those who watched it holding the staff and dragging after him were terrified and greatly amazed, for it was a fearful beast, sixty cubits long. When he came near the furnace burning with a great flame he said to it: «In the name of Christ, whom I also, unworthy, preach, I command you to go into the midst of this fire.» And the fearful dragon bent itself and threw itself swiftly into the burning furnace and was burned up in the middle of it, and all the people who watched in terror glorified God.

The emperor gave great thanks to God and to His servant and honoured him as a father; and he ordered the saint's likeness painted on a small board and set in the imperial treasuries, to drive off the power of the adversary. Then, honouring him with gifts and kissing him with love, he let him go to his own place.

But as the saint was going back to his see, some wicked men of the heresy of Novatian, burning with envy against the bishop of God, hid on his road in a narrow place, by a mountain gorge and a bank, armed with swords and clubs. Falling on him suddenly like wild beasts and beating him much, they threw him from the high bank into that ravine; and the saint, barely alive, lifted up his hands and raised his eyes to heaven — as the first martyr Stephen did once — and prayed for those who had killed him, saying: «Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge.»

While he was still praying, a woman who held the heresy of Arius took a stone and struck him hard on the head and killed him, and his holy soul, loosed at once from the bonds of the body, went up to God. The people afterwards took the honoured body of their father and shepherd and buried it with honour in a marked place; and the murdering woman followed the body as it went to the burial, beating herself with the same stone with which she had killed God's bishop, confessing her sin before all. When the body had been buried she was healed of the devil's torment, and so were the others who had taken part in the killing and had become possessed. All of them found healing at the tomb of the saint, who after his death showed his goodness to his enemies and made them well; and other miracles and healings were shown to those who came with faith to his tomb.
