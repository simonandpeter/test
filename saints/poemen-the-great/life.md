# Poemen the Great

Poemen and his brothers made a small monastic brotherhood in a little skete in Egypt, and Poemen was its abbot, with every quality of a true shepherd of souls. His fame reached far, and many came to see him and ask his counsel; but he received only the small and the humble, and those who came out of curiosity he would not receive, even when they were magistrates.

One such visitor, a judge, angry at being refused, arrested the only son of the abbot’s sister, thinking Poemen would then come to him. Poemen wrote instead: examine my nephew according to the laws; if he is guilty, punish him; if not, do as you please — and the judge, admiring the letter, released the young man. All this Poemen achieved because he cultivated the foundation of the virtues, humility; a man needs humility, he often said, as much as the air he breathes. He died in peace in the year 450, at the age of 110.
