# Theodore the Sanctified

When the report of the angelic life of Pachomius the Great was spreading and the faithful were glorifying Christ for him, many whose love for God was warmer grew ashamed of the world and went to that father for the monastic life; and among them, doxologia says, was the blessed Theodore whose memory is kept on this day. He was born of rich and honoured Christian parents and chose poverty of his own will, counting everything as refuse that he might gain Christ.

The beginning of his zeal was this. A great feast was kept in his parents' house on the sixth of January, the day of the divine Baptism of the Lord, and Theodore, then a boy of fourteen, seeing the house adorned for the feast and full of every worldly good thing, considered the emptiness of it all and said within himself: what profit have I from these, or what shall I gain, wretched that I am, if by taking my sweetness in the passing pleasures of this life I am deprived of the everlasting ones?

He sighed from the bottom of his heart, and at the hour of the meal he went into a hidden room and fell on his face and prayed with tears: Lord, who knowest hidden things and triest the secrets of men, Thou knowest that I honour nothing in this world above Thy love; guide me on Thy way, that I may do Thy will, and enlighten my soul, that I may glorify and praise Thee always. His mother came in and found him rising with his eyes swollen from weeping, and asked who had grieved him and why he had left the table; and he told her to go and eat without him, and would not be persuaded.

From then, while he went daily to school at his letters, he fasted until evening, and sometimes tasted nothing at all for a day and took a little food towards the evening of the next. For two years he touched nothing costly or sweet, training himself in the full abstinence of a faster. Then he left his house secretly, leaving his parents and their wealth, and finding in a monastery some monks pleasing to God he began to live with them in the fear of God, following their struggles as far as his strength allowed.

He went from there to Pachomius, and after the death of that father of his, and after the quick end of the blessed elder Petronius who succeeded Pachomius, Theodore became the guide of the holy flock and was enriched with many wonders. For these he was known and honoured by Athanasius the Great of Alexandria; and having profited many by his deeds and his words, he went to the Lord in deep old age.

A note appended to the life weighs his epithet. The great synaxarion says he is called sanctified because, when his mother conceived him, she saw in a dream a shining star coming down from heaven towards her womb; but since that is not found in Metaphrastes, nor in the Greek manuscripts, nor in the great Menaion, nor in the prologues, the life does not put it in the body of the text, and suggests it belongs rather to Theodore of Sykeon, who is kept on 22 April. What the note holds to instead is that among the Tabennisiote monks of the common life nobody at that time held priestly ordination, and Theodore was the first compelled to receive it — as Sabbas is called sanctified for having been the first ordained priest in his lavra — and that it is therefore fitting to believe this Theodore was a priest, and so is called sanctified.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 16 May and begins where doxologia's life does not. It says he was born and brought up a heathen and came to the knowledge of the true faith only as a young man; that he was baptised; and that, hearing of Saint [Pachomius the Great](/saints/pachomius-the-great), he fled to him in the monastery secretly from his parents, who knew nothing of it. The two books do not agree here, and this life chooses between them no more than they do: doxologia has him born of rich and honoured Christian parents, with the beginning of his zeal at a feast kept in their own house on the day of the Lord's Baptism. Pachomius tonsured him, the Prologue says, and came to love him for his uncommon zeal and his obedience.

When his mother came to call him back home he would not so much as show himself to her, but prayed God to enlighten her too with the truth; and she neither took her son home nor went home herself. Seeing a women's monastery not far off, governed by Pachomius's own sister, she entered it and was tonsured. After some time his own brother came to the monastery as well and was tonsured there too. When the bishop of Panopolis asked Pachomius to set up a monastery for the many who wanted the monastic life, Pachomius took Theodore with him and gave the chief charge of the work to him. After Pachomius's death Theodore became abbot of all Pachomius's monasteries, and to deep old age he guided a multitude of monks on the way of salvation. He fell asleep in peace in 368.
