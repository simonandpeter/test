# Constantine of Georgia

Constantine and his brother [David](/saints/david-of-georgia), the Romanian synaxarion says, were brought up in the Orthodox faith, and were not only worthy and brave rulers but devout Christians. The two brothers defended Georgia against the Muslim invasion, fighting valiantly though their army was much smaller than the invaders’. Taken prisoner, they were pressed by the commander of the Arab armies, Murvan-Abdula-Kasim, with promises and tricks to go over to Islam, and stood firm; Murvan-Kru — a nickname, the synaxarion says, meaning “the hard of hearing” — then called in sorcerers to turn them from Christ, and the confessors escaped all their spells by prayer.

Seeing at last that he could not move them, the Muslims tortured them terribly and drowned them in the river Rioni, in the year 740. The water carried the bodies downstream with three pillars of light always seen above them, so that the Christians could tell where they were; they took the relics out of the water and buried them in a cave on Mount Tskal-Tsiteli, near the city of Kutaisi.

In the twelfth century King Bagrat the Great (1072–1117), out hunting, found the relics in the cave, incorrupt and shining, and at once built a church in their honour, around which the Motsameta monastery, the monastery of the holy martyrs, was founded; from then, the synaxarion says, God has given healing to many through the relics of David and Constantine, and these wonders go on to this day. The Romanian calendar keeps the two brothers on 2 October.

The Russian church calendar keeps the two princes on the Julian 2 October, marked «(Груз.)» as one of the Georgian commemorations it carries, and agrees with doxologia.ro on the year 740. Its index of names gives him the house the Romanian page leaves out, «Константин Арагветский, князь, мч.» — Constantine of Argveti, prince, martyr.

*After doxologia.ro, [Viața Sfinților Mucenici David și Constantin, prinți ai Georgiei](https://doxologia.ro/viata-sfintilor-mucenici-david-constantin-printi-ai-georgiei), from the Sinaxarul mare for October; read 17 September 2026; the Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 2 October — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261002.html); read 4 October 2026.*
