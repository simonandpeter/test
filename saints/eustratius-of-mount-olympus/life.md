# Eustratius of Mount Olympus

Eustratius came of the country about Tarsus, born to well-off and right-believing parents, George and Megheti, who brought him up well in learning. At twenty he was seized with divine zeal and left them, going to the region of Olympus, to the monastery of Augarus, where his mother's brothers Gregory and Basil had shone in the ascetic life and in every other virtue. Gregory was abbot of the house at the time. Eustratius was received there, his hair was cut, and he entered the laborious and honourable life of the monks.

He served all the brothers with a willing heart and a humble mind, and cared for nothing of this age. He owned a cassock and a sheepskin to sleep on, and lay down wherever he happened to be, having no place set aside for rest. It is said of him that from the day he renounced the world he never once lay on his back, nor on the left side of his body, in the seventy-five years of his fasting. When his uncles died, the guidance of the brotherhood they had led was laid on him, and he bowed to the community's wish.

When Leo the Armenian, who had risen against the right-believing emperor Michael and driven him from the throne, set about raising again the heresy against the holy icons, the faithful left their houses and their monasteries; and Eustratius, urged on by the great Joannicius, went back to his own country. After the Church had taken up her ornament again and the holy icons were set in place and venerated, the fathers returned to their houses and he with them.

From then on he worked all day at the body's labour with the brothers, and stood at vigil through the night without sleep, bowing to the ground, with prayer never out of his mouth. At the church singing he would stand inside the holy altar and say to himself, from beginning to end and with all his strength, "Lord, have mercy." His miracles, the life says, were too many to be given to the hearing. When his time came he called the brothers under his obedience, told them that the season of his life had reached its end, that they should give back the pledge they had received, and that all things present are for a time and the things to come are eternal. Then he prayed, blessed them, lifted his eyes to heaven and said, "Into thy hands I give up my spirit." He had lived ninety-five years.
