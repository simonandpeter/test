# Luke (Shvets)

Luke — Leontius Shvets in the world — was born in 1838 of peasants of the
Sumy district of Kharkov province, and came to the Glinsk hermitage in
1864, where he worked two years in the kitchen and then twenty-two in the
bakery. Made a rasophore in 1869 and put in charge of the bakery, he cared
for the spiritual growth of the novices under him in an odd way, often
scolding them so that, finding anger in themselves and seeing the truth of
their unripe state, they should learn to overcome that passion by patience
and begin the road to guilelessness without which there is no true
monasticism — and, drawing the fire on himself, he bore their resentment
humbly. In 1875 he was tonsured with the name Longinus; in 1888 he was made
overseer of the Psalter, answerable for the order of the elders’ reading
of the Psalter for the monastery’s benefactors; in 1891 he took the schema
with the name Luke and settled in the Near Skete, and in 1893 moved to the
Far Skete. His elder was [Archippus (Shestakov)](/saints/archippus-shestakov),
at whose direction he added five hundred Jesus prayers to his cell rule,
and, it seems, did not at once learn to keep his whole attention through
them: once the Glinsk image of the Saviour Not-made-by-hands appeared to
him in the air, and from it a voice bade him say the prayers slowly and
attentively. Like many true ascetics he suffered demonic terrors — once,
going out of my cell, he confessed to his disciples, a great white horse
flew at me and began to bite — and came out of the fight a victor: the
enemy drove me from the skete, but I overcame him. With disciples he knew
how to instruct each wisely, one with a frank word, another by a parable;
several times, one monk said, Father Luke gave me to read exactly what I
wanted to know and was ashamed to ask; true love gives what it can to all,
and not to the chosen, was one of his sayings. The demons took revenge on
him for guarding his children so watchfully from spiritual danger — because
of you I rolled on the floor all night, he told them once. Having the gifts
of foresight and healing, he hid his prayer for the sick under some outward
act, offering a plain remedy to eat: to a monk who came with fever he
soaked a piece of bagel and handed it over — ill? here, eat! — and the
monk left the cell well. In his cell he was warm and cheerful, but always
carried the fear of God in his heart: once, when his cell had been
whitewashed and he came back to find the icon of the Mother of God, Joy of
All Who Sorrow, set on the bed, he moved it at once to its place, prayed
earnestly before it, and heard a voice: servant, always keep the good
faith; by this you will be justified. He died on 1 May 1898; the Council of
Bishops of 2017 entered him in the calendar of the Russian Church, which
keeps him on 9 September of the old style, 22 September, in the synaxis of
the Glinsk fathers.
