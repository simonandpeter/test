# Matrona, companion of Tryphaena of Cyzicus

saint.gr keeps two women together on 11 April, and is careful about which of them its sources carry: «Στους Συναξαριστές αναφέρεται μόνο η μνήμη της Τρυφαίνης της εν Κυζίκω. Στον Λαυριωτικό όμως κώδικα 170 μνημονεύονται και οι δύο» — in the synaxaristes only the memory of [Tryphaena](/saints/tryphaena-of-cyzicus) of Cyzicus is mentioned, but in Laura Codex 170 both of them are remembered. Everything the page says of Matrona it says of the two of them at once, and it gives her no year and no other name.

The two of them came from the city of Cyzicus, and from a young age they longed for Christ, and so followed the monastic life. They came to such a degree of ascesis and of fasting that the joints of their bones could be seen. They had no cell, but wrapped in one small garment they prayed to God continually, night and day, and rested a little on the ground.

A little before their holy end, which they foresaw, they made the sign of the cross and gave up their souls into the hands of God; and after their falling asleep, the page says, they worked many wonders. It does not say where they were buried, names no elder and no monastery of theirs, and prints no hymn; its one couplet is Tryphaena's.
