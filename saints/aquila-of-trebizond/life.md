# Aquila of Trebizond

Aquila is one of four men whom doxologia.ro keeps together on 21 ianuarie, and the life the site prints under his name is the passion of all four. It opens with three of them: Aquila, [Valerian](/saints/valerian-of-trebizond) and [Candidus](/saints/candidus-of-trebizond), taken in the mountains above Trebizond in the reign of Diocletian and Maximian, at the hands of a man the page names Lysias. Before they were taken they had left their houses and their goods and gone out into the hills, choosing, the life says, to live among wild beasts rather than among men who hated God.

They were seized in the land of Lazica, in a small fortress the page calls Până, and shut up there in a narrow prison. After a time they were brought down to Trebizond and set before Lysias. Questioned about the faith of Christ and pressed to sacrifice to the idols, they would not; they were stripped and beaten with sinews, then hung up, torn with iron claws and burned with lighted torches. The life says that the tormentors themselves fell to the ground like dead men, and that Lysias in his fright sent the three back to prison.

Not many days after, [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-trebizond) was taken as well, and from then the four are one company. All four were at last thrown into a burning furnace, came out of it unhurt, and were put to death by the sword.

The Greek calendar keeps the four together on the same day and gives him a year the Romanian page did not: the heading for him on their joint page reads «Αγιος Ακυλας ο Εκ Τραπεζουντας (; - 292)». saint.gr adds a note on his name, which it derives from the Latin *acula*, a small needle — one who is sharp and useful as a needle — or else from *aquila*, an eagle. It names the four patrons of Trebizond, in Turkey, and of Nea Trapezounta in Pieria, and says of their end that they remained unshaken and victorious in their faith through the torments. It prints no hymn for them.
