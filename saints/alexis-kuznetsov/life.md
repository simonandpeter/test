# Alexis (Kuznetsov)

Alexis Kuznetsov was born in 1873, finished a teachers’ seminary, and first taught in the village school at Gorbunovskoye in the Kamyshlov district. In February 1898 Bishop Christopher of Yekaterinburg ordained him deacon and sent him to the Vyisko-Nikolaevsky cathedral at the Nizhny Tagil works, where he also taught in the parish school, which the diocesan inspector’s report for 1900–1901 counted among the best, naming him among the teachers most devoted and successful. In September 1909 Archbishop Vladimir of Yekaterinburg ordained him priest, to the second place at the Ioanno-Bogoslovsky church at the Verkhnyaya Salda works, whose rector was Father [Peter Dyakonov](/saints/peter-dyakonov); he served there until his death. In 1914 he was given the nabedrennik for zealous service, and he sat on the missionary committee at Nizhnyaya Salda, a place with many sectarians and Old Believers.

In 1917, about a month after the Soviet power was set up in Petrograd, a soviet of workers’, peasants’ and soldiers’ deputies was formed at Verkhnyaya Salda, and early in 1918 the ironworks was nationalised; the two priests watched many of their parishioners, the young among them, fall away from the Church. As the Red units fell back through Nizhnyaya Salda at the end of September 1918, many hostages, clergy among them, were shot near the railway station there and buried where they fell, and on 1 October 1918 Father Alexis and Father [Peter](/saints/peter-dyakonov) were killed there by Red Army men. When the townspeople came afterwards they found cut-off heads, mutilated bodies and blood mixed with earth. On 9 October, the feast of the apostle John, the two priests and the other villagers killed for the faith were given a Christian funeral: Alexis was buried inside the church fence, Peter in the parish cemetery. They were glorified in 2002.

The Russian calendar keeps the two on 18 September old style.
