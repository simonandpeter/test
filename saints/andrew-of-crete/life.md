# Andrew of Crete

Andrew's homeland was the famous city of Damascus. He was born of God-fearing and virtuous parents named George and Gregoria. Until he was seven years old the child had no voice and could not speak at all, so that his parents grieved much over him, thinking he would be dumb all his life. When the seven years had passed, he went with his parents to receive the most pure Mysteries, the Body and Blood of the Lord, and a wonder happened: as soon as he had communicated his tongue was loosed and he spoke without hindrance.

Afterwards his parents sent him to school to learn the holy Scriptures. Being very sharp of mind, he worked at his lessons with great diligence, and made great progress in philosophy under practised teachers. So he made his tongue skilled and pleasant in speech, his soul apt for the gaining of virtue and of truth, and his mind able to rise to the higher contemplations; and pondering the divine Scriptures with understanding, he became a burning lover of truth and of the wisdom of God.

As a bishop, the life says, he fed the hungry, helped those who were wronged, defended widows, sheltered orphans and comforted the afflicted. At the last he was preparing to return to his own diocese, and although he knew beforehand that he would not see his flock again, he gave himself to the Holy Spirit who moved him, boarded a ship and set out for Crete.

The ship came as far as Mytilene and stopped there. Asking the name of the place and hearing that it was called Eressos, the saint answered: Here I must give back the image of God to Him who gave it me — that is, here I am to die. And so it happened: he was loosed from the bond of the body and went with joy to the God he longed for. His holy relics were laid in the church of the holy and victorious martyr Anastasia, an unplundered treasury and an ever-flowing spring of wonders.

Doxologia's life names his parents and the place of his death and gives no year, so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 4 July. saint.gr adds that he was born at Damascus of devout parents, George and Gregoria; that he was ordained deacon at Constantinople and set over the orphanage of Saint Paul before Crete had him as archbishop; and that as metropolitan he took part in the synod Philippikos Bardanes convoked in 712 and there supported Monophysitism, returning to the right faith after Bardanes's death. It says he died in 740 aboard ship on the way back from Constantinople and was buried at Eresos on Lesbos, that his skull is at Vatopedi on the Holy Mountain, and that the Great Canon — 261 troparia in nine odes — was probably written at Constantinople or at Eresos in his old age.
