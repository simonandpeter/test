# Raphael of Lesvos

Raphael was George Lascaridis in the world, born in the village of Milos in the province of Ithaca. He left home young, studied medicine and philosophy, and then enrolled as an officer in the Byzantine army; he was energetic and decisive, the life says, and had about a thousand men under him when he was sent on campaign. «Am fost trimis cu o armată din Mistra ca să ajutăm creștinilor care luptau împotriva turcilor» — he was sent with an army out of Mistra to help the Christians fighting the Turks, but the Christians had already been beaten before he could reach them.

«Am îmbrățișat monahismul în urma înfrângerii creștinilor în lupta de la Varna», he told others afterwards: he took up the monastic life after the defeat at Varna. He lived three years in the monastery of St John the Baptist in Macedonia, under an abbot named John whom he called a holy elder, and at his tonsure received the name Raphael. He went on to Athens and served there as priest and preacher at the church of St Demetrius Lombardiari.

On one of his missionary journeys he met in France a young Greek student, [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-of-lesvos), who was taken by his preaching, left the world and followed him, and was ordained deacon not long after. Constantinople fell to the Turks in 1453 and a year later Thrace was invaded; on 14 March 1454 the two sailed from Alexandroupolis to the island of Lesvos, to the village of Thermi, and settled in the monastery of the Nativity of the Theotokos, where Raphael in time became abbot.

Lesvos was taken by the Turks in 1462, though they did not climb as far as Thermi. In 1463 the Christians rose, the rising was put down in blood, and the monastery was suspected of a part in it and destroyed. «În Vinerea Mare turcii ne-au arestat când săvârșeam slujba Prohodului» — they were seized on Great Friday at the Lamentations. Raphael was beaten with rifle-butts until his hands were useless, dragged by the beard and the hair and thrown down; showing them the cross at his neck he said that this they worshipped and would never deny. He was killed by being sawn through the mouth, on Bright Tuesday.

His relics were found in 1959 under the rebuilt church on the hill of Karyes above Thermi, and the finding is most of what the page has to tell: it was by appearing in dreams and visions, it says, that he made his own name and history known. [Irene](/saints/irene-of-lesvos), a girl of twelve, died in the same days, and the village dead are named with them. doxologia.ro's life page heads itself «prăznuire în ziua de marți din Săptămâna Luminată și pe 9 aprilie», and the calendar does keep them twice — on Bright Tuesday and on 9 April.
