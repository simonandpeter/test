# Basil

Vasily Dmitrievich Bogoyavlensky was born in February 1867 in the village of Staroye Seslavino in Tambov province, a priest’s son. He finished the Tambov seminary in 1888, was ordained in 1890 and served at Ovsyanka in the Tambov diocese; in 1896 he entered the Kazan Academy, finished in 1900, and in 1907 took his master’s degree with a study of the Second Book of Maccabees.

Widowed in 1908, he was tonsured and soon made rector of the Chernigov seminary and archimandrite; in 1909 he was consecrated bishop of Sumy, and he held the see of Chernigov from 12 May 1911 to 6 May 1917. In 1917 the Provisional Government dismissed the members of the Holy Synod, all but one, and Basil among them, charged with attachment to the old order and “not answering to the revolutionary spirit of the times”, was retired to the Nikolaev Terebenskaya hermitage in the Tver diocese, and afterwards administered the Zaikonospassky monastery in Moscow.

He took part in the Church Council of 1917–18, which in 1918 sent him to Perm to investigate the arrest and murder of Archbishop Andronik. When the inquiry was done he left Perm with the other members of the commission, the monk-martyr [Matthew](/saints/matthew-monk-martyr-1918) and the layman [Alexius](/saints/alexius-martyr-1918) among them; their train was stopped between Perm and Vyatka, Red Army men burst into the carriage, killed the members of the commission and threw the bodies out, and threw the archbishop himself from the bridge over the Kama into the river. This was on 14 (27) August 1918. The local peasants buried him, but when pilgrimages to the grave began the Bolsheviks dug up the body and burned it. He was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of 2000.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 4 June, not on the Julian 14 August the Russian entry gives, and saint.gr's account of him is the same account with one year changed: its head line reads «(1867 - 1919)» and its narrative dates the murder to 1919, where the Russian source dates it to 1918. It adds a few particulars of its own. It gives his birth as 1 February 1867 at Tambov, of a priestly family, brought up in the discipline and admonition of the Lord; his finishing of the Tambov school in 1888 and his ordination as deacon in the same year; his ordination as priest on 11 March 1890, to cover the liturgical needs of the village of Ovsyanki in the Tambov province; and, after his wife died of an incurable illness, his entry into the Lavra of Saint Alexander Nevsky and his tonsure as a monk on 14 August 1908 — the day the Russian calendar keeps his memory on.

On 15 October of that year, the page says, he was appointed director of the theological school of Chernigov and made archimandrite; on 26 July of the next year he was elected bishop of Sumy in the province of Kharkov, and in 1911 he was moved to Novgorod-Seversk in the diocese of Chernigov, where he worked as a true shepherd and was distinguished for his works of mercy. On 5 October 1916, in recognition of his God-pleasing service, he was raised archbishop of Chernigov and Nezhin; but he resigned in 1917 and withdrew to the Zaikonospassky monastery in Moscow. When Andronicus, archbishop of Perm, was murdered in 1918, Basil was called by the church council of Moscow to investigate it; and the work of that commission enraged the guilty men, who were trying to hide.
