# Chrysogonus

Chrysogonus was a Christian of Rome, a man renowned for wisdom and honourable in life, faithful and perfectly versed in the things of God. [Anastasia](/saints/anastasia-pupil-of-chrysogonus), the daughter of a pagan nobleman of the city named Pretextat and of a Christian mother, Fausta, was put into his hands as a child for her schooling, and from him she learned not only the understanding of letters but to know and to love the true God, the Maker and Master of all. Afterwards he was imprisoned for Christ, and had been two years in prison enduring every kind of torture while she, shut up and guarded by her pagan husband, could no longer come to him.

Letters passed between them by an old woman. She wrote that her husband was spending on idolaters the wealth she had promised to God, that he held her like a slave and that she looked only to die. Chrysogonus wrote back to call her to patience: Christ who walks on the water would come to her quickly and calm with one word of his command the devilish winds raised against her; she should wait as though in the middle of the sea, expect a double gift, since the temporal inheritance would return to her and the heavenly be made ready, and keep herself watchfully from every sin, seeking her comfort from God whose commandments she kept.

When she wrote that the end of her body had come, he answered that darkness always goes before the light, that health returns after sickness and life is promised after death, and that the contrary things of this world end as the fortunate ones do, so that neither should despair reign through hardship nor pride through empty joy; and he told her to bind herself with her whole mind to the Cross of Christ and make herself ready for the Lord's work. In that letter he foretold the ruin of her husband, who was sent shortly after with letters to the king of the Persians and drowned in a storm at sea.

At that time the emperor Diocletian was staying at Aquileia and taking pains that no Christian should hide from him or escape his hands, and word came from Rome that the prisons there were full of Christians who would not deny their Christ under any torture and who all had Chrysogonus for their teacher and held to his teaching. The emperor ordered them all given to torture and death, and Chrysogonus brought to him, reckoning that if he overcame Chrysogonus he would overcome the other Christians too. Anastasia followed him at a distance.

The emperor spoke gently at first, telling him to take his good counsel and share their faith, do what was pleasing to the gods and choose what was joyful and useful rather than what was hard and profitless, and promising that he should not only escape torture but be given the sweet light and made prefect of Rome itself. Chrysogonus answered that he knew one God, whom he reckoned sweeter than all light, more loved than all life and more profitable than all treasures; that he believed in him with his heart and confessed him with his mouth, honoured him with his soul and bent his knees to him before them all.

The multitude of the emperor's gods, he said, and the fables, and the devil, he would never honour; and the gifts and honours promised him he reckoned no better than a dream and a shadow. Unable to bear such words, the emperor ordered the soldiers to take him to a desert place and cut off his head, and when it was done his body was thrown into the sea. Not far from where it was cast lived a priest named Zoil, a holy man, and three sisters in body and in spirit, [Agape](/saints/agape-of-aquileia), [Chionia](/saints/chionia-of-aquileia) and [Irene](/saints/irene-of-aquileia). Warned by some divine revelation, the priest took up the body with the head, laid it in a coffin and hid it in his house.

At thirty days Chrysogonus appeared to him in a dream: within nine days those three virgins of Christ who lived beside him would be taken to martyrdom, and he was to tell the Lord's servant Anastasia to have a care for them and rouse them to a manly contest until they should be crowned by their suffering; and he himself should hope well, for he would soon be freed from that place and go to Christ with those who had suffered for him. The same revelation came to Anastasia, who was moved by the Spirit of God to come to the priest's house, which she had never known, and to ask where the virgins were of whose martyr's end it had been revealed to her.

Finding their house, she spent a night with them in love, speaking many words that save the soul and are full of the love of God, by which she roused them to endure manfully to blood for Christ their Bridegroom. There too she saw the relics of Chrysogonus, the martyr of Christ and her own beloved teacher, weeping beside them with hot tears and commending herself to his prayers. Then she went back to Aquileia; and it fell out as Chrysogonus had said to Zoil in the dream, for within those nine days the priest departed to the Lord. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mucenic Hrisogon», gives no year, and its page for him prints no hymn.
