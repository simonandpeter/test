# Christopher

An early cult is well attested: a church was dedicated to a martyr of this name
in Bithynia by 452, which is early enough that something lies behind it. What
that something was is not recoverable. The surviving passions are late, mutually
inconsistent, and place him variously in Lycia, Syria and Canaan.

The story known in the West is later still. A giant of enormous strength
resolves to serve only the greatest king; finding that even the devil fears the
cross, he seeks out Christ, and is set to carry travellers across a river. One
night he carries a child who grows unbearably heavy — the weight of the world
and its maker — and is named Christ-bearer, *Christophoros*, for it. Eastern
accounts run differently and sometimes give him a dog's head, a detail that has
been read as a mistranslation, a symbol, or a memory of something else entirely.

He was dropped from the General Roman Calendar in 1969 for want of historical
evidence. He was not decanonised, and both traditions here still keep his feast
— on different days. This entry has no coordinates: everything said about where
he lived is part of the legend rather than evidence for it, and a dot on the map
would be a claim the sources cannot support.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same menologion day as the Russian, the
Romanian and the Greek, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović —
as the Православни подсетник serves it for 9. мај ст. ст. on
[the day read on 5 October 2026](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-05-22&prolog=1)
— gives him four sentences and no more. It calls him a great wonderworker; it
says he is especially honoured in Spain; it says the people pray to him above
all against infectious diseases and great mortality; and it closes that he was
tormented for Christ and glorified by Christ in the year 249. The Spain and the
plagues are in no other calendar read for this folder. The year is not taken
here: the death already stands as 249 to 251 on the passions' account, and one
book's single year inside that interval would narrow it rather than widen it.
