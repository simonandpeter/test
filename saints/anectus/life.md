# Anectus

Anectus lived in the days of Diocletian and was arrested because he was a Christian. Because he would not sacrifice to the idols he was bound to four oxen and stretched out, then beaten with rods, mutilated and pierced with spears, and at the last they cut off his head.

That is the whole of the entry doxologia prints for him — three lines and no more. It gives no country, no city, no judge's name, no year, no place of burial and no hymn, and nothing more is set down here than the calendar sets down. It names Diocletian, and prints no year, so no date is recorded.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 27 June, and sets the martyrdom in a named place and year. saint.gr says he was a devout and zealous man who by his words and his works was a valuable strength to the Church at Caesarea of Cappadocia; that in 298 the governor of that city, Urbanus, arrested him, and failing to tear him from his faith fell back on the raw and brutal violence of torture. First they beat him with rods. Then they tore his sides with iron claws, pierced his ankles, and burned his wounds with lit torches; and when they saw that he was still breathing they beheaded him, and so this true Christian took his martyr's crown. The particulars are not quite those the Romanian calendar gives, which has him bound to four oxen and pierced with spears; both have the rods and the beheading, and the Greek year falls inside the reign the Romanian life names. The page's couplet turns on his name: «Οὐκ ἦν ἀνεκτὸν τῷ Ἀνέκτῳ μὴ στέργειν τὸν διὰ Χριστὸν θάνατον μέχρι τέλους» — it was not bearable to Anectus not to love, to the very end, death for Christ's sake.
