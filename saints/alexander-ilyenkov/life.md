# Alexander (Ilyenkov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar gives him a line of his own, the second to last of the Julian 1 March — «Cщмч. Александра пресвитера (1942).» — and prints no life for him. Alexander Alexandrovich Ilyenkov was born on 9 April 1896 in the settlement of Sudak in the province of Taurida, the son of the priest Alexander Ioakimovich Ilyenkov. He finished the Taurida theological seminary and went to his father, who after 1917 had moved with the family to the village of Chernigovka in the Berdyansk district, and there he met Paraskeva, who became his wife; they had three children.

He decided to take holy orders at a time when the cruel persecution of the Russian Orthodox Church had already begun. His wife, learning of it, set herself against it and tried to talk him out of a step she thought improvident, but he would not be moved, and in 1924 the bishop ordained him priest and sent him to serve in the village of Novopavlovka. By his fervent and seemly serving he quickly won the regard of his parishioners, for whom the church service was then often the one consolation in a sorrowful life.

In 1930 drunken Komsomol members tried to wreck the priest’s house. Coming right up to it, threatening and shouting, they fired through the window, and the bullet went into the samovar; a woman who saw what was happening cried out «Батюшку убили, ироды!» — they have killed the priest, the monsters — and they took fright at the noise in the street and ran off. In the thirties the plundering of the peasant holdings began under the name of dekulakisation, and even in that hard time, when they were hard put to it themselves, the peasants did not abandon him but brought food and grain to his house.

A search came to the house, and finding two sacks of grain the men began to take them; he resisted, saying he had a large family, and they set on him and beat him and dragged him by the beard. He could not bear it and let the sacks go, the grain spilled on the ground, and he was charged with scattering and rotting collective property and arrested on the spot and put in prison for a week. Half a year after that the church in the village was closed, and he and his family had to leave for Berdyansk.

There he served in the church of the Protection with other priests who had been left without places, earning the family’s bread as a watchman, as an odd-job man, as a loader; sometimes he hired himself out for day-labour, which consisted of cleaning the public lavatories. In the summer of 1937 all the priests who had gathered around that church were arrested, and he with them. At first he was held in the prison at Berdyansk, where at the interrogations the investigators beat him cruelly to make him sign false evidence, and to every question he answered in a word that he did not admit himself guilty.

When the investigation was over he was moved to the prison at Zaporozhye, and on 29 October 1939 the troika of the НКВД sentenced him to five years in a camp; he was sent to Usollag, not far from the town of Solikamsk in the Perm region. He died in confinement on 14 March 1942 and was buried in an unmarked grave. His daughter was in a German concentration camp at the time and knew nothing of what had become of him; in the night of 13 to 14 March she dreamed that a beautiful bud opened in the sky and out of it came the face of the Saviour, and beside it her father on his knees at prayer in a blue vestment shot with pink. Many years later she learned that the dream had fallen on the day of his death.

The year on the calendar’s line is the year he died in the camp and not a year of execution: he was sentenced to five years and not to death, and the sentence had three and a half years still to run. On the grade the two hosts read differently and the reading is the calendar’s own index: the line calls him «пресвитера» and azbyka.ru heads him «пресвитер», while the name link behind his name reads «иерей», a priest, which is what the office here records.
