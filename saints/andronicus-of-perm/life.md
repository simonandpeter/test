# Andronicus of Perm

saint.gr says the holy hieromartyr Andronicus was born on 1 August 1870 in the village of Povodnevo in the province of Yaroslavl, in central Russia, and received the name Vladimir at holy baptism. At ten he was entered in a church school, and after finishing it he went in 1885 to the church seminary of Yaroslavl. In 1891, as the seminary's first graduate, he was received into the Theological Academy of Moscow, and in those years the young student met [John of Kronstadt](/saints/john-of-kronstadt), whom the page marks as kept on 20 December, and asked for his enlightened counsel.

His monastic tonsure was performed in the Academy's church on 1 August 1893; on 6 August of the same year the monk Andronicus was ordained deacon, and on 22 July 1895 presbyter. On 3 September 1897 the Holy Synod appointed him to the Orthodox mission in Japan, and in March 1899 he received the office of archimandrite; on 23 October 1906 he was appointed assistant to the bishop [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-of-japan) in Japan, but his health was frail, he fell gravely ill, and on 7 July 1907 the Synod decided to recall him.

On 26 October 1907 the Synod gave him the temporary care of the province of Cholm, and on 15 March 1908 he was appointed assistant to the archbishop of Novgorod. On 8 March 1913 he took up the care of the provinces of Omsk and Pavlodar in western Siberia, and in August 1914 the Synod moved him once more, to the church province of Perm in northern Russia.

On 3 June 1918 the hierarch learned of the plans for his arrest from someone who had chanced to hear them. He decided nevertheless not to leave the province but to stay in his place among his flock and, if it were the Lord's will, to be martyred for him. He told his fellow workers, and they sent the monk Michael to the bell tower of the cathedral with orders to sound the alarm on the bells at the moment of the arrest. That same night, an hour after midnight, the whole block in which the bishop's house stood was surrounded by soldiers, with a carriage and fast horses brought to take the archbishop out of the city as quickly as possible to the neighbouring town of Motovilikha.

Figures came up to the front door in the dark, found it locked, tore it off its hinges and went in. Only three men climbed the stairs — Malkov, the president of the Executive Committee of Perm; Ivantsenko, the police director of Perm; and Zuzgov, the assistant police director of Motovilikha. The bishop was keeping vigil with two clergy. They asked which of them was the archbishop Andronicus, and he answered calmly that he was. At that moment the cathedral bells sounded the alarm; some shots were heard, and the ringing stopped. They ordered him to follow them without delay, and he obeyed without a word of protest; in a few minutes he was getting into the carriage in a worn cassock, a monk's cap, his bishop's panagia and his staff in his hand, and the carriage set off for Motovilikha.

On 6 June 1918, the page says, the agents of the atheist regime covered him alive with earth in the grave, fired several times at the brave contestant of the faith, the archbishop Andronicus, and a little after his martyred body was buried in the ground.
