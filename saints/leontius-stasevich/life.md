# Leontius (Stasevich)

The Russian calendar keeps him on this day as «Прп. Леонтия исповедника (1972)», and the life behind that line is long. Lev Fomich Stasevich was born on 20 March 1884 in the township of Tarnograd in the Biłgoraj district of the Lublin province, in the Kingdom of Poland and so within the Russian Empire. His parents, Foma Fyodorovich and Ekaterina Stefanovna, were peasants, and an uncle of his, Adam, was an archpriest. He was their only child, born when his father was forty-four, and he finished the parish school and helped at home.

His father, he remembered, never came back from market without some wanderer who had nowhere to sleep, and the wanderers paid for their lodging in spiritual songs and accounts of the holy places they had walked to. From fifteen he worked as a clerk in the Tarnograd court; he buried his father in 1905 and became his elderly mother's only support, and he never married. Having provided for her, on 1 January 1910 he entered the monastery of St Onuphrius at Yablochna, and in 1911 was tonsured there as Leontius, after the hierarch Leontius of Rostov whose memory the same calendar keeps on 23 May. A year later he buried his mother.

In 1914 the war emptied the monastery, which lay close to the front, and he was sent to the Theophany monastery in Moscow; there he finished the church school in 1916, was ordained deacon and then priest, and completed the seminary in 1919. In 1922 the Patriarch appointed him vicar of the Saviour-Euthymius monastery at Suzdal with the rank of archimandrite. He found the services neglected, the estate in disorder and much of the brotherhood in sympathy with the renovationist schism; some of the monks beat him and tried to drive him out, while the pilgrims and the townspeople loved him. He stayed faithful to [Patriarch Tikhon](/saints/tikhon-of-moscow).

He was arrested in 1930, and the pretext was bell-ringing. «Залез на колокольню и давай звонить», he said of it — the ringing was forbidden, and he wanted so much to glorify God with it that he climbed the tower and rang a long while, and came down to men waiting with handcuffs. He served that term in the Komi republic as a medical orderly on a road-building section, came back in 1933 to a parish at Borodino, and was arrested again on 19 October 1935: his few things were loaded on a cart and he was tied to it by the neck and driven through a jeering crowd to the district NKVD. He admitted nothing, and was given three years.

That winter a trainload of prisoners stood on the platform at Ivanovo, many of them priests, shaven and cropped; they knew each other all the same and sang «Царю Небесный» aloud until the guards stopped them, and the wagons were shunted into a siding in a hard frost where many froze. In his wagon everyone lived, because he had them make prostrations with the Jesus prayer all night. In the camp the guards tried to re-educate him, and one Pascha night they demanded he deny God; when he refused they tied a rope to him and lowered him head-first into the latrine, hauled him up and shouted «Отрекаешься?», and he answered «Христос воскресе, ребята».

Released at the end of 1938, he lived at Suzdal and went about the villages serving what was asked of him. In June 1947 he was made rector of the Trinity church at Vorontsovo, and on 2 May 1950 he was arrested a third time, straight after the Liturgy; three days before it he had begun giving away everything he owned, his cell icons included. At sixty-six he was sentenced to ten years and sent to the Ozerny camp in Komi. He returned, served again at Mikhailovskoye from 1963, and was mocked there in his old age by men who tripped him during the censing and dropped banners on him.

He had said he wished to die at a service. He served his last Liturgy on 7 February 1972; on the eighth he weakened and lifted his hands, saying «К Богу идем», we are going to God; on the ninth he was communed at home, the singers came and sang to him, and at four in the afternoon he died. Miracles at his grave were confirmed by the diocesan commission. He was canonised in 1999 as a locally venerated saint of the Ivanovo diocese, and numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
