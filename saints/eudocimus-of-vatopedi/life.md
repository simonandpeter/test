# Eudocimus of Vatopedi

Eudocimus is known only from his relics. He lived and struggled at the monastery of Vatopedi on the Holy Mountain, but his real name, monastic or baptismal, is not known — Eudocimus, “worthy of honour”, was given him after his remains were found — nor when he lived, nor where he came from, nor anything of his ascetic labours.

The Romanian life says that in 1841, when the monastery’s ossuary was being restored, the workmen found the bones of a monk kneeling and holding an icon of the Most Holy Theotokos with both hands to his breast; the Greek entry says the relics gave off a divine fragrance. The Greek entry dates the translation of the relics to 5 October 1840, after a manuscript of Meletius, hierodeacon of Vatopedi; the Romanian has the fathers bring them into the church with an all-night vigil on 5 October 1840, a year before the finding it dates to 1841, and both years are given here as it prints them.

Many wonders have been worked through the relics, the Romanian life says. The first healed was an old monk of Colciu, paralysed for five years, who recovered at once, and the silver reliquary of his head still bears the words that it was made by the monk Gabriel, whom the saint had healed of a terrible illness. Some sources, the Greek entry says, hold that the relics are those of Sabbas of Vatopedi, kept on 15 June; the Romanian gives the same as an oral tradition and calls Sabbas a fool for Christ of the fourteenth century. His memory is kept every year at Vatopedi with a vigil on 5 October.
