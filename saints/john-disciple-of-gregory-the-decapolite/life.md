# John, disciple of Gregory the Decapolite

The Decapolis, the life begins, was once two lands of that name: the one near Galilee in Palestine, which the Evangelist Matthew remembers when he writes that many peoples followed him out of Galilee and out of the ten cities, and a smaller one in Isauria. It was from the Isaurian Decapolis that Gregory the Decapolite came, the teacher of this John whose memory is kept today.

John left the world in his youth for the love of Christ, came to Gregory and was tonsured a monk by him. He lived with his teacher, labouring in everything and pleasing God, and came to such humility, such obedience and such readiness for every good work that Gregory himself rejoiced over him and glorified God.

When the emperor Leo the Armenian renewed the heresy of the iconoclasts and raised a persecution against the Church, John went to Byzantium with his teacher and with Joseph the writer of hymns; and they walked about the city strengthening the Orthodox to stand in their confession. Joseph was then sent to Rome and never reached it, for he fell into the hands of the heretics and was held in chains in Crete; and after he was sent away, Gregory the Decapolite departed to the Lord.

John stayed on in Byzantium at his accustomed labours, caring not for his own salvation only but for that of others. When Joseph was at last freed and came back, John too departed to the Lord, to take the wages of his labours, and Joseph buried him beside Gregory's grave. The life of Joseph the hymnographer, which the page quotes at length, tells the rest: that Joseph afterwards settled in a quiet place outside the city, not far from the church of Saint John Chrysostom, built there a church in the name of the hierarch Nicholas, and translated the relics of both fathers, Gregory's and John's, to it. No year is given anywhere in the notice.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day as the Romanian calendar and gives him five sentences, two of which the Romanian notice does not have. In the time of the iconoclast heresy, it says, the emperor Leo the Armenian put this John to the torture together with his teacher [Gregory](/saints/gregory-the-decapolite) and with [Joseph the Hymnographer](/saints/joseph-the-hymnographer) — where the Romanian life has the three of them walking about the city strengthening the Orthodox and says nothing of torture. When Gregory ended his earthly life, John became abbot of the Decapolite's monastery in Constantinople, and having become abbot he redoubled his struggle for the kingdom of God. He reposed peacefully about the year 820 — the only year any source read for this folder gives — and after his death Joseph buried him honourably near Gregory's grave, which is where the Romanian life leaves him too.

**The Russian calendar keeps him seven days before the day the Romanian and Serbian rows on this folder carry, and its own life page prints both.** The day that lists him is 11 апреля ст. ст., and the page behind the line heads «Дни памяти: 11 апреля , 18 апреля» — the eleventh and the eighteenth. Seven days is not the thirteen of the style conversion, so the two are two commemorations and not one read twice; the Russian row below stands on the day the day page printed, and the eighteenth is recorded in its note as the day the other two calendars keep.

The life itself is the fullest account of him this corpus has read. He was born at the end of the eighth century, and in his youth became the disciple of [Gregory the Decapolite](/saints/gregory-the-decapolite) and was tonsured by him in a monastery of Thessalonica, and under that experienced teacher came to a high spiritual perfection. When the emperor Leo the Armenian, whose reign the page dates 813 to 820, renewed the persecution of the Orthodox for the honouring of the holy icons, Gregory set out from Thessalonica for Constantinople to work against the iconoclast heresy, and took with him [Joseph the Hymnographer](/saints/joseph-the-hymnographer) and his own disciple John.

For several years, the page says, and in the face of the persecution, Gregory and John defended Orthodoxy fearlessly and preached the honouring of the icons. After many labours Gregory reposed, about the year 820, and soon after him his faithful disciple John departed to the Lord; and Joseph the Hymnographer carried the relics of them both into a church he had built in the name of [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-of-myra) the Wonderworker. The birth at the end of the eighth century is the folder's first birth date and comes from this page; the death it already carried, about 820, from the Ohrid Prologue, is what this page says of him too, by way of Gregory's own year.

The day page prints one hymn under his name, a kontakion in the fourth tone, which is given below with its English. It also prints a troparion under the name of his teacher — Gregory, whose own `russian` row this corpus holds on the Julian 20 November, a different day — and that hymn is his teacher's proper and not his, so it is not taken here.
