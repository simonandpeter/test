# Eutropius, companion of Tigrius

Eutropius is the second of the two men the Greek calendar keeps in one line on 12 Ιανουαρίου: «Άγιοι Τίγριος ο Πρεσβύτερος και Ευτρόπιος», [Tigrius](/saints/tigrius-the-presbyter) the presbyter and Eutropius. The line gives him no rank and no epithet, and the page behind it has one sentence for the two of them together.

That sentence is «Οι Άγιοι Τίγριος και Ευτρόπιος έζησαν κατά τους χρόνους του βασιλέως Αρκαδίου (395 - 408 μ.Χ.). Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο των Αγίων» — they lived in the years of the emperor Arcadius, whom the page dates 395 to 408, and we have no details of the lives of these saints. The calendar prints no city, no office, no manner of death and no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 16 June, where the Greek calendar keeps him and his companion on 12 January, and it gives the life saint.gr said it did not have. He and [Tigrius](/saints/tigrius-the-presbyter) were of the clergy of [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom); and when malicious men drove Chrysostom out of Constantinople, and the cathedral church caught fire, and the flame fell on the houses of his persecutors, his enemies laid the fire at the door of his adherents. Many of those men suffered for it, and these two were among them, under a city governor — an unbaptised Greek named Optatus — who raised the hunt with particular malice.

This book gives him the rank the Greek line withheld: Eutropius the reader. It calls him a virgin from his birth, pure, blameless and without malice — words of the body and not of the heading, so the folder takes no type from them. He was beaten with ox sinews and with staves, and at the last he was hanged. And when the Christians carried his body away to bury it, sweet angelic singing was heard in the air. No year is given for any of it, and the reign the Greek page named is left to stand as the only bracket there is.
