# Jeremiah

Jeremiah was of Anathoth, the city of the priests in the portion of Benjamin, about three miles from Jerusalem, and his father was Helkias, a chief among the priests. The Romanian life says he was chosen and sanctified a prophet from his mother's womb, as the Lord told him; that on the witness of Ignatius the God-bearer he was a virgin all his life; and that he began to prophesy while still a child, saying to God that he did not know how to speak, for he was a boy — and the Lord touched his mouth with his hand and put his words there. The word of the Lord came to him in the thirteenth year of Josiah, king of Judah, when he was fifteen years old.

The life then follows the fall of Jerusalem: Pharaoh Necho against Josiah, Jehoahaz set up and put down, Jehoiakim's tribute, and Nebuchadnezzar carrying off the vessels of the house of God. Jeremiah was nearly condemned to death by the priests and the false prophets, and was saved when the elders recalled the days of Hezekiah, and again when Ahikam the son of Shaphan held them back. He was put in irons, and dictated to [Baruch](/saints/baruch-the-prophet) the book that Jehoiakim burned, and then a second and longer one.

Under Zedekiah he wore a wooden yoke on his neck before the kings' envoys; the false prophet Hananiah broke it; and at the last the princes had the prophet let down on ropes into the pit of Malchiah, where there was no water but mire, and he stood in it up to his neck until an Ethiopian of the court, the life calls him Avdemeleh, had him drawn out. He carried the tabernacle and the Ark to the mountain where Moses had looked out on the land of promise, and hid the Ark in a cave there, sealing the door with letters written in the stone by his finger.

After the city fell, the people would not hear him and took him down into Egypt against his will, and Baruch with him. He lived there four years, held in great honour for his holiness and for his good works, killing the asps and the crocodiles that destroyed the people by his prayer. He foretold that the king of Babylon would come upon Egypt, and the Jews settled there, angry at him, stoned him to death. In the same year the king of Babylon came and the whole company perished as he had said. Long after, his relics were carried to Alexandria by Alexander of Macedon. The page gives no year for any of it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's notice fills in the beginning. He was born, it says, probably about 650 BC at Anathoth, a small town of the tribe of Benjamin north-east of Jerusalem; his father was a priest named Chelkias, and he was brought up strictly in that priestly household and studied the prophets before him, Isaiah and Hosea. Very young, about twenty-three or twenty-five years old, he was called by God to the prophetic office, and he shrank from it, pleading the weakness of his youth, until God promised him not rewards or honours but the most precious thing of all, His help.

The page sets his public work out in four periods: under Josiah before the reform, under Jehoiakim and up to Zedekiah, under Zedekiah, and after the fall of Jerusalem and the king's captivity. Against the false prophets who promised that the stolen vessels of the temple would come back he put a yoke on his own neck, and when Hananiah broke the wooden yoke he answered that God would set iron ones on their necks. The rulers threw him into a miry pit because his words unnerved the fighting men, and Abdemelech had him drawn out of it. Of his ascetic life the page keeps one detail: he wore nothing but a linen girdle.

At the end, taken to Egypt against his will with the people who fled there, he preached to them too and foretold their ruin; and he was stoned by his own countrymen at Taphnas in Egypt, or, as some rabbinic tradition has it, carried off captive with [Baruch](/saints/baruch-the-prophet) by Nebuchadnezzar in an invasion of Egypt in 568 BC. His synaxis, saint.gr adds, used to be kept in the church of the Apostle Peter near the Great Church.
