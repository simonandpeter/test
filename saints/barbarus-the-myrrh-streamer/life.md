# Barbarus the Myrrh-streamer

saint.gr says that, according to his encomiast Constantine Akropolites and to the Synaxarion, Barbarus belonged to a band of Arab raiders who fell upon southern Epirus and Aetolia in the days of the emperor Michael the Stammerer, who reigned from 820 to 829. His companions were killed in one of the fights, and from then on he went about alone, the page says, «λῄσταρχος γενόμενος, καὶ ποιῶν ἀβάτους τᾶς ὁδούς, οἰκῶν ἐν ὄρεσι καὶ ἀλυσώδεσι τόποις» — become a chief of robbers, making the roads impassable, living in mountains and in thickets.

By God's providence, the page continues, he went one day into a church dedicated to the Great Martyr George at a place called Nisa, where a priest named John was serving. At the elevation of the Holy Gifts the priest saw him and prayed in fear; and at that moment the Lord opened the robber's eyes, and he saw angels concelebrating with the priest. When the Liturgy was over Barbarus asked him where those who had been with him were, and the priest explained that God's providence had counted him worthy to see what human eyes cannot see, so that he might be led to repentance.

He put off his robber's weapons at once, repented, began to live as an ascetic, and was baptised. His struggle grew harder in the region of Tryphos of Xeromeros in Aetolia and Akarnania, where for three years, the page says, he «πεποίηκε χρόνους τρεῖς κυλιόμενος ὡς τετράπους καὶ ἐσθίων χοῦν καὶ βοτάνας», rolling on the ground like a four-footed beast, eating dust and the plants that grew there, weeping and lamenting while his flesh was cut about him. One night a farmer eating in the place where he was living killed him by mistake, taking him for a wild animal.

His tomb gave off myrrh, saint.gr says, and he worked many wonders. The page adds that the local hagiology of the Metropolis of Aetolia and Akarnania and of Corfu calls him Barbarus the Pentapolite and keeps his memory on 23 June, and that people in Aetolia still speak of the cave where he kept his ascesis and of his holy spring. In 1571 a Venetian soldier named Sklavounos, who had fought at the battle of Lepanto, fell suddenly ill, saw the saint in a vision calling him to venerate his tomb, went there and was healed; and when he took up the relics to carry them to Venice and stopped at Corfu for supplies, a paralysed young man was healed at the village of Potamos, where a church to him stands to this day.

The Serbian church keeps him on the Julian 6 May, a fortnight before the Greek day this folder was read from, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells him in five sentences which are the same man and not the same story. After many crimes, it says, he repented and sentenced himself: first, that for three years he should go on all fours and eat with the dogs, and second, that for twelve years he should live in the forest with no clothing, no roof and no food of any kind except grass and leaves. **He received word from an angel that his sins were forgiven**, which the Greek page does not have.

Merchants were travelling through the forest, and seeing him from a distance they took him for a beast and not a man, drew their bows and shot him. As he lay dying he begged them to tell the priest nearby about him; the priest came and buried him honourably, and from his body there flowed healing myrrh, which cured various sicknesses and torments in men.

**The two accounts part in four places and neither was chosen.** This book sets the self-imposed penance at three years on all fours **and twelve more in the forest**, where the Greek page has the three years in Aetolia and no twelve; it has **merchants with bows** kill him from a distance, where the Greek page has one farmer kill him by mistake; it has **a priest come and bury him** after the killing, where the Greek page's priest John is the man who showed him the angels at the Liturgy long before; and the myrrh here flows **from his body**, where the Greek page has it from his tomb. **No year and no reign is printed here**, so nothing was done to this folder's `floruit`, which stands at the 820 to 829 of Michael the Stammerer's reign that saint.gr dates the raid by. The Prologue names no country, no band, no church and no emperor.
