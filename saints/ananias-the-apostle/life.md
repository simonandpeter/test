# Ananias

The Greek synaxarion holds Ananias up as a man of obedience. When God told him in a vision to go to Saul, who was the fear and terror of the Christians, he obeyed at once: he went to the street called Straight, sought out the house of Judas where Saul was, healed him and baptized him, and Saul, as Paul, became the great apostle of the nations. Ananias went on to Eleutheropolis, where his teaching drew many souls to Christ.

The stir his preaching made led the governor Lucian to arrest him, and when every means had failed to turn him from the faith Lucian had him flogged with ox sinews, his sides torn with iron claws and the wounds burned with lighted torches, and at last had him led out of the city and stoned. The synaxarion gives no year. It says fragments of his relics are at the monasteries of Ypsilou on Lesbos and of Kykkos in Cyprus, and keeps him with two apolytikia, the second the apostle’s common, and a kontakion.

The Romanian life counts him one of the Seventy and makes him bishop of Damascus. In its telling he first shrank from the Lord’s command, knowing what harm Saul had done the saints in Jerusalem, and went when told that Saul was a chosen vessel; he laid his hands on him so that he saw again, baptized him, and a few days later, when the Jews plotted to kill Paul, let him down over the wall in a basket with the other disciples. He preached to Jews and Gentiles, and went from Damascus to Eleutheropolis, healing the sick and bringing many to faith.

The idolatrous governor Lucian there had ordered that anyone who named Christ be tortured; Ananias was seized and brought before him, refused to sacrifice, confessing the Lord who had appeared to him at Damascus, and was beaten, his ribs scraped with iron hooks and his wounds burned with candles. Then Lucian had the people take him out of the city and stone him, as Stephen was stoned, and he died commending his spirit to Jesus Christ. His body was left unburied, and believers passing that way from Damascus carried it back and buried it on his land. The Romanian calendar keeps him with a troparion, the apostle’s common, and a kontakion.
