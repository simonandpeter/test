# Diadochus of Photike

Diadochus was born about the year 400 in the Roman province of Illyricum. From his youth he was diligent at his learning, and he chose the monastic life, labouring in obedience and in the struggle with temptations towards the gaining of dispassion. He became known to all as a spiritual father, and was chosen bishop of the city of Photike in Greece, whose ruins can still be seen today near the town of Paramythia, in the old region of Epirus.

He took part in the Fourth Ecumenical Council, at Chalcedon in the year 451, where he defended the Orthodox teaching of the two natures, divine and human, in the person of the incarnate Son of God. In 458 he signed, with other hierarchs, a letter to the emperor Leo I concerning the disputes between the Orthodox and the Monophysite heretics. He was a defender of the right faith against Monophysitism, and also against Messalianism, the heresy which did not give its due weight to grace and to ascetic labour in the spiritual work.

Of his writings there survive the Century — the hundred chapters on spiritual perfection, which is in the Philokalia; the writing called the Vision, on the sight of God on earth and in the life to come; a sermon on the Ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ; and a homily of which doxologia.ro's list gives more than is quoted here. From him is the counsel that the man who warmly calls upon the holy name of the Lord Jesus, and uses that most holy and glorious name as a weapon, will see the crafty deceiver depart.

His pastorate was troubled by the invasion of the Vandals between the years 467 and 474, who carried off many of the notable citizens of Photike into captivity in North Africa, near Carthage. Because of those troubled times it is not known how Saint Diadochus ended his life; his passing to eternity took place a little before the year 486. For the holiness of his life, for his teachings, and for his defence of the right faith the Church numbered him among the saints, honouring him on 29 March and also in the Synaxis of all the Venerable Fathers, on Cheesefare Saturday.
