# Azas, companion of James of Pharatha

saint.gr says that [James](/saints/james-of-pharatha) the presbyter and Azas the deacon lived under Shapur II, king of the Persians, whom it calls the fighter against Christians; that the presbyter James was from the village of Pharatha, and the deacon Azas from the village of Bithykora. In the year 332 they were denounced as Christians and examined before the chief magus, whose name the page gives in two forms, Achoschargan or Archogaichar.

Because they stood by their confession of Christ, they were first beaten hard, and then left naked through the night in a bitter and unbearable cold. In the morning, when they were barely breathing, but the life of faith kept all its vigour and its edge in their souls, they were asked: do you at last renounce Jesus? «Να πεθάνουμε θέλουμε γι' Αυτόν» — we wish to die for him, the martyrs answered. Then the ruler gave the order and they were beheaded.

The page's couplet for Azas turns on his order and on his name: the Levite of Christ, cut in the head, puts to shame Leviathan, the enemy of Christ. Nothing else is told: no age, no year of birth, no relic, and no hymn. The page names the two of them together and no one else with them.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 10 Απριλίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/04/10/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1304/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
