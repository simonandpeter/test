# Elesbaan of Ethiopia

He was king of Ethiopia in the days when Arabia was governed by a Jewish persecutor of Christians, and the Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery, which keeps him on 24 октября ст. ст., tells his story as the story of a vengeance God would not have. He could not look on while those who believed in Christ were destroyed, and declared war on the persecutor; and his campaign failed.

Wishing to know why he had been beaten, he was directed from above to a certain recluse, who told him that he had done wrong in resolving to avenge himself, because God says «Мне отмщение, Я воздам!» — vengeance is mine, I will repay. The recluse counselled him to give the last days of his life to God, so as to escape the wrath of God for a vengeance taken on his own authority, and so to conquer. He made that vow to the Lord, went out against the enemy with his army, defeated him, took him prisoner and put him to death.

After the victory he left his kingdom, shut himself into a monastic cell and spent fifteen years there in strict abstinence and ascetic labour. The calendar closes his line and his life with the same span of years and no single year, «(553-555)», and it gives him a second name in brackets which it does not explain. The day page carries his troparion and his kontakion, both of them about the crown he laid down; the troparion sets the recluse who prophesied to him beside the prophet who anointed David.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 24 October and is the second calendar to keep him at all. Fired with zeal for the faith of Christ, this pious emperor raised an army against the prince Dunaan, the wicked tormentor of the Christians in the land of the Homerites. But at the beginning of the war he had no success, and much of his army perished in a waterless desert.

Then he wept bitterly before God, and promised to become a monk if God would help him to defeat the murderer of Christians. Having defeated Dunaan, Elesbaan returned to Ethiopia and at once left the imperial court and entered a monastery, where he struggled strictly as a true monk for a full fifteen years. God gave him the grace of wonderworking both before and after his death. He fell asleep, this book says, in 555 — which is one end of the span this folder already records, and so nothing here is narrowed.
