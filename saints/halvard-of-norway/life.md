# Halvard of Norway

Halvard is honoured to this day as the patron of the innocent and of the city of Oslo, whose arms show him holding three arrows and a millstone. He was born about the year 1020 at Husaby in the south-east of Norway, into a family of means: his father Vebjørn was a merchant on the northern seas, and his mother Torny was a close kinswoman of the holy king Olav, who brought Norway to the faith. His parents were Christians and raised him from childhood in the Christian teaching and above all in the giving of oneself for others; and like his father he became a merchant.

In the spring of 1043 he was on the shore, making his boat ready to cross the Drammen fjord, when he saw a young pregnant woman running towards him in terror, her clothes torn and her eyes full of tears. She was a slave, and she told him between sobs that three men were pursuing her, accusing her of having stolen something from their tent, and she swore she was innocent and begged him not to leave her in their hands. Halvard was filled with pity, told her to get into the boat and fear nothing, and the two set out across the fjord.

The pursuers caught them and demanded the woman. He answered that he could not give her up, because she had sworn she had stolen nothing, and promised to pay himself for everything they were missing. That angered them the more, and they told him that if he did not hand her over at once they would kill him too. He would not yield. They took up their bows and shot at the two of them: three arrows went through Halvard's body and left him dead in the boat, and the woman also died under the rain of arrows.

When the deed was done the men tied a millstone to Halvard's neck and threw him into the sea; and by tradition the body of the young martyr, instead of sinking, floated on the water. A crowd soon gathered about him and understood by that wonder that they had gained a new saint in heaven. They took up the relics of the Viking martyr with great love and laid them reverently in a church in the city of Oslo, keeping as their model and intercessor the confessor of Christ who had given his life to fulfil the Saviour's word that no man has greater love than this, that he lay down his life for his neighbour.

Wonders began at his relics and people came from everywhere to venerate them. In 1130 a stone church was raised on the place where they rested, and Halvard became the protector of the city of Oslo. Only the ruins of that church survive today, the page ends, and the relics of the saint have disappeared.
