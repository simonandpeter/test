# Antoninus, companion of Zebinas

saint.gr's calendar for 20 Οκτωβρίου names four martyrs in one line and tells a single story for all of them. [Zebinas](/saints/zebinas-20-october), [Germanus](/saints/germanus-companion-of-zebinas), [Nicephorus](/saints/nicephorus-companion-of-zebinas) and Antoninus suffered, the page says, under Maximian, whose reign it dates 286 to 305. Antoninus was an old man; the other three were young. They were arrested at Caesarea because they were Christians, by a man the page names only as Mazos, and they were beheaded there.

That is the whole of what saint.gr prints of them. It gives no country beyond the city of their arrest, no trades, no families, no words of their own, and no year inside the reign it names. The couplet set over the entry plays on one of the four names: the sword, it says, shows Zebinas, Germanus and Nicephorus, together with Antoninus, to be bearers of victory. No hymn is printed for them.

The page closes by noting that their memory is kept again on 12 November, there together with the virgin Manatho, whose name it says is wrongly given as Maratho on that day. Both days are the Greek calendar's own; this folder records the day page read here, and the second day is kept as a note on the attestation.

The Russian calendar keeps this company too, and on a day of its own. days.pravoslavie.ru's line for 13 ноября ст. ст. reads «Мчч. Антонина , Никифора и Германа (ок. 308).» — three of saint.gr's four, with Zebinas not among them — and the name link behind his place in it, page 225, gives «Антонин (Антоний) Кесарийский (Палестинский), пресвитер, сщмч.» It names the province that saint.gr leaves at the bare city: Caesarea in Palestine. The Russian calendar prints no life for the three of them and no hymn under any of their names; the day page's troparion and kontakion both belong to the great hierarch who heads the day.

Two things on that name link are not in the Greek line, and both are now in this folder. It gives him a grade, «пресвитер», and a rank, «сщмч.» — a presbyter and a hieromartyr, where saint.gr calls all four simply «Άγιοι» and says of him only that he was an old man while the other three were young. And it dates the martyrdom «ок. 308», which falls outside the reign of Maximian saint.gr dates them by. Neither host is preferred: the death interval is widened to hold both readings and both sources' words stand in its note. The next line of the same Russian day, «Мц. Манефы (ок. 308).», is the virgin kept with the four on the Greek 12 November; the Russian gives her a line of her own, and nothing on either host says she was taken or killed with them, so no relation is written to her.

The Ohrid Prologue gives the four of them one entry and divides it as the deaths divided. The first three of them saw the heathen at one of their festivals bowing to idols with shouting and dancing, and came out fearlessly before the crowd and began to preach the one God in Trinity. The governor of Caesarea in Palestine, where this happened, was so exasperated by what the three Christians had done that he ordered their heads struck off at once.

[Manatho](/saints/manatho) was a Christian virgin, and she followed the martyrs as they were led to the place of execution; she too was seized, and after bitter examinations was burned in a fire. All of them suffered in 308, the entry says, which falls inside the interval this folder already holds for the four. The governor is named in the book and has no folder here.
