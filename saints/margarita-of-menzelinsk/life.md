# Margarita of Menzelinsk

The nun Margarita, abbess of the monastery of Saint John the Forerunner in the town of Menzelinsk, was of Greek descent. saint.gr says she was marked out by an uncommon education, by prudence, and by the strictness of her ascetic life, and that she ordered her monastery after the pattern of the old monasteries of Greece. The nuns under her kept a strict monastic rule and the services without fail, and the monastery had orchards, kitchen gardens, fields and beehives.

The account, the page says, was preserved by one of her nuns, Aleftina, who lived into our own days and was blind in her last years. In September 1918, when the armies of the Whites had left Kazan and the towns around it, the abbess feared the Bolsheviks and decided to leave for a time along with the other refugees. She reached the river harbour where the refugees were boarding the steamers; and there Saint Nicholas appeared to her and said: «Γιατί φεύγεις από το στεφάνι που σε περιμένει;» — why do you flee from the crown that awaits you?

She turned back at once. She returned to the monastery, called a priest, and, certain now that martyrdom awaited her, asked him to make ready her coffin and her grave, and to bury her on the same day if he could. The next day, during the Divine Liturgy, a band of revolutionaries came into the monastery church and seized her. She begged to be allowed to receive communion; they dragged her into the outer narthex and, without further explanation, executed her as a counter-revolutionary. The nuns took up her body, sang the funeral service and buried her behind the sanctuary.

The next day the priest understood why she had insisted on being buried that same day. The Bolsheviks had brought a Muslim hodja to the monastery and shot him there, and wanted to bury him in the same grave as the Orthodox nun; and they could not. The monastery was later closed and left desolate. In the nineteen-seventies, saint.gr says, workmen digging near the sanctuary found the incorrupt body of a nun in her habit, with the monastic schema and the cross on her breast, and in fear closed the grave again and dug elsewhere.

The page adds that in the nineteenth century [Ambrose of Optina](/saints/ambrose-of-optina), whom it keeps on 10 October, had foretold that a monastery would work in the town of Menzelinsk and win fame and glory; that under the first abbess a new church would be built; that the second abbess would become a martyr; and that when the third came the bells of the monastery would fall. The first abbess built the katholikon, the second was Margarita, and under her successor the monastery was closed by force, the nuns driven out, and the bells thrown down and broken.
