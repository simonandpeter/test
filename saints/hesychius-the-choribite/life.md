# Hesychius the Choribite

He began badly, which is the point of him. The Russian calendar's life says he lived in the sixth century in one of the monasteries and at first was not a very diligent monk. Then he fell gravely ill and died — and by God's wondrous providence came back to life an hour later. After that he shut himself in his cell and stayed there twelve years in complete solitude, and all the brethren ever heard out of the enclosure was the singing of psalms and weeping for his sins.

To the monks who came to him before his death he said one sentence: «Простите меня, братия. Имеющий память смертную никогда не согрешит» — forgive me, brethren; whoever keeps the memory of death will never sin. The page adds that it is with his name that the skete of the Silent Ones on Athos is connected, the hesychasts who gained their particular experience of the inward prayer of Jesus, so a monastery of silence traces itself to a man who had nothing to say but that.

**Two places, one man, and the source gives both.** His own epithet, Хоривит, is from Horeb, which is Sinai; the life as printed says he lived in a monastery «на Афоне», on Athos, and it is Athos again at the end for the skete. The calendar does not reconcile them and this folder does not either. The Russian index of names glosses him twice over, «безмолвник (исихаст)», and the Russian calendar is the only one of the four read here that keeps him at all.
