# Euthymius of Novgorod

Euthymius, John in the world, was the son of a priest of Novgorod named Theodore and of Anna. He had been conceived after years of barrenness, and his parents dedicated him to God before he was born. The young John grew up in piety, given to study, and at the age of fifteen entered the monastery of St Nicholas at Vyazhishchi, near Novgorod, where he received the monastic habit and was named Euthymius.

He lived afterwards for a time as an ascetic in the monastery of St Barlaam of Khutyn. Early on the archbishop of Novgorod, Symeon, admiring his spiritual gifts, made him his general steward, and in the year 1429 he was chosen archbishop of the city. For all that he went on living a strict monastic life of prayer and repentance, giving help with love and sympathy to the destitute, and showing an artist's care in the raising and the painting of churches and in the copying of sacred codices and books.

A little before his death he wrote a letter to Jonah, metropolitan of Moscow, calling on him for forgiveness for his great unbearable weakness in the world, and withdrew to the monastery of Vyazhishchi, where he fell asleep in peace in the year 1458. His glorification was made in 1547. The page closes with the iconographic manual's description of him: grey-haired, with a beard like that of St John the Theologian but thinner, with the skoufos and the episcopal vesture, the omophorion and the Gospel.
