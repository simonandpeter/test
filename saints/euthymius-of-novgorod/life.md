# Euthymius of Novgorod

Euthymius, John in the world, was the son of a priest of Novgorod named Theodore and of Anna. He had been conceived after years of barrenness, and his parents dedicated him to God before he was born. The young John grew up in piety, given to study, and at the age of fifteen entered the monastery of St Nicholas at Vyazhishchi, near Novgorod, where he received the monastic habit and was named Euthymius.

He lived afterwards for a time as an ascetic in the monastery of St Barlaam of Khutyn. Early on the archbishop of Novgorod, Symeon, admiring his spiritual gifts, made him his general steward, and in the year 1429 he was chosen archbishop of the city. For all that he went on living a strict monastic life of prayer and repentance, giving help with love and sympathy to the destitute, and showing an artist's care in the raising and the painting of churches and in the copying of sacred codices and books.

A little before his death he wrote a letter to Jonah, metropolitan of Moscow, calling on him for forgiveness for his great unbearable weakness in the world, and withdrew to the monastery of Vyazhishchi, where he fell asleep in peace in the year 1458. His glorification was made in 1547. The page closes with the iconographic manual's description of him: grey-haired, with a beard like that of St John the Theologian but thinner, with the skoufos and the episcopal vesture, the omophorion and the Gospel.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery is the first source read for this folder to tell his life rather than its end. He was born at Great Novgorod to a devout Christian family, it says; his parents were long childless and prayed hard for it, and when the child came they gave him the name John at his baptism and promised him before the icon of the Mother of God to the service of God. Set to his learning, the boy studied the divine Scriptures and gave himself to nothing that did not lead him to think on divine things, and at fifteen he wished to take the monastic habit, and took it at the Vyazhitsky house of Saint Nicholas the Wonderworker.

There he was obedient in everything, and, the page says, had the mind of a man of many years while still young, so that he became a guide to others in the monastic life before he was of age. Archbishop Simeon heard of him, called him and made him steward of the archbishop's house; after that archbishop's death he withdrew to the Khutyn monastery, was in time appointed abbot of the house of the Nativity of the Mother of God on Fox Hill, and was soon elected archbishop of Novgorod. Having taken that high rank he gave himself to the labours of it with still more zeal, caring now not for his own soul only but for the souls of the flock the Lord had entrusted to him; and he died in deep old age in 1458, the year this folder already held from the Greek. The day page prints a troparion and a kontakion for him.
