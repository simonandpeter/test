# Blandina of Lyons

saint.gr prints Blandina on one page with three companions and gives all four a single notice. These saints were martyred, it says, in the years of the king of the Romans Marcus Antoninus. Sanctus — or Sanctius, as the page allows — and Maturus were from Vienne, while Attalus and Blandina were from Pergamum. They were arrested because they were Christians, and were tortured horribly with various torments: with sharp knives, with red-hot irons, with wild beasts, with scourgings and with other things.

In the end they were hanged in the prison, and their bodies were thrown into the fire and after that into the river Rhone, having first been pierced with javelins. That is the whole of the Greek notice: it gives no year, no judge, no city of the trial and no hymn. Above it the page sets two distichs, one for the three men and one for her alone: «Nίκης αγώνος ιππικού τη Bλανδίνη, Δίδωσι λαμπρά παμβασιλεύς τα στέφη» — the King of all gives Blandina the bright crowns of the victory of the arena.

The corpus already keeps her three companions out of saint.gr's own 2 June page, where the same calendar lists nine martyrs of Lyons by name — [Sanctius](/saints/sanctius-of-lyons) the deacon, [Maturus](/saints/maturus-of-lyons) and [Attalus](/saints/attalus-of-lyons) among them — and their Greek feast stands on that day. Blandina is not in that list of nine, and this day in July is where the Greek calendar names her.
