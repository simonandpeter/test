# John of the Old Lavra

The Old Lavra was the house of [Chariton](/saints/chariton-the-confessor), near Jerusalem in the wilderness of Palestine, and it was called old because he built it before the other lavras, as his own life tells at length. In that old lavra of Chariton this John struggled as a hermit. From his youth he had been wounded with the love of God and clove to him, after what is written: but for me it is good to cleave to God.

He passed over the pleasures and the pride of the empty world, left his own country for the sake of the Lord, who was himself a stranger upon earth with nowhere to lay his head, and took up the cross of the monastic life in a foreign land. When he had come to the holy places of Jerusalem and venerated the precious Cross and the life-receiving Tomb of Christ, he went to the lavra spoken of above; and there, for the goodness of his life, he was accounted worthy of the priesthood.

He showed himself a follower of the hermits of old, restraining the passions of the body with great abstinence, with standings at prayer through whole nights, with the remembrance of death and with labours of many kinds, by which he so wore his flesh thin that he became, the page says, an earthly angel in the body. After a long life lived out to God's pleasure he departed to the Lord, his soul carried by the hands of the bodiless angels to the dwelling in heaven that does not grow old.

Some call him John of the Old Cave rather than of the Old Lavra, and the page explains why: the old lavra of Chariton had been at first a robbers' cave. Chariton, bound there by the robbers, was loosed by a miracle when they all died at once of wine poisoned by a serpent's venom; finding much gold in the cave, he made a church in the cave itself, gathered brethren, and afterwards built the monastery above it. Because John lived in that old cave and served in its church, the second name was given him as well. No year is given.

The Greek calendar keeps him a day later than the Romanian, on 20 April, and saint.gr's notice begins where the Romanian one does not, with his childhood. Within his own family, it says, John kept all the virtues that Holy Scripture enjoins upon children: he honoured his father and his mother, obeyed their wishes, showed them the tenderest affection, was ready to anticipate what they wanted, and was a pattern of love and of mutual help among his brothers.

Later he went about many places, trying to school himself more spiritually, and was enrolled in the monastic life; and there too he kept the obligations of his habit exactly. Then a holy longing brought him to Jerusalem, where he venerated the Holy Places. At the last the monastery of the venerable Chariton received him, and that house saw his great spiritual struggles. He departed this life to the Lord full of faith and hope, having used his life for the glory of the Church and the love of his brethren. The page adds that a portion of his relic is at the monastery of the Panagia Tourliani on Mykonos.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day as the Romanian calendar and gives him the one century any source read for him prints. He struggled, it says, in the so-called Old Cave, or Lavra of [Chariton](/saints/chariton-the-confessor) the Great, in Palestine. Having come to love Christ the Lord with all his heart and all his soul and all his mind, he began from his youth to travel about the holy places and to listen to the teaching and the counsel of holy men; and at the last he settled in Chariton's cave, where he gave himself to a hard struggle, passing days and years in fasting, in prayer and in vigil, thinking unceasingly upon death and learning humility. Like well-ripened fruit he was gathered by death and carried over into Paradise. He lived and reposed in the eighth century — the only date this folder holds, and it holds it from this entry alone.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day as the Romanian and the Prologue, and is the first source read for this folder to explain the name and to place the house. He is called Ветхопещерник, the page says, because he struggled in the eighth century in the Lavra of [Chariton](/saints/chariton-the-confessor), which was called the Old — that is, the ancient — as the eldest of the Palestinian houses; and that Lavra stood not far from Bethlehem, near the Dead Sea. It gives Chariton’s repose as about 450 and his memory as 28 сентября.

The rest is four sentences. In his young years he left the world, went to venerate the holy places of Jerusalem, and settled in that Lavra, where he came to a high spiritual perfection. He was ordained to the rank of presbyter and became renowned for the ascetic life. No year of his own appears anywhere on the page, only the century the line prints as «(VIII)», which is the interval this folder already held off the Prologue; and the page gives him no country, no family and no hymn.
