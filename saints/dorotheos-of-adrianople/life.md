# Dorotheos Proios

saint.gr says that Saint Dorotheos was a national martyr and one of the most considerable learned hierarchs of the nineteenth century, metropolitan of Philadelphia from 1805 to 1813 and of Adrianople from 1813 to 1821. He was born on Chios, where he learned his first letters, and then studied at the school of Patmos under Daniel Kerameus; he was bound in friendship to Benjamin of Lesbos, as their correspondence shows, and the friendship was never disturbed, because they had the same interests as friends of education and of the nation.

In 1786 he was ordained deacon and left for wider studies in Italy and France. In September 1793 he was appointed a teacher in the school of Chios, where he taught philosophy and the sciences under Athanasios Parios as director; in 1796 he gave the post up to teach the children of Konstantinos Hantzeris, dragoman of the Turkish fleet, and settled at Constantinople. When Hantzeris was made prince of Wallachia, Dorotheos followed him, taking his own pupil Neophytos Vamvas as his assistant; and after Hantzeris was beheaded by the Turks he had to return to Constantinople, where the Patriarchate named him preacher of the Great Church and advanced him to archimandrite.

When Demetrios Mourouzis founded the School of the Nation at Kuruçeşme in 1804 its direction was given to Dorotheos, who kept it until 1807, having been elected metropolitan of Philadelphia in the meantime. He taught physics, mathematics, geometry, philosophy and logic, and did not confine himself to his administrative and teaching duties: he set his hand to the great dictionary of the Greek language called «Κιβωτός», the Ark, supported morally and financially by the Zosimades brothers, with Vamvas and Nikolaos Logadis as his collaborators, and its first volume appeared in 1819. Moved to Adrianople in 1813, he tried without success to found a clerical school there, but did organise a school and a library with the help of a rich Adrianopolitan merchant, and taught theology, philosophy and the sciences himself.

In 1820 he was called to Constantinople as a synodal bishop, and a year later, in April 1821, he was seized by the Turks together with the Ecumenical Patriarch Gregory V and other bishops and eminent laymen, and hanged at Mega Revma. He belongs, the page says, among the great teachers of the nation in the declining years of the Turkish rule, and he worked for a Greek-centred enlightenment within the frame of its own tradition. He published no scientific studies, confining himself to compiling aids for his pupils; but the many copies of them surviving in libraries confirm how widely his worth as a teacher was recognised. What survives in manuscript includes arithmetic, geometry, algebra and the Logic of Genovesi, and he saw the first edition of Nikodemos the Hagiorite's Pedalion through the press in 1800.
