# Mary, sister of Lazarus

doxologia.ro's calendar names her and gives no life. Her page carries no Viață tab and no Tropar tab, only three sentences of its own: after the Lord's Ascension, it says, Lazarus set out to preach the Gospel, and his two sisters went with him, being always in his company; and where the two sisters of Lazarus passed into eternity is not known. That is the whole of what the source prints about her.

The day's line calls her and her sister myrrh-bearers — «Sfintele Mironosițe Maria și Marta, surorile Dreptului Lazăr» — and identifies them by their brother, the Righteous Lazarus, for whom this corpus keeps no folder. No year, no place of death and no hymn is given here. Her sister is [Martha](/saints/martha-sister-of-lazarus), kept on the same day.

The Greek calendar keeps the two sisters on the same day, 4 June, and saint.gr's account is the Gospel's. Martha and Mary, with their brother Lazarus, were for the Lord Jesus Christ the dearest and holiest household of Bethany. The Gospel shows Mary absorbed in the teaching of Jesus while Martha, who was the elder sister, takes much trouble over the table of hospitality — and hears for it from the Teacher's own mouth the best known and most instructive of words, that Martha is anxious and troubled about many things while Mary has chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken away from her. Mary also anointed the feet of Jesus with precious myrrh and wiped them afterwards with her virgin hair.

Both sisters, the page says, devout and distinguished members of the first Christian Church, were counted worthy to die peacefully and not under persecution: for the Lord did not wish to leave in pain the hearts under whose roof he had enjoyed so much holy quiet in the days before his passion. The page's couplet says that the sisters of Lazarus, out of Bethany, are to be believed able to save even the dead; and its kathisma, which this folder does not carry because the schema holds no such text, calls them both deacons of Christ, full of faith and honourable vessels.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the two sisters together on the Julian 4 June, the menologion day both the other calendars keep them on, and its entry is four sentences long. They are the sisters of Lazarus, it says; after the Lord’s ascension Lazarus went out to preach the Gospel; in this his sisters helped him; and it is not known where they ended their lives.

That last sentence is the reason this folder records no date and no place, and a third book saying it outright is worth more here than a new scene would be. The Romanian page says the same in its own three sentences, and the Greek page goes no further than that the two were counted worthy to end peacefully rather than under persecution. Of Martha her sister this book says exactly what it says of her, giving the two one entry, one sentence of help to their brother and one confession of ignorance between them.
