# Timothy (Kucherov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him last of the twenty-four and gives no life; the life here is the one azbyka.ru prints for the civil 2 декабря, out of fond.ru and the Moscow diocese’s lives. He was born in 1871 in the village of Stepanovo in the Mikhailov district of the province of Ryazan, in the family of a peasant, Ivan Kucherov. He was called up in 1893 and served until 1896, and when he came back from the army he went into the police, and in time acted as senior constable of the First Khamovniki precinct of Moscow, with more than seventy constables of the precinct under him; he was given two silver medals for good service.

In 1917 he was called into the army again, came back in 1918 and worked as a labourer. In the hard years for the Russian Church he grew settled in the wish to serve it, and in 1930 he was elected chairman of the parish council of one of the oldest and most beautiful churches of Moscow, Saint Nicholas in Khamovniki, whose parishioner he was; what gift he had for organising and leading he put, as well as he could, into the keeping up of the life of that church. Meaning to arrest him, the NKVD questioned witnesses who signed the evidence the investigation needed, in which he was shown up as a former policeman and an active churchman.

The chairman of the house committee of the building he lived in drew up a note for the NKVD which, after the facts of his life that the man knew, said that through the open windows looking on to Pugovishnikov lane the gramophone record «Боже, царя храни» and other divine anti-Soviet records had been heard more than once. He was arrested on 31 October 1937 and put in the Taganka prison, and questioned on 18 November.

He was asked what part he had taken in the execution of revolutionaries on Khamovniki square. Revolutionaries were hanged there about 1906 and 1907, he answered, and he took no part in it himself; told that the investigation had evidence that no execution went by without him, he said again that it was all done by a special detail and that he did not know who was in it. Asked about being an active churchman he said that from 1930 to 1935 he was, and that as chairman the whole of the organising work was his and two other members’, but that he had recruited nobody and those came who wished to. He denied every anti-Soviet utterance. On 29 November 1937 the troika sentenced him to be shot, and the warden of the church of Saint Nicholas in Khamovniki was shot on 2 December 1937 at the Butovo range near Moscow and buried in a common unmarked grave.
