# Methodius of Moravia

Methodius was born at Thessalonica, the elder son of Leo — a man of good family and wealth, a soldier by office and a centurion in rank — and of his wife Maria. His younger brother was Constantine, who took the name [Cyril](/saints/cyril-the-philosopher) in the holy schema at the end of his life. The two were born in the days of the emperors who fought the holy icons: Leo the Armenian, Michael the Stammerer who was also called Balbus, and then Theophilus, Michael's son.

Methodius, being the elder, served in the ranks of the army, and being known to the emperor was made a voivode and sent to the parts that bordered on the Slavs. The life reads this as God's own ordering, so that he should learn the Slavonic tongue against the day when he would be their teacher and shepherd. After about ten years in that office — seeing the emptiness of the world's quarrels, and grieved at the persecution Theophilus had raised against the Orthodox — he left the office and everything fair in this world, went to Mount Olympus, and was tonsured a monk.

When his brother came back from his embassy to the Saracens he joined Methodius on Olympus. Then envoys of the Khazars asked Constantinople for a man learned enough to dispute with Jews and Saracens, and Constantine begged Methodius — who knew part of the Slavonic tongue — to come with him on that apostolic errand. They stayed long at Cherson and learned the Khazar and the Hebrew speech; there they urged the bishop to seek out the relics of Clement of Rome, which were found in the sea at midnight by a light, and they carried a portion of them ever after. Before the Kagan, Constantine disputed and Methodius helped him with prayer; Methodius afterwards set down those disputations in eight discourses.

Rostislav and Svyatopolk of Moravia then sent to the emperor Michael for a teacher who could show them the faith in their own tongue. Constantine fasted forty days and found the Slavonic alphabet of thirty-eight letters, Methodius helping him at the work; together they translated the Gospel, the Apostle, the Liturgy and the rest, and taught in the Slavonic lands for more than four years. When bishops and priests of the Latin tongue objected that the Liturgy might be served only in the three tongues of the title on the Cross, the two answered that God sends rain and sun upon all, and that since the Lord came to save all nations, every nation should glorify Him in the speech of its own tongue.

Pope Nicholas of Old Rome called them to him with love, and they went; he died before they arrived, and his successor Adrian received them gladly, blessed their labours, approved the Slavonic books, and laid an anathema on any who should slander the Slavonic reading, singing and Liturgy. They served the Liturgy in Slavonic in the middle of Rome. There Constantine fell ill, took the schema with the name Cyril, entrusted his see to his elder brother and died, and was buried in the church of St Clement.

Methodius was then set as bishop of Moravia. He had his seat in the city of Pannonia, in the place of the Apostle Andronicus, the disciple and kinsman of Paul who had once been bishop there. He widened the holy faith, disputed with Jews and with heretics and overcame them by words and by miracles, bore dangers and banishments, and translated many books from Greek into Slavonic; and having guided his flock well for many years he passed to the Lord, whom he had served apostolically together with his brother.

The Greek calendar keeps him with his brother on the same day and gives him a career of his own before the mission. The holy [Cyril](/saints/cyril-the-philosopher) and Methodius, in the world Constantine and Michael, were sons of the droungarios and military commander Leo, saint.gr says, and were born at Thessalonica: Michael the elder in the year 815, Constantine about 827, and there were five more brothers and sisters. Michael followed their father's career. He became a soldier and took over the command of the region of the sources of the Strymon, on what are now the borders of Bulgaria and Serbia, and it was there that he came to know the Slavs well. For all the success of both brothers, the page says, the zeal for the spiritual life shook them deeply: they had a monastic inclination, but they believed in a martyric ministry of that calling. [Angelarius](/saints/angelarius-of-bohemia), whom the same calendar keeps on this same day, it calls their fellow-worker and helper.

The reader who wrote this paragraph did not read the middle of that page, and the mission itself is not retold here. What was read at its end is the page's own summing up of how much travelling and trouble his life held, which it offers to show that a comparison with the labours of the Apostle is not excessive: the journeys to the Crimea, to Khazaria and to the land of the Phoulloi; the going over to Moravia; the journey to Rome by way of Pannonia and Venice; the return to Pannonia and Moravia; his arrest by the Franks, his trial and condemnation, two and a half years of imprisonment, and his release; the slanders raised against him; and his going to Rome and then to Constantinople.

The Ohrid Prologue, read for the Julian 11 May, keeps the two brothers under one heading and begins the whole mission with him. They were brothers born of one womb, of Thessalonica, of notable and rich parents named Leo and Maria. Methodius, the elder, spent ten years as an officer among the Macedonian Slavs, and learned the Slavonic tongue that way; then he withdrew to Mount Olympus and gave himself to the monastic struggle, and there [Cyril](/saints/cyril-the-philosopher) joined him later. When the Khazar king Kagan asked the emperor Michael for preachers of the faith, the brothers were sought out at the emperor's command and sent among the Khazars, and they baptised the Kagan with a great number of his chief men and a greater number of the people.

Returning to Constantinople, they put together the Slavonic alphabet of thirty-eight letters and began translating the church books out of Greek. At prince Rastislav's invitation they went to Moravia, spread and strengthened the faith, multiplied the books and gave them to the priests to teach the young with; and at the pope's invitation they went to Rome, where Cyril fell ill and died. Methodius then returned to Moravia and laboured until his death at establishing the faith of Christ among the Slavs. He reposed in the Lord, this book says, on the sixth of April 885 — a year later than the 884 the Greek page's head line gives, so his death now stands as the two of them, and the day is new.

And the book carries the work past his death. His disciples, the Five, with holy [Clement](/saints/clement-of-ohrid) as bishop at their head, crossed the Danube and went down southward into Macedonia, where, out of Ohrid, they continued among the Slavs the work the two brothers had begun in the north.

**The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 6 April, which is not the day the other three books keep him on.** doxologia, saint.gr and the Ohrid Prologue all keep him on 11 May with his brother; this calendar prints «Свт. Мефодия , архиеп. Моравского (885).» on 6 апреля ст. ст., which is the day of his falling asleep, and its troparion for the day says so — «творящим светло торжество успения», to those who keep the festival of the repose brightly. One row per church is the schema, so the russian row is this day, and 11 мая, which the same calendar also keeps, is recorded and not rendered. The name link gives him as the calendar’s own index titles him, «Равноапостольный Мефодий, учитель Словенский» — teacher of the Slavs, where the day’s own line calls him «архиеп. Моравского».

**The year agrees and the day of the month agrees with it**: the line closes «(885)», and this folder holds «884 or 885» because two books could not be made to agree, so the 885 falls inside the range and `dates` are untouched. The life behind the index — and the index is an index, with two readings on it, both followed — gives the date in full: «Святитель предсказал день своей смерти и скончался 6 апреля 885 года в возрасте около 60 лет». He foretold the day of his own death. **The age of about sixty is not turned into a year of birth**, an age being not a year, and it would not square with the 815 this folder holds in any case.

What the two Russian readings add to the three books before them is mostly his own half of the story, which the joint Greek entry gave to his brother. They were of a devout family of Solun in Macedonia, sons of one voivode, a Bulgarian Slav by descent, and he was the eldest of seven brothers where [Cyril the Philosopher](/saints/cyril-the-philosopher) was the youngest. He served first in the army, as his father had; the emperor, hearing of him as a good soldier, made him governor of a Slavonic principality called Slavinia that lay under the Greek power — which happened, the page says, by a particular providence, so that he might learn the Slavonic tongue the better, being the future spiritual teacher and shepherd of the Slavs. After about ten years in that office he knew the vanity of the world and went away to be a monk on mount Olympus.

The second reading adds the abbacy: that when the brothers came back from the Khazars with the relics they had found, his brother stayed in the capital and he was given the abbacy of a small monastery called Polychron, not far from the Olympus where he had struggled before. Then the embassy of the Moravian prince, the alphabet and the translations of 863 made with his brother and five disciples, Moravia, the German bishops’ complaint, the summons to Rome in 867 and the relics carried there, the pope’s confirmation of the Slavonic liturgy; and his brother’s death in Rome on 14 February 869 at forty-two, and the words he said to him before it: that the two of them had drawn one furrow like a well-matched pair of oxen, and that now he was spent the other must not think of leaving the work of teaching and going back to his mountain.

Then his own episcopate, which is the part this calendar keeps him for. At the petition of the Slavonic prince «Коцела» the pope sent him into Pannonia, having ordained him archbishop of Moravia and Pannonia on the ancient throne the page calls that of the apostle «Антродина». He had much unpleasantness to bear from missionaries not of his confession, but went on preaching among the Slavs, and baptised the Czech prince «Боривоя» and his wife «Людмилу», whose day the page gives as 16 сентября, and one of the Polish princes besides. In his last years he translated, with two priest-disciples, the whole of the Old Testament except the books of the Maccabees, and the Nomocanon and the books of the fathers.

His funeral was sung in three languages — Slavonic, Greek and Latin — and he was buried in the cathedral church of Velehrad, the capital of Moravia. The page closes with what the Russian church did with him afterwards: that his memory and his brother’s have been kept in Russia since the eleventh century, that the oldest surviving services to them are of the thirteenth, that the solemn celebration was established in 1863, and that a decree of the Synod of 1885 put the two among the middle church feasts and ordered that their names be commemorated, in every prayer where the ecumenical hierarchs of the Russian Church are commemorated, immediately after the name of «святителя Николая архиепископа Мирликийского чудотворца», as the decree words it.

Its iconographic note for 11 May describes him as old, grey-haired, with a long beard, in hierarch’s vestments and the omophorion, with the Gospel in his hands; and beside him his brother in the habit and the schema, with a book in his hands in which the Russian alphabet is written out, A, B, V, G, D and all the rest of the letters in their order.
