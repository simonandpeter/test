# Methodius of Moravia

Methodius was born at Thessalonica, the elder son of Leo — a man of good family and wealth, a soldier by office and a centurion in rank — and of his wife Maria. His younger brother was Constantine, who took the name [Cyril](/saints/cyril-the-philosopher) in the holy schema at the end of his life. The two were born in the days of the emperors who fought the holy icons: Leo the Armenian, Michael the Stammerer who was also called Balbus, and then Theophilus, Michael's son.

Methodius, being the elder, served in the ranks of the army, and being known to the emperor was made a voivode and sent to the parts that bordered on the Slavs. The life reads this as God's own ordering, so that he should learn the Slavonic tongue against the day when he would be their teacher and shepherd. After about ten years in that office — seeing the emptiness of the world's quarrels, and grieved at the persecution Theophilus had raised against the Orthodox — he left the office and everything fair in this world, went to Mount Olympus, and was tonsured a monk.

When his brother came back from his embassy to the Saracens he joined Methodius on Olympus. Then envoys of the Khazars asked Constantinople for a man learned enough to dispute with Jews and Saracens, and Constantine begged Methodius — who knew part of the Slavonic tongue — to come with him on that apostolic errand. They stayed long at Cherson and learned the Khazar and the Hebrew speech; there they urged the bishop to seek out the relics of Clement of Rome, which were found in the sea at midnight by a light, and they carried a portion of them ever after. Before the Kagan, Constantine disputed and Methodius helped him with prayer; Methodius afterwards set down those disputations in eight discourses.

Rostislav and Svyatopolk of Moravia then sent to the emperor Michael for a teacher who could show them the faith in their own tongue. Constantine fasted forty days and found the Slavonic alphabet of thirty-eight letters, Methodius helping him at the work; together they translated the Gospel, the Apostle, the Liturgy and the rest, and taught in the Slavonic lands for more than four years. When bishops and priests of the Latin tongue objected that the Liturgy might be served only in the three tongues of the title on the Cross, the two answered that God sends rain and sun upon all, and that since the Lord came to save all nations, every nation should glorify Him in the speech of its own tongue.

Pope Nicholas of Old Rome called them to him with love, and they went; he died before they arrived, and his successor Adrian received them gladly, blessed their labours, approved the Slavonic books, and laid an anathema on any who should slander the Slavonic reading, singing and Liturgy. They served the Liturgy in Slavonic in the middle of Rome. There Constantine fell ill, took the schema with the name Cyril, entrusted his see to his elder brother and died, and was buried in the church of St Clement.

Methodius was then set as bishop of Moravia. He had his seat in the city of Pannonia, in the place of the Apostle Andronicus, the disciple and kinsman of Paul who had once been bishop there. He widened the holy faith, disputed with Jews and with heretics and overcame them by words and by miracles, bore dangers and banishments, and translated many books from Greek into Slavonic; and having guided his flock well for many years he passed to the Lord, whom he had served apostolically together with his brother.
