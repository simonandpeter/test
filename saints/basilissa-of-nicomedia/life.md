# Basilissa of Nicomedia

Basilissa suffered for the faith of Christ under Diocletian. Alexander, governor of Nicomedia, had the nine-year-old girl seized and pressed her to deny Christ, and the child showed an unshakeable firmness in loyalty to her Lord, for which she was put to long and ingenious tortures — denounced, the Greek synaxarion says, for drawing young pagan women away from the worship of the many gods, scourged cruelly, thrown into the fire and coming out untouched, then thrown to two hungry lions. By the grace of God she stayed alive and unharmed, and this open showing of God’s power so shook the governor that he too believed in Christ and confessed himself a Christian; baptised by the bishop Antoninus, he lived a short time in true repentance and died in peace, and after him Basilissa — she went out into a field, the Prologue says, knelt to pray and give thanks for the torments she had borne, and so gave her spirit to God, about 309.
