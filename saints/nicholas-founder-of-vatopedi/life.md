# Nicholas, founder of Vatopedi

doxologia.ro prints one notice for the three founders of Vatopedi, taken from Moses the Athonite's *The Saints of the Holy Mountain*, and this is read out of it. By holy tradition, it says, the founders of the monastery of Vatopedi are held to be the noble officials of Adrianople, Athanasius, Nicholas and Antony, who in the middle of the tenth century, while the Great Lavra was being built, came to its founder [Athanasius the Athonite](/saints/athanasius-the-athonite), who also drew up the typikon of the new monastery. Listening to the great man's urging, they refounded and rebuilt a monastery that had been laid waste by pirates. The three are presented as rich men, as brothers, as monks and as founders.

They had come to the Holy Mountain meaning to build a monastery, drawn there like many others by the fame of Athanasius, and proposed to stay with him, offering him the nine thousand gold pieces they had brought for the purpose. Athanasius told them: this monastery is dedicated to its founder, the emperor Nicephorus; but if you want to build a monastery, see, the monastery of Vatopedi is a ruin — renew it, and you will have your reward from God.

In the year 985 Nicholas signs as abbot of the monastery of Vatopedi: Nicholas, monk and abbot of the monastery of Vatopedi. The same man signs the documents of 998, of 1001 and of 1012. His successor [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-founder-of-vatopedi), who is described as very active, signs documents between 1020 and 1048, and later [Antony](/saints/antony-founder-of-vatopedi). Their fame drew many monks to them, and the monastery very soon gained wealth and glory.

The notice says that research, study and comparison lead to the conclusion that the first superior of the monastery was the founder Nicholas the monk, who was the first to be buried in the tomb in the naos, after 1012 — the year in which he signs for the last time. Of the founders' tomb, a Russian monk, Basil Barsky, tells in 1744 that although it is never opened their lamp burns without ceasing. Above the tomb, in an apse, the three are shown beside the founding emperors in monastic habit and with shining crowns, in a fresco painted in 1760; the tomb is on the right, northern side of the monastery's naos, and in 1992 it was opened and the bones of the three were found, with a lead tablet in which Athanasius orders that nobody else be buried in his tomb after the old custom.

The last remaking of the tomb into a reliquary, and the painting of the three just above the wall which in 1312 bore the Mother of God of Blachernae, shows that the three founders were honoured as venerable very early. Their sung service was composed by X. M. Bousias, and their memory, the notice ends, is kept on 17 December.
