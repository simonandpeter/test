# Melitina of Marcianopolis

Melitina lived about the year 160, when Antoninus called Pius was emperor. The governor of Thrace then, Antiochus, a harsh enemy of the Christians, ordered the arrest of Melitina of Marcianopolis too, denounced for working actively to spread the Gospel; since threats could not frighten her, the governor’s wife herself undertook to change her faith, received her into her house and used skilfully all the seductive and wily means of women — but she who undertook to conquer was conquered, a defeat of the kind that is at the same time a glorious victory: she wanted to make Melitina a pagan, and ended by becoming a Christian herself. Then the two women together, hiding the fact from Antiochus, worked to bring many pagans to the Christian faith, and Antiochus, furious when he learned the truth, beheaded Melitina, who walked with courage to death and at the same time to eternal delight and glory — her head cut off by the sword, the verse says, she offered her blood to Christ like sweet honey. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 16 September.
