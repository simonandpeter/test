# John Colobos

John is counted among the greatest ascetics of the Egyptian desert. *Kolovos* means *dwarf*, and Abba John was called so because he was very small of stature. He came to Scetis together with his own brother Daniel, and gave himself to such great labours, with such zeal, that Daniel had to urge him towards more measure in them.

He was the disciple of [Saint Pambo](/saints/pambo-of-egypt), and later the teacher of [Arsenius the Great](/saints/arsenius-the-great). A fellow disciple under Abba Pambo was [Paisius the Great](/saints/paisius-the-great); and once, when John and Paisius were sitting in counsel together, asking each other how they might better their labours, an angel of the Lord came before them and commanded John to stay where he was and be a teacher to the brethren and to those who should come, and commanded Paisius to go out into the desert and live as a hermit.

This is the Abba John whom his teacher Pambo ordered to water a dry stick planted in the garden, to try his obedience, telling him to water it until it should turn green. He watered it with zeal and with love for three whole years, until by the power of God the stick turned green, budded and bore fruit; and Pambo gathered that fruit, brought it into the church and shared it out to the brethren to taste, saying: come and taste the fruit of obedience.

Abba John had a great many disciples, and some of his sayings for the building up of the soul have been kept to our own day. He ended his course to the Lord in peace. The notice doxologia.ro prints is taken from the Prologue of Ohrid and gives no year and no century, and his page carries no Tropar tab, so the folder has no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 9 November, and says where he came from and what he was known for. saint.gr has him of Thebes in Egypt, called Kolobos because he was short of body, one of the great ascetics of the desert, distinguished above all for the restraint of his tongue and for his humility; in the synaxarists, it says, a good many of his wise counsels are found, and it then quotes Nikodemos of the Holy Mountain at length. There is the extreme and undiscriminating obedience he showed his elder, a Theban, who planted a dry stick and told him to water it daily with a jug of water until it should sprout and bear fruit — the water so far off that a man had to go in the evening and return in the morning — which he did for three whole years, until the dry wood sprouted and bore walnuts, and the elder took the fruit to the church of the Skete and said: take and eat, brothers and fathers, the fruit of obedience. There too are his sayings: that humility and the fear of God are above all the virtues; that humility was what sold the all-fair Joseph, since he could have said when he was being sold that he was the brother of those selling him, and because he kept silent and humbled himself he was sold, and so humility made him governor and ruler in Egypt; and that letting go the light burden, which is to blame ourselves, we carry the heavy one, which is to justify ourselves. A father said of him that abba John hung the whole Skete on his little finger by his humility. He died in peace.
