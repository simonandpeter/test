# Lucian of Belgium

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps five men together on 3 июня and gives them one reading, headed for the first of them. It has Лукиан living at Rome under the pagan name Лукий, enlightened and baptised by the apostle Петр, and preaching in Italy after the apostle's death. Then Дионисий Ареопагит, a disciple of the apostle Павел, came to Rome and agreed, at the asking of Климент the pope, to go and preach in the western countries, and began to choose companions; Климент consecrated Лукиан bishop and sent him with the four others in Дионисий's company.

From Italy, the account says, they sailed to Gaul and there divided: one went on into Spain, one stayed in Gaul, Дионисий went with the rest towards Paris, and the bishop with his presbyter and his deacon went into Belgium. The preaching in Belgium was successful enough, it says, that almost the whole population turned to Christ; the bishop ate a piece of bread and a little water in a day and was mild, always glad and bright of face. Then Домициан, emperor from 81 to 96, raised the second persecution after Нерон's and ordered that whoever refused to sacrifice be tortured and put to death, and three officials were sent into Belgium to carry the order out.

The three in Belgium were taken on a mountain top, where the bishop had gathered the people to teach them after telling them not to fear. The presbyter and the deacon were told to deny Christ and sacrifice, refused firmly, and were beheaded. The bishop was then charged with sorcery and with disobedience to the emperor and the senate, answered that he was no sorcerer but the servant of the true God, and was scourged, saying only «Никогда не перестану сердцем, верою и устами хвалить Христа, Сына Божия», and beheaded. The account closes with a heavenly light over his body and a voice calling him, with the beheaded man rising, taking up his own head, crossing the river and lying down at the place he had chosen for his burial; about five hundred pagans are said to have turned to Christ at the sight, and a church was later raised over his grave, into which the remains of the presbyter and the deacon were brought.

He is the Лукиан of that account: the bishop of the five, whom the calendar heads «Святитель Лукиан Бельгийский, епископ, сщмч.» on name page 1382 and whose see the line gives as еп., a bishop, in Belgium. His presbyter was [Maxian of Belgium](/saints/maxian-of-belgium) and his deacon [Julian of Belgium](/saints/julian-of-belgium), and the two who turned aside in Gaul and Spain were [Saturninus of Gaul](/saints/saturninus-of-gaul) and [Marcellinus of Spain](/saints/marcellinus-of-spain). The calendar gives no place in Belgium a name, not even the river he is said to have crossed, and this folder records none.
