# Hyacinth of Vicina

Vicina was an important town in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, in the northern parts of Dobrogea, most probably on an island opposite the present Isaccea, the old Noviodunum. Its metropolitan see was founded before the thirteenth century and stood under Byzantine jurisdiction. The middle of the fourteenth century, when Hyacinth became metropolitan, was the time of the empire's crisis: weakened by its own wars, Byzantium could no longer protect Vicina, which the Tatars had taken and plundered about 1337–1338 and which fell under Genoese occupation from 1352.

In that state of things, doxologia's life says, it is possible that Metropolitan Hyacinth withdrew to the princely court at Argeș, under Basarab I and Nicolae Alexandru. There is no certainty about the date he had to leave the see of Vicina; what is certain is that in 1359 he was in Wallachia, at the court of Nicolae Alexandru Basarab, as metropolitan of Ungro-Wallachia.

At the Romanian prince's insistence, and after a correspondence between his chancery and the Ecumenical Patriarchate, a patriarchal document of May 1359 approved the hierarch's move from Vicina to Curtea de Argeș. The synod of Constantinople, under Patriarch Callistus and in the reign of John V Palaiologos, named Hyacinth lawful pastor of all Ungro-Wallachia with the rank of most honoured metropolitan, for the blessing and the spiritual setting-right of the prince, his children and his whole rule.

He was metropolitan under Vladislav I as well, the third Romanian voivode in whose reign he shepherded. In 1370 he wrote to the Ecumenical Patriarch Philotheus to explain that he was absent from the sessions of the patriarchal synod not out of ill will but because of his age; his years and his poor health had made Vladislav I stop him from so long a journey. In the same letter, knowing his end near, he asked the patriarch that Daniel Critopoulos, the patriarchal jurist sent into Wallachia to look into church affairs, should be consecrated and blessed as bishop of all Ungro-Wallachia.

In the first half of 1372 Metropolitan Hyacinth died and was buried in the metropolitan complex at Curtea de Argeș. The Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church, meeting on 8 July 2008, resolved on the canonisation of the first metropolitan of Wallachia, and the solemn proclamation took place on 26 October 2008.
