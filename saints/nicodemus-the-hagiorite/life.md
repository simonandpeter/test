# Nicodemus the Hagiorite

Nicodemus was born on Naxos in 1749 to pious and virtuous parents, Antony
and Anastasia Kallivoursi — his mother afterwards a nun of the Chrysostom
monastery of Naxos with the name Agatha; his name in the world was Nicholas,
and from childhood he showed a great virtue and a formidable intelligence.
He learned his first letters on Naxos and went on at the school of the same
town under the archimandrite Chrysanthos, the brother of Kosmas of Aetolia,
and at sixteen to the Greek school at Smyrna, where under famous teachers he
took a higher education and virtue together. After certain adventures he
came in 1775 to the Holy Mountain and was tonsured at the monastery of Saint
Dionysius with the name Nicodemus; the fathers, seeing his great natural and
acquired gifts, made him reader and secretary of the house, where he was a
pattern of service and of works of virtue, and then he withdrew to a cell
and gave himself, ascetically, to the study and writing of many edifying,
theological and hagiological books — the Synaxaristes, the Eortodromion, the
New Ladder, the Unseen Warfare and many more. After various adventures in a
short life he died of a stroke at sixty, in the early hours of 14 July 1809,
in the cell of the Skourtaioi at Karyes; his last words were his answer to
the disciples who asked whether he was at rest — I have put Christ within
me, and how should I not be at rest? He was buried at the Lavriote cell of
the Skourtaioi at Karyes, leaving an immense spiritual and written work that
is a capital of the people of the Orthodox Church, which entered him in its
calendar, worthily, in 1955. The Greek calendar keeps him on 14 July, and
this entry stands under 20 September, where the synaxarion’s day list
carried him when the corpus was first read.
