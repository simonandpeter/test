# Arcadius, teacher of Julian and Euboulos

saint.gr gives the venerable Arcadius three sentences. He was an ascetic in Cyprus in the fourth century, and the page describes him with a phrase out of its own distich, «ὀσφὺν νοητὴν ἀρεταῖς ἐζωσμένος» — his spiritual loins girded about with the virtues. He made many struggles in the years of the reign of Constantine the Great, and did good to many, not only by his life and his virtue but by his teaching and his counsel. And he fell asleep in peace.

What his teaching came to is on another page of the same day. saint.gr's notice for [Julian](/saints/julian-companion-of-euboulos) and [Euboulos](/saints/euboulos-the-physician) calls them disciples of this Arcadius, who came to him and, leaning on his words, took care for their virtue and their growth towards God; they were seized under Julian the Apostate and beheaded, and when Arcadius learned what had happened he grieved, but gave thanks to God who had arranged it so.

The page gives him no city in Cyprus, no monastery or cave, no elder, no year of birth or of repose, no relic and no hymn. Two other men of this name stand close to him and are not him: the same Greek day keeps an [Arcadius](/saints/arcadius-archbishop-of-cyprus), archbishop of Cyprus, on a page of its own, and the corpus keeps [Arcadius the Wonderworker](/saints/arcadius-of-arsinoe), bishop of Arsinoe in Cyprus, whom saint.gr keeps on the twenty-ninth of August. Nothing on any of the three pages connects them, and nothing is decided here.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 6 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/06/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3849/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
