# Pausikakos of Synada

Pausikakos came from Apamea in Bithynia and lived in the years of the emperor Maurice, who reigned from 582 to 602. His parents were devout and notable in their family. He was on God's road from childhood, saint.gr says, by his sincere faith and his blameless life, and he studied not theology only but medicine, so as to practise it free of charge for the poor.

When Kyriakos was patriarch of Constantinople — from 595 to 606 — he valued Pausikakos's great virtues, persuaded him to be enrolled in the holy clergy, and then made him bishop of Synada. From that place Pausikakos worked for the better strengthening of the faithful and for the driving of heresies out of his province, using, in the page's words, the beneficent and admirable knife of the teaching of the divine word. The emperor Maurice held him in high regard, because Pausikakos had cured him of a very grave illness, and out of gratitude he settled an annual money grant on the see.

He died in peace, labouring for his diocese to his last breath. saint.gr gives no year for that death, and the dates on this folder are only the bracket its two named reigns allow.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same menologion day, 13 мая ст. ст., and gives the year the Greek page would not. He lived at the end of the sixth century in the Syrian city of Апамея; instructed in the faith from childhood by pious parents, he took up an ascetic life young, in prayer, in vigils and in hard fasting, and the Lord gave him the gift of healing sicknesses of soul and of body. The patriarch Кириак of Constantinople, who held the see from 592 to 606, ordained him bishop of Synada.

As bishop he became known for his zeal that there should be neither heretics nor corrupted men among his flock, and he taught them a virtuous life in words the page calls strong and effective. Being at Constantinople on the business of the Church he healed the emperor Маврикий of a sickness; and on the road home he asked the Lord for water to quench his companions' thirst, and after his prayer a spring of clean water came out of the ground. He fell asleep in peace in 606. That year is the one thing this folder most needed: it stood on a bracket built out of two reigns because the Greek page named no year, and the Russian names one inside it.
