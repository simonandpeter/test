# Basil (Shikalov)

Basil Yakovlevich Shikalov was born on 12 April 1875 in the village of
Bolshaya Dubrovna in the Bologoye district of Tver province, and was a
parishioner of the Ascension church of Voznesenye and latterly its
warden. When the godless authorities began their persecution of the
Church at the end of the twenties, pursuing the Orthodox laity too, they
demanded of him a “firm assignment” of farm produce that was impossible in
itself; for failing it he was arrested, sentenced to five years in a
corrective labour camp, and stripped of all his property. Returning to
his village at the start of 1937 he petitioned for a review of his case;
that summer the petition was granted, he was found innocent and his
property returned — but earthly justice did not last long, and in the
autumn he was arrested again.

The priest who came to the Ascension church at the start of 1937, the
archpriest [Alexius Uspensky](/saints/alexius-presbyter-martyr-1937-9-september) — born 22 September 1879 at Sholy in the
Belozersk district of Novgorod province, a psalmist’s son, once already
imprisoned for six months over a tax he could not pay, and appointed by
Archbishop Nicephorus (Nikolsky) when the authorities took Archbishop
Thaddeus’s registration from him — showed himself a zealous pastor and a
man of prayer: he sent children given un-Christian names back to the
rural soviet to be re-registered before he would baptise them, never held
a general confession however many came but spoke with each, taught his
parishioners to pray and to teach their children to, never to change their
faith and never to take off the cross; and since neither crosses nor
prayers of absolution nor icons could be had any more, he sent his warden
Basil to the big cities to buy what was needed. An informer noted the
revival in the parish: people going to church, baptising children they had
not baptised before — a high-ranking Red Army officer had come to the
village on purpose to baptise his; talk of restoring the bells; many nuns
gathered round the church doing missionary work; the wives of party
workers attending. When in the summer of 1937 the government decided on
arrests of clergy everywhere the NKVD used the report, and on 2 September
arrested the priest and the warden. Questioned on 6 September — the inquiry
knows that you, together with the priest Alexius Uspensky, carried on
counter-revolutionary activity — Basil answered: as church warden I
distributed crosses among the people, up to a hundred and fifty in all,
and the distribution of prayers and chaplets was laid on me too; the
prayers I bought in Leningrad, in the market by the church of John the
Forerunner, from a man unknown to me. — The inquiry knows that you
gathered and carried on counter-revolutionary activity in the priest’s
house. — It is true I was at the priest’s house in June, but why I went to
him I now do not remember. — The inquiry knows that while distributing
the prayers and crosses you carried on counter-revolutionary agitation. —
While distributing the prayers and crosses I carried on no
counter-revolutionary agitation. The priest for his part admitted
baptising newborn children in the villagers’ houses without the local
authorities’ leave, distributing crosses and icons with prayers to
strengthen the faith, sending back children with names not in the
calendar, and commemorating the tsar Nicholas and Alexandra among the
departed at the Liturgy, “without any intention”; of any
counter-revolutionary sayings of Shikalov’s he knew nothing. On 19
September the NKVD troika sentenced both to be shot, and the archpriest
Alexius Uspensky and the warden Basil Shikalov were shot on 21 September
1937 — 8 September of the old style; the Russian calendar keeps Basil on
9 September of the old style, 22 September, and in the synaxis of the
saints of Tver. Both were numbered among the new martyrs and confessors
of Russia by the jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000; the life is by
Igumen Damascene (Orlovsky).
