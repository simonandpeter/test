# Pharmouthius the Anchorite

saint.gr's whole account of him is one sentence: «Ο Όσιος Φαρμούθιος απεβίωσε ειρηνικά» — the venerable Pharmouthius departed in peace. The day's line adds one word to it, ο Αναχωρητής, the anchorite, and the page's heading gives him no years at all.

What is left is the couplet the page prints over the entry, which plays his name against the calendar: Pharmouthius went out from the things here in the month Pharmouthi — plainly, it says, in April. That is the Egyptian month whose name he bears, and it is the only thing on the page that points to where he lived.

Nothing else is told. The page gives no century, no desert and no monastery, names no teacher and no disciple, lists no relic, and prints no hymn. The corpus holds no other man of this name, so nothing here had to be told apart from anybody.

**The Russian calendar keeps him on the same menologion day, and what it adds is one sentence and a century.** Its line is «Прп. Фармуфия (IV).» and its whole life is this: that he lived in the fourth century in the same desert in which a venerable John ascetised in a well, and that it was Pharmouthius who fed him. The name link behind the line gives «Фармуфий Египетский, пустынник», Pharmouthius the Egyptian, a hermit of the desert, which is the first form of his name a calendar has printed beyond the bare Greek forename this folder already held, and which names the country the Greek entry did not.

The John he fed is kept by this calendar on 29 March, and this corpus has no folder for him, so he is named here without a link and without a relation; nothing read links Pharmouthius to any other saint the corpus holds. The century is the folder's first date of any kind: it stood undated because the Greek page gave no year, and the reading taken here is the page's own words, that he *lived* in the fourth century, so it is recorded as a floruit and not as a death.
