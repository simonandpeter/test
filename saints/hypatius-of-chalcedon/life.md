# Hypatius of Chalcedon

doxologia.ro's calendar for 25 februarie prints his line as «Sfântul Mucenic Ipatie», the holy martyr Hypatius; the life the page links under that name calls him instead «Cel între sfinţi părintele nostru Ipatie», our father among the saints, and tells of a man who died in peace at eighty. The two do not agree, and what follows is the life, not the line. He was born in Phrygia in the days of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius. At eighteen, still at his books, he was beaten by his father for some fault, ran away from home and crossed into Thrace, where he entered a coenobium and became a monk, full of reverence and of spiritual profit through fasting and abstinence.

Once, tormented by the passion of lust, he passed eight days fasting and tasted neither food nor drink. When the superior learned of it he gave him with his own hand a cup of wine and a loaf after compline, in front of all the brethren, and at once he was freed of the torment and gave thanks to the Lord and to his teacher. Some years later, on his confessor's advice, he went into the city to help a man who had fallen into temptation, and helped his own father in the flesh as well, setting him up and sending him home.

Then he joined two other brothers in the Lord, crossed the sea, and came to Chalcedon, where they settled in the old monastery of Rufinus, which stood empty. Rufinus had come from Rome, built it, and brought Egyptian monks there who glorified God; when he died and was buried in it the monks scattered to their own places, and the house was left uninhabited, thorns and thistles grew up, the buildings fell, and unclean spirits came in and lived there, so that the place was dreadful to see. Hypatius drove out the demons by prayer, cleansed the monastery of its foulness, and with those who were with him brought it back to its first beauty.

Settling there, one of them wove hair-cloth, another baskets, another worked the garden. After a time he crossed into Thrace and went back to his own monastery, and the brethren from Rufinus came and asked for their abbot Hypatius and took him away with them; and from then on he showed such labours that many were drawn to his life, and in a short time they were thirty. Taking the gift from God, he worked many healings on men and on beasts alike. His food was vegetables and greens and a little bread late, at the ninth hour; in old age he took a little wine as well.

He reached eighty, and his head and beard went white as snow, and he was reverend both in what he did and in how he looked. Having pastured such a flock of Christ for forty years and honoured the priesthood, and having sent eighty of his disciples on to the Lord before him, he went himself, glorifying God. The page gives no year for any of this, and prints no hymn for him.
