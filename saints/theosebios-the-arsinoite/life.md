# Theosebios the Arsinoite

saint.gr's calendar for 12 October keeps «Όσιος Θεοσέβιος ο Αρσινοΐτης», and gives him a long life. He was born in the small village of Melandra, twelve kilometres south-east of ancient Arsinoe — the Polis Chrysochous of today, in the district of Paphos — to devout and well-off parents, Michael and Anna, and his brother was [Arcadius](/saints/arcadius-of-arsinoe), afterwards bishop of Arsinoe, whom the page sends the reader to find on 29 August. The exact year of his birth, it says, is unknown to us.

While his brother went to Constantinople to study, Theosebios stayed by his parents and became a shepherd. Pasturing his sheep gave him the chance to give himself to prayer and to the study of the word of God; with the pasture for a pretext he would go three kilometres up the mountain to a quiet and out-of-the-way place and pray there undistracted, and he found a cave in those parts which he often entered to pray with fasting and prostrations. He ate the least he could, only to live, and on the days of fasting gave away all the food he had brought — usually only bread — to whoever he met who had need; and if he met nobody, he gave it to the birds, so as not to break the commandment of the fast.

When he came of age he obeyed his parents' wish and married a virtuous girl from the next village, Philousa; but he had already gone so far in the spiritual life that he decided, his wife agreeing to it, to live a chaste and virgin life within the marriage, keeping with her the relations of a brother. One day, sensing his end, he called his wife and told her in a parable that he was going further off to pasture the sheep and would stay there a long while, urging her to go on in patience and sobriety; then he went up to the place of his prayer, left the flock grazing, went into the cave, prayed fervently and quietly gave up his spirit to God. It was 12 October.

His dog, which had been keeping the mouth of the cave like a doorkeeper, gathered the flock next day and led it down to Melandra, and his family, alarmed at the sheep without the shepherd, were guided by the dog and after three days found the body fragrant in the cave. They could not move it, and the saint appeared that night to his father and asked to be buried there; a church was later built over the grave, and it became a refuge for Christians from the villages near and far, who came to it in faith and were healed.

Saint Neophytus, the page says, records very many wonders done by him after his death — he showed a monk who wanted to build cells by his church where the lime was to be found, he guarded that monk's little garden from the crows, he opened the eyes of the blind and healed the deaf, and two shepherds who robbed his church confessed it, gave back what they had taken and were forgiven. The cave of Saint Theosebios, it adds, is still shown today.
