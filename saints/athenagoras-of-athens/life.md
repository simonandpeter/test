# Athenagoras of Athens

saint.gr says Athenagoras was a Christian philosopher and apologist of the second century after Christ. He came most probably from Athens, where he studied Middle Platonism and Stoic philosophy, and he flourished in the years of the Roman emperors Marcus Aurelius, from 161 to 180, and Commodus, from 180 to 192. The page explains his name as Athens and agora taken together: the one who gathers the Athenians, the wise speaker.

The basic facts about the man, his education and his work, the page says, are drawn from his own two writings, which survive in a codex of the year 914 made in the bibliographical workshop of Arethas: the Embassy concerning Christians, and On the Resurrection of the Dead.

Athenagoras stands out among the apologists of his own time for the finish of his learning and the care of his style, the page says, while in theology he puts forward the Orthodox teaching on the Trinity, the inspiration of the Holy Scriptures and a strict ascetic standard in the moral life of Christians; and for that his written work holds a place worth noting in the church literature of the first centuries. The notice stops there: it gives no account of his death, no place of burial, no relics, no distich and no hymn.
