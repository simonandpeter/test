# Photinos, son of Photini

saint.gr's calendar for 26 Φεβρουαρίου prints «Άγιος Φωτεινός» as a line of its own, and the page standing under it gives one sentence: «Ο Άγιος Φωτεινός ήταν ο πρωτότοκος υιός της Αγίας Φωτεινής και μαρτύρησε δια ξίφους» — Photinos was the firstborn son of St Photini, and he was martyred by the sword. The fuller story is on the page of [Photini the Samaritan](/saints/photini-the-samaritan), which says that her sons and her five sisters were martyred together with her: she had been baptised among the first women of Samaria after her meeting with Christ at Jacob's well, had preached the Gospel in Africa and at Rome, and was put to death at Rome by the emperor Nero.

The rest of the household the calendar prints beside his on this day is [Anatole](/saints/anatole-sister-of-photini), [Photo](/saints/photo-sister-of-photini), [Photis](/saints/photis-sister-of-photini), [Paraskeve](/saints/paraskeve-sister-of-photini), [Kyriake](/saints/kyriake-sister-of-photini) and [Joses](/saints/joses-son-of-photini). This page gives Photinos no birthplace, no year, no relics and no hymn of his own — the second apolytikion on Photini's page names all eight of them together — and over the one sentence it prints a couplet, «Τὶς οὗτος ἀτμὸς ἐκ ζεόντων αἱμάτων; Φωτεινὸς ἄρτι Μάρτυς ἐτμήθη κάραν»: what is this steam off boiling blood? Photinos the martyr has just had his head struck off.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 26 Φεβρουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/02/26/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3648/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
