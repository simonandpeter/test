# Theodosia of Constantinople

saint.gr says Theodosia was of Constantinople, born of rich and devout parents in the years of the emperor Theodosius the Adramyttene. Left fatherless at seven years old she was put into a monastery, and a little after was tonsured a nun. When her mother died too she sold what she had and shared it among the poor, and so, freed of earthly cares, gave herself with greater zeal to the monastic virtues, in the house that stood near the «Σκοτεινόν φρέαρ», the dark well, and was called «Άσπαρον στέρνην».

When Leo the Isaurian came to the throne, in the years 717 to 741, a savage persecution broke out against the friends of the icons and against the holy images themselves. The patriarch [Germanus](/saints/germanus-of-constantinople), a firm bulwark of Orthodoxy, was driven out and replaced by the iconoclast Anastasius; and at the opening of the persecution the order went out that the image of Christ which stood over the Chalke Gate should be taken down and destroyed.

Then Theodosia, at the head of a company of nuns and other women, rushed upon the guardsman who had climbed the movable ladder to destroy the image and threw him down from it, and with stones and staves they set upon the Patriarchate, so that Anastasius was forced to abandon it. The soldiery that came in killed some of the women and seized others, Theodosia among them; and of those taken some were set free, some shut in prison and some sent away into exile.

Theodosia they ill-used, and then led her out to the place called the Ox and cut her throat, running a ram's horn through it, in the year 730. Her honourable relic was gathered up and buried in the monastery of Dexiokrates, and the page says it worked many wonders on the faithful who came to it with faith and reverence. saint.gr prints no hymn for her.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the same day and tells her beginning quite differently. She was born, it says, by the prayer of her mother to the holy martyr Anastasia, who appeared to her mother and told her that she would bear a child. Her parents dedicated her to God and gave her early into a women’s monastery, the monastery of Saint Anastasia — where the Greek page has her left fatherless at seven and put into the house near the dark well that was called the Asparos cistern.

After her parents died she had an enormous fortune, and out of it she ordered three icons from a goldsmith: of the Saviour, of the Mother of God and of Saint Anastasia. All the rest she gave away to the poor. So the two books agree that she gave her inheritance away and differ on what she kept back for herself.

She suffered in the days of the wicked emperor Leo the Isaurian, the persecutor of the icons, and received the double crown, the virginal and the martyric, in the year 730 — the same emperor and the same year the Greek page gives. Of her death this book says nothing further: it has neither the image over the Chalke Gate, nor the ladder, nor the place called the Ox.

The Russian church calendar keeps her on 29 мая ст. ст. and gives her a life. She lived in the eighth century, was born at her parents’ earnest prayer, and after their death was brought up in a women’s monastery at Constantinople dedicated to the martyr Анастасия. She took the tonsure there after giving the property left her by her parents to the poor, spending part of the money on the painting of icons of the Saviour, of the Mother of God and of the martyr Анастасия.

When Leo the Isaurian came to the throne, whom the calendar dates 717 to 741 and calls a cruel persecutor of those who honoured the icons, he gave an order that the holy icons be destroyed everywhere. There were then at Constantinople gates called the brazen gates, and above them a brazen image of the Saviour had stood for more than four hundred years. In 730 the false patriarch and iconoclast Анастасий ordered the image taken down; and the Orthodox people, with her and other nuns at their head, rushed to the icon’s defence and overturned the ladder with the soldier on it who was carrying out the order.

The false patriarch, fearing that the disturbance would grow, told the emperor what had happened; and at his order the soldiers killed all the nuns, and shut her up in prison as the most zealous defender of the icons. For a week she was given a hundred blows every day, and on the eighth day she was led along the city and savagely beaten on the way, until one of the soldiers struck her a mortal wound of which she died at once. Her body, thrown on the ground, was reverently buried by the Christians in the Diokritis monastery at Constantinople, and the place of her burial became famous for many healings of the sick.
