# Metrophanes Ji-Sung

saint.gr keeps him at the head of the company of the Chinese martyrs of 1900, and tells their story first. In that year the secret martial-arts society of the «Boxers» — the page glosses the name as Yihetuan, the militias of righteousness and concord, whose members trained at boxing and wore amulets believed to turn bullets — with the backing of the dowager empress, who was known for her hatred of foreigners, let loose a persecution against the Christians, on whom they laid the blame for all the calamities of China. On 10 June proclamations were posted up in the streets of Peking calling on the idolaters to slaughter the Christians and threatening any who dared hide them.

The next night the Boxers went with lighted torches through every quarter of the city, seizing in their houses such Orthodox Christians as they found and torturing them to make them deny Christ. Many, terrified by the torments, burned incense before the idols to save their lives; others confessed their faith bravely and endured fearful things. Their houses were burned, and they were led outside the city to the idol-temples of the Boxers, where they were disembowelled, beheaded, or burnt on pyres.

Father Metrophanes Ji-Sung was the first Chinese priest to have been ordained by [Nicholas of Japan](/saints/nicholas-of-japan), whom the page sends the reader to find on 3 Φεβρουαρίου, and he had served the mission tirelessly for fifteen years. He was slaughtered with the greater part of the seventy Christians, women and children, who had taken refuge in his house after the buildings of the Russian mission were set on fire. When the Boxers broke in they found him sitting in the courtyard and stabbed him through and through in the breast. They beheaded his wife [Tatiana](/saints/tatiana-of-peking) and their son [Isaiah](/saints/isaiah-of-peking), who was twenty-three; they cut off the toes, the nose and the ears of their other son [John](/saints/john-of-peking) at the very hour of his father's martyrdom.

Among those killed with him were [Maria](/saints/maria-of-peking), the betrothed of Isaiah, and the teacher [Ia](/saints/ia-wen-of-peking), and the [two hundred and twenty-two](/saints/two-hundred-twenty-two-chinese-martyrs) others whom the page counts but does not name. saint.gr sends the reader for more to the «Συναξάριον Ορθοδόξων Κινέζων Μαρτύρων» of Georgios E. Piperakis, Athens 1997.
