# Gennadius

Gennadius was set on the throne of Constantinople in 458, in the reign of the emperor Leo the Great, after Anatolius. What is known of his life comes from the Spiritual Meadow, which records the stories of the monks of the monastery of Salama near Alexandria, Sophronius and John, who were clerics of the Church of Constantinople in his time; he was known for meekness, patience, purity and abstinence, and the power of his prayer is shown by the case of Charisimus, an unworthy cleric of the church of the martyr Eleutherius who lived in idleness and impurity and even practised robbery and sorcery — the life breaks off there as read.

The Prologue adds that in his time the senator Studius came from Rome and with his blessing built the church of John the Forerunner and the monastery of the Studion; that he would ordain no one who did not know the whole Psalter by heart; that he presided over the council at Constantinople which anathematised simony; that he worked miracles and learned his end in a vision; and that he governed the Church thirteen years and reposed in peace in 471.

The Romanian calendar keeps him too, on 17 noiembrie, where doxologia's line reads «Sfântul Ierarh Ghenadie, patriarhul Constantinopolului». The page behind that line prints no life of him at all: its one further tab is a recording of his troparion in Greek, with no text, so the Romanian calendar adds his day and his name and nothing more.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 17 November, in one line with a patriarch [Maximus](/saints/maximus-patriarch-17-november), and says of the two that both were worthy in every way as shepherds of the Church. saint.gr makes this Gennadius the **first** of the name: he succeeded the patriarch of Constantinople Anatolius in 458, having been before that a presbyter of the same Church, and worked fruitfully for the return of many heretics to Orthodoxy. Among them was Marcianus, who had embraced the errors of the Novatians and whom Gennadius, after his return, made steward of the Great Church.

He also persuaded the emperor Leo the Thracian to exile the heretic Peter the Fuller, when Peter seized the throne of Antioch from the Orthodox patriarch Martyrius; and in his time the emperor Leo Makelles made the hallowing of Sunday law. Gennadius would ordain no man priest who did not know the interpretation of the Psalms well, and he called a synod at Constantinople which published an encyclical against the taking of money for ordination. After that conscientious and priestly governance, the page says, Gennadius I died in 471.
