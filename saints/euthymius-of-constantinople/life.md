# Euthymius of Constantinople

saint.gr records that the memory of Saint Euthymius is set down by Gedeon in his Eortologion, at page 149, and that this is the Euthymius who held the patriarchate from 907 to 912, after the removal of Alexander, brother of Leo the Wise. He had followed the monastic life from very early, the page says, and was the spiritual father of the emperor Leo VI the Wise, who reigned from 886 to 912 and who honoured him with the office of synkellos.

When the patriarch Nicholas I the Mystikos was put out over the emperor's fourth marriage, Euthymius was persuaded to come up to the patriarchal throne of the reigning city, and he recognised that fourth marriage «κατ᾽ οἰκονομίαν», by dispensation. But after the death of Leo the Wise he was driven from the throne, deposed at the synod which met in the Magnaura, and exiled to the monastery of Agathos, where he died on 5 August 917.

Besides the chroniclers of the time, the page says, the events of his life and work are told by a Life written between 920 and 925 by an unknown author, which it lists in four modern editions; and the Life of Euthymius of Constantinople is an important historical source for the reign of the emperor Leo VI the Wise. Two of his homilies on the Mother of God survive, and a canon to the Theotokos which is unpublished, in codex Xenophontos 69 on the Holy Mountain.
