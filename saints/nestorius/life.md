# Nestorius

A monk of Antioch made archbishop of Constantinople in 428, Nestorius objected
within months to the title *Theotokos* — God-bearer — as applied to Mary,
proposing *Christotokos* instead on the grounds that the divine nature is not
born. Cyril of Alexandria took the objection as a denial that the incarnate
Christ is one subject. The dispute was over within three years: the Council of
Ephesus in 431 opened before the bishops sympathetic to Nestorius had arrived,
condemned him, and deposed him.

He returned to his monastery, was exiled to Egypt in 435, and died there around
451, having written a long defence, the *Bazaar of Heracleides*, which was lost
until a Syriac manuscript surfaced in 1889. Its rediscovery reopened a question
that had been settled for fourteen centuries — whether the position condemned at
Ephesus was in fact the position he held.

The Church of the East never accepted the condemnation and honours him among the
Greek Doctors. Every other communion in this registry records a refusal. This
entry does not adjudicate between them; it shows both, which is the whole reason
the attestation model exists.
