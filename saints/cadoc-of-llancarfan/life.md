# Cadoc of Llancarfan

Saint Cadoc, whose name saint.gr prints in Latin letters and glosses «Κάντοκ», came of a noble family of Wales, and his own parents became monastics. He founded the famous monastery of Llancarfan near Cardiff, but his missionary work reached out as far as Cornwall, Scotland and Ireland. He was a contemporary of [David of Wales](/saints/david-of-wales) and the teacher of Saint Finnian, for whom saint.gr gives no day.

A few of his sayings survive, and the page prints them. Truth, he said, is the firstborn daughter of God; without the light nothing is good, without the light there is no reverence, without the light there is no faith. The best of ways is humility; the best of occupations the working of the divine commandments; the best of feelings justice; the best sorrow repentance; the best quality magnanimity.

Asked what love is, he answered: love is paradise. And what hatred is: hatred is hell. And conscience? Conscience is the eye of God within the soul. He was martyred in the year 590, killed by Anglo-Saxon pagans while he was celebrating the Divine Liturgy, and his last words, the page says, were «Αόρατε βασιλεύ, Σώτερ Ιησού, Σε ικετεύω, προστάτευσε τους Χριστιανούς της χώρας μου» — Invisible King, Saviour Jesus, I beseech thee, protect the Christians of my country.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 24 Ιανουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/01/24/index.aspx) and [his page](https://www.saint.gr/3532/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
