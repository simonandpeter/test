# Audactus of Ephesus

Audactus — or Adauctus — was from Ephesus, the Greek synaxarion says, and for his prudence and wealth had been honoured by Maximinus with the rank of eparch. When Maximinus asked for his daughter [Callisthene](/saints/callisthene-daughter-of-audactus) as his wife, Audactus would not give her to an idolater, so his goods were seized and he was exiled to Melitene, where he was beheaded. Its verse has him, dead by the sword, living with the martyrs and seeing the living God.

The Romanian synaxarion tells it the same way, making him a duke over other dukes and eparch of Maximinus, first among them in riches and wisdom, and the emperor a stranger to the faith; and both say that his daughter later brought his relics away and built a church in his name — to Ephesus from Melitene in the Greek, from Mesopotamia to Asia in the Romanian. Neither gives a year. Both calendars keep him on 4 October, the Greek with his daughter.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 4 October and gives this folder its first date — as a reign rather than a year, which is how the source states it: «Мчч. Давикта (Адавкта) (ок. 305-313)». It prints his name twice over, «Давикта (Адавкта)», and its index of names adds a second place, «Давикт (Адавкт) Ефесский, Мелитинский» — of Ephesus and of Melitene. His daughter gets her own clause and her own year on the same page.
