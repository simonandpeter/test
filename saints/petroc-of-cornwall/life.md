# Petroc of Cornwall

saint.gr says that the venerable Petroc, the protector of Cornwall, was born in the year 468 in southern Wales, and was the younger son — or, by others' account, the nephew — of Saint Claudius Kerniv, king of Glywysing in Wales, whom the same calendar keeps on 3 May. For a number of years he settled in Ireland, and later returned to Great Britain, where he founded a mission station in the town of Hailesmouth and a monastery in the region of Lanwethinoc, which later took the name Petrockstow and is known today as the town of Padstow, and where he lived as an ascetic for thirty years.

The venerable one went on pilgrimage to Rome and to the Holy Land, and finally got as far as India, to live as a hermit on an island in the Indian Ocean. Returning to Cornwall, he founded another monastery at Petherick and a hermitage at Bodmin, where he met the venerable Goran, whom the same calendar keeps on 7 April. The venerable Petroc fell asleep in peace in the year 564, and his holy relic was buried in the town of Padstow and moved afterwards to the church at Bodmin. Three Irishmen the same day keeps — Cruidan, Dagan and Medan — are said on their own page to have been his disciples.
