# Michael Choniates

Michael Choniates was the last metropolitan of Athens before the Frankish conquest, holding the see from 1182 to 1204. saint.gr calls him a learned metropolitan and the brother of the historian Niketas Choniates, a lover of the glorious past of Athens, and says he was the man who defended the city not only from its enemies and their sieges but from the licence of the state's own officials. His letters, the page says, are a rich source of information about Attica at the end of the twelfth century and the beginning of the thirteenth.

In those letters, it goes on, one can make out an aggressive disposition towards Athens; but a closer look will easily find an essential and deep love, both for the city and for its people. In 1203 he successfully defended the city when it was endangered by the Peloponnesian landholder Leo Sgouros. A little later, in 1204, immediately after the Frankish conquest, he exiled himself to Kea, not ceasing even from there to defend and protect his flock.

Afterwards he went to the monastery of Saint John the Forerunner near Thermopylae, where he died in 1222. His service was written by Charalampis Bousias, the Great Hymnographer of the Church of Alexandria, and saint.gr notes that his memory is recorded, together with that of [Andrew of Crete](/saints/andrew-of-crete), in the Vatopedi Codex with a service common to the two of them. That is everything the page says of his life.
