# Eudocia (Tkachenko)

Eudocia was a nun of the Iveron-Seraphim convent at Verny in the Semirechye
region, founded in 1910 under its first abbess Paisia, who had come from the
John and Mary convent at Stavropol. In 1913 Bishop Innocent (Pustynsky), who
had taken the diocese, set over the convent, against the wish of the sisters
and of the hieromonk Seraphim (Bogoslovsky) who was their spiritual father,
the daughter of General Bakurevich, chairman of the Cossack host’s board — a
young rasophore novice Taisia whom he tonsured with the name Euphrosyne;
brought up in a soft home, she was spoiled and needed a strict teacher
herself, and the devil, seizing on the bishop’s mistake, set briskly about
destroying the common life: disorders began in a house that had lost
experienced government, and the nuns began to leave it. Seraphim was living
then in the skete on the mountain Kyzyl-Zhar near Verny, and Eudocia often
went to him. In 1918 the godless took power in Semirechye, and the
executions and persecution of believers began as everywhere; in the summer
of 1918 Eudocia went to Seraphim, who was then serving in the skete on
Medeo, to confess and communicate. That evening Red soldiers came to the
convent and demanded that the nuns hand over the abbess Euphrosyne as a
general’s daughter; the nuns hid her in the barn among the flour sacks, and
the soldiers, balked, took out the nuns Eudocia and Animaisa to be shot in
her place, stood them against the convent wall and fired. The bullet struck
Eudocia in the head and disfigured her face; Animaisa was wounded in the
shoulder, and the sisters carried her in and she recovered. Eudocia’s friend
the nun Theodora was so shaken by the shooting and by what the bullet had
done to the face of the one who shared her secrets that she fell ill from
the shock and for a long time could not forget the bloodied, ruined face;
then Eudocia appeared to her in a dream and said, Theodora, why do you weep,
what are you afraid of? do not weep; you see that I am just as I was — and
after that she no longer saw the face disfigured, and was healed. The life
is from the igumen Damascene (Orlovsky)’s lives of the new martyrs; she is
kept on 15 September of the old style, 28 September.
