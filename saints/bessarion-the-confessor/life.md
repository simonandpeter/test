# Bessarion the Confessor

Bessarion was a Serb, born in Bosnia in 1714, and he became the great defender of the Orthodox faith in the Banat and in Transylvania against the forced union of the Orthodox with Rome. Devout and drawn to the desert life, he was tonsured a monk in 1738 at the monastery of Saint Sabbas in the Holy Land; returning to Serbia, he struggled some years in a cave as a hermit, and for the holiness of his life was given the gift of working wonders.

The Orthodox patriarch Arsenie of Karlovci heard of him, and — the passing of the Romanian Orthodox of the Banat and Transylvania to the union being then at its most violent — called him, ordained him priest, and sent him to defend the faith north-west of the Carpathians. In January 1744 he set out for the Banat and was received by the people as a saint. Hundreds and thousands came out to meet him and returned to the Orthodox Church, and everywhere he urged them not to leave the "ancestral law".

From Timișoara he went to Lipova near Arad, where a great crowd heard him, and set up a wooden cross in the middle of the village, to which the Orthodox made real pilgrimages. He was taken under guard to Vienna, tried, and thrown by order of the empress Maria Theresa into the fortress prison of Kufstein, where he suffered heavy torture for confessing the right faith. After nearly a year of torment in chains he gave up his soul.

The Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church canonised him in February 1950, declaring him a confessor saint, and keeps his memory on 21 octombrie.
