# Conon, father of Conon

The Ohrid Prologue gives this father and his son three sentences and no more. The father, it says, was already an old man, and the son a youth of seventeen years. In the time of Dometian the two of them were sawn through with a saw for the faith of Christ, and were glorified in the Church on earth and in heaven; and they suffered honourably, the entry ends, in the year 275.

That is the whole of it. The Prologue does not say who Dometian was — it calls him neither emperor nor governor — and it names no city, no province and no judge. It gives neither man a second name, so that the only thing that tells them apart is which of the two was the father, and it prints no hymn. The one kept here is the father; the son is [Conon](/saints/conon-son-of-conon), under the same heading.

The Russian church keeps the two of them on this same day, on one line, «Прмчч. Конона и сына его Конона (270-275).», and its life is the first full account either folder has carried. He lived at Иконии in Asia Minor. When he was widowed he withdrew into a monastery, taking his seven-year-old son with him, and by the holiness of his life was granted help from above: he drove out demons, healed the sick, gave sight to the blind, preached the teaching of Christ among the pagans and turned many of them. Report of him reached the governor Дометиан, a persecutor of Christians, who called him to judgment and pressed him to sacrifice to the idols; the preacher would not give way and was handed over to the torturers.

Then the son was brought to the same court, seventeen years old by now and a deacon — ten years in the monastery, which is the one arithmetic the page allows and the only indication of how long they were there. Flattering persuasion failed on him as it had on his father, and the two were tortured together: stripped and laid on a red-hot iron bed, drenched with scalding oil, thrown into a cauldron of boiling tin, sulphur and pitch, hung head downward and scorched with foul smoke — and kept unharmed, the life says, by a power from heaven. Shamed and furious, their tormentors found a terrible way to make an end and sentenced them to be sawn apart with a wooden saw. Hearing the sentence, the two asked for time to pray, and prayed «Благодарим Тебя, Господи, что сподобил нас пострадать за Имя Твое!» — we thank thee, Lord, that thou hast counted us worthy to suffer for thy name — and asked for peace for the Church, the shaming of its persecutors, the strengthening and increase of those who believe, and rest for their own souls. A voice of God came from heaven calling them; they signed themselves with the cross and gave up their souls. An earthquake followed at once and every idol temple in the city fell down.

The monks buried the two bodies secretly in the monastery where the saints had lived, and the Russian page closes with two things the Serbian entry behind this folder had not: that it happened in the reign of Aurelian, 270 to 275, which is the span this folder already records, and that the relics were afterwards carried into Italy, to the city of Ацерра in Campania. The grade on the line is прмч., a venerable-martyr, and it is the calendar's own reason for `venerable` beside `martyr` here: these are monks of a monastery put to death, not soldiers or townsmen. **He is the elder of two men of one name in one dossier**, and the Russian form kept on this folder, «Конон Иконийский, Старший (отец)», is the title of his own name page, 1265, where the son's is 1264 — two pages, two numbers and two bracketed words from the one calendar that distinguishes them in writing at all.
