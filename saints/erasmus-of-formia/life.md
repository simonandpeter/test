# Erasmus of Formia

From his youth he served the Lord earnestly, and in his grown years he was raised to the rank of bishop of the city of Formia in Italy. In the persecution of the Christians under the emperors Diocletian and Maximian he left his diocese and withdrew into the mountains of Lebanon, where he hid for seven years. Then an angel appeared to him and said: «Еразм! никто не победит врагов, если будет спать. Иди же в город свой, мужественно подвизайся и победишь врагов» — nobody will overcome his enemies if he sleeps; go to thy city, strive manfully, and thou wilt overcome them.

Heeding that voice he came out of his solitude, and the first to ask him about his faith were soldiers coming to meet him. He confessed himself a Christian, and they brought him for judgment to Antioch before the emperor, where he confessed his faith in Christ without fear and boldly reproached the emperor for his impiety. He was put to cruel tortures and remained unshaken; after them he was bound in iron chains and thrown into prison, from which an angel brought him out in a marvellous manner, saying: follow me, I shall take thee into Italy, and there too thou wilt bring many to salvation.

In the city of Lycia he preached Christ boldly to the people and raised the son of a man of rank from the dead, and the account says that after that wonder ten thousand people were baptised there. The emperor of the western half of the Roman empire, Maximian Herculius, ordered him seized and brought to judgment; before him too he confessed his faith boldly. They beat him and threatened him with crucifixion if he would not deny Christ, and then made him go into the temple of the idols — but at his entering, every idol in it fell and was broken, and fire came out of the temple and burnt many of the pagans.

Left at liberty he baptised a great many people and then went to the city of Sirmium, where he was seized again and tormented again: they put him into a red-hot furnace and he came out alive and unharmed. That wonder so shook those present that the emperor, fearing a rising of the people, withdrew into his palace. An angel freed him from his chains and brought him to Formia, to his own see, where he baptised a great many people and where he died, in the year 303. The Christians buried his remains with honour.
