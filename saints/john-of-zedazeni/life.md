# John of Zedazeni

John was a Syrian. The exact place of his birth is not known, nor are his parents' names, but the life says he was born in a village near Antioch at the end of the fifth century. He studied in that city with exceptional results, and his spiritual growth went with his bodily growth until he was an example of a virtuous Christian; the teachings of Scripture took deep root in him, and he gathered into his heart as into a treasury everything of spiritual use. While still young, hearing a voice within him, he became a monk in a coenobium.

His humility, the page says, was so deep that he saw himself as a worm and not a man; he fought manfully against evil and against the devil's temptations, the springs of his tears never dried, and he asked God night and day for strength against the passions of the body. God heard him and gave him both that strength and the gift of working wonders, and among the monks of the coenobium he was marked out by his struggles.

Then God showed him his will: he saw the Most Holy Mother of God in a vision, commanding him to choose twelve worthy disciples and take them with him into Georgia, which the page calls Iviria, to strengthen the Christian faith in her newly founded Church there. He told the brothers of the vision, and to make the choice easier they spent the whole night in prayer and began the Liturgy about three in the morning; after communion he told them all to raise their hands and say the Greek «Doamne miluiește!», and they did so for a full hour, until the angel of the Lord took twelve names from the holy table before them all.

He comforted those who stayed, appointed Euthymius abbot in his place, blessed each of the twelve with his hands on their heads, and set out with them on the long journey. In Georgia he settled on Mount Zaden, on the left bank of the Aragvi about eight kilometres north-east of Mtskheta and fifteen north of Tbilisi — a mountain named for the idol that stood there in the first years of Christianity — and from it he is called Zedazneli. He sent the twelve out to preach and kept only the deacon Elias with him, as a support in his old age, and gave himself to struggles that dried his body up entirely. The unclean spirits could not bear his presence on the mountain and came to drive him off with shouts and threats; he signed himself with the cross and answered that if the Lord had given them power over his body, as once over Job's, they should do what they had to do, for he was the servant of his Lord Jesus and did not fear their threats.

The twelve he brought with him were [Abibus of Nekresi](/saints/abibus-of-nekresi), [Anthony of Martkopi](/saints/anthony-of-martkopi), [David of Gareji](/saints/david-of-gareji), [Zeno of Ikalto](/saints/zeno-of-ikalto), [Thaddeus of Stepantsminda](/saints/thaddeus-of-stepantsminda), [Jesse of Tsilkani](/saints/jesse-of-tsilkani), [Joseph of Alaverdi](/saints/joseph-of-alaverdi), [Isidore of Samtavisi](/saints/isidore-of-samtavisi), [Michael of Ulumbo](/saints/michael-of-ulumbo), [Pyrrhus of Breti](/saints/pyrrhus-of-breti), [Stephen of Khirsa](/saints/stephen-of-khirsa), [Shio of Mgvime](/saints/shio-of-mgvime). At the last, having charged his disciples to watch at the hour of death, when the devil lays his most frightful snares, and to remember him in their prayers, he gave orders to be buried in the cave of his struggles, communicated for the last time, saw an army of angels and many saints come for his soul, and gave it up. His memory, the page says, is kept on 7 mai, when the thirteen Syrian fathers are celebrated, and also on 12 December.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and tells the mission the Romanian line did not. John came out of Syria, saint.gr says, and lived about the end of the fifth century; he studied the wisdom of this world at Antioch, took the ascetic life while young and became a monk, and his humility was so deep that he thought himself a worm and not a man. Day and night he begged God to bless him, that he might fight the passions of soul and body; and God heard him and gave him dispassion and the gift of working wonders. When the fame of his holiness had spread through all Syria and the crowds that came to the holy elder disturbed his stillness, he left the place where he was living and hid deep in the desert.

But God ordered it otherwise. The saint saw the Mother of God in a dream, and she commanded him to choose twelve of his disciples and go with them into Iberia, to establish the Christian faith of its people. He called his subordinates together and told them; all of them wished to follow him, so he wrote every name on a separate paper and laid the papers on the holy table, and all night the fathers prayed. In the morning, after the Liturgy, he told them to lift their hands to heaven crying Lord have mercy; and during the prayer, in the sight of them all, an angel of the Lord took twelve papers from the holy table and put them into his hands. The chosen were Shio, David, Anthony, Thaddeus, Stephen, Isidore, Michael, Pyrrhus, Zeno, Jesse, Joseph and Abibus.

He then chose a monk named Euthymius as abbot of the skete and set out with his disciples. An angel of the Lord appeared to Eulogius, bishop of the Georgian capital, and told him of the coming of the servant of God John and of his disciples; and that blessed bishop set out with clergy and people to receive them. John made a prostration before the bishop and took his blessing, and then the wonder happened that is like the one on the day of Pentecost: the venerable one spoke the Georgian tongue. He and his disciples preached through all Georgia and venerated all the blessed places through which Saint Nina had passed. When the time came for him to rest, the Lord revealed the hour of his death to him; he called his disciples, counselled them as a father, blessed them, communicated of the immaculate Mysteries and gave up his spirit.
