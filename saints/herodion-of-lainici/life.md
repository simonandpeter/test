# Herodion of Lainici

He was born in 1821 in Bucharest and given the name Ioan at his baptism. At about twenty he entered the monastery of Cernica, drawn by the hesychast life lived there under the elder Calinic, and in 1846 he was tonsured with the name Irodion. Once tonsured he added labour to labour: he loved the brethren, discredited no one, kept clear of any evil speaking, fasted much, slept only three or four hours a night and made hundreds of prostrations a day.

In September 1850 his elder Calinic was consecrated bishop at Râmnicu Vâlcea and took a number of his disciples with him to help restore a diocese in visible decay. Irodion was among them, and towards the end of 1851 Calinic sent him on to the skete of Lainici in the valley of the Jiu. He was ordained deacon and then priest in 1853 and made ecclesiarch, and in 1854 abbot. Between 1854 and 1900 he governed the skete for forty-one years, with short interruptions — the longest-serving abbot Lainici has had.

After the death of his own spiritual father, Bishop Calinic took his disciple Irodion as confessor, so that the disciple became the elder's guide. Won over by his devotion, Calinic gave him the name by which he is still known, «Luceafărul de la Lainici», the Morning Star of Lainici, and came often to Lainici for counsel and confession; towards the end of his life he held three houses close — Cernica, Frăsinei and Lainici.

He departed to the Lord on 3 May 1900. Seven years later the abbot Teodosie Popescu asked the bishop of Râmnic for a blessing to open the grave, following the old monastic custom, kept on Athos to this day, of disinterring a monk seven years after his death and singing the funeral service over him again. The relics were deliberately hidden a second time in the nineteen-thirties, two and a half metres down, and were found a third time on 10 April 2009 and laid in a reliquary in the church for veneration.

The page calls him the last link in the chain of the Romanian neo-hesychasm of the nineteenth century, a chain it traces from Vasile of Poiana Mărului, Paisius Velichkovsky of Neamț, George of Cernica, Grigorie Dascălul and Calinic of Cernica. Metropolitans Nestor and Teofan of Oltenia had both wished to see him canonised, and the process was set in motion — the historical inquiry and the opening of the grave — by Metropolitan Irineu.
