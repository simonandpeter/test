# Constantine from the Jews

saint.gr's calendar for 26 December keeps the venerable Constantine, whom it calls «ὁ ἐξ Ἰουδαίων», the one out of the Jews. He was from Synada of Phrygia, and while still young he tried to follow the life of the virtuous Christians. The young Jew, who already carried the Christian faith in his soul, decided to leave the noise of the world, and went to the monastery of Phouvoution, whose monks were famous for a life pleasing to God.

When he had been tested long enough and they were satisfied of the sincerity and the brightness of his faith, they gave him holy baptism and named him Constantine. So he became a brother of the house and gave himself to the study of spiritual things. But that was not enough for him: he wanted to teach other men too the love of Christ, and he began to go from one city to another preaching the word of God. Among other places, the page says, he visited Olympus, Myra of Lycia, Cyprus, Attaleia and Atroa, and it was at Atroa that he died in peace.

The distich above the life turns on the epithet: that out of the thorns of the Jews, the divine Constantine flowers even in death. The calendar gives him no year at all, no emperor, and no hymn, and marks the feast fixed. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence by which saint.gr marks an entry it will not vouch for.

The Охридски пролог keeps him on 26. децембар ст. ст., „Преподобни Константин Синадски.“ — Constantine of Synada. As a Jew he turned to Christianity, it says. At his baptism he set a cross upon his own head, and a wonderworking imprint of the cross stayed there on his head until his death. He was famous for his fasting and for many wonders, and seven years beforehand he foretold the day of his own death.

«Скончао у Цариграду у VII веку» — he ended at Constantinople, in the seventh century. That century is the first date this folder has carried; the place is another matter, the Greek calendar having him die in peace at Atroa after preaching through Olympus, Myra of Lycia, Cyprus and Attaleia. The two are left standing side by side here, and neither is claimed as his.
