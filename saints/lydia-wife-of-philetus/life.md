# Lydia, wife of Philetus

Lydia suffered and received the martyr's crown together with her husband, [Philetus](/saints/philetus-the-senator) of the senate, and with their four sons: Macedon, Theoprepius, Cronides the commentarisius and Amphilochius the duke. This was in the time of the emperor Hadrian, and it came upon them for their faith in Christ and because they worshipped God without ceasing.

They were tied to a pillar and beaten with swords and then thrown into prison — Lydia and the first three sons joining Philetus on the road to it, after he had been brought before the emperor alone. Afterwards they were put to terrible torments, being thrown into great vessels of boiling oil mixed with resin; but by the power of God they were left unharmed. Seeing this, the emperor was ashamed; and the saints, after some time which they spent in prayer, departed this life.

doxologia.ro's calendar gives them no year, and no troparion, and says only that they suffered under Hadrian.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and inside the same one clause, «Λυδία σύζυγος αυτού», Lydia his wife. saint.gr names the whole company — the senator, his wife, their two sons [Theoprepius](/saints/theoprepius-son-of-philetus) and [Macedon](/saints/macedon-son-of-philetus), [Amphilochius](/saints/amphilochius-the-duke) the duke and [Cronides](/saints/cronides-the-commentarisius) the commentarisius — and sets their trial in a persecution of the year 125, under the emperor Hadrian. Mother, father and sons were seized together and required to deny Christ, and all of them kept the confession; then, because the accusers could not stand against the wisdom of [Philetus](/saints/philetus-the-senator), the family was handed to the duke of Slavonia, who hung husband and wife on a tree and flayed them. The commentarisius believed at the sight of it and went to prison with them; angels came to them in the night as they sang; the next day the cauldron of oil and resin went cold at the sign of the Cross, and the duke believed and threw himself in. The page's last sentence has the emperor set the saints free when he heard they had been kept safe, so that they ended their lives praying, and its couplet for the couple says the same of them both: «Ὥσπερ Φιλητοῦ καὶ Λυδίας σάρξ μία, Οὕτως ἓν αὐτῶν καὶ μετ' εἰρήνης τέλος» — as Philetus and Lydia were one flesh, so one also was their end, and that in peace.
