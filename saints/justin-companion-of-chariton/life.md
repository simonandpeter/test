# Justin, companion of Chariton

When the idolaters had published through every land and city that decree against the Christians, that everyone who believes in Christ should be forced to sacrifice to the idols, there were seized — so doxologia's life begins — the holy martyrs Justin, [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), Euelpistus, Hierax, Paeon and [Valerian](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin), and a woman named Charito. They were brought to Rome before Rusticus, the prefect of the city, and set on trial. Submit to the gods and to the emperors' commands, the prefect told Justin, and you will not be condemned. No one can ever be condemned, Justin answered, who keeps the commands of our Saviour Jesus Christ.

Asked what teaching he held, he said he had laboured at all the outward learning and, having tried every philosophy, had joined himself at last to the true teaching of the Christians. Asked what its doctrines were, he said: to know one God, the Maker and Fashioner of all things visible and invisible, and to confess our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God, foretold by the prophets, who is to come and judge the race of men. Asked where the Christians gathered, he said that each goes where he can and where he wishes, for the God of the Christians is not contained by place but fills earth and heaven.

Pressed again for the place, he said he had been lodging near the house of a man called Martin, by the bath called Timiotini, and that this was his second time in the city of Rome; he knew no other place, and whoever wished to come to him he received and gave a share in the true teaching. Then the prefect turned to the rest. Chariton said: with God's help, I too am a Christian. The woman Charito said: by the grace of God I am a Christian. Euelpistus said he was a slave of the emperor, but a Christian set free by Christ Himself, and a sharer by His mercy in the same hope as the others.

Hierax said that he too honoured and worshipped the same God. Did Justin make you Christians, the prefect asked; I have been a Christian and I shall be, he answered. Paeon said: I also am a Christian, and I had this good confession from my parents. Euelpistus said that he had been taught by his parents too, but that hearing Justin's words he was strengthened further in the faith, and that his parents were in Cappadocia. Hierax said his true father was Christ and his mother the faith by which they believe in Him, that his earthly parents were dead, and that he had come there out of Iconium in Phrygia. Valerian said: I also am a Christian, for I honour and worship the one true God, and your gods I despise.

The prefect turned back to Justin and asked whether, wounded from the head over all his body, he expected for that to go up to heaven. Justin said he looked for the same reward from his Lord that is kept for those who guard Christ's teaching, if he endured the torment; and asked whether he merely supposed it, he said he did not suppose but knew it firmly and hoped it without doubt. Gather together, said Rusticus, and all of you sacrifice to the gods. No one with right understanding, Justin answered, is willing to fall away from the right faith into error and lawlessness.

If you will not obey our commands, said the prefect, you will suffer torments without any mercy. We truly desire to endure torments for our Lord Jesus Christ and so be saved, Justin answered, for they win us salvation and boldness at His dread judgement, before which the whole world will stand. The others said the same, and added: do quickly what you mean to do, for we are Christians and will not sacrifice to idols. Rusticus gave sentence that those who would not sacrifice and would not obey the imperial command should be beaten with rods and put to death by beheading, as the laws of the Romans command. They were led out to the usual place, beaten, and cut down with the axe; and afterwards some of the faithful took their honoured bodies secretly and buried them in a worthy place.

This Justin is not [the philosopher of the same name](/saints/justin-the-philosopher) whom the same day's calendar prints a line above him and gives a life of his own; doxologia keeps the two on separate pages, and prints a single troparion for both of them and for the martyrs with them. Of the company the life names, doxologia's calendar prints a line of its own only for Justin, Chariton and Valerian, so Euelpistus, Hierax, Paeon and Charito are named here and have no folder. The life gives no year, and none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and names eight where the Romanian named four. These, saint.gr says, received the martyrs' crowns together with [Justin the philosopher and apologist](/saints/justin-the-philosopher), whom the same calendar keeps on this day as a separate entry, in the persecution against the Christians under Marcus Aurelius. They belonged to the brave phalanx of the Christians of Rome, where only genuine Christian courage could keep a man from being dismayed by the power of the idol-worship that the palace and the army upheld; and having confessed Christ repeatedly they were condemned to die by beheading, and all of them underwent it untroubled and joyful. The four the Greek adds are [Charito](/saints/charito) the virgin, [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june) and [Paion](/saints/paion). Over Justus and Justin together it prints a distich: the sword had not yet cut Justus down, and Justin was already bowing his head.
