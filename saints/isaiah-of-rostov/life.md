# Isaiah of Rostov

saint.gr says Isaiah was born in the land of Kyiv to noble and God-loving parents who brought him up as a Christian. From his youth he loved Christ and despised the pleasures of the world, and he came to the monastery of the Caves to become a monk. The abbot then was Saint [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves), who saw with his gifted mind what the young man would become and clothed him in the monastic habit; and from then on, the page says, Isaiah gave himself «ψυχὴ τε καὶ σώματι» to Christ the Bridegroom and began a severe ascetic life.

He was simple, humble, obedient, free of avarice and full of love for people. Because a city set on a hill cannot be hid, the fame of his virtue spread through the whole country and reached the ears of the great prince Izyaslav Yaroslavich, who began to press Theodosius to give his blessing for Isaiah to be made abbot of the monastery of Saint Demetrius, the blessed Varlaam having fallen asleep. Theodosius was told inwardly by the Holy Spirit that it was God's will, gave his consent, and Isaiah, unwilling to disobey, took up the heavy burden with pain.

Neither his manner of life nor his humble mind was altered by the office. His thought stayed fixed on the memory of God and of death, of the judgement and of the kingdom of heaven, and he went on with his labours more zealously than before, asking nothing of his monks that he had not first done himself. Then, after the falling asleep of Leontius, bishop of Rostov, Isaiah was raised by the common counsel of God and of men to be bishop of that province.

In Rostov he found many Christians newly baptised and unsteady in the faith, holding to old pagan customs and sinning gravely out of ignorance, and he set himself to a hard pastoral labour. He travelled without ceasing through the towns and villages of Rostov and Suzdal, catechising, preaching and teaching; where he found idols or pagan temples still standing he ordered them pulled down or burnt, and then taught the people the Orthodox faith in the Trinity. Those of the unbaptised Russians who believed he baptised, and those whom catechism and preaching did not persuade were persuaded by the wonders God worked through him. He was a comfort to the sorrowing, a feeder of the hungry, a protector of widows and orphans and a defender of the wronged, and he fell asleep in peace in 1090.
