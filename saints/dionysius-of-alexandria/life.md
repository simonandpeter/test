# Dionysius of Alexandria

Dionysius was bishop of Alexandria, the Greek synaxarion says, and was martyred in the reign of Valerian, in 254. The prefect Aemilian tried in vain to draw him away from the Christian faith with promises, threats and punishments, for Dionysius was an athlete of Christ already proven in the persecution under Decius. With him were gathered eight of his disciples, in every way worthy of their teacher in the fervour of their piety and the firmness of their faith; all of them were bound and shut in a narrow place, harshly tortured there, and so gave up their holy souls to God.

The entry names six of the eight: [Faustus](/saints/faustus-disciple-of-dionysius-of-alexandria), [Gaius](/saints/gaius-disciple-of-dionysius-of-alexandria), [Peter](/saints/peter-disciple-of-dionysius-of-alexandria), [Paul](/saints/paul-disciple-of-dionysius-of-alexandria), [Eusebius](/saints/eusebius-disciple-of-dionysius-of-alexandria) and [Chaeremon](/saints/chaeremon-disciple-of-dionysius-of-alexandria).

Its verse has Dionysius, with an eightfold company of fellow athletes, pass from the darkness and dwell in the place of light. The Greek calendar keeps them on 3 October, and the entry says that the memory of Eusebius and Chaeremon, with Faustus and Gaius, is repeated on 4 October. The two disciples it does not name have no folder.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him second on this day — the Serbian church and the Russian keep him on 5 October, where the Greek keeps him on 3 October — and it is the only one of the three to tell his life rather than his death. He was born at Alexandria of notable heathen parents and brought up on Greek philosophy, and afterwards studied under Origen. As a young man he read the epistles of the apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), believed in Christ, and was baptised by Demetrius, then bishop of Alexandria. In 247 he became bishop of that same city.

He served God and God's people as a true shepherd, the entry says, and under very hard circumstances: outside, the church was persecuted by the heathen, and inside it was torn by heretics; and on top of that the plague struck and wore the people down for several years. Three years he lived outside Alexandria, hidden by the faithful so that he should not perish before his time, and in those three years he wrote many epistles and other compositions to his flock, teaching it and giving it courage to hold to Orthodoxy.

Among his writings are several canons which the Church received, and his letter against Novatian is likewise reckoned a canonical letter. He governed the church seventeen years and reposed in 265.

That last is a disagreement, twice over, and both halves of it stand. The Prologue has him **repose** where the Greek entry has him **martyred** — «μαρτύρησε όταν αυτοκράτορας ήταν ο Ουαλεριανός», under Valerian, with the six disciples whose folders this one is tied to. And its year, which the Russian day line's «(264-265)» agrees with and which its own arithmetic reaches from 247 plus seventeen years, is a decade later than the Greek's 254. The recorded interval now holds both and prefers neither.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 3 Οκτωβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2582/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026; the Russian church calendar's [5 October](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261005.html), read 4 October 2026; and the Serbian church's [Ohrid Prologue for the Julian 5 October](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-10-18&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
