# Demetrius of Moscow

saint.gr says that Demetrius Ivanovich was born at Moscow in 1581, the son of Tsar Ivan IV the Terrible by his seventh marriage, and that the title of tsarevich, heir to the throne, was given him because his elder brother Theodore was feared likely to die childless. Theodore, when he came to the throne, left all power in the hands of his brother-in-law Boris Godunov, who, ambitious to seize the throne himself, resolved to be rid of Demetrius; and so he confined the young tsar at Uglich together with his mother Maria Theodorovna.

The young Demetrius had raised great hopes among the Russians, the page says, and they were lost by his sudden death. His death, in 1591, remained mysterious, and there is disagreement among historians whether it came about by accident or by crime; but history has marked Godunov as the moral author of the murder.

On 3 June 1606 his relics, which had been brought to Moscow in order to expose the imposture of the false Demetrius, were found incorrupt and worked many wonders, especially in the healing of children; and the Church, the page adds, keeps the translation of those relics on 3 June.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 3 June, and that is this book’s own day for him rather than a second one: its Julian 15 May, the menologion day the Greek calendar keeps him on, prints three other saints and does not name him at all — the three are named in the attestation note and not here, because sharing a day, or not sharing one, is no relation. The entry is five lines, and its first is the one the Greek page would not write: he was treacherously murdered by the power-hungry Boris Godunov, in the eighth year of his life, in 1591, in the town of Uglich. The Greek page calls the death mysterious, says historians disagree whether it was accident or crime, and goes no further than to call Godunov its moral author.

What follows is in neither other account. After his death he appeared to a monk and foretold that a false Demetrius would arise who would be the undoing of Godunov his murderer, and so it afterwards fell out. Countless wonders happened at the boy’s grave. After fifteen years his relics were uncovered whole and incorrupt, carried to Moscow with ceremony, and laid in the church of the Holy Archangel Michael, which the Greek page does not name — that page says only that they were brought to Moscow to show up the impostor, were found incorrupt and healed children above all.

On the year of the murder the two books agree and nothing is widened. On his age they do not: this folder holds a birth of 1581 off the Greek page, which would make him in his tenth year and not his eighth, and a length of life is not a source for a year, so nothing has been derived from the eighth and the dates stand as they were. The rank word of the Serbian heading is the plain martyr, which this folder had no rank at all to set against it.

The Russian calendar tells it as a crime and names the men. It opens with a birth on 19 October 1582, a year later than the date recorded above, and makes him a son of the tsar Иоанн Грозный; under his brother Феодор Иоаннович, with Борис Годунов governing in fact, the boy and his mother the tsaritsa Мария Феодоровна were sent away from court to Uglich. The page has Годунов move against the heir as against a personal enemy: first the rumour that he was no lawful son, then the tale that he had his father's harshness, then poison, which it says did the child no harm. On the Saturday of 15 May 1591, while he was out in the yard with his nurse, three men sent for the purpose — Осип Волхвов, Данило Битяговский and Никита Качалов — cut him down. He was buried at Uglich in the palace church of the Transfiguration, where the page says healings followed in number and those of the eyes most often of all, and on 3 July 1606 his relics were found incorrupt. A second reading on the same page follows the translation to Moscow in that year, undertaken, in the words it gives the tsar Василий Шуйский, to stop the mouths of those who said the boy had escaped alive — a pretender was then claiming to be him. The relics were laid in the Archangel cathedral of the Kremlin, and the three feasts the calendar keeps were settled then: his birth, his killing and that translation.
