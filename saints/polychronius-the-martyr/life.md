# Polychronius

Polychronius was born in the province the Romanian life calls Gamfanita, the son of a ploughman named Vardenie, who taught him his letters and sent him to school with the other children. Water was far from the house, and at the boy's prayer a well of very good water sprang up in his father's yard; the life says he had great understanding and great self-restraint even then.

Grown, he joined the men who worked the vines and went to Constantinople. He worked with the other labourers, but ate and drank only every second or third day, so that the owner of the vineyard marvelled at him, gave him a sum of gold and sent him home with the words: go to your house and pray to God for me. The man kept the saint's hoe for love of the faith, and the life says many miracles were worked through it.

With the gold Polychronius built a church. He was present at the Council held at Nicaea as a reader, and was afterwards made deacon and priest. When the great Constantine died the teaching of Arius began to take hold of many, and Polychronius held firmly to the right faith and worked always to strengthen it. The heretics, eaten by envy, found him standing at the altar, fell on him without warning and cut him down with their swords, mingling his blood with the holy blood, and so sent him against their own will as a sacrifice to God.
