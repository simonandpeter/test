# Eustathius I

saint.gr's notice for him is one sentence: Saint Eustathius I was Archbishop of Serbia, and he fell asleep in peace in the year 1286. The day's line writes the see «Αρχιεπίσκοπος Ζερβών» and the notice behind it «Αρχιεπίσκοπος Σερβίας»; both spellings are the page's, and the see recorded here is the one the notice gives.

Beyond that the page prints only a note on the name, that Eustathius means the man steady in a decision, the undisturbed, the settled character, and a list of three other bearers of it who are not saints. There is no birthplace, no account of his years in the see, no hymn and no manner of death, and nothing is supplied here.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 4 января old style and holds two readings of him, which agree. He lived in the second half of the thirteenth century, under the Serbian king Stephen Uroš II Milutin, who ruled from 1262 to 1320. He was born in the region of Budimlja to a devout Christian family, was given over by his parents to the learning of books, and studied the Holy Scriptures with particular diligence. He was tonsured in the monastery of the Archangel Michael in the Zeta country, in Montenegro, and led a severe ascetic life, giving away to the poor his appointed share of food and clothing.

After a pilgrimage to Jerusalem and the Lord's tomb he settled on the Holy Mountain, in the Serbian monastery of the Most Holy Mother of God at Hilandar, which the readings say was built by Stephen Nemanja — Symeon in the monastic life — and by his son Sava. There he gave himself for many years to the monastic struggle and was raised to be abbot of the house. Some years later he was ordained bishop of the diocese of Zeta and returned to his own country.

On the death of the archbishop Joannicius a council of bishops, abbots and nobles chose him for the archiepiscopal throne of Sava. He governed the Serbian Church seven years and died in peace. He was buried in the church of the Saviour at Žiča, and many wonders, signs and healings were worked at his tomb; afterwards his incorrupt and wonderworking relics were carried to Peć and laid in the archiepiscopal cathedral of the holy apostles Peter and Paul.
