# Gabriel (2 February)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its page for him prints the line «Sfântul Mucenic Gavriil» over the date, 2 Februarie, with an icon and the readings appointed for a martyr — the Apostle from the second letter to Timothy and the Gospel from John — and nothing besides: no homeland, no century, no account of his suffering, and no troparion. The rank the calendar gives him is mucenic, martyr.

He is kept on the day of the Meeting of the Lord, together with two other martyrs the same calendar names and leaves equally unwritten, Agatodor and Iordan. Whether he is one of the martyrs of that name whom other calendars keep on other days is a question this page does not answer.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and calls him «ο Οσιομάρτυρας ο εν Κωνσταντινουπόλει», the venerable-martyr in Constantinople, and where doxologia left him unwritten saint.gr gives him a life. He came from Allone in Prokonnesos, became a monk, and served as preacher of the parish of the Patriarchate of Constantinople. Accused of insulting the Muslim religion, he was imprisoned and tortured at Constantinople; he stayed firm in his faith, refused to become a Muslim, and was beheaded on 2 February 1676. The Turks threw his body into the sea. Ioannes Karyophylles wrote his martyrdom, and the page prints the longer account St Nikodemos gave in the «Νέον Μαρτυρολόγιον» of 1799, which has him jostled in the street by an Agarene, answering back against that man's religion, and telling the kaimakam that he was neither drunk nor out of his wits. saint.gr prints no hymn for him; over him it sets a couplet that cut off for the love of the Lord, Gabriel dances with Gabriel in the vault of heaven.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same menologion day, the Julian 2 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue gives him no entry of his own. It names him in one closing sentence of the entry before his, the new martyr of Trebizond's: the monk Gavril who suffered likewise, канонарх Велике цркве, canonarch of the Great Church at Constantinople, in 1672. Both halves of that sentence disagree with saint.gr, which makes him the preacher of the Patriarchate's parish and has him beheaded in 1676, and neither disagreement is settled here. His recorded year is therefore the interval the two sources leave between them.
