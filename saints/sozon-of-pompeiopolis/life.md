# Sozon of Pompeiopolis

Sozon lived at the end of the third century; his homeland was Lycaonia, and as a pagan he was called Tarasius, taking the name Sozon at baptism. A shepherd by trade, he tried to imitate the gentleness of the sheep he so admired — the other shepherds often troubled and wronged him, and he was always meek with them: it would be a shame to me, he said, to be lower than the sheep I pasture. He studied the Scriptures diligently, and when he met a pagan in the countryside tried to catechise him in Christ. Once he went to Pompeiopolis in Cilicia, where there was a golden idol; the soul of the meek Sozon was roused at the sight, and with great boldness he broke off the statue’s right hand, sold it and gave the proceeds to the poor. The governor Maximian was in uproar and imprisoned many innocent people, and when Sozon learned of it he presented himself to the governor and answered his threats calmly — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here; its verse says he bore up against the torments of the body, looking to the only Saviour of the soul, and died on the seventh, beaten on his bright skin. The Romanian Lives tell the same story of the young Lycaonian in Pompeiopolis, learned day and night in the law of the Lord, at the festival of the golden idol; the Greek and Romanian calendars keep his troparion and kontakion with the day.
