# Philip

Philip was from the same city as [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle) and [Andrew](/saints/andrew-the-first-called). He was a skilled knower of the books of the prophets and of the old Law. It was he who brought Nathanael to Christ, saying: We have found the Christ, of whom Moses wrote in the Law, and the prophets. Come and see.

He preached Christ in the parts of Asia together with the apostle [Bartholomew](/saints/bartholomew-the-apostle) and with Mary his sister. He was killed by the pagans at Hierapolis in Phrygia, being crucified head downwards.

Doxologia prints this as one paragraph of its account of the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles, which the Romanian calendar keeps on 30 June. The page gives no year, so none is recorded here.

The Romanian calendar keeps him again on 14 noiembrie, his own day, and prints a full life there. It begins at Bethsaida on the Sea of Galilee, near Chorazin and Capernaum — the name means the House of the Hunters in Hebrew, the page says, because fishermen lived there — and it makes Bethsaida the birthplace of three apostles, [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle), [Andrew](/saints/andrew-the-first-called) and Philip. Philip, it says, was given as a child to book-learning and grew skilled in the Scriptures, understanding the prophecies of the awaited Messiah well, and burning with love for a Jesus he had not yet seen. It ends at Hierapolis, where he was buried, and where [Bartholomew](/saints/bartholomew-the-apostle) and his sister Mariamne stayed some days after the burial to strengthen the new-founded Church before they went their ways.

The Greek calendar names him again on 30 Ιουνίου, where saint.gr keeps the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles. The page lists all twelve whom the Lord first chose, Judas Iscariot excepted and the twelfth place filled by the apostle elected in the upper room on the eve of Pentecost, and it says plainly that it sketches each man's life at his own feast and not here. The first of the three apolytikia it prints names him in its roll of the twelve. What the synaxis is for, the page says, is the reminder of the unity the twelve had among themselves and of the manner of life that did so much for the world's rebirth in Christ; and the duty it draws from that is that Christians should move in their tracks with the same warm zeal for spreading the Gospel.
