# George the Iberian (2 January)

George — saint.gr prints his name three ways, Γεώργιος or Ζώρζης or Γκιουρτζής — came of Iberian stock. While he was still young a Turk bought him as a slave, circumcised him and gave him the name Salis. After his master's death he stayed on at Mytilene, unmarried, living quietly in a workshop and buying and selling.

The page says that through the abuse he was paraded with in the streets, and the blows of sticks and knives, George stayed unmoved, confessing the faith of his fathers. At last his executioners took him to the place called Parma-Kapou, where he met a martyr's end by hanging.

saint.gr dates it 2 January 1770, and adds that Chrysostomos Papadopoulos puts the martyrdom in 1777; both readings are set down here as the page sets them down. He is not the George the Iberian of Mount Athos whom the calendars keep in May: the Greek calendar gives this man his own day, his own century and a death of his own.
