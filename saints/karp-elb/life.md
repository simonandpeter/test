# Karp (Elb)

Karp Elb was a deacon of the Pühtitsa convent without a theological
education when the rector of the Estonian parish of St Petersburg, the
priest Paul Kulbush — the future hieromartyr Platon, bishop of Reval, and
dean of the Estonian parishes of the diocese, a man most particular in
choosing his clergy — invited him to fill the vacant post of deacon; that a
Pühtitsa deacon without schooling was set in a parish of the capital says
enough about his gifts. Though the city had no lack of educated clergy the
church authorities made him at once teacher of the law of God in two Kolomna
girls’ elementary schools, and he taught besides in the Estonian parish’s
two-class school and, from 1914, in Baron Stieglitz’s model orphanage of two
hundred poor children — four schools at once, two of them, the orphanage and
the Estonian school, without pay; on 13 February 1902 he was given the
silver medal for zeal on the Alexander ribbon, on 6 May 1904 the Synod’s
blessing with its certificate, and in May 1911 he was put forward for the
order of St Anne for his work in popular education. From 25 February 1903 he
was a member and secretary of the commission for building the church of St
Isidore, and often in effect the manager of the whole work: in the rector’s
long absences the deacon was his trusted man, answerable for the raising of
the church and the parish house beside it, and Kulbush, leaving town, relied
wholly on his deacon’s diligence, quickness and industry. In 1917 the parish
lost its rector, consecrated bishop on 31 December for Estonia; the day
before, the parish meeting asked Metropolitan Benjamin (Kazansky) of
Petrograd to ordain their deacon priest and keep him at St Isidore’s as
second priest, and on 8 January 1918 the hieromartyr Benjamin ordained him —
a pastor, then, from the year in which the Church’s service promised neither
gain, nor honour, nor a settled life, with twenty years of slow crushing in
the vice of the godless regime ahead and a bullet at the end. Of those years
little is recorded, but what is recorded is telling: St Isidore’s was one of
the few parishes of the city that never went over to Renovationism, its
priests would not take the new calendar after Patriarch Tikhon’s decree on
the reform of the church reckoning, and neither he nor the rector, the
archpriest Alexander Paklyar, ever thought of leaving the obedience of the
ruling bishop, judging like many others that Metropolitan Sergius’s policy,
a forced and humiliating concession to the godless power, did not touch the
purity of the dogmas of the faith. The surest witness to his faithfulness is
the record of his one interrogation, a rare monument of confession for its
time: arrested for counter-revolutionary propaganda, he answered that he had
conducted none; told he was lying and that the investigation had evidence,
that he confirmed his first answer; that he had never called the Soviet
power a persecutor of the Church, nor a gang of robbers, nor said that the
workers and peasants groaned in want, nor spoken of Soviet aeroplanes for
Spain, nor of innocent people persecuted on the anniversary of Kirov’s
death, nor of Tukhachevsky’s treason; and when the investigation confirmed
every charge and demanded sincere testimony, that he confirmed he was
speaking sincerely and with a whole heart. He seems to have known by
instinct that if he agreed with the interrogator for a moment, the man
trained in lies would twist his words and add what he had never said, and
that to be drawn into conversation with a mind bent only on ruining as many
people as possible was not permitted him. The first interrogation was also
the last: on 24 September 1937 the sentence of death passed by the troika of
the Leningrad NKVD on the archpriest Karp Elb was carried out. He is kept on
11 September of the old style, 24 September, and in the synaxes of the
saints of the St Petersburg metropolia and of the Estonian land.
