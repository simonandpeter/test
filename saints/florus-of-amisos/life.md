# Florus of Amisos

saint.gr's calendar for 18 December keeps the venerable Florus, bishop of Aminsos or Amisos, and dates him by the reigns he lived under: Justin II, which it gives as 565 to 578, Tiberius, 578 to 582, and Maurice, 582 to 602, and under the patriarchs of Constantinople Eutychius and John the Third. His father was called Florus as well and his mother Euphyia.

Because he was educated and knew the divine Scriptures he was marked out at Constantinople and taken on as a secretary in the palace, and soon, for his abilities, he was raised to be a patrician. He married a modest and faithful girl and had one child; but he saw both his wife and his child die, and for that he decided to withdraw to a small estate at the place called Anaploun, having first shared out the whole of his property.

There, as he studied the religious books and prayed, he decided to enter the clergy. He was marked out for his aptness to teach and for his virtue, and he became bishop of Amisos, which the page calls a city of Cappadocia; and he was, it says, a hierarch of those who both speak and do. He gave up his spirit to the Lord in deep old age and in peace.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and prints the same two years the Greek page reckoned from three reigns: he lived in the time of the emperors Justin II and Maurice, 565 to 602. He was of noble family and a noble himself. He left the turmoil and the vanity of the world and withdrew into a monastery for the ascetic struggle, for the salvation of his soul; and afterwards he was chosen bishop of the city of Amisos.

The book puts that city in the region of Cappadocia, which the Greek page does not say and which is the one thing in the two accounts that does not agree; nothing here is renamed on it. Both as an ascetic and as a hierarch he pleased God, it says, and passed over peacefully into the Kingdom of God. It gives no year for his birth and none for his death.
