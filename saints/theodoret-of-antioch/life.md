# Theodoret of Antioch

saint.gr says Theodoret was a presbyter of the church of Antioch in the hard years of the emperor Julian the Apostate, who tried to bring back the religion of the idols. The persecutions of the Christians under Julian reached as far as Antioch, and among the clergy who stayed unshaken in their faith was Theodoret; and for that he was arrested by the eparch of the city.

The eparch was himself called Julian, and the page adds, on the authority of S. Eustratiades, that he was the Apostate's uncle and had formerly been a Christian and a reader of the church of Antioch. He began by flattering the prisoner, praising his learning and his virtues; but when he failed to persuade him to sacrifice and do reverence to the idols, he ordered him beheaded, and so ranked him in the sacred phalanx of the martyrs of the Church.

That is the whole of the notice. The head of the page dates his death to 361; the body gives no year of its own and none for his birth, and it names no relic and prints no hymn. The distich over him says only that the country of Eden has room somewhere for the great Theodoret within it.

The Serbian church keeps him on the eighth of March, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović begins with the building he had charge of. [Constantine](/saints/constantine-the-great) built at Antioch a cathedral church of particular beauty, which the people called the golden church, because of the gilding outside and inside and because of the many vessels of gold and silver in it; and the emperor granted the church great estates for the keeping of its clergy, who were a considerable number. The keeper of those vessels and of all the church's precious things was the presbyter Theodoret, a priest of great faith and of rare piety.

When Julian the Apostate began to reign, although he had been baptised, he renounced Christ and raised a persecution against the Christians. His uncle, also named Julian, came to Antioch and plundered the golden church, and took Theodoret the treasurer to trial and counselled him to renounce Christ. Theodoret not only would not renounce his Lord, but reproached the emperor Julian to his face for falling away from the true faith and turning back to the worship of idols, as a dog to its vomit.

When the wicked judge made water in the golden church out of wantonness, Theodoret foretold him a horrible death, and it overtook him soon. Theodoret was cut down with an axe for Christ; and the judge Julian felt pains in the lower part of his body from the hour he had defiled the church, and worms ate the whole of it away, so that he vomited out his apostate soul in the most dreadful torments. Felix his helper, by Theodoret's prophecy, made an end as quickly, taken with a flow of blood at the mouth as soon as the just man was beheaded. The Prologue puts the beheading in the year 362, one year later than the year this folder already holds from the Greek calendar; the disagreement is recorded and nothing was changed.
