# Theodoret of Antioch

saint.gr says Theodoret was a presbyter of the church of Antioch in the hard years of the emperor Julian the Apostate, who tried to bring back the religion of the idols. The persecutions of the Christians under Julian reached as far as Antioch, and among the clergy who stayed unshaken in their faith was Theodoret; and for that he was arrested by the eparch of the city.

The eparch was himself called Julian, and the page adds, on the authority of S. Eustratiades, that he was the Apostate's uncle and had formerly been a Christian and a reader of the church of Antioch. He began by flattering the prisoner, praising his learning and his virtues; but when he failed to persuade him to sacrifice and do reverence to the idols, he ordered him beheaded, and so ranked him in the sacred phalanx of the martyrs of the Church.

That is the whole of the notice. The head of the page dates his death to 361; the body gives no year of its own and none for his birth, and it names no relic and prints no hymn. The distich over him says only that the country of Eden has room somewhere for the great Theodoret within it.
