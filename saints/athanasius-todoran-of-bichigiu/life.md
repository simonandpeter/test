# Athanasius Todoran of Bichigiu

The Romanian calendar keeps four Martyrs and Confessors of Năsăud together on 12 noiembrie, and doxologia's life for them opens by naming all four and saying that they suffered martyrdom on 12 November 1763: Atanasie Todoran of Bichigiu, Vasile Dumitru of Mocod, Marin Grigore of Zagra and Vasile Oichi of Telciu. They were defenders, the page says, of the ancestral faith and of the Romanian people. Almost the whole of what follows is about the first of them, and the other three are named again only at the execution.

Atanasie Todoran was born at Bichigiu, a village on the valley of the Sălăuța, in a family of free peasants, kin to the priestly family of the Coșbuc of Hordou, the forebears of the poet George Coșbuc. The page says there is rather little information about his life. He was a leading man in his commune, able to read, and had been a judge and a collector of dues in the villages of the Bichigiu and Sălăuța valleys.

It seems that while still young he had been in a regiment stationed somewhere near Vienna, and, his discharge being put off and put off, he deserted and came home. Hunted by the men of the empire, he took refuge in the Țibleș mountains, in Maramureș and in the Land of Chioar, and reached Moldavia, where he served for many years. A fragment of a document of the prince Mihai Racoviță releases from the army an Atanasie aged seventy-four, after thirteen years in the rank of captain, and raises him to the rank of a freeholder.

For the old desertion he was shut up some years in the Coopers' Tower of the fortress of Bistrița. Released, he returned to Bichigiu; and there being no Orthodox priest in the village, he set himself fiercely against his son being communed with unleavened bread or confessed by a uniate priest. The old man buried his boy in the rite of the ancestral faith. His daughter had died years before, while he was a fugitive in the mountains.

In 1761 and 1762 he treated with the government at Vienna, along with other leading men, over the militarisation of twenty-one communes on the Bichigiu, the Sălăuța and the Great Someș. He went to Vienna himself with other Romanians; the pact was concluded and assurances given that on entering the frontier regiment the Romanians of the Năsăud country would have relief. He asked for his own part that the founding of the frontier regiment should not force Romanians to cast off their faith.

Home again and waiting for the fruit of the understanding, he saw that no account had been taken of what the Romanians wanted, and began to oppose the militarisation openly; the people of Năsăud saw plainly that the frontier system was meant to convert them, and that the promised lifting of serfdom was not being applied to them. On 10 May 1763, at Salva, on the plateau called «La mocirla», the battle flags of nine companies of the new frontier regiment were to be blessed and their oaths taken.

General Bukow, sent by the court at Vienna to watch over and press forward the passage to Catholicism in Transylvania, came to receive the oath and bless the standard. As the soldiers stood ready to swear, «moș Tănase Todoran», a hundred and four years old, rode out in front of them and made them a speech. Among other things he said: «De doi ani noi suntem grăniceri și carte n-am primit de la înalta împărăteasă că suntem oameni liberi! - și apoi așa nu vom purta armele, ca sfânta credință să ne-o batjocorească! Jos armele!»

The old man's words bore fruit: the soldiers who were to swear allegiance to Vienna went over to him and threw their arms on the ground in protest. Soon afterwards inquiries were made so that the guilty should be punished, and on 12 November 1763, on the same plateau at Salva, those found guilty of the May revolt were executed. Atanasie Todoran was broken on the wheel from the top downwards and his head bound to a wheel, the sentence saying it was «pentru că i-a reținut pe oameni de la unire și de la înrolarea în statutul militar grăniceresc…».

Martyred with him, by hanging and for the same fault, were [Basil Dumitru of Mocod](/saints/basil-dumitru-of-mocod), [Gregory of Zagra](/saints/gregory-of-zagra) and [Basil Oichi of Telciu](/saints/basil-oichi-of-telciu); another nineteen were beaten with rods and many of those died under the blows. The heads of the martyred were set on poles at the gates of the houses they had lived in, and pieces of their hacked bodies laid at the crossroads. Of Atanasie the page says that he poured out his blood for the ancestral faith and for the rights of the Romanians of Transylvania, and that the memory of him has been kept unbroken among the Orthodox of Năsăud ever since.
