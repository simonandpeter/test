# Ephraim of Rostov

Ephraim was born in Russia towards the end of the fourteenth century, and on 13 April 1427 he was consecrated bishop of the city of Rostov by [Photius](/saints/photius-of-kyiv), metropolitan of Kyiv, whose memory saint.gr puts on 2 July. According to the local chronicles, the page says, he began as soon as he came to the throne to rebuild the Varnitsy monastery of the Trinity and of St Sergius, on the spot where the house had stood of the noble Cyril, father of [Sergius of Radonezh](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh). He showed himself a friend of the monastic polity and a defender of the coenobitic system, and gave his blessing to the founding of monasteries.

He also played a considerable part in the political and military affairs of his time, and particularly in the conflicts that arose between 1430 and 1440 over the primacy among the Russians. Although the district of Galich lay in the canonical jurisdiction of his metropolia, Ephraim was a steady opponent of the princes of Galich and a supporter of the princes of Moscow. In the year 1453, the page says, the prince of Galich, Vasily Kosoy, carried him off and forced him, together with other bishops, to sign letters and encyclicals against his rival, prince Dmitry Shemyaka.

He was a fierce opponent, too, of the union of the Orthodox and Roman churches decided in 1439 at the Council of Ferrara and Florence and signed by Isidore, metropolitan of Moscow, who had taken part in it; for that reason he sat in the extraordinary synod of the Russian bishops of 1440 and 1441, which condemned the Latin-minded Isidore's conduct. In 1448 he presided at the Synod of Moscow at which [Jonah](/saints/jonah-of-moscow), whose memory the page puts on 31 March, was elected metropolitan of Moscow and all the Russias. He fell asleep in peace in the year 1454 and was buried in the cathedral of Rostov; the page adds that the Church honours his memory also on 23 May, the feast of the Synaxis of the Saints of Rostov and Yaroslavl.
