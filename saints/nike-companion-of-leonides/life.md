# Nike, companion of Leonides

saint.gr keeps her on 16 April with [Leonides](/saints/leonides-of-epidaurus), whom it calls the great champion of Epidaurus, and with six other women. They lived in the years of the king Decius, which the page dates 249 to 251, and were of the Peloponnese; they were arrested in the days of the Lord's Resurrection at Troezenia and brought before Venustus, the governor of Corinth. They would not move from their faith, and the governor ordered Leonides torn and the whole company thrown into the sea.

On the road they sang psalms, and then the women were bound with stones and cast into the water with him, one day before holy Pascha, that is on Great Saturday. The page gives her a couplet shared with [Galene](/saints/galene-companion-of-leonides), and it turns on both their names: «Βυθῷ Γαληνὴ καὶ Νίκη βεβλημέναι, Νίκην ἐφεῦρον καὶ γαλήνην ἐκ σάλου» — cast into the deep, Galene and Nike found victory and calm out of the swell. Nothing else is printed of her.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the whole company on the same day as the Greek calendar and tells only the end of it, in four lines, and tells it as a wonder the Greek page has nothing of. They were thrown into the sea, and the sea would not receive them: they walked upon it as upon dry land and sang to God that they had run one course while an army pursued them, and had not denied Him, and that He should save their spirit. The pagans who saw it marvelled at first; then they tied stones about their necks and threw them into the deep of the sea a second time, and drowned them. That song is the entry's own narrative of what the martyrs sang and not a hymn of the Church, and the Prologue prints no troparion and no kontakion.

The entry closes with a year, 281, and the year is a disagreement rather than an addition. saint.gr, the source the rest of this folder comes from, puts the company in the years of the king Decius and dates that reign 249 to 251; the Prologue puts their death thirty years later. Nothing is chosen between them: this folder held no date at all before, and it now carries the whole of the disagreement as one interval, 249 to 281, with both readings named in its note. The Prologue has nothing of Troezenia, nothing of the governor the Greek page names, and nothing of the Great Saturday it sets the death on.

**The Russian calendar gives the company a year the Greek does not, and sets the manner of the death out in full.** Leonid and the holy women with him suffered at Corinth in the year 258, it says. They were thrown into the sea, and they did not drown but walked upon the water as upon dry land, singing spiritual hymns; the tormentors came after them in a ship, hung stones about their necks and drowned them. That is the whole of the calendar's own life for the company, and it is four sentences; the Greek page this folder was first written from tells the trial before the governor of Corinth at greater length and the drowning in two lines, and the two accounts agree in everything they both say.

Where they do not agree is the year: the Russian line and life both say 258, and the Greek sets the martyrdom in the reign of Decius, 249 to 251. This folder's interval already held both, so nothing was changed. The Russian line also names a ninth woman inside the company whom the Greek keeps beside it rather than in it, and closes with «и иных» — and others — who are not named.
