# Quintus of Phrygia

saint.gr says Quintus lived in the third century, in the years of the emperor whom it calls iron-handed Aurelian, and came of Phrygia. He had been an idolater before, but with a love of the truth and a longing for virtue, and so he found it no hard thing to embrace the Christian faith when he heard its teaching and saw the virtues of those who held it.

Once he went to a village called Aiolis, or Neolis, and was handing out alms to the poor when the governor Rufus arrested him; and when Rufus ordered him to sacrifice to the idols, a demon took hold of the governor there and then. The saint prayed to God and the governor was freed of it, and Rufus not only let Quintus go but gave him many gifts for the benefit done him. Afterwards he went to another city, Kyme, where the idolaters forced him into a temple to make him sacrifice; and because an earthquake came at the saint's prayer and the temple of the idols fell down, they let him go in terror.

Forty days later the archon Clearchus, a fanatical idolater, seized him and ordered the martyr's legs crushed; and when it was done they were immediately made whole and sound again by the power of Christ. After that Quintus went about various cities and villages for ten whole years, curing every sickness and helping the poor, and with such wonders and works pleasing to God he gave up his blessed soul to the Lord. The page gives him no year, no relic and no hymn, and it adds that his memory is kept again on the second of July.
