# Mardarije of Libertyville

Mardarije was born at Podgorica on 22 December 1889, the son of Pero and Jela. He finished elementary school at Cetinje and began the lycée there, going on with it at Belgrade, and from there withdrew to the monastery of Studenica. In 1906, with the blessing of Bishop Sava (Barać) of Žiča, he received the monastic habit and was ordained deacon. He finished the seminary at Kishinev, where he published a collection of sermons, and went from there to Saint Petersburg, graduating from the Theological Academy in 1916.

In 1917 he was sent to America and appointed administrator of the Serbian Orthodox diocese for America and Canada, with its seat at Chicago. He built a monastery at Libertyville, Illinois, and in 1925 was elected the first bishop of the Serbian Orthodox Church for America. Gravely ill though he was, doxologia's page says, he was a tireless missionary.

He went to the Lord after ten years of pastorate, on 12 December 1935, and was buried at his own foundation, the monastery of Saint Sava at Libertyville. The Serbian Church canonised him in 2015, and his relics were found intact in 2017.

The Russian calendar keeps him too, on the same day under the old reckoning, and marks the line as a Serbian commemoration: «Свт. Мардария (1935) (Серб.).» Its name page gives his surname in Cyrillic, «Мардарий (Ускокович)», and the one day; it carries no life for him, and the day page carries no hymn under his name. The year it prints is the year the Romanian page already gave.

This folder’s serbian row stands, from 8 October 2026, on the Julian 29 November — and it stands on a second-hand witness, which is worth saying plainly. The Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, out of which the whole Serbian side of this corpus is read, cannot name him: its day for 29. новембар ст. ст. carries five entries, a martyr with a company of three hundred and seventy, a novice of Sinai, a bishop of Corinth, a king of the Armenians and a senator of Rome, and not one of them is he; the book is eighty years older than the glorification of 2015. What the row rests on instead is the Russian calendar’s own marking of its line for that day as «(Серб.)», a commemoration of the Serbian church, together with the Romanian page’s statement that the Serbian Church canonised him. The Serbian Patriarchate’s own calendar has not been read for him, here or anywhere in this corpus, and that is the page that would put the matter first-hand.
