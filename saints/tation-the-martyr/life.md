# Tation

Tation came from Mantineion, in the metropolis of Claudiopolis in the province of Honorias. He was arrested as a Christian and brought before the governor Urbanus, who asked whether he persisted in confessing Christ; he answered boldly that he would confess him to his last breath.

They beat him savagely and tore him with iron claws, and then dragged him through Claudiopolis until he died, after he had first made the sign of the cross. The Romanian Lives give the same outline.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep him on 24 August (Julian): he lived in Bithynia — from Mantineum near Claudiopolis, the Prologue says — and suffered under Diocletian; when the persecutors learned that he believed in Christ they seized him and brought him to Claudiopolis before the governor Urban, who many times pressed him to deny Christ, imprisoned him and gave him to various tortures. Beaten with rods and dragged outside the city to execution, he signed himself with the cross and died on the way, in 305 — buried alive in the earth after great torments, the Prologue says.
