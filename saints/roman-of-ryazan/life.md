# Roman of Ryazan

Roman came of a line of princes who, under the Tatar yoke, were known as defenders of the Christian faith and of their country; both his grandfathers, the readings say, died for it in the battle with Batu. Brought up in love of the holy faith — the second reading adds that he lived in tears and prayers — and of his homeland, he did all he could for his ruined and oppressed subjects and defended them against the violence and the plundering of the khan's baskaks, the collectors of tribute.

The baskaks came to hate him and slandered him before the khan Mengu-Timur, saying that he had spoken against the khan's religion. Summoned to the Horde, he confessed himself firmly a Christian. The second reading has the khan set him a choice, a martyr's death or the Tatar faith, and his answer that a Christian cannot exchange the true faith for a false one.

For that firmness he was tortured. The second reading lists what was done: his tongue cut out, his eyes put out, his ears and lips cut off, his hands and feet struck off, the skin flayed from his head, and his head, when it had been cut off, set on a spear. This was in 1270.

His veneration began at once after his death. The chronicle says of him, in the reading's own words, *«Купи себе страстию Царствие Небесное и венец прият от руки Господней со сродником своим великим князем Черниговским Михаилом Всеволодовичем, пострадавши по Христе за Православную христианскую веру»*. Since 1854 a procession and a service of intercession have been kept at Ryazan on his day, and in 1861 a church was consecrated there in his honour.
