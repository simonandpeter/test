# Bacchus, companion of Sergius

Bacchus and [Sergius](/saints/sergius-companion-of-bacchus) were Romans of senatorial rank and the first men at the court of the emperor Maximian, who loved them for their skill and their courage in war and let nobody approach him except through them. They were Christians and kept it hidden, until men who envied the emperor's love for them brought the charge. Tested by a sacrifice they would not enter, the two were stripped of the marks of their rank, dressed in women's clothes, collared with iron and paraded through the city.

When neither argument nor the promise of greater honour moved them, Maximian sent them east to the governor Antiochus, who owed his office to their petition and was known through the empire for his cruelty to Christians. Antiochus heard them out and grew angry. He had Sergius thrown into prison, and Bacchus stripped, stretched on the ground and beaten without mercy, the servants relieving one another as they tired; the Romanian life says the flesh fell from his bones, and that under that beating he gave up his soul into the hands of the Lord.

Faithful people who were hiding from the persecution in the ravines and caves outside the city came out at night, took his body and buried it with honour in one of the caves where they themselves lay hid. Sergius, hearing in prison that his friend had died under torture, wept for the parting from him, and was himself beheaded a few days later at Rosaf.

The Greek calendar keeps the two on the same day, 7 October, and fills the account out. saint.gr says that they served in the military ranks of the emperor Maximian, that they were marked out both by great courage in the field and by sober living, and that for it the emperor gave them the dignities of primicerius of the school of the Kentelioi and of secunducerius. When he learned that his two chosen soldiers were Christians he would not believe it, and arranged sacrifices in an idol temple and summoned them to be present; they refused and confessed Christ, and he had the insignia of their offices stripped from them.

They were then mocked, paraded and sent to a harsh duke of the East, Antiochus, who scourged Bacchus to death. To Sergius, whom he had once favoured, he offered his life if he would deny Christ, and Sergius answered with the Apostle's words, «Ἐμοὶ τὸ ζῆν Χριστὸς καὶ τὸ ἀποθανεὶν κέρδος» — to me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain — and was beheaded at once. In 547 the emperor Justinian raised a splendid church to the two martyrs in Constantinople, the one called «Μικρή Αγία Σοφία», and laid their relics in it; saint.gr adds that their skulls are at the monastery of Curtea de Argeș in Romania.

The Russian calendar keeps the two together on its own 7 October and gives them the head of the day, «Мчч. Сергия и Вакха (290-303)», with the day's one rank icon standing immediately before the line — «Совершается служба на шесть», a service of six stichera. Its index of names calls him «Вакх Римлянин, мч.», Bacchus the Roman, and sends his name page and [Sergius](/saints/sergius-companion-of-bacchus)'s to a single life written for the pair. The Russian prints a span of years, 290–303, where neither the Greek nor the Romanian page prints one at all, and this folder now carries it.

The day's troparion, in tone 5, names them both and calls him «Вакше преславне», most glorious Bacchus. Its kontakion is the Greek one in Slavonic, the same text in the same tone, ending «добро и красно еже быти с Богом» — good and fair it is to be with God — where the Greek has «τὸ συνεῖναι Χριστῷ», to be with Christ.
