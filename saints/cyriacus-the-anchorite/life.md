# Cyriacus the Anchorite

Cyriacus was a man who cultivated patience and meekness, the Greek synaxarion says, and that is why he succeeded in the ascetic life. He was born at Corinth in the fifth century, the son of a priest, John; his mother was Eudoxia, and his brother was Peter, archbishop of Corinth. Of a priestly family, then, he went young to Jerusalem and from there to the lavra of Euthymius the Great, who made him a monk and sent him to the ascetic Gerasimus. When Gerasimus died, Cyriacus went back to Euthymius’s lavra, until a disturbance there drove him to the lavra of Souka, where at forty he was ordained priest and put in charge of the vessels.

What marked him among his fellow monks was the calm with which he met them, and all of them took him for an example. At seventy he left Souka too, and went patiently round many monasteries and sketes, living in the strictest asceticism. He died at a hundred and seven. The verse makes him an eater of squill, the bitter sea-onion, who shut his eyes on the twenty-ninth. The Greek calendar keeps him with three apolytikia and a kontakion, one of the apolytikia being the desert-dweller’s common «Τῆς ἐρήμου πολίτης».

The Romanian calendar keeps him on the same day with a troparion, which is that common, and two kontakia, and says of him in a line that however old he grew he loved labours and struggles, standing at the services with zeal, and was never found sitting idle, but either praying or working at something.
