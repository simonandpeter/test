# Processus the Jailer

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps him on the Julian 11 April with his fellow, and what it gives is five sentences and no more. They were jailers at the Roman prison in which the apostles [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle) and [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) were held. Hearing the apostles' words and seeing their wonders, the two men were baptised, and they let the apostles out of the prison.

The apostles went out of Rome; but the Lord met Peter on the road, walking towards the city. Where are you going, Lord? Peter asked him. And the Lord answered that He was going to Rome to be crucified again. Ashamed, the apostles turned back into the city, where they were seized and killed; and with them these two brave martyrs, Processus and [Martinian](/saints/martinian-the-jailer), were beheaded.

That is the whole of the account. The entry gives them no year, no birthplace, no relics and no hymn, and it says nothing of either man apart from the other: the two are named together in its heading and told of together in its body, and nothing in it distinguishes them. No year has been set down here, because none is printed and the apostles' own deaths are not a date this corpus may reason from.

**The Russian calendar keeps the two on the same menologion day and tells the story differently, at four points.** Its line is «Мчч. Прокесса и Мартиниана (ок. 67).» and its life has them pagans and guards of the Mamertine prison at Rome, in which men held for crimes against the state were kept, and Christians counted among them. Watching the Christian prisoners and listening to their preaching, the two came little by little to the knowledge of the true faith; and when the apostle [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle) was shut in that prison they believed firmly, received holy baptism from him, and let him out.

Where the Ohrid Prologue has both apostles in the prison and both let out, this page names Peter alone and says nothing of [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle). Where the Prologue has the two martyrs seized and beheaded with the apostles after the Lord met Peter on the road, this page has them tried by a man of their own service: the head of the prison, Paulinus, learned what they had done and demanded that they deny Christ. They confessed the Christian faith without fear and spat on the golden statue of Jupiter, and Paulinus had them struck on the mouth, and then, seeing how firm they were, had them beaten with iron rods and scorched with fire and thrown into the prison.

A woman of rank and piety named Lucina got in to them there and comforted and heartened them. The punishment of God came on Paulinus soon after: he went blind and died within three days, and it was his son who went to the prefect of the city and demanded that the martyrs be put to death at once. They were beheaded, and the pious Lucina buried their bodies. **And where the Prologue printed no year at all**, this page closes the account «+ ок. 67», about 67, and prints the same year in the day's own line; so the folder, which stood undated because the Serbian entry gave nothing to date it by, now carries that year as the Russian calendar states it.

It still says nothing of this man apart from his fellow. The name link behind his place in the line gives «Прокесс Римский, мч.», Processus the Roman, which is the first form of his name any of the four calendars has printed beyond the bare forename, and it is stored for that reason; but the life names the two together in its heading and tells of them together throughout, and nothing in it distinguishes one from the other.
