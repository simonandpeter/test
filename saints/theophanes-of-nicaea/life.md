# Theophanes of Nicaea

Theophanes was born of right-believing parents who lived in Palestine and vied with one another in the love of strangers. His brother was Theodore, afterwards marked out in the suffering for the holy icons. By their parents’ diligence both were trained in all the wisdom of books and became skilled philosophers; and knowing the inconstancy and vanity of this world they left everything and went to the lavra of Saint Sabbas, where they entered the monastic life and lived in fasting, in prayer and in every good work. Theodore was accounted worthy of the priesthood for the goodness of his life.

Then the unlawful war against the icons arose and troubled the whole Church of God, and many were persecuted and tortured for honouring them. The patriarch of Jerusalem sent these two most wise teachers and defenders of the right faith to the emperor Leo, as lambs before a wolf, to rebuke him for his impiety; and they went, stood before the adversary of God, and rebuked him boldly.

For this they suffered much, not only from that emperor but from those who came after him, Michael the Stammerer and Theophilos: every kind of torment, beatings and prison, hunger and thirst, wounds, exile and many sufferings. For more than twenty years, the page says, from the year eight hundred and seventeen to the year eight hundred and forty-two, the iconoclasts persecuted and tormented them; and in those sufferings his brother died, whose memory is kept on the twenty-seventh day of December. At that sentence the page prints the name Teofil rather than Teodor.

Theophanes lived to see the peace of the Church. Michael the son of Theophilos, taking the sceptre of the Greek empire with his mother Theodora, brought the holy icons back into the Church of God, venerated them, and recalled all the saints from exile with great honour. Theophanes then returned and was set over the church of Nicaea as its metropolitan, ordained by the patriarch Methodios, who had overthrown the heresy of the iconoclasts; the calendar line for the day calls him its bishop.

He composed a canon of hymns for the honouring of the holy icons, and leaving many other canons and songs useful to the Church of Christ he passed from this life to the Lord, and after many painful labours dwells in the rest of heaven. The page gives no year for his birth or his death, and none is supplied here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 11 October, and gives the shape of his life. saint.gr says Theophanes was the brother of Theodore the Branded, whose feast it gives as 27 Δεκεμβρίου, and that he was marked out for his learning in the holy Scriptures and in sacred theology, and for an exact knowledge of the ancient Greek writers. In 838, it says, he buried his brother Theodore with great grief when he died in exile, and afterwards Theophanes himself was exiled to Thessalonica. When the iconoclast emperor Theophilus died and the management of the imperial power passed on, the patriarch Methodius made Theophanes metropolitan of Nicaea; he carried out his pastoral duties with great exactness and died at peace with his conscience, having fulfilled his duties to Christ and the Church. The page records a portion of his relic at the monastery of Prousos in Eurytania.

The Russian calendar keeps him on its own 11 October and describes him where the Greek page named him: «Прп. Феофана Исповедника, творца канонов, еп. Никейского (ок. 850)» — the confessor, the maker of canons, bishop of Nicaea, about 850 — under the second of the day's two plain-service icons. Its first life says he was the younger brother of Theodore the Graptos, kept on 27 December; that the two were excellently educated and were taken above all with philosophy; and that, seeking the knowledge of God, they withdrew to the lavra of St Sabbas, where Theophanes was tonsured and some years later made presbyter.

The brothers became famous as champions of the veneration of icons. They carried out boldly the commission of the patriarch of Jerusalem to go to Constantinople and rebuke the iconoclast emperor Leo the Armenian, whose reign the page dates 813 to 820, and afterwards they rebuked the iconoclast emperors Michael the Stammerer and Theophilus as well. The second life says the two were sent after Leo had driven out the pious emperor Michael I, and that in the exchange of words Leo was put to shame.

They had to bear imprisonment, hunger and heavy torments. The emperor Theophilus had verses insulting the two confessors written on their faces with red-hot needles, which is why they are called the Graptoi, the Written-On; write what you like, the brothers told the emperor, you will read your own writing before the dread judgement. Theodore was put in a prison and died there — about 833, says the first life; about 840, says the second — and Theophanes was sent into exile.

With the restoration of the veneration of icons he was brought back from exile and ordained bishop of Nicaea. He wrote about a hundred and fifty canons, among them a fine canon in defence of the holy icons. He died in peace about the year 850.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 11 October as Theophanes the Branded, a confessor and a writer of canons, born in Arabia of rich and devout parents. With his brother [Theodore](/saints/theodore-brother-of-theophanes), whose day it gives as the twenty-seventh of December, he became a monk in the monastery of Saint Sabbas in Palestine; and because the two of them were very well educated the patriarch Thomas of Jerusalem sent them to the emperor Leo the Armenian, to set out and defend the veneration of the icons before him.

The wicked emperor tormented the two brothers horribly and threw them into prison, and the later iconoclast emperor Theophilus went on tormenting them: to expose them to the mockery of the world he gave orders that some derisive words should be written on their faces. When iconoclasm was over Theophanes was set free and soon consecrated bishop, and he ended in peace. The book gives three numbers: he suffered for the icons for twenty-five years, he wrote a hundred and forty-five canons, and he died in 847 — where the Russian calendar puts his death about 850, so this folder now carries both years and chooses neither. The twenty-five years and the canons are what the book says and are not dates.

The same Охридски пролог keeps him again on 27. децембар ст. ст., not in an entry of his own but inside his brother’s heading: „Преподобни мученик Теодор и Теофан Начертани.“ — the venerable martyr Theodore, and Theophanes, the Graptoi. That is the day his 11. октобар entry sends the reader to, and what it says there it says of the two of them together. They were brothers by birth, of Palestine, very well taught both in worldly wisdom and in the wisdom of the spirit; monks in the house the book calls «обитељи Светог Саве Освећеног» and afterwards presbyters.

They suffered bitterly for the icons in the time of three emperors, «Лава Јерменина, Михаила Валвоса и Теофила». Theophilus beat them with his own hands, and gave orders that mocking verses be drawn with iron across their faces, «због чега су и прозвати Начертани» — for which they were called the Written-on, which is the by-name this folder carries. Both were thrown into prison in the city of Apamea in Bithynia, and there [Theodore](/saints/theodore-brother-of-theophanes) ended of his torments and his wounds.

Theophanes was set free in the time of Theodora and Michael, and was appointed by the patriarch Methodius metropolitan of Nicaea. The entry gives no year of his own end — the one year it prints is his brother’s — so the two years this folder already carries from its other books stand untouched. The word metropolitan is this book’s; the other calendars that make this folder say bishop, and the folder’s own office is left as they had it.
