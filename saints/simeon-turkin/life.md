# Simeon (Turkin)

Simeon was born on 1 February 1870 at Simbukhovo in the Vereya district of
Moscow province, the son of the peasant James Turkin, and finished the
village school; from 1894 to 1897 he served in the army as a
non-commissioned officer, was for a few months a police sergeant at
Naro-Fominsk, and then lived in his own village, for a time as the volost
elder. In 1918 he was a witness of the peasant rising in the Vereya
district: hearing of it he went first to look and then tried to persuade the
peasants to disperse, and when no one listened went home, and on the road
was arrested by a detachment of Latvians on their way to put the rising
down, taken under guard to Vereya, questioned and released. In the twenties
he organised a building gang, took contracts and built houses, and in 1933
he was chosen warden of the Dormition church at Simbukhovo. When the
merciless persecution came at the end of the thirties he and his priest
[Peter Yurkov](/saints/peter-yurkov) were arrested together on 8 September
1937 and put in the Mozhaisk prison, and the duty witnesses said that he
started religious talk with the collective farmers in the village tea-room
to win supporters, threatened those who did not go to church, argued that
under the new constitution the church had large rights and no one could
touch it, and told his building gang that the Soviet power was an unstable
thing and would not last. Questioned, he said he had been warden four years
and naturally dealt with his priest over the work, but they had never
agitated; that there had been talk of the constitution in the tea-room, but
it went like this — a man whose name he did not remember said to him, they
are bringing out a new constitution and we will close your church, and he
answered, now you will not, since they have given freedom and separated the
Church from the state, and we may now preach freely — and nothing more; that
in 1936 they had gathered in the church to settle church business, and
Yurkov had not been there; and that he had never spoken against the Soviet
power and did not admit guilt. On 22 September the troika sentenced priest
and warden to death, and on 23 September 1937 both were shot and buried in
an unknown common grave at Butovo. The life is by the igumen Damascene
(Orlovsky); the Church keeps him on 10 September of the old style, 23
September, and in the synaxes of the new martyrs of Butovo and of Moscow.
