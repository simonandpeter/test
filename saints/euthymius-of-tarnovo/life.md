# Euthymius of Tarnovo

Euthymius lived between the fourteenth and the fifteenth century in Bulgaria. He was born about 1325 to 1330 in Tarnovo, then the Bulgarian capital, of a noble family, perhaps that of the Kamblaks, and he had the fortune of a good biographer out of his own house: Gregory Kamblak, who afterwards became Metropolitan of Kyiv and took part in the Council of Constance, which the page dates 1414 to 1417.

While still fairly young he received the monastic habit at the monastery of the Mother of God the Hodegetria, in the outskirts of the Bulgarian capital. In 1350 he was brought into the monastery that Saint Theodosius of Tarnovo had founded at Kilifarevo, in the same neighbourhood, and by Theodosius himself he was led into the spiritual and hesychast life and advanced in Christ. At that time Theodosius, prophesying his disciple's future, revealed that one day Euthymius would be bound with chains and sent into exile.

On the patriarchal throne, the page says, he carried on the work of revising the texts, wrote letters to various persons of the Orthodox world which became pastoral and teaching writings, and composed lives of saints. He fell asleep in peace in the year 1402. The page prints no hymn for him.
