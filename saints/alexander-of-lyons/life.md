# Alexander of Lyons

saint.gr's calendar for 2 June keeps a company of the martyrs of Lyons and names nine of them. Its whole narrative is one sentence: the holy hieromartyrs Photinos, who was a bishop, and Sanctius the deacon, and the holy martyrs Vettius, Epagathus, Ponticus, Biblis, Attalus, Alexander and Maturus were perfected in martyrdom, together with other martyrs, in the year 177, under the emperor Marcus Aurelius, who reigned from 161 to 180, at Lyons in Gaul.

The page sets each of the nine in a head line of its own, with the same date against each of them, «(; - 177)», and it gathers the rest under one further heading which names nobody — the holy martyrs who bore witness with Saints Photinos and Sanctius. So the nine who are named have a folder each, which is what this corpus does with a synaxis that enumerates its members, and the unnamed have none, because there is nothing to count them by. The page gives no manner of death for any of them, no order of suffering, no place of burial and no hymn.

The other eight of the nine are [Photinos of Lyons](/saints/photinos-of-lyons), [Sanctius of Lyons](/saints/sanctius-of-lyons), [Vettius of Lyons](/saints/vettius-of-lyons), [Epagathus of Lyons](/saints/epagathus-of-lyons), [Ponticus of Lyons](/saints/ponticus-of-lyons), [Biblis of Lyons](/saints/biblis-of-lyons), [Attalus of Lyons](/saints/attalus-of-lyons) and [Maturus of Lyons](/saints/maturus-of-lyons). The same calendar keeps on this day a tenth martyr of Lyons of the same year and the same reign, Alcibiades, and gives him a separate entry and a separate page without numbering him among these nine; his own page wonders whether he is the Alcibiades kept on 16 August. That question belongs to his folder and not to this company.

Of Alexander the page says nothing but his name, his rank of martyr and the year. The heading above his head line is a lexicon entry on the name and its bearers, from Alexander of Macedon downwards, and it is the page's own furniture and not a word about this man.

The Russian calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 24 апреля ст. ст. reads «Мч. Александра Лионского (ок. 177).», the martyr Alexander of Lyons of about the year 177, and the name page behind it prints the title in the nominative, the one day, and nothing more. The year is the one this folder already had.

One thing about that line is worth setting down, and it was not acted on. Two days earlier the same calendar prints «Мч. Епиподия Лионского (ок. 177).» in exactly the same shape — a martyr of Lyons, about the same year, a name page and no reading — and the corpus now keeps a folder for him. The Latin tradition holds the two to have been taken and killed together. But neither of the Russian pages names the other man, and neither gives a word of either story, so no relation was written between the two folders from this reading; it is left as a question for somebody with a source that joins them.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 2 Ιουνίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/06/02/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/4407/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 24 апреля ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260424.html), which names him and gives no life, read 8 October 2026.*
