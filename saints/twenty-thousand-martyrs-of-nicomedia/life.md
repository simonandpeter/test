# The Twenty Thousand Martyrs of Nicomedia

doxologia.ro's line for 28 decembrie names a company and not its members: «Sfinții 20.000 de Mucenici arși în Nicomidia». The life it links tells the story of the burning, and names only some of those who died in that persecution, so this folder is the company and the folder of each man it names is his own. In the second year of Maximian's reign, as the persecution was being prepared, the faith was flourishing at Nicomedia, and the best adorned in word and in life was Chiril, bishop of that Church, by whose preaching the faith had spread as far as the imperial palace, where Maximian was then living.

A multitude of the emperor's closest servants began to turn from the idols to Christ. Learning of it, Maximian meant to raise a persecution at once, but a war against the barbarians stood in his way; so he chose to go first against the enemies outside and beat them, and then to make war within, not on enemies but on the household and the helpers of the empire, that is on the Christians, who strengthened their country with prayer and with right faith. While he was gone out of Nicomedia to that war the light of the faith shone the brighter, and the number of the armies of Christ grew and made itself ready for the contest that was coming.

There was at that time a very beautiful maiden brought up in the imperial palace, named [Domna](/saints/domna-of-nicomedia), whom the emperor had given to the unclean gods for the keeping of her virginity and had made the first priestess of the idols of his palace. By God's providence the book of the Acts of the Apostles and the Epistles of the apostle Paul came into her hands, and reading them she began to be enlightened in her soul: she rejoiced at having found such a spiritual treasure and wondered at the strong faith of the Christians in one God, but grieved that she had spent so long in the darkness of ignorance.

Wishing to know the right faith fully and to have part in it, she called one of the chosen girls, who was a Christian virgin, and guided by her went by night to the bishop Chiril, nobody knowing. He taught her much out of the divine Scripture, signed her with the sign of the cross, and entrusted her to a holy deacon named Agapie, that under his guidance she might prepare herself with fasting and prayer for Baptism; and she did all that was ordered her in secret.

A priest of that Church, [Glycerius](/saints/glycerius-of-nicomedia), wise and hot with zeal, withstood the emperor in the church with courage and shamed him to his face, saying that they did not want his promised gifts nor fear his threats, since everything in the world was to them as a dream; and he reminded him of the thunder and lightning, the hail and the strange rain that had lately killed many of his own and destroyed the fruits of the earth. For that boldness he was arrested, beaten until the flesh fell from him and the bare bones could be seen, and, still confessing, was burned outside the city.

At another pagan festival, when sacrifices were offered to the twelve chief gods in the palace temple, the emperor looked at the servants in their white robes and did not see [Indes](/saints/indes-of-nicomedia) among them; for he had put on poor clothes and shut himself in his house, grieving and weeping over the perdition of the pagans. Brought before the emperor in his clothes of mourning, he was not even questioned about his faith or his life, but was at once ordered to be put in chains.

When Domna could not be found by those who sought her — her abbess Agatia having cut off her hair, dressed her in man's clothes and sent her out of that holy chamber to be as a man among men — the emperor was the angrier, and ordered all the monasteries destroyed and the virgins outraged and shamed; and there began through the city a scattering and a distress like a captivity of barbarians, the stronger of the virgins fleeing to the mountains and the deserts. One of them, the holy virgin Teofila, dragged by shameless soldiers to a house of debauchery, prayed to Christ her Bridegroom to keep her virginity; and the first soldier who came near her was struck with a dreadful trembling and fell dead at her feet, and the second, entering, fell in terror at the sight, so that she came out whole and was received back by the Christian people who were keeping vigil in the church.

Meanwhile Maximian sent his servants to hunt Christians as men hunt hares. One of his nobles, the prefect of Italy, [Dorotheus](/saints/dorotheus-of-nicomedia), most manifest in faith, and two others, [Mardonius](/saints/mardonius-of-nicomedia) and [Migdonius](/saints/migdonius-of-nicomedia), were denounced to the emperor as Christians; and being brought into his court and reproached with ingratitude they kept silence, and let him bark like a dog without order. Then he had their hands and feet stretched out and them beaten without mercy with raw sinews until the sun set, so that the ground was reddened with their blood; and they endured without a murmur, glorifying God in their thought alone, and were bound and thrown into prison.

When the feast of the Nativity of Christ came and all the faithful were gathered in the church, the pagan servants told the emperor not to let the game escape out of the net, but to have the soldiers open the doors of the churches and set the altar of their gods before the door, that the Christians coming out should be made to sacrifice. Maximian swore by the great idols that he had long thought of it himself, and ordered the chief of his nobles to go with a multitude of soldiers, with wood and tow and other things easy to burn, and set them round the Christian church, and to guard the doors well with swords, that not one should escape.

The archdeacon, whose heart was kindled with the fire of God, stood by the altar and asked the people whether they did not know what the three young men had done in Babylon, whose memory they had kept a little before those days and whose unshaken courage in the right faith they had marvelled at — how they stood as in a field of dew and not in the midst of the fire, and made a dance and sang, and called all nature to the praise of God. Since the present time called them to the same lot, he said, they should look at the altar of the Lord and understand that their Lord, the true God, was being sacrificed for them there, and should lay down their own souls for him in that holy place, offering them to him through the fire as a whole burnt offering.

At his words all resolved to die for Christ, and all answered together: «We are Christians, and your lying gods, O emperor, we do not honour». When this was told to Maximian he ordered the fire to be lit at once round the church. In that hour the faithful gathered all the catechumens who were among them and divided them into four parts, so that they might be baptised the quicker; and after Baptism and the anointing with holy Myrrh all communicated in the holy Mysteries.

Then the soldiers, by the emperor's command, set fire to the brushwood round the church, to the tow and to its walls; and the great flame reached the roof and, entering within, consumed everything quickly. The Christian people, burning alive, cried out to God with great joy, sounding the song of the three young men and calling all God's creation to praise; and before they had finished the song they gave their holy souls into the hands of the Lord, and made an acceptable sacrifice to the spotless Lamb who was slain for the world. The number of those burned was about twenty thousand.

Afterwards Domna herself was killed with the sword while she prayed at the tombs of the holy martyrs, and the pagans burned her honoured body with fire; and the hallowed bishop [Anthimus](/saints/anthimus-of-nicomedia), who by his most wise teachings and letters had brought many to the knowledge of God and urged many to suffer, was sought out with care until he was found, and, tortured bitterly and in many ways without being overcome, was beheaded, and so went to the Lord adorned with a double crown, of the high priesthood and of suffering.

So Nicomedia was adorned with the stars of the holy martyrs, twenty thousand in number, with the addition — as the life ends — of «the wonderful Glycerius and the most blessed [Zeno](/saints/zeno-of-nicomedia), with Teofil, Dorotheus, Mardonius, Migdonius, Indes, [Gorgonius](/saints/gorgonius-of-nicomedia), [Peter](/saints/peter-of-nicomedia) and the three holy virgins Agapia, Teofila and the most beautiful Domna»; and the end of the whole company is Saint Anthimus. The day's line gives no year, and the page the site keeps for the company's troparion is an audio recording of the hymn in Greek and prints no text.

The Greek calendar keeps them on the same day, 28 December, and tells the burning over again with one difference. saint.gr says that in the fourth century, under Diocletian and Maximian, the Christians of Nicomedia were numerous enough, and that the bishop [Anthimus](/saints/anthimus-of-nicomedia) — not the Chiril of the Romanian life above — a worthy man and a self-denying one, laboured night and day for the souls of the faithful. That very growth pricked the envy of the idolatrous rulers, who meant to destroy the Church in its largest and most crowded centres, and who therefore planned a general slaughter of the Christians of Nicomedia for Christmas Day itself.

The faithful had gathered and were keeping the feast of the Nativity. As soon as the bishop learned that an army and a mob of idolaters with weapons and clubs had surrounded them, he ordered that the communion of the undefiled Mysteries be given quickly, and then baptised the catechumens, so that they should have sure provision for their eternal salvation. The idolaters set fire to the church, and thousands of the faithful were burned. The page says the number of the Church's members was not lessened by it but multiplied, and it ends on the word of Christ which that day proved: «και πύλαι άδου ου κατισχύσουσιν αυτής».
