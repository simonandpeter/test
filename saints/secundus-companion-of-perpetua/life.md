# Secundus, companion of Perpetua

Secundus was one of the five catechumens arrested with [Perpetua](/saints/perpetua-of-carthage) at Carthage, saint.gr says — four men and one woman, whose eyes she had opened to the light of Christianity. The page names him fourth of the five on its own headings, beside [Satyrus](/saints/satyrus-companion-of-perpetua), [Revocatus](/saints/revocatus-companion-of-perpetua), [Saturninus](/saints/saturninus-companion-of-perpetua) and [Felicitas](/saints/felicitas-of-carthage).

Perpetua, it says, was a married woman and the mother of a small child, marked out not only for her reverence but for her work on behalf of the faith, though she was barely twenty-two; she was denounced in the year 203 to the tribune of her own country, and the six of them were taken together. The men, and so Secundus among them, were killed with knives by the idolatrous mob; Perpetua and Felicitas were set before a wild heifer and torn apart by it.

That is the whole of what the Greek calendar records of him: the name, the number of the company, the year, and the manner of the men's deaths. There is no homeland of his own, no year of birth, no trial and no burial, and the page prints no apolytikion and no kontakion for the six. Its couplet counts them: Perpetua exchanged the five who were slain, being slain with them into a company of six.
