# Theodore the Varangian

doxologia's Russian counterpart, the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery, keeps on 12 July the first martyrs of the Russian land. Theodore was a Varangian of Kyiv — of the Northmen who, the reading says, were merchants and soldiers, who opened the trade roads to Byzantium and the East and made up a large part of the old city's people and of the princes' hired companies. He had served a long time in the Byzantine armies and was baptised there, and on his return he taught the holy faith to his son John.

In 983, after a victory over the Yatvagians, the prince of Kyiv — then still a zealous pagan — wished to offer sacrifice to the idols, and his elders and boyars advised that a youth or a maiden be chosen by lot. The lot fell on John. Theodore refused to give him up: *«Не боги это, а дерево. Нынче есть, а завтра сгниет … Не дам сына моего бесам»*, these are not gods but wood, today here and tomorrow rotten, and I will not give my son to the demons.

The reading says he stood with his son on the gallery of his house, armed, and that the crowd, unable to overcome two trained soldiers in open fight, cut away the posts beneath them and killed them in the wreckage. The place of their burial is unknown. The chronicler of the Caves, writing less than a hundred years later, already counted them among the saints, and the Paterikon's compiler called them the first Russian citizens of the heavenly city.

The two readings the source prints differ on the year. The day's line and the first reading give 983; the second offers both, saying the deaths may belong to the first pagan reaction of 978 and dating them in that case to 12 July 978, or else to the summer of 983. This folder keeps the year its line prints.
