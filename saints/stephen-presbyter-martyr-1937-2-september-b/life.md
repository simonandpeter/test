# Stephen (Yermolin)

Stephen Vasilievich Yermolin was born on 22 May 1891 in the village of
Chasovo to a peasant family, busy from small in the peasant holding, and
finished the primary school; by unconfirmed reports he fought in the First
World War, and in 1919–1922 served in the Red Army as a platoon commander
and assistant company commander, returning after demobilisation to
peasant work. A deeply believing man, he resolved to give himself to the
service of the Lord and in 1926 was ordained; in the mid-thirties he was
priest of the Exaltation church at Nizhny Konets in Syktyvkar, once
attached to the Trinity cathedral of Ust-Sysolsk, and in 1936 on an
anonymous denunciation was prosecuted by the NKVD for “speculation in
church valuables”, and soon acquitted. Knowing he was secretly watched, he
kept up warm relations with the exiled clergy all the same. On 24
February 1937 he was arrested at Syktyvkar in the fabricated case of the
“Sacred Company”; on 13 September 1937 the troika of the Komi NKVD
sentenced him under articles 58-10 and 11 to the highest penalty, and the
sentence was carried out at Syktyvkar on 15 September 1937 — 2 September
of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him, with Bishop
[Herman](/saints/herman-of-vyazniki) and the martyr
[Paul Yelkin](/saints/paul-martyr-1937-2-september). By a decision of 6
October 2001 the Holy Synod numbered him among the saints, in the synaxis
of the new martyrs and confessors of Russia; the Russian calendar keeps him
also in the synaxis of the saints of Komi.
