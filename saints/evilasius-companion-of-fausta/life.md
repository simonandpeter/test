# Evilasius, companion of Fausta

Evilasius was the chief servant of the idols in the palace of the pagan emperor Maximian, and he was eighty years old. When word came to the emperor of the virgin [Fausta](/saints/fausta-of-cyzicus) of Cyzicus, Maximian sent him to find her, with the command that if she would sacrifice to the gods it should go well with her, and if not that he should throw her into the depth of the water. He found her young in years but old in faith and wisdom, brought her to his judgement seat and pressed her to sacrifice, and she refused him, saying she had Christ as her Bridegroom.

He set her to the torments and none of them held: the coffin they tried to saw through wore out six saws and would not burn, and she sang inside it. Then he said to her: "Woman, you frighten me with these wonders. I am eighty years old and have never seen the like; I adjure you by the God you believe in to tell me the whole truth." She told him of the everlasting God who keeps His servants, who was born of the most pure Virgin, came of His own will to the Cross, rose the third day and will come again to judge the living and the dead. Hearing it, Evilasius began to know the power of the living God, was filled with the Holy Spirit, and loosed her from her bonds and set her free.

One of his own servants ran and told the emperor that he had forsaken his master's love and wished to become a Christian, and Maximian in anger sent his eparch [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-fausta) to Cyzicus. Maximus asked him how he had dared to leave the great gods and believe the Christians' folly, and Evilasius answered: "I swear by my own head that you too, if you listen to the maiden Fausta, will quickly know the living God, and will be blessed." The eparch had him hung on a beam and cruelly beaten, and under the beating he cried to Christ to deliver him; and when his ribs were burned with lighted torches he asked Fausta, who stood watching, to pray for him, and she prayed that God would receive him into the fold of His flock.

Afterwards the soldier Eusebius had a great cauldron filled with pitch and brimstone and boiled hard, and threw Evilasius into it together with Fausta, with a great fire beneath; and the two of them stood in it as in a green and cool place, singing and glorifying God, until the fire went out and the cauldron grew cold. Seeing their faith and their patience, the eparch Maximus asked God to receive him with them; and the three of them gave their souls into God's hands on the sixth day of the month of February.
