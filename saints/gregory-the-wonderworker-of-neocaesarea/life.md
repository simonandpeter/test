# Gregory the Wonderworker of Neocaesarea

Doxologia's life gives him as born in the great and glorious city of Neocaesarea, of unbelieving parents, and orphaned in childhood. He was set to Greek learning, and there began to grasp the perfect wisdom, which is the knowledge of the true God; for from the creatures he came to know the Creator, and sought to please Him with a wise life. Hearing the teaching of the Gospel, he became at once its follower, and after his baptism lived out its perfection in purity and poverty.

He kept the purity of his virginity with great abstinence, the page says, and to his blessed end never knew sin of the body. For this he was loved by God and by good men, and hated by the bad: among the crowd of young men studying philosophy and medicine with him there were many, slaves of their passions, who could not bear a youth who surpassed in continence not only the young but the old, and they set out to blacken the good name everybody praised in him.

Once, when he was in company with famous philosophers and the foremost teachers, a harlot put up to it by his fellow students came to him and shamelessly demanded payment for a sin of the body she said she had committed with him. Some who heard it were troubled, believing it true; others did not believe her, knowing his life. But God, the faithful witness in heaven, uncovered the wrong: when she took the unjust payment in her hands a devil fell upon her and tormented her before them all, and she fell to the ground crying out and trembling.

While Gregory was living in the desert, given to the thought of God, the blessed Phaedimus, bishop of Amasea in Cappadocia, learning of him, wanted to draw him out of it for the help of the Church, foreseeing in him a great pillar of the faith. Having the gift of foresight himself, Gregory hid from him, judging himself unworthy of such a rank, and moved from place to place through the desert so that he could not be found. Phaedimus, unable to part the lover of the desert from it, took no account of the distance between them and consecrated him bishop of the Church of Neocaesarea while he was not there, praying: Thou who knowest all things and canst do all things, look this hour upon me and upon Gregory, and accomplish his ordination by Thy grace.

The life that follows is long and is mostly wonders. Once, having gone to the city of Comana for the election of its bishop — where a worthy man was chosen, [Alexander](/saints/alexander-of-comana), who afterwards was found worthy of a martyr's crown — some unbelievers tried on his way home to prove he had not the Spirit of God in him, and were answered.

Coming at last to deep old age and near his blessed end, he asked those standing before him how many unbelievers were left in Neocaesarea. Only seventeen, they said, still held to the worship of idols, and the whole city besides believed in Christ. Then he said: Glory to God, for when I came to Neocaesarea to the bishopric I found only seventeen Christians, the whole city being idolatrous; and now, going to God, I leave as many unbelievers as there were believers then, for now the whole city is Christ's. Saying this he gave up his soul into the hands of God. The page gives no year for anything in his life.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 17 November, and adds his beginnings. saint.gr says Gregory was born about 210 to 215, that he was first called **Theodore**, and that his parents were Greek idolaters of high standing at Neocaesarea in Pontus — a city known in antiquity, it adds, as Kabeiria, Diaspolis and Sebaste, and today as Niksar. After his elementary schooling he set out with his brother for Berytus to study law; the brother the page names Gregory, or, by some hagiological sources, Athenodorus, and sends the reader to 7 November for both of them. But God had other plans.

Passing through Caesarea, Gregory heard the formidable interpreter of the Scriptures, Origen, and was so taken with him that he stayed. The page records parts of his skull at the monastery of Saint Dionysios at Litochoro in Pieria and part of his hand at the Great Meteoron, and it describes the column of Saint Gregory the Wonderworker in Hagia Sophia at Constantinople, with its copper-lined hole which people believe works wonders.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 17 November and prints two lives behind one index, both of which close his years the same way: «Скончался около 266-270 г.» and «Перед его кончиною (+ ок. 266 - 270)». The fuller of the two has him born at Neocaesarea in a pagan family, finding the truth in the Gospel where the thinkers of antiquity could not slake his thirst to know, and going to Alexandria to study under the presbyter Origen, with whom he spent eight years and from whom he received baptism.

In a persecution he led his flock to a far mountain, and when soldiers surrounded it he came out into the open, raised his hands to heaven and bade his deacon do the same, and prayed. The soldiers searched the whole mountain, passed the two of them several times without seeing them, and went back to report that there was nowhere to hide there — nobody was on it, only two trees standing not far from each other. The informer who had betrayed their hiding place was shaken by the wonder, repented and became a fervent Christian. The same page ends by telling that when he came to the see there were seventeen Christians in the city, and at his death seventeen pagans.

The Охридски пролог calls him a man of God and a mighty wonderworker, by-named a second Moses. Born of heathen parents who were notable and rich, he first learned the Hellenic and the Egyptian philosophy, and finding it barren and insufficient turned to Christian teachers and above all to Origen at Alexandria, with whom he studied some years and from whom he received baptism. Pure in soul and body, he wished to consecrate the whole of himself to Christ alone, and withdrew for that into the desert, where he spent a long time in hard ascetic labours.

The report of him went everywhere, and Phaedimus, bishop of Amasea, meant to consecrate him bishop of Caesarea. Gregory saw through the intention and hid about the desert from the men sent for him, so that they should not find him; at last Phaedimus consecrated him by a wonderful means the book does not describe, and he had to accept the office of a chief shepherd. The Most Holy Theotokos appeared to him with [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), and at her command John delivered to him the Symbol of Faith that is known under Gregory's name.

Who will count all the wonders of this second Moses, the entry asks. He commanded evil spirits; he commanded mountains and waters; he healed every torment and sickness; before his persecutors he became invisible; and he saw at a distance not only events but the thoughts of men. He ended his earthly life in the year 270, in deep old age — a year this folder already holds from another book, which is therefore not narrowed by it. The entry closes on a count: when he came to Caesarea as its bishop he found the whole city heathen with only seventeen Christians in it, and when he departed this life he left the whole city Christian with only seventeen heathen in it; and for that, it says, he received the crown of glory from his Lord in the kingdom of heaven.
