# Euphrosyne of Polotsk

saint.gr says she was born about the year 1105 in Russia and called Predislava in the world, the daughter of Svyatoslav George Vseslavovich, prince of Polotsk, niece of the royal prince Vseslav Bryachislavich and cousin of the Byzantine emperor Manuel Comnenus. She lived with her father at the court of her great-uncle Boris Vseslavich in Polotsk, and from infancy, the page says, she heard the Lord's call. So when she reached the age at which princesses were customarily married, twelve, she began to refuse everyone proposed to her, and resolved to live in a monastery her aunt had founded until she should get the bishop's leave to stay at the cathedral church of Holy Sophia.

Because she was very well educated she gave herself to the copying of books, and with what that earned she helped the suffering and the poor. Bishop Elias entrusted to her the church of the Saviour together with the neighbouring district known by the name of Selko, and Boris, her father's elder brother, who died in 1128, was present at the act of handing it over. At once she began to build up a women's monastery, in which her sister Gorislava, or Gradislava, who took the name Eudoxia, and a cousin of hers were to be nuns; her old biographer passed on the record of her spiritual conversation with them and with others who had the monastic calling. Later she laid the foundations for a men's monastery dedicated to the Mother of God.

In the same period, the page says, her family was passing through a dramatic moment, having been attacked by the Polovtsy. Of her repose the page's own head line gives the year 1173. Most of what it prints after that is the history of her relics, which were brought to Polotsk and laid in the monastery of the Saviour of Saint Euphrosyne on the morning of 23 May; removed again after the coming of the Soviets and placed at first in a museum of atheism; taken up by the faithful during the evacuation of August 1941 and put in the church of the Protection of the Mother of God at Vitebsk; and returned at last to the monastery of Polotsk on 23 October 1943.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the same day and tells her beginning and her end differently. It opens by calling her the daughter of Vseslav, prince of Polotsk, where the Greek page makes Vseslav the generation above her and her father Svyatoslav George Vseslavovich; the two books are left to disagree, and no kinship is settled on the strength of either. When her parents wished to betroth her, this book says, she fled away to a women’s monastery and was tonsured there.

Three times an angel of the Lord appeared to her and showed her the place where she was to build a new convent for virgins. She drew her own sister Eudokia into the monastic life, and many other girls of the noble class besides. A kinswoman of hers, Zvenislava, a princess Borisovna by birth, brought all her treasure, her garments and her precious stones, and said that she counted the beauties of this world as nothing; the ornaments made ready for her marriage she gave to the church of the Saviour, wishing to be betrothed to Him instead in a spiritual marriage and to bow her head under His good and light yoke. Euphrosyne tonsured her too, and gave her the name Eupraxia.

In her old age Euphrosyne longed to die in Jerusalem, and prayed God that it might be so; and God heard her prayer, for she did indeed visit Jerusalem, and there she came to her end. She was buried in the monastery of Saint Theodosius on 23 May 1173, the Prologue says — the year the Greek page gives for her repose, and the same day of May that both calendars keep her on.
