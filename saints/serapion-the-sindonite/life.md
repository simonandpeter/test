# Serapion the Sindonite

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him on 7 апреля ст. ст. in four words and, unusually for this calendar, with no year at all: «Прп. Серапиона монаха.» — the venerable Serapion, a monk. The name link behind the line, page 1967, gives what the line withholds, «Серапион Синдонит, Египетский, прп.», and lists two days for him, 7 апреля and 14 мая. One row per church is the schema, so this folder renders on the day it was read from and the May day is recorded here and nowhere else.

He lived in the fifth century in Egypt, and was called the Sindonite because he wore nothing but a coarse linen garment called a sindon. From his youth he lived like a bird of the air, with no shelter, and went several days at a time without eating, having nothing to buy bread with. To a beggar shivering with cold he gave his sindon away and was left half naked.

A Greek philosopher, wanting to test whether he really possessed nothing, once gave him a gold coin and watched what he would do. He went to a bread-seller, took one loaf from him, gave him the gold coin and walked off, attaching no importance to what the coin was worth.

He brought many to the way of salvation by a way of his own. Once he sold himself into slavery to a Greek actor whom he wanted to turn to Christ; and the actor, struck by the example of the righteous man’s holy life, believed and was baptised with his whole household. He begged Serapion to stay with him, not as a slave now but as a teacher and a friend, but the ascetic went away and would not take the money he was offered.

Setting out for Rome he went aboard a ship and paid the sailors nothing. At first they reproached him for it; then, noticing that the old man had eaten nothing for five days, they began to feed him for God’s sake, and so fulfilled the Lord’s commandment. In Rome he went on wandering, passing from house to house, owning nothing, and gathering only spiritual riches for himself and for his neighbour. That is where the page leaves him: it gives no year of death, no burial and no hymn.

**The corpus already holds a Serapion whom the Greek calendar calls ο Σιδώνιος, and this folder is not him.** That one — `serapion-venerable-21-march`, kept by the Romanian, the Greek and the Serbian on 21 March — is the companion of «Антония Великого» who was superior of the Arsinoite houses in the Nitrian desert, whom Palladius and Sozomen call the Great, and who reposed about the year 366. This one is of the fifth century, has no monastery and no disciples, and is known for the sindon, the gold coin and the actor. The two by-names look alike and the two men do not, so a separate folder is made and the difference is stated here rather than assumed.
