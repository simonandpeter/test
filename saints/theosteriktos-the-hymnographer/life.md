# Theosteriktos the Hymnographer

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 17 martie reads «Sfântul Cuvios Teostirict imnograful», and the page it links to carries neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab: there is the heading, the day, and nothing further. What the line says is that he was a monastic and a hymnographer, and that the Romanian church keeps him on this day; that is the whole of what has been read here.

No year, no place, no account of his life and none of his hymns has been taken from anywhere else, and none has been supplied from memory. If a life of his, or a canon of his, is printed elsewhere, it has not been read, and this page will say only what doxologia says until it has been.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and puts flesh on the bare Romanian line. saint.gr has him born at Trigleia in Asia Minor — a small town of Bithynia on the shore of the Propontis — at the beginning of the eighth century, devout and a lover of monks from childhood; as soon as his schooling was done he went to the nearby monastery of Pelekete, where he was tonsured and in time became its abbot. Under Constantine V Copronymus (741–775) the terrible persecution of the icons broke out, and the emperor ordered Michael Lachanodrakon, the governor of Asia, to punish by every means those who venerate the holy icons.

Lachanodrakon came to Pelekete on Great Thursday during the Divine Liturgy and seized the monks: some he killed with barbarous tortures, and others, Theosteriktos among them, he tortured harshly and then sent to the prisons of Constantinople. When the saint came out of prison he was an old man, and he went back to the ruined monastery of Pelekete, where he met the venerable confessor [Nicetas of Medikion](/saints/nicetas-of-medikion), abbot of the neighbouring house of Medikion, whose memory the page puts on 3 April. It is here too that saint.gr says he composed a supplicatory canon to the Most Holy Theotokos. He lived to a great age and died in peace; the page gives no year for either end of his life.
