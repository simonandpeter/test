# Stephen of Kazan

saint.gr's calendar for 24 Μαρτίου names two martyrs together, and gives each of them a heading of his own and the two of them one sentence: the holy martyrs Peter and Stephen were martyred in the year 1552 in the district of Kazan, by the Mongols. That is the whole of the entry, and this life says no more than the page does. It heads each of them «(; - 1552)», so the year of birth is a question mark for both.

The page says nothing of any kinship between Stephen and [Peter](/saints/peter-of-kazan), and nothing of their station, their faith before that year, the manner of their deaths or any relic; it prints neither a couplet nor a hymn. Nothing has been taken from any other source and nothing supplied from memory, and nothing here identifies either of them with the several men of their names kept on other days.

The Russian calendar, which is their own, makes both of them Tatars of Kazan and converts from Islam. Stephen had been palsied in the legs for more than twenty years; when Kazan was taken in 1552 he believed in Christ and was healed, and was baptised by Timothy, the archpriest of the Moscow cathedral, who had brought the metropolitan’s letter to the Russian army. After the army withdrew from the city his own countrymen cut him to pieces for his firmness in the Christian faith, scattered his body and plundered his house.

The day page carries a troparion and a kondak for the two of them together: a pair of martyrs of one mind who rebuked the unbelief of their own countrymen, most excellent champions of the Trinity, who exchanged the storm of torments and a dreadful death for life eternal and bore their suffering in the city of Kazan. Both are recorded here with their English.
