# Akakios of Lete and Rentina

saint.gr says that Saint Akakios lived at the end of the fifteenth century, and that he was consecrated bishop of the historic bishopric of Lete and Rentina by [Niphon](/saints/niphon-patriarch-of-constantinople), then metropolitan of Thessalonica and afterwards patriarch of Constantinople, to whom he was bound spiritually. He had a close spiritual relation as well with [Theophilos the Myrrh-streamer](/saints/theophilus-the-myrrh-streamer), who came of Zichne and whom Akakios ordained priest; Theophilos took him for his elder, the page says, «διότι εὗρε τόν Ἀκάκιον εὐλαβῆ καί ἐνάρετον», because he found Akakios reverent and virtuous, a vessel of guilelessness indeed.

So it was through Akakios that Theophilos came to be joined to Niphon and to the whole holy company about him: the later venerable-martyrs [Makarios](/saints/macarius-disciple-of-niphon) and Ioasaph, the elder Iakovos the Dionysiate and the deacon Iakovos who suffered at Adrianople, Saint Theonas, the brothers Apsarades Theophanes and Nektarios, and the well-known [Maximus the Greek](/saints/maximus-the-greek). When Niphon, as patriarch of Constantinople, learned of the great and wonderful things that had happened in Egypt, he sent there in 1486 perhaps the only man he trusted absolutely and who was marked out for his wisdom and virtue — Akakios, bishop of Lete and Rentina, whom he revered and respected greatly — along with his disciple Theophilos and a party of other clergy, to learn the matter in detail by ear and by eye.

They carried a letter of Niphon's to the patriarch of Alexandria [Joachim the Great](/saints/joachim-i-pany-of-alexandria), in which he thanked the Lord for having heard Joachim's prayers and worked those great wonders in a marvellous manner. Joachim received his guests with much love and joy and kept them a long time at the patriarchate, where, in the synaxaristes's words, «τούς ἐπεριποιεῖτο καί τούς ἐφιλοφρόνει», he looked after them and treated them kindly, showing his admiration for the men the patriarch of Constantinople had sent him.

Afterwards Akakios and Theophilos with the rest of their party departed for the God-trodden mountain of Sinai, and having venerated there reverently went on into the desert to know the ascetics of it, and so came to the holy City, Jerusalem. Having venerated the Life-giving Tomb of the Lord as they had longed to, they went to Mount Tabor, then to Damascus, where they met the patriarch of Antioch, who gave them a letter for the patriarch of Constantinople, and returned to Jerusalem. There Akakios suddenly fell ill and fell asleep in the Lord.

He was buried by his disciple Theophilos with much reverence, after the monastic and ascetic customs of the time; and because he was an utter enemy of vainglory and of the vanity of the world, the page says, Theophilos probably buried him somewhere humble, as Akakios himself would have wished — the man who signed himself «ὁ ταπεινός ἐπίσκοπος Λητῆς καί Ρεντίνης», the humble bishop of Lete and Rentina, always hidden from the glory of men.
