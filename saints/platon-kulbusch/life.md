# Platon Kulbusch

Platon was born on 13 July 1869 at Pootsi, in the Pärnu region of Estonia, and was given the name Paul at his baptism. He went through several theological schools and was ordained priest, and travelled long through the country, preaching the word of God in the Orthodox churches of Estonia. On 31 December 1917 he was chosen as the first bishop of Estonia, and he threw himself into a great deal of work for the support of the Orthodox parishes.

Less than two months after his appointment Estonia was occupied by the German army. Travel was very hard in those days, but it did not stop him from visiting nearly every Orthodox parish in Estonia during that summer. In 1919 he was arrested by the atheist regime in Moscow, and after being tortured he was shot on 14 January 1919. His relics are in the cathedral of the Transfiguration in Tallinn, and he was canonised by the Ecumenical Patriarchate in the year 2000.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 1 January, not on the Romanian day, and it names with him the two presbyters who died beside him. saint.gr's whole account is one sentence: the hieromartyr Platon was bishop of the city of Reval in Estonia and was martyred together with the presbyters [Michael](/saints/michael-companion-of-platon-of-reval) and [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-companion-of-platon-of-reval) in the year 1919.
