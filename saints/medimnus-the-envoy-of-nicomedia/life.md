# Medimnus, envoy to Valens, martyr

Medimnus suffered at Nicomedia under the Arianising emperor Valens (364–378), with Urban, Theodore and Medimnus and seventy-seven men of the clergy — eighty priests and deacons, the Greek synaxarion counts, seventy, the Prologue. Under Valens the Arians drove the Orthodox bishop Evagrius from the church of Constantinople and imprisoned and abused the Christians who would not join their heresy; driven to despair, the Orthodox resolved to ask the emperor’s protection and sent him an embassy of eighty chosen men of clerical rank headed by Urban, Theodore and Medimnus — the most honourable and respected citizens of the capital, the Prologue says. Hearing their just complaints the emperor flew into a rage, but hid his anger, secretly called the eparch Modestus and ordered him to put the envoys to death. Modestus put them on a ship, spreading the false word that they were all going into exile, and ordered the sailors to set the ship on fire in the open sea and escape in the boat; the burning ship drifted for a time, and at last, reaching the place called Dakizis, burned to the ground with the holy martyrs on board, in 370 — so evil servants of a worse master, the Prologue says, and their bodies were burned and sunk in the sea, but their souls swam into the harbour of eternal blessedness. Its verses ask what burns so at midnight on the sea: a great ship on the water, the emperor-heretic mocking Orthodoxy and burning seventy companions — a heretic is often fiercer than an unbeliever — the men in the flames all on their knees, their prayers rising to God and the saints, their bodies smelling in the fierce fire like pure incense before the holy altar.
