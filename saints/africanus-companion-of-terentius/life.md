# Africanus, companion of Terentius

Africanus was one of the forty who refused to sacrifice when the edict of the emperor Decius reached Africa and the governor Fortunatianus set the instruments of torture out before the people. Most of the forty were judged together and put to death together; but Fortunatianus separated four of them — [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa), Africanus, [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius) and [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius) — and had them shut in the innermost prison under close guard while he dealt with the rest.

Brought out and told again to sacrifice, the four answered that they were Christians and had put their hope in Christ, that they would not serve his gods and did not fear his torments. He sent them back with heavy irons on their necks, their hands and feet bound, iron nails strewn under them and no Christian allowed in with food. At midnight a great light filled the prison and an angel called them by name — «Terentie, Africane, Maxime și Pompie» — touched their chains so that they broke and fell, and set a table before them full of good things.

Serpents, asps and vipers were then loosed on them; the guards watching from the roof saw the saints sitting and the Angel of the Lord standing and keeping the creatures off, and in the morning the enchanters could not call them back, but were themselves killed by them. Fortunatianus, seeing the four wholly unhurt, condemned them to the sword. They went to death singing, and devout men buried them about two stadia from the city.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and enumerates it. saint.gr's line for 10 Απριλίου is «Άγιοι Αφρικανός, Θεόδωρος, Μάξιμος, Πομπήιος, Τερέντιος και άλλοι τεσσαράκοντα Μάρτυρες», and its page dates the martyrdom and names the authorities: «μαρτύρησαν το έτος 250 μ.Χ., επί αυτοκράτορα Δεκίου (249-251 μ.Χ.) και ηγεμόνος Φουρτουνιανού, στην Καρθαγένη της Αφρικής» — in the year 250, under the emperor Decius and the governor Fortunianus, at Carthage in Africa. The torments it tells are one set of torments for them all: they were beaten until their inward parts could be seen, their hips were pierced with red-hot nails and vinegar and salt were thrown on the wounds, they prayed and pulled down the wooden images, the altars and the temples of the idolaters, and at the last they were beheaded.

The page then prints a name and a gloss for each of the rest, and the names are largely those of the Greek past: Alexander, Anaxarchus, Anaximenes, Aristides, Demaratus, Democles, Demosthenes, Dionysius, Epaminondas, Eteocles, Zeno, Elias, Heracles, Isaiah, Hephaestion, Themistocles, Theophrastus, Theseus, Thomas, Isocrates, Luke, Macarius, Miltiades, Mnesarchus, Xenophon, Homer, Parmenion, Pelopidas, Pericles, Pindar, Polybius, Polynicus, Prometheus, Sophocles, Socrates, Timothy, Titus, Philopoemen, Phocion and Chronius. That list holds forty names, while the prose above it says «μαζί με άλλους τριάντα εννέα Χριστιανούς», another thirty-nine, and the day's line says forty others; the page reconciles none of the three counts. Δημᾶς, whom the Romanian calendar keeps in this company, is not in saint.gr's list at all.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps the same company on a day of its own, the Julian 28 October, and prints it as «Мчч. Африкана, Терентия, Максима, Помпия и иных 36-ти (III)» — four men by name and thirty-six more unnamed, in the third century. Each of the four has a `/name/` page of its own behind the line, this one titled «Африкан, мч.», and each of those four pages lists this day and no other; so the Russian church has one commemoration of them where the Greek and the Romanian have theirs on 10 April. The calendar gives no place, no life page and no hymn: the forename and the rank word are the whole of what it says about this one, and the thirty-six it counts beside them it does not name.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same day and names him second of the four its heading enumerates, [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa), Africanus, [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius) and [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius), with another thirty-six it does not name. Its account makes those four the men who were kept alive when the company was divided. Under Decius the governor of Africa ordered the whole people to sacrifice and threatened the obstinate; many fell away, but these forty stood unshaken and were given over to torment. The governor divided them, beheading the thirty-six after scourging, scraping and salting their open wounds, and throwing these four into prison in heavy irons at the neck, the hands and the feet.

There an angel of God came to them, touched the irons so that they fell off, and set them an abundant table and fed them. They were brought out and tortured and shut up again; and the governor had his sorcerers gather snakes and scorpions and shut them into the same room, but the creatures huddled in a corner for three days without touching the servants of God, and on the third day, when the prison was opened, turned on the sorcerers and bit them. At the last the four were sentenced, and they went to the beheading singing psalms and praising God, who had counted them worthy of a martyr's death, in the year 250 — the year this folder already holds.
