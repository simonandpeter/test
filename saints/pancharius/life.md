# Pancharius

saint.gr's calendar for 19 Μαρτίου keeps a martyr Pancharius whose fall and recovery are the whole of his story. He came, the page says, from the country of the Ousanoi, and specifically from the city of Villapati — or Bithlapati, according to others, and perhaps, the page guesses, the city of Villa in Germany. His parents had brought him up in the Christian faith, and he was marked out for his manly bearing and his good looks.

When he came to Rome his excellent presence and his sparkling wit drew the notice of the court, and word of him reached Diocletian, who took him into his household and made him a senator. But the honours and the imperial favour made Pancharius deny Christ. When his mother and his sister learned of it they grieved deeply, and wrote him a letter so moving that it begged him to come back to the faith of Christ even at the cost of his life.

The letter, the page says, brought the young man back to himself: he understood the slip he had fallen into, and a flood of repentance came over his soul. Without delay he confessed the Christian faith in front of the court. Diocletian supposed at first that Pancharius had gone out of his mind, but when he was convinced that the thing was serious he flew into a rage and ordered him beheaded. It was the year 305.

The page's guess at his homeland is its own and has not been made a coordinate here; no relic is named, and no hymn stands under his name. It adds that a church of St Pancharius existed in Constantinople before the tenth century, which is a dedication and not a relation.
