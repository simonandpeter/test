# Alexander the Sleepless

The Greek calendar keeps him on 15 Ιανουαρίου as «Όσιος Αλέξανδρος ο Ακοίμητος», Alexander the Sleepless, and its header dates his death to 430. saint.gr's account of his life is longer than what is set down here, and this life does not attempt it; what it ends with is that the venerable Alexander fell asleep in peace.

What the page explains at length instead is the name. Alexander, it says, is in effect the first founder of the monastery of the Akoimetoi — the sleepless ones. That was what the monks of the East were called who lived in community and were divided into groups, praising God in succession through the whole of the day and the night, so that prayer never once stopped in their monastery.

The page prints no hymn for him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 3 July, where the Greek calendar keeps him on 15 January — two calendars and two days, and both are recorded. It tells at length the life the Greek page tells and this life did not attempt. Born in Asia, schooled at Constantinople, he gave himself after his schooling to military service and became an officer. Reading the Holy Scripture he came upon the Saviour’s words: if thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven, and come, follow me. They worked on him so that he sold everything at once, gave it all away, and withdrew into the desert.

After long struggles and labours at the cleansing of himself he founded the Monastery of the Unsleeping with a rule of its own, under which the divine service ran through the day and the night without a break. The brethren were divided into twenty-four turns; each turn knew its own hour of the day or of the night, and went into the church to carry on the reading and the chanting of the turn before it. That is the thing this folder was made to explain, and the Greek page explains it in the same terms.

He travelled much, this book says, with nothing anywhere, through the eastern parts, enlightening men with the faith of Christ, disputing with heretics, working wonders by the grace of God; he grew old in the service of Christ the Lord, and ended his earthly life at Constantinople in the year 430 — the year the Greek page’s own header gives, so that two books now agree on it and nothing in the folder’s date changes. The place of the death is this book’s addition, and a place is not a date. There, it ends, his relics showed a wonderworking power and the glory with which God glorifies His holy servants.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 3 июля ст. ст. — «Прп. Александра , обители "Неусыпающих" первоначальника (ок. 430).» — the first founder of the monastery of the Unsleeping Ones, about the year 430. That is the day the Ohrid Prologue keeps him on and not the January day the Greek calendar gives him, and the year is the one this folder already held. Its name page calls him Alexander of Constantinople. No hymn stands under his name on that day.
