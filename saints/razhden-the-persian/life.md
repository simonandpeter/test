# Razhden the Persian

The calendar marks him «(Груз.)» — a Georgian commemoration — and calls him the first martyr of the Church of Georgia. He was a Persian of a noble family and a worshipper in the religion of Zoroaster, and he came to Georgia as the tutor of the Persian princess Balendukht, daughter of king Hormizd, when she married the Georgian king [Vakhtang Gorgasali](/saints/vakhtang-gorgasali), whom the reading calls Vakhtang the Great and dates 446 to 499. The king honoured him for his birth, made him a counsellor, and the court and the people came to love the plain and good-natured foreigner. There he learned the Christian faith and was baptised, and took to long conversations with the archbishop of the place and to frequenting the churches.

When the new Persian king demanded that Georgia break its alliance with Greece and was refused, war followed. The reading says the women were given over to shameless outrage and the men to dreadful torments, and that the Christians held firm. Razhden took command of the troops in the capital and the forts about it, fought four months and drove the Persians off; then, in a sortie from the fortress of Armazi, he was betrayed by men who envied his standing and taken alive to the king.

Asked why he had left his country and its gods, he answered that those gods serve man and were made to adorn the world, and that he now served the one true and living God who made heaven and earth, known to him in three Persons and one Essence. Neither promises of treasure nor torture moved him. He was sent back to his family at Mtskheta on his word that he would return, and he returned; his family begged him to spare himself and he said that nothing would turn him from the love of his Lord.

He was tortured again at Tsromi in Upper Kartli and thrown into prison, where, the reading says, the Saviour appeared to him in the night and healed every wound. The Persians then crucified him among four condemned men, and shot him on the cross with poisoned arrows, so that he died there in 457. The sun was hidden and a long darkness came on, and in the night a storm so great that nothing could be seen, while the body alone shone with a heavenly light. Christians who had hidden nearby took him down and buried him close by.

The place of his burial stayed unknown until, the reading says, he told the priest who had buried him to disclose it to the king, and his relics were carried with great honour to the church at Nikozi. The name Razhden, it closes, means the light of the faith.
