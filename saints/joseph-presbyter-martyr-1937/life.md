# Joseph (Arkharov)

Joseph Ivanovich Arkharov was born on 1 April 1874 in the village of
Rogachevo in the Dmitrov district of Moscow province, the son of a tanner
who owned a leather works, and worked in the leather trade until 1930,
when he took holy orders. In 1933 the Vereya people’s court sentenced him
under article 74 to two years’ imprisonment; on 1 January 1936, barely
free, he was arrested again and the next day sentenced by the troika of
the NKVD under article 58-10 to three years in the camps, and on 8 March
1936 he reached the Karaganda camp. From the end of 1936 the informers of
the camp’s third section reported on him: on 26 October, when a prisoner
asked for a book to read, he had advised him to read the Gospel or the
Psalter, other books being useless to read; he said openly that the
churches were being restored and services would begin again; a prisoner
named Tsvetikov, a former archbishop, rebuked him for coarseness and they
nearly came to blows; working as cashier of the section, he told the office
staff in the technical barrack how he had been feasted and paid for the
rites in the old days, and now take your camp soup and do not ask; he
“always strives to sing something divine”, suspects everyone of informing,
and shook the commandant’s hand with joy when Tsvetikov was moved out of
the barrack as a slanderous man. He was arrested in the camp on 3
September 1937. The witnesses said: prisoner Arkharov loves the Church
very much; he was a church warden; in the camp he guards his cassock, once
keeping it in the cash safe, and when it was asked for the stage, to play
a priest, he answered, I will not let religion be mocked; he quarrelled
with the Chinese cook over a small portion and complained of the bad food;
he was never content with a single moment of the Soviet power, and said
more than once in conversation that he acknowledged it as God’s punishment
sent on men, which therefore had to be borne. The indictment charged that,
working as cashier of the finance section of the Volkhov section, he
systematically carried on counter-revolutionary provocative agitation
among the prisoners, aimed at discrediting the policy of the party and the
Soviet power and spreading rumours of a coming war and the fall of the
Soviet power: telling a prisoner named Li-U-Fa that the Japanese were
beating his people, would take on the Soviet Union next, and life would be
better once they had beaten it — remember how we lived before, good cheap
food, and now camp soup and the skin flayed off you with work; standing up
in the barrack to say that the old landlords were abused for tormenting
the workers with lard, white bread and a hundred holidays a year, while
now the skin was flayed off them with work and they were fed what the
landlords’ pigs ate, and above all not allowed a word of truth; calling
the Stakhanovites sold men who had sold themselves for flats, pianos and
resorts and crushed honest people together with the communists, “but their
little shop will soon be shut”; and, given a small portion at dinner,
turning on the Chinese cook — with, the indictment added, breaches of camp
discipline, drunkenness and women. Questioned as the accused he did not
admit guilt, “but is fully exposed by the witnesses’ testimony”. On 14
September 1937 the troika of the NKVD for the Karaganda region sentenced
him to be shot, and the sentence was carried out on 21 September 1937, 8
September of the old style; the Russian calendar keeps him on 9 September
of the old style, 22 September. His burial place is unknown. He was
rehabilitated by the Karaganda prosecutor on 3 April 1990.
