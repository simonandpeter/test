# Theodore of Rostov and Suzdal

saint.gr's calendar names him and gives him three sentences, of which one says there is nothing more. The line for 8 June reads «Όσιος Θεόδωρος Επίσκοπος Ροστώφ και Σούζνταλ Ρωσίας», the venerable Theodore, bishop of Rostov and Suzdal in Russia, and the page behind it says this: the venerable Theodore was a Russian bishop; he died in 1023; we have no other details of his life.

What the page does have is his relic. Under the heading of holy relics it says that parts of the saint's incorrupt relic are in the church of the Panagia of Kazan at Suzdal in Russia, and that an incorrupt finger of his is in the church of Saints Constantine and Helen at Suzdal. So what is recorded of him is two sees, a year, a country, and two churches that keep pieces of him — and no birth, no consecration, no work and no hymn. The Russian form of his name is not on the page.

The Russian calendar does supply it. The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the Julian 8 June and its line is as short as the Greek one — «Свт. Феодора , еп. Суздальского (ок. 1023).» — but it grades him a hierarch, it gives the year as about 1023, and the name page behind it heads him «Святитель Феодор Ростовский и Суздальский, епископ», which is both of the sees the Greek page gave him and is the form this folder now stores. That page adds a second day, 23 мая, the synaxis of the saints of Rostov, which was not read.

What that calendar has and the Greek one had not is his service. It prints three hymns over his own name, two troparia and a kontakion, all in the eighth and third tones, and they are copied here with their English typed. All three say the same two things of him: that he kept vigil in prayer while grieving continually for his flock, and that he enlightened everyone by his teaching — which is more than the Greek entry's three sentences gave, though it is the language of a bishop's service and not a biography. The same page prints four further hymns that join him with another bishop of Suzdal whose relics lie beside his; those are not copied here, because a hymn sung to two saints would assert between two folders a bond no page of this calendar states in words.
