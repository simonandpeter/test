# Vladimir (Chetverin)

Vladimir Chetverin was born in 1874 in the village of Barakhman-Gart in
the Ardatov district of Simbirsk province, to a simple, pious family. From
youth he loved the teacher’s path and longed with all his heart to bring
people the light of the faith of Christ: first a teacher in a literacy
school, then for seven years teacher of the parish school at Kutyevo in
the Karsun district, at twenty-nine, burning with love for the Lord and
wishing to give all his strength to His service, he petitioned the
Simbirsk examining commission for ordination, and the diocesan
authorities, noting the humility and firm faith of the young ascetic, made
him psalmist, first at Yulevo in the Karsun district, then at Bolshaya
Ryazan, Vyrypayevka and Sursky Ostrog. Wishing to be an example to his
pupils in the knowledge and keeping of Christ’s commandments, and feeling
a boundless responsibility for them before the Lord, he took the
missionary courses at the Kazan Academy, finished them well, and at
thirty-six was ordained priest to the church of Syryatino in the Ardatov
district, where he managed not only to serve the Lord faithfully in the
sacraments and the needs of the people but to teach in the parish school
and the Law of God in the school of the next village — everywhere an
example in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in
purity (1 Tim. 4:12), the words engraved on the priest’s cross, as if
written on the tablets of his heart. By 1915 he and his wife Maria had
eleven children, three of them dead in infancy; the rest they raised
worthily, many following their father into teaching, and three of his sons
were shot and died martyrs too. From Syryatino he passed to the Nativity
church of his native Barakhman-Gart, whose most honoured icon was the
Kazan icon of the Mother of God, before which he prayed with tears for all
his flock.

Then came the time of the great turmoil in Russia. In those days bread was
taken from people without trial, leaving them to starve, so the
parishioners entrusted the keeping of their grain to their beloved priest,
and he took the charge with all care; when some of the villagers wanted to
seize all the bread in exchange for false money, he saw through the plot
and stood up for his parishioners without fear of the offenders. Then the
persecutors demanded that he break the seal of confession and hand over
the parish registers, and he chose to suffer rather than betray his
priestly oath and the church’s property; false witnesses appeared, and he
was charged with failing to carry out the order of the Soviet power on the
surrender of the registers, with the ruin of the country’s finances and
with not surrendering firearms in his possession, while the villagers were
tricked into seeking another priest. On 19 August 1918 he served the
Liturgy for the last time and was arrested without any charge being laid.
From that day his sufferings began: he was worn down with interrogations
meant to make him renounce the faith and accused of propaganda against
the Soviet power, and answered his slanderers with honour that a priest’s
service lies not in resisting the earthly power but in the doing of God’s
will on earth as it is in heaven. He accepted slander and arrest without
murmur and even with joy; the people of Barakhman-Gart, left without a
pastor, strengthened him with unceasing prayers and asked for his release,
and the dean of the Ardatov district, Fr Constantine Benevolensky,
interceded for him, but the persecutors were unbending and he was
condemned to death. At one in the morning of 2 September 1918 — 20 August
of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him — he was led out
to be shot and, comforted and strengthened by the Holy Spirit, bore all as
a meek lamb and received a martyr’s end. His relics, with the cross on his
breast and the Gospel laid in his hand, were found incorrupt in a grave
through which a cold spring ran; they are sewn into the antimension of the
church of St Alexander Nevsky at Zelenograd, whose patronal feast is his
day, and the land of Mordovia has gained in him a sleepless intercessor.
