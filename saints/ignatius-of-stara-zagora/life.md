# Ignatius of Stara Zagora

Ignatius was born at Eskee Zagora, that is Stara Zagora, a village of the Bulgarian eparchy of Tarnovo, and baptised John. His parents, George and Maria, moved while he was a child to Philippopolis, now Plovdiv, and put him to school to learn the holy writings in Slavonic, where he came to be unmatched at reading and grammar. Wanting the monastic life, he left home with his parents' leave for the monastery of Rila in the west of Bulgaria, and stayed six years under a harsh monk, serving the church as a reader; in the end that monk's harshness turned to hatred and he set fire to his disciple's cell, so John went home.

Worse waited for him there. The Serbs had risen against the janissaries, and his father had been made commander of an Ottoman brigade; George refused to fight Christians, saying it was impossible to take up arms against brothers of his own faith, and was stabbed and beheaded for it. The Romanian life says his memory too is kept on this eighth of October. John's mother and his two sisters, taken at the same time and threatened without end, went over to Islam; and when he hid in the house of an Orthodox widow, it was they who denounced him. Caught later by Turkish soldiers, stripped of what he had and pressed to convert, he promised out of fear that he would, and was let go.

At Eskee Zagora he met an Athonite of the monastery of Gregoriou and went with him to the Holy Mountain, settling at the Skete of St Anne, where he came to know a virtuous monk named Basil. Going with him once to Thessalonica, he saw the martyrdom of Saint David, kept on 26 June, who had entered monasticism at the same skete, and from that day he longed for martyrdom himself. After Basil died he moved to the Skete of Kapsokalyvia; there he heard that his friend [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-of-dimitsana) had been martyred at Constantinople, was tonsured with the name Ignatius, and at last received the blessing of his elder, Father Acacius, to go and do the same.

He left on 12 September, by way of Iviron and the icon of the Portaitissa, and came to Constantinople with Father Gregory. He prayed before a wonderworking icon of the Mother of God, then went to the Divan in Muslim dress and confessed Christ before the judge, drawing out an icon of the Lord and a cross from his breast. He was held two days and two nights and tortured, brought back, confessed again, and was condemned. At Daktyloporta, which the Turks call Parmakkapi, he knelt and stretched out his neck for the sword, but the executioner remembered his orders, put a noose on him and hanged him from a tree there at noon. The page gives the year as 1914, which cannot stand with the rest of what it tells; the reading is printed here as the page prints it and nothing is corrected. After three days Gregory bought the body from the guards, sailed on 14 October, took up at Prote the relics of Euthymius, who had suffered seven months before on 22 March, and brought both to Athos on 20 October. The page cites the great Synaxarion for October published at Iasi in 2015.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 8 October, and tells the same life with its own details. saint.gr makes his birthplace the village of Zagora in the eparchy of Tyrnavos, names his parents George and Maria and his baptismal name John, and says that the family moved to Philippoupolis. As a small child he showed great zeal for the virtues and went to a strict elder; and in that time the Turks killed his father and forced his mother and his two sisters to turn Turk.

When he learned of it he went to Bucharest and from there to the Holy Mountain, and on the road he was seized by Ottomans and promised, to escape death, that he would become one. On Athos he came to the skete of the Honourable Forerunner and was tonsured a monk with the name Ignatius, and the abbot Nikephoros the Elder entrusted him to the elder Akakios. Later he received the blessing to go to martyrdom, and on 29 September 1814 he reached Constantinople, where before the judge he threw off the Turkish fez he had put on for the purpose and confessed Christ. The tortures were savage, and he stayed firm; they hanged him on 8 October 1814, at the sixth hour. His companion Gregory bought his relic and carried it, with that of the new martyr Euthymios, to the Holy Mountain. saint.gr adds that Ignatius keeps 1 May together with his fellow ascetics, the martyrs Euthymios and Akakios.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 20 October, and gives his birthplace a sentence of its own: he was from Eski-Zagora in Bulgaria, from that famous Zagora which has given the Church of God many holy ascetics and martyrs. He struggled in the Skete of Saint John the Forerunner on Athos.

He gave himself up to the Turks voluntarily, to be tortured for Christ, and he was hanged at Constantinople on the eighth of October 1814 — which is the day the Romanian and the Greek calendars keep him on, and the year and day this folder already recorded. His relics are wonderworking, and his head, the entry ends, is kept with honour in the monastery of Saint Panteleimon.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 1 мая ст. ст., not on the day of his own martyrdom, and it keeps him there with the two other new martyrs of the Holy Mountain: its line reads «Прмчч. Афонских Евфимия (1814), Игнатия (1814) и Акакия (1816)», and the day page carries a troparion and a kontakion that name all three together. The calendar gives him no life of his own. The life it serves under that line is the life of the youngest of the three, [Acacius the New of Neochorion](/saints/acacius-the-new-of-neochorion), who heard on Athos of «недавно пострадавшие Евфимий и Игнатий» and wished to become like them; it closes by saying that the holy heads of the three — that of [Euthymius of Dimitsana](/saints/euthymius-of-dimitsana), his own and that of Acacius — are now kept in the Russian house of the Great Martyr Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain.
