# Thaddeus

Thaddeus of the Seventy, a Jew by descent, was born in the Syrian city of Edessa — to be distinguished, the Sretensky calendar says, from the apostle of the Twelve, Jude called Thaddeus or Lebbaeus. Coming to Jerusalem for a feast he heard the preaching of [John the Forerunner](/saints/john-the-baptist), was baptised by him in the Jordan and stayed in Palestine; seeing the Saviour he became his disciple, and the Lord chose him among the seventy whom he sent two by two into the towns and places he meant to visit himself (Luke 10:1).

After the Ascension he preached in Syria and Mesopotamia, and came with the Gospel to Edessa, his own city — by the Lord’s promise, the Prologue says, to the prince Abgar, to whom the Lord had sent the cloth with his image: kissing it Abgar had been healed of his leprosy, but not wholly, a little remaining on his face, and when Thaddeus came to him he received him with great joy, was taught the true faith and baptised, and as he came up out of the water the rest of the leprosy fell from him. The prince called all the citizens of Edessa before the apostle to hear the preaching of Christ, and hearing his words and seeing their prince healed they threw down the idols, received the faith and were baptised; Abgar brought out much gold, and Thaddeus said: having left our own, how should we take what is another’s? He preached the Gospel through Syria and Phoenicia and reposed in the Lord at Beirut in Phoenicia.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 26 mai and prints his life there. He was of the city of Edessa, it says, a Jew by birth who knew the Scripture of the Old Law perfectly; he went up to Jerusalem to worship in the days of John the Baptist, wondered at his preaching and his angelic life, and was baptised by him, and afterwards, seeing the Lord living among men and hearing His teaching and seeing His wonders, followed Him and was numbered in the company of the seventy lesser apostles. After the Ascension, when the apostles divided the world between them, he was sent by the Lord to Edessa, as the Lord had promised Abgar before His passion when He sent him the image of His face not made by hands, writing that when He ascended to the Father He would send one of His disciples, who would heal him wholly of his disease and give everlasting life to him and to those with him. The same page distinguishes him carefully, on the authority of Nicephorus Callistus, from the other Thaddeus, the one of the twelve, whom Nicephorus calls Levi.

The Romanian calendar keeps him twice, on 26 mai and on 21 august. Doxologia.ro's life says he was of the city of Edessa, a Hebrew by birth, who knew the divine Scripture of the Old Law perfectly; that he went up to Jerusalem to worship in the days of John the Baptist, marvelled at his preaching and at his angelic life and was baptised by him; and that afterwards, seeing the Lord living among men in the flesh, hearing his teaching and seeing his miracles, he followed him and was numbered in the company of the seventy lesser apostles. After the Ascension, when the apostles divided the world between them for the preaching, Thaddeus was sent by the Lord to Edessa, because the Lord had promised Abgar the ruler of Edessa, before his voluntary Passion, that when he had ascended to the Father he would send one of his disciples to him. The same page ends by distinguishing him, with Nicephorus, from the other Thaddeus, the one of the Twelve whom Nicephorus calls Levi.

The Greek calendar keeps Thaddaeus on the same day, 21 August, and tells his story from his baptism. saint.gr says he was a Hebrew of Edessa, much educated in the divine Scriptures, who had gone up to Jerusalem on pilgrimage in the time of John the Baptist; that hearing John's preaching and seeing his angelic life he was so struck by it that he sought baptism at his hands; and that afterwards, hearing the teaching and seeing the miracles of the Lord, he followed him as far as the saving Passion.

After the Ascension, the page says, he returned to his own country, Edessa. There he cleansed the toparch Abgar of leprosy and then baptised him a Christian. Having taught and enlightened many with the word of truth, and founded many churches in the cities of Syria, he came to Beirut, where by the grace of God he preached the Gospel and baptised many; and there he gave up his spirit in peace, having fulfilled in his life the commandment the Lord gave his disciples, to go and make disciples of all the nations.

The page then sets its own account against itself. It quotes M. Galanos: some suppose that this is one of the seventy apostles, from Edessa but a Jew; «but it may be held certain that the apostle Thaddaeus is the one borne under that name among the Twelve» — otherwise called Lebbaeus, brother of James the Less, to whom the epistle of Jude belongs, since he was properly called Jude and the other two names were additions, as always happens among the Jews to this day; and that Thaddaeus preached the Gospel in Mesopotamia, where he died a martyr's death. The page prints both readings side by side and settles neither, and that is how they are left here.
