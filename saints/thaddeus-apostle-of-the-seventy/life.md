# Thaddeus, Apostle of the Seventy

Thaddeus of the Seventy, a Jew by descent, was born in the Syrian city of Edessa — to be distinguished, the Sretensky calendar says, from the apostle of the Twelve, Jude called Thaddeus or Lebbaeus. Coming to Jerusalem for a feast he heard the preaching of John the Forerunner, was baptised by him in the Jordan and stayed in Palestine; seeing the Saviour he became his disciple, and the Lord chose him among the seventy whom he sent two by two into the towns and places he meant to visit himself (Luke 10:1). After the Ascension he preached in Syria and Mesopotamia, and came with the Gospel to Edessa, his own city — by the Lord’s promise, the Prologue says, to the prince Abgar, to whom the Lord had sent the cloth with his image: kissing it Abgar had been healed of his leprosy, but not wholly, a little remaining on his face, and when Thaddeus came to him he received him with great joy, was taught the true faith and baptised, and as he came up out of the water the rest of the leprosy fell from him. The prince called all the citizens of Edessa before the apostle to hear the preaching of Christ, and hearing his words and seeing their prince healed they threw down the idols, received the faith and were baptised; Abgar brought out much gold, and Thaddeus said: having left our own, how should we take what is another’s? He preached the Gospel through Syria and Phoenicia and reposed in the Lord at Beirut in Phoenicia.
