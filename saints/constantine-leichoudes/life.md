# Constantine Leichoudes

saint.gr's notice for 29 Ιουλίου opens by saying that this is the patriarch Constantine I, who succeeded John V in the year 674, having been before that deacon and sacristan, and who held the see for two years and three months. We have not many of his biographical details, the page says; but it is certain that he was marked out by a strictly Orthodox mind, by zeal in defending the Church against the wrong-believing and against the Monothelites, and by a character free of money and friendly to the poor.

The page then sets another opinion against its own. S. Eustratiades, it says, supposes rather that the saint of this day is Constantine III Leichoudes. Nikodemos accepted Constantine I, and Gedeon followed him; but in Delehaye's Synaxaristes the entry reads «μνήμη Κωνσταντίνου του νέου πατριάρχου Κωνσταντινουπόλεως» — the memory of Constantine the new patriarch of Constantinople — and a distinction is therefore being drawn between an older patriarch of the name and a younger one. The younger is to be taken as Constantine III, the second of the name having been an iconoclast.

That Constantine, Eustratiades goes on, served from 1059 to 1063; he had been protovestiarios and president of the senate, and afterwards a eunuch and a monk, and he succeeded Michael Keroularios. He was virtuous and the founder of a monastery of the Theotokos in Constantinople, as Psellos witnesses in his funeral oration for the patriarch, and that is what justifies his being numbered with the venerable.

saint.gr then closes by siding with him: according to the official website of the Ecumenical Patriarchate this is Saint Constantine III Leichoudes, and Saint Constantine I is kept on 9 August. So this folder is Leichoudes's, as the page's own head line already has it, and the older patriarch is a different man on a different day. The page prints no hymn for him.
