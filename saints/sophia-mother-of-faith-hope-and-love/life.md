# Sophia, mother of Faith, Hope and Love

Sophia and her three daughters Faith, Hope and Love were martyred in the
years of the emperor Hadrian (117–138); the three daughters took their
names from the New Testament — now abide faith, hope, love, these three,
but the greatest of these is love (1 Corinthians 13:13). Sophia, an
honourable and God-fearing woman, was soon widowed and came with her three
daughters to Rome, where they were denounced as famous Christians; the
emperor, learning that the four women were Christians, had them arrested,
and isolating the mother began to question the daughters.

First the twelve-year-old Faith was brought before the king; with tempting
words Hadrian tried to persuade her to deny Christ, promising her
everything for a happy life, but met the girl’s unbending spirit, and the
words of Scripture were her answer — I live by faith in the Son of God, who
loved me and gave himself for me (Galatians 2:20); then, after tortures,
they beheaded her. With the words of Scripture too the ten-year-old Hope
answered when asked whether it was worth undergoing such tortures: we have
hoped in the living God, who is the Saviour of all men, especially of the
faithful (1 Timothy 4:10) — and at once she too was beheaded.

Nor was the nine-year-old Love behind in her answer: her whole being, she
said, was turned to the love of God and the patience of Christ (2
Thessalonians 3:5), and they were not slow to behead her as well. Proud of
her children, Sophia buried her daughters with honour and stayed three days
at their graves, begging God to take her to himself; God heard her prayer,
and she gave up her spirit beside her children’s graves. The synaxarion’s
verse says that by faith in you, Faith, Hope and Love, the three, O
Trinity, bow their necks to the sword, and Sophia the mother rejoices,
psalming like David, in her children according to her prayers. The Greek
and Romanian calendars keep the four on 17 September with a troparion and
kontakion — rational lambs, the Romanian troparion says, brought by
martyrdom to the Lamb and Shepherd.

The Russian calendar keeps the four on 17 September old style and dates them about 137, with a troparion and a kontakion. Its life says that [Faith](/saints/faith-daughter-of-sophia), [Hope](/saints/hope-daughter-of-sophia) and [Love](/saints/love-daughter-of-sophia) were born in Italy to Sophia, a devout Christian widow, who named them for the three Christian virtues and brought them up in the love of Christ; they confessed their faith openly, and the governor Antiochus denounced them to the emperor Hadrian (117–138), who had them brought to Rome. Faith was twelve, Hope ten and Love nine, and none of them would sacrifice to Artemis.

They were burned on an iron grate and thrown into a furnace and into a cauldron of boiling pitch, and kept unharmed; Love was bound to a wheel and beaten with rods; and Sophia’s own torment was to be made to watch, while she urged her daughters on. The three were beheaded. Sophia was allowed their bodies, laid them in a chest, took them out of the city and buried them on a high place, and after three days sitting by the grave gave up her soul there, and the faithful buried her beside them. The life adds that their relics have rested since 777 in Alsace, in the church at Eschau.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, sets their life and death in Rome under Hadrian and gives the daughters the same ages. It says the four stood before the emperor holding one another by the hand and would not sacrifice to Artemis, that the mother strengthened her daughters before their suffering, that the three were beaten, cut, thrown into fire and boiling pitch, and beheaded one after another, and that Sophia buried them outside the city and stayed three days and three nights at their grave in prayer, and gave up her spirit there.
