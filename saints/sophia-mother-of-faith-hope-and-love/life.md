# Sophia, mother of Faith, Hope and Love

Sophia and her three daughters Faith, Hope and Love were martyred in the years of the emperor Hadrian (117–138); the three daughters took their names from the New Testament — now abide faith, hope, love, these three, but the greatest of these is love (1 Corinthians 13:13). Sophia, an honourable and God-fearing woman, was soon widowed and came with her three daughters to Rome, where they were denounced as famous Christians; the emperor, learning that the four women were Christians, had them arrested, and isolating the mother began to question the daughters. First the twelve-year-old Faith was brought before the king; with tempting words Hadrian tried to persuade her to deny Christ, promising her everything for a happy life, but met the girl’s unbending spirit, and the words of Scripture were her answer — I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me (Galatians 2:20); then, after tortures, they beheaded her. With the words of Scripture too the ten-year-old Hope answered when asked whether it was worth undergoing such tortures: we have hoped in the living God, who is the Saviour of all men, especially of the faithful (1 Timothy 4:10) — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here; its verse says that by faith in you, Faith, Hope and Love, the three, O Trinity, bow their necks to the sword, and Sophia the mother rejoices, psalming like David, in her children according to her prayers. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep the four on 17 September with a troparion and kontakion — rational lambs, the Romanian troparion says, brought by martyrdom to the Lamb and Shepherd.
