# Conon of Cyprus

saint.gr says the venerable Conon lived about the fourth century. He was born in the region of Akamas in Cyprus, a few years after the repose of [Hilarion the Great](/saints/hilarion-the-great), of pious and God-loving parents; and from his childhood he loved self-restraint, ascesis and virtue. So he decided to leave the world and settle in a cave, far from his village.

There the venerable one fought the good fight and went forward in the life according to Christ. Little by little a group of young men gathered about him, and so a monastic brotherhood came into being. The years were hard, the page says: poverty and sickness wore the people down. And so Conon, who was marked by love of God and love of man alike, took care to found beside the monastery a hospital and a home for the aged — the known Ptocheion — so that the sick and the poor might find hope and refuge there.

Having struggled in a manner pleasing to God, with a perfect love towards God and towards man, he fell asleep in peace at a great age. His holy relics, the page says, are kept at the monasteries of Kykkos and Machairas in Cyprus, and a portion of them at Kykkos in particular. Two other men of this name stand on the same Greek day — the Isaurian and the Gardener, each on a page of his own — and none of the three is told in terms of the others.
