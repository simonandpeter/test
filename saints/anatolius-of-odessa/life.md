# Anatolius of Odessa

Anatolius was born in the year 1880 in the city of Kovel in Volhynia. He studied at the theological academy of Kyiv and went on with his studies in history and in the reading of the ancient manuscripts of the library of the Patriarchate of Constantinople. In 1913 he was elected Bishop of Chistopol, and in 1927 he was translated to the diocese of Odessa.

He was driven out and imprisoned by the Soviet regime, saint.gr says, because he did not agree with what it was trying to impose on the administration and the life of the Church. The hardships wore him down and destroyed his already shaken health, so that his heart did not hold out, and he fell asleep in the year 1938.

The calendar gives him the rank of hieromartyr; the page names no sentence, no camp and no day of his death within that year, and prints no hymn for him.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 10 января ст. ст. and fills out the Greek account. Born Andrei Grigorievich Grisyuk on 20 August 1880 at Kovel in the Volhynia province, the son of a district treasurer in a very poor household, he finished the Volhynia seminary in 1900 and entered the Kyiv theological academy, was tonsured Anatoly and ordained hieromonk in 1903, took his master's degree in 1908 and was made archimandrite and lecturer in 1911, working two years on the oldest manuscripts in the patriarchal library at Constantinople for a study of Syrian monasticism before the Arab conquest.

Consecrated bishop of Chistopol in 1913 and soon rector of the Kazan academy, he kept the academy teaching after the decree that closed every theological school, and when the Cheka opened his report to the Patriarch he was arrested in March 1921 and given a year. Arrests followed upon arrests — Samara in 1922, prison again in February 1923, and after his release, for resisting the Renovationist schism, a beating in the G.P.U. cells that broke his jaw and two ribs, then three years' exile to Poltoratsk and Krasnovodsk in Turkmenia.

He was archbishop of Odessa from 1928 and metropolitan from 21 October 1932, and watched many of the city's churches pulled down; by the end he served in the Saint Demetrius church, the last one open, walking there on foot with his eyes lowered, and went to the N.K.V.D.'s night interrogations only after the liturgy was finished. Arrested in the night of 27 July 1936, held half a year in Kyiv and sentenced early in 1937 to five years in the camps, he was driven north on foot with criminal convicts who robbed him, and set to heavy labour at the Kyltovo camp in the Ukhta-Pechora system, sick with a stomach ulcer, myocarditis and bad lungs.

His sister Mary came to the camp and was not allowed to let him see her face. He died in the camp hospital on 10 January 1938 — 23 January in the new style — of what the Russian account calls torture; when they came to take his pectoral cross and his Gospel he gave up the Gospel but held the cross in his stiffening hands, and fellow prisoners buried him in the permafrost under a little cross of twigs. The Ukrainian Orthodox Church canonised him locally for the Kherson diocese in 1997, and the Jubilee Council of Bishops glorified him for the whole church in August 2000.
