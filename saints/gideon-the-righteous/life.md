# Gideon

Gideon was a judge of Israel, the deliverer of his people from the
Midianites, and the Romanian synaxarion’s one line for him is that he saw
the Nativity of Christ before it came to pass, in the dew upon the fleece;
the Russian church calendar tells the story of the Book of Judges. The
history of the Hebrew people is full of its forgetting God and falling into
paganism, and of God withdrawing his help and giving them into the power of
the pagan nations round them until calamity brought them back; to the
penitent he sent deliverers, the judges, fourteen in all, who did God’s
judgement on earth, struck the pagans, freed Israel and ruled it. After the
death of Joshua the Israelites soon fell into idolatry, and the Lord gave
them for seven years to the Midianites, who came up with the Amalekites and
the tribes of the east as soon as the sowing was done, numberless as
locusts, with their herds and their families, destroyed the harvest through
the land and left neither food nor sheep nor ox nor ass; ruined by the
raids, Israel cried to God, and the merciful Lord sent them Gideon, the
youngest son of Joash of the tribe of Manasseh, of Ophrah, who was threshing
wheat in a winepress, hurriedly and by stealth so that the enemy should not
see, when the angel of the Lord stood before him and bade him gather an army
and go out against the foe. He began the deliverance with the uprooting of
idolatry: he broke down the altar of Baal and cut down the Asherah beside
his father’s house, built an altar to the true God on the spot, and burned a
seven-year-old bull on the wood of the Asherah. The men of Ophrah were
outraged and meant to stone him, and his father Joash, their priest, talked
them out of it — will you plead for Baal? if he is a god, let him contend
for himself when his altar is thrown down — so that they left Gideon alone
and gave him the name Jerubbaal, let Baal contend against him, and were
convinced by it of Baal’s utter helplessness. When the Midianites and
Amalekites and the tribes of eastern Arabia crossed the Jordan again and
camped in the valley of Jezreel, he raised the cry of deliverance; his own
clan of Abiezer answered, and the neighbouring tribes to whom he sent
messengers, and thirty-two thousand men of Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun and
Naphtali gathered to him. Before the battle he asked a sign: he would lay a
fleece of shorn wool on the threshing floor, and if the dew lay on the
fleece alone and the ground was dry he would know that God would save Israel
by his hand; in the morning he wrung a bowl of water from the fleece. He
asked again, praying God not to be angry if he spoke once more, that the
fleece alone be dry and the dew on all the ground; and so it was. And that
Israel might see more clearly that God’s saving help is joined not to the
number of those who do his will but to their quality, God bade him send home
all who were fearful and timid, and twenty-two thousand went; then to lead
the rest to the water and watch how they drank, and to keep those who lapped
from the hand rather than kneeling to the stream — three hundred men. By
three hundred I will save you, said the Lord, and with them, against a
hundred thousand Midianites, Gideon went out, on nothing but firm faith in
God’s help. Dividing them in three at God’s prompting, he gave every man a
trumpet and a lamp hidden in a jar; at night they surrounded the enemy camp,
and at the signal broke the jars, blew the trumpets and shouted, the sword
of the Lord and of Gideon; the sudden lights, the noise and the shouting
struck the Midianites with terror, and in the darkness and confusion they
turned their swords on each other and fled. He came home with enormous
spoil, and the grateful Israelites offered him and his descendants the
kingship; the mighty warrior refused, knowing how small his own part had
been in the deliverance, which was God’s wonder, and that God alone is
Israel’s king. He lived to a great age in peace, and his story, the Russian
calendar says, is the story of how the true faith and love of God of one man
saved a whole people, and a figure of the greatest event in the history of
mankind, the saving passion of the Son of God. The Russian calendar keeps
him on 26 September of the old style, 9 October, and in the Sunday of the
Holy Forefathers before the Nativity; the Romanian calendar keeps him on 26
September.
