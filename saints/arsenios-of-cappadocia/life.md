# Arsenios of Cappadocia

Theodore by his baptismal name, father Arsenios the Cappadocian was born in the village of Kefalohori, in the district of Farasa, in the year 1840. Orphaned of both parents, he entered after his studies the monastery of Saint John the Baptist in the town of Flaviana, fifteen kilometres from Caesarea; at twenty-six he was made a monk and then ordained hierodeacon. What decided him for the monastery was a thing that happened in his childhood: going with his brother Blasius towards their parents' field he fell into the water and was near drowning, and his brother began to pray to the Martyr George, whose church was nearby — and then saw Theodore beside him, who told him that a monk had taken him out of the water and set him on his horse.

He loved not only God but his neighbour, the life says, whether Christian or Turk, rich or poor. People felt the grace at work in him when he healed the sick, drove out devils, freed people from the power of the evil one, and helped young families to have children. To those who were ill and could not come he blessed their clothes and wrote out prayers on paper for them to read, sending them by relatives or acquaintances; in grave cases he stayed beside the sick man, prayed and even made prostrations for him, and he was healed. Where children were possessed or paralysed, understanding that the parents were at fault, he healed the child and gave the parents a rule of amendment.

For all these benefits he never took money and would not even hold it in his hand, saying: the Orthodox faith is not for sale. Neither would he take gifts. A Turk brought him many presents in thanks, because his wife had borne two children by the father's prayer, and he told him sharply: I do not gather gifts; have you no poor in your village? Give them to them. Those who wished to leave something went into the church, where there was a niche, and left it there, and the poor came by themselves and took as much as they needed — and took no more, for fear God should punish them.

On Wednesdays and Fridays he shut himself in his house, which was simple but clean. Every day he kept vigil from nine at night until three in the morning, and on feasts it was drawn out until morning and ended with the Liturgy; and he did this in the church of Saint John Chrysostom, or of the Mother of God, or of the Martyrs Barachisius and Jonah. He had a great reverence for Saint John Chrysostom and for the Martyr George.

God gave him also the gift of seeing before him. Many years before they went to Greece he prophesied that they would go, that he would live forty days and die, and that their village would be scattered; and he foretold the hard years of the Second World War and of the civil war in Greece.

On 14 August 1924 they set out for Greece and reached the port of Piraeus on 14 September. He served a liturgy there for the Exaltation of the Cross, and then they went to the island of Corfu, where he served two liturgies in the church of Saint George and, after a short illness, gave up his soul into the hands of the Lord on 10 November 1924, holding in his hands the relics of Saint John Chrysostom.

He was the godfather and the spiritual father of [Paisios the Athonite](/saints/paisios-the-athonite), who later went with great zeal through the whole of Greece where the people of Farasa were living, those who had lived beside Arsenios, and gathered together all their testimonies. He was canonised in 1986 by the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople, and his commemoration is kept on 10 November.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 November. saint.gr puts his birth about 1840 at Pharasa, or Varasio, the chief village of the six Christian villages of the district of Pharasa in Cappadocia; his parents, it says, were rich in virtues and middling in goods, and had two boys, Blasios and Theodore — Theodore being the saint. They were orphaned young and their mother's sister took them in; and a wonderful thing that happened to the children, the saving of the young Theodore from certain drowning by Saint George, turned Blasios to glorify God in his own way as a teacher of Byzantine music, ending at Constantinople, and turned Theodore to wanting to be a monk. Growing up, he was sent to Nigde and then to Smyrna, where he finished his studies, and at about twenty-six he went on. The page adds that his relics are kept at the monastery of Saint John the Theologian at Souroti near Thessalonica.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the Julian 28 October, which is the civil day the Greek and Romanian pages already gave him, and in four words — «Прп. Арсения Каппадокийского (1924)» — agreeing on the year he died. It carries no life. His name page spells the by-name «Капподакийский», with a syllable turned about, where the day line spells it properly; and it carries two notices rather than a life, one of them recording that an icon of him with a portion of his relics was received at the Сретенский monastery in Moscow on 17 July.
