# Acacius the Kausokalyvite

saint.gr says Acacius lived and was made known in the dark years of the Turkish rule. He was born, probably a few years after 1630, in the village of Golitsa in Agrafa, in what was then the province of Phanari and Neochorion, in the community that now bears his own name in the district of Karditsa. Anastasius, as he was called in the world, was left fatherless very young; his mother, with her deep Christian faith, took the whole weight of the family on herself and worked without complaint to keep her two children and to raise them in the teaching and admonition of the Lord, and the small Anastasius was soon at her side taking his share of it.

The word of the Gospel had shaken his heart early, and he felt the call to the monastic life keenly; he kept away from the noise of the world and often sought the stillness of desert places, where he gave all his time to prayer and fasting. At twenty-three he left the world for the country of Zagora of Volos and came to the monastery of Sourvia, which [Dionysius of Olympus](/saints/dionysius-of-olympus) had built in the country of Makrinitsa and which is dedicated to the Holy Trinity. The abbot explained to him in detail the difficulties of the monastic life and the strict rule of the house; the young man insisted, promising that with God's help he would overcome every obstacle, and the abbot, an experienced spiritual father, saw the godly zeal in him and received him. There he was tonsured with the name Acacius, and on the very night he received the angelic habit he was granted a vision: he seemed to hold in his hands a lighted lamp whose light was exceeding bright and lit up all that place.

Between 1660 and 1670 he left for the Holy Mountain. He went first to the country of the Great Lavra and took shelter in a cave near the skete of the Kausokalyvis, and lived the ascetic life there for a time; wanting to be as well formed as he could, he visited monasteries and sketes, the hermitages of hesychasts and the caves of ascetics, and sought out the chosen and tried monks as a deer athirst seeks the springs of water, submitting to them willingly and learning patiently beside them. He came at last to the monastery of Saint Dionysiou and, after a short stay, withdrew to a desert place above it for stillness, going down to the monastery every Saturday for the services. At the skete of the Pantokrator he met his old spiritual father from Sourvia, who had come from Zagora to study the Byzantine music and who asked to take him along as his monk; but Acacius asked his blessing and begged him not to insist, because he wanted to live the ascetic life alone.

On the advice of the elder and spiritual father Galaktion he went to Kausokalyvia on the Holy Mountain, above the Transfiguration, and stayed there living the ascetic life for twenty whole years. Once, the page says, he saw Maximos the Kausokalyvite, whom the same calendar keeps on 13 January, in a white and shining priestly vestment, going about censing the whole church with a multitude of monks in the same white vesture following him; and when Acacius asked who they were who went with him, Maximos answered that they were all those venerable fathers from the country of Kausokalyvia who had found their salvation through him.

As the years went on and the place where he lived was hard of access and without water, he was forced to move lower down towards the sea, towards the cape of the Athonite peninsula where the present skete of Kausokalyvia, of the Holy Trinity, stands; and there he sought his dwelling in a small cave which bears his name to this day. By his rare virtues he was shown, in the hymnographer's words, the foremost of ascetics and the boast of the God-bearing fathers.

He foresaw and foretold his falling asleep to all the disciples who lived near him, and above all to the monk Athanasius, who came up to his cave from the skete of Saint Anne to take his blessing, and to whom he said: «Εγώ τώρα Αθανάσιε, πηγαίνω στράτα μακρά και πλέον δεν θα βλέπουμε ο ένας τον άλλον. Να έχεις την ευχή της Παναγίας μας» — I am going now, Athanasius, on a long road, and we shall not see one another any more; have the blessing of our Lady. Those were his last words. He then blessed the four points of the horizon and fell asleep in peace on the Sunday of the Myrrhbearers, in the year 1730, at about a hundred years of age. The head is in the Kyriakon of the skete of Kausokalyvia, the jaw in the kalyve of his name there, and a portion of the relic at Dionysiou.
