# Alexander of Thessalonica

saint.gr says Alexander, archbishop of Thessalonica, lived in the third and fourth centuries and took part in the First Ecumenical Council, which met at Nicaea in Bithynia in 325 to condemn the heretical opinions of Arius, and that he played a weighty part in it, enjoying particular honour and authority as a leader and the representative of the Church of the Eastern Illyricum. On the authority of Gelasius of Cyzicus the page gives the form in which he signed at Nicaea, for the first and second Macedonia together with Hellas, all Europe, both Scythias, Thessaly and Achaia.

Two of his letters, the page says, are preserved in Athanasius the Great's Apology against the Arians, and Athanasius himself vouches for them: one sent to Athanasius in 322, rejoicing that the charge of having procured the murder of the Melitian bishop Arsenius had been shown to be false; and one to the imperial commissioner, the count Dionysius, denouncing the machinations of the heretical bishops who took part in the Synod of Tyre in 335 against Athanasius. In the same work Athanasius rebukes the Arians for trying to present Alexander as a fellow author of crimes their own party had committed. Eusebius of Caesarea, in the Life of Constantine, confirms indirectly his presence at Tyre and at the dedication of the church of the Resurrection in Jerusalem on 17 September 335.

The page adds the view of some researchers that the letter of Alexander of Alexandria which Theodoret of Cyrus included in his Church History, and which brands the Arian heresy, is addressed to this Alexander of Thessalonica and not to Alexander of Constantinople.

Two notices touch his work in the city itself: the narrative of Ignatius, abbot of the monastery of Akapniou, on the mosaic of Christ the Saviour in the monastery of Latomou, which tells at length of the catechising and the baptism of Theodora, daughter of Maximian, by Alexander the bishop of the city; and the synaxarion of [Matrona of Thessalonica](/saints/matrona-of-thessalonica), which says that after the persecutions ended Alexander, the first bishop of Thessalonica after the edict of Milan in 313, brought her martyr's relic inside the city and built a church there for it. Having lived according to God and contended stoutly for the Orthodox faith, the page closes, he reposed in peace.
