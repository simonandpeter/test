# Symeon of the Wonderful Mountain

The life doxologia prints opens by saying that Symeon, called of the Wonderful Mountain, who carried his ascetic struggle on a pillar, had a life and wonders more in number than the sand of the sea, and that time, which covers much, had made most of them forgotten, because nobody had taken enough trouble to set down in writing his whole angelic manner of life on earth. The writer says he gathered what he could keep from those before him and saw some of it with his own eyes.

A man named John, of the metropolis of Edessa, came young to the great city of Antioch with his parents, who kept themselves by the trade in aromatics; and there his parents spoke with the parents of a maiden named Martha about a marriage. She refused altogether at first, wishing to give herself to God. Married in the end, she ran to the church of the holy Prophet and Forerunner John the Baptist and stayed a long time there, praying with tears for a child who should serve the goodness of Christ, tasting neither bread nor water and sleeping on the floor.

The Forerunner appeared to her and told her to rise and go to her chamber, and that she would conceive a son and should call him Symeon; he would touch neither meat nor wine nor anything cooked by human craft, and she should feed him from the right breast only, for the child was of the right hand, and give him bread and honey and salt. She tried the left breast some days later and the child turned away from it weeping, and did so every day; and in one night her left breast wasted until it was like a man's, while the right stayed as it had been.

When he was five, an earthquake threw down the city of Antioch; his father was caught under a ruin and died there, and the house was levelled to its foundations. His mother had gone out to pray, and the child, by God's providence, was in the church of the holy protomartyr Stephen beside the city; when that church too fell he wandered through the city, and in the confusion of the ruins his mother could not find him.

He went up the side of the mountain and found a small monastery where a devout man named John sat on a pillar with other monks about him. Before the child came up, John had seen a series of revelations about him: the child in a white garment brought to the monastery on a chariot; the same child winged and flying up to it; the same child walking in the air on a shining pillar and drawing near; and an angel showing him the child. He was received there, and the life tells how the Lord sealed the child's victory over the demon and enlightened him by the Holy Spirit, so that the kingdoms of the devil and his devices were shown to him in an instant.

A pillar of forty feet was made for him, the life says, fulfilling the word of the Gospel that no one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel but on a lampstand, for he was truly a lampstand of the Holy Spirit; and when he was to go up on it the archbishop of Antioch and the bishop of Seleucia came, and with lighted candles and much brightness, with prayers and singing, they led him into the holy altar and ordained him deacon. Near the end of the extract the calendar prints, a father whose child had died was brought to him; the saint smiled at the man's unbelief, had the body set before him, knelt, and called the dead child with the words: He who called Lazarus, four days dead and buried, calls you through me His servant. The child rose before them all, and fear and amazement took everyone who saw it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and under the same epithet, «ο Θαυμαστορείτης» — the man of the Wonderful Mountain — which is the name the Romanian line gives him too. saint.gr adds nothing to his life that the calendar's own line does not carry, and prints no hymn to him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and is the first book read for him to put years to his life. This wonderful saint was born at Antioch in the year 522, it says, in the days of the emperor Justin the Elder. His father perished in an earthquake, and he was left alone with his mother [Martha](/saints/martha-of-antioch). In the sixth year of his life he withdrew into the wilderness to a certain spiritual father named John, and under his guidance gave himself to a great labour of fasting and prayer, to the astonishment of all who saw him.

Having endured terrible temptations from the demons, he received great consolation and grace from the Lord and from His angels; and the Lord Christ appeared to him in the form of a beautiful boy, after which vision a great love for Christ burned up in his heart. Many years he spent upon a pillar, praying to God and chanting psalms. Then, at God’s direction, he withdrew to a mountain which the Lord Himself had named Wonderful; and after the name of that mountain Symeon too was called the Wonderful-Mountaineer.

According to his love for God a rare grace was given him, the Prologue says, by which he healed every sickness, tamed wild beasts, saw into far countries of the world and into the hearts of men, went out of himself and beheld the heavens, conversed with angels, frightened the demons and drove them off, and foretold things to come; and he lived sometimes thirty days without sleep and longer still without food, receiving food from the hands of angels. In him, it says, the Saviour’s words were fulfilled completely: he that believeth in me, the works that I do shall he do also, and greater works than these shall he do. In the year of the Lord 596, and in the seventy-fifth year of his life, he was taken to the Lord.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 24 мая ст. ст. and gives him two readings, the first of which opens by telling the reader to distinguish him from the elder stylite of the same name who died in 459 and is kept on 1 September — «преподобного Симеона Столпника (+ 459 г.; память 1/15 сентября), подвизавшегося также в окрестностях Антиохии Сирийской» — who laboured in the same country round Antioch of Syria. He was born at Antioch of Syria — in 512 by the first reading, in 521 by the second, against the 522 this folder holds from the Ohrid Prologue, and none of the three has been preferred. His mother had asked for him by prayer to John the Forerunner, promising to give the son to God, and from infancy he was marked by strict fasting; several times in his childhood the Lord appeared to him and foretold his labours and the reward of them.

At six years old he went away to a desert mountain, and then settled in the house of Иоанн Столпник near Seleucia of Syria, where he went up on a pillar. For his strictest and more than natural labours of fasting and vigil he was granted a particular grace of God and the gift of healing sickness. In his twenty-second year, by a revelation from above, he moved to the Wonderful Mountain, where he founded a monastery; and reaching a high degree of monastic perfection he worked a great many wonders, saw what was hidden, and was granted the sight of the Lord and converse with him.

So he foretold the taking of Antioch by Chosroes I and the earthquake there; he healed many sicknesses and even raised the dead; men of Antioch held captive by the Persians were set free at the calling of his name; and at his prayer the wheat in the monastery granary did not fail for three years, though it was taken in great quantity to feed the multitude of those who came. Having spent sixty-eight years on the pillar, he died in 596. The second reading names his parents, Иоанн and Марфа, and tells how Марфа, who from her youth had meant to stay unmarried, was shown in a vision in the church of the Forerunner that she should obey her parents and marry; how she prayed often for a child and promised to give him to the Lord’s service; and how the Forerunner appeared to her and opened to her that she would bear a son who would serve God. The child was named Симеон and baptised at two years old.
