# Marianus the Deacon

Marianus was a deacon, and he suffered with [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) and [Daria](/saints/daria) at Rome. Doxologia gives him the same few sentences it gives [Diodorus](/saints/diodorus-the-presbyter) the priest: in a cave near the place of execution the Christians gathered to keep the memory of the martyrs' death, and they held the services there and communed of the Holy Mysteries.

The pagan authorities heard of it, came to the cave and closed its mouth, so that those inside received the crown of martyrdom as well; and two of those martyred in the cave are Diodorus the priest and Marianus the deacon. The longer life of Chrysanthus and Daria says that many clergy died there with them, and that the faithful who ended in the cave were past counting.

That is the whole of what the Romanian calendar prints about him: his rank, his companions and the manner of his death. It gives him no country, no year of his own and no hymn, and none has been supplied here from anywhere else.

The Greek calendar keeps him with [Diodorus](/saints/diodorus-the-presbyter) the presbyter on the same day, and says less than the Romanian does. saint.gr's whole entry for the two is one sentence: they were martyred after being shut up hermetically inside a cave. Over them it prints a couplet which makes the cave a sanctuary — the cave is as a bema, for within it it bears a sacrificer of Christ and a Levite with him, which is the deacon's own order. The page gives no emperor, no year and no country, and no hymn stands under either name.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same menologion day and tells the whole of it in one entry. [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) was the only son of a great boyar, Polemius, who had moved from Alexandria to Rome. As the son of rich parents he went through all the schools of the world, with the most learned men for his teachers; but worldly wisdom confused him and left him uncertain what the truth was, and he grieved over it. Then a written copy of the Gospel and of the Acts of the Apostles came into the young man's hands, and reading them he was lit up by the truth. He wanted a teacher, and found one in a priest named Carpophorus, who instructed him and baptised him.

His father was not pleased, and tried everything to turn him from the faith of Christ. Failing in that, the wicked man first tried to corrupt him, shutting him up alone with shameless girls; but Chrysanthus overcame even himself in this and kept his purity. Then his father forced him to marry a pagan girl, [Daria](/saints/daria) — and Chrysanthus counselled her to accept the faith of Christ as well, and to live as brother and sister though married in appearance. When his father died he began to confess Christ freely and to live as a Christian, he and his whole household.

In the reign of the emperor Numerian both he and Daria were terribly tortured for the faith. But their torturer [Claudius](/saints/claudius-husband-of-hilaria) himself, seeing the patience of these honourable martyrs and the wonders shown at their torture, received the faith of Christ with his whole house; and so Claudius was drowned in water, his two sons were beheaded, and his wife, having said a prayer, breathed her last at the place of execution. Daria held out so far under the torments that the heathen shouted: Daria is a goddess! At the last it was ordered that Chrysanthus and Daria be buried under stones in a deep pit, and a church was raised on that place afterwards.

Near that pit was a cave, in which some Christians once gathered for prayer and communion in memory of the holy martyrs Chrysanthus and Daria; the heathen, learning of it, fell on the place and shut the cave up, and so drove those Christians out of this world by death into a better one, where Christ the Lord reigns in eternity. These glorious martyrs, Chrysanthus and Daria, and the rest with them, among whom [Diodorus](/saints/diodorus-the-presbyter) the priest and Marianus the deacon, suffered for Christ at Rome in 283 and 284 — the years these folders already record.
