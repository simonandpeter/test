# Barlaam of Vazsky

saint.gr's calendar for 19 June names Barlaam the Vazsky and heads his page «(; - 1462)», which is the only number it gives him. The whole of what it says is one sentence: we have no details of the life of the Russian venerable Barlaam. There is no birthplace, no monastery, no epigram and no hymn, and the page prints not even its usual note on the name.

So the calendar keeps the name, the epithet it spells «ο Βάζσκυ», the statement that he was Russian, and the year of his death, and nothing else. This life records that and no more, and nothing here identifies him with any of the other men of the name whom the calendars keep on other days.

The Russian calendar gives him the life the Greek page said it did not have, and it is the whole of what this folder now holds about him. In the world he was called Василий, and he was one of the richest men of Great Novgorod; he held Заволочье and loved that country, and he brought corn-growing into it, where the people before that had lived by hunting and fishing alone and were dying of sickness. He spent much on their teaching and built the «Пинежский городок», set above the river Пинежка, which fell into the Вага sixteen versts below Шенкурск.

He governed honestly and hated injustice after the Saviour's commandment, the page says, and fed orphans, clothed the naked, had the sick cared for and gave rest to travellers. He built many churches along the Вага, and in 1444 he founded a monastery in honour of the evangelist and spent much on its adorning. Loving prayer and seeking salvation, he left the world's business and his wealth and his children and his wife and entered the monastery he had built himself, and was tonsured with the name Barlaam.

He gave himself wholly to the monastic labours of prayer and fasting, and in 1462, in great old age, departed to the Lord — which is the year the Greek page already gave him and the only one either source states. After his death, the page says, he appeared to the monks of his own monastery and urged them to give alms generously and to give away everything to the poor in time of famine; and at his tomb many of the sick and the blind were healed.
