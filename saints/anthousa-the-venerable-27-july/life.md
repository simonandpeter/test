# Anthousa (27 July)

doxologia.ro's calendar names her and gives no life. Its line for 27 iulie is «Sfânta Cuvioasă Antuza» — the venerable Anthousa — and her own page on the site prints only the day and the epistle and gospel read on it. There is no Viață tab, no troparion, no city and no century.

The corpus already keeps two women of this name, and neither of them is claimed here: Anthousa of Seleucia, a martyr, whom the Russian and Serbian calendars keep on 22 August, and Anthousa the New, whom the Russian and Greek calendars keep on 27 August. The Romanian line gives a venerable, not a martyr, on a July day, and there is nothing on the page by which to say she is either of them; so this folder stands for that line alone and is marked by its day.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 27 July, and calls her the confessor. saint.gr says the venerable Anthousa the confessor lived in the years of the emperor Constantine Copronymus, which it dates to 741; her parents, Strategios and Phebronia, were marked out for their piety and brought their daughter up in the same way. For all the offers of marriage she had, Anthousa stayed a virgin, and when her parents died she did not change her decision but dedicated her father's property to works of charity and to sacred ends. Two monasteries, the page says, are owed to her: that of Mantineon, with a church dedicated to Saint Anna, and that of the Holy Apostles, which was used as a women's monastery.

When a hard persecution was ordered by the emperor Constantine Copronymus against the holy icons and those who upheld them, the monastery of the venerable Anthousa was among the most fervent centres of Orthodoxy, and for that the venerable one was tortured at first. But when she foretold that the sick empress would escape death and would bear twins, she was then much loved by her, and the empress supported her monastery in various ways; and Anthousa, undisturbed from then on, died in peace. Above the notice the page sets a distich: «Xριστόν μεν αινεί Aνθούσα παρά πλάνοις, Xριστός δε αύθις Aνθούσαν παρ’ Aγγέλοις» — Anthousa praises Christ among deceivers, and Christ in turn praises Anthousa among the Angels.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 27 July and gives her two sentences. After a long struggle in solitude she founded a monastery of ninety sisters. In the time of the iconoclasm under Constantine Copronymus all ninety nuns were killed, and the venerable Anthousa herself came to her end after them, in the year 759.
