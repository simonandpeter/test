# David of Gareji

David of Gareji is one of the thirteen Assyrian fathers who were sent into Georgia to preach the holy Gospel. He settled with his disciple Lucian in a cave in a mountain near the city of Tbilisi, in the region of Kartli, which in that time was under the constant threat of the Persians. David would pass whole days in prayer, begging the Lord for the forgiveness of men's sins, and once a week he and Lucian would go down into the city to preach; a church dedicated to Saint David was later built on the mountain where the two fathers laboured.

But the devil, who hates what is good, envied the saint for his virtue and his work, and set a woman with no fear of God to speak evil of him and say he had sinned with her. That woman, who was unmarried and a virgin, was with child; the fire-worshippers of Georgia paid her a great deal of money and persuaded her to accuse the righteous David and to say he had deceived her. So the saint was taken to the court and examined face to face with his accuser. He gave no answer to the judges' questions about her charges; and having heard them out in silence and calm, he raised his staff, on which a cross was engraved, touched the woman's belly and asked the child: "Am I thy father? Answer at once!" And the child, from its mother's womb, made the truth plain, answering three times, "No! No! No!" — and named its father straight away, who was a certain smith. After that wonder the court set the saint free.

After this David left the world and went into the desert of Gareji with his disciple Lucian. The two fathers settled in a small cave in the wilderness and spent all their time in prayer, eating nothing but herbs and the bark of trees. When the herbs withered in the summer heat, the Lord sent them deer; Lucian milked them and brought the milk to David, and when the elder made the sign of the cross over it, it was turned miraculously into cheese. Shaken by the wonder, Lucian said to him: "Even if my body rot and be scattered with hunger and thirst, I will not let myself fear the things of this earthly life."

A fearful dragon lived in a cave not far from the place where they lived and attacked all the animals around it; but at David's command it left that place. Once the local hunters came upon Lucian milking the deer, which stood quietly as though they were sheep, and the hunters understood then what great men were living there, and told what they had seen when they came home. Soon the desert of Gareji filled with those who longed to draw near to Christ. A monastery was founded, and for centuries it was the centre and the foundation-stone of the faith and of learning in Georgia.

After some time David set out on a pilgrimage to Jerusalem. He left Lucian to carry out his duties at the monastery and took some of the other brethren with him. When they reached the Mount of Olives, from which the holy city of Jerusalem is seen, David fell on his knees and glorified God with tears; reckoning himself unworthy to walk in the footsteps of Jesus Christ, he was content to look at the city from afar. He turned back from there, taking with him three stones in a basket.

That night an angel appeared to Elias, patriarch of Jerusalem, and told him that a certain devout man named David had taken away with him all the holiness of Jerusalem: "My chosen one David has come as far as the gates of Jerusalem to receive the grace of the city. Send swift-footed men at once to overtake him. He is already going away from the city. He is dressed in old clothes and has a basket in his hand, in which he has put three stones. Let them take two of them from him and say to him: God gives thee this command — thou hast taken, according to thy faith, the grace of my city, holy Jerusalem; but for thy desert only one part is enough, and the other two parts must be left here. Go, then, in peace with this stone, which will heal the bodies and the souls of all who touch it with faith."

The patriarch woke troubled by the dream. He called swift-footed men without delay, told them what he had seen and bade them run and overtake the venerable David; and after a time they stood with respect before the man of God, who heard their story of the angel's appearing to the patriarch with wonder and fear. "Blessed be my Lord Jesus Christ," he cried, raising to heaven his weeping eyes and his trembling hands, "blessed be the Deliverer and Saviour of the world, who has shown his good will to me, his sinful servant, by giving me his grace." Then he took two stones out of the basket and gave them to the patriarch's messengers, who carried them to Jerusalem and delivered them to the patriarch Elias.

The wonder was told quickly through the holy city and the country around it, and all who heard it asked to meet Saint David; but the father had already gone. The stone he brought into the desert of Gareji began to work many wonders, and many of the sick and the crippled who kissed it with faith were made whole by the power of Christ; that stone has kept the grace of wonder-working to this day. A little later the brethren who had venerated the Holy Places returned to Georgia, and among their other impressions of the places where God had walked they told what the people of Jerusalem had been saying about father David and the patriarch Elias's dream.

Very soon, hearing of the saint's ascetic labours and of the stone brought so wonderfully from Palestine, many anchorites gathered in Gareji. They lived in caves in fasting and prayer, in obedience to the blessed David and following his saving counsels faithfully; and so that wilderness filled with ascetics like the far-off Thebaid of Egypt, and for that reason it was called the Georgian Thebaid.

The angel of God told David beforehand of his going out of this world. Then he called all the brethren and gave them his last counsels; and after he had communicated he raised his hands to heaven and gave up his holy soul in peace to God, who in that very moment showed the honour due to his chosen one by a wonderful healing: a monk, blind from birth, touched the saint's dead body and received his sight. Countless other wonders followed, for which the divine David was called a worker of wonders; and in our own hard times too, any believer who anoints himself with earth from his grave, which gives off a fragrance beyond telling, is healed of every sickness of body and of soul. The saint fell asleep on the first Thursday after the feast of the Ascension, and on that day his memory is kept each year; the year of his repose is not exactly known, but it was in any case in the second half of the sixth century. The page prints no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and tells how it was chosen. saint.gr prints one line and one page for [John](/saints/john-of-zedazeni) and his disciples, and gives each of the thirteen a head line of his own, his reading «Οσιος Δαβιδ Μαθητης Του Οσιου Ιωαννη». John came out of Syria, it says, and lived about the end of the fifth century; and when the crowds that came to him had broken his stillness and he had hidden deep in the desert, he saw the Mother of God in a dream, who commanded him to choose twelve disciples and go with them into Iberia to establish the Christian faith of its people. All his subordinates wished to go, so he wrote every name on a separate paper and laid them on the holy table; and after a night of prayer, in the sight of them all, an angel of the Lord took twelve papers from the altar and put them into his hands.

David is named among the twelve the papers chose. An angel warned Eulogius, bishop of the Georgian capital, of their coming, and the bishop went out with his clergy and his people to receive them; and there followed a wonder like the one at Pentecost, for John spoke the Georgian tongue. The thirteen preached through all Georgia and venerated the blessed places through which Saint Nina had passed, whom this calendar keeps on 14 January. The page gives no year for any of them, no hymn, and no word of where each of the twelve settled afterwards.
