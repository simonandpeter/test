# Theodotus of Marcianopolis

Theodotus was a devout man of Marcianopolis in Thrace who, when Tiris,
governor of Thrace, had the Christians
[Maximus](/saints/maximus-of-marcianopolis) and
[Asclepiodote](/saints/asclepiodote-of-marcianopolis) beaten for refusing to
abandon the faith, reproached him for his cruelty — and was seized for it,
hung on a tree and torn with iron hooks. That was in the persecution of
Maximian Galerius, between 305 and 311. The three were thrown into prison
and carried along on the governor’s circuit for two weeks; at Adrianople
their bodies were scorched with white-hot plates, and in the middle of it
they heard a voice from heaven telling them to hold out. Thrown to the
beasts in the circus after several days of torture, they were not touched —
the she-bear loosed on Maximus and Theodotus nuzzled them, and the bull
Asclepiodote was tied to would not move — and at the village of Saltis,
short of Philippopolis, when they refused Tiris once more, he had them
beheaded. He was struck by lightning where he sat on the judgement seat.

That is the Orthodox Church in America’s account. The Greek synaxarion’s is
shorter and differs in the manner: scourged, their hands and feet cut off,
imprisoned and at last beheaded — in one woman and two young men, its verse
says, there was one fervour of heart towards the cutting. The Greek calendar
keeps the three on 17 September; the Russian calendar keeps them on the
15th of its own reckoning, the day the Orthodox Church in America prints.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps Theodotus, Asclepiodote (its form is Асклиад) and Maximus together, calls them Thracians of distinguished birth, and says they suffered for Christ near Philippopolis, in the village of Saltis, between 305 and 311, where the other sources of this folder name Marcianopolis.
