# Christos of Andravida

Christos was one of the two new martyrs of Elis whom saint.gr keeps together on 9 Μαρτίου. He was born, as the page has it, «εις ένα χωριόν της αυτής πόλεως Ανδραβίδα», in a village of Andravida in the old Elis, and his parents, like those of [Panagos](/saints/panagos-of-gastouni), had been forced over to Islam under the pressure and the persecutions of the Turkish conquerors. Unlike them, he advanced in virtue and was fed with the fear of God and the commandments of the Gospel, and was held in esteem among his countrymen.

He turned away from public affairs and had a keen longing «να γένει ιερεύς υπανδρός», to be a married priest, and not to have the understanding of his soul clouded by the deceits and vanities of the world; and so he was later ordained priest at Patras. In 1715 the Peloponnese passed back under the Turks after thirty years of Venetian rule, and those who would not bow to Allah were plundered and slaughtered. Christos was arrested and lay in irons in prison, while the judge urged him not to throw away this sweetest life, and his two innocent babes, and his wife.

He did not give way to the threats, and was beaten and thrown back into prison. His wife was sent in to him, and under her warm and unceasing entreaties he at last gave way and promised her that the next day he would do as she advised; and the news went round the whole city, to the joy of the Muslims and the grief of the Christians. But when the other priests of the place were shut up in the same prison, the eldest of them reproached him before them all for his decision. Christos groaned from the bottom of his heart and repented at once, to the joy of his fellow presbyters, who strengthened him to go on to martyrdom; and he spent that whole night in prayers, psalmody and tears.

In the morning he stood before Murat and said that he thanked his Christ and King of creation, who had not let him fall into perdition but had sent him saving guides and led him back by the hand to piety; and looking up to the heavens he said «Μη γένοιτο, Χριστέ μου, να σε αρνηθώ ποτέ» — may it never be, my Christ, that I deny you. To his executioners he said that he had been a Christian and was one still, and worshipped Christ with the Father and the Holy Spirit.

His words shook the impious badly, and they ordered him beheaded without delay. He bent his knee and was killed by the sword, and lay unburied four days; then, with the leave of his killers, he was laid in the same church of St Nicholas, in the same tomb into which Panagos had been put. saint.gr dates his end to the ninth of March 1716 and says that the service of the two is sung together with that of the forty martyrs of the same day.
