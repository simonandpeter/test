# Meletius of Stânișoara

All his care was to please God and to live in fasting and prayer and
purity of body and soul — to keep his thoughts always cleansed, to cast
down every high thing that rises against the knowledge of God, and to
bring all his understanding captive to the obedience of Christ. So the
Romanian life of Meletius, who advanced well in secular learning and
surpassed many, and was persuaded that it is more profitable to renounce
the world and follow Christ, giving up all that is of this world to gain
the true wisdom, which is union with God glorified in Trinity and life
with Him for ever, for the wisdom from above is first pure, then
peaceable, gentle, easy to be entreated, full of mercy and good fruits.
Knowing well that the soul cannot be divided in two, to live in pleasure
and in restraint, in pride and in humility, he chose the good — a choice
by which we are condemned or saved; what we choose we have, and with it we
remain — and left the delights of the world as false and passing things to
inherit the true and eternal, entering the community of the monastery of
Cozia, where he found zealous fathers longing to live in God, ready to
guide souls to heaven. In full obedience there he wrote every word of the
fathers on the tablet of his heart and fulfilled it in deed; weighing the
vanity of the world and turning over the saying of Macarius, that the
soul not freed from worldly cares will neither truly love God nor hate the
devil as he deserves, he judged he must flee the world further, since
every day all praised him — one for his love to all, another for his
humility, his wisdom, his restraint, his contempt of worldly glory and
vanity — and he bore the trials of the common life hoping to be freed of
them, longing for the hermit’s life. He did not seek it out of order but
took counsel with the other fathers who desired perfect stillness, Daniel
and Misail, then Neophytos and Isaiah, with whom he went to the mountain
called Sălbaticul; they urged him to endure in the community to be
tempered further, quoting Sabbas, that as the flower goes before the fruit
so the common life must go before the hermit’s, in which beginners, like
newly planted trees, flower in the first-fruits of their labours, to bear
in the desert the perfect fruit.

After Daniel and Misail had settled in the caves they dug at Turnu, and
Neophytos with the other hermits had gone up to make caves on the
Sălbaticul, Meletius too went to the abbot for the customary blessing to
live in solitude, was told to stay near someone, and went to make his cave
beside Neophytos, to have him as guide in the things of the desert. He
found a place on the north-eastern slope; the cave, as it can be seen
today, is little more than a hollow he did not deepen much, its mouth
turned upward so that from within nothing is seen but the sky, and the
climb to it is harder than to Neophytos’s, so that it is less visited.
There he lived more than twenty years, settling his nature on the
hardness of the stones, speaking only with God and conquering the unseen
enemies by unceasing prayer; there was no water at all in those parts, and
he had to go a long way to a waterfall to fetch what the body needs, and
bore that labour many times, with no one to help him, into deep old age.
All the hermits founded their struggle on enduring in the cave where they
first settled, not wandering the forests and mountains, knowing that
patience and steadfastness are not won that way, and that, as the
Paterikon says, as a fish taken from the water cannot live long, so a monk
who leaves his cell cannot remain alive in the work of God’s
commandments. So Meletius stayed long years in his cave and there saw his
hair and beard turn white. From Neophytos, who had finished the work of
the desert and passed the devices of the enemy, he received good counsel:
to keep no worldly fantasy in his mind, to keep his mind whole and clean
of every vain passion and conceit, to desire no revelations or knowledge
of hidden mysteries, and not to trust his own understanding, to the end of
his life, lest he give credence to visions; and doing these things in
deed, by thought of God and frequent prayer he was kindled with the fire
of love for God, as it is written, my heart grew hot within me. Every
Sunday he came down with Neophytos to the hermitage of Daniel and Misail
at Turnu to receive the Holy Mysteries, and after communion wet his face
with tears, bringing all who saw him to compunction by his sighs and
prayers of the heart. The devil, hater of good, grieved at his zeal and
strove to trap him in some fall of soul, but could not, for the athlete of
Christ had conquered every passion; he tried to sow tares in his mind, but
nothing grew, for the saint’s ground was sown only with the words of the
Saviour, which gave a great harvest, since he had not sown sparingly; all
his longing was turned to God alone, who repaid him with rich gifts, so
that he became wholly a chosen vessel like the great Paul, with Christ
living in his soul, no longer living after the flesh but after the spirit,
in Christ and Christ in him. Reaching forward to what was before and
forgetting what was behind, he did not count the struggles and good deeds
done but strove to do more, going from strength to strength each day, and
bore the infirmities of old age with perfect greatness of soul and great
thanksgiving.

So his labour was accepted and God heard his prayers wonderfully: when
for age he could no longer go to the place where he drew water, he turned
in that want to almighty God — Lord, You who gathered all the waters into
the seas and ordered the rivers well, who by Your will make springs gush
forth for good drinking water and from the heavens make the clouds drop
rain — and, a wonder, a spring of good cold water burst out strongly
beside his cave, seen to this day and known to all as the spring of St
Meletius, from which the monastery of Stânișoara draws all its water, its
only source. By such wonders the saints are assured of entering the
heavenly dwellings, and in that hope Meletius gave up his blessed soul
with a peaceful end into the hands of God, known to none but Him who
received him into His bosom, and was numbered in heaven with those he had
longed for, the hermits Daniel, Misail and Neophytos. Not long after his
falling asleep his relics were found by the monks still living as hermits
there, and many of the faithful who came to Neophytos’s cave climbed on to
Meletius’s, lighting candles, praying, venerating the relics and drinking
their fill from his spring; the cave being unguarded and open to all, the
more devout among faithful and monks took away pieces of the relics,
wishing to have so great a treasure with them, until with the passing of
many years nothing of them remained — so God perhaps willed, He who can
reveal all things at the appointed time — and Meletius, unseen in the
body, entered in spirit the incorruptible, undefiled and unfading
inheritance kept for him in heaven by the springs of eternal life, in
which spirit he guards the monastery of Stânișoara and all who worship
there, working wonders for those who come to him with faith and ask his
help. What reward the holy hermits have from God we learn from the life of
Onuphrius the Great, where it is asked whether those who live in the
desert are greater than those in community, and answered: they are greater
than we, for we see one another every day and sing the church’s song
together with joy; if we hunger we find bread ready, if we thirst water
enough; if one of us falls ill the brothers comfort him, for we live in
common and help and serve one another for the love of God; but those in
the desert lack all this — if grief comes on one of them, who comforts
him? in sickness, who helps and serves him? if a war comes on him from
Satan, where will he find a man to change his thought and counsel him,
being alone? There their labour is beyond compare greater, for those who
enter the hermit’s life serve God more, give themselves to greater fasts,
bear hunger, thirst, the heat of the day and the cold of the night
bravely, resist the wars of the unseen enemy stoutly, force themselves in
every way to conquer him, and strive to pass the whole strait and hard way
that leads to the kingdom of heaven. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 3
September with [Neophytos](/saints/neophytos-of-stanisoara), the two
hermits of the monastery of Stânișoara, with a common troparion and
kontakion; the life is from the archdiocese of Râmnic.
