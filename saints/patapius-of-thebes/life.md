# Patapius of Thebes

Patapius was born at Thebes in Egypt, the city the Nile goes round and waters, of Christian parents, and was brought up in the faith and the fear of the Lord. Come to full age, he looked past the vanity of this world and left house, parents and friends to be a monk, going out into the Egyptian desert to live to God in fasting, prayer and every kind of monastic and hermit's labour. When he became known and many began to come to him and to praise him, the noise of it troubled his quiet, and for that reason he left Egypt.

He came to Constantinople and made himself a hut near Blachernae, against the city wall, and shut himself into it and rested there as in the desert, known to nobody but God, talking with him in unceasing prayer. But a city on a hilltop cannot hide, doxologia's life says, and neither can a man come to holiness; so he was found out like treasure hidden in a field. A young Christian blind from birth was led to his hut and begged him to ask God for his sight. Patapius, thinking himself unworthy, first asked him what he had seen in him and why he asked of a man what only the Maker of the world can do; and when the young man wept and said he believed without doubt, he said: in the name of Jesus Christ, who gives the blind sight and the dead life, see. The eyes opened at once.

After that many came. A well-known man of Constantinople, swollen with dropsy and past the help of the physicians he had spent his fortune on, was carried to him; Patapius — the physician who took no silver, the life calls him — prayed, signed him with the Cross and anointed him with blessed oil, and the whole body was made sound. A young man whom a demon drove over mountains and into fire and water met him on the road to the sea, where the demon meant to drown him; the spirit cried out that it was beaten and driven out by the sign of the Cross and the Name alone, and at his word it threw the young man down and went out of his mouth like smoke. A woman with an incurable hurt in her breast was healed when he signed the wound.

These and many other wonders he did; and in deep old age he came to his blessed end and passed over to God, and was buried with honour in the church of the Forerunner.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 8 December, and reads his childhood through a verse of Paul. saint.gr has him born in Egypt and possessed from a small child of a spirit of love and of sober-mindedness — the words of the second letter to Timothy — so that he governed himself prudently and sensibly, avoiding every moral straying and keeping his purity, and at the same time set an example to those about him; and this, the page says, he proved still more when he grew up.

Having shared out his inheritance among the poor he withdrew to the desert, where he filled his time with prayer, with study and with works of mercy. To every tired traveller who passed his cell he offered rest and hospitality, and taking the occasion he gave him, with discernment, spiritual directions and counsels useful for the saving of his soul; so his fame spread quickly and every day many came to his cell to hear profitable teaching from his lips.

After a time Patapius went to Constantinople, and wishing to stay unknown there he chose a hesychasterion at the Blachernae. But his humble and clean life, the page says, made him worthy to work wonders, and so he became known again; and he died healing the sick. His relic lies incorrupt at the monastery of his name at Loutraki in Corinthia.
