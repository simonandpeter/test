# Nicholas (Zhitov)

Nicholas was born in 1888 in the village of Pyzhovo in the Zaraisk district
of Ryazan province, the son of the psalmist James Zhitov, finished the
church school, and from 1905 was psalmist of the Resurrection church in his
own village. In 1915 he was ordained deacon for St George’s at Zaraisk and
in 1917 moved to the cemetery church there; in 1920 he was ordained priest
for the Resurrection church at Pyzhovo, moved in 1930 to St Nicholas at
Aponitishchi, in 1931 to the Kazan church at Slemskiye Borki in the
Lukhovitsy district, and in 1934 back to Aponitishchi; he was given the
pectoral cross in 1930 and made archpriest in 1936. In August 1937 the
cruellest of the persecutions began, and on 4 September he was arrested. The
NKVD called the chairman of the collective farm and a married couple he used
to visit, who told all they knew — that he had grown up among poor peasants
and lost his last property in the persecution, which did not trouble him
since he was used to every kind of work, that he was first of all a
conscientious pastor but scorned no peasant labour, and often earned his
bread glazing the peasants’ windows and making them tubs; while they talked
the interrogator wrote, and when they finished handed them the record to
sign, and so great was the trust of some and the fear of others that without
asking to hear it or reading it themselves they signed what he had written
of the priest’s counter-revolutionary and anti-Soviet activity. On 5
September he told the interrogator that he had done no counter-revolutionary
work among the people and had not slandered the Soviet press; the
interrogator, having signed false witness enough, did not press him, drew up
the indictment on 8 September, and on 22 September the troika sentenced him
to death. He was shot on 25 September 1937 and buried in an unknown common
grave at Butovo. The life is by the igumen Damascene (Orlovsky); the Church
keeps him on 12 September of the old style, 25 September, and in the synaxes
of the new martyrs of Butovo and of Moscow.
