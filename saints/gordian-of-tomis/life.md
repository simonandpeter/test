# Gordian of Tomis

Gordian and Seleucus took the crown of martyr’s glory in the fourth century under Licinius; Gordian, Seleucus and Macrobius were martyred in Galatia, the Greek synaxarion says — having confessed Christ they were thrown by the unbelievers as food to the wild beasts, the beastly men giving them to the savage beasts, its verse says. The Romanian Lives tell the martyrs of Dobrogea together: Gordian, Macrobius, Helias, Valerian, Zoticus, Seleucus and Lucian suffered about 323–324 in the persecution of Licinius. Macrobius and Gordian were two young men of Asia Minor, one of Paphlagonia and one of Cappadocia, both serving at the court of Licinius; one day it was found that they were Christians, and they were exiled to Scythia Minor, to Tomis.

There the trial and execution of Zoticus, Lucian and Helias took place, beheaded at the last for refusing to deny the Christian faith, and the two exiles were made to watch; afterwards Macrobius and Gordian received martyrdom too, condemned to be burned, and the last of those kept this day, Valerian, died weeping on the graves of his brothers in the faith. The Romanian calendar keeps the six on 13 September with a troparion — let us honour with faith the holy martyrs who in Tomis, by torments, sword and fire, found a good end.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps [Macrobius](/saints/macrobius-of-tomis) and Gordian together and says only that they were from Paphlagonia and had first been the emperor’s table-servants; when they made themselves known as Christians the emperor banished them to Scythia, where they were thrown into the fire at Noviodunum, in 320. It names neither Tomis nor their companions, and its year is a few earlier than the Romanian Lives’ 323–324.
