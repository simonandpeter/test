# Eadbert of Lindisfarne

What is known of him, saint.gr says, is told by Saint Bede in the Ecclesiastical History. He was marked out for an excellent knowledge of the Scriptures and for his love of the poor, since he distributed a tenth of the income of his bishopric; and he was appointed successor to Saint [Cuthbert](/saints/cuthbert-of-lindisfarne), who is honoured on 20 March, in the bishopric of Lindisfarne in the year 687, and governed that church for eleven years.

In Great Lent, and again in the fast of the Nativity, he used to withdraw to a deserted place by himself, mourning, fasting and praying; and he had the gift of tears. In his own days there took place the translation of the holy relics of his predecessor Cuthbert, who was found incorrupt. He fell asleep in peace in the year 698. The page heads him «Άγιος Eadbert εκ Σκωτίας», Eadbert from Scotland, and links the synaxis of the saints who shone in Scotland, though the see it gives him lies in Northumbria; both are printed here as the page has them.
