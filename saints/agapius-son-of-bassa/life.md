# Agapius, son of Bassa

[Bassa](/saints/bassa-of-edessa) lived at Edessa in Macedonia, married to a pagan priest; brought up from childhood in the Christian faith, she handed it on to her sons Theognius, Agapius and Pistus. Under Maximian Galerius (305–311) her husband denounced his wife and children to the governor; all of them, despite the threats, refused to sacrifice to the idols, and the children were tortured before their mother’s eyes — the eldest, Theognius, hung up and raked with iron claws; the boy Agapius flayed from head to breast without a sound; then the youngest, Pistus — while the mother never stopped heartening them to endure for Christ. The boys were beheaded. The Prologue adds that Bassa, full of joy at seeing her sons finish their contest, went the more willingly from torment to torment; thrown into the sea, she was carried by angels to an island in the Sea of Marmara, where she was beheaded — doubly worthy of the kingdom, as martyr and as mother of martyrs.
