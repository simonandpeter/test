# Boniface of Tarsus

Boniface was the household servant of [Aglaia](/saints/aglaia-of-rome), a rich woman of Rome, the daughter of a man named Acacius who had been proconsul of the city. The life is plain that the two lived together in impurity, and plainer still about why it says so: the saints are praised without their earlier sins being passed over in silence, so that nobody should despair of his own salvation. It adds that Boniface was a drunkard, and that he was also merciful to the poor, hospitable to strangers, and grieved with those in trouble.

Aglaia was seized with a longing to have the relics of martyrs in her house, and sent Boniface east with a great sum of gold to buy them, with spices and clean linen for the bodies. Going out of the door he asked her, laughing, whether she would receive his own body with honour if he were martyred instead. On the road he began to fast, to eat no meat and drink no wine, and to pray, reckoning his hands unclean for the relics of saints.

At Tarsus, the famous city of Cilicia, in the reign of Diocletian and Maximian, he left the servants at the inn and went straight to the place where Christians were being tortured. He saw twenty martyrs under torments the life describes one by one, embraced each of them, kissed their feet, and cried out in the hearing of the crowd that great is the God of the Christians. The judge had him seized; asked his name, he answered first that he was a Christian.

He was hung head downward and beaten until his flesh fell away, had sharpened reeds driven under his nails, and had molten lead poured into his mouth, which did not harm him. The crowd rioted and the judge fled. The next day he was thrown into a cauldron of boiling pitch and came out unhurt, and the judge ordered him beheaded. Blood and milk ran from the wound, and the life says five hundred and fifty of the unbelievers turned to Christ.

His companions, who had thought he was drunk somewhere, found the body under guard, recognised it, and bought it with five hundred gold pieces; and when they looked at his face he opened his eyes a little, smiled at them and forgave them. An angel warned Aglaia in a dream to receive the man who had been her servant as her lord. She built a church in his name in a village of hers fifty stadia from Rome and laid his relics there, where the sick were healed and the possessed delivered.
