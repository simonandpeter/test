# Barlaam of India

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 19 November on one line with the prince and the prince’s father, «Прпп. Варлаама и Иоасафа, царевича Индийского, и отца его Авенира царя (IV).», and the name link behind his place in it, name page 353, gives «Варлаам Индийский, иерей, пустынник» — a priest and a hermit. Its life page, titled for the three of them, has him a wise solitary struggling in a far desert, who learned by a revelation of God of a young man suffering in the search for the truth.

Coming out of the desert he went to India in the guise of a merchant and, reaching the city where the prince’s palace stood, announced that he had brought with him a precious stone having the wonder-working property of healing sicknesses. Brought in to [Joasaph, son of Abenner](/saints/joasaph-of-india), he set the Christian faith before him in parables and then out of the holy Gospel, baptised him, and went back to his own desert.

When the prince in his turn had left the kingdom he wandered the desert two years, bearing troubles and temptations, until he found the cave where the elder was being saved in silence; and the elder and the young man struggled on together. When the time of his end drew near the hermit served the liturgy, communicated of the holy mysteries and gave communion to his disciple, and so departed to the Lord, having been seventy years in the desert out of the hundred he lived. The prince buried him and stayed on in the same cave.

The line gives the century, «(IV)», and no year; the page gives his years in the desert and his age at his death and no date at all, and this corpus derives no year by arithmetic from either. It names India, the desert and the cave and gives no coordinate for any of them, so this folder carries no place. The rank words on the name page are the whole of what it says about his standing: a priest and a hermit, under the line’s «Прпп.»

The Охридски пролог calls the two of them ascetics of India and tells their story as one. [Joasaph](/saints/joasaph-of-india) was a king's son, whose father the entry names and whom this folder has no row for. By God's providence the elder Barlaam visited him, taught him the faith of Christ and baptised him; after which the elder withdrew into the mountain to his ascetic labour, while Joasaph stayed to struggle with many temptations in the world and to overcome them by the grace of God.

Joasaph's one desire on earth, the entry says, was to see his spiritual father the elder Barlaam once more, and the merciful God fulfilled it: one day Joasaph stood before Barlaam's cave and called out, Bless, father! Barlaam laboured in the desert seventy years and lived a hundred years in all. Both of them had great love for the Lord Jesus; they brought many over to the true faith, and passed into the eternal joy of their Lord. The book gives him no year of birth or death, only those two ages, and neither is turned into a date here.
