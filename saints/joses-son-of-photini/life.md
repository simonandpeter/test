# Joses, son of Photini

saint.gr's calendar for 26 Φεβρουαρίου prints «Άγιος Ιωσής» as a line of its own, and the page standing under it gives one sentence: «Ο Άγιος Ιωσής ήταν ο δευτερότοκος υιός της Αγίας Φωτεινής και μαρτύρησε δια ξίφους» — Joses was the second-born son of St Photini, and he was martyred by the sword. The fuller story is on the page of [Photini the Samaritan](/saints/photini-the-samaritan), which says that her sons and her five sisters were martyred together with her: she had been baptised among the first women of Samaria after her meeting with Christ at Jacob's well, had preached the Gospel in Africa and at Rome, and was put to death at Rome by the emperor Nero.

The rest of the household the calendar prints beside his on this day is [Anatole](/saints/anatole-sister-of-photini), [Photo](/saints/photo-sister-of-photini), [Photis](/saints/photis-sister-of-photini), [Paraskeve](/saints/paraskeve-sister-of-photini), [Kyriake](/saints/kyriake-sister-of-photini) and [Photinos](/saints/photinos-son-of-photini). This page gives Joses no birthplace, no year, no relics and no hymn of his own — the second apolytikion on Photini's page names all eight of them together — and over the one sentence it prints a couplet, «Τομεὺς τραχήλων ἐξεγύμνου τὸ ξίφος, Ἰωσῆ δὲ τράχηλος οὐ φρίττει ξίφος»: the cutter of necks bared the sword, but Joses's neck does not shudder at the sword.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 26 Φεβρουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/02/26/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3649/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
