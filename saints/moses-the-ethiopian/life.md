# Moses the Ethiopian

Moses was a bought slave of a rich landowner, hard and unruly, making trouble daily until his master lost patience and threw him out. He found refuge in a band of robbers and, with his enormous bodily strength, soon imposed himself and became their chief.

Hunted by the authorities for his many crimes, he went to hide deep in the desert where the most famous ascetics lived, and their company slowly tamed him; the grace of God overshadowed him, his heart softened, he repented truly and asked for redemption. The change was radical, and in a short time he reached the measure of the great fathers of the desert; after baptism he was even ordained priest. Two virtues adorned him above all, true repentance and deep humility: to his last breath he wept bitterly for his sins and held himself lower not only than men but than the dumb creation. Once four robbers, his old companions, broke into his hut not imagining whom they would find; he tied them up with ease, carried them to the assembly of the elders and asked what he should do with them — it is no longer fitting for me to punish a man — and they confessed, repented and became monks. On the day the patriarch of Alexandria ordained him, and said kindly as he vested him that he had become white as a dove, Moses asked whether he judged by the outside or the inside; the patriarch, to test his humility, had the clergy drive him from the sanctuary with insults, and he went at once without a word, reproaching himself. At seventy-five he left this life violently: pagan robbers broke into the cave where he was living and killed him with knives, and so the Lord’s word to Peter came true once more, that all who take the sword die by the sword.
