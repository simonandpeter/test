# Jerome of Simonopetra

Jerome was born in 1871 at Reiz-Dere in Asia Minor, and was called Ioan Diakoghorghis in the world. He came to the monastery of Simonopetra on the Holy Mountain on 28 October 1888 and was tonsured a monk on 21 March 1893, Palm Sunday. In February 1914 the brotherhood set him at its head; he was ordained deacon on 11 April 1920 and priest the following day, and on 20 April, the Sunday of the Myrrh-bearing Women, he was enthroned abbot of the house of his repentance.

On 15 June 1931 he was exiled to the monastery of Koutloumousiou, and three months later sent to the church of the Ascension of the Lord in Athens. In 1937 he was asked to come back to the abbot's throne at Simonopetra and refused, quietly. Twenty years later, on 7 January 1957 — the eve of the Nativity by the old calendar, as the life reckons it — he gave up his last breath. He had lived seventeen years in his own country, forty-three at Simonopetra, and twenty-six at the Ascension, which the life calls his guiding light.

doxologia.ro's shorter notice on the saint's own page differs from the life at two points, and both readings are the source's own. The notice says he entered Simonopetra at seventeen and was tonsured five years later, and that in 1937 he was appointed abbot of the Monastery of the Ascension in Athens and led it for twenty years. They are left standing side by side here rather than reconciled.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the ninth of May, and saint.gr's page adds his childhood. He was born, it says, in the village of Reiz-Dere in the district of Krini in Asia Minor in 1871, of poor and pious parents, Nikolaos Diakogiorgis and Maria. The village was purely Christian, five kilometres north-east of Alatsata and two and a half from the sea, and most of its people were farmers and vine-growers whose families had come from Crete and the Peloponnese. At his baptism he received the name John. At school he was a good pupil and outdid his fellows in quickness and good sense, so much so that his teacher sent him for a short while to act as schoolmaster in a nearby town as soon as he had finished the elementary school.

The village church became the centre of his life, where he found what his soul was asking for: the joy and the blessing of God poured out on him through the mysteries, the prayers and the services. He loved the sacred offices, the priests, the chanters, the vigils and the country chapels, and he helped the chanters at the lectern and the priests in the sanctuary; from a boy he seemed grown up in his silence, his seriousness and his reverence. Born to poor people, he tasted poverty early, and later, as a monk, would follow it faithfully by his own will. Very little is known of his early years. His mother left living marks of her love on him; from her he first heard the lives of the saints and learned to fast, to pray and to love God, and the saints became his friends early. His own people lost sight of him more and more, but they knew they would find him at the country chapels. Saint Demetrius healed him twice, once when he had fearful pains in his legs and again when he had chickenpox, and both times he stayed forty days in that saint's church, fasting.

One evening his sister heard him saying the Salutations of the Mother of God, and in the morning she asked him whether he knew them. No, he said. Then here is your chance to learn them, she told him. «From seven years old I knew the Salutations by heart», the elder used to say afterwards. His mother's great faith showed itself before her end, when she put on the monastic habit she had loved since she was a girl and was renamed Melane; his brother became a monk with the name Maximos, and all three of his sisters nuns, Magdalene, Melane and Kassiane. The couplet over him on the page asks only this: Jerome, remember all of us who honour thy memory in hymns — «Ἱερώνυμε μνήσθητι ἡμῶν πάντων, / τῶν τὴν μνήμην σου ἐν ὕμνοις γεραιρόντων».
