# Jerome of Simonopetra

Jerome was born in 1871 at Reiz-Dere in Asia Minor, and was called Ioan Diakoghorghis in the world. He came to the monastery of Simonopetra on the Holy Mountain on 28 October 1888 and was tonsured a monk on 21 March 1893, Palm Sunday. In February 1914 the brotherhood set him at its head; he was ordained deacon on 11 April 1920 and priest the following day, and on 20 April, the Sunday of the Myrrh-bearing Women, he was enthroned abbot of the house of his repentance.

On 15 June 1931 he was exiled to the monastery of Koutloumousiou, and three months later sent to the church of the Ascension of the Lord in Athens. In 1937 he was asked to come back to the abbot's throne at Simonopetra and refused, quietly. Twenty years later, on 7 January 1957 — the eve of the Nativity by the old calendar, as the life reckons it — he gave up his last breath. He had lived seventeen years in his own country, forty-three at Simonopetra, and twenty-six at the Ascension, which the life calls his guiding light.

doxologia.ro's shorter notice on the saint's own page differs from the life at two points, and both readings are the source's own. The notice says he entered Simonopetra at seventeen and was tonsured five years later, and that in 1937 he was appointed abbot of the Monastery of the Ascension in Athens and led it for twenty years. They are left standing side by side here rather than reconciled.
