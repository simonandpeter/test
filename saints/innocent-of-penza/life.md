# Innocent of Penza

doxologia.ro gives him four lines and no more. It calls him Innocent Smirnov, gives his years as 1784 to 1819, and says that he has been honoured for many years by the Russian people for his ascetic life and for the miracles worked through his prayers. It adds that he wrote spiritual works which had a wide echo, and that the Russian Church recognised him officially as a saint in August 2000.

The calendar's own line for the tenth of October calls him Innocent of Penza. Nothing else about him is set down here — not his see, not his birthplace, not the manner of his death — because the page read for this folder does not give it.

The Russian calendar keeps him on its own 10 October, «Свт. Иннокентия , епископа Пензенского (1819)», and that is the day of his death: the life it reaches says he fell asleep on 10 October 1819. It also confirms, in the Russian Church's own calendar rather than at second hand, what this folder had until now only from the Romanian page — that he was numbered among the saints of the Russian Orthodox Church for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.

The life is short and all of it is new here. He was born in 1784 into the family of a church servant. A scholar by calling, he was a gentle man, inclined to prayer and to the ascetic life. As a professor of the St Petersburg theological academy he wrote a work in two volumes, an Outline of Church History from biblical times to the eighteenth century, which served as the textbook in the church schools for half a century.

He was a friend of the metropolitan [Philaret](/saints/philaret-of-moscow), and an active opponent of the mysticism then in fashion, and for it he was removed from St Petersburg to a see — first at Ufa and then at Penza. The people honoured him for an ascetic life and for the wonders worked at his intercession.

*After doxologia.ro's calendar for 10 octombrie — [the day](https://doxologia.ro/10-octombrie) and [the life](https://doxologia.ro/sfantul-inochentie-al-penzei); read 24 September 2026; and the Russian church calendar's [10 October](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261010.html), [its index entry for him](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/name/7007.html) and [the life it reaches](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life4816.htm), read 4 October 2026.*
