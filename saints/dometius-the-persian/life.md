# Dometius the Persian

Dometius was a Persian by birth and lived, doxologia.ro says, in the days of the emperor Constantine the Great. A Christian named Uar brought him to the faith, and leaving behind his father's religion and his kinsmen he crossed into Greek territory, to the city called Nisibis. There he entered a monastery, was baptised, took the monastic habit and gave himself to the whole struggle of the ascetic life.

Through the envy of the devil he became hated by the monks of that house and fled to the monastery of the holy martyrs Sergius and Bacchus in the city of Theodosius, where he followed the life of the archimandrite Nurvel — of whom it is said that for sixty years he tasted no cooked food and slept very little, never lying down and never sitting, but leaning on his staff. This archimandrite ordained Dometius deacon; and when he learned that he meant to press him to the priesthood as well, he left and climbed a desert mountain, living there to God and bearing the heat, the cold and every change of the weather.

Afterwards he went into a cave and worked many wonders there in the name of Christ, healing those who came to him and bringing them from the deceit of idols to faith in Christ. When Julian the Apostate passed that way and was told what the saint was doing, he gave orders that he be stoned. The men sent found the father with his two disciples singing the office of the third hour, fell upon them, and killed them with stones; and so Dometius finished his course together with his two disciples.
