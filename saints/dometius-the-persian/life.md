# Dometius the Persian

Dometius was a Persian by birth and lived, doxologia.ro says, in the days of the emperor Constantine the Great. A Christian named Uar brought him to the faith, and leaving behind his father's religion and his kinsmen he crossed into Greek territory, to the city called Nisibis. There he entered a monastery, was baptised, took the monastic habit and gave himself to the whole struggle of the ascetic life.

Through the envy of the devil he became hated by the monks of that house and fled to the monastery of the holy martyrs Sergius and Bacchus in the city of Theodosius, where he followed the life of the archimandrite Nurvel — of whom it is said that for sixty years he tasted no cooked food and slept very little, never lying down and never sitting, but leaning on his staff. This archimandrite ordained Dometius deacon; and when he learned that he meant to press him to the priesthood as well, he left and climbed a desert mountain, living there to God and bearing the heat, the cold and every change of the weather.

Afterwards he went into a cave and worked many wonders there in the name of Christ, healing those who came to him and bringing them from the deceit of idols to faith in Christ. When Julian the Apostate passed that way and was told what the saint was doing, he gave orders that he be stoned. The men sent found the father with his two disciples singing the office of the third hour, fell upon them, and killed them with stones; and so Dometius finished his course together with his two disciples.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 7 August, and saint.gr fills in the road he travelled. Dometios was a Persian of the years of Constantine the Great, taught the Christian faith by a Christian called Abaros; and when the people of his house learned of it they rose against him and he had to leave them.

He took refuge in the city of Nisibis on the Byzantine frontier and shut himself up in a monastery there, and from there went on to Theodosioupolis, to the monastery of Sergius and Bacchus, where he cultivated the virtues of the Gospel to a high degree. The superior of that house, Ourvel, seeing his spiritual superiority, wished to make him a presbyter; but Dometios's whole struggle was not to seize office but to escape it, since he had learned from his Lord Jesus Christ to be humble in heart. For that reason he went off into the hills and lived in a cave with two disciples; and when Julian the Apostate was once passing that way he ordered him killed. The soldiers found Dometios and his disciples chanting inside the cave, and there they stoned them to death.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him the first entry of its Julian 7 August and a fuller life than either of the other two books. He was born in Persia a heathen, it says, in the time of the emperor Constantine, and as a young man came to know the faith of Christ, left heathenism behind and was baptised. He loved the true faith so much that he gave up everything worldly and became a monk in a monastery near the city of Nisibis.

He lived in the brotherhood for a time and then withdrew into silence to an archimandrite named Nurvel, of whom it is said that for sixty years he ate nothing cooked. The elder Nurvel ordained him deacon, and when he wanted to force the rank of priest on him as well, Dometius fled to a desert mountain and settled in a cave. Through fasting, prayer, vigil and the thought of God he reached such perfection that he healed the sick.

When Julian the Apostate came into those parts he heard of Dometius and sent men, who walled him up alive in the cave with two of his disciples. So this saint of God ended, the entry says, in 363. That year is the first this folder has held: neither doxologia nor saint.gr gave it a date at all.
