# Limnaeus

Limnaeus was drawn to the solitary life while still very young, and went to [Thalassius](/saints/thalassius), who had made himself a hermit's dwelling on a mountain and surpassed all men in purity of life and humility of mind. From him Limnaeus learned how a solitary keeps his days; and from there he went on to the ever-blessed Maron, whose life he so admired that he resolved to live with nothing over him at all.

He climbed to the top of a mountain above a village called Targala and stayed there, making himself neither hut nor tent nor cabin, but only a low ring of stones set about him, with the sky for his covering. For this, doxologia's life says, he received from God the gift of wonders, so that he drove out demons and healed diseases, following the holy apostles in everything.

Those who had lost their sight and were forced to beg he gathered together, and built cells for them according to their number, and told them to live in them, bringing them the food they needed from the people who came out to him. When he had passed thirty-eight full years without a roof, he gave up his spirit to God in peace. The page gives no year for any of it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and on one page with [Thalassius](/saints/thalassius), and rests them both on Theodoret of Cyrrhus and the Synaxarion of Constantinople. saint.gr makes Limnaeus the younger of the two: he heard of Thalassius and came to him and took from him the pattern of a true monastic life, and after Thalassius's repose he took refuge with [Maron](/saints/maron-the-hermit), whom the site keeps on 14 February, and imitated his way. He climbed the top of the mountain near the city of Targala and fenced a place round with dry stones, and lived in it as an ascetic, without a roof, for thirty-eight whole years, exposed to the cold and to the burning heat, and God counted him worthy of the gift of wonderworking.

But the greatest of Limnaeus's glories, the page says, is the work he put in for the blind. He built other cells beside his own and made them shelters for blind men, and gave them not only a roof but food out of the alms devout Christians made him, and cared for their souls as well. «Τι σημαίνει, έλεγε, να φροντίζουμε μόνο για τα σώματα των δυστυχών;» — what does it mean, he would say, to care only for the bodies of the wretched? To keep our love of man within those bounds is as though we showed it to animals; the great thing is to complete the good by caring for the enlightenment of the spirit as well. So his blind charges were happy, the page ends, for though they had no bodily eyes they received spiritual ones and enjoyed the true light of Christ; and the venerable Limnaeus died mourned by those blind children of his, who had been the great care and tenderness of his life.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day in one entry with [Thalassius](/saints/thalassius). The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the two of them three sentences: Syrian hermits, one of whose particular labours was silence. After Thalassius died in 440, it says, he went over to [Maron](/saints/maron-the-hermit) and struggled there on a mountain top under the open sky. It gives him no year of his own.
