# Eustratius the Faster

Eustratius came of a rich family of Kiev, gave all his wealth to the poor and entered the Lavra of the Caves of Kiev, where he gave himself to ascesis and to fasting. As soon as he was a monk, saint.gr says, he began to fight the passions of the flesh and the devil with the weapons of light — vigil, prayer, and above all the fasting that imitates Christ. He remembered always that his Lord overthrew the evil one by forty days of fasting and prayer, while Adam, failing to be temperate, fell and was driven out of Paradise. So he wore his body away with strict fasting, and with it wore away the passions; and for that he was surnamed the Faster.

On 20 July of the year 1096 the Lavra was suddenly attacked by the Polovtsy under Bonyak the money-lover, who plundered the monastery, took the monks and the labourers captive, and sold them as slaves in the Byzantine city of Cherson in the Tauris. Eustratius and fifty other captives were bought by a Jew of Cherson, who, to force them to take up the Jewish faith, left them to suffer hunger and thirst. Since accepting Judaism meant release from slavery, after six years of hard servitude the captives were ready to deny Christ, and Eustratius persuaded them not to break the promise they had made at their baptism. After fourteen days they all died of hunger and thirst except Eustratius, who was used to fasting for many days together.

Their owner, enraged, charged him with the death of his companions and ordered him crucified on the very day of the Christian Pascha. According to the life, the page says, Eustratius lived fifteen days more upon the cross, and found strength to argue with the Jew who owned him whether death on a cross was a disgrace or a privilege, and to prophesy a coming visitation upon his masters; and as soon as he had said it, he was stabbed. Those who crucified him took the holy relic down and threw it into the sea, and God's unsearchable providence carried it, with no human hand, to the caves of the Lavra of Kiev, where the monks who had survived the Polovtsy and come back found it with astonishment and awe and buried it with honours and doxologies. There it remains to this day, the page says, incorrupt and glorified, and working countless wonders.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and tells the captivity at length. He had been very rich, but moved by the love of Christ he gave away all his property for Christ's sake, entered the monastery of the Caves and was tonsured. When the Polovtsy made war on Kyiv — in 1097, the entry says, where the Greek page gives 1096 for the same raid, and this folder keeps that year, which is attested — they plundered the monastery, cut down many Christians and monks, and sold Eustratius with some other faithful into slavery to a Jew in the city of Korsun.

That man mocked the faith of Christ and pressed the Christians to go over to the Jewish faith. Seeing no other way out, they all resolved to kill themselves with hunger rather than renounce the true faith, and Eustratius was the one who gave them heart for that decision. All of them died of hunger, some after three days, some after four, some after seven. Eustratius, who was trained in fasting, was left alive alone and held out fourteen days without food.

Angry at the loss of the money he had paid for his slaves, the Jew took his revenge on Eustratius by nailing him to a cross. But from the cross Eustratius gave thanks to God and foretold his master a bitter and a quick death; and the man, raging, ran him through with a spear, and so God's saint gave up his soul to his Saviour. They threw his body into the sea, but it floated, and great wonders happened over that martyr's body. Soon afterwards the Byzantine emperor ordered the Jews of Korsun punished for their malice against the Christians, and this tormentor was hanged on a tree and received the wages of Judas.
