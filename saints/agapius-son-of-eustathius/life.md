# Agapius, son of Eustathius

Agapius was a son of [Eustathius the Great
Martyr](/saints/eustathius-the-great-martyr), a distinguished officer at
Rome called Placidas before his baptism, and of
[Theopiste](/saints/theopiste-wife-of-eustathius), called Tatiana before
hers. Eustathius came to the faith in a wonderful way, seeing while he
hunted a stag that bore a cross between its horns and hearing a voice call
him to the true faith, and was baptised with his wife and their two sons,
Agapius and [Theopistus](/saints/theopistus-son-of-eustathius). When the
emperor Trajan learned that he had embraced Christianity he stripped him of
his high military rank and exiled him with the whole family, and on the road
the boys and their mother were parted from him, to his great grief; years
later, when Trajan was in great difficulty in war, he remembered his most
able officer, recalled him to service, and Eustathius by his courage and
generalship did much for the victory — and on the road found his family
again, with great joy. Trajan’s successor Hadrian demanded that he attend
the sacrifices to the pagan gods; he refused, and he and his family were
tortured, their love of Christ strengthening their souls under the torments
as they remembered the word, blessed is the man that endureth temptation,
for when he is tried he shall receive the crown of life. In the end
Eustathius and his family died inside a brazen bull heated red, in 117. The
Greek and Romanian calendars keep Agapius with them on 20 September.

*After the Greek synaxarion’s page for the family — [saint.gr, Άγιος Ευστάθιος](https://www.saint.gr/985/saint.aspx), read 26 August 2026 and whole on 6 September 2026.*
