# Theodore of Mytilene

Theodore, called Hagiul, lived in the eighteenth century under Ottoman rule. He was a native of Mytilene, married, and had children. One day, the Romanian page says, he fell into anger and denied Christ and became a Muslim; and when he understood what he had done he repented, left his house and went to the Holy Mountain of Athos.

He stayed there a long time. He confessed, fulfilled what was given him, was anointed with the holy chrism and received communion, and with his confessor's blessing went back to his family. Once home he went to the Turkish judge and asked him whether a man who has been wronged or cheated may take back what is his. Then he said that his own faith had been good and pure as gold, that his mind had been darkened by the devil, that he had been deceived into throwing it away and taking the judge's faith in the belief it would be better, and that now he had come to himself and saw his own faith was as good as gold and the other as brass.

The judge had him thrown into prison at once, and brought him out a second and a third time and tried in many ways to turn him back to Islam. Seeing him upright and unshaken, the judge sent him to the agha of the district, who also tried flatteries and promises and could do nothing; the saint answered with the same words, that he had taken back his own gold and given up the copper, and that he confessed himself a Christian and that his name was Theodore.

The executioners seized him and beat him savagely, struck him in the thigh with a knife and hung him from a ladder; then they took him up again and led him to the place of execution, and he did not resist — his face, the page says, was full of joy, and he spoke to his executioners and told them his death would be a joy to him. They handed him the rope, and he took it, kissed it and put it round his own neck.

They led him to Parmak Kapi, where he prayed, asked forgiveness of all the Christians who were there, and himself climbed to the place where the executioners hanged him. It was 30 January 1784. His relics were thrown into the sea, but were found after some days, and the Christians got leave to bury him in a church of Saint John at Móthona in Mytilene.
