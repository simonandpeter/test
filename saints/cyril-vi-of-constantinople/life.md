# Cyril VI (Serpetzoglou)

saint.gr says the holy hieromartyr Cyril VI, the patron of Pythio, called Serapetzoglou — his name in the world was Konstantinos Serpentzoglou, the page adding the second spelling without choosing between them — came from Adrianople and was taught his letters in the school of his birthplace. He served as archdeacon at the patriarchate of Constantinople; in 1803 he was consecrated metropolitan of Iconium and in 1810 translated to Adrianople; and on the fourth of March 1813 he was elected patriarch of Constantinople. It was he, the page says, who set up the school of ecclesiastical music under the three teachers of the new method in 1815; he was a friend of letters and preached the divine word continually.

On the thirteenth of December 1818 he was removed from the patriarchal throne and went away to his own country, Adrianople, and there in 1821 he suffered death by hanging along with twenty-seven other clergy and notables, as being implicated — so the firman that ordered the hanging said — in the movement that was preparing the freedom of the Roman nation. The page quotes the sentence in full: it was established that Cyril, the deposed and exiled patriarch of the Romans, predecessor of the murdered patriarch, was concerned in the movement being prepared among the Roman nation and must also be wiped from the face of the earth as an example; and the order is to seize him at once and hang him in his vestments inside Adrianople.

His body hung for three days and was then thrown into the river Hebrus, to be found and buried a little later by a villager named Christos Argyriou; years afterwards his relics were brought to the metropolis of Adrianople. The page lists his writings: a chorographic table of the great satrapy of Iconium published at Vienna in 1812, a historical description explaining it published at Constantinople in 1815 which also describes Adrianople and the parts of Thrace around it, a collection of verse called «Ιερογραφική Αρμονία» printed at Constantinople in 1802, 158 surviving sermons, and a collection of ancient inscriptions published in the journal Ερμής ο Λόγιος.
