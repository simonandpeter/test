# Chryse

Chryse came of an aristocratic family, saint.gr says, and bore witness in the years of the emperor Claudius, about the year 269. When the idolaters arrested her they began by opening her sides and burning the wounds with lighted torches. Then they broke her jaws with stones, and her back with pellets of lead. But she, wounded through and dying, went on confessing her faith.

The brutality of her killers was such, the page says, that they then put a great stone about her neck and threw her into the depths of the sea. And yet, though her body was lost in the waters, her memory remained everlasting and undying, more golden than her own most shining name — which is the page's own play on it, χρυσή being the Greek for golden.

The Greek calendar keeps on the same day her servant [Sabainos](/saints/sabainos-the-martyr), who died for the same faith. The page gives her no year of birth, names neither the place of her death nor the governor who ordered it, and prints no apolytikion and no kontakion; above the account it sets a couplet that makes the sea her bridal bath: Chryse, cast into the deep, comes to the bridal chamber of heaven newly washed.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 30 Ιανουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/01/30/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3565/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
