# Odilia of Alsace

Odilia is kept in the Orthodox Church on 13 December, doxologia's page says, and is reckoned the protectress of the blind and of those with disorders of the sight; her relics are in Alsace, and in 1946 she became officially the spiritual patroness of Alsace. She was born at Obernai in Alsace, in north-eastern France, about the year 662. Her father Adalric was duke of Alsace under Childeric II, king of Austrasia, a man of warlike character who hoped, after his marriage to Bereswinda, for a son to leave as his heir.

Tradition says that both of them prayed long for a child, and that when it came the father's disappointment was double: it was a girl, and about two months after the birth the parents found that she was blind. Adalric's decision was merciless — the child was to die, being taken for a divine punishment — and it was the pious and good-hearted Bereswinda who at last talked him out of it. The mother gave her into the care of a servant in a nearby village for about a year, and then she was taken to the monastery at Palma, whose abbess was an aunt of Bereswinda's; and at a sign from God the Irish bishop Erhard of Ardagh, who was then travelling through Bavaria, came and baptised her with the name Odilia, which the page reads as «the light of God».

After her baptism she gave herself to the study of the Scriptures and sank into prayer and the common life in the service of her neighbour, and was envied by some of the sisters for the gifts she had; she went on in modesty, simplicity and perfect humility. Wishing to know the family she came from, she got into touch with one of her brothers, who tried to persuade their father to take his elder sister back and, against Adalric's prohibition, sent a cart to bring her to Hohenburg — today Mont Sainte-Odile. The duke, seeing her, struck his son with his staff, and the young man died of the blows soon after.

Realising what he had done, the father withdrew to the monastery he had founded to spend the rest of his days in tears of repentance, and put Odilia in the charge of a nun of British origin, giving her enough to live on in the house, where she had been received as a servant. Some while after the death of the woman who looked after her, Adalric met her carrying a little flour secretly to some poor people; moved, he forgot the hatred he had borne her, left her all the goods of the monastery, and asked her to forgive the sin he had committed against her. He died shortly afterwards, and by much fasting and prayer she obtained his deliverance from the torments of hell.

She afterwards became abbess of the monastery, where more than a hundred and thirty nuns lived. Since the place was hard to reach for the pilgrims and the sick who came to ask her prayers, she had a second monastery built, with a church dedicated to Saint Martin and a hospice for the poor; and then, after an appearance of [John the Baptist](/saints/john-the-baptist), a church in his honour as well.

Knowing her end, she gathered the sisters in the chapel of the Forerunner and gave them her last spiritual counsels, and they went to the Divine Liturgy; coming back, they found to their grief that she had gone to the Lord without receiving communion, and they prayed until her soul returned to her body so that she could receive the Body and Blood of the Lord. So she fell asleep, and was buried in the chapel of Saint John the Baptist, which today bears her own name and to which some seven hundred thousand pilgrims come every year.
