# Jerome

Jerome was born in the small town of Stridon, near Aquileia, in the year 347. He had his first lessons in his parents' house, and then went to Rome, where he learned Greek and Latin, the art of speaking and philosophy. He was baptised at twenty by the hands of Pope Liberius. At Trier in Gaul he first found the monastic life; back at Rome he joined a community of friends, where he began the study of Scripture and came to know the catacombs.

After a falling-out with his own people, and sickened by the vanities of Rome, he made his first journey to the East, visiting the monasteries of Greece, Thrace, Asia Minor and Cilicia, and settled at Antioch. He then withdrew into the desert of Chalcis in Syria, where he lived four years of ascetic struggle against the passions and learned Hebrew. Returning to Antioch he received the priesthood, and went on to Constantinople, drawn by the fame of the sermons of Gregory of Nazianzus, and stayed two years listening to him.

At Rome again his life took a settled course: Pope Damasus took him as his secretary, with the charge of revising the Latin text of the Bible, the work later called the Vulgate. When Damasus died he withdrew once more to Palestine and settled at Bethlehem, where he founded three monasteries and began thirty years of scholarly labour. He wrote many letters urging the faithful to a clean life and the keeping of the Lord's commandments, and taught the monks the art of copying manuscripts.

Worn down by age and by unceasing labours, he died at Bethlehem in the year 420. There remain from him the Vulgate, many other translations, homilies, commentaries on the books of Scripture, works of church history and letters of spiritual direction; doxologia calls him, for us, the very image of the Christian scholar, and says his commemoration in the Orthodox Church is kept on 15 June.
