# The Blind Man healed by Panteleimon

saint.gr's whole notice of him is one sentence: the holy Blind Man was healed by Saint [Panteleimon](/saints/panteleimon-the-healer), confessed Christ, and was martyred by the sword. The Greek calendar gives him no name — «Άγιος Τυφλός», the holy Blind Man, is all it calls him — and no country, no year, no emperor and no judge, and it prints no hymn for him. Its heading gives the feast as fixed, kept on 27 July of every year.

Panteleimon's own page on the same calendar tells the rest of the story from the other side: the saint had healed a blind man, who then reported the fact of his healing to the king, telling him that Panteleon had healed him in the name of Christ, in whom he too now believed; and the king, when he had heard him, ordered him beheaded on the spot. That execution was the occasion of Panteleimon's own trial. Above this man's notice the page sets a distich: «Αὐγὴν ἐφευρὼν σαρκικῶν τυφλὸς λύχνων, Καὶ ψυχικὴν ἤθρησεν αὐγὴν ἐκ ξίφους» — having found the light of the lamps of the flesh, the blind man beheld the light of the soul as well, by the sword.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 27 Ιουλίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/07/27/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2076/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026.*
