# Titus of the Kiev Caves

saint.gr says Titus was born in Russia and practised his asceticism in the Lavra of the Caves of Kiev, and that his priestly life was pleasing to God and equal to an angel's, his love for all the brothers disinterested and unfeigned. There lived in the Lavra at that time a deacon called Evagrius; and the devil who hates the good, who is always sowing weeds, sowed enmity between Titus and him. Where before they had each borne the other a deep mutual love, they came to the point of not wanting even to see one another, and anger and the nursing of grievance so darkened them that when one censed in the church the other would leave, and if he did not leave, the first would pass him by without censing him.

Sunk in such a darkness of passion, the page says, the two brothers still dared to serve the Liturgy and offer the Precious Gifts and communicate, forgetting the Lord's own commandment — that if you bring your gift to the altar and remember there that your brother has something against you, you must leave your gift before the altar, go and be reconciled with your brother first, and then come and offer it.

Then Titus fell very seriously ill, and came near to death; and the brothers brought Evagrius to him by force, so that the two might be at peace. This reading of the page did not follow the whole of the passage that stands here. What it prints next is that as soon as Titus saw him he raised himself with difficulty and begged him, weeping, to bless him; that the pitiless Evagrius turned away from him without mercy and declared before everyone that he would never be reconciled with him, neither in this life nor in the other; and that he did not finish the sentence before he fell down. The fathers ran to lift him and found that he was dead, and his body froze at once like marble, while at the same moment Titus stood up entirely well, as though he had never been ill at all; and everyone looked with horror and awe on the inglorious death of the grudge-bearing Evagrius and the miraculous healing of the saint.

After that shattering experience Titus put away from his life for ever not only outward anger but every evil thought about any brother, until the day he fell asleep peacefully and gave his spirit to God. It was the year 1190. His body, the page says, lies incorrupt in the Lavra of the Caves of Kiev. The calendar prints a second Titus of the same Lavra on this same day, a soldier who became a monk, and keeps the two apart by their epithets, which is how they are kept apart here.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells the quarrel. He was a presbyter and had an unfeigned love for a deacon named Evagrius, brother to brother; and as great as their first love was, so great was the enmity and hatred that came between them afterwards, sown by the devil. They came to hate each other so much that when one of them was censing in church the other would turn round and go outside. He tried more than once to be reconciled with his adversary, and in vain.

Then he fell ill, and everybody thought he would die; he asked for the deacon to be brought so that they could forgive each other. They dragged the man to his bed by force, but he tore himself away and fled, saying that he would not forgive him in this world or the next — and as he said it he was stretched out on the ground and breathed his last. The sick man got up from his bed well, the Prologue says, and told how the demons had circled round him until he forgave; and when he had forgiven, the demons ran off and fell on the other man, while angels of God surrounded him. He ended in 1190, which is the year the folder already holds.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day and tells the quarrel at greater length. Titus was a priest-monk of the Kiev Caves monastery, remarkable for being at peace with everyone, and he came to love peace through what happened to him. He had a friend, the black-robed Evagrius, a deacon by rank, and the brethren all marvelled at the love between them, until the devil, who hates every good, sowed enmity between the two; they came to hate each other so much that they would not look at one another and avoided meeting.

When they served in church together there was no agreement even there: if one censed before an icon the other fled the censing, and they dared to receive the holy Mysteries without being reconciled. Titus fell gravely ill and sent to ask forgiveness; Evagrius refused, and when the brethren brought him by force to the sick man's bed he declared that he would not forgive him either in this age or in the one to come. Saying it he fell down dead, struck by an angel's spear, and in that same moment Titus was healed. The brethren wept for the lost brother and learned from it to forgive one another every hurting word.

Titus doubled his labours after that, was known for his humility and became a wonderworker, and his relics rest in one of the caves at Kiev. The Russian calendar's own life pages give his death as about 1190 and as not earlier than 1190, while the line on its day page prints «после 1196» — after 1196. The year recorded above is the one the Greek calendar gives, and the disagreement is left standing rather than settled here.
