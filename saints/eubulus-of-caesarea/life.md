# Eubulus of Caesarea

Eubulus and [Adrian](/saints/adrian-of-caesarea) were from the country of Vanea, which saint.gr explains as the part of Armenia lying round Lake Van. They travelled to Caesarea because they wished to see at close hand the martyrs and confessors of Christ there. In the city they declared themselves Christians and were brought before the governor Firmilian, where they confessed Christ boldly and were beaten mercilessly on the back and the ribs for it.

A second hearing produced the same confession, and the governor in his anger gave them to the beasts. Adrian faced a lion, took no hurt, and was beheaded; Eubulus went through the identical trial, and the sword ended him too. So both received the unfading crowns of eternal glory together.

saint.gr gives no year and no century, and prints no hymn. Its couplet for him turns on his name: dying by the sword for the Lord's sake, Eubulus, you came to know what counsel it is that pleases the Lord.
