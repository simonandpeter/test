# Paul the Simple

Our father among the saints Paul, surnamed the Simple, or the Foolish, was a ploughman, an altogether ordinary man, but without malice and without pretence as no one else was. He had a wicked and unfaithful wife, and for a long while he knew nothing of it. One day he came in from the field unexpectedly, out of the usual hour, as sometimes happens, and found her dishonouring his house with a stranger.

Smiling, full of gentleness, he said to them: well, well, I do not care what you are doing, I take Jesus Christ as my witness; from now on I do not wish to see her before my eyes. Let her be yours, and her children with her; and I will go and become a monk. And at once, leaving everything and setting it in order, he went off to the blessed [Antony](/saints/anthony-the-great) and knocked at his door.

Antony came out and asked who he was and what he was looking for, and Paul answered that he was a stranger and had come to be made a monk. The saint told him that at sixty years old he could not become a monk, nor bear the hardships and the narrowness of the desert; better to go to a community, where he would find everything the body needs and the brethren would carry his weakness, whereas Antony lived alone and ate only once in five days, and then went hungry. Paul would not hear it, and pressed to stay with him; so the saint shut the door of his hut and left him outside three days without coming out to see him, and Paul stayed there hungry and did not go away.

On the fourth day Antony had to come out, and finding Paul still by the door told him to go away and not to force him. Then he set him to work: to take a garment to pieces and sew it carefully together again; to draw water and pour it out all day to no purpose; to make a thing and leave it so that it could not be used. Paul did all of it without hesitating, without murmuring and without being put off by anything, so that Antony said to him: see, brother, if you can do this every day, stay with me; and if not, go back where you came from. Paul answered that he did not know whether he could do whatever else might be shown him, but everything he had seen so far he could do easily.

So great was the obedience and the humility the blessed Paul came to, that he received grace against the demons to drive them out. The great Antony, being satisfied about him, kept him with him for a time; then he made him a cell and settled him in it, so that he should learn all the wiles of the demons and fight with them there. Having passed a whole year in that solitude and shown himself a worker of wonders, serving God worthily, he went over afterwards to the dwellings of heaven.
