# Stephen of Triglia

Stephen, the confessor of Christ, lived in the years of the emperor Leo the Armenian, 813 to 820. Loving the hermit's life from his youth, he adorned himself with every good work, and at the request of the monks there he became abbot of the monastery of Triglia; and by his teaching he counselled many to live in a way pleasing to God and to pass their lives in purity.

Afterwards Leo of evil faith began to dishonour the holy icons, and, raising a terrible persecution against the Orthodox, summoned Stephen and pressed him to renounce the veneration of the holy icons and to sign the renunciation with his own hand against the faith. The venerable man refused; and not only did he not do it, but he reproached the emperor, calling him a pagan and a stranger to the true faith.

So he was tormented with beatings and with chains in prison, and then sent into exile; and there, tried with hard sufferings and pains, he passed to Christ God, for whom he had endured many struggles and borne many torments.

The Greek calendar keeps him too, on 26 March rather than the Romanian 28th, and saint.gr tells the same life with one act of his own added. It has him give himself to the ascetic life from a young age and be quickly marked out among the brethren for his self-control, his character and his high ascetic mind, and as abbot of the monastery of Triglia for his gift of governing, his justice towards the brethren and his care for the monastery's spiritual progress. When Leo V issued his new orders against the holy icons and against those who venerate them, the abbot Stephen left his monastery and went down into the city, to hold the persecuted up in their faith and to strengthen their resistance; for this he was seized, imprisoned and exiled, and in exile he breathed his last, still contending for the rights of the Church. The page adds a couplet for him, «Θείου Στέφανος ἀμπελῶνος ἐργάτης, Θεῷ παραστάς, καμάτου μισθὸν λάβῃ» — Stephen, a labourer of the divine vineyard, standing before God, may he receive the wage of his toil.
