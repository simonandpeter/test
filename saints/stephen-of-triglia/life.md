# Stephen of Triglia

Stephen, the confessor of Christ, lived in the years of the emperor Leo the Armenian, 813 to 820. Loving the hermit's life from his youth, he adorned himself with every good work, and at the request of the monks there he became abbot of the monastery of Triglia; and by his teaching he counselled many to live in a way pleasing to God and to pass their lives in purity.

Afterwards Leo of evil faith began to dishonour the holy icons, and, raising a terrible persecution against the Orthodox, summoned Stephen and pressed him to renounce the veneration of the holy icons and to sign the renunciation with his own hand against the faith. The venerable man refused; and not only did he not do it, but he reproached the emperor, calling him a pagan and a stranger to the true faith.

So he was tormented with beatings and with chains in prison, and then sent into exile; and there, tried with hard sufferings and pains, he passed to Christ God, for whom he had endured many struggles and borne many torments.
