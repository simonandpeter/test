# Athanasia of Antioch

Athanasia was the daughter of a goldsmith of Antioch named John, and the wife of [Andronicus](/saints/andronicus-of-antioch), who was of the same trade. The Romanian life says her name means immortality, and that she won an imperishable glory by her life. The two divided their wealth in three parts, for the poor, for the adorning of the church and for their own house; they had a son, John, and a daughter, Mary, and after the children they lived as brother and sister, tending the sick and keeping Wednesday and Friday in fasting and prayer.

Twelve years into their marriage she came back from the morning service and found both children groaning, and they died. She would not return to the house, but stayed at the grave weeping, until the martyr Julian appeared to her at midnight in the shape of a monk and told her not to weep for children who were in the hands of God. She understood that it was a vision, went home and told her husband, and then said to him that she wished to leave the world.

They went to Jerusalem and then to Alexandria, and the elder Daniel of Sketis placed her in a women's monastery. Twelve years later, when Andronicus set out again for Jerusalem, she met him on the road in a man's habit and under the name Athanasius, and he did not know her. They travelled together, came back to Alexandria and asked to live near one another in silence, and so they passed another twelve years, she never telling him who she was.

When she died the brothers who came to prepare the body found that it was a woman's, and Abba Daniel found under her pillow the letter in which she had written it down, read it and gave it to Andronicus, who learned only then whom he had lived beside. Andronicus followed her shortly after and was buried at her side.

The Greek calendar keeps the two together on the same day, 9 October, and sets them in the year 540. saint.gr says they were virtuous spouses of Antioch in Syria, well off and living according to the will of God, and that Andronicus worked as a money-changer in the city. Two children were born to them of that marriage, and both died; and the grief of it drove the two parents to seek consolation in a pilgrimage to the Holy Places. From there they went on to Egypt, where by a common decision they became monastics and entered monasteries — Andronicus the monastery of the abba Daniel, and his wife Athanasia the women's monastery of the Tabennesiotes — and there, having passed the rest of their lives in asceticism, they fell asleep in peace. The same page records a portion of the relics of each of them at the monastery of Kykkos in Cyprus.
