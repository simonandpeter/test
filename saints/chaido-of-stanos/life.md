# Chaido of Stanos

Chaido came from the village of Stanos in Chalkidiki and lived in the
nineteenth century. After the rising of 1821, to escape the immoral
attentions of the Turkish commander, she had to leave the village with her
mother and settle on Thasos, where she entered a metochion of the
Pantokrator monastery at Kallirachi, served in its church and lived a
virgin’s life given to God; after her mother’s death she gave herself wholly
to prayer and fasting and came to spiritual contemplation. By the local
tradition, when the Turks raided the metochion angels snatched her away and
saved her from the fury of the unbelievers, and two days later she came back
and told her story to the hieromonk Gerasimos. Another tradition has her
cruelly tortured by the Turks. At her death her body gave out a fragrance as
a sign of her holiness.

Her memory and her life stayed alive among the Christians of Stanos, who
dedicated an icon of her in their parish church of the Entry of the Mother
of God — painted in 1960 by the brotherhood of the Kyrillaioi at the New
Skete on the Holy Mountain, and showing her in the Chalkidiki dress of her
day. Since 1988 her feast has been 1 September: the day of her death is not
known, so she is kept on the day of the older St Chaido, one of the forty
women martyrs of Thrace, as is the custom when a saint’s own day is lost. A
church has been built to her in recent years at the initiative of the parish
priest Nicholas Chalkias and with the help of the people of Stanos.
