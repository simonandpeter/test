# Tertius

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 10 noiembrie reads «Sfântul Apostol Terţiu», and the page it points to has no Viață tab at all: it carries the name, the day, and a hymn — a common of the apostles, headed «Troparul Sfinților Apostoli Olimp, Rodion, Sosipatru, Erast, Terțiu, Cvart» for the six of them together and naming none of them in its text.

So the calendar calls him an apostle and sets him in that company of six kept on this day, and says nothing further: no see, no country, no century, no year, no manner of death. His is a name the corpus has not held before.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 30 October, which is not the day the Romanian one keeps him on, and adds two facts to his name. saint.gr puts him first in the line «Άγιοι Τέρτιος, Μάρκος, Ιούστος και Αρτεμάς οι Απόστολοι από τους Εβδομήκοντα», and of him says: he became the second bishop of Iconium, after Sosipater, and he wrote the Apostle Paul's letter to the Romans, as he bears witness himself at Romans 16.22. The epigram the page sets over his name makes a pair of the two scribes: you were the writer-down of Paul's words, and I am your new writer, Tertius. The page gives him no year, no birthplace and no account of his death, and prints no hymn for him.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps the same four apostles of the Seventy together on the Julian 30 October, and its one life for the four adds to his name a people and a death. It says that he was the second bishop in Iconium, after the holy apostle [Sosipater](/saints/sosipater-of-iconium) — the order the Greek page gave as well — and that there he turned many of the pagans to Christ, and there also ended his life as a martyr. It mentions the Apostle Paul's reference to him at Romans 16.22, as the Greek page did, and gives him no year. The day's line closes the four of them with «(I)», the first century, which is the only date any of the three calendars has given him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 30 October, second of five apostles of the Seventy under one heading, and gives him two facts neither of the other calendars gave this folder. He wrote Paul’s epistle to the Romans — and the book prints the reference, Romans 16:22, the verse in which Tertius who wrote the letter sends his own greeting. And he ended in martyrdom as bishop of Iconium, after the apostle [Sosipater](/saints/sosipater-of-iconium), with a cross-reference to the book’s own entry for 10 November. The martyrdom is the first statement of his death this folder has; the succession is why it already related Sosipater, written off another source and now grounded again here.
