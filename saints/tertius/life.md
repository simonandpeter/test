# Tertius

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 10 noiembrie reads «Sfântul Apostol Terţiu», and the page it points to has no Viață tab at all: it carries the name, the day, and a hymn — a common of the apostles, headed «Troparul Sfinților Apostoli Olimp, Rodion, Sosipatru, Erast, Terțiu, Cvart» for the six of them together and naming none of them in its text.

So the calendar calls him an apostle and sets him in that company of six kept on this day, and says nothing further: no see, no country, no century, no year, no manner of death. His is a name the corpus has not held before.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 30 October, which is not the day the Romanian one keeps him on, and adds two facts to his name. saint.gr puts him first in the line «Άγιοι Τέρτιος, Μάρκος, Ιούστος και Αρτεμάς οι Απόστολοι από τους Εβδομήκοντα», and of him says: he became the second bishop of Iconium, after Sosipater, and he wrote the Apostle Paul's letter to the Romans, as he bears witness himself at Romans 16.22. The epigram the page sets over his name makes a pair of the two scribes: you were the writer-down of Paul's words, and I am your new writer, Tertius. The page gives him no year, no birthplace and no account of his death, and prints no hymn for him.

*After doxologia.ro's calendar for 10 noiembrie — [the day](https://doxologia.ro/10-noiembrie) and [his page](https://doxologia.ro/sfantul-apostol-tertiu); read 25 September 2026; and saint.gr's calendar for 30 Οκτωβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/10/30/index.aspx) and [the notice of the four](https://www.saint.gr/2847/saint.aspx), read 1 October 2026.*
