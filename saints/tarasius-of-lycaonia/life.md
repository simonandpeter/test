# Tarasius of Lycaonia

saint.gr has no narrative life of him, and says so by printing instead the note it found in a manuscript. His memory, it says, is entered in Delehaye's synaxarist on 8 May and in the Lavra codex I 70, folio 217b, on 7 May, with this notice: this our holy father was from infancy a vessel of election and a dedicated offering, giving himself over to hard living, to fasting, to prayer, to tears, to sleeping on the ground and to every hardship and affliction; and for the height of his virtue he was counted worthy by God of the greatest wonders.

He drove away demons, the note goes on, raised the dead, cleansed lepers, healed incurable afflictions and simply cured every disease and every infirmity; and while he was still contending in all these good works he fell ill a little while, and, having foreknown his own departure to God, went away to the Lord in good cheer with the holy angels, who took his honoured soul.

The page then follows his relics rather than his life. They were carried from Constantinople to Venice, it says, probably in the reign of Basil the Bulgar-slayer, between the years 1018 and 1025, in the days of the Doge Otto Orseolo, and carried as the relic of Saint Tarasius Patriarch of Constantinople; today they are kept in a sarcophagus that stands above the altar of the church of San Zaccaria in Venice. A fragment of the relic, it adds, is at the monastery of Panagia Malevi in Kynouria.
