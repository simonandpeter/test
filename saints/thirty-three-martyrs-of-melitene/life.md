# The Thirty-three Martyrs of Melitene

doxologia.ro's calendar names them and gives no life. The line for 7 noiembrie reads «Sfinții 33 de Mucenici din Melitina», and the page it points to has no Viață tab at all: it carries the name, the day, the readings appointed, and a Tropar — a common of the martyrs, sung to no one of them by name. So the calendar gives their number, thirty-three, and the city they are counted from, Melitene; it names not one of them, gives no year, no emperor, no governor and no manner of death.

Because the notice enumerates nobody, this is one entry and not thirty-three: a company whose members the source does not name cannot be given thirty-three folders without inventing thirty-three people. They are not the six martyrs of Melitene the corpus already keeps, whom the Greek synaxarion places on 30 August and says were drowned in the sea.

The Greek calendar keeps them on the same day, 7 November, and does what the Romanian page did not: it names all thirty-three. Its list runs Hieron, Nikandros, Hesychios, Barachos (or Barachios), Maximianos, Kallinikos, Xantikos (or Xanthias), Athanasios, Theodoros, Doukitios, Eugenios, Theophilos, Valerios, Theodotos, Kallimachos, Ilarios, Gigantios, Longinos, Themelios, Eutychios, Diodotos, Kastrikios, Theagenes, Mamas, Nikon, Theodoulos, Bostrykios (or Oustrichios), Victor, Dorotheos, Klaudianos, Epiphanios, Aniketos, and another Hieron. The first of them, saint.gr says, was Hieron of Tyana in Cappadocia: his father died early and his mother Stratonike brought up him and his two brothers, [Matronianus](/saints/matronianus-brother-of-hieron) and [Antonius](/saints/antonius-brother-of-hieron), by herself; and though he was well educated he took up farming, which the pagans counted degrading and the Christians did not, since Christ had not despised the sweat of the humble labourer. When the persecution began under Diocletian the eparch Agricolas arrested him on the charge that on Sundays and other feasts he went about preaching Christ to the workers and had drawn many away from the worship of idols. His two brothers were taken with him, and thirty more of his fellow-workers in the service of the Gospel; and after prison and frightful tortures Agricolas beheaded them outside the city of Melitene. The couplet the Greek books give the company counts them as they died: thirty men by the sword, says the distich, and one pair besides.

The Russian calendar enumerates this company where the other two count it. days.pravoslavie.ru's line for 7 ноября ст. ст. prints all thirty-three names in the genitive under one class and one century — it opens «Мучеников в Мелитине: Иерона , Исихия , Никандра , Афанасия , Маманта …» and closes «и Амонита (III).» — and behind every one of the thirty-three names stands a name page of its own, «Иерон Мелитинский, мч.» and the rest in the same form. The calendar gives the company itself no name, so this folder stores no Russian name form.

The life behind those links is headed for the first of them, «Мученик Иерон Мелитинский». Hieron was born at Tyana in Great Cappadocia and brought up by a devout mother, a meek and good Christian. The co-emperors Diocletian and Maximian (284–305) sent a large detachment into Cappadocia under «Лизий», both to root out the Christianity spreading widely there and to levy sound and strong men for the imperial army; among many others Lysias ordered Hieron taken, for his great bodily strength and skill. He refused to serve emperors who were driving out Christians.

When they came to take him by force, he armed himself with a beam, put the soldiers sent for him to flight, and hid in a cave with eighteen men of one mind with him; Lysias's men could not take the cave even by storm. On the advice of «Кириак», one of Hieron's own friends, Lysias lifted the siege and led the detachment away — and Kyriakos then calmed Hieron and talked him into not resisting the authorities, so that he went with the other recruits, under escort, to the neighbouring city of Melitene.

There a dream foretold him his death. Lysias put it to the soldiers gathered at Melitene that they sacrifice to the pagan gods; Hieron refused, and thirty-two soldiers after him, confessing their faith in Christ openly. The persecutor had the martyrs beaten, and Hieron's hand struck off besides; barely alive they were thrown into prison, and in the morning they were beheaded.

A rich and notable Christian named «Хрисанфий» bought the head of Hieron from Lysias. When the persecutions stopped he built a church on the place where the martyrs had been put to death and laid the honoured head in it. The bodies of all of them were buried secretly by Christians, and in the reign of the emperor Justinian, while a church «во имя святой Ирины» was being built, their relics were found incorrupt.

**The line prints thirty-three names and this folder is one folder.** This run's standing ruling makes a synaxis that enumerates its members one folder for each member; but this corpus already holds the company as a single folder, written from the Greek and Romanian lines for this same day, which give the number and no names at all, and two men the Greek wave made folders for — [Antonius](/saints/antonius-brother-of-hieron) and [Matronianus](/saints/matronianus-brother-of-hieron), the brothers of Hieron — are named neither on this line nor anywhere in this life. Splitting one folder into thirty-three, or making thirty-three beside it, is a merge and not a reading, so nothing was split here and the thirty-three name links are reported instead.
