# Eleazar the Scribe

Eleazar was one of the chief scribes of Jerusalem and a priest by order, old in years, of honourable countenance, adorned with grey hairs and with a wisdom of true faith. He was known to everyone as one of the foremost teachers of the law in the city, and the Romanian life says he was one of the seventy-two interpreters who translated the Hebrew books into Greek at the command of Ptolemy Philadelphus, king of Egypt.

In the persecution of Antiochus surnamed Epiphanes, who had ordered the Jews driven from the laws of their fathers and made to worship idols and to eat swine's flesh, and the temple of the Lord turned into a shrine of Olympian Zeus, Eleazar was arrested. When they forced swine's flesh into his mouth he chose the glorious death of a martyr for the law of the Lord rather than a dishonoured life, spat out the unclean meat he had touched, and went willingly to the torments, making himself an example to the God-fearing who would have to die for that law.

Some of the pagans, out of old acquaintance, made a show of pity for him and secretly brought him other meat, not swine's, whispering that he should eat it in front of everyone as though it were what the king commanded, and so escape the torments and death. He answered that it were better for him to be cast at once into hell than to anger his God by breaking His holy law; that it did not become his old age to dissemble and so stumble the young, who would say, See, Eleazar in his deep old age has left the law of his fathers and joined himself to the heathen, and would perish through his pretence, while he brought reproach on his own grey hairs by being the cause of so many souls' ruin.

Though he escaped the torments of men, he said, he would not escape the torments of the Almighty, alive or dead; better to die now, adorning his old age with a manly endurance and leaving the young a good example when with greatness of soul and strength he suffered for the honoured and holy laws. He was dragged away to the torture, and those who had seemed to pity him turned at his words to anger and fury. The priest of God was tortured with many torments and cruelly beaten.

Near death he groaned and said to God: You who know all things and look upon all, Lord, You know that, being able to save myself from death, I have borne these hard wounds willingly and endured heavy pains in my body, and yet I receive them with joy and with love, because I suffer for the glory of Your holy name. So he died, leaving the memory of his death as an example to follow, not to the young only but to the whole people. The church books add, the life says, that after the cruel beating they poured sharp and foul-smelling vinegar over his wounds and threw him into the fire; and he, praying that his blood and his death might be accepted for the whole Hebrew people, gave up his soul.

After his martyrdom seven brothers of an honoured family, who had been his disciples, were taken with [their mother](/saints/solomone-mother-of-the-maccabees) and sent to the king at Antioch, and died there remembering his teaching; they are kept in this corpus as [the seven Maccabee brothers](/saints/seven-maccabee-brothers).
