# Michael (Nekrasov)

Of some of the Russian martyrs, this life begins, nothing survives but the precious acts of their suffering: the form the investigator filled in at the arrest, the sheets of the interrogation, the unjust sentence and the paper recording the shooting. Michael Nekrasov was one of these. He was born on 1 November 1883 in the village of Sergino in the Staritsa district of the province of Tver, the son of a man who served the church — priest, deacon or psalmist, the page says, we do not know. He finished two classes of the Staritsa church school, was ordained deacon on 12 February 1908 to the church of Alferyevo, and afterwards priest, and served in villages of the Tver diocese.

In 1930 he was arrested, sentenced to a labour camp and sent to the White Sea canal; he was released on 25 October 1932 and came back to the diocese. In 1934 he was given the church at Degunino in the Staritsa district, where he was a zealous pastor and often walked out to his parishioners in their villages. He was arrested on 15 November 1937 and held in the Rzhev prison. The investigation took three days: there was no material against him, so two peasants of his own village were called in to sign a protocol the investigator had written, and only on the third day was the priest himself brought in.

He answered every question the same way — that he had carried on no counter-revolutionary agitation and would not admit himself guilty: not of discrediting the constitution of the USSR and the leaders of the government by the church in August, not of taking counter-revolutionary instructions from his dean. «Снова заявляю, что контрреволюционную агитацию я не проводил, виновным себя не признаю и показать больше ничего не могу.» With that the investigation closed. The troika sentenced him on 25 November 1937 and he was shot two days after, on 27 November. He was canonised for the whole church in August 2000.

The day's line names twenty-seven new martyrs of 1937 together — twenty-three presbyters, a deacon, a hieromonk, a lay martyr and a woman — and the calendar carries a life page for ten of them, of whom he is one. The men of that line were tried separately, in different districts and different prisons, and no one of these lives names another of them, so no relation is recorded between them here.
