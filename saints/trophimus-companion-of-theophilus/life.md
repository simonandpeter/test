# Trophimus, companion of Theophilus

Trophimus and [Theophilus](/saints/theophilus-companion-of-trophimus), and with them other faithful and true servants of Christ, thirteen in number, suffered in the reign of Diocletian. They would not submit to the will of the pagan tormentor, nor sacrifice to the soulless idols, nor deny Christ, and for that they were hung up to be tortured and their bodies torn with sharpened irons.

Then the pagans stoned the patient sufferers, and having broken the bones of their legs threw them into a fire. But by the all-powerful strength of God they were kept alive and whole in the middle of the great flames, and came out of the fire unharmed. At that the tormentor, burning the more with anger, as with the fire of hell, commanded that they all be killed with the sword; and their honourable heads were cut off for the sake of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Head of the whole Church.

That is the whole of what the Romanian calendar prints of them. It names these two and counts the others without naming any of them, so the thirteen are left here as the page leaves them, unnamed; and it gives no country, no city and no year.

The Greek calendar keeps them on 21 July and in one line: «Άγιοι Θεόφιλος, Τρόφιμος και άλλοι Δεκατρείς Μάρτυρες». saint.gr tells the passion as doxologia tells it and adds the years. These saints lived in the days of the emperor Diocletian, whose reign the page dates 284 to 304, and they were distinguished for their Christian courage in the persecution. They remained faithful to the confession of Christ and were put through a series of torments: stoned, their flesh torn with iron instruments, their wounds then burned with lighted tapers; and because their life still held out and their mouths still repeated the confession, they were beheaded. The page's verse for Trophimus says that he had thirteen men as his messmates and fellow-contenders and was cut down together with them — counting the thirteen, as the Romanian page does, and naming none of them. The same day's Greek listing names «Άγιοι Ιούστος και Ματθαίος», whose own page says they were martyred together with Theophilus and Trophimus.
