# John Kochurov

John Kochurov was a missionary hieromartyr from America, doxologia.ro says, a priest and martyr of the Russian Revolution. He was born on 13 June 1871, the son of a priest. He studied at the theological seminary of Ryazan and then at the theological academy of Saint Petersburg, and after finishing his studies in 1895 he married Alexandra Chernisheva. He was ordained deacon, and on 27 August 1895 ordained priest in the Saint Alexander Nevsky Lavra in Saint Petersburg by Bishop Nicholas (Ziorov) of the diocese of the Aleutians and Alaska.

Having expressed the wish to be a missionary priest in the United States, he was soon transferred, and became the first priest of the church of Saint Vladimir in Chicago — a parish that would later become Holy Trinity Cathedral. Since the parish had no building of its own yet, his first great undertaking was to build the church. The work began in April 1902 and was finished the following year for the consecration by Bishop Tikhon, later [Tikhon, Patriarch of Moscow and All Russia](/saints/tikhon-of-moscow).

He helped to found many parishes in the Chicago area. Together with [Alexis Toth](/saints/alexis-toth) he gave support to the many immigrants in America, and translated a good number of religious texts into English for the faithful. In 1907 he returned to Russia and was assigned to Narva, in what is now Estonia, where he put into practice what he had learned in the United States, teaching the catechism in the schools. Then in 1916 he was transferred to the cathedral of Saint Catherine at Tsarskoye Selo, just outside Saint Petersburg.

On 31 October 1917 the Bolsheviks came in force into Tsarskoye Selo and arrested him. He was taken out of the town and shot, and so became the first hieromartyr of the Soviet Revolution. He was buried a few days later in the crypt of the cathedral of Saint Catherine, and was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church in 1994. doxologia.ro's page for him carries no Tropar tab, so the calendar prints him no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 31 October, and calls him the first martyr of the newer Russia. The archpriest John Kochurov was the first cleric to be a victim of the October Revolution, saint.gr says. In 1901, after graduating from the Theological Academy of Saint Petersburg, he was appointed priest of the Russian community of Chicago; and having returned to Russia he was murdered by the Bolsheviks at Tsarskoe Selo, in November 1917. His name was commemorated second, after that of Vladimir, metropolitan of Kyiv, by Patriarch Tikhon at the Divine Liturgy of 31 March 1918. The page prints no hymn for him, and it dates the murder only to November 1917 while keeping his memory on 31 October, a difference it does not explain.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 31 октября ст. ст. — the day he was killed — and its life is the fullest any calendar in this corpus has of him. He was born on 13 July 1871, by this page's reckoning, in the village of Bigildino, also called Surki, in the Dankov district of the province of Ryazan, into the godly and many-childed family of a village priest, Alexander Kochurov, and his wife Anna; his father combined the parish for many years with teaching scripture in the village school. The son followed him: the Dankov church school, then the Ryazan seminary, finished in 1891, then the Petersburg academy in the same year.

Out of the academy he was sent, as he had long wished, to the mission of the Aleutian and Alaskan diocese. There the bishop ordained him priest and set him over the church of St Vladimir in Chicago. **In his first three years in that parish he received 86 Uniates and 5 Catholics into the Orthodox Church**, and the regular congregation of the Chicago and Streator parish grew to 215 at Chicago and 88 at Streator, with children's church schools working at both churches. A new church of the Holy Trinity was built at Chicago by his labours, blessed by the future Patriarch [Tikhon](/saints/tikhon-of-moscow), and the money for it was gathered by him in Russia in 1900, on leave.

In 1907 he came home to the clergy of the Transfiguration cathedral at Narva, and taught scripture in the gymnasia there; from November 1916 he was parish priest of the Catherine cathedral of Tsarskoye Selo, where from the first months he was known not only as a zealous and reverent celebrant but as an eloquent and learned preacher who drew the Orthodox of the whole town under the cathedral's vaults. On 30 октября 1917, while the artillery was firing on Tsarskoye Selo, he served a special moleben in that cathedral; and after it, with the shelling still going on, a procession went out with prayers appointed for the ending of the fratricidal strife, and in his sermon he called the frightened people to be calm in view of what was coming.

On 31 октября 1917, the civil 13 November, the Bolshevik detachments entered the town, which the cossacks had left, and the priests were arrested. That same day he was beaten for his fearless preaching, and then, half alive, the red guards dragged him a long way along the railway sleepers towards the Tsarskoye Selo aerodrome. There they shot him, in the sight of his son, a schoolboy. The page says his death was not instantaneous: the killers dragged him by the hair, offering one another to finish him off like a dog.

His body was brought to the chapel of the palace hospital that evening and carried from there into the Catherine cathedral, where he was buried on 4 ноября 1917 and laid, at the parishioners' request, in the vault beneath it; the cathedral was blown up in 1939. Three days after the shooting his son died too, unable to bear the shock, a youth of seventeen. The page calls him the first hieromartyr to suffer at the hands of the communists after the revolution, and quotes the letter Patriarch Tikhon, who had known him, wrote to his widow: that he held in his heart the firm hope that the shepherd who had fallen asleep, crowned with martyrdom, now stood before the throne of God among the chosen of Christ's faithful flock. Where his grave was, the page says, there now stands a monument to the leader of the communists. He was canonised by the Bishops' Council of the Russian Orthodox Church in 1994.
