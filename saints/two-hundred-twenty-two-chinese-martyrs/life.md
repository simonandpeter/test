# The Two Hundred and Twenty-two Chinese Martyrs

saint.gr counts them at the end of the entry it keeps on 11 June: father [Metrophanes Ji-Sung](/saints/metrophanes-of-peking) and those who were martyred with him — his wife [Tatiana](/saints/tatiana-of-peking), his sons [Isaiah](/saints/isaiah-of-peking) and [John](/saints/john-of-peking), [Maria](/saints/maria-of-peking) the bride of his house, the teacher [Ia](/saints/ia-wen-of-peking), and two hundred and twenty-two other Chinese martyrs. Of the thousand souls that made up the Russian mission at Peking, the page says, three hundred were lost in the bloody events of 1900, and two hundred and twenty-two of them received the unfading crown of martyrdom.

The persecution was loosed by the secret martial-arts society of the «Boxers» — Yihetuan, the militias of righteousness and concord, whose members trained at boxing and wore amulets held to be proof against bullets — with the backing of the dowager empress, who was known for her hatred of foreigners, and the Christians were blamed for all the calamities of China. On 10 June proclamations were posted in the streets of Peking calling on the idolaters to slaughter them and threatening any who dared hide them. The next night the Boxers went with lighted torches through every quarter of the city, seizing the Orthodox in their houses and torturing them to make them deny Christ. Many, terrified, burned incense before the idols; others confessed bravely. Their houses were burnt, and they were led outside the city to the Boxers' temples, where they were disembowelled, beheaded, or burnt on pyres.

The page names a few of them beyond the six it enumerates. Paul Wan, an Orthodox catechist, died with the prayer on his lips. Among the martyrs, it says, were also descendants of the people of Albazin in Russia, who had brought the light of Christ to Peking in 1685: Clement Kui Kin, Matthew Hai Chuan, his brother Vitus, Anna Sui and many others, who, not fearing them that kill the body but cannot kill the soul, met the torments and the death bravely, and begged God to enlighten their persecutors and forgive them their sins.

For more, saint.gr sends the reader to the «Συναξάριον Ορθοδόξων Κινέζων Μαρτύρων» of Georgios E. Piperakis, Athens 1997.
