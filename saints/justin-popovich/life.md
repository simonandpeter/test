# Justin Popovich

Justin Popovich was archimandrite and spiritual father of the monastery of Ćelije near Valjevo, a doctor of theology and a professor in the Faculty of Theology at Belgrade, and doxologia calls him one of the most prolific theologians of the twentieth century: he wrote more than forty books and theological treatises. Together with his teacher Nikolaj Velimirović he stands in that account as an inspirer of the spiritual revival of the Serbian people and a zealous defender of the right faith in a hard time.

He was born Blagoje Popović at Vranje on 6 April 1894 — 25 March by the old calendar, the day of the Annunciation, from which his name came — the son of Spiridon and Anastasia. The family had been priests for seven generations, though his own father was only a sacristan. He finished the primary school at Vranje at the head of his class and went on to the seminary of Saint Sava in Belgrade, from 1905 to 1914; among his teachers were Nikolaj Velimirović and the Serbian hymnographer Stevan Mokranjac.

He had wanted the monastic life from youth and asked to be tonsured when the seminary ended, but gave way to his parents, who would not agree. At the outbreak of the First World War he was mobilised into the medical brigade at Niš, and during the Serbian army's retreat through Albania, at Scutari, on the night of the New Year of 1915 and with the blessing of Metropolitan Dimitrije, he was tonsured by Archimandrite Benjamin Taušanović, taking the name Justin after Justin the Martyr and Philosopher.

With the same blessing he was sent to study at Petrograd, where he stayed until June 1916; the disorders before the Bolshevik revolution took him on to England, where Velimirović, then a hieromonk, received him and helped him enter a college at Oxford. His doctoral work was on the theology and philosophy of Dostoevsky, and its last chapter set out a hard criticism of Western humanism and anthropocentrism, Roman Catholic and Protestant alike. His examiners would not accept such a thesis and asked that its contents be changed; he refused the compromise.

During the Second World War he served in several monasteries in Serbia. In 1948 the communist authorities condemned him and placed him under house arrest at the monastery of Ćelije near Valjevo, where he remained to the end of his life. There he wrote his most important works, and was at the same time confessor to the nuns of the house and to a whole company of monks who would later stand at the head of the Serbian Patriarchate. He departed to eternal life on the Annunciation of 1979, the day on which he had been born, and his grave became a place of healings and of continual pilgrimage. The Holy Synod of the Serbian Orthodox Church entered him in the diptychs of the saints on 29 April 2010.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and tells where he began. The venerable and God-bearing father Justin was born on 25 March 1894, saint.gr says, at dawn on the feast of the Annunciation, in the town of Vranje in southern Serbia; his father was called Spyridon and his mother Anastasia, and at his baptism he was given the name Evangelos. His father's family was priestly by tradition and had given the Orthodox Church at least seven clergy, which is what the surname Popovic means. From a small child he often visited, with his parents, Saint Prochoros the Wonderworker at the nearby monastery of Ptsinjski, and there he saw with his own eyes his mother healed of a grave illness. A second spring of piety for him was the regular reading of the Gospel from his fourteenth year, and a third the reading of the synaxaria and later of the works of the Fathers. He used to say: Orthodoxy is not a library which you can study, but an experience which you are called to live; Orthodoxy is first of all a manner of life, and a holy manner of life at that, and only afterwards a teaching, and then a teaching of life.
