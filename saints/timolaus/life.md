# Timolaus

Timolaus came from the shore of the Black Sea — «din Pontul Euxinului», as doxologia's life for the day has it — and was one of six young men who suffered at Caesarea in Palestine under the governor Urban, in the second year of Diocletian's persecution. A pagan feast was being kept in the city that year, and the country people had crowded in to watch the Christians already in bonds be tormented.

The six, the life says, were kindled with zeal for Christ. They bound their own hands behind them, as a sign that they wished to suffer without fear and were ready to be given either to the fire or to the beasts, then ran into the middle of the spectacle and cried out before the governor with a great voice: «Suntem creștini!» — we are Christians.

Urban saw how young they were and did not want to destroy them at once. He coaxed them a long while to worship the idols and not throw away lives so young, and when that failed he had them put in prison. Some days later [Agapius](/saints/agapius-of-caesarea-in-palestine) was shut up with them, together with his servant, making eight; and all eight, tried and tormented many times over, were at last beheaded. Timolaus suffered with [Plesius](/saints/plesius), Romulus, two called Alexander and one called Dionysius.
