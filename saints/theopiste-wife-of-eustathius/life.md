# Theopiste, wife of Eustathius

Theopiste was Tatiana before her baptism, the wife of the Roman general
Placidas — [Eustathius](/saints/eustathius-the-great-martyr) — and the
night before the stag with the cross between its antlers spoke to him on
the hunt, she had dreamed that she, her husband and her sons would come to
the true God tomorrow. The bishop baptised the four together: Eustathius,
Theopiste, and the boys Agapius and Theopistus. Then the misfortunes the
Lord had foretold him fell on the house — plague took the servants, the
cattle died — and the family slipped away in secret to live poor and
unknown, and took ship at Egypt for Jerusalem. The shipowner, taken with
her beauty, put Eustathius and the boys ashore and kept her; the boys were
carried off at a river crossing by a lion and a wolf, and rescued unknown to
their father by shepherds and farmers; and the shipowner died of a terrible
disease with Theopiste untouched, so that she lived free where the ship had
landed, in a house beside which, years later, two young soldiers pitched
their tent. Trajan had recalled Placidas to lead an army, and the two
recruits he loved for their daring were his sons; she overheard them piece
together the lion and the wolf, knew them, and, without telling them who
she was, went to their general to ask to be taken home to Rome — and knew
him too, and told him. So the whole family was found again.

Hadrian, who had succeeded Trajan, wanted the victory celebrated with
sacrifice, and Eustathius would not go to the temple; the emperor took his
belt of office and had the four brought before him, and when neither he nor
his wife and sons would sacrifice, they were thrown to the beasts, which
would not touch them, and then shut alive in a red-hot brazen bull. Three
days later it was opened and their bodies were found unharmed, not a hair
singed, their faces shining. The Greek calendar keeps her with her husband
and sons on 20 September, and the Romanian calendar the same day.

The Russian calendar keeps her on 20 September old style with her husband and sons, about 118. Its life adds that on the night before the family was baptised she was told in a dream that she, her husband and her sons would come to Christ the next day; that on the voyage to Egypt the shipmaster, taken with her beauty, kept her and put her husband and children ashore, but soon died of a cruel illness, and she lived on untouched, working in peace; and that years later, when the army camped by her house, she overheard two young soldiers in a tent discover that they were brothers, knew them for her sons, and going to their commander to ask leave to follow the army knew him for her husband. The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, gives the day’s entry to Eustathius; its text names her Теопистија, has a wicked barbarian carry her off in Egypt, and tells of her martyrdom with him.
