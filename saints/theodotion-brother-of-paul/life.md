# Theodotion, brother of Paul and Pausirius

Theodotion was the brother in the flesh of [Paul](/saints/paul-brother-of-pausirius) and [Pausirius](/saints/pausirius-brother-of-paul), and saint.gr places the three of them in the reigns of the emperors Diocletian and Maximian, when Arianus was governor in Cleopatris — the town the page identifies with the modern Suez — in Egypt. His two brothers became monks while they were still young. Theodotion was drawn away by bad friends, went off into the mountains, and lived there among brigands.

When Diocletian's persecution began, Arianus arrested Paul and Pausirius. As soon as Theodotion heard of it he left the mountains and the brigands and came down to see his brothers and to say goodbye to them, and there, by the grace of God, his heart was warmed and he too confessed that he believed in Christ. He then rushed at the tyrant and threw him down from his throne. At Arianus's order red-hot nails were driven into his ribs and belly, and at the last he was beheaded.

So Theodotion received the crown of martyrdom first, and his brothers after him, thrown into the river. The distich saint.gr prints over him puts the words in his own mouth as the sword comes: «Ἰδοὺ τράχηλος ἐλθέτω δὴ τὸ ξίφος, Θεὸν ποθῶν ἔκραζε Θεοδοτίων» — here is my neck, let the sword come, cried Theodotion in his longing for God.
