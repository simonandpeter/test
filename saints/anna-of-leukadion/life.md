# Anna of Leukadion

The venerable Anna in Leukadion, or Leukate as the page also calls the place, lived in the years of the emperor Theophilus the iconoclast, which saint.gr dates 829 to 842. She was the daughter of a rich and distinguished family, and had gifts of body and of soul in plenty, because she was brought up in the instruction and admonition of the Lord. After her parents died she inherited the great family property and shared part of it out among the poor.

But an Agarene living in Constantinople came to love this beautiful girl and asked for her in marriage, with the consent — so the page says — of the emperor Basil. Anna would not have him, and prayed to God in tears to rid her of that temptation; and God heard her prayers, and the Agarene died. Then Anna joined a monastery in Constantinople and gave herself to the hardest ascesis and to prayer.

Having passed fifty years so, she gave up her blessed soul to God after a short illness. Her honourable relic, when it was dug up years afterwards, was found whole and gave off a divine fragrance from end to end, and it worked many and various wonders. The page gives no year for her death, and the two reigns it names — Theophilus's and Basil's — do not sit together.
