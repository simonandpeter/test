# Theodotus (Shatokhin)

Theodotus Trofimovich Shatokhin was born in 1888 in the village of
Zaitsevo in Ekaterinoslav province, today the Horlivka district of
Ukraine; in the First World War he was a soldier of the Oka infantry
regiment. In 1926 he was arrested and imprisoned for a year on the charge
of spreading religious convictions among the peasants; he was arrested a
second time on 2 April 1935 at Donetsk, by then already a priest — who
ordained him is unknown, because at interrogation, so as to incriminate no
one, he took a very careful position: he knows nothing of himself and can
say nothing of anyone, the case file records. Only about his religious
feelings he would not keep silent, so that even in captivity he did not
leave off his pastoral preaching: at interrogation, the file says, he
declared that science and all else are from God alone, that God created
men, and that God will do to every unbelieving man whatever He wills. On
14 July 1935 the special collegium of the Donetsk regional court sentenced
him to seven years in a corrective labour camp, and he was sent to the
Koktun-Kul section of the Karaganda camp of the NKVD. On 20 August 1937 he
was arrested in the camp with other priests of one mind with him; like the
others in the case of the priest Basil Zelensky and others he was charged,
among other things, with belonging to a counter-revolutionary group which
held illegal prayers and had set up a cross, with anti-Soviet agitation,
with refusing to go out to the shock-work of stacking hay, and with taking
part in a memorial service for shot enemies of the people. He did not admit
guilt. On 10 September 1937 the troika of the NKVD for the Karaganda region
sentenced him to the highest penalty, and on 15 September 1937 — 2
September of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him — he was
shot. The calendar adds that his name means given by God, the Slavonic
Bogdan, and that men so named, there being no saint Bogdan, should turn to
Theodotus.
