# The Six Thousand Martyrs of David Gareji

doxologia.ro's calendar names them and gives no life. Its line for 26 April reads «Cei șase mii de mucenici de la Mănăstirea Sfântul David Gareji, Georgia», and the page beneath it has tabs for icons, relics, places of pilgrimage, monasteries, photographs and video, but no Viață. The whole of the prose the site prints about them is a single sentence, set under the icon as a caption.

That sentence reads: «La un semnal, soldaţii s-au aruncat fără milă asupra părinţilor şi i-au înjunghiat pe toţi. Le-au tăiat trupurile în bucăţi, lăsându-le ca hrană sălbăticiunilor pustiei.» — at a signal the soldiers fell upon the fathers without mercy and cut them all down; they hacked their bodies in pieces and left them as food for the wild beasts of the desert.

The page names no year, no ruler and no commander, and not one of the six thousand by name. It calls them the fathers of the monastery, and the monastery it calls Saint David Gareji, in Georgia. Nothing further is printed, and nothing further is set down here.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps them on the Julian 10 April, where the Romanian calendar keeps them on 26 April, and it tells the history that one caption sentence leaves out. In the David Gareji desert in Georgia there were twelve monasteries, it says, in which many monks had struggled for centuries. In 1615 Shah Abbas I fell upon Georgia and laid the whole of it waste, and cut down Christians without number. Once, going out hunting at the very dawn of Pascha, he saw many lights on the mountain: it was the monks of all twelve houses in procession round the church of the Resurrection of Christ, with candles in their hands. When the shah learned that they were monks he asked in astonishment, Is not all Georgia given over to the sword yet? — and ordered his soldiers to go at once and cut down every monk.

An angel of God appeared in that hour to the abbot Arsenius and told him of his near death, and Arsenius told it to his brethren; and they all communicated of the pure Mysteries and made themselves ready to die. Then the attackers came, and cut to pieces first the abbot, who went out before them, and after him all the rest. All suffered honourably and were crowned with unfading crowns in the year 1615 — the only year any source read for this folder gives them. So ended the history of these famous monasteries, which for more than a thousand years had served the Georgians as a hearth of spiritual learning; of the twelve only two remain today, Saint David's and Saint John the Forerunner's. The king Archil of Georgia gathered all the martyrs' relics and buried them honourably, and those relics give out healing myrrh and heal the sick to this day. The abbot and the king are named here as the entry names them, and neither is identified with anybody: it gives the abbot no by-name and no house of his own, and the king nothing beyond the name and the title.
