# Zeno (20 April)

Zeno is one of nine martyrs saint.gr prints together on 20 April, and the page makes the cause of all nine the same: «αίτια του ενδόξου μαρτυρίου τους, ο μεγαλομάρτυς και τροπαιοφόρος Γεώργιος» — the cause of their glorious martyrdom was the great martyr and trophy-bearer [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer). They all suffered, it says, in the years of the king Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305.

Zeno was one of five — Victor, Acindynus, Zoticus, Zeno and Severian — who, when they saw George remain untouched upon the torturing instrument of the wheel, with one voice all five confessed Christ true God. Their beheading was immediate, and so they took the crown of martyrdom.

The couplet the page sets over them says that the sword kills four together with Acindynus, men who had escaped the danger that comes of error. The other four of the nine were guards of the king — Christopher, Theonas, Caesarius and Antoninus — who confessed when they saw George raise a dead man by prayer, and who were hung up, torn, burned with torches and at the last cast into the furnace. It was the year 303.

The page gives Zeno no country, no city, no rank beyond that of the guards, and no relic, and it prints no hymn. Its whole account is the shared one, and the nine stand in it as they stand in the calendar's line: [Victor](/saints/victor-20-april), [Acindynus](/saints/acindynus-20-april), [Zoticus](/saints/zoticus-20-april), [Severian](/saints/severian-20-april), [Christopher](/saints/christopher-20-april), [Theonas](/saints/theonas-20-april), [Caesarius](/saints/caesarius-20-april) and [Antoninus](/saints/antoninus-20-april).

The Ohrid Prologue keeps five of this company on the Julian 18 April, two days before the day the Greek calendar keeps all nine on, and tells their conversion in three sentences. All of them were tortured in the time of the emperor Diocletian, it says. They were pagans until they saw the torments of [George the Great Martyr](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer); but seeing those torments, and the courage of that glorious martyr, and the many wonders that appeared then, they received the Christian faith, and for it they suffered soon afterwards and were crowned with glory.

Its heading names Zeno among five — Victor, Zoticus, Zeno, Acindynus and Severian — and not the other four the Greek page counts in the company, so this folder's Serbian row rests on a heading of five and the four unnamed took none. The entry gives no year, naming only Diocletian, so the 303 this folder already held off saint.gr stands untouched; and it gives no city, no trade and no manner of death for any of the five.
