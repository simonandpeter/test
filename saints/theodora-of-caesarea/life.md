# Theodora of Caesarea

The venerable Theodora lived as a recluse in the monastery of Saint Anne which is called Reghidion, in the days of the emperor Leo, the father of Constantine Copronymus. She was of an illustrious and honoured family: her father, Teofil, held the rank of patrician, and her mother was called Teodora. For many years her mother wept over her barrenness and prayed God and the most holy Mother of God to give her a child; and she received the grace from heaven, and received it, the life says, through the intercession of the holy and righteous Anne, the mother of the Mother of God.

When the girl had been born and had grown a little she was brought to the church of Saint Anne and received into the monastery as a divine gift. The abbess brought her up in the fear of God and taught her the holy Scriptures, and the honoured child lived well. But the cunning demon would not bear to be trodden underfoot by a child, and stirred up the emperor Leo, the fighter against God, to give her as wife to his son Hristofor, whom he had made caesar; so she was torn out of the monastery by force and by tyranny and made to come to Constantinople, and the wedding was held and the bridal chamber made ready.

That was Leo's design; but God kept her undefiled from union with her betrothed. Unlooked for, the Scythians made war on the empire in its western part, and the bridegroom was sent in haste to withstand the barbarians, and fell in the first battle. When the pure lamb Theodora learned of it she took from the palace, without anyone knowing, gold, silver, pearls and costly clothes, went aboard a ship and returned to the monastery, rejoicing and thanking God.

When her flight was found out, the second son of the man with the name of a beast — that is, of Leo — went to the monastery, but found her tonsured a nun and dressed in rags, and by God's providence he left her in peace; and so the venerable Theodora found her quiet and her full freedom.

She punished her body so much that the joints of her bones could be seen through the skin. Her food was a small piece of bread every second or third day, and nothing else; she had one garment only, and that of hair. Her bed was covered on top with a hair blanket and had stones laid under it, and so she slept; and often she kept vigil whole nights through. Nor was she content with these recluse's labours only, but bound her body with iron chains, which so wore her limbs that she was covered with sores. So, shining for very many years with every kind of virtue, she passed to the blessed life.
