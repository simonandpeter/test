# Maria (Mamontova-Shashina)

Maria was born in 1890 in the village of Dedinovo in the Zaraysk district of Ryazan province, the daughter of the peasant Ivan Shashin, and went to the village school. After her father died she worked for hire, and in 1914 she went as a novice to the Kolychevo convent of the Kazan icon of the Mother of God, eighteen versts from Yegoryevsk. When the authorities closed the convent in 1920 she settled in her home village with other sisters, sewing quilts, singing on the kliros and, when asked, reading the Psalter for the dead. In 1930, when the authorities meant to close the village church because the community could not pay its heavy tax, the sisters went round the farms of the nearby villages and collected the sum, and went to Moscow to plead for the church; some of them were arrested that year. Maria was arrested on 1 June 1931 and held in the Ryazan prison, and the investigator wrote that she and the nun Valeria Dobryakova had collected money from the peasants for the clergy and their taxes and had campaigned against closing the church. On 28 June 1931 an OGPU troika sentenced her to three years in a labour camp, and she was held at Akmolinsk in Kazakhstan until August 1934.

At home again she was elected to the church council. In 1936 the authorities turned the church into a storehouse, and witnesses later reported her saying that the Orthodox had been robbed of their last consolation, that the Stalin constitution promised equal rights while anyone who spoke was arrested, and that the women who stayed away from church even on its feast would be punished by God. She was arrested on 24 September 1937 and held in the Ryazan prison. Asked about her double surname, she said Mamontova was her mother’s name and Shashina her father’s, and that the village knew her better as Mamontova. She admitted only that she had lent some women of the collective farm a panorama of churches and monasteries to look at, and denied every other charge and the testimony of seven false witnesses. On 13 October 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced her to eight years in a labour camp; she was taken to the Butyrka prison in Moscow and then to Bamlag, where she died on 2 October 1938 and was buried in an unknown grave.

The Russian calendar keeps her on 19 September old style, on one line with the priest [Nilus (Smirnov)](/saints/nilus-smirnov), who died in Bamlag the same day.
