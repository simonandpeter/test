# John of the Ladder

Which country and which city brought forth and raised this brave struggler, before his desert labours, the monk Daniel who wrote his life says plainly that he cannot tell. What place holds him now, and with what sweetness of undying food it feeds him, is certain: he is in that country of which the Apostle Paul cried, «our citizenship is in heaven», where he receives the rewards worthy of his sweat, and for his pains the honour that has no pain in it.

At sixteen years of age in the body, and a thousand years of age in the sharpness of his understanding, he offered himself to God as a blameless and acceptable sacrifice: he raised his body to Mount Sinai and his soul to heaven. Seeing with his mind the God who cannot be seen, and made a stranger to the world, he loved from the first the meekness that is adorned with humility, cutting off all boldness and pride of speech and all slander.

His elder was Martyrius, who tonsured him. Once Martyrius came with his disciple to the great Athanasius of Sinai, who looked at John and asked him where he had this disciple from and who had tonsured him, and then said in wonder: «Father Martyrius, you have tonsured the abbot of Mount Sinai.» Another time Martyrius took him to the great elder John the Sabbaite, in the desert of Goudda; and the elder rose, took water, and washed not Martyrius's feet but John's, and kissed his hand. When Stephen, his own disciple, asked why he had washed the disciple's feet and not the teacher's, the elder answered: «Believe me, son, I do not know who that young monk is; I received the abbot of Mount Sinai, and it was his feet I washed.»

When his father in God had been sent ahead to the King on high, John went out to a place of stillness in Sinai, about five stadia from the church, called Tholas; and there he spent some forty years from the beginning of his monastic life without slackening, always burning with the desire of the divine love. By that hermit's life, and by the rarity with which he saw a human face, he quenched the furnace of the flesh and turned it to ashes; the passion of anger had already been killed in him by the weapon of obedience. Long before he slept he would pray and compose books, and so he composed the book called the Ladder, for which he was afterwards called the Ladder-writer; for writing books was how he drove off his sloth.

A monk named Moses, envying his manner of life, begged to be received as his disciple, and, pressed by honoured elders who spoke for him, John took him. One day he sent Moses to bring earth from a certain place for the garden; and at noon, in the burning heat — for it was the month of August — Moses grew tired, went into the shade under a great rock and fell asleep there. Sitting in his cell at prayer, and in a light sleep, John saw a man of holy dignity who reproached him: «So, John, you sleep without a care? Moses is in danger!» — and the Lord, not willing to grieve His servants, kept Moses from a sudden death and John from grief.

Afterwards, when all had marvelled at him in every rectitude, they set him as a new Moses, against his will, over the common life, putting that shining lamp on the first lampstand; and receiving unwillingly the abbacy of Mount Sinai he drew nearer in spirit to the mountain of God. After he had taken the abbacy — that is, after forty years of monastic life, as Synchronus writes — six hundred strangers once came to the monastery, and sitting at the meal they saw a young man clothed in a Jewish shroud who went everywhere and gave orders with authority to the stewards and the cellarers, the cooks and the servers. When they looked for him and could not find him, John said: «Leave off looking for him: Moses the prophet and lawgiver has served in his own place.»

One year there was no rain in those parts and a great drought, and the people of the cities round about gathered and came to him, begging him to ask rain of God by his prayers; and when he had prayed a great rain came down at once, and watered the dry ground and made it bear fruit. At the end of his temporal life he was in no way unlike Moses, save in this: that John entered with his soul into the Jerusalem above, while of Moses's body it is not known where it was laid. It was John, abbot of Raithu, who had asked him, and at whose asking he came down from Mount Sinai, like a new seer of God, and showed them his own tablets written by God — the words that are called the Ladder.

His brother, Abba George, wept that he was being sent on ahead to God and left behind; and John answered him: «Do not weep and do not grieve, for if I find boldness before God I shall not wait for you to finish this year here, but shall ask the Lord and take you to myself.» And so it was: after the blessed end of John, Abba George went to God in the tenth month. A note at the end of the life adds that Daniel the monk covers the saint's birth and city, and that some say he was the son of Xenophon and his brother George the Arsealite, called Arcadius from birth, who changed his name in the monastic order as John did not — and it sends the reader to the twenty-sixth day of January.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and puts years, a teacher and relics beside the book. saint.gr has him born most probably in 523 in Syria, the son of a rich and devout family, and educated in a higher course of study so that he stood out among his fellows, though he cared more for prayer, for theological study, for writing and for ascesis. He went to Mount Sinai, to the famous anchorite Martyrius, who guided the young John; after four years of ascesis he was tonsured a monk, and the fame of his virtues and his wisdom spread, so that many monks and laymen and officials came to the monastery to ask his counsel. He had the gift of wonderworking, and from his living at the holy monastery of Sinai he is called the Sinaite too. After the abbot's death, and at the demand of the brethren, he agreed to be abbot of Sinai for some years; but the longing for the desert life made him withdraw again and give himself back to his studies, and he fell asleep in peace about the year 606, leaving two writings of the first importance, the «Κλίμακα» and the «Λόγον προς τον Ποιμένα», the Discourse to the Shepherd. The Ladder, the page says, holds thirty discourses on virtue, each discourse one virtue, beginning from the most practical and climbing like stairs to the theoretically high ones, because in the spiritual life there are low steps and high ones, lower states and higher; and that is why the book is called the Ladder of the virtues. Of his relics the page names part of the skull at the monastery of the Great Meteoron and portions at the monasteries of the Panagia of Damasta in Phthiotis and of the Panagia Tourliani on Mykonos. Its couplet is the obvious one and the good one: «Ἐπὶ κλίμαξι κλίμακας πυκνῶς, Πάτερ, Τὰς σὰς ἀρετὰς θείς, ἔφθασας πόλου μέχρι» — setting your virtues thickly, Father, as ladders upon ladders, you reached as far as heaven.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and tells the story at some other lengths. It has him come to Mount Sinai from somewhere as a boy of sixteen and stay there — first as a novice, then as a hermit, and at last as abbot of Sinai to his eightieth year, when he died about the year 563. That does not agree with the years this folder holds from saint.gr, a birth probably in 523 and a death about 606, and neither reading was changed to fit the other: the sourced interval stands and the Prologue's reading is recorded beside it. It quotes his biographer the monk Daniel in a sentence the Romanian life gives in its own words — he raised himself in body to Mount Sinai and in spirit to the mountain of heaven. It gives the time he spent under his spiritual father Martyrius as nineteen years, and names the elder who looked at the young John and foretold that he would be abbot of Sinai as Anastasius of Sinai, where the Romanian life calls that man the great Athanasius of Sinai; no source read for this folder decides between the two names, and both stand.

After his elder's death, this entry says, he withdrew into a cave and lived twenty years there in hard ascesis, and it was during that silence that he wrote the many useful books of which the Ladder is the most famous, where the ascent of the soul to God is described as a climb up a ladder. It tells the saving of his disciple Moses more fully: Moses fell asleep one day in the shade of a great rock, and John, at prayer in his cell, saw that his disciple was in danger and began to pray to God for him; and when Moses came he fell on his knees and thanked his spiritual father for saving him from certain death, telling how in his sleep he had heard John calling him and had leapt up, and in that moment the rock fell, and would have killed him had he not jumped. One thing more is here that is in neither of the other accounts: some of the brethren once reproached him as a man of too many words, and he was not angered at all but fell silent and did not speak a word for a whole year, until they begged him to speak again and go on teaching them out of his God-given wisdom. His brother George, whom he appointed abbot before his death and promised to ask for within the year, followed him after ten months.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day and, unlike the Greek and the Romanian pages, tells his years. By tradition, its two readings say, he was born about 570, the son of «Ксенофонт и Мария», whose own memory the calendar puts on 26 января; at sixteen he came to the monastery of Sinai, and after four years there he was tonsured. The elder «Мартирий» was his instructor and he lived nineteen years in obedience to him. On a visit the two paid to the elder «Иоанн Савваит», the old man rose, washed the boy’s feet and kissed his hand, and afterwards told his own disciple: believe me, child, I do not know who this youth is, but I received the abbot of Sinai and washed the abbot’s feet. «Авва Стратигий», who was present at the tonsure, foretold that he would be a great lamp of the Church of Christ.

After the death of his elder he withdrew to a desert place the second reading names «Фола», at the foot of the mountain, and spent forty years there in silence, fasting, prayer and tears of repentance. One of the two readings keeps a story of that time: his disciple, a monk named «Моисей», lay down to rest in the shade of a great crag while carrying earth for the garden beds, and a man of venerable look woke the saint in his cell with a reproach — why do you rest quietly here, John, while Moses is in danger? He rose and prayed, and that evening the monk told him he had dreamed that his teacher called him, had jumped up and run, and that a huge stone had fallen with a crash on the place he had left.

At seventy-five, after the forty years alone, he was chosen abbot of the house of Sinai, and he governed it about four years before returning to solitude and silence; and it was during that time, at the asking of «Иоанн», the abbot of the monastery of Raithu, that he wrote the Ladder of thirty steps, naming it for the thirty years of the Lord’s coming to manhood. He is said to have accepted the commission with reluctance, having a modest opinion of himself; and when some reproached him with talking too much he kept silence for a whole year, until the same men asked him not to deprive them of the profit of his conversation. The reading closes «Скончался в 649 г.»

THE YEARS THIS CALENDAR GIVES AND THE YEARS THIS FOLDER HOLDS DO NOT AGREE AND NOTHING WAS CHANGED. The folder’s 523 and about 606 are saint.gr’s, which heads him «(523 - 606)»; the Russian source has him born about 570 and dead in 649, and the day line prints «(649)». That is a difference of about forty-three years at each end, and it is a difference between two traditions rather than a misreading of either page. The Russian figures are set down here in words and the `dates` object was left exactly as the Greek wave wrote it: this is a question for the author and not for a reader.
