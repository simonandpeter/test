# Dadas, companion of Gobdelaas

Gobdelaas was the son of King Sapor, the Greek synaxarion says, and became a Christian through one of his father’s officers, Dadas. When Sapor learned of it he had them both arrested, and when they refused to sacrifice to the gods he had Dadas cut to pieces alive, and his son [Gobdelaas](/saints/gobdelaas-son-of-sapor) tortured: he flayed his head, cut off his limbs and pierced his body with reeds. [Casdoos](/saints/casdoos-companion-of-gobdelaas), or Casdius, was a kinsman of the king, and for becoming a Christian was flayed alive.

[Casdoa](/saints/casdoa-sister-of-gobdelaas) was the king’s daughter and the sister of Gobdelaas: she visited her brother in prison and he made her a Christian, and when it became known the king arrested her and, unable to change her faith, tortured her cruelly to death. The verses add that Gobdelaas was pierced with reeds for honouring Christ, who was struck with a reed; that Dadas was cut limb from limb by the sword; and that Casdoos was put to the sword and Casdoa crushed with wood. The entry gives no date.

The Russian calendar keeps the company on the same 29 September, dates it to the fourth century, and names three of the four the Greek names: «Мчч. Дады, Гаведдая и Каздои (IV)». Its index of names makes the Persian setting explicit where the Greek leaves it to be inferred — he is «Дада Персидский, мч.», Dadas the Persian.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps the company second of the four it enumerates on this day and tells it in its own order. Dadas it makes a Persian grandee and a kinsman of king Sapor — where the Greek entry makes him one of the king's officers and gives the kinship to [Casdoos](/saints/casdoos-companion-of-gobdelaas) instead — and Gobdelaas the same king's son. When Dadas confessed his faith in Christ openly, Sapor had him tortured with bitter torments; but under them Dadas worked great wonders in the name of Christ, and those wonders worked so strongly on the king's son that he too believed.

The heathen king spared not even his own son but put him to heavy torments as well, and both of them glorified God by their endurance and by many wonders and gave up their souls under the torture. They suffered in the fourth century, the Prologue says, which is the «(IV)» the Russian calendar's day line also prints. With them suffered, because they believed in Christ, Gobdelaas's sister [Casdoa](/saints/casdoa-sister-of-gobdelaas) and [Gargal](/saints/gargal-companion-of-gobdelaas), the chief priest, whom no other of the four calendars names.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 29 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2403/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026; the Russian church calendar's [29 September](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260929.html), read 4 October 2026; and the Serbian church's [Ohrid Prologue for the Julian 29 September](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-10-12&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
