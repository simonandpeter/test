# Basil (Kozyrev)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him eighth of fifteen on 21 октября ст. ст., in a line of eleven priests, two deacons and two monks of 1937, and the name link behind his place and behind the seventh lead to one and the same life page: he and [John (Kozyrev)](/saints/john-kozyrev) were brothers, sons of a priest of the village of Pozharye in the Sandovo uyezd of Tver province, and the calendar tells their story once. He was the younger, born in 1889.

He studied at the Bezhetsk theological school until 1907, finished five classes of the Tver seminary, and entered the Demidov juridical lyceum at Yaroslavl, which he could not finish because the family had no money to keep him there. In 1914 he became a teacher in a village school and stayed in it until 1919, when the new power made him leave; he worked as a bookkeeper and then as an instructor in the Rybinsk provincial consumers' union until 1921, and then decided to become a priest. He was ordained that year to one of the churches in Bezhetsk.

The persecution of the turn of the thirties did not pass him by. In 1930 he was arrested, sentenced to three years and sent first to Solovki and then to the camp on the Svir; after two years, in 1932, he came back to Bezhetsk and served again. On 16 February 1934 he and his brother were arrested together, on the testimony of a Bezhetsk priest who had believed his investigators and confirmed the denunciations laid before him. His brother agreed with the investigator and signed a confession of membership in a counter-revolutionary group of churchmen. Fr Basil admitted nothing.

What he did set down, on the same 22 February, was his own account: that until 1919 he had taught in the village school at Smerdyni, that he had then worked as a bookkeeper and an instructor in the consumers' co-operative, and that being discontented with the Soviet power, and in particular with its ending the teaching of the Law of God in the schools, he had decided to become a priest of the St Nicholas church in Bezhetsk in order to strengthen the Orthodox faith; that although he was discontented with the existing order he had talked of it only with a narrow circle of people close to him; and that he could show nothing more.

A doctor found both brothers ill, Fr John with pleurisy after pneumonia and both of them wasted by prison food, and the court's expert wrote on their papers that they were fit for heavy physical labour. The troika gave each three years in a corrective labour camp. The first convoy's wagon never came, so they waited in Bezhetsk prison for the next, and from Vladivostok were taken to Kolyma, which they barely survived. At the end of October 1936 they returned to Bezhetsk and to their church; in July 1937 their third brother, bishop of Bezhetsk since 1929, was moved to the see of Barnaul and arrested the moment he arrived.

The two priests were arrested in September 1937. Fr Basil was questioned about the bishop's name-day of 1936, and answered that it had not been kept, because the bishop had spent a month of that year under guard at Kalinin. Asked what the conspiracy of closed doors in his flat had been for and what the three of them talked about, he said they talked about their households and their families, that politics came up sometimes and that they spoke of it favourably, and that the doors were shut as doors usually are. Asked whether he confirmed that he and his brother had carried on agitation after their return from exile, he denied it, saying he had stopped any such thing in 1934 when he was arrested, sentenced and sent away.

The last interrogation came on 1 October. Told that in his sermons he had made counter-revolutionary sallies against the Soviet power and depicted the Bolsheviks as the antichrist come down to earth, he answered that his sermons had been of religious content only. Told that he had spread slander on the Stalin constitution by telling the faithful that the Soviet power removed people it disliked from the elections to the Supreme Soviet by arresting them, he denied it and said he had never given the faithful such explanations — the same charge his brother had met by admitting he had said it. On 1 November 1937 the NKVD troika for the Kalinin region sentenced both brothers to be shot, and they were shot on 3 November 1937. They were numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
