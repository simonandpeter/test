# Euphemia the Great Martyr

Euphemia lived and was martyred in the years of the emperor Diocletian. She was born at Chalcedon to a God-fearing and noble family; her parents Psilophron and Theodoriane took care that their daughter should grow in every Christian virtue, and she grew into a person of rare gifts and strong Christian spirit, which she showed when the pagan proconsul of Asia Minor, Priscus, ordered all the inhabitants of Chalcedon to attend a festival he was holding in honour of the pagans’ god Ares: Euphemia resolved with other Christians to stay away from the pagan festival, and for this was seized and imprisoned. During her captivity the enemies of Christ tried in every way to persuade her to deny her faith and embrace the idols, and when they realised that she would not change her faith for words they tortured her horribly; but by divine grace she suffered nothing from the tortures, and at last the executioners threw her to wild beasts, and Euphemia found — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here; its verse says, slain for God by the jaws of a bear, you must be crowned with praises, Euphemia. The Romanian Lives call her a very beautiful virgin of good family, daughter of the devout Philophron and Theodorosia; the Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 16 September with a full service and kontakia — her relics, the Romanian kontakion recalls, received the decree of faith from the six hundred and thirty God-bearing fathers at Chalcedon.
