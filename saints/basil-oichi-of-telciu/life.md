# Basil Oichi of Telciu

The Romanian calendar keeps four Martyrs and Confessors of Năsăud together on 12 noiembrie, and doxologia's life for them opens by naming all four and saying that they suffered martyrdom on 12 November 1763: Atanasie Todoran of Bichigiu, Vasile Dumitru of Mocod, Marin Grigore of Zagra and Vasile Oichi of Telciu. They were defenders, the page says, of the ancestral faith and of the Romanian people. Almost the whole of what the page tells is about the oldest of the four, and the other three are named again only at the execution.

The narrative belongs to Atanasie Todoran of Bichigiu: a free peasant, literate, once a judge and a collector of dues, who had deserted an imperial regiment near Vienna, hidden in the mountains and served years in Moldavia, and who was shut up afterwards in the Coopers' Tower at Bistrița for the old desertion. Released, and there being no Orthodox priest in his village, he refused to have his son communed with unleavened bread or confessed by a uniate priest, and buried the boy in the ancestral rite.

In 1761 and 1762 he treated at Vienna with other leading men over the militarisation of twenty-one communes on the Bichigiu, the Sălăuța and the Great Someș, asking that the frontier regiment should not force Romanians to cast off their faith. Nothing was done for them, and he began to oppose the militarisation openly, the people of Năsăud seeing plainly that the frontier system was meant to convert them and that the promised lifting of serfdom was not applied to them.

On 10 May 1763, at Salva, on the plateau called «La mocirla», nine companies of the new frontier regiment were to swear and have their flags blessed, and General Bukow came from Vienna to receive the oath. Before the soldiers rode «moș Tănase Todoran», a hundred and four years old, who told them that for two years they had been frontier guards without a paper from the empress that they were free men, and that they would not bear arms so that the holy faith might be mocked: «Jos armele!» The soldiers threw their arms on the ground.

Inquiries followed, and on 12 November 1763, on the same plateau at Salva, those found guilty of the May revolt were executed. Atanasie was broken on the wheel from the top downwards and his head bound to a wheel; another nineteen men were beaten with rods, and many of those died under the blows. The heads of the martyred were set on poles at the gates of the houses they had lived in, and pieces of their hacked bodies laid at the crossroads.

Of this man the page says only what it says of his two fellows: that he was martyred by hanging together with [Athanasius Todoran of Bichigiu](/saints/athanasius-todoran-of-bichigiu), «pentru aceeași vină» — for the same fault — beside [Basil Dumitru of Mocod](/saints/basil-dumitru-of-mocod) and [Gregory of Zagra](/saints/gregory-of-zagra). It is the life of the four that gives his surname, Oichi, which the day page's line leaves out. Doxologia prints no separate life and no hymn for him.
