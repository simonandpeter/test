# Elpidephorus the Persian

Shapur, the emperor of Persia, darkened with the worship of idols and full of every lawlessness, raised a great persecution against the Christians in his land. Among the servants of his own household there were at that time three secret Christians, [Acindynus](/saints/acindynus-the-persian), [Pegasius](/saints/pegasius-the-persian) and [Anempodistus](/saints/anempodistus-the-persian), who served Christ in hiding and brought many by their teaching to the same right faith. Somebody slandered the three to the emperor, saying that they did not only believe in the Crucified themselves but injured others with that faith as with a poison.

The emperor sent men to seize them, and they found the doors locked, for the faithful servants of the Lord were standing at prayer and would not open. The doors were broken in and they were brought bound before the emperor, who began by questioning them gently: where were they from, his sons? They answered that their country and their life was the All-Holy Trinity, of one essence and undivided, the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God. He told them they were very bold, and did not yet know what wounds and tortures were; they answered that they were bold for their God and ready for every kind of wound.

He had them stretched on the ground and beaten with thorned staves by four men each, and they blessed God with one voice while they were beaten; the beaters weakened and others were put in their place, and they were beaten so long that they ought to have died of it. Seeing their patience, that they neither cried out nor weakened, the emperor was seized with terror and fell off his throne; the saints called out that the Lord who gave him his life would raise him up again to see God's power in them, and those standing by lifted him, and he came to himself angrier than before, thinking it was sorcery.

He had them hung up with fire lit beneath them; and as they hung they sang to the Lord who was sold and spat upon and hung on the wood for them, asking him to look on their suffering. Then the Lord appeared to them as a man with a face bright as the sun, and at his appearing the bonds were loosed, the fire went out, and they were whole; and they stood before the emperor as though they had suffered nothing. When he blasphemed Christ they said, let your cunning lips be dumb — and he was struck speechless, could not answer them, and at last threw his purple on the ground and trampled it like a madman before everybody.

A shining company of angels was seen out of heaven, and many of the people fell down for fear and believed in Christ. Acindynus wept over the emperor's confusion and said, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, speak; and his tongue was loosed at once, and instead of thanks he threatened worse. He had an iron bed heated and the martyrs laid on it, and while they burned they sang the psalm, thou hast tried us as silver is tried, we went through fire. A voice came out of heaven saying that since they had confirmed their faith by deed their requests would be granted; and at their prayer for the people lightning and thunder and a great rain came down, in which, they said, the mystery of holy baptism was completed over those who had believed, and angels were seen clothing the newly enlightened in white. The fire went out and the bed cooled and they rose alive, only their bodies black as wood burned in a fire.

The emperor then tried cunning: since they honoured one God, let them honour with him the one god he loved best, the great Zeus. They answered that he should pray as he had been taught and they would pray as they had long been taught, and he took it for consent and rejoiced and asked their pardon for having tortured them. He brought them in his own chariot's company to the temple of Zeus — they would walk, they said, that there be no suspicion — and there, when he cried out for them to pray first to the great god, they signed their foreheads with the cross, knelt, lifted their hands and prayed to the One God in Trinity. The ground shook, the temple began to break, the emperor fled outside, and the temple fell with its idols and was ground to dust, while the saints stood untouched in the ruin.

Three cauldrons were then filled with lead, sulphur and pitch, and wood cut from old ships was burned under them; and the saints were chained and let down into the boiling, first to the waist, then to the breast, then to the neck. Each of them sang a verse of David: Pegasius, with thee is the fountain of life, in thy light shall we see light; Anempodistus, my feet have stood in uprightness, and thy law is a lamp to my feet; Acindynus, the pains of death have taken hold of us, but since we have passed through fire, do thou thyself bring us out into rest. They stayed unhurt, the chains fell off of themselves, and they came out whole in the sight of everybody.

At that wonder many knew the truth and believed; and one of the torturers, [Aphthonius](/saints/aphthonius-the-persian), cried out that the God of the Christians is great, and turned on the emperor: how long would he not leave this innocent people in peace, when the torturers were more worn out with torturing than the martyrs with enduring, while he himself was iron and stone-hearted. The emperor ordered him beheaded at once, and he lifted his eyes and said, glory to thee, Lord Jesus Christ, God in whom the Christians believe; I too believe in thee and worship thee and die for thee. The executioner put the rope on his neck to lead him out of the city, and he looked at the martyrs and begged them not to remember the evil he had done them at the emperor's order, but to pray that he be joined to the company of those who believe; and they said, rejoice, brother, you go to Christ before us.

Aphthonius bowed to them, went out of the city, called on the name of Jesus Christ, bent his neck under the sword and went rejoicing to the Lord, and the Christians buried his body honourably as a martyr's. Then the three were sewn into leather sacks and thrown into the sea; and Aphthonius appeared walking on the sea with three angels, drew them out, untied the sacks and brought them to land alive and whole as though they had never suffered anything. Hearing that they lived, the emperor supposed the four soldiers had disobeyed him, and had their hands cut off and them drowned in the sea; and they died calling on the Lord Jesus Christ and confessing his name.

The three were taken again and shut in prison in irons, and the emperor went to his chamber and reproached his nobles with leaving him to labour alone at these judgements. They answered that it was no profitable work to busy oneself with such judgements and to torture innocent Christians; and when he asked what they had been thinking the day before, when they held their mouths with their hands, one of the nobles, Elpidephorus, laughed and said that they had been mocking his madness in their own minds and thinking that they had been mad themselves to obey him. The emperor had a servant strike him across the face, and all the nobles were grieved and told him plainly: know, emperor, that we are not like you. Seeing them all of one mind with Elpidephorus he took fright and asked their pardon, saying his mind was troubled with much grief.

The martyrs were thrown next day into a pit full of wild animals and stayed unhurt, comforted by the appearing of an angel, and were taken out whole; then hung up and raked to the bones, and again unhurt. Not knowing what else to do, the emperor gave them up to be beheaded; and as they went out of the city to the place of execution many of those who had believed followed them weeping, asking why they were being left without teaching, and the saints answered that the merciful God would provide for them as he knew and willed, only let them believe in him without doubting.

A servant ran to tell the emperor that all the people had joined themselves to the three Christians and would not let them be killed, and that some of the nobles were in that crowd, Elpidephorus among them. Elpidephorus was called before him with three other nobles, and the emperor sat a long while with his head on his breast, then lifted it and asked what they meant by leaving the gods of their fathers for the Christian deceivers, and swore to spare nobody who believed in the Crucified. Elpidephorus answered: do what you will, for we are ready to die for the crucified Christ, who alone is the true and just God; your gods are all demons, whose unclean sacrifices we renounce, and for you, the demons' servant, we care nothing.

The sentence was given, that Elpidephorus and all with him, who had left the gods and chosen death, should be cut down with the sword, and that anybody who wished might take and care for their bodies without fear. The soldiers led them out of the city to the martyrs and to all the people who had believed, and when the decree was read out they all lifted their voices, glorifying God for showing them the good way out of this dark and deceiving world; and they began to kiss one another. The soldiers ringed them and cut them down, and there fell by the sword in that hour as many as seven thousand of those who had believed in Christ, together with Elpidephorus.

Acindynus, Pegasius and Anempodistus were not beheaded but shut in prison again, and the next day a furnace was heated for them. When they were brought out the emperor said, do you see this furnace? it is made ready for you; and Acindynus answered that a greater furnace was made ready for him in gehenna, to burn in for ever with the demons who were like him, and that his very name meant the king of the demons, so that his mother had named him Shapur well. The emperor called his mother in, gave her the honour due to her, set her beside him and asked her what his name was; she said he bore his grandfather's name, Shapur.

He pointed at the saints and said that they called his name a demon's name; and his mother laughed, for she had believed in Christ and hid it from her wicked son. Seeing her laugh he flew at her in such a fury that he began to beat her about the face; and she ran and fell at the martyrs' feet weeping, saying, save my old age, servants of Christ, for I know not by the name only but by the deed that it is a devil and a satan that I have borne in this wretch. Seeing that his mother too believed, he condemned her to be thrown into the burning furnace with the martyrs; and of the soldiers standing by, twenty-eight believed, and all of them were thrown in with the three and with the emperor's blessed mother.

Praying there in the fire they gave up their holy souls into God's hand, and those who were worthy saw a company of holy angels round the furnace, singing and receiving their souls. A sweet fragrance came out of the martyrs' bodies and the furnace went out, and the emperor went to his palace and the crowd scattered. Then some of the faithful came to the furnace and found the bodies whole and unharmed by the fire, and took them and buried them with honour.

Elpidephorus was not of the three either but a noble of the court, who mocked the emperor's madness openly, was struck in the face for it, refused before him to leave Christ, and was cut down with the sword outside the city together with as many as seven thousand of those who had believed.
