# Samuel

Samuel was the fifteenth and last judge of Israel, living — the Sretensky calendar’s life says — 1146 years before the birth of Christ. Of the tribe of Levi, the son of Elkanah of Ramathaim-Zophim in the hill country of Ephraim, he was born asked of the Lord by the prayers of his mother Hannah — hence his name, “asked for” — and was dedicated to God before his birth; when he was three she took him to Shiloh and by her vow gave him to the tabernacle, into the care of the high priest Eli, who was also judge over Israel. He grew in the fear of God, and at twelve had the revelation that God would punish the whole house of Eli for not restraining his sons.

The Prologue, which keeps him for the Serbian calendar, carries the story on: the Philistines’ victory, the death of Eli’s sons and of Eli at ninety-eight, twenty years of bondage, Samuel’s preaching of repentance, the people’s turning from the foreign idols and the Philistines beaten with God’s help; his judging of the people in peace into old age; the people’s demand for a king, which displeased God, and at God’s command Samuel’s anointing of Saul, and when Saul was rejected for his insolence and disobedience, of David the son of Jesse; and how before his death he gathered all the people and took his leave, and all Israel wept for him and buried him with honour in his house at Ramah.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 20 August. Doxologia.ro's life opens before there were kings in Israel, while the people of God were still led by judges, with a man of the tribe of Levi named Elkanah who lived in the city of Ramah by the mountain called Ziph in the portion of Ephraim — the city later called Armathem or Arimathea, from which came the Joseph who buried the most pure body of the Lord. Elkanah had two wives, Hannah and Peninnah; Peninnah bore sons and Hannah did not, and because Elkanah loved Hannah the more, Peninnah hated her and grieved her. Each year he went up with both wives to the great feasts at Shiloh, where before Jerusalem the tabernacle and the ark of God were and where all the tribes of Israel came to worship, and where Eli was then high priest and judge of Israel, with his two sons Hophni and Phinehas. The life closes with the testimony of Sirach: that before his end Samuel witnessed before the Lord and before His anointed that he had taken nothing from any man, not so much as a sandal, and that no man could reproach him; and that after his repose he prophesied and showed King Saul his end.
