# Sergius (Kazansky)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him twelfth of fifteen on 21 октября ст. ст., the first of the two deacons in a line of eleven priests, two deacons and two monks of 1937. He was born on 23 September 1879 in the village of Ivanovskoye in Yaroslavl province, the son of a priest, Alexei Kazansky. He finished two courses of the Yaroslavl theological school and then, not wishing to serve in the Church, left for St Petersburg, went through a military school and was commissioned an officer.

He had a fine singing voice, and was invited into the imperial chapel. There he was presented to the imperial family, who sometimes had him come to Livadia. It was apparently in those years, the calendar's life says, that he was ordained deacon and raised to protodeacon, the rank in which he served until his martyr's death; the page gives no year for either. In 1932 he was arrested, sentenced to five years' exile and expelled from the diocese of St Petersburg, and so came to the town of Rzhev in the Tver region. In July 1937 he moved to Torzhok and served in the Nikolo-Pustynsky church.

On 17 August the assistant plenipotentiary of the district office of the state security administration summoned his salaried informer, a man who knew nothing about anything but was ready to sign whatever was put to him. The protodeacon was arrested on 21 August and taken to the prison at Tver, and the duty witnesses were questioned at once. They spoke of his closeness to the imperial family and his singing before the revolution in the Anichkov palace; of his praising life under the tsar; of his saying that life had grown hard under the Soviet power, that the people were worn out, that a peasant is made to work day and night and everything he earns is taken from him, and that the churches were given no peace but taxed enormously.

The day after all this testimony he was brought to interrogation. Asked to give a full account of the anti-Soviet agitation he had carried on, he said he had carried on none. Told he was lying and that the investigation insisted on truthful testimony, he said a second time that he had carried on none. Told that on 5 August he had held an anti-Soviet conversation about the hard life of the working people and praised life under the tsar, he said he had held no such conversation. Asked whether he admitted holding, at the beginning of August, a plainly fascist and insurrectionary conversation in favour of Germany, Spain and Japan, he answered that he did not admit guilt, since he had held no such conversation. On 1 November the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 3 November 1937. He was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
