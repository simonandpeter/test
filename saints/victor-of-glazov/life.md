# Victor of Glazov

Victor, Constantine Alexandrovich Ostrovidov in the world, was born on 20 May 1875 in the village of Zolotoye in the Saratov region. His father was a chanter, and his family brought him up in the teaching and admonition of the Lord. He studied first at the seminary of Saratov and then at the academy of Kazan, and while still a student was tonsured a monk with the name Victor. In 1903 he graduated a doctor of theology and was put in charge of the church of the Trinity in the town of Khvalynsk.

From 1905 to 1908 he held the place of hieromonk of the Jerusalem mission, and after 1908 became inspector of the seminary of Archangelsk. Soon he was moved to the capital and appointed hieromonk of the Lavra of St Alexander Nevsky; in 1910 he was made archimandrite and took up the duties of abbot of the Holy Trinity of Zelenetsk in the province of St Petersburg, and through the hard months of the civil war, from 21 February to December 1919, he acted as the Lavra's superior.

In 1919 he was arrested at St Petersburg and soon released. In January 1920 he was consecrated bishop of Urzhum and vicar of the Vyatka diocese, and that same year the revolutionary tribunal of Vyatka sentenced him to prison until the end of the war with Poland; five months later he was let out. For his outspoken protests he was arrested again on 12 August 1922 and deported three years to the Narym country. Released in 1924, he was forbidden to live in the large cities, returned to Vyatka, and was made bishop of Glazov and temporary administrator of the dioceses of Vyatka and Omsk.

In October 1927 he wrote to the metropolitan Sergius warning him of the evil consequences of compromise with the godless, and was transferred to Shadrinsk; he refused to obey the synod's decision and did not go. He rejected the idea of a lawful existence for the Church secured through a central administration recognised by the authorities, holding that such a plan of submission was an estrangement that would turn the Church from the house of God into a worldly organ of the state. In March 1928 he wrote again to the priests: «Το θέμα μας ευρίσκεται όχι στην αποχώρηση από την Εκκλησία, αλλά στην υπεράσπιση της αλήθειας» — our business is not withdrawal from the Church but the defence of the truth.

On 22 March 1928 he was arrested at Glazov and sentenced to three years in the concentration camps. All through his stay on the island of Solovki he worked as a bookkeeper, served the liturgy in secret and, with other bishops, ordained priests for the Church. The camp forbade long clothing and required every prisoner to be shorn; the bishop refused, and for that they shut him in isolation, shaved him by force, cutting his face, and cut his garments short.

Released in 1931 he lived in the village of Ust-Tsilma in northern Russia, but within months, in 1932, he was arrested again and exiled to the Komi autonomy in Siberia, to the village of Neritsa, where he lived three years, helping the villagers at their work, talking with them about the faith, and often going off into the forest to pray. He took all of it, the page says, with spiritual joy and patience, in imitation of the martyrs of the early Church, and he fell asleep in 1934 of pneumonia. On 18 June 1997 his relics were found incorrupt in the village cemetery, though they had lain sixty-three years in marshy ground; they were carried to Moscow, and on 2 December 1997 solemnly translated to the convent of the city of Vyatka.

The Russian church calendar is the first source read for this folder to give his life year by year, and it agrees with saint.gr at both ends: born 20 мая 1875 in the village of Золотое in the Камышинский district of the government of Saratov, the son of a psalm-reader, and dead in 1934 of inflammation of the lungs. His name in the world was «Константин Александрович Островидов»; he finished the Kamyshin church school and the Saratov seminary, took the monastic habit with this name while a student of the Kazan academy, and graduated from it in 1903 a candidate of theology. He was then rector of the Trinity cathedral at Khvalynsk, a hieromonk of the Jerusalem mission from 1905 to 1908, inspector of the Archangel church school from 1909, a hieromonk of the Alexander-Nevsky Lavra, superior of the Zelenetsky Holy Trinity monastery from 1910 with the rank of archimandrite, and in 1919, through the civil war, the Lavra’s own deputy superior.

From then on the page is a list of arrests. Petrograd 1919, released soon; consecrated bishop of Urzhum, vicar of the Vyatka diocese, in January 1920, and sentenced by the Vyatka revolutionary tribunal to imprisonment until the end of the war with Poland, released after five months; arrested again on 12 (25) августа 1922 for speaking against the Renovationists and exiled three years to the Narym country; released in 1924 and forbidden the large cities, he returned to Vyatka and was made bishop of Glazov; arrested on 14 мая 1926 for organising an unlicensed diocesan chancery and exiled three years, settling in Glazov itself.

What the page is really about is 1927. He received the Declaration of that year for reading out to the clergy and faithful of the Votkinsk diocese, would not read it, sealed it in an envelope and sent it back to its author, the metropolitan «Сергий (Страгородский)»; the page adds that as early as 1911 he had written to the same man that he would shake the Church by his error. He wrote to him again in October, respectfully, warning that if the position were not reconsidered «в Церкви произойдёт великий раскол» — a great schism will come about in the Church — and begging «пощадите Русскую Православную Церковь». The answer was first an instruction to know his place as a vicar and then an appointment to Shadrinsk with the Yekaterinburg diocese, which he refused to obey and did not take up. In December the diocesan administration broke prayerful and canonical communion with the metropolitan until he should repent and renounce the Declaration, and he endorsed the decision; on 8 (22) декабря the Glazov administration declared him its spiritual head, and he wrote on the protocol that he rejoiced in the grace of God that had enlightened their hearts in this difficult and great business of choosing the path of truth. He was among the first bishops to go over to self-government, and the opposition he led in the Vyatka and Votsk dioceses was called after him. On 23 декабря 1927 the temporary synod forbade him to serve, and he did not recognise the ban.

He was arrested at Glazov on 22 марта (4 апреля) 1928 and sentenced to three years in the camps, handing over his parishes first to the bishop of Gdov. At Solovki, from June 1928 to 1930, he kept the accounts of a rope factory and took part in secret services in the woods of the island at the risk of being tortured and shot, and — the page says — in secret consecrations of bishops, kept hidden even from those closest to the men concerned. It quotes the memoirs of «Д. Лихачёв», who was in the camp with him: that the great majority of the clergy there were of his party and all the believing young men with them, and that this was not only the usual radicalism of the young but because at their head stood a wonderfully attractive man, very learned, with printed theological works, from whom there came a kind of radiance of kindness and cheerfulness, who tried to help everybody and could, because everybody thought well of him and believed his word. When an order came that all prisoners be shorn and long clothing forbidden, he refused, and was taken to the punishment cell, shaved by force with his face badly cut, and his clothing cropped crooked; and Лихачёв thought that he resisted without bitterness and counted his suffering a mercy. Every parcel he got from the mainland he gave away to other prisoners.

In the spring of 1930 he was moved to the mainland, sentenced on review to three years’ exile in the Northern country, and after his release from the camp in the summer of 1931 banished to the village of Усть-Цильма. Arrested again within months, in 1932 he was taken to Syktyvkar and sentenced to three years in the Komi-Zyryan region, where he lived in the village of Нерица in the house of the chairman of the village soviet, helping the family with plain work about the place, splitting firewood for the peasants, talking about the faith with the exiled Old Believers who lived there, and often going off into the taiga to pray at length. He died on 19 апреля — 2 мая new style — 1934, of inflammation of the lungs; the river was in flood and they could not get him to the district centre.

On 18 июня (1 июля new style) 1997 his relics were found incorrupt in the village cemetery at Нерица after sixty-three years in boggy ground. They were sent to Moscow and on 2 декабря 1997 translated to the church of Alexander Nevsky in the Holy Trinity Makariev convent at Vyatka, where the page says they remain, giving out fragrance and granting healings. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
