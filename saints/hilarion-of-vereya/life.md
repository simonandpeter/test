# Hilarion of Vereya

Born on 13 September 1886 in a village priest’s family at Lipitsy, in the Kashira district, he was Vladimir Troitsky in the world. One reading puts the village in the Tula province and the other in the Moscow province, and the two are left standing side by side here rather than one chosen. He read the hours and the six psalms in church at five years old; he finished the Moscow Theological Academy in 1910 at the head of his year and was kept there as a professorial fellow, and in 1913 he took the degree of master of theology for a long study of the history of the doctrine of the Church.

He was tonsured on 28 March 1913 at a skete of the Trinity-Sergius Lavra, ordained hieromonk on 2 June and raised archimandrite on 5 July, and was made inspector of the academy that same year and extraordinary professor of the New Testament before it ended. His whole theology turned on one sentence he repeated: outside the Church there is no salvation, and outside the Church there are no sacraments. As a member of the Council of 1917 and 1918 he spoke for the restoration of the patriarchate, and he was consecrated archbishop of Vereya.

From 1923 he was a prisoner at Solovki, where he was one of the authors of the memorandum of the imprisoned bishops of May 1925 on how the Church might live beside a state whose principles were against it. He was taken out of Solovki to a prison at Yaroslavl to be pressed into one of the schisms of those years, and refused: he would sooner rot in prison, he said, than change his course. A fresh three-year term followed, and then Solovki again.

In the autumn of 1929 he was sentenced once more, to three years’ exile in Central Asia, and sent there by stages from one transit prison to the next. He was robbed on the road and caught typhus among the prisoners, and arrived at Leningrad in rags and already ill. He died in the prison hospital on 15/28 December 1929, after telling the doctor who said the crisis had passed how good it was, and was buried in the cemetery of the Novodevichy monastery there.
