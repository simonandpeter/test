# Isidore (10 May)

The venerable Isidore was born in the year 319 and came of Alexandria, saint.gr says. He was a disciple of the venerable [Antony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great) and a fellow-ascetic of the venerable Macarius. He went with Saint [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria) to Rome, and from there he set out with the venerable Melania for the desert of Egypt. The day's line calls him ο μωρός, the fool.

He was marked out by his meekness and, besides the gift of tears, by the ease with which he would fall into ecstasy while he was eating. With the Fathers of the desert Macarius and Pambo he used to strike at the monk who would not go without possessions: let thy silver be with thee unto perdition.

He suffered a great deal from Theophilus, Patriarch of Alexandria, who at first wanted to make him a bishop and afterwards drove him out, not from the Church of Alexandria only but from the desert where he practised his asceticism. Isidore then, with the monks who were his disciples, sought the protection of the holy [Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom). He fell asleep in peace in the year 404. The page names no monastery of his own, no writings, no relics and no burial place, and prints neither apolytikion nor kontakion for him.
