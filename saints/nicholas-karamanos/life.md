# Nicholas Karamanos

saint.gr's calendar for 6 December keeps the new martyr Nicholas Karamanos, whom the same page also calls Kassetis. He was born in 1623, was a married man, and lived at Smyrna. Having once lost his temper, he said in his anger that he would become a Turk; and the Turks standing by, as soon as they heard that word, seized him and took him before the judge.

Under examination in the court Nicholas said with boldness that he would never deny his Maker and Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ, who is to judge the living and the dead. Neither the torments nor the beatings nor the imprisonment moved him, nor even the pressure his mother and his wife put upon him; and the judge then ordered that the martyr be circumcised by force. He went on confessing and was imprisoned a second time.

His endurance and patience astonished everybody, the page says, among them the Jesuit Vabois, who wrote the account of his martyrdom. After hard tortures which lasted thirty-six days he was taken to the gallows, and so was perfected as a martyr on 19 March of the year 1657, which was Great Thursday, at nine in the morning. His body was tied to a block of stone and thrown into the sea; but a certain Latin who was at Smyrna took it out and carried it to Europe.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and gives him two lines, which agree with the Greek page on the year and add the place and the manner: bitterly tortured by the Turks for the faith of Christ, and hanged at Smyrna in 1657. It gives no year of his birth, no hour and no day of the week, where saint.gr gives all three and dates the martyrdom itself to 19 March of that year — a day the Greek calendar does not keep him on either, both calendars printing him on 6 December. The book calls him simply a martyr; the fuller new-martyr this folder holds is the Greek page's word and its 1657 is this book's year too.
