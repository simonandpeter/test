# Gurias of Edessa

The life doxologia prints for 15 noiembrie is one account of three men, and it begins with two of them. When the Church of God was hard pressed by Diocletian and Maximian, there were near the city of Edessa two right-believing men of good works, Gurias and [Samonas](/saints/samonas-of-edessa). They had been brought up in Edessa but would not stay in it, for the quarrels and lawlessness done there; so they went out of the city, keeping away from the unbelieving and serving God day and night in fasting and prayer.

They did not serve alone but taught others, and turned many from the worship of idols to the true God. Learning of them, Antoninus, then set by the Roman emperors as governor in Edessa, ordered them seized along with all who followed their teaching; and the confessors of Christ were held under guard with a multitude of the faithful.

After their imprisonment and torments the governor set them before him again and told them they had had time enough to choose between life and death. They answered that they had chosen what would profit them: rather to receive death for Christ than life for this empty world. He offered them their houses and peace for a grain of incense on the altar of Zeus, and they said that many words were not needed, and that they would not cease to say they were servants of the Lord Jesus Christ.

So he gave sentence that they be beheaded, and the saints rejoiced to hear of the sword, because they would the sooner be loosed from the body. The tyrant had them put in a cart and taken far outside the city to be beheaded there; they were led out by the northern gates, none of the citizens knowing, because all were asleep at that hour, and were brought up a mountain called Vetilavicle, where they asked a little time and were killed.

Some years afterwards, on the same day, [Abibus the deacon](/saints/abibus-the-deacon-of-edessa) suffered too and was buried beside them, and when the persecution ceased the faithful built a church in the name of the three. The page gives no year for any of it, naming only the emperors under whom they suffered.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 15 November, in one line with his two companions, and calls all three confessors. saint.gr says that Gurias and [Samonas](/saints/samonas-of-edessa), contending in the holy struggle of the Christian faith, were seized by the governor Antoninus during the persecution under Diocletian, and that after bearing many torments with wonderful patience they were beheaded. [Abibus](/saints/abibus-the-deacon-of-edessa), it adds, lived a few years later. The calendar's kontakion for the three is their own and not a common: it says that having received grace from on high they stand by those in trials, that they delivered a maiden from a bitter death, and that they are the glory of Edessa and the joy of the world.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 15 November in one line with his two companions and calls all three martyrs and confessors. Its life says that he and [Samonas](/saints/samonas-of-edessa) were two friends, preachers of the word of God, seized in the city of Edessa in the persecution under Diocletian (284–305) and Maximian (305–311); that they refused outright to sacrifice and confessed their faith in Christ; that they were beaten, hung up by the hands with a heavy weight tied to their feet, and thrown into a narrow prison; and that they were carried out of the city by night and beheaded. This is the one source of the three to give years, and it gives theirs as 299–306.

The same page copies out the prayer one witness of their torments wrote down, in which they ask the God who widened David’s heart in affliction to look on them and keep the lamp of His commandments in them unquenched. The day prints a troparion of глас 5 over the three names and a kontakion of глас 2, the Slavonic of the kontakion the Greek calendar prints; the kontakion is their own, for it names Edessa and the girl their miracle delivered.
