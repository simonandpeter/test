# Alexander (Vinogradov)

Alexander was born on 1 June 1884 in the family of the retired psalmist
Ivan Mikhailovich Vinogradov, who served as refectorian at the
Resurrection church of the Vagankovo cemetery in Moscow. In 1899 he
finished the Donskoy church school and in 1905 the Moscow seminary; on 1
October 1909, the feast of the Protection of the Mother of God, he was
ordained priest and appointed to the Transfiguration church of Lyubertsy
in Moscow province. In 1920 he was called up for some months and served in
the rear militia; in 1929 he was raised to archpriest and in 1932 awarded
the palitsa. When the priest of Perkhushkovo in the Zvenigorod district
died in November 1933, the church authorities on 21 November appointed him
to its Protection church, where he served until his arrest. In the mass
arrests of believers that began in the summer of 1937 the authorities
resolved to arrest him: on 27 November 1937 he was taken to the cell at
the Zvenigorod militia station, and a few days later to the Taganka prison
in Moscow. The night after his arrest the interrogation began before
midnight and lasted until five in the morning of 29 November. With whom do
you keep up connections? — I keep up written connections with no one; in
person with my dean, and with Ivan Alexeyevich Tsvetkov, priest of the
Yudino church, who is my confessor; I visit him only in the Great,
Dormition and Nativity fasts, and he visits me. — You are charged with
carrying on counter-revolutionary activity against the Soviet power; I
demand frank testimony on the substance of the charge. — I admit myself
guilty in that, before the new constitution was confirmed, in talk with
clergy and with some parishioners and singers who came to my flat, I voiced
discontent with the rightless position of the clergy; of nothing else do
I admit myself guilty. On the evening of 29 November, soon after nine, he
was called to another interrogation of almost five hours. The inquiry has
established that you carried on counter-revolutionary activity against
the Soviet power; we demand truthful testimony. — I carried on no
counter-revolutionary activity. — When and why did former exiles from
among the clergy come to you? — In May 1937, at Pascha, a subdeacon came
to my church, introduced himself, said he had come from exile with the
bishop and asked help in his name; I gave him fifteen roubles, and after
an hour or two he went to look for lodgings. In about September a Tambov
priest returning from exile at Arkhangelsk came to my flat and also asked
help; I gave him three roubles, bread and herring, and after five or ten
minutes he left, where to I do not know. Three or four days later a
deacon, also back from exile, came asking help, took three roubles from me
and left at once. In October a priest from the village of Kudinovo, back
from exile in the Northern region, came and asked help; I gave him three
roubles, dined with him, and he left for Perovo station. — You are lying;
the inquiry demands truthful testimony on the reasons the exiled clergy
visited you. — The exiled clergy who came to me asked for help; there was
no other reason. On 5 December 1937 the NKVD troika sentenced him to ten
years in a corrective labour camp, and he was sent with a convoy of
prisoners to the Far East. On 22 September 1942 he died of pellagra at
Svobodny and was buried in an unknown grave — 9 September of the old
style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him, with the synaxis of the
saints of Moscow. He was glorified in 2000; the life is by Igumen
Damascene (Orlovsky).
