# Pinna

Pinna was one of three martyrs whom doxologia.ro's calendar keeps together on 20 ianuarie, with [Inna](/saints/inna) and [Rimma](/saints/rimma), and whose life it tells in a single paragraph. They were of a country in the north, the page says, and were taken by idol-worshipping barbarians and brought before the ruler of the place, who, seeing that they confessed Christ, condemned them to die by freezing. No year is given, and no name for their country.

They were bound to stakes driven into the water of a river, and it was winter. The water froze and became as motionless as the motionless bodies of the saints, and so they ended their lives and gave up their blessed souls into the hands of God. The Romanian page says nothing that would distinguish one of the three from another, and nothing further is put here.

The Greek calendar keeps the three together on the same day and says where they came from and what was done to them. saint.gr says that [Innas](/saints/inna), Pinnas and [Rimmas](/saints/rimma) were from the region of Scythia and were called «οι κρυστάλλινοι», the crystalline ones; that they bore witness after being bound to a piece of wood, in a time of terrible frost, and set in a frozen river; that dreadful and unbearable were the pains their naked flesh was tried with there, but that their souls cast off nothing of their warm devotion, and so they received the crown of witness. The epigram over them plays on the same word: «Θάλψις δεχέσθω τοὺς Ἀθλητὰς τοῦ κρύους, Ἰννᾶν, Πιννᾶν, Ῥιμμᾶν τε τοὺς κρυσταλλίνους». The page gives them no year.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the three of them together on this day and says more of them than the other three calendars do. It reckons them the first Slavic martyrs named in history, calls them Scythians and disciples of the holy apostle [Andrew the First-Called](/saints/andrew-the-first-called), and puts their suffering on the right bank of the Danube, near Varna, at the hands of their own heathen neighbours: bound on the ice, it says, they froze and fell asleep in the Lord. Its heading names the three „Енен, Нирен и Пен“, and nothing on the page says which of those forms belongs to which of the three men.

Инны, Пинны и Риммы (I-II)» and makes each of them «Мученик», a man; and the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 20. јануар ст. ст. — [the day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-02-02&prolog=1), read 3 October 2026.*
