# Senuphius the Standard-bearer

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him one short paragraph on the Julian 28 June, and it is the whole of what is written here. Senuphius was a great faster and a wonderworker out of the Egyptian desert, a contemporary of the patriarch Theophilus and of the emperor [Theodosius the Great](/saints/theodosius-the-great). He was called the Standard-bearer, the book says, because his prayers once helped that emperor to hold a victory over an enemy army.

When the emperor called him to come to Constantinople he answered that he could not come, and sent him instead one poor habit of his own and his staff. Going out to the war, the emperor put on the habit of Senuphius and took up the staff; and he came back from the war victorious.

That is the entire entry. It gives no year for anything, names no place but the Egyptian desert and Constantinople, and says nothing of his birth, his tonsure, his monastery or his death. The two men it sets beside him, a patriarch of Alexandria and an emperor, are all the chronology there is, and a reign is not a date.
