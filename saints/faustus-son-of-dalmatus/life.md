# Faustus, son of Dalmatus

Faustus was the one son whom [Dalmatus](/saints/dalmatus-of-constantinople) took with him when, after serving as a soldier in the reign of Theodosius the Great and standing in honour with the emperor, he despised all worldly things for God's sake and left his wife and his other children. Father and son went to [Isaacius](/saints/isaac-of-constantinople), who had a monastery in the town before Constantinople, and Isaacius tonsured them both into the monastic order.

The two lived there together in a way pleasing to God, striving well in the labours of the desert; and afterwards, when Isaacius died in a deep old age, Dalmatus was set as abbot in his place. Of Faustus himself the day's page says only that he passed through all that is pleasing to God and through the whole monastic struggle, and, finishing well the ascetic course, went over to the eternal dwellings and was numbered with the venerable fathers in the life that has no death.

That is all doxologia.ro prints of him: no year, no place of his own, and no hymn but the one it gives to the three of them together.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them on the same day, 3 August. saint.gr says that Dalmatios was a soldier who left the service when the desire to give himself to his Maker mastered him, and that he set out with his son Faustos to find the monk Isaac, whose fame had drawn many men to him; that Dalmatios was chosen abbot after Isaac's repose and was honoured by the Third Ecumenical Council at Ephesus in 431, where the Fathers declared him venerable archimandrite; and that he ended his life in peace. Of Faustos himself it says only this, that he followed his father's road and showed himself a worthy successor to him. The page prints an apolytikion and a kontakion for the three together, and it adds that a portion of Dalmatios's relic is at Nea Thebais on the Holy Mountain and a portion of Isaac's at the monastery of Kykkos in Cyprus.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović names him third in the heading of its Julian 3 August and gives him one sentence of its own. His father [Dalmatus](/saints/dalmatus-of-constantinople) was an officer under the emperor Theodosius the Great who despised the world, left his rank and took his only son with him to the house of [Isaac](/saints/isaac-of-constantinople) in the suburbs of Constantinople, where both of them were tonsured as monks.

His own sentence is the last of the entry: his son Faustus imitated his father in everything, and after a God-pleasing life ended peacefully in that same house of Dalmatou. The book gives no year for him, no age and no reign. The fifth century it gives two sentences earlier is stated of the father and not of the son, and it is not recorded here.
