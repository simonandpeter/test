# Maria (Volnukhina)

She was born on 3 March 1876 in Moscow, in a trader's family, and married the manufacturer Ivan Gerasimovich Volnukhin. Both of them, the page says, were deeply believing people, for whom the faith was the chief value and the content of their life. Soon after the revolution she lost her husband. In 1927 all her property was nationalised. In 1932 she was sentenced to three years of exile in the Northern Krai, in the town of Kotlas, but she left there before long, and from then on she wandered with no fixed place to live. Among the faithful she was honoured for an ascetic life and for seeing what others could not.

Arrested on 29 September 1937 with [Demetrius (Dobroserdov)](/saints/demetrius-dobroserdov), archbishop of Mozhaysk, and seven others, she admitted no guilt. The life quotes two of her answers, and both of them are about the destitution the exile had left her in. Asked what she had lived on, she said: I lived on what I got from begging. Asked where she had lived after her return from exile, she said she had no fixed place to live, that she stayed with acquaintances, and that she refused to name them.

The NKVD troika sentenced the company to be shot on 17 October 1937, and on 21 October she was shot at the Butovo range outside Moscow and buried in a common unmarked grave. She was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
