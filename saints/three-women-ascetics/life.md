# The Three Women Ascetics

The Ohrid Prologue keeps three holy women together: a noblewoman of Constantinople and her two maidservants, who despised the vanity of the world and withdrew into solitude, where after eleven years of ascesis they reposed in the Lord. It gives no year, and none of their names.

Their story is told in the Prologue’s reflection for the day, which has it from Paul, later bishop of Monemvasia. While still a layman and a collector of the imperial taxes he stayed at a monastery, and seeing ravens settle on the fruit trees, break off laden branches and carry them away, he followed them with the monks into a trackless wood, where the birds let the branches fall and flew straight back. There they found a cave, and in it three women ascetics.

The eldest told them her life: she was a noblewoman of Constantinople, and when her husband died a great man meant to take her as his wife by force, though she had resolved to spend the rest of her life in virginity; so she gave away her wealth to the poor, took her two maidservants and fled with them to that empty place. Eleven years they spent there in fasting and prayer, seeing no one and seen by no one but God, and God’s providence had the birds bring them fruit to eat. They asked the abbot to bring them Holy Communion, and three days after they had received it all three reposed; the monks buried them with honour.
