# Therapon of the White Lake

saint.gr says he was Theodore in the world, born in 1337 in the town of Volokolamsk to the devout family Poskochin. His parents brought him up in the instruction and admonition of the Lord, and from childhood he showed his bent and his love for the monastic life. So he was tonsured a monk and settled north of the White Lake together with [Cyril of the White Lake](/saints/cyril-of-the-white-lake), whom the page marks as kept on 9 June, and there he lived as a hermit practised in silence.

The holiness of his life drew disciples around him, and they organised a monastery which later took his own name. In 1398 he built a wooden church dedicated to the Nativity of the Mother of God, the monks helping him in the work and copying church books beside it; about 1500 a stone church was raised on the same spot, famous for its wall-paintings, the work of the iconographer Dionysius and his sons Vladimir and Theodore.

Out of humility he gave up the place of abbot of his monastery, put his own spiritual guidance into the hands of one of its fathers, and went back to his counsellor Cyril. In 1408 the prince Andrew Dmitrievich asked him to found a new monastery in the town of Mozhaysk, and there, on the hill of Luzhki, he raised it and received the office of archimandrite from [Photius](/saints/photius-of-kyiv), metropolitan of Moscow. He lived there eighteen years and reposed in peace in 1426; his relic was buried in the north aisle of the church of the Nativity of the Mother of God and was found in 1514.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 27 мая ст. ст. and gives him two readings. He was Феодор in the world, born in 1337 at Volokolamsk into the noble family of the Поскочины, and brought up from childhood in a deep faith and piety. At forty, without a preliminary novitiate, he was tonsured by Феодор, abbot of the Moscow Simonov monastery and nephew of Сергий; and in that house he became close to Кирилл Белозерский, with whom he went through the labour of salvation in fasting and prayer, and the two of them heard the spiritual instructions of Сергий Радонежский when he visited the monastery to teach the brethren.

Sent north to the Belozersk country on the monastery’s business, he was drawn by that hard northern land and resolved to stay there for his labours. He went back, and then, with Кирилл — to whom the Mother of God had appeared and bidden him go north — and with his abbot’s blessing, set out for the White Lake. For a time the two lived together in a cell they built; then, by mutual agreement, he moved to a new place fifteen versts from Kirillov, between the two lakes Бородавское and Павское. Clearing a small patch for a garden and setting a cell in the deep forest by a watercourse, he went on in solitude and silence, bearing many hardships and more than once attacked by robbers who tried to drive him out or kill him. In time monks began to come to him, and the wilderness place turned little by little into a monastery, afterwards called the Ferapontov. In 1398 he built a wooden church in honour of the Nativity of the Most Holy Mother of God.

The house was ordered by the rule of common life, strictly kept; and he, not taking the abbacy out of humility, gave it to one of his disciples and still went for spiritual counsel to his friend at the White Lake. In 1408 the Mozhaysk prince Андрей Димитриевич, son of the grand prince Димитрий Иоаннович, having heard of the high spiritual life of the Belozersk ascetic, asked him to found a house in the town of Mozhaysk. It was hard to leave the house where he had laboured more than ten years, but on the brethren’s advice he went; and he was met at Mozhaysk with great honour, and founded his second monastery near the town, at Luzhki on the hilly right bank of the Moskva, with its chief church again in honour of the Nativity of the Mother of God, in memory of the Belozersk house. The prince, who deeply honoured him for his true humility, gave generous help in the building and adorning of it. By the blessing of Фотий, metropolitan of Moscow, an archimandriteship was established in the house and he was raised to that rank.

He lived eighteen years in the new house and departed to God in deep old age, on 27 мая 1426, and was buried by the north wall of the church of the Nativity of the Mother of God. A church was afterwards built over the burial place, renamed in 1720 in his own name. His relics were found incorrupt in 1514 and were glorified by many wonders, and after the Moscow council of 1547 he was canonised, the abbot of the Ferapontov monastery having brought the metropolitan Макарий his life and the testimonies of his sanctity. The calendar closes by setting his memory twice over: «27 мая (преставление, 1426) и 27 декабря (обретение мощей, 1514)».
