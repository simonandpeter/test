# Dionysius the Areopagite

Dionysius came from Athens and lived and suffered, the Greek synaxarion says, in the reign of Domitian. He was known for his philosophical training and deep learning, and was at first a pagan and a member of the council of the Areopagus, until the preaching of the apostle Paul touched his educated and sensitive soul and he was baptised. He later succeeded the devout [Hierotheus](/saints/hierotheus-of-athens) on the episcopal throne of Athens, and God rewarded his work with the gift of miracles.

He travelled through many parts of the West preaching the Gospel and interpreting the Scriptures, and at Paris he was arrested and beheaded, and with him two of his disciples, [Rusticus](/saints/rusticus-disciple-of-dionysius) and [Eleutherius](/saints/eleutherius-disciple-of-dionysius); the governor forbade anyone to bury the martyrs, but some Christians kept their relics and buried them with honour when there was nothing more to fear. The entry quotes Michael Galanos (1951), for whom the synaxaria and the Menaion overturn the historical truth on this day, so that the martyrdom at Paris is a mistake; it says he wrote a great many theological works, that no more is known for certain of his life than these few facts, and that the rest the synaxaria tell belongs to tradition alone. Since an encyclical of the Archdiocese of Athens of 30 September 1999 he is also kept on 12 October, with the synaxis of the saints of Athens.

doxologia.ro’s life tells the tradition at length. Born at Athens of unbelieving but noble parents, he outstripped the older philosophers by the age of twenty-five and went to Heliopolis in Egypt with his friend Apollophanes to learn the reading of the stars, and there he saw the sun darkened at noon for three hours and said that either God the Creator was suffering or this visible world was ending. Back at Athens he married and governed the city.

When Paul preached on the Areopagus, Dionysius disputed with him and showed him the altar to the unknown God, saying that god had not yet appeared but would come and reign over heaven and earth. A man blind from birth, healed by Paul as he left the city, was sent to Dionysius with Paul’s message, and Dionysius was baptised with his wife, whom the life calls Damar, his children and all his household; then he left them and followed Paul for three years, and Paul made him bishop and sent him from Thessalonica to Athens.

The life has him with the apostles at the burial of the Mother of God, and says he wrote of being at the Saviour’s tomb with Hierotheus and Timothy and seeing James the Lord’s brother, Peter and [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), from whom he learned the mysteries of the faith. Setting another bishop in his place at Athens, he went to Rome, where Clement received him and sent him to Gaul with the bishop Lucian, the priest Rusticus, the deacon Eleutherius and other brethren, and he built a church at Paris.

Under Domitian the governor Sisinnius came to Paris and had the aged Dionysius bound and dragged before him with Rusticus and Eleutherius. The three said with one voice that they were Christians with one God in heaven and would not obey the emperor; they were beaten without mercy and imprisoned. Dionysius was laid on an iron bed over a fire, thrown to beasts that did not touch him and into a great fire that did not burn him, and in prison he served the Liturgy for the faithful, who saw a great light over him. Condemned to the sword, he prayed on the way to the hill called the mount of Ares, and was beheaded with a blunt axe, and Rusticus and Eleutherius with him.

The life says that his headless body rose and carried its head two stadia to a believing woman named Catulla and fell there, where a church was built, and that Catulla, keeping the guards at her table, let the Christians take the body and bury it on that spot. He suffered, it says, in the ninetieth year of his life and the ninety-sixth from the birth of Christ. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 3 October; whether the Damar of the Romanian life is the Damaris the Greek calendar keeps the same day is not said on either page, and her Greek entry does not call her his wife.
