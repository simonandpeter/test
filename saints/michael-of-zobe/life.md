# Michael of Zobe, with thirty-six venerable martyrs

The Greek synaxarion keeps Michael, abbot of the monastery of Zobe, with thirty-six venerable martyrs. They lived in the years of the emperor Constantine VI and his mother Irene, about 780, at the monastery of Zobe near Sebastopolis. When the emir of the Hagarenes, named Aleim, was making war on that country, he seized the fathers and urged them to deny the faith of Christ; the abbot Michael rebuked him sharply and encouraged his monks to bear death bravely for Christ, and all of them were beheaded. The synaxarion names none of the thirty-six. Its verses count thirty disciples beheaded with Michael and six fellow monks besides, who reddened their mantles in the contest of the sword.

The Russian church calendar keeps the abbot and his company on the Julian 1 October and counts them exactly as the Greek does: «Прмч. Михаила, игумена Зовийского, и с ним 36-ти преподобномучеников» — thirty-six venerable martyrs with him. Where saint.gr gives the one year 780 it gives a decade, «(780-790)», and the decade is now the death interval here, because it contains the year rather than contradicting it. Its index of names adds a second form of the place, «Михаил Зовийский (Севастийский)».
