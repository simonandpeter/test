# Nicholas (Smirnov)

Nicholas Petrovich Smirnov was born on 6 May 1886 in the village of Staraya Kashira in the Kolomna district of the Moscow province, the son of a peasant named Peter Smirnov. He finished school and worked as a village teacher until 1922. That year he moved to Kaluga, and until his arrest in 1937 he was psalmist in a city church and sang in its choir.

His interrogation is printed and it is short. Told that he was arrested for counter-revolutionary activity and asked to give evidence, he said he had carried on none. Told that he was lying and that the investigation knew for certain he had been hostile to the Soviet order and had agitated against it, he said he had not and did not admit the charges. Told a third time to stop denying, and reminded that a frank repentance would be taken into account by the Soviet power in the twentieth year of the revolution, he said he did not admit the charges and had nothing to add.

Signing the record of the interrogation, he wrote at the foot of the page that he had read it, that he did not — and here he underlined the words — admit himself guilty, and that he held himself, in accordance with his convictions, incapable of such things. On 19 November 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced him to ten years in a corrective labour camp, and he died soon after reaching it. No day, no month, no year and no camp is named, and the day's line closes the entry with the decade alone.

He was glorified among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia in August 2000 by the jubilee council of bishops. The city church he served is named in the page for a saint this corpus holds, and the dedication is deliberately not in this life: a dedication is not a relation.
