# Eugraphus

doxologia.ro prints one life for the three martyrs of 10 decembrie, and this is it. Diocletian and Maximian had laid down the sceptre, and the persecution went on in the East under Maximinus, a hot partisan of his fathers' many gods. Word came to him that Alexandria was in an uproar, the Christians there having grown very many, and since he had to stay at Byzantium he sent one of his officers to quiet the city, put down the Christian name and bring back to the old worship those who had left it. The man he sent was [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian), an Athenian by birth, a good rhetor and skilled in Greek learning — and secretly a Christian, who had hidden his faith until the call should come.

He reached Alexandria and quieted the trouble without labour, making peace between Christians and pagans and ordering that each should keep his own faith unhindered; then, having set the city's affairs in order and written to the emperor, he decided to bring his own faith into the open. If I give myself to the torments another time, he said in himself, I alone am crowned; now I can bring others to the crown with me. So he praised the name of Jesus Christ openly and taught the faith, and healed the lame, the blind, the dumb, the deaf and the possessed in the middle of the city by calling on the Name and making the sign of the Cross, and many believed. Those who loved the old feasts sent secretly to the emperor to say what he was doing.

The emperor, in anger, sent the eparch of the city, [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-alexandria) — also an Athenian, brought up in the worship of the idols but of a kind and merciful disposition — to set right what had been overturned and to bring the officer back to the gods. He sat in judgement instead, and [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian) answered him out of the Scriptures, comparing the Christian writings with the Greek learning; and the people listened. When the crowd grew bold the judge had him bound and tortured, and had his tongue cut out, and then his eyes put out, and threw him back into the prison, saying he would give his body to the birds in the morning.

At the third hour of the night a light like lightning shone in the prison, and Christ stood by him, healed his tongue, gave him back his eyes, made him whole, and told him that the judge who warred against him would be humbled before him by morning and would afterwards be his companion in the struggle, because it was not right that so many good works should perish for want of knowledge.

So it came about. The judge, seeing him whole in the middle of the theatre, was struck with fear and then with belief, and would have fallen at his feet; thirteen bishops gathered from the country round and out of the desert and baptised him before the people with three immersions, and a great multitude was baptised with him. Within a few days [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-alexandria) was chosen bishop of Alexandria, gave all he had to the poor, pulled down the pagan temples and built churches in their place, and brought a countless number of Greeks to Christ. A hardened Greek named Rusticus carried the news to the emperor, who came himself to Alexandria in a fury.

He tormented them both, and both were preserved; and when they appeared alive and whole before him in the theatre the whole people cried out with one voice that the God of the Christians is truly great. Then Eugraphus, a man of Greek learning who had been one of the notaries under [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian) when he was judge of the city, was filled with zeal at the sight, signed himself with the Cross, stepped out into the middle and told the emperor to his face that he too was a Christian, that dying for Christ was life, and that they laughed at him as at an old fox. The emperor snatched a sword from one of the men standing by him and cut him down with his own hand, hacking him to pieces; and he gave thanks as he was cut that he went to God before the others and by many wounds rather than one.

Then, since many were touching the other two with their hands and finding their bodies whole and their wounds healed, the emperor gave up and ordered their heads struck off. They prayed at the place of execution for peace to the churches and that no one who asked their help should go away empty, gave one another the kiss of peace and stretched out their necks. [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian), while still alive, had asked that his body be carried to Byzantium.

The emperor had an iron coffin made, put the bodies in it and threw it into the sea so that Christians should not honour them; but the iron did not sink, and went to Byzantium ahead of him. The bishop of Constantinople, warned in a vision, came down to the shore by night with his clergy and saw a pillar of light and two shining men drawing the coffin to land, and buried the relics by the city wall, to be a guard to those who sail and physicians to the sick. Maximinus went blind on his road, said he had been beaten by unseen hands, and died a few days after.

Of the three, Eugraphus is the notary who had served under Menas in the city's court, who stepped out of the crowd in the theatre when he saw the two martyrs alive, told the emperor that he too was a Christian, and was cut to pieces by the emperor's own hand. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mucenic Evgraf» and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 December, with the two he suffered beside, and tells what he was. Eugraphus was the secretary of [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian), saint.gr says, the Athenian whom the emperor had made eparch of Alexandria without knowing he was a Christian, and who refused to carry out the persecution there.

When the new eparch [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-alexandria) had tortured Menas and shut him in prison to die of his wounds, and had then found him alive and his wounds healed, he asked him publicly how it had happened; and Menas answered that he was healed as he lay on the ground chanting the psalm, though I walk in the midst of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me, Lord. That answer, the page says, made Christians of Hermogenes and of Eugraphus; and afterwards all of them together were beheaded.

His own couplet plays on the writing in his name: bearing the wounds of the knife, Eugraphus, you were seen to be a sharp pen of God, writing well. The page's note of relics stands under his heading and names an incorrupt tongue at the monastery of Leimonos on Lesbos and portions at Docheiariou and in the Benaki Museum, without saying which of the three saints they belong to.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the three of them on the same menologion day under one heading, and it gives him the one scene this folder had never carried. He was the secretary and the friend of Saint [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian). When the emperor Maximinus came to Alexandria and put Menas and [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-alexandria) to great torments, and when Eugraphus saw the courage of these soldiers of Christ and the wonders of God upon them, he came forward before the tribunal of his own accord — he was not on trial — and shouted in the emperor's face: I too am a Christian.

The emperor raged, drew his own sword and cut him down, before any executioner was called and before the other two were beheaded with the sword. The book gives him no year, no birthplace and no age, and its emperor it names without dating.
