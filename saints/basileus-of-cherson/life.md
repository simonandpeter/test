# Basileus of Cherson

In the sixteenth year of the reign of Diocletian the patriarch of Jerusalem sent out many bishops into different countries to preach Christ after the manner of the apostles. Two of them, [Ephraim](/saints/ephraim-of-tomis) and Basileus, went into the country of the Tauroscythians and came out at the city of Cherson, where both laboured, preaching the true God to a godless people. Ephraim then left the people of Cherson in Basileus's care and went off to the Scythians who lived along the Danube.

Basileus, seeing the error of the unbelieving people and showing them the straight way of salvation, provoked them to anger; the pagans seized him, beat him without mercy and drove him out of the city. He went up into a mountain about a hundred stadia from Cherson, called Parthenon — that is, the maidens' mountain, because there had been on it a temple of a Greek goddess who was a virgin — and lived in a cave there, rejoicing that he had been counted worthy to suffer wounds and banishment for Christ, and weeping and praying for the souls that were being lost.

Not long after, the only son of a Cherson nobleman died and was buried outside the city, and his parents sat by the grave in grief. At night the dead boy appeared to them in a dream and told them that their gods could not raise him, being lifeless idols; but if they wished to have him alive again they should beg that stranger whom they had beaten and driven out to pray for him to his God, and should themselves believe in that God. Waking and finding they had seen the same thing, they ran into the city and told their neighbours.

At daybreak they searched everywhere for the man of God and found him in the cave. The nobleman and his household fell at his feet and begged him to raise their son; he refused, calling himself a sinful man, but told them that if they would believe in God it could be done. The dead man rose at once and spoke, glorifying God; great fear came on all who were there, and unspeakable joy on the parents, who fell at the bishop's feet and called him great and his God the true God.

At this the devil entered the hearts of the Jews living in Cherson, who stirred up the Greeks against the Christians, and above all against Basileus as their chief, to kill him; and he was killed. After the murder of the bishop Basileus, one of his disciples, putting in for the night on the coast of the Hellespont, found three bishops labouring there at the good news of Christ — [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-cherson), [Elpidius](/saints/elpidius-of-cherson) and [Agathodorus](/saints/agathodorus-of-cherson) — and they came to Cherson in his place.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives him the fullest part of the company's story. saint.gr says that in the reign of Diocletian the patriarch of Jerusalem Hermon sent him as bishop to Cherson to spread the Christian faith, and that the idolaters drove him out for preaching Christ. He was called back by the ruler of the place and, on the Synaxarist's authority, raised the ruler's son; at that wonder the archon and a crowd of people believed and were baptised. The idolaters were enraged, seized him, bound him and dragged him by the feet, and as he was dragged he died. The page's distich has it that Basileus, dragged by the hands of the superstitious, tears apart the hands of superstitious error.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day among the seven bishops of Cherson, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the seven of them one entry. All of them were bishops in Cherson at different times, it says, and all of them suffered at the hands of unbelievers — whether of Jews, or of Greeks, or of Scythians — except Aetherius alone, who died in peace. All of them went out into those wild parts as missionaries, sent by the patriarch of Jerusalem to carry the light of the Gospel; all were tormented, and all suffered for the sake of their Lord.

He is the one the entry tells first. Basileus raised the son of a prince in Cherson, which embittered the Jews, and they laid an accusation against him for it; he was bound by the feet and dragged through the streets of the city until he gave up his soul.

The Russian church keeps all seven of them on this day, on one line — «Священномучеников, в Херсонесе епископствовавших: Василия , Ефрема , Капитона , Евгения , Еферия , Елпидия и Агафодора (IV).» — and its life is the fullest account of the company any of the four calendars read has given. They carried the gospel, it says, into the lands of the northern Black Sea from the Danube to the Dnieper, the Crimea among them, and sealed their apostolate with a martyr's death in the fourth century at Херсонес Таврический. The page sets them in a history: the faith had reached the Crimea, anciently Таврида and subject to the Roman emperors, long before the baptism of Владимир; its beginning there was the preaching of the apostle Андрей Первозванный; and its spread was helped against their will by its own enemies, since the emperors used the place for state criminals, which in the first three centuries meant confessors of Christ — «в правление Траяна (98–117) был сослан на заточение в Инкерманские каменоломни святой Климент, епископ Римский», who went on preaching in the Inkerman quarries and was martyred there. The pagans of the Crimea resisted hard and many preachers died in the struggle.

The episcopal see at Херсонес was founded at the beginning of the fourth century, in a turning time when the city, a base for Roman soldiers, was passing into dependence on Byzantium. In the reign of Diocletian, which the page gives as 284 to 305, and in the year 300 — «то есть еще до начала гонения, которое император начал с 303 года», before the persecution the emperor began in 303 — the patriarch Ермон of Jerusalem, whose years the same sentence gives as 303 to 313, sent many bishops out into various countries for the preaching of the gospel. **The two numbers do not agree and nothing is done about it here**: a patriarch whose reign the page dates from 303 cannot have sent anybody in 300, and both figures are the page's own.

His is the fullest of the seven lives inside the one life, and **the Russian page dates his death to the day**. He came to Херсонес in 300 with Ефрем, sent by the patriarch of Jerusalem, and the two planted the word of God there; when Ефрем went on to the peoples of the Danube, Василий stayed on as his fellow labourer and set many idolaters on the way of truth. Others of the city, hardened by what he was doing, resisted furiously: he was seized, beaten without mercy and driven out. He withdrew into the mountains and lived in a cave, praying without ceasing for the men who had expelled him, that God would light them with the knowledge of himself — and God sent the unbelievers a miracle. The only son of a notable of Херсонес died, and appeared to his parents in a dream to tell them that a certain holy Василий could raise him from the dead by his prayers to the true God. When they found the bishop and asked for the miracle he answered that he was himself a sinful man with no power to raise the dead, but that God is almighty and could grant what they asked if they would believe in him; he prayed a long while calling on the name of the Holy Trinity, blessed water and poured it on the dead man, and the dead man lived. He was brought back into the city with honour and many believed and were baptised. Then, by the order of the emperor Максимиан Галерий, whose years the page gives as 305 to 311, the persecution broke out again with new force, and his enemies armed themselves against him: «7 марта 309 года они ночью ворвались в его дом, связали его, поволокли по улицам и забили до смерти камнями и палками» — on 7 March 309 they broke into his house by night, bound him, dragged him through the streets and beat him to death with stones and clubs. His body was thrown outside the city for the dogs and the birds and lay many days unburied and untouched, and the Christians afterwards buried it secretly in a cave.

The preaching of the hieromartyrs, the page ends, established the faith at Херсонес: from the beginning of the fifth century the city was a spiritual centre from which Christianity spread north towards Rus, from the ninth the Russians were settling in it, modern excavation has found more than fifty churches there of the fifth to fourteenth centuries, and in 987 Владимир was baptised in it. «Этот древний город явился колыбелью христианства для Руси» — this ancient city was the cradle of Christianity for Rus.
