# Benjamin the Deacon

doxologia.ro prints one notice for Benjamin the deacon and for the bishop [Abda](/saints/abda-bishop-of-persia), and it begins with the bishop. In the reign of Theodosius the Younger, Yazdegerd, emperor of the Persians, made war on the churches, and took his occasion from this: a certain bishop, Abda, adorned with many kinds of virtue and driven by divine zeal, destroyed the Pyreion — that is, the temple in which the Persians worshipped fire.

Learning of it from the magi, the emperor had Abda brought before him, asked him first gently why he had done it, and ordered him to build that temple again. When Abda resisted and said he could by no means do such a thing, Yazdegerd threatened to destroy all the churches, and began his threats by ordering the divine Abda killed first; and hearing it the saint rejoiced greatly, and so, rejoicing and glad, received his end.

The holy martyrs Abda the bishop, Benjamin the deacon, nine martyrs with them and many other saints who confessed in Persia were thrown into prison, and were eaten alive by the mice and the cats shut in with them. Thirty years later the storm of persecution loosed by the magi, like strong winds, brought the trials of torment on the faithful again: many were consumed with every kind of suffering and then put to death, and others, made to bear long exiles and every kind of torture, were released from life by deaths of every kind.

We should not wonder at it, says the notice, for these things happened by the permission of God, and our Master said that struggles of this kind would come; and for this these saints received, by their patience and their martyrdom, the crowns of victory. The Romanian calendar gives them no year, and no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and sets him thirty years apart from the bishop he is commemorated with. saint.gr puts the whole company in the years of Theodosius the Younger, emperor of the Romans from 408 to 450, and of Yazdegerd, king of the Persians from 399 to 420; in the year 412 Yazdegerd raised a hard persecution, because the bishop [Abda](/saints/abda-bishop-of-persia) had in holy indignation pulled down the temple in which the Persians worshipped fire and would not rebuild it, and the king then pulled down all the churches of the Christians and put Abda to death with [nine other leading Christians](/saints/nine-martyrs-with-abda). After thirty years, the page continues, a new persecution was raised against the Christians, in which many saints were sacrificed on the altar of the true faith — among them Hormisdas, of noble birth, and Benjamin the deacon, the great martyr. The Church, it says, appointed an honourable memory of all who contended and were sacrificed in that persecution together with the memory of the bishop Abda, to show that heroes of the faith known and unknown to men have a common honour in heaven and will enjoy in common the crowns of their great struggles. His own couplet is grim and precise: «Ἀθλητικῷ κλυστῆρι, τῷ πάλῳ λέγω, Πᾶν Βενιαμὶν ψυχικὸν κενοῖ βάρος» — with a contender's clyster, the stake I mean, Benjamin empties out every burden of the soul.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 13 October as well as on 31 March, and his own index page names both days and links the life of Abdas, bishop of Persia, and Benjamin the deacon, which is how the two readings are shown to be of one man. Abdas was brought before the Persian king Yazdegerd — whose reign that page dates 401 to 402 — for pulling down a temple of the fire-worshippers, and was ordered to rebuild it; when he refused, the king had all the Christian churches destroyed and the Christians hunted and given over to torture. Abdas was the first to die, beheaded after long torments.

Thirty days later the rest were executed, the deacon Benjamin among them, and his torments were the worst of them: sharp spikes were driven under his nails and he was set on a stake. The page closes by putting the death at Susa, the ancient Persian city, «в 418 или 424 гг.» — and the day line for 13 October prints «(421-424)», while saint.gr gives 442. The shorter of the Russian lives says only that he turned a great many pagan Persians to Christianity and suffered in Persia in the fifth century for his zeal in preaching the Gospel.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, inside the entry headed for his bishop [Abda](/saints/abda-bishop-of-persia), whom it calls bishop of the city of Susa and has beheaded for Christ in Persia in 418 by the king Izdegerd. Of Benjamin it says that his tormentors let him go on the condition that he preach the Gospel no more; that he agreed to the condition at first, but could not hold his heart to it, and went on spreading the truth of Christ among the people; and that for this he was seized and killed three years after Saint Abda, in the year 421. That is a fourth reading of his year beside the three this folder already carries, and it falls inside the interval between 418 and 442 and is narrower than it, so the interval was not narrowed and stands as it was. What is worth saying of it is that this page's 421 is counted three years from its own 418 for the bishop, so the two years here are one reckoning and not two independent ones.
