# Nicodemus of Serbia

The venerable Nicodemus, Archbishop of Serbia, was abbot of the holy monastery of Chilandar on the Holy Mountain, saint.gr says, and he was consecrated bishop in the year 1316. In 1319 he translated into the Slavonic tongue the Typikon of [Sabbas the Sanctified](/saints/sabbas-the-sanctified) of Jerusalem, and he required that it be used without fail in the Church of Serbia. Nicodemus fell asleep in peace in the year 1325.

That is the whole of the page's account. It gives no year and no place for his birth, no family, no account of his tonsure or of how long he was abbot, no writings of his own besides the translation, and no manner of repose; it says nothing of his relations with the Serbian crown. It does say where he lies: the relics of the saint are at the Patriarchate of Peć in Kosovo. It prints neither apolytikion nor kontakion for him, and adds that the name Νικόδημος is νίκη and δῆμος together, the people's victor.

The Ohrid Prologue, read for the Julian 11 May, is the Serbian calendar this folder's own notice said would be the one to settle his day and his rank, and it keeps him on the same day saint.gr does. This great hierarch, it says, was a Serb by birth. He struggled on the Holy Mountain and was abbot of Hilandar. After the death of Sava III he was chosen archbishop of all the Serbian and the maritime land in the year 1317 — a year later than the 1316 the Greek page gives for his consecration, and neither reading is chosen here. In 1321 he crowned King [Stephen Milutin](/saints/stephen-milutin).

He translated the Jerusalem Typikon into Serbian, which is the Typikon the Greek page names as Sabbas the Sanctified's, and in the preface to that book he wrote: the Almighty God, who knows our weakness, will give us strength of spirit — but only if we show the labour first. He loved the ascetic life sincerely, and laboured at rooting out the Bogomil heresy and at establishing the Orthodox faith. He reposed in the Lord in 1325, which is the year the Greek page gives too, and his wonderworking relics rest in the monastery at Peć.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 11 мая ст. ст. and gives a short life of four sentences. He was abbot of the Hilandar monastery and was raised to the rank of a hierarch in 1316. He is best known, the life says, for what he did in 1319: he translated into the Slavonic tongue and brought into use in Serbia the Typikon, the Rule, of Савва Освященный of Jerusalem. Святитель Никодим fell asleep in 1325. The calendar lists one other day for him, 30 августа, and marks that one as the Serbian.
