# Alexander of Thessalonica the Dervish

Alexander came from Thessalonica and lived in the district of Laodigia, or Lagodiani, which the page says is the modern Laodigitria. Young, he came to Smyrna trying to escape the lewd designs of a Turk; and there, for reasons unknown to us — the page's own words — he renounced his faith and became a Muslim. Afterwards he visited Mecca and entered the order of the dervishes.

But his conscience convicted him. He feigned madness, and under the habit of a dervish went about the subject countries for eighteen years, rebuking the Turkish tyranny most bitterly; and as often as he had a chance to show his faith in Christ he did so by doing good works. Being on Chios, he attended the services of Great Lent fearlessly in a Christian church, still wearing his dervish's clothes.

From Chios he went to Smyrna and presented himself to the judge of the city, confessing with courage that he repented of the state he had been in and was coming back to Christianity; and in front of him he took off the dervish's clothes and trampled them. He was thrown into prison at once, and brought out of it three times, with flatteries and with threats, to bend his resolve and his faith; but Alexander stayed unbroken and firm in his decision to be martyred for his holy faith.

When he came to the place of execution he prayed on his knees, unshaken, before a numerous and raging crowd of Turks, Greeks, Franks and Armenians. Full of joy and courage, he bore his martyr's death by beheading with endurance, on 26 May 1794, and so became, saint.gr says, a citizen of the Jerusalem above and a sharer in the heavenly glory.
