# Auxentius of Bithynia

In the reign of Theodosius the Younger there was in Constantinople an honoured man, famous in the order of soldiers and in the imperial palaces, named Auxentius, the son of Adda the Persian, skilled in the divine Scripture and in the outward wisdom and adorned with virtues. He came to know the venerable Marcian, who was afterwards steward of the Great Church, and John the monk, who lived beside the church of Saint John the Forerunner before the city at the place called Hebdomon, and other God-fearing men with them, and was moved to emulate their life.

Leaving the vanity of this world, its glory and its noise, he became a soldier of the heavenly emperor Christ; and taking the monastic habit he served the Lord first in the order of the diaconate and then in the priesthood, and received power over the devil to drive him out of men, so that he was famous in the imperial city. Fleeing human glory and desiring a quieter life, he left the crowded city and went into Bithynia, and from there into a desert mountain called Oxia, about ten stadia from Chalcedon.

Loving that place, he climbed a hill and stood on a rock, raised his holy hands and praised God, saying: «Thou, Lord, hast made me dwell apart in hope.» He lived on that mountain, which was afterwards called by his own name, Auxentius — as it is written in the life of the venerable Stephen, who lived there later as a hermit and suffered for the holy icons. Shepherds looking for a strayed flock found him, and found their sheep by his prayers; from that time he became known, and many began to come, some for the healing of the body, for he cured every sickness by his prayers, and others for the profit of their souls.

Devout people from the villages round about gathered and built him a cell on the summit of the mountain, in which he shut himself up, speaking through a little window with those who came to him and guiding them. Between that and his end the page prints a long account which is not set down here, because it was not read; what follows is its close.

One day he opened the little window without warning and cried aloud three times, «Blessed be the Lord God!»; then he sighed heavily and bowed his head to the ground, and none of those standing there dared ask him anything. He said to them: «The light that was in the east, O my sons — Symeon our father — has fallen asleep.» And weeping much he said again: «Our holy father, the pillar and ground of the truth, [Symeon the Stylite](/saints/symeon-the-stylite), has departed, and his innocent and pure soul did not disdain to bow to me, the unworthy and the sinful.»

Those who heard were astonished at such a sight, and they noted the hour in which he said it. Some time afterwards word came from the right-believing emperor Leo, who had taken the empire after Marcian, of the death of Symeon the Stylite, and the news went everywhere at once; and the disciples of Auxentius, examining the time, found it true, that Symeon had died in the very hour when Auxentius spoke of his falling asleep.

Not long after Symeon's end the blessed end of Auxentius drew near, for he had come to deep old age. Having lived in holiness and in right belief, founded many monasteries in various places, been abbot throughout the parts of Bithynia and guided many to salvation, he passed over to the Lord. The page gives no year for his birth or his death.
