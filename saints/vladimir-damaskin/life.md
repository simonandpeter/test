# Vladimir (Damaskin)

He was born on 27 February 1870 in the town of Torzhok in the Tver province, a son of the psalmist Nicholas Damaskin, whose last years were served in a village of the same district and who died in 1914. Soon after his own ordination he was sent to the church of the village of Ostrovno in the Udomlya district, and he served there for the greater part of his life. In 1930 the local authorities arrested him, held him two and a half months, and let him go, and he went back to his own church.

In August 1937 the decision was taken to arrest and shoot everyone the state held hostile to it, and the NKVD required reports from the local authorities, the chairmen of the village soviets above all, on the people living in their villages. So the denunciations came: that he was collecting the signatures of the faithful in the hope of saving his church from closure, and that going about the villages he said the collective farms were bondage for the peasants and would starve those in them.

Questioned, he denied all of it. He did not read sermons, he said, and had never carried on anti-Soviet agitation in church; he had never told collective farmers that Soviet power would soon fall; he did not admit that he had called the people to overthrow it. The same day the investigator drew up an indictment repeating every one of the false statements, resolved himself to send the case to the troika, and himself concurred in that resolution.

A few days later, on 27 December, the troika of the NKVD resolved to shoot him, and he was shot on 29 December 1937. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
