# Gerasimos of Little St Anne's

Gerasimos of Little St Anne's, Anastasie by his baptismal name, was born on 5 September 1904 in the village of Droviani in Northern Epirus, and went to the village school there. After a time he came to Athens and gave himself to the study of the Fathers of the Church. Drawn early to the monastic life, in 1923 he went to the Holy Mountain and settled at the skete of Saint Anne — at Little Saint Anne's, in the cell of the Honourable Forerunner.

On 20 October 1924, at the vigil of Saint Gerasimos of Kefalonia, he was tonsured a monk and took the name of the saint being kept that day. From the elder Meletios he learned the craft of carving the seals for the prosphora, and worked at it with much zeal; and when Meletios left for Athens for good in 1928 the young monk was left entirely alone. He served over the years as librarian and as typikarist at the kyriakon of the skete of Saint Anne, and in 1956, in the cave where [Dionysius the Rhetor](/saints/dionysius-the-rhetor) and [Mitrophan](/saints/mitrophan-disciple-of-dionysius) had lived their ascetic life, he built a small chapel, consecrated in 1960.

He received from God the gift of a hymnographer, and was known too for his love of strangers, a virtue he breathed into his disciples; besides lay people, clergy and monks came often to hear the elder, to profit spiritually and to learn from the example of his life. In the Greek Orthodox Church a great part of the hymns he composed have been taken into the service books, and some of his akathists and little paraklesis services have been translated into Romanian as well. He went to the Lord in 1991 and was buried near the cave of Saints Dionysius and Mitrophan. On Tuesday 10 January 2023 the Holy Synod of the Ecumenical Patriarchate numbered him among the saints.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 7 December, and gives his secular name and the shape of his working life. saint.gr has him born at Droviani of Northern Epirus — in 1903, it says, and not 1905 as the archives wrongly have it — and christened Anastasios, or Athanasios, Grekas. From his father John he took his severity towards himself, and from his mother Athena a deep, guileless and unfeigned reverence. He got his schooling at Piraeus and at Athens, where he came to know Saint Nektarios of Pentapolis, and on 15 August 1923 he left Piraeus by ship for the Holy Mountain.

He went to the hut of the Honourable Forerunner in the skete of Little Saint Anne's, under the elder Meletios Ioannidis, and after a year's good probation was tonsured a monk on 20 October 1924, during the vigil for the memory of Saint Gerasimos of Kephallonia. Five years later his elder left for the world and he stayed alone in the desert; prayer and study were his only comfort, and after twenty years he gathered a small brotherhood about him. Silence he called the mother of the wisest thoughts.

But it was in hymnography that he became known everywhere. He began about 1926, he says, with a canon to the Mother of God, which he carried to the enclosed Kallinikos at Katounakia for judgement; and he wrote always after close study and much prayer — everything I do, he said, I owe to prayer. His hymnographic work is reckoned at more than two thousand services.

He had asked the Mother of God to keep his reason to the end so as to tire nobody, and he was clear to his last breath: he said three times, Saint Nektarios, help me, and breathed out. A sweetness was painted on his face, the page says, as though he were sleeping quietly. On 10 January 2023 the Holy Synod of the Ecumenical Patriarchate numbered him among the saints of the Orthodox Church.
