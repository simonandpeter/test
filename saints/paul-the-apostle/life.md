# Paul

Doxologia keeps Paul on the same day as [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle) and tells the two lives together on one long page. What is written here follows what that page says of Paul's last journeys and of his death; the middle of it, his conversion and his labours through the east, is longer than this reading and is left to the page itself.

Preaching in the west, he brought a woman named Xanthippe to confess that Christ is the true God and to ask for holy baptism; she was baptized first, then her husband Probus and all their household, and after them Philotheus, the chief man of the city, and many others. He went through all those parts of the west and gave them light by the light of the holy faith.

Foreseeing his own martyr's end, he turned back to Rome and wrote to his disciple [Timothy](/saints/timothy-of-ephesus): I am now being offered up, and the time of my departure is come; I have striven the good strife, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith; from now the crown of righteousness is prepared for me, which the Lord will give me in that day.

The church historians do not all write the same thing about when he suffered. Nicephorus Callistus, in the second book of his history, says that Paul suffered on account of Simon the sorcerer in the same year and on the same day as Peter, having helped Peter to overcome him. Others say that Paul suffered a year after Peter's death, in June, on the twenty-ninth day, the same day on which Peter had been crucified a year before.

The life holds that the difference is not great, since Paul lived many years in Rome and in the western parts round about at the same time as Peter, and both things could have happened. Provoked by the two of them, Nero sought them out and condemned them to death: Peter, as a foreigner, to the cross, and Paul, as a Roman citizen whom it was not fitting to kill by a dishonourable death, to beheading with the sword. When his head was struck off, blood ran from the wound with milk; and the faithful took his body and laid it with Peter's.

So ended the chosen vessel of Christ, the teacher of the nations, the preacher to the whole world, who bore the wounds of the Lord on his body. The life gives no year of his death, and none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps him with [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle) on the same 29 June, and saint.gr's shared page adds some particulars of his origins and his reach. He was born at Tarsus in Cilicia, in a place the page calls Gischala, and was at first a hard persecutor of the Christians, until about the year 36, on his way to Damascus to drive out the Christians there too, Christ appeared to him and sent him to Ananias, who catechised and baptised him. The page names him the apostle of the nations for his four great missionary journeys, calls him the founder of the Church of Greece, and counts fourteen epistles to the churches he had planted. It says he was beheaded at Rome, and adds that this too is not fully corroborated.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps him with Peter on the same day and gives him an entry of his own. He was of Tarsus by birth, it says, and of the tribe of Benjamin. He was called Saul at first, studied under [Gamaliel the Teacher of the Law](/saints/gamaliel-the-teacher-of-the-law), and was a Pharisee and a persecutor of Christianity until he was converted by a wonder, by the Lord Himself, who appeared to him on the road to Damascus; he was baptised by the apostle [Ananias](/saints/ananias-the-apostle), renamed Paul, and numbered in the service of the great apostles.

With a flaming zeal he preached the Gospel everywhere from the borders of Arabia to Spain, among the Jews and among the gentiles, and got the name of the apostle of the gentiles for it. His sufferings were as terrible as his patience was more than human: through all the years of his preaching, the book says, he hung day after day as though by one weak thread between life and death. Having filled all his days and nights with labour and suffering for Christ, having organised the Church in a great many places, and having reached that measure of perfection in which he could say that he no longer lived himself but Christ lived in him, he was beheaded at Rome in the time of the emperor Nero, at the same time as the apostle Peter.

The entry gives no year for anything, and names a reach the Greek page does not: from the borders of Arabia to Spain, where that one counted four journeys and made him the founder of the Church of Greece. Both readings stand here and neither is reconciled with the other.

The Russian calendar keeps the two apostles together on 29 июня ст. ст., the same menologion day as the other three calendars here, and its line is the joint feast: «Славных и всехвальных первоверховных апостолов Петра и Павла (67).» — of the glorious and all-praised chief apostles, with the year 67, which is the only year any source of this folder has given and is recorded here as traditional.

The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion of the two together, titled simply «Тропарь апостолов» and «Кондак апостолов», and both are written on both folders. They are the Slavonic of the same two hymns this folder already holds in Greek and in Romanian. The calendar's index of readings for each apostle was not opened: the life above is written out of the other sources and this paragraph records only what the day page itself prints.
