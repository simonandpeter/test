# Paul

Doxologia keeps Paul on the same day as [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle) and tells the two lives together on one long page. What is written here follows what that page says of Paul's last journeys and of his death; the middle of it, his conversion and his labours through the east, is longer than this reading and is left to the page itself.

Preaching in the west, he brought a woman named Xanthippe to confess that Christ is the true God and to ask for holy baptism; she was baptized first, then her husband Probus and all their household, and after them Philotheus, the chief man of the city, and many others. He went through all those parts of the west and gave them light by the light of the holy faith.

Foreseeing his own martyr's end, he turned back to Rome and wrote to his disciple [Timothy](/saints/timothy-of-ephesus): I am now being offered up, and the time of my departure is come; I have striven the good strife, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith; from now the crown of righteousness is prepared for me, which the Lord will give me in that day.

The church historians do not all write the same thing about when he suffered. Nicephorus Callistus, in the second book of his history, says that Paul suffered on account of Simon the sorcerer in the same year and on the same day as Peter, having helped Peter to overcome him. Others say that Paul suffered a year after Peter's death, in June, on the twenty-ninth day, the same day on which Peter had been crucified a year before.

The life holds that the difference is not great, since Paul lived many years in Rome and in the western parts round about at the same time as Peter, and both things could have happened. Provoked by the two of them, Nero sought them out and condemned them to death: Peter, as a foreigner, to the cross, and Paul, as a Roman citizen whom it was not fitting to kill by a dishonourable death, to beheading with the sword. When his head was struck off, blood ran from the wound with milk; and the faithful took his body and laid it with Peter's.

So ended the chosen vessel of Christ, the teacher of the nations, the preacher to the whole world, who bore the wounds of the Lord on his body. The life gives no year of his death, and none is recorded here.
