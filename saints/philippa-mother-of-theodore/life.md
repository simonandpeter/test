# Philippa, mother of Theodore of Perga

Philippa is named on saint.gr's page as the mother of [Theodore of Perga](/saints/theodore-of-perga), and one sentence is all the page has of her: «Η Αγία Φιλίππα, μητέρα του Αγίου Θεοδώρου, μετά τον σταυρικό θάνατο του υιού της, ομολόγησε τον Χριστό ως Θεό αληθινό και μαρτύρησε διά ξίφους» — after her son's death upon the cross she confessed Christ as true God, and was martyred by the sword.

The couplet the page sets over her name is the only other thing it says of her: I love Philippa as an athlete's mother, I love Philippa as one contending by the sword. She is given no city of her own beyond her son's Perge, no year, no judge by name, and no hymn.

What the page tells at length is her son's passion, in the years of the emperor Antoninus and of a governor called Theodotus. Theodore of Perge in Pamphylia destroyed idols and temples; he was stretched on a red-hot gridiron, dragged behind wild horses, cast into a furnace with the soldiers [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-socrates), imprisoned, and at last crucified and shot with arrows, giving up his soul after three days upon the cross. [Dioscorus](/saints/dioscorus-19-april), a priest of the idols, was brought to the faith by his wonders and burned.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the young martyr alone — „Св. муч. Теодор.“ — and then opens its body with „И други с њим.“, *and others with him*, before naming the rest. [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-perga) suffered for the faith of Christ at Perga in Pamphylia in the time of the emperor Antoninus. He was young and fair of face; and when the governor of that region chose him with other youths to be sent for service at the imperial court, he refused and declared that he was a Christian. For that he was tortured with various torments and then thrown on a fire — but water welled up out of the earth and quenched it.

The governor put that down to some magic of Theodore's, and the martyr answered him: this is not the work of my power but of Christ my God; and if you wish to know the power of your gods, lay another fire and throw in one of your own soldiers, and you will know their power and the omnipotence of my God. The governor meant to do it; but the soldiers begged him in fear to throw in the priest [Dioscorus](/saints/dioscorus-19-april) instead of them, and Dioscorus begged him to throw in the idol of Zeus and the rest of the idols, for if they are gods they will easily save themselves — which he said because his heart was already turned to Christ, having seen the wonder of the fire.

Learning this, the governor condemned Dioscorus to death and burned him; and he gave Theodore to death too, with the two soldiers [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-socrates), and with Philippa, Theodore's mother. Theodore they stretched on a cross, on which he gave up his spirit only on the third day; Socrates and Dionysius were run through with a spear, and Philippa was beheaded with the sword. All of them, the entry ends, were crowned with the crowns of glory in the kingdom of Christ.

What the entry adds to this folder is that Philippa was given to death with her son and the two soldiers, and that she was beheaded with the sword. It says nothing else of her — no city of her own, no age, no word about her faith before his — and it is the mother's name that the Prologue's verse for the day returns to. The entry gives no year for this company, only „у време цара Антонина“, in the time of the emperor Antoninus. More than one Roman emperor bore that name, so no interval was read out of the reign and no date was added here; this folder stays undated, as it was.
