# Leontius of Rădăuți

Leontius was born of right-believing Christian parents in the town of Rădăuți at the beginning of the fourteenth century. From his infancy, going with his parents to church, he was taken with a great love for the house of God; afterwards he went there daily and listened to the whole service with all attention, seeking to come close in soul to the clergy, to the venerable fathers and to the labourers of that princely church.

The fathers of the cathedral's brotherhood, seeing so much love in him for the house of God and for the holy services, received him among themselves, where in everything he showed zeal and humility. For his spiritual life he was tonsured a monk and given the name Lavrentie, and his zeal for the labours of the spirit grew greater still.

Taking a blessing from the elder of the brotherhood, he went off to a hermits' place not far from Putna, where a few solitaries lived. The metropolitan of Moldavia of that time, Joseph Mușat, hearing of that hermitage and of its very beautiful order, came and consecrated the church of the skete, and at the same time ordained the venerable Lavrentie priest and named him abbot, as that brotherhood wished. Into that community came also the venerable Daniel the Hermit, to struggle under the guidance of his spiritual father Lavrentie.

When the episcopal see of Rădăuți was founded in the days of Alexander the Good, not long after the founding of the bishopric Lavrentie was called to that high office, and out of obedience he received the grace of the episcopate and left the place of his hermit's labours. As a bishop he did not for a moment give up the labours of the spirit. He ordained priests and deacons, consecrated churches, and watched over the keeping of the canonical order, observing both strictness and condescension in all that the Church of Christ has appointed for the saving profit of those he shepherded.

After many years as a bishop, feeling his body grow weak, he withdrew from the episcopal throne and returned to his monastery of Laura. There he received the great schema and the name Leontius, and struggled day by day, living truly an angel's life in the body, until his passing. Knowing his end beforehand, he called the whole brotherhood together to give them his last fatherly counsels, appointed as abbot of that holy monastery his nearest disciple, Daniel the Hermit, and blessing him fell asleep in the Lord.

The faithful and his spiritual children came to his grave and received blessing and healing from every kind of sickness. Soon his body was taken up and brought to Rădăuți, as the faithful wished, and many came from round about and from far off to the reliquary. The village of Laura, where the monastery stood in which he struggled as abbot before he was called to the episcopate, still carries the monastery's name. Doxologia's troparion page for him is a recording and prints no hymn text, only the refrain «Sfinte Ierarhe Leontie, roagă-te lui Dumnezeu pentru noi!», so no hymn is recorded here.
