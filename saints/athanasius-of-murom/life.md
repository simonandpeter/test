# Athanasius of Murom

saint.gr keeps two ascetics of the Murmansk country together on this day, and of the second it says plainly that nothing is known. The abbot Athanasius struggled at the monastery of [Lazarus](/saints/lazarus-of-murom) of Murom, or Murmansk, in the middle of the fifteenth century — a lifetime after its founder — and unhappily, the page says, there is no information about his life. That sentence is the whole of his biography, and everything else the page gives is about what was done with his memory afterwards.

After his repose his body was placed in a chapel of its own, and his chains are kept there as evidence of his struggles. He is venerated locally at Lazarus's monastery together with Lazarus himself, and the veneration is old: in the second half of the seventeenth century the abbot Athanasius is described as a venerable wonderworker. A troparion and a kontakion in his honour are known to exist, the page adds, though it prints neither of them.

It describes his icons instead. He is shown with the prayer-rope in his left hand, and in his right a scroll standing for his teaching, which reads: listen, my brother, and do not sin until the end of the age. Sometimes he is shown bare-headed, with short hair and a long dark beard tapering to a point, in a light green garment and a brown cloak with the blue schema and the cowl on his shoulders, and above him the inscription: Saint Athanasius of Murom, Wonderworker. On one icon at the Dormition monastery of Murom he and Lazarus stand at full length, praying to the Saviour in heaven.

The Russian calendar gives him four sentences of his own and a year, and it is the first year this folder has carried. He laboured about the middle of the fifteenth century in the house of [Lazarus of Murom](/saints/lazarus-of-murom), who had died in 1391, and was its abbot; the shared life of the two of them puts it the same way. After his death his body was laid in a chapel of its own, where his chains were kept — the witness of his labours. The veneration of him is ancient, the account says, and in the second half of the seventeenth century the abbot Athanasius was being called a venerable wonderworker. In his own house, it adds, he was honoured equally with Lazarus. A troparion and a kontakion to him are known to exist; the calendar prints neither.
