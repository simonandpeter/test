# Maximus, companion of Fausta

Maximus was eparch to the pagan emperor Maximian, a man the life calls very harsh and inhuman. When word reached the emperor that [Evilasius](/saints/evilasius-companion-of-fausta), the chief servant of the idols, had been won to Christ by the virgin [Fausta](/saints/fausta-of-cyzicus) at Cyzicus, Maximian swore him by his gods and sent him there in anger. He came and demanded of Evilasius how he had dared to leave the great gods and believe the Christians' folly, and Evilasius answered that he too, if he would listen to Fausta, would quickly know the living God.

In his rage he had Evilasius hung up and cruelly beaten and his ribs burned with torches, and then set Fausta before his judgement seat for having turned him, and had iron nails driven into her feet; and she felt nothing. He gave her to the beasts, and the lioness bowed before her and the rest of the beasts fell at her feet. He had her dragged naked by the feet along the ground, and a cloud came down and covered her. Then the soldier Eusebius took her, had long nails driven into her head and body, and cast her with Evilasius into a cauldron of boiling pitch and brimstone; and the two of them stood in it as in a green and cool place, singing, until the fire went out and the cauldron grew cold.

Seeing the faith and the patience of the saints, Maximus prayed: "Everlasting God, who hast made Evilasius a partaker of the sufferings of Thy servant Fausta, receive me also, wretched and sinful, and number me with these two, that I, the least, may complete the number of three after the image of the Holy Trinity." As he said it the heavens opened and he saw the Son of God with the ranks of the saints and the angels. Fausta gave thanks that God wills that none should perish, saying she stood between these two servants of His like a vine bearing clusters and asking that the three of them never be parted; and a voice was heard from heaven calling those who had laboured and were laden with sufferings for His name to their rest. The saints were filled with unspeakable joy, gave thanks, and gave their souls into God's hands on the sixth day of the month of February.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and its page gives him the office and the turn that made him one of the three. He was the prefect, and the news that the senator [Evilasius](/saints/evilasius-companion-of-fausta) had set [Fausta](/saints/fausta-of-cyzicus) free enraged him; he summoned Evilasius and reproached him sharply for his stand and his simplicity. Evilasius, unshaken by the insults, told him the history of the martyr's torments and the wonder of her rescue, and the reasons why he had judged the truth to be Christ. Then the prefect put Evilasius and Fausta to frightful tortures, which the two of them bore with spiritual courage; and God, the page says, worked his wonder again, so that Maximus, faced with what had happened, confessed Christ, and with a broken and humbled heart knelt before the saint and asked her forgiveness. The thing rang through all Cyzicus, the news reached Diocletian, and by his command all three were put to death and received with joy the crown of martyrdom.

The Serbian church keeps the three of them on the Julian 6 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue gives him half a clause. Fausta's courage, it says, astonished her torturers and turned them Christian: the eighty-year-old priest of the idols Evilasius, and Maximus the eparch. That is the whole of him here — the office the other two sources give him, and nothing of the prayer by which he asked to make the third of the three, nor of how he died. The Prologue puts the reign of Maximian «between 305 and 311», where saint.gr dates him 285 to 305.
