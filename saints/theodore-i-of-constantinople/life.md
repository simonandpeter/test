# Theodore I of Constantinople

saint.gr's calendar for 27 December keeps Theodore, the first archbishop of Constantinople to bear that name. The page says he was born and bred in Constantinople itself and lived in the years of the emperor Constantine IV Pogonatos, whose reign it dates 668 to 685. For his great virtue and reverence he was ordained presbyter of Hagia Sophia, and afterwards became its syncellus and the keeper of its vessels.

When the patriarch of the day, [Constantine](/saints/constantine-i-of-constantinople), died, the emperor and the senate obliged Theodore to be ordained patriarch. He governed the Church in a way pleasing to God for two years and three months, and was then put off the throne by that same emperor Constantine Pogonatos. The removal, the page says, did not wither the divine zeal of the patriarch Theodore: he passed the rest of his life as pleasingly to God as before, and died in peace.

Then, in the page's own order, it adds that he came back to his throne after the death of father [George I](/saints/george-i-patriarch-of-constantinople), and was patriarch until 687. Its head gives his dates as «(; - 687)» and no year of birth. It prints no hymn for him, and closes by sending the reader on to the synaxis of all the archbishops and patriarchs of Constantinople who are among the saints.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 27 Δεκεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/12/27/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3151/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
