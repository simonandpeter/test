# Hermes of Dalmatia

Hermes — Ermis in the Romanian, Hermes in the page's own gloss — is numbered among the seventy apostles. He was bishop in Dalmatia, and he is one of those the apostle Paul greets in the sixteenth chapter of the letter to the Romans. The day keeps him with five other apostles: [Agabus](/saints/agabus-the-apostle), [Herodion](/saints/herodion-the-apostle), [Rufus](/saints/rufus-apostle-of-thebes), [Phlegon](/saints/phlegon-of-marathon) and [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle); the page for Agabus says of all six that they ended on the same day as a result of the torments they were put to.

Two things on his page do not agree with the day that carries him, and both are left standing here rather than smoothed. The first is the date: his own page says his commemoration is kept on 8 March and on 4 January with the seventy, and does not name 8 April at all, while the calendar for 8 aprilie prints him and the troparion of the day is headed with his name among the six. The second is a caution the page itself raises: he is not to be confused with the apostle Hermas, who is credited with the work called The Shepherd and who is mentioned in the same verse of the letter to the Romans.

That second caution is why this entry stands apart from the Hermas the corpus already keeps on the Russian 18 August, one of three Roman martyrs with Serapion and Polyaenus; the sources are different, the day is different, and neither page says they are the same man. Dionysius of Fourna, in his Hermeneia, says that the apostle Hermes is to be painted as an old bishop with a long broad beard, holding a parchment scroll in his hands, which stands for the apostles' preaching to all nations.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 8 March, and its notice is four sentences. saint.gr says the apostle Paul mentions Hermes in his letter to the Romans, at 16:14; that by tradition he became bishop of Dalmatia and died peacefully; that in Delehaye's synaxarist he is called Hermylus; and that in the Parisian Codex 13, folio 296, his memory is borne on 8 April together with Herodion, Asyncritus, Phlegon, Rufus, Epaphras and Agabus. Two of those sentences are warnings rather than facts about him. The corpus keeps a Hermylus, the deacon martyred at Singidunum, whom saint.gr itself keeps on 13 January, and he is not this man; and the 8 April of the Parisian codex is the day, and the company, on which the Romanian calendar already prints him — so the two records agree about that, and the Greek calendar's own day is the March one. No year is given.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the six together on this day, on the Romanian calendar's 8 April rather than the Greek 8 March, and gives this one a single line: Saint Hermes, mentioned with the rest, was bishop in Dalmatia — which is what both the other calendars give him. The entry adds nothing else of him, and no year. It closes of all six that, like bees of Christ, they carried the honey of the Gospel about into various countries, suffering much for the love of Christ, and that all of them passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ the beloved.

**The Russian church calendar keeps the six of them together on the Julian 8 April**, which is the day the Romanian and the Serbian keep, and its line is «Апп. от 70 Иродиона, Агава, Асинкрита, Руфа, Флегонта, Ерма и иже с ними (I).» — six genitives under one rank word, a century in a Roman numeral, and a company left open at the end, «и иже с ними», *and those with them*, which is a phrase no other calendar read for this folder prints. The six name links behind the line, pages 1871, 46, 282, 1886, 2313 and 791, all lead to one life of the company; three of them give in their own titles the see the day’s line withholds, and those same three close with «сщмч.», hieromartyr, which is a rank none of these folders carries. The rank is set down here and `types` is left as it was.

That life sets them in the number of the seventy apostles chosen by Christ and sent out to preach, points to the synaxis of the Seventy on 4 января, and then disposes of four of them in a single sentence: «апостол Руф, которого приветствует святой апостол Павел в Послании к Римлянам (Рим. 16, 11), был епископом в греческом городе Фивы; апостол Асинкрит (Рим. 16, 14) епископом в Гиркании (Малая Азия); апостол Флегонт – в городе Марафоне (Фракия), апостол Ерм – в Далматии». It closes that all of them bore cruel sufferings for their fearless service to Christ and were counted worthy of a martyr’s end — which is what the Romanian page says of all six and the Greek page says of two.

**His name page on the Russian calendar is one page for two men, and the page itself says so in a parenthesis.** Page 791 is titled «Апостол Ерм», lists three days — 4 января with the Seventy, 8 апреля, and 31 мая — and carries two lives. One is the life of the company, which says «апостол Ерм – в Далматии» and then adds in brackets that there is known also **another** apostle of the Seventy of this name, who held the see of Филиппополь in Thrace. The other, titled «Апостол Ерм» alone, is four lines and says he was bishop «в Филиппополе Фракийском», suffered much from the pagans and died in peace — that is, it is the life of the man the first life distinguishes from this one. The page also files its hymns by day: the 8 апреля kontakion is the company’s, and the 31 мая troparion and kontakion address a «Ермие» whom the Dalmatian life does not claim.

**azbyka.ru, read for the heading, states the case from the epistle.** Its page separates the names in the title and then quotes archbishop Dimitry: that the two are named in one and the same verse of the letter to the Romans in every edition of the New Testament, Greek, Slavonic and Latin, Ερμᾶς beside Ερμής and Hermain beside Hermen in the Vulgate, so that this one is **a wholly distinct person from the Ерм who is the supposed author of the Shepherd**.

It says further that nothing definite survives of his enlightening work in Dalmatia beyond the general expressions of the service to him on 8 апреля — that he was the teacher of Dalmatia and enlightened those who sat in the pagan night, working healings and great signs, which it places in the eighth ode of the canon — and that from the Greek Synaxarion he died in peace after many labours and sorrows, where this folder’s other three calendars give all six a martyr’s end. Both ends are now on the page and neither is chosen.

**The day prints one hymn for the company, and it is the first hymn in Russian this folder has had.** It is a kontakion in the second tone, «Явистеся Христовы ученицы и апостоли всечестнии», and it names all six of them in order before asking them to pray for the forgiveness of the sins of those who sing them. He is named last in it, «Флегонте со Ермом», and the folder held no hymn in any language but Romanian before. **azbyka gives him the same three days the Russian name page gives him**, the synaxis of the Seventy, this day, and the civil 13 июня which is the Julian 31 мая; the corpus keeps a separate folder on 31 May off the Romanian and the Serbian, and whether the two are one man is not settled here. Nothing on 31 May has been touched.
