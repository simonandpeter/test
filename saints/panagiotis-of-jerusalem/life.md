# Panagiotis, martyred at Jerusalem

saint.gr says the new martyr Panagiotis was from the Peloponnese and was martyred at Jerusalem on 5 April 1820. Its account rests, the page says, on the narrative of the English missionary Joseph Wolff, written on 2 April 1839: a young Greek called Panagiotis was in the service of a Turkish gentleman named Osman Efendi.

When his master once went into the Mosque of Omar at Jerusalem, Panagiotis followed him in. The fanatical Turks held that by entering he had defiled their mosque, and accused him to the Pasha of Damascus. The Pasha asked the young man to accept Islam so as to escape death; and Panagiotis, as soon as he heard it, cried out boldly before the governor and a crowd of Muslims: «Ο Χριστός είναι ο Υιός του Θεού του ζώντος, θανάτωσε με, δεν φοβάμαι. Χριστός ανέστη» — Christ is the Son of the living God; put me to death, I am not afraid. Christ is risen. They beheaded him there and then.

Still on the English missionary's information, who had watched the martyrdom, the «Ἑλληνικὸν Μοναστήριον» of Jerusalem bought the new martyr's body from the Turks for five thousand piastres and buried it with honours. The page gives him no year of birth, no village in the Peloponnese and no family, prints no couplet and no hymn under his name, and names his relics as being at the monastery of the Forerunner in Jerusalem.
