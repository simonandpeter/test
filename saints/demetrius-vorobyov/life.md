# Demetrius (Vorobyov)

He was the younger of the two brothers of his name kept on this day, born in 1873 in the village of Klyuchevoye in the Maksatikha district of the province of Tver, to a devout peasant family; his brother is [Simeon (Vorobyov)](/saints/simeon-vorobyov). Neither was educated to any purpose, the reading says, but both could read and were very well read in the books of the Church, and both had been brought to prayer and to the services from childhood. He served in the army as a private soldier in 1914 and 1915.

Before the revolution he had a small holding — two cows, two sheep and a windmill. In the early thirties the brothers were arrested, everything was confiscated and they were sent with their families to the Urals; he had two daughters of fourteen and thirteen and a son of eleven. A year later they were allowed home, and nothing was given back; to keep from starving in his own village he got together some small holding again.

What the reading says was chief in the two brothers was their faith and their great love of the church, and it was in their two households that the priest newly appointed to Klyuchevoye lodged, and in them that the parish found its support. He was arrested on 23 July 1937 with his brother and his brother's son, and shut in the prison at Bezhetsk. His answers at the interrogation are printed: he did not admit himself guilty of what he was charged with, and he explained that his connection with the priest, with his brother and with his nephew consisted in going to church. The reading remarks that this was all the two brothers thought it necessary to answer, and that their only guilt was their zeal for the church.

On 13 August the NKVD troika sentenced them both to be shot, and they were shot on 17 August 1937. The reading closes with their glorification among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of August 2000.
