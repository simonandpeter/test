# Nilus (Smirnov)

Nilus Mikhailovich Smirnov was born on 5 November 1881 in the village of Peresypkino in the Kirsanov district of Tambov province, the son of Michael Smirnov, a village schoolteacher, and of a priest’s daughter. After the Kirsanov town school he worked on the railway from 1897 to 1921, as clerk, cashier and telegraphist and at last as manager of the Jubilee settlement for the workers of the Moscow–Kazan railway.

In 1921 he asked to be ordained for the Moscow clergy; he served as deacon at the church of the Resurrection in Sokolniki, and two weeks later, on 22 October 1921, was ordained priest for the church of the unmercenaries Cyrus and John at the Serbian podvorye; that November Patriarch Tikhon confirmed him as priest of the Resurrection church in Sokolniki. From 1923 he served at the Dormition church at the Kartino pogost in the Mozhaisk district and from 1925 at Sokolniki again, where he took charge of the parish in 1930, was confirmed as its rector a year later, and was made archpriest in 1931.

He was arrested on 17 February 1933 and held in the Butyrka prison, a witness having told the investigator that he preached fiery sermons and spoke too openly against the Soviet power. He answered that he had taken holy orders when the struggle against unbelief had grown fierce and the people were leaving the Church, that his sermons called for the moral life and against godlessness, and that he was not guilty. On 22 March 1933 he was sentenced to three years’ exile in the Northern Territory, which he served at Kargopol, unloading timber from rafts and sawing firewood.

Coming back in 1936 he was sent to the Epiphany church at Buigorod in the Volokolamsk district, and in May 1937, at the invitation of the priest Boris Nedumov, went to serve at the church of the Forerunner at Ivanovskoye-on-Lama. The district prosecutor reported that the two priests had a harmonium, an accordion and a violin, gathered people for evenings meant to raise the spirit of religion, formed a choir and collected money; and the headmaster at Buigorod remembered Nilus asking that his daughter Catherine, a believer who went to church and wore a cross, be spared the school’s anti-religious work and not made to join the Pioneers.

He was arrested on 20 August 1937 and held in the Taganka prison. He admitted asking his parishioners to help the heavily taxed clergy and telling them at Pascha that the Church was no longer a state church and the government had no right to touch it, and denied the rest; on 9 October 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced him to ten years in the camps. He was sent to Bamlag in the Amur region, died at Izvestkovaya station on 2 October 1938, and was buried in an unknown grave. The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 September old style, on one line with the novice [Maria (Mamontova-Shashina)](/saints/maria-mamontova-shashina), who died in Bamlag the same day.
