# George (Kossov)

George Alekseevich Kossov, a true confessor of the faith of Christ, was
born on 4 April 1855 in the village of Androsovo in the Dmitrovsk district
of Oryol province, in the middle of the nineteenth century, when Russians,
especially in the villages, kept firmly and sacredly all the traditions of
the nation’s piety and could not imagine life without the baptism of
children, the crowning of couples and Orthodox burial. His parents were
very devout — his father a priest, his mother raising the children — and
named their firstborn for George the Victorious, much honoured in Russia
as the conqueror of the dark powers, and the boy’s heart took in many
stories of wonders, healings, help in need and even rescues from death.
They strove to give him a good education: after the village school he
followed his parents’ advice, who never tired of instructing him in
Christian piety, and at the seminary was marked by purity of soul,
humility and unfeigned love, a reverent interest in Scripture and the
Fathers, and a love of prayer and psalmody. After the seminary he went
home to teach in a zemstvo school of the Dmitrovsk district; his lessons,
prepared with special care, were like little sermons addressed to the
children’s hearts, he was always rewarded at the examinations for his
pupils, and when he came to head the school his gift showed in the
God-fearing, disciplined and practical young people it turned out. In his
free time he was drawn irresistibly to church, loving the services as if
the church were his own home — God’s call to a conscious ascetic life —
and a serious illness did not hold him back but strengthened his hope in
God’s mercy as his prayers grew. He married a girl of humble station, an
orphan without a dowry but devout, and in 1884 was ordained priest and
sent to the poorest parish in the Oryol diocese, Spas-Chekryak in the
Bolkhov district, where the Lord glorified his name. The wretched state of
a parish of fourteen households appalled him, and he understood why no
clergy stayed: two versts from the village stood a decayed and empty
church where even the Holy Gifts froze during the service, and the
villagers’ hearts were so far from church and from God that the young
priest fell at once into confusion. After many sleepless nights, when the
hopelessness grew unbearable, he went to Optina, to the great ascetic
Ambrose, who received people sometimes singly and sometimes all together
for a general blessing; George stood in the crowd far from the door, and
when the elder came out in his white smock and fur riassa, prayed before
the icon of the Mother of God “It is truly meet” and looked closely into
the crowd, he beckoned George to him through it — a man he had never seen
or heard of, and who had come without his priest’s dress. What is this
you have thought up, priest — abandoning your parish? Your church is old,
you see, and falling down. Then build a new one, big, of stone, and warm,
with wooden floors, for they will bring the sick to you and they must be
warm. Go home, priest, go, and throw the folly out of your head; remember,
build the church as I tell you. Go, priest; God bless you. In great
bewilderment at the seer’s command — how raise a stone church in what was
practically a desert! — he went back to Spas-Chekryak and resolved to go
on serving there, not yet knowing that this was to be the place of his
labours, and served with reverent awe, lit in prayer by the joy of
expecting the glory of God; the temptations he had borne bred humility,
the fear of God strengthened his striving, grace enlightened his mind. On
the feasts, above all on 22 October, the day of the Kazan icon, he served
especially long and fervently, and pilgrims from elsewhere began to come
with the parishioners; after the service he answered their questions
fully and to the soul’s profit, grasping at once a person’s spiritual
state and sickness and pointing first to the Christian remedies, and the
fame of his ascetic life and abundant virtues spread fast, so that people
came in crowds to see the confessor, take his blessing and hear his word,
and never forgot the sweetness of the services at which the Queen of
Heaven comforted them through her wonderworking icon. From every side —
Spas-Chekryak lies on the border of three provinces, Oryol, Kaluga and
Tula — people of every rank, condition, sex and age came for counsel, and
for them he built a pilgrims’ house of brick from his own brickworks,
which he had set up in 1896 as the first of his enterprises and where the
parishioners worked; the visitors had not only a roof but his company at
table and answers to a multitude of questions. By God’s grace he healed:
the possessed were brought to him, tormented, raving, screaming, falling
at his feet, and he prayed and sprinkled them with holy water and the dark
powers left them — healings were daily and beyond counting, and many a
paralytic carried in walked out of Spas-Chekryak on his own feet praising
God’s mercy. For the gravely ill he opened a hospital, going round the
patients every day and curing those whose families had given them up;
people so trusted his intercession that they held whatever he prayed for
the Lord would grant, and God did heed the righteous man, glorified by
the gift of foresight, whose prophecies came as instruction in God’s
commandments and whose word was a spring of healing. In 1896 the building
of the church began in obedience to Ambrose, and by 1905 a splendid
three-altared church of the Transfiguration stood there — the
transfiguration indeed of a whole life in what had lately been a
backwater of grey, dull, hungry existence called by no other name than
God-forsaken, and now a place of God’s glory. Without means, trusting only
in wondrous help, he set about an orphanage in the village — a refuge for
a hundred and fifty peasant orphans, unheard of till then in Russia,
built with the kopecks and the fortunes of simple people and of the
well-born alike; faithful teachers and tutors gathered to him, and he took
the direction and the spiritual guidance himself, an example of industry,
temperance and humility, shunning the world’s vanity. On 22 October 1903,
the feast of the Kazan icon, a second-class school was consecrated at
Spas-Chekryak, such as the district itself did not have, in a festival to
which clergy, provincial and district officials, his spiritual children
and venerators came; several more schools were built in his parish, at
Sigolaevo, Shpilevo and Merkulovo, with him as trustee and catechist, and
he was honoured as the benefactor of the Bolkhov district and of all the
Oryol land, where no undertaking was begun without his blessing. People
of every station came to see for themselves whether the fame of that
bright corner in the depths of Russia was true; the writer Prishvin came
and wrote his sketch “New Land” about the wonders there, the holy well of
“Father Yegor”, the devoted faith of his venerators and the cases of
foresight and healing; John of Kronstadt honoured him highly, and scolded
Oryol pilgrims for coming to him when they had such a holy priest at home.
The day after the October coup Seraphim (Ostroumov), bishop of Oryol and
Sevsk and a future new martyr, visited him at Spas-Chekryak and left his
house in tears. Committing himself to God’s providence he went on
comforting his flock in a rebellious world, and by his holy efforts
Spas-Chekryak was an island in a raging sea. The Church was separated
from the state, hatred stirred up against it and its clergy, and all
clergy and their families persecuted; in the autumn of 1918 Red Army men
came to take him to prison, and were somewhat at a loss at his
extraordinary meekness, humility and love — how to arrest such a man? In
the district prison they did not know what to do with the famous new
prisoner, or how to write him down as an enemy of the people, who kept an
orphanage, several schools, a brickworks and a hospital at his own
expense, always helped the peasants, and was loved and defended by the
people like a wall; the Bolsheviks looked in wonder at a pastor in whose
eyes shone true love for God and men and the wish to serve them to his
last breath, and let him out, fearing the people’s anger. In the early
twenties a commission for the seizure of church valuables came to
Spas-Chekryak and demanded everything of gold and silver; the priest had
no valuables, was accused of hiding them, arrested and sent this time to
the provincial prison, where the warders gave him the dirtiest and most
degrading work to humble his rank — and the other prisoners did it
themselves to shield the priest they had come to love; again the
authorities could produce no evidence and had to let him go. Hardship and
unresting pastoral labour ruined his health; pains in the stomach and
liver so bound him that he could not stand, yet on his deathbed he kept
his care for others, received those who came lying down, and told them:
it is over; I am leaving you; hope in God now, and come to me at my grave
with your troubles as to a living man — as before, I shall pray for you
and help you. Before his death the holy well at Spas-Chekryak fell in and
a new spring broke out nearby; they brought him its water, he prayed over
it and had it poured back into the spring, which, he said, would now be
the holy well and heal the faithful. He died on 26 August 1928 of the old
style — the day the Russian calendar keeps him — aged over seventy-three;
crowds hurried to Spas-Chekryak to pay their debt of honour and love and
take leave of one who had lived for God, the authorities could not forbid
the veneration, the lamentation was great, and many understood only then
what holiness they had touched; the coffin was carried round the church
in a great procession and he was buried beside the church by the altar.
Decades passed and much changed in Russia and at Spas-Chekryak, but not
what no human hand can destroy, the works of God, nor the certainty of his
holiness: his spiritual children set a monument and a railing on the
grave, the people’s path to it never grew over, people felt the help of
his prayers, saw wonders by his intercession, met him in dreams, and were
healed bathing in his holy well. In August 2000 the jubilee Council of
Bishops canonised him as a hieroconfessor, and the Oryol clergy found his
relics on the feast of George the Victorious, his guardian angel on earth,
so that 9 December is kept as the day of their finding; the Russian
calendar keeps him also in the synaxis of the saints of Oryol.
