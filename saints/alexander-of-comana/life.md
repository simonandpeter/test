# Alexander, Bishop of Comana

Alexander lived in the third century near Neocaesarea. He had studied the Scriptures and knew many sciences, but took on the feat of folly for Christ and lived in poverty, selling charcoal in the city square; many, seeing his face always black with coal dust, despised him.

When the bishop of Comana died, some wanted a nobleman for the see, others a scholar or an orator, others a rich man. Gregory of Neocaesarea, invited to consecrate the chosen man, said that a bishop needed not outward distinction but a pure heart and a holy life — at which some smirked: if looks and birth do not matter, Alexander the charcoal-burner could be made bishop. Gregory thought the name had not been spoken without God’s providence and had him called; his appearance raised laughter, but he stood absorbed in himself, paying no heed, and when Gregory examined him he had to admit that he had been a philosopher and had studied the Scriptures, and had taken voluntary poverty and humility on himself for God’s sake. Gregory took him home, had him washed and dressed in clean clothes, and back at the council questioned him on the Scriptures before everyone; he answered as a wise and knowing pastor, and the assembly, amazed at his humility, elected him with one voice. Gregory ordained him presbyter and then bishop, and his first sermon was full of power and grace. Under Diocletian he confessed Christ courageously, refused to worship the idols, and after tortures was thrown into the fire, where he went to God; some sources, the life adds, put his death under Decius. The Prologue tells the same story for the Serbian calendar, with its own verse: men look at clothes and face, God at soul and heart.
