# Euthymius the Iberian

Euthymius was the son of [John](/saints/john-the-iberian), the founder of the monastery of the Iberians, and received the monastic schema from him while he was still small. He was his father's disciple on Olympus in Bithynia and followed him to the Great Lavra. At the urging of Saint Athanasius he received the priesthood, and later, for a short time, the office of abbot. Together with his father he built the monastery of Iviron in 980, and then followed him in its abbacy.

He built churches and infirmaries and adorned the whole Mountain with hermitages. In 1012 he gave up the abbacy, so as to give himself without distraction to ascetic struggle and to writing. His struggles increased his virtues, and he became a clean vessel of the Holy Spirit for the rich spiritual work he had to do.

His fine education and his great learning helped him to write original books, and his excellent knowledge of Greek to translate the divine Scriptures, the synaxaria, and patristic and ascetic texts. His work was a spiritual food of great price in the Iberian Church. He was a prolific teacher and translator, and because of his brilliant classical style he became the most read writer of the Iberian Middle Ages; his countrymen called him, rightly, a new Chrysostom.

His ascetic struggles and the gift of his gentle teaching spread his fame far beyond Mount Athos, and his teaching and his miracles at Thessalonica brought many Jews to Christ. He ended his life at Constantinople, and at his burial many healings were worked, so that his holiness and his boldness before God should be shown. His life and his service were written by Manuel the Great Rhetor. His memory is honoured on 13 May.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day and tells the founding of Iviron from his side. saint.gr has him out of the city of Tao in Iberia, living through the second half of the tenth century and a good many years of the eleventh. In 965 the counsellor of David Kouropalates, king of Georgia — [John Varasvatse](/saints/john-the-iberian) — came and settled beside [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-the-athonite) at the monastery of the Lavra, and shortly afterwards brought his son Euthymius and other notable Georgians, and all of them became monks. The space granted them being small, John took the initiative of founding an independent monastery, which was done with the help of the emperor Basil the Bulgar-slayer and called the monastery of the Iberians, because its founders were Iberians.

When John died — in 998, or by other accounts in 1003 — his son and fellow worker Euthymius succeeded him. He governed the house with success and cultivated the spiritual life of its brotherhood with great zeal, offering himself first as a perfect example in every virtue. So great was the repute of his holiness that the emperor Basil proposed him for the archbishopric of Cyprus, and he preferred his beloved monastery and would not have it. In 1028 he went to Constantinople on the business of the Holy Mountain, and there, on 13 May, that virtuous and devout man died.

The Ohrid Prologue, read for the Julian 13 May, keeps the four of them under one heading as the founders of the famous Iberian — that is, the Georgian — monastery on the Holy Mountain. [John](/saints/john-the-iberian) struggled first in the lavra of [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-the-athonite) and afterwards founded his own monastery, Iver, and fell asleep in 998. Euthymius and [George](/saints/george-the-iberian), it says, translated the Holy Scriptures into the Georgian tongue; and Euthymius fell asleep in 1028, which is the year this folder already had from the title of its Romanian page. [Gabriel](/saints/gabriel-the-iberian) was counted worthy to receive the wonderworking icon of the Mother of God which came to the monastery over the sea.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same menologion day, 13 мая ст. ст., marks him «(Груз.)» as a saint of the Georgian church, and gives his life in eight sentences. He was a Georgian of a noble and wealthy family, given as a child to the Greek emperor at Constantinople as a hostage, where he finished his book-learning with success. Then he came to the Holy Mountain, to the Iberian house his father Иоанн had founded, and there he worked at translating the Holy Scriptures and the service books out of Greek into Georgian; and after his father's death he took the abbacy and governed the brotherhood twelve years.

Being famous for his virtuous life and his labours, he was asked by the brethren of the lavra of Афанасий to be their superior as well, and he laboured much at the ordering of the houses of Athos. Towards the end of his life he gave up the abbacy for the sake of the translations, but still kept watch and care over the Iberian house and over that lavra. In 1028, in the sixty-fifth year of his life, being at Constantinople on the monastery's business, he gave up his soul into the hands of God, and after his death the Lord glorified him with wonders. The page lists two other days for him besides this one, 1 июня among the Athonite saints and 12 июля, which is his father's day; neither is the day that printed him here.
