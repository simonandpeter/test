# Nicholas (Rozov)

The Russian calendar prints four words for him and no life: «Сщмч. Николая пресвитера (1930)» — the hieromartyr Nicholas, presbyter, 1930 — and its index of names adds the surname, «Николай (Розов), иерей». He was born in 1870 at Ivankovo in the Rostov district of the Yaroslavl province, the son of a priest who bore the same name. Out of the Yaroslavl seminary he was ordained to the church at Spassk-Gorodets in the same district, served there until 1928, and was then moved to the church at Chashnitsy.

Collectivisation reached the villages while he was there. Some peasants were driven into the collective farms; others had their land and their goods taken and were deported by whole families into unsettled country. The people took it for a war beginning against them, and they came to the churches to confess and to communicate. On the feast of the Meeting of the Lord, 15 February 1930, the church at Chashnitsy was full, and almost everyone who came confessed and communicated. Before the confession he told them: «Православные, эта исповедь проходит, может быть, в последний раз» — this confession is perhaps the last; I wish you a better life in the new conditions with all my heart, but do not forget the faith of Christ.

At the end of the service he said it again, that the time had turned troubled, that all the churches were being closed and theirs would surely be closed too and they would have nowhere to serve. The people wept, the account says, because none of them wanted to lose the house of God. Two days later the district's officer for collectivisation sent his superior a report: the priest of Chashnitsy hears confessions on Sundays and feasts, people come to him from the whole neighbourhood, and after the confessions those who had signed up for the collective farm are observed withdrawing — «со своей стороны я считаю необходимым попа села Чашницы выслать — чем скорее, тем лучше», the sooner he is deported the better. After the service of 18 February the same officer reported that there had been twice as many penitents as the first time and that the service had run until one in the afternoon where services usually ended at eleven, and he demanded the deportation again.

He was arrested on 22 February 1930 and shut in the prison at Yaroslavl. Questioned, he said that about fifty of his parishioners had indeed been in the church for the Meeting of the Lord and had come to confess, that he had preached no sermon about the persecution of religion, and that he did not admit the charge of agitating against the Soviet power and the collective farms. The investigation closed on 28 February, and on 16 March 1930 an NKVD troika sentenced him to three years of exile at Arkhangelsk. He died in the Yaroslavl prison on 20 March 1930, before he could be sent, and was buried in an unmarked grave. That civil day is the Julian 7 March on which the calendar keeps him, so the day of his death is his feast; the same calendar also names him on 26 января with the whole company of the new martyrs, and azbyka.ru adds the synaxis of the saints of Rostov and Yaroslavl.
