# Anthousa of Seleucia

Anthousa, a native of Seleucia in Syria, was the daughter of noble pagans. Learning the teaching of Christ she went, on the pretext of visiting her nurse, to [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-tarsus), bishop of Tarsus, and received baptism from him — angels appeared at her baptism, the Prologue says. Her parents were angry that she had become a Christian, and taking the monastic tonsure from Athanasius she withdrew into the desert, where she spent twenty-three years in ascetic labours and died in peace at the end of the third century, kneeling in prayer on a stone under which she asked to be buried. The martyrs [Charisimus](/saints/charisimus-servant-of-anthousa) and [Neophytus](/saints/neophytus-servant-of-anthousa), baptised with her, were her servants and died for Christ.

The Romanian calendar keeps her on 22 August, without her servants, though its life of her names them. It says Anthousa was of Seleucia and lived in the days of the emperor Valerian, the daughter of Antonius and Maria, who were very rich but worshippers of idols; that believing in secret she wished to receive the baptism of Christ and to see the bishop [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-tarsus) who was preaching at Tarsus in Cilicia, and so asked her mother for two mules and set out with two servants, [Charisimus](/saints/charisimus-servant-of-anthousa) and [Neophytus](/saints/neophytus-servant-of-anthousa), saying she was going to her nurse. On the road the bishop was carried by angels and stood before her; she fell at his feet and begged for baptism, and when there was no water he prayed and a spring came out, and two angels appeared in the likeness of servants holding two white garments. She and her two servants were baptised; she gave the bishop her gilded robe to sell for the poor, and put on a plainer one. Her nurse would not receive her, and her mother grieved that she had been baptised, so she went out secretly to Athanasius, took the monastic habit and the Cross of the Lord, and went into the wilderness, where she lived twenty-three years among the beasts, fed by them through God's providence and much assailed by demons, and gave up her soul in peace on the rock where she used to sleep.

The Greek calendar keeps the four together on 22 August and names the parents. saint.gr says that Anthousa lived in the years of the king Valerian, 253 to 259, and came of Seleucia in Syria, the daughter of rich idolatrous parents, Antoninus and Martyria — or Maria. Wishing to see the bishop [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-tarsus), who was teaching at Tarsus in Cilicia, she persuaded her mother to go there with her two servants, [Charisimus](/saints/charisimus-servant-of-anthousa) and [Neophytus](/saints/neophytus-servant-of-anthousa), on the pretext that she would be visiting her nurse.

When she found Athanasius she was baptised by him and tonsured a nun. Afterwards she withdrew into the desert, and there, having lived an ascetic life for twenty-three whole years, she reposed in peace. Athanasius was taken before Valerian, and because he would not consent to sacrifice to the idols he was beheaded. So were the two servants: Charisimus and Neophytus confessed Christ with courage before the king, and were beheaded.

The page gives each of them a distich. Anthousa's plays on her name — «Ἀνθῆσαν ἐκ γῆς τῆς Σελευκείας ῥόδον, Ἀνθοῦσαν ἐδρέψαντο χεῖρες Ἀγγέλων», a rose flowering out of the land of Seleucia, Anthousa, whom the hands of angels plucked. Athanasius's makes the same play on his: though I die by the sword, Athanasius, I shall be numbered a living man among the living friends of Christ. And of the servants it says «Δοῦλοι δύο τμηθέντες, εὗρον οἱ δύο Τὴν εὐγένειαν, ἣν ἀπώλεσαν πάλαι» — two slaves, cut down, the two of them found the nobility they had lost long ago. The years the page gives for Valerian's reign do not agree with the dates this folder already carries, and both are left standing.
