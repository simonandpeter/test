# Mary Magdalene

Mary, called the Magdalene, was the first of Christ's women disciples and the greatest of the myrrh-bearers. The Romanian life says she came of the tribe of Naphtali, whose country lies within the borders of upper Galilee, from a place called Magdal, and that from it she was named Magdalene. She lived in the days of the Lord's coming upon earth, and from her youth she was tormented and troubled by seven unclean spirits — whether by God's unsearchable judgements, that the works of the Son of God might be shown in her, as He said of the man born blind, or by a divine permission for some good of the soul.

Hearing of Christ, who went about the cities and villages of Galilee healing every sickness and casting out devils and preaching the gospel of the kingdom, she came to Him and was at once received into His mercy. He drove out of her those seven cruel tormentors and made her whole in body and in soul, and lit her mind with the knowledge of the truth, that she might know Him to be the awaited Messiah, the Son of God sent by the Father for the salvation of the world.

From then she followed Him and served Him with the other holy women, to His voluntary passion, to His death on the cross and to the burial of His body; and Matthew, naming the women who had come after Jesus out of Galilee, sets her first, as the evangelist's own witness that she clave to Christ and to His Mother more than all. At the crucifixion she stood by the cross with the Theotokos, weeping for Him and suffering for His Mother, whom she upheld and comforted as well as she could.

When Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus had taken the body down, she kissed it with the Mother of God and went with it to the tomb, and when the stone had been rolled to the door the others went away, but she and the Theotokos sat over against the sepulchre. They rested on the sabbath as the Law commanded, and when the first day of the week was dawning the two of them came, not with spices, for there was no time, but only to see the tomb; and that, the life says, is the first going of the women, which Matthew tells. The life goes carefully through all four evangelists' accounts, counting four journeys of hers to the tomb, and telling which company of women each belongs to.

After the Ascension she went out, like an apostle, to preach, and travelled through many countries proclaiming Christ to the heathen, and brought many to the knowledge of God. She went to Rome and stood before Caesar Tiberius, healed him of an eye that was ill and told him all that concerned Christ, and — as some say, the life adds — brought him to believe, so that he turned in anger against Pilate and the chief priests and punished them after their deeds.

From Rome she went to Ephesus, to [Saint John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), and serving there apostolically for the salvation of men she departed to the Lord by a blessed falling asleep. John buried her at the entrance of the cave in which the seven holy youths afterwards slept. Later, in the reign of the wise emperor Leo, her honourable relics were brought from there to Constantinople and laid in the monastery of Saint Lazarus, which he had built, and her memory was kept there.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 22 July, with the same two titles, myrrh-bearer and equal to the apostles. saint.gr tells her course briefly and adds two things the Romanian life does not. She came from Magdala, it says, a town lying west of the sea of Galilee; when she learned of Christ she went to him and was rid of the seven demons that troubled her, and afterwards became his disciple. At the Lord's Resurrection it was she who came first to his tomb with spices, and angels clothed in white announced his rising to her. After the descent of the Holy Spirit she went on serving in the first Christian Church at Jerusalem; then she went to Ephesus, to [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), and there she died. She was buried at the entrance of the cave in which the seven youths of Ephesus later fell asleep. The page lists where her relics are kept: her right hand at the monastery of Dionysiou on Athos, her left incorrupt at Simonopetra, part of her left foot at Esphigmenou, and portions at the monasteries of Saint George Pheneos in Corinthia and of the Forty Martyrs at Sparta, at the Lateran basilica in Rome, at the Roman Catholic basilica of Saint Maximin at Marseille, and at the church of the same name in France which the page spells Vezelau. It gives no year for anything it tells.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 22 July and gives her two words before anything else: a myrrh-bearer, equal to the apostles. She was from the place of Magdala, by the lake of Gennesaret, out of the tribe of Issachar. She was tormented by seven evil spirits, from which the Lord Jesus freed her and made her whole; and she was a faithful follower and servant of the Lord during his earthly life. Magdalene too stood under the cross on Golgotha and grieved bitterly together with the Most Holy Theotokos.

After the death of the Lord she visited his tomb three times; and when the Lord was risen she saw him twice, once by herself and the second time with the other myrrh-bearing women. She travelled to Rome, came before Caesar Tiberius, and handing him an egg dyed red greeted him with the words: Christ is risen.

At the same time she accused Pilate before Caesar for his unjust condemnation of the Lord Jesus. Caesar received her complaint and moved Pilate from Jerusalem to Gaul, where that unjust judge came to his end in the emperor’s disfavour and in a grievous sickness. After that she returned from Rome to Ephesus, to [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), whom she helped in the work of preaching the Gospel; and with great love for the risen Lord and with great zeal she proclaimed the Gospel to the world as a true apostle of Christ.

She came to her end peacefully at Ephesus and was buried, by tradition, in the same cave as the seven youths who were wonderfully put to sleep for hundreds of years, and afterwards came alive, and then died — and here the entry sends the reader to the 4 August. Her relics were later translated to Constantinople. And near the garden of Gethsemane, the entry ends, stands a fine Russian church dedicated to Saint Mary Magdalene.
