# Ignatius of Kios

saint.gr's calendar for 8 Οκτωβρίου keeps a second Ignatius besides the new martyr of 1814, and calls him simply «Άγιος Ιγνάτιος ο Οσιομάρτυρας». Its page says he rose like another sun out of the east, from the renowned city of Kios, an old Greek colony of the Ionians, and that he was born there of pious and virtuous parents who taught him virtue while his teachers taught him the Scriptures. He thought as a young man of renouncing the world, but his love and obedience towards his parents held him back until they died.

When they had fallen asleep he sold all he owned, gave the money to the poor, to widows and to orphans, kept three days' food and no more, and went to the Olympus of Bithynia. There he found the monastery of Saint Auxentius, and the abbot clothed him at once in the great and holy angelic habit, knowing by the Holy Spirit that he would be a chosen vessel and would confess the name of Christ before kings and tyrants. He longed for a martyr's end and confessed that longing to his abbot, who tried to hold him back.

When the war against the holy icons began under Leo III the Isaurian, he left the life of stillness and came home to Kios, where he met the bishop of the city, [Eustathius](/saints/eustathius-of-kios), and learned from him; and the two resolved together to be put to death for the icons. Ignatius then went up to Byzantium, stood before Leo, drew out an icon of Christ the Saviour that he carried in his bosom and kissed it, and was beaten until the ground reddened with his blood. In prison he was forty days without food or drink, and Christ appeared to him in the night to give him courage.

Seeing him unmoved, Leo exiled him to a stony desert islet between Mytilene and Tenedos, with no human help, to die of hardship; fishermen who came there to fish recognised him and brought him rusks and water, for there was none on the island. Leo died of a disease of the bowels, and his son Constantine Copronymus ordered all his father's exiles to remain in exile. Worn out by hardship and old age, Ignatius called the fishermen to bring a priest, communicated of the immaculate mysteries, said «Lord, into your hands I commit my spirit», and fell asleep on the eighth of October.

The priest and the fishermen buried him where he had lived, and each night they saw a heavenly light above his grave. Two men of Kios, kinsmen and friends, came for a portion of his relics for their homeland and found other Christians already dividing them; they took the right forearm, and in a storm on the voyage home they cried «Άγιε Ιγνάτιε βοήθησον ημίν» and saw him at the stern of the boat holding the icon of Christ, and the sea grew calm. They hid the treasure in a shop by the shore, and when Copronymus died they built the shop into the church that stands there now. saint.gr gives no year for any of this, so this folder records none. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.
