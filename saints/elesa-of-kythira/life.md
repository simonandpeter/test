# Elesa of Kythira

Elesa lived in the middle of the fourth century in the Peloponnese, in Greece. She came of a well-off family: her father was a pagan named Helladius, her mother a good Christian named Eugenia. She was born in answer to her devout mother's prayers, for Eugenia was barren and prayed without ceasing that God would give her a child; and one day, as she was praying, she was assured in the depth of her heart that the Lord had had mercy on her and that she would be a mother. Elesa was brought up by her mother in the spirit of the Gospel, and grew with her years in faith and in love towards the Lord.

At fourteen she lost her mother. When her father wanted to marry her to a pagan ruler, she fled to the island of Kythira, where she lived a life full of asceticism and prayer. After a long search her father found her, and beheaded her on 1 August 375. Her body was buried at the place of her martyrdom. A small church was built in her honour at first, and in 1871 a larger one was raised, which stands to this day.

doxologia.ro prints no more of her than that, and no hymn: its page for her closes with the single petition «Sfântă Muceniță Elesa, roagă-te lui Dumnezeu pentru noi!» The calendar's line gives her name in two forms, Elesa and, in a parenthesis, Eliza, and both are kept here as the page prints them.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 1 August, and saint.gr tells the same story with more in it. Her father, it says, was a rich Greek lord but an idolater, named Helladius, while her mother Eugenia was a holy woman of many virtues and rich gifts. Eugenia had no children and begged God to pity her and make her worthy to bear one; and one day, while she was alone in the house and praying, she heard a voice from heaven saying to her, «Σε ελέησε ο Θεός σε ότι του ζήτησες, και σου έδωσε καρπόν κοιλίας» — God has had mercy on you in what you asked of him, and has given you fruit of the womb. They named the child Elesa from that voice, and her mother dedicated her to the Lord and baptised her a Christian.

After her mother's holy repose, when the saint was fourteen, she thought that she could not live with her idolatrous father, who wanted to marry her to a lord; so after much prayer, when she found a suitable opportunity, she gave away many alms to the poor and to orphans and left, with two maidservants of hers, and lived as an ascetic on a mountain of Kythira. Her father searched and found her and tried to bring her back home, and at her refusal pursued her in a rage. Fleeing, she reached the foot of the mountain that is today called the mountain of Saint Elesa, and begged God, «σκίσε γη και κρύψε με» — split, O earth, and hide me. Through the cleft that opened in the mountain she passed and reached the summit, where her father arrived beside himself and beheaded her, on 1 August 375. Her servant buried her at the place of her martyrdom.

The page then gives the shrine's history. The first Christians who came to the island to venerate her tomb raised a little church sunk for the most part into the ground, which pilgrims went down into by five or six steps, with its altar set over the saint's grave; tradition says that in the oldest times pilgrims came from the Mani on 1 August to honour her memory.

That little church survived until 1867 as the property of the Kasimatis-Gerakas family, and in 1871 the present spacious church was raised with the contributions of Christians on the ruins of the old one, which was filled in to level the ground; the sanctuary was built directly over the old church and the new altar over the place of the grave. The first cells went up around it at the same time; in 1945 the church became the parish church of the neighbouring village of Pourko, and in the nineteen-fifties the shrine was enlarged, with a new belfry, a hegumen's house, electric light and, hardest of all, a road.

Saint Elesa and [Theodore of Kythera](/saints/theodore-of-kythera), the page adds, are reckoned patrons of Kythira, and the people believe that the saint has bridled the snakes of Kythira so that they are not venomous. The page ends with a note of its own: «Η μνήμη της συγκεκριμένης Αγίας δεν αναφέρεται πουθενά στους Συναξαριστές, τη βρίσκουμε σαν μάρτυρα μόνο στα Κύθηρα» — the memory of this particular saint is nowhere mentioned in the Synaxaristes; we find her as a martyr only in Kythira.
