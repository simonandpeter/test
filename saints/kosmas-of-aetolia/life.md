# Kosmas of Aetolia

Kosmas was born in 1714 in the village of Taxiarchis, in the district of Apokouro near Mega Dendro in Nafpaktia, of devout parents. At twenty he went to the Holy Mountain to study at the newly founded school at Vatopedi; Konstantinos by baptism, he became a monk at the monastery of Philotheou in 1759 and then a hieromonk, Kosmas.

Knowing the nation to be in danger, he burned to go out and teach the enslaved Greeks the holy letters, but thought himself too humble for it. He went to Constantinople, where his brother Chrysanthos, a teacher, gave him some lessons in rhetoric, and with the permission of Patriarch Seraphim he crossed Greece preaching the word of God: Constantinople first, then Aetoloakarnania, the Dodecanese and Athos, Thessalonica, Veroia and all Macedonia, Himara, southern Epirus, Lefkada, Kefalonia, Zakynthos, Corfu and northern Epirus again. Wherever he passed he built schools and churches, and crowds gathered to hear him.

He was hanged at Kolikontasi in northern Epirus in 1779, after a denunciation, and his body thrown into the river Apsos with a stone about its neck; it floated, was found by the priest Markos and buried at the monastery of the Theotokos at Ardenitsa. The Ecumenical Patriarchate recognised him as a saint on 20 April 1961; Nikodemos the Hagiorite wrote his service and his life, and the Church calls him Equal to the Apostles. Once he told a village: I came and preached to you, so it is right that you pay me for my trouble — not with money, what would I do with it; my payment is that you put the words of God in your heart, to gain eternal life.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 24 August (Julian) as equal-to-the-apostles and hieromartyr: Constantine in the world, of Aetolia, he studied first at home under the archdeacon Ananias Dervishanos and then on Athos, at the Vatopedi school, under the most famous teachers of the day, Nicholas Tzartzoulias of Metsovo and Eugenios Boulgaris, later archbishop of Ekaterinoslav and Kherson; staying on Athos at Philotheou he was tonsured Kosmas and ordained hieromonk, and the longing to set his Christian brothers on the way of salvation and confirm them in the faith led him, with his spiritual fathers’ blessing, to Constantinople, where he mastered the art of eloquence and, with written leave from Patriarch Seraphim II and later Sophronius, preached the Gospel first in the churches of the capital and the villages round it, then in the Danubian principalities, Thessalonica, Veria, Macedonia, Himara, Acarnania, Aetolia, the islands of Santa Maura and Cephalonia and elsewhere — preaching full of the grace of the Spirit, simple, calm and meek, which the Lord confirmed, as for his apostles, with signs and wonders.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps him on the Julian 4 August, twenty days from the 24 August the Russian, Romanian and Greek calendars all give him, and tells the whole of his life in one entry. He was of Aetolia by birth, it says, from the village of Megadendron, which is to say the Great Tree. As a young man he went to the Holy Mountain, and in the monastery of Philotheou he was tonsured.

Driven by a constant longing to preach the Gospel to the people, he went to Constantinople and asked a blessing for it from the patriarch there. Then he went round all the lands along the Danube preaching the Gospel, and stayed longest of all in Albania, where he suffered at the hands of a pasha. He was strangled by the Turks and then thrown into the river, in 1779, in the sixty-fifth year of his life. His wonderworking relics rest in the village of Kolkoidas, in a church of the Holy Mother of God not far from the town of Fier.
