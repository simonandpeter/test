# Ambrose of Milan

Ambrose — the man named with the name of sweetness, as doxologia's life puts it — was born in the great and much-renowned city of Milan, of right-believing and well-born parents. His father, prefect of Gaul, bore the same name and gave it to the son he loved. Later, grown, he lived at Rome with his mother, by then old, and his sister. Having learnt the Scriptures and been trained in good rhetorical speech, he became a famous and powerful orator, and from that came to the government of Milan.

When Auxentius, the Arian bishop of the city, died, the Orthodox and the Arians fell to quarrelling over the vacant see, each side wanting a bishop of its own, and the dispute grew into a tumult. Ambrose came to quiet the crowd, and the people cried out for him instead. Thinking himself unworthy of such a degree, he went out of the church, and then tried to get away by night; the citizens set a watch on him so that he should not escape, and sent to the emperor Valentinian, who commanded that he be given them.

Seeing God's good pleasure in it, he submitted to the emperor's order and the people's wish. He would not be baptised by an Arian bishop but by an Orthodox one, keeping himself carefully from the Arian faith; and being baptised, he passed through all the degrees of the hierarchy in a single week and on the eighth day was made bishop. The emperor was present at his enthronement and gave thanks that the man to whom he had entrusted bodies had been entrusted by God with souls.

As bishop he rebuked emperors to their faces. He would not be moved by the Arian empress Justina, who tried by a shameless woman, by soldiers sent to seize the church, and by a public disputation to break him, and failed at each. He stood in the door against Theodosius after the killing at Thessalonica and shut him out of the church until he had repented, and turned him out of the sanctuary where the emperors of the East had been used to communicate, teaching him that the purple makes an emperor and not a priest; and the life says it wonders at the two of them equally, at the bishop's boldness and at the emperor's obedience. God showed him the relics of the martyrs Protasius and Gervasius, and afterwards those of Nazarius and Celsus, and he laid them in the churches with preaching.

He was a man of great abstinence, a lover of labour, quick-witted, fasting every day but Saturday, Sunday and the feasts of the great martyrs. In his old age he foresaw his departure and told his clergy that he would be with them only until Pascha. Paulinus the priest, who wrote his life, says he saw with his own eyes, while writing at his dictation on the forty-third Psalm, a fire in the shape of a shield go round his head and enter his mouth, and his face turn white as snow. When the deacons whispered by the door about who should be bishop after him and named the presbyter Simplician, he called out three times from his bed, «Bătrân, însă bun» — old, but good. Having prayed and received the holy Gifts, he gave up his soul as the day of Pascha was dawning, and his body was laid in the great church of Milan.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 7 December, and its page gives the years and the career the Romanian life does not. saint.gr has him a distinguished Roman citizen, born in 340, who studied rhetoric, philosophy and law and then practised at Milan as a judge; and it says that he kept the truth in word and in deed and gave out justice impartially although he had not yet been baptised a Christian, which it answers with the word of the Acts that in every nation he who fears God and works righteousness is acceptable to him.

And indeed, the page goes on, Ambrose by his life pleased God, and so God counted him worthy to be baptised a Christian, then to be made a reader, and then, having passed through all the degrees of the Church in a short space of time, to be ordained bishop of Milan by the decision of the emperor Valentinian I. As bishop he shepherded his flock excellently and fought against the heresies; and he would not let the emperor Theodosius enter the church until he had sincerely repented of the killings he had done in the hippodrome of Thessalonica. He died in peace in the year 397, at the age of fifty-seven.
