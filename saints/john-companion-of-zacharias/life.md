# John, companion of Zacharias

saint.gr's calendar for 17 November keeps «Άγιοι Ζαχαρίας ο Σκυτοτόμος και Ιωάννης», and John is the man through whose eyes the story is told. The two venerable men, the page says, are mentioned in a certain narrative, in which John saw [Zacharias](/saints/zacharias-the-cobbler) the cobbler coming out of the church of Hagia Sophia accompanied by divine light; he followed him to his house and learned that although Zacharias was married he lived in virginity and chastity, that he gave half of what his craft earned to the poor, and that he had lived a life dear to God. The page sets the story in the seventh century.

Of John himself it says this much: that he was rich and an officer, but despised worldly things and lived a simple and ascetic life, frequenting the churches every day, and that it was there that he happened to meet the cobbler Zacharias. In the long passage the page quotes from Nikodemos the Hagiorite's synaxarion, John hears Zacharias explain how he divides his wage in two, the first and chief part dedicated to Christ in his poor brothers; and John, amazed at the clean and blessed life of the man, praises him highly, says goodbye, and goes out of the house rejoicing and glad.

He went to the house where he was lodging, the page ends, and gave thanks to God for the wonderful greatness he had seen. The calendar gives him no city, no year of his own and no hymn, and nothing of his life outside this one meeting; the verse over the entry says of the two together that Zacharias and John had a cherubic mind from their vigils.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 17 Νοεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/11/17/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3026/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
