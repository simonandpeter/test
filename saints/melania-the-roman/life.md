# Melania the Roman

Rome, the great city, does not boast, the life begins, nor is it honoured so much for the beauty of its place, for its victories or for its antiquity, as it is rightly glorified for the holy servants of Christ the Master, the venerable and the martyrs, who lived in good works and fought manfully against the enemy; and among these is Saint Melania, who sprang from Christian parents of the right faith and was the granddaughter of that Saint Melania who was called the elder, who visited many holy fathers on the mountain of Nitria and served many out of her own wealth.

In the holy city of Jerusalem the elder Melania laboured thirty-seven years, and like Abraham in her love of strangers gave rest to many Christians who came from the east and the west, from the north and the south; she did much good to the churches and the monasteries, supplied monks and nuns with all they needed, and filled the prisons with her benefactions. In Rome, her own country, she was the cause of salvation to many women and men, guiding them by useful counsel to the way that leads to eternal life. Of such a woman, pleasing to God, this venerable Melania was the granddaughter, born in old Rome of her son, and of a good and most illustrious family, for both her grandfather and her father were among the foremost and richest of the Romans.

Coming of age, Melania wished to keep her virginity, and for that she troubled her parents often with many entreaties; but since they had no other child but her, she was married. She did not change her mind after the wedding: if she could not keep her virginity she would at least live in purity, and so she begged her husband, Apelian, to live with her in continence, or else to let her go and not stand in the way of her purpose. He asked her first for children, and God gave the fruit of her womb a daughter, whom she promised to God for the keeping of virginity as soon as she was born, paying in this way her debt to her marriage.

Seeing her husband unpersuaded, Melania thought of fleeing secretly to some unknown country, leaving father, mother, husband, daughter and all her riches, so taken was she with the divine longing; and counting it a great hardship to be bound by the law of marriage she wore in secret, on her body, a shirt of harsh hair, except at the times when she knew she would be seen by her husband. After a time she conceived again, and when the commemoration of the holy martyr Lavrentie came she spent the whole night without sleep, kneeling and reading psalms.

After that birth she was seized by harder pains and was in danger of death; and her husband, standing before her bed, suffered greatly for her, and, loving her very much and valuing her health above his own, submitted to her will and made a promise in church, before God, that they should live from then on in continence. Melania rose from the bed of pain, and not long after her daughter died too, the fair offspring of her virginity, who had been promised to God; and that death roused Apelian the more.

When Melania's parents heard their resolve they tried to stop them, and the two grieved much over it and took counsel one night how they might be delivered from the snares of the world. But since they had a multitude of goods which they had promised to give to Christ by the hands of the poor, they did not leave the world and their country at once, but chose their dwelling near the city of Rome. When this blessed pair chose that pilgrim life, pleasing to God, Apelian was twenty-four years old and Melania was twenty.

Living such a wonderful life, they sold their properties and shared them out without sparing to those who needed them; and the devil, who hates what pleases Christ, rose against them with malice. But the Lord, who defends his servants and takes them out of the hands of those who oppress them, enlightened the pious empress Verina, who, hearing of their life that was pleasing to God, greatly desired to see them. When they came where she sat and bowed to her as was fitting, the empress, seeing such humility, rose from her throne out of reverence and called them to her side; and leave was given to Apelian and Melania to sell their goods without hindrance.

So much wealth had Melania inherited from her parents that after the emperor there was nobody like them; and from this it is seen how great their riches were, that nobody could buy the houses they had in Rome at their true price — and when these were later burned by the barbarians they were sold for much less. Of one of their estates she used to say herself: «I had a village, and in it a most beautiful palace on a high place, which surpassed all our villages. On one side was the sea, which could be seen from above». Out of the properties they had in Italy their mercies ran like great rivers through the whole world, to the ends of the earth, and they sent much alms everywhere, into Mesopotamia and elsewhere.

Then, leaving very few properties unsold in Italy, they took Melania's mother with them, who was still living, and went aboard ship for Sicily, partly to sell what they had there; and from Sicily they went to Libya and Carthage. On the way a fierce storm rose, and they came to an island which barbarians had fallen on unawares a little before them and plundered, carrying off a multitude of men, women and children. Leaving that island with a gentle wind to help them they came quickly to Carthage, and there, going ashore, they carried on their accustomed work, that is the work of mercy, doing good.

The work of her hands was writing, for she wrote very beautifully and quickly, so that nobody wrote better than she; and the books she wrote, being very good, she ordered to be sold, and the price given away. She laboured much at the reading of the divine Scriptures: when the work of her hands or her writing had tired her she busied herself with reading, and when her eyes grew weak from much reading her ears took over.

After seven years in Carthage she wished to see the holy places at Jerusalem, and went aboard a ship with her mother and with Apelian, who had been her husband after the flesh and was now her brother in the spirit. They wished to go to Egypt as well, to visit and see the fathers of the desert there and to serve them out of their goods, and so they left her mother, who was very old and worn, in the holy city. Going round the cell of one of those fathers and looking at the wealth of his hermit's life, Melania found nothing but a mat, a vessel of water, a little dry bread and a small basket.

From there they came again to Alexandria and then to Nitria, going round everywhere among the hermits' dwellings like bees flying over different flowers and gathering the sweetness of honey; and then they returned to Jerusalem. When her mother died Melania did the service due to the dead and shut herself again in a little cell, narrower and darker, and lived there a year; and afterwards the report of her spread everywhere.

Then her brother in the spirit, who had first been her husband after the flesh, the blessed Apelian, having pleased the Lord in the monastic life, went to God; and Melania did for him what was due. When she travelled by land, wherever she happened to enter a city great honour was given her by everyone, for God glorified her who glorified him, and bishops, priests and others came out to meet her. But what need is there of much telling? For the venerable Melania, partly by herself and partly through others, and above all by God-inspired speech and useful counsels, brought many to salvation.

After this the blessed one returned again to Jerusalem, and as her end drew near she prepared herself for the going out of this life. She had received also the gift of healing sicknesses: to a woman who had a child in her womb, whose time to give birth had come and who could not, because the child had died in her and she was held by great pains so that she was near death, she gave help. Foreseeing her own going to God, she went round all the holy places in Jerusalem and about it, in Bethlehem and in Galilee.

On her last day she was at the accustomed singing of the church and gave the sisters much teaching from morning until evening; then she communicated in the most pure and life-giving Mysteries from the hands of the bishop of Eleutheropolis, who had come with his clergy to visit her. Then, comforting with words her kinswoman, who wept bitterly for her, and the other sisters, and giving them all the last kiss, she said this word: «As the Lord willed, so it is done».
