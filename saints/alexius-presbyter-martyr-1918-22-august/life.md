# Alexius (Naumov)

Alexius Nikolaevich Naumov was born on 6 February 1876 and baptised the
next day in the church of Peter and Paul at Sludka. Leaving the first
class of the Perm seminary in 1894 he was made teacher of the Dobryanka
parish school, passed the examination at the Perm church school in 1895
for the title of parish-school teacher, and taught at Ilyinskoye in the
Perm district; in 1896 he was made supernumerary psalmist at the village’s
church of the Prophet Elijah, keeping his teaching post, and in 1897
senior teacher of its two-class parish school. He married Maria
Filippovna, and they had two children, Demetrius and Olga. In 1899 he was
ordained deacon for the same church, still teaching; in 1900 he moved to
Perm, as psalmist of the St Nicholas church at the Mariinsky girls’
gymnasium and teacher, then senior teacher, at the Stepanov two-class
parish school, keeping the lessons in the Law of God for himself. On 10
July 1910 he was ordained priest for the Trinity church of the village of
Visim in the Perm district, where he ran the parish school, taught the Law
of God and singing, and gave up the teaching in 1915 while keeping the
school and the catechism. In May 1916 he was moved first to Yurla in the
Cherdyn district and then to the fourth priest’s place at the church of
the Archangel Michael of the Ochyor works in the Okhansk district — a
share of 379 households, 1,003 men and 1,072 women — where he also taught
singing at the commercial school and the Law of God at the primary school
across the river, and from 1917 was candidate to the investigator of the
first deanery. In 1918 he was arrested on a denunciation for anti-Soviet
activity: at that time the appeal of Bishop Andronicus (Nikolsky) was
being read in every church, and the Soviet power could not fail to answer
it. On 4 September 1918, after cruel tortures and mockery, he was shot by
Red Army men in the village of Sepych in the Okhansk district. Old
inhabitants of Sepych, Timothy and Eudocia Katayev, tell what their
grandmother saw, being about forty then: the Reds gathered the Orthodox
priests from the whole district, eighteen of them, and forced in many
people from the villages round about, their grandmother among them; the
priests were led to the “lay” cemetery — the upper or new cemetery now —
made to dig their own common grave, given time to pray, and shot, and all
were thrown into that pit and barely covered with earth; some twenty years
ago the Katayevs walked that cemetery and came on a pit, in the middle of
the copse, which may well be the grave. He was numbered among the new
martyrs and confessors of Russia by the Council of Bishops of August 2000,
and the Russian calendar keeps him on 22 August (Julian) and in the
synaxis of the saints of Perm.
