# Nadezhda (Abbakumova)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names her last of the fourteen people it puts on one line for the Julian 1 March, and the only one of them it grades simply a martyr: «мц. Надежды (1938).» Nadezhda Petrovna Abbakumova was born in 1880 in the village of Vanilovo in the Bronnitsy district of the province of Moscow, into a peasant family. In 1899 she married a peasant of the village of Martynovskoye in the same district, Basil Sergeyevich Abbakumov, and moved to him; they had four children. In the civil war her husband was called to the front and killed there, and she had to bring the children up alone.

A woman of deep faith, she was elected to the church council in 1925, and from 1928 she was warden of the church of the Nativity of Christ in Martynovskoye — which at that time meant taking on herself all dealings with authorities hostile to the Church. Until 1932 she still had a small holding, a cow and a horse, and that year the authorities took everything from her for unpaid taxes; in 1933 the court fined her for unpaid taxes and in 1934 fined her again.

In 1936 the priest serving at Martynovskoye was arrested, and she invited the priest of the neighbouring parish, [Peter (Lyubimov)](/saints/peter-lyubimov), to serve there; from that time he had two parishes to look after and she helped him keep the church from being closed. She was arrested together with him on 2 March 1938 and held in the prison at Kashira.

Asked what her connection with the priest consisted in, she said it was the work of the church. Asked what they talked about when they visited one another, she said that when he came to her lodging he said the church had to be kept up through the faithful, and that perhaps in a year or two something would change for the better; asked whether he had meant that the government would change, she said he had said that perhaps a time would come when the church would serve as it had before, and had said nothing about a change of government.

Then she was told that as church warden she had gathered the independent farmers and carried on anti-Soviet agitation among them, that they should not join the collective farm and should not pay their taxes to the state. She answered that she had gathered them and had talked with them about collective farming and about not paying taxes, since the village soviet had taken her own cow for unpaid taxes. She would not admit the agitation: she had only spoken against the collective-farm order, that collective farming was a bad thing. Told she had said the Soviet power robbed the people, she said she had not, and that what she had said was that she would not pay the tax.

On 9 March 1938 the troika of the НКВД sentenced her to be shot, the same day it sentenced the priest. She was shot on 14 March 1938 and buried in a common unmarked grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow. The office this corpus would have written for her is church warden, and there is no such key in the five locale packs, so none is written. The surname is spelt two ways by the two hosts and neither is corrected here: the calendar’s name link and the Сретенский line give «Аббакумова», with two б, and azbyka.ru writes «Абакумова» throughout, in its heading, in its day line and in its life.

azbyka.ru keeps her on 14 марта and on two moveable days besides, the synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered at Butovo and the synaxis of the saints of Moscow, and neither is a fixed day nor an offset from Pascha this corpus can hold, so neither is recorded as a feast.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 1 марта ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260301.html), which names her on one line with thirteen others and prints no life under her name; and the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru for 14 марта, which prints the long life Игумен Дамаскин (Орловский) wrote — [the life](https://azbyka.ru/days/sv-nadezhda-abbakumova); read 5 October 2026.*
