# Alexius (Belkovsky) of Veliky Ustyug

He was born in 1842 in the village of Rozhdestvino in the Kashira district of the Tula province, the son of a priest, and in the world he was Peter Filippovich Belkovsky. His father served more than forty years in one place, in the church of that village, and was ten years rural dean of his district and some twenty years the confessor of his brother priests by their own election; the family was poor, and those who knew the father said that he had never in his life known what luxury or wealth or fame was, and that want had taught him moderation from childhood.

In 1864 Peter Filippovich finished the Tula theological seminary; in 1867 he was ordained priest, and served at the Michael church attached to the Michael children’s home in the town of Tula, and from 1886 at a second church in the same town, the one named for a prince of Novgorod. In 1874 he was appointed teacher of religion in the school of the burghers’ society, and at the hierarch’s services in the cathedral it was he, more often than others, who was given the sermon to preach.

He was consecrated bishop as a vicar of the Vologda diocese and arrived at Veliky Ustyug on 28 September, where the archpriest of the town cathedral greeted him with a speech that said the new bishop was the sixth that vicariate had met in sixteen years, and that the country was cold but its hearts were warm. Like the men before him he gave himself to the schools: in the summer of 1908 teachers’ courses were held at Veliky Ustyug for the parish schools of the vicariate, and opening them he said that true wisdom lies not in the mind alone but in the heart, and not in heaping up knowledge but in gaining virtues. A women’s diocesan school was built while he was bishop, and several churches were built and consecrated, one of the last of them in 1916 at the town prison castle — the prison in which he was to die twenty-one years later.

On 12 October 1916 the vicar of Veliky Ustyug was ruled by the Holy Synod to be styled bishop of Veliky Ustyug and Ust-Vym, and at the beginning of the twenties he was raised archbishop; on 30 July 1923 he took over the government of the Veliky Ustyug diocese, which had by then become independent. In 1924, at the age of eighty-two, he was retired. Living at Veliky Ustyug beside a church, he served the liturgy every day; and when that church was closed he served at two others in the Dymkovo settlement, making his home in a church watchman’s hut and beginning the service at four in the morning before the few who came. So it went on until the beginning of 1937, when infirmity made it hard for him to move and he could walk only with the help of the nuns who lived with him.

He was arrested in the autumn of 1937, when he was ninety-five years old. He could not leave the house at the order of the men of the NKVD, and they carried him out themselves on a sheet. After a short time in prison he died, in November 1937, and was buried in the town cemetery. He was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia at the jubilee council of bishops of the Russian Orthodox Church in August 2000 for veneration by the whole Church.
