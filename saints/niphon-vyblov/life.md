# Niphon (Vyblov)

Niphon was born in 1882 at Yeysk, the son of a poor peasant, and the calendar's life for him is the life of a man the state could find nothing to charge. At ten his parents sent him to the two-class village school in the town, which he finished in 1894; then he helped his father on the land, and when his father died in his seventeenth year he and his younger brother kept the holding between them.

In 1913 he went to the village of Подлесное in the Khvalynsk district of the Saratov province, to a missionary hieromonk well known in those parts who ran the mission school there. He stayed a while, settled in his mind that this was the road he wanted, and entered the men's monastery at Khvalynsk, where he was soon tonsured with the name Niphon and ordained hieromonk. In 1925 the bishop of Volsk appointed him to the church at the village of Березовый хутор, and he served there until the day he was arrested.

On 28 December 1930 the local OGPU, whose object the page states plainly as the closing of every church in the district, sent two policemen to the village for the priest. They found the service going on and went to wait in his house, sending word to the village soviet again and again to ask when it would end — so that the whole village knew what was coming. He had a burial to take after the liturgy and walked the coffin to the cemetery, and came home about two in the afternoon with his own spiritual father, the hieromonk from Khvalynsk, who was visiting him.

The house was searched, the two monks were brought out and told to get on the cart, and a crowd of about forty people, most of them women, gathered and demanded their release. The policemen drew their weapons and made the priests get up on the cart at the threat of shooting; the people shouted that this was robbery and that innocent pastors must be let go; the policemen began writing down the names of the loudest and threatening them with arrest, and a rider was sent to the next village for reinforcements. That broke the crowd, and the two were taken to the prison at Syzran.

**The OGPU could not frame a charge.** Its own officer wrote that no link with the local kulaks had been established, but that bread and milk were brought to him daily and mostly by the better-off, and that the daughter of a kulak deported to the Northern Territory kept house for him, baked his bread and washed his linen — and that this made one think «поп Выблов имел связь с кулацкой частью села». Questioned, he admitted nothing, and of the hieromonk arrested with him said only that he had known him from the Khvalynsk monastery since his youth and had been going to him to make his confession.

He died in the Syzran prison at half past nine in the morning of 30 August 1931, and was numbered among the saints of the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000. **The calendar does not keep him on the day he died**: its line sets him on 10 ноября with the man named beside him, and says only «(1931)».
