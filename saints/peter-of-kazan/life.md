# Peter of Kazan

saint.gr's calendar for 24 Μαρτίου names two martyrs together, and gives each of them a heading of his own and the two of them one sentence: the holy martyrs Peter and Stephen were martyred in the year 1552 in the district of Kazan, by the Mongols. That is the whole of the entry, and this life says no more than the page does. It heads each of them «(; - 1552)», so the year of birth is a question mark for both.

The page says nothing of any kinship between Peter and [Stephen](/saints/stephen-of-kazan), and nothing of their station, their faith before that year, the manner of their deaths or any relic; it prints neither a couplet nor a hymn. Nothing has been taken from any other source and nothing supplied from memory, and nothing here identifies either of them with the several men of their names kept on other days.

Peter suffered at the same time and for the same cause, the turning from Islam to Christ. His own family took him home by force once the troops had gone and called him by his former Muslim name, hoping he would give Christ up; and to all their coaxing he answered that his father and mother were God glorified in Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit — if you believe in them, then you too are my kin; at holy baptism the name Peter was given me, and not the one you call me by. Seeing him unshaken, the family gave him up to the torturers, and he did not stop confessing the name of Christ among the torments until he died, crying out, I am a Christian. He was buried at Kazan where the old church of the Resurrection stood, on the grain market.

The day page carries a troparion and a kondak for the two of them together: a pair of martyrs of one mind who rebuked the unbelief of their own countrymen, most excellent champions of the Trinity, who exchanged the storm of torments and a dreadful death for life eternal and bore their suffering in the city of Kazan. Both are recorded here with their English.
