# Theodora of Alexandria

Theodora came from Alexandria and lived in the years of the emperor Zeno (474–491), married to a devout man, Paphnutius; her life was honest, virtuous and devoted to her husband. But the devil, hater of good, in a moment of weakness pushed her secretly into adultery — no one saw it, no one learned of it, and she could have gone on living in harmony with her husband. When, though, she heard the words of the Gospel in which the Lord teaches that there is nothing hidden which shall not be made manifest (Luke 8:17), she thought on the depth of her sin and wept bitterly — the enemy had told her, the Romanian Lives say, that a sin done in the dark, which the sun does not see, God does not know; and when she learned that nothing is hidden before God, with what great repentance she set herself right! She dressed as a man, went to a monastery and was tonsured with the name Theodore, and there repented and wept for her sin day and night. After two years she was slandered with having fornicated with a woman, when a newborn baby was brought to the monastery door; Theodora took the infant and for seven whole years, outside the monastery and in every hardship, raised it as her own. When she returned to the monastery her worn-out body gave up its spirit after a little, and then the monks, when — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here; its verse says she made both her habit and her mind manly, and before the end shamed the great mind. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 11 September with a troparion and kontakion.
