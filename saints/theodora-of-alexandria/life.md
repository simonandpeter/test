# Theodora of Alexandria

Theodora came from Alexandria and lived in the years of the emperor Zeno (474–491), married to a devout man, Paphnutius; her life was honest, virtuous and devoted to her husband. But the devil, hater of good, in a moment of weakness pushed her secretly into adultery — no one saw it, no one learned of it, and she could have gone on living in harmony with her husband.

When, though, she heard the words of the Gospel in which the Lord teaches that there is nothing hidden which shall not be made manifest (Luke 8:17), she thought on the depth of her sin and wept bitterly — the enemy had told her, the Romanian Lives say, that a sin done in the dark, which the sun does not see, God does not know; and when she learned that nothing is hidden before God, with what great repentance she set herself right! She dressed as a man, went to a monastery and was tonsured with the name Theodore, and there repented and wept for her sin day and night.

After two years she was slandered with having fornicated with a woman, when a newborn baby was brought to the monastery door; Theodora took the infant and for seven whole years, outside the monastery and in every hardship, raised it as her own. When she returned to the monastery her worn-out body gave up its spirit after a little, and then the monks, when they discovered her sex, marvelled, and all together glorified God; its verse says she made both her habit and her mind manly, and before the end shamed the great mind. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 11 September with a troparion and kontakion.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, tells it shortly. The wife of a young husband, she was talked into adultery with another man by a sorceress, and at once her conscience began to gnaw at her. She cut her hair, put on men’s clothes and entered the men’s monastery of Oktodekatos under a man’s name, Theodore, where her labour, fasting, vigils, humility and tearful repentance amazed the brethren. Slandered by a loose girl as the father of her child, she would not defend herself, taking the slander as God’s punishment for her old sin; driven out, she spent seven years in the forest and the wilderness, nursing the girl’s child besides, and overcame every temptation of the devil — she would not bow to Satan, would not take food from a soldier’s hand, would not heed her husband urging her to come back to him, for all of it was the devil’s phantasm and vanished like smoke when she signed herself with the cross.

After seven years the abbot took her back into the monastery, where she lived two more years and reposed; only then did the monks learn that she was a woman, and an angel appeared to the abbot and explained everything. Her husband came to the burial and stayed in his former wife’s cell until his death. She tamed wild beasts, healed sicknesses and brought water up in a dry well; so God glorified a true penitent, who for nine years repented of a single sin. She reposed, the Prologue says, in 490.
