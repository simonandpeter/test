# John (Vasilyev)

John, Ivan Ivanovich Vasilyev, was born on 18 June 1876 in the village of Malaya Kiselenka in the Novotorzhok district of Tver province, into a devout peasant family whose father, against the barriers of class, got him into the Tver seminary; he finished it in 1898 and was ordained priest in 1901 to the church at Osipovo in the same district, where he served without a break, later as archpriest, until 1928. That year he was moved to strengthen the parish of Talozhnya, where the life of the church had been let slide; he served often, in the church and in parishioners’ houses, preached at every service, and filled the church again.

When the one Communist in the place moved at the village meeting in 1929 to abolish the feasts of Elijah and the Transfiguration, the churchwarden Anthony Kuzmin and the parishioners resolved to keep them, and Father John served as before; in 1930 they saved the church from closure with signatures, and when a tax was laid on the building that no one could pay, the parish collected it in half an hour, the poorest woman giving five roubles first.

On 11 March 1931 he was arrested with five of the most active parishioners, and on 18 July 1931 the OGPU troika sentenced him and Anthony Kuzmin to five years of exile in Kazakhstan. He served the term to the day and came home to the Tver diocese in the middle of 1936, and was blessed to serve at the church at Yakonovo. On 5 August 1937 the chairman of the Yakonovo village soviet, at the NKVD’s demand, asked for measures against him for agitation, and he was arrested that day and taken to the Tver prison; only local officials would bear witness against him, and none of the peasants. He was questioned on 13 August after being starved and kept from sleep; on 29 September the NKVD troika sentenced him to death, and he was shot on 1 October 1937.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 18 September old style, and he is also remembered on 26 January with the new martyrs.
