# John of Spetses

John came from Spetses and was the brother of [Stamatios](/saints/stamatios-of-spetses); their father was Theodore Ginis and their mother Anezo. In 1822 the two set out for Constantinople with five other sailors who lived by trade, among them [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-of-spetses), the old captain. A violent sea drove them ashore opposite Chios at a place on the Asia Minor coast called Çeşme, and there the Christian they paid to help mend their boat handed them to the agha instead.

Soldiers ran them down: two of the seven were killed, two escaped by sea, and the brothers and Nicholas were taken. Nicholas was beheaded at once on the plain outside the castle; the brothers were put in prison, and for seven days on end the Turks tried by every method to make them turn Muslim, and failed. They got a written confession out to the Metropolitan of Chios, who sent his blessing back and arranged, by a woman, that they should receive Communion.

Brought again before the pasha they declared their faith in Christ unshaken, and on the road to the execution they cried out to the crowd that they were Christians and were going to their death for Christ. John was beheaded at twenty-two years old. saint.gr prints no hymn for the three of them.
