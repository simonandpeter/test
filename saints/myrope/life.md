# Myrope

Myrope was born in the city of Ephesus to Christian parents and was enlightened with holy baptism after her father had departed this life. Her mother raised her in the fear of God, and she loved to go often to the tomb of the holy martyr [Hermione](/saints/hermione-daughter-of-philip), one of the daughters of the Apostle [Philip](/saints/philip-the-apostle), from whose holy relics there flowed myrrh full of healings; and taking that myrrh and giving it to the sick, Myrope healed them. When Decius reigned and the persecution of Christians began, her mother took her to the island of Chios, where she had an inheritance, and they shut themselves in a house and prayed to God.

The governor Numerian came once to that island, and the blessed [Isidore](/saints/isidore-of-chios) was taken there, a wonderful and reverent man who held the rank of a soldier. Because he kept the faith in Christ and refused the worship of idols, the governor tormented him with every kind of torture and then killed him with the sword, and threw his body into a ravine to be eaten by beasts and birds, setting a watch at a distance so that Christians should not steal the martyr's body.

Moved by divine zeal, Myrope went by night with her servants, took the body away in secret and laid it with honour in a marked place. When the governor learned that Isidore's body had been stolen he put the guards in irons and ordered them carried through the whole island and questioned, saying that if they did not find the stolen body by the day appointed they would lose their heads. Seeing them tormented in chains, bound and dragged about, and hearing what awaited them, Myrope was pricked in her soul and gave herself up.

At her words the proud governor was moved to anger and ordered her beaten with rods without mercy, and she was beaten long; then she was dragged by the braids of her hair through the whole island, all her limbs being torn, and shut up in prison, being close to death. At midnight, as she prayed, a light shone through the whole prison, and a company of angels stood before her, and in the midst of them Isidore, all of them singing the thrice-holy hymn. Isidore looked at the martyr and said: "Peace to you, Myrope; your prayers have been heard by God, for behold, you shall be with us and shall receive the crown prepared for you."

At that word she gave up her spirit to the Lord, rejoicing, and the prison was filled with an unspeakable sweet fragrance; the guards smelled it and were afraid and wondered. One of them, having seen and heard all of it, ran to the priest and told him everything in detail, and received holy baptism, and afterwards obtained a martyr's end. The Christians took Myrope's body and buried it with honour in a marked place, glorifying God.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, and saint.gr gives the story with its places in it. She was one of the bravest of the women who were martyred in the first years of the Christian Church; she lived in the middle of the third century and was born in the city of Ephesus. She lost her father early and was brought up in the faith of Christ by her mother, who came of Chios; and it is said that at Ephesus she went daily to the tomb of Saint Hermione, the daughter of the apostle Philip, and took the myrrh that welled from it and gave it out in abundance to the faithful, which is how she came to be called Myrope.

When Decius loosed his savage persecution she and her mother left Ephesus for Chios, where they did good to the poor and the sick. But the persecution spread to peaceable Chios too, and among its victims was Saint Isidore, whose body the idolatrous ruler Numerius ordered to be left unburied. Myrope slipped past the guard with her maidservants, took the body and buried it with great reverence; and when Numerius ordered the guard severely punished for it, the news shook the justice-loving and truthful girl, so that she came forward without hesitation and told the ruler the whole truth.

Numerius, unmoved by her courage and full of anger and revenge, ordered her punished to death. The soldiers laid her on the ground and beat her hard with thick rods, and her manliness bore the deadly wounds without a cry and without losing heart. Of good courage by the divine grace, she stayed unshaken in the faith, and a little afterwards gave up her spirit in the prison, going up to the immortal dwellings of the righteous. A piece of her relic, the page says, is in the chapel of Saint Xenia the Russian at Mandra in Attica.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 2 December and gives her the year the other two calendars withheld. She was born at Ephesus of Christian parents, and after her father died she moved with her mother to the island of Chios, where she suffered for Christ. Her passion came soon after the passion and death of [Isidore](/saints/isidore-of-chios) the soldier, whom this same book keeps on 14 May: when his torturers had beheaded him, Myrope took his body away in secret, censed it and buried it honourably in a place of her own choosing.

The wicked prince Numerian heard that the martyr’s body had been stolen and meant to put the guards to death for it. Learning that innocent men were to suffer for what she had done, she presented herself to the authorities and confessed that she had taken the body and buried it. At the prince’s order she was savagely scourged over her whole body and then, one wound from end to end, thrown into prison.

At midnight a heavenly light filled the prison and a host of angels appeared with Isidore in the midst of them. Peace be to you, Myrope, he said; your prayer has come before God, and soon you will be with us and will receive the crown prepared for you. She rejoiced, and in that hour gave up her spirit to God, and a sweet fragrance came out of her body and filled the whole prison. One of the guards, seeing and smelling all this, believed in Christ, was baptised, and soon received a martyr’s death himself. The book says she passed into eternity in the year 251.

The Russian calendar keeps her on the Julian 2 December and its life orders the story differently from the Greek. She was born at Ephesus at the beginning of the third century, lost her father early, and was brought up in the Christian faith by her mother. Visiting the tomb of the martyr [Hermione](/saints/hermione-daughter-of-philip), daughter of the apostle [Philip](/saints/philip-the-apostle), she took myrrh from her relics and healed the sick with it; and in the persecution of Decius, which it dates 249 to 251, she withdrew with her mother to the island of Chios and spent her time in fasting and prayer.

When the cruel governor of the island had the soldier [Isidore](/saints/isidore-of-chios) tortured to death, she took his body secretly and buried it. The soldiers who had been ordered to keep Christians from the body were condemned to die for its loss; she took pity on the condemned men and told the soldiers herself, and then the governor, what she had done, and before the court confessed herself a Christian. For that she was cruelly tortured and thrown into prison.

At midnight a light shone in the prison: Isidore appeared, ringed with angels, and they received her soul, about the year 251. The prison filled at once with fragrance, and the pagan watchman, shaken by what he had seen, told a priest of it, was baptised, and died a martyr himself for confessing Christ. That last man the other books do not carry, and this calendar gives him no name.
