# Procopius of Jerusalem, son of Theodosia

Procopius grew up in Jerusalem, which the pagans of that time called not Jerusalem but Aelia, after the emperor Hadrian, whose birth-name was Aelius and who rebuilt the ruined city under his own name and forbade the old one, hating the Lord and wishing the place of His passion forgotten. His parents did not name him Procopius but Neanias; Procopius he was called afterwards by Christ Himself at his baptism. His father Christopher, a senator's family and a Christian, died a martyr's death soon after the boy was born; his mother [Theodosia](/saints/theodosia-mother-of-procopius), left a widow, raised him in the Greek religion and was a zealous servant of the idols herself.

He went quickly through the whole of the outward Greek learning, and when he was grown his mother took him to Antioch, where Diocletian then was, and put him into the imperial service. The emperor loved the handsome, quick young man, kept him near him in the palace, and before long made him a commander and sent him with troops to Alexandria to persecute, torture and kill the Christians there and take their goods for the treasury. Neanias said the Christians were obstinate people who would sooner die than leave their Christ, and thought they would be hard to bend; Diocletian answered with blasphemies against the crucified.

On the road past Apamea in Syria, at the third hour of the night, the earth shook with lightning and thunder and a voice from heaven asked him where he was going and against whom he was rising. A cross shone in the air as of crystal, and the voice from it said, I am Jesus the crucified, the Son of God; and when Neanias objected that the Jews had mocked and killed Him, it answered that He had suffered all of it willingly, and that if He were not the Son of God He would not be alive after death and speaking to him. By this sign you shall overcome your enemies, said the voice, and My peace shall be with you.

At Scythopolis he had a goldsmith make him a cross in the likeness of the one he had seen. When it was finished three figures appeared on it, marked by an unseen hand — Emmanuel in Hebrew letters above, Michael and Gabriel at the sides — and the goldsmith, trying to scrape them off, found his hand withered. From then on Neanias took arms not against Christians but against the barbarians, and with that cross he drove off the raiders who were carrying away the people of his own city, killing six thousand of them and losing not one man.

His mother met him with joy and told him to thank her gods for the victory. He took her into the room of the gold and silver idols and asked them who had helped him in the war, and they were silent; and when she knelt and asked them herself and got no answer, he threw off his outer garment, put her aside, broke them in pieces, trampled them, and gave the gold and silver to the poor. Theodosia went to Diocletian at Antioch and denounced her own son, and the emperor wrote to Justus, governor of Palestine, to bring him back to the gods by persuasion or by torture.

Neanias tore the letter for the blasphemies in it, threw down the belt of his office before the governor and said that he offered himself as the sacrifice to his God. He was taken to Caesarea in Palestine, hung up naked and torn with iron claws until his bones showed; and when some of the crowd wept for his youth he told them to weep instead for their own souls. At midnight, in the prison, the earth shook, a great light shone, the doors opened, the prisoners' bonds fell off, and the Lord Himself appeared, healed his wounds, baptised him with water and said, From now on you shall not be called Neanias but Procopius.

Brought out next day with his body whole and white, he offered to go to the temple so that it might be seen which God had healed him. The governor, thinking he would sacrifice, had the way spread with carpets and a herald proclaim the news; but Procopius made the sign of the cross over the idols and bade them fall, and they fell with a terrible noise and all their substance turned to water, which ran out of the doors like a stream. That night two companies of soldiers with their tribunes Nicostratus and Antiochus asked to be made Christ's soldiers; he led them out to the hidden bishop Leontius to be baptised, and returned to the prison himself. They were beheaded the next day, and Procopius was brought out in chains to watch.

Twelve noblewomen then came to his prison window and said that they too were servants of Christ, and were shut in with him and taught by him. When they were brought to trial and tortured, Theodosia came to watch, and seeing women bear so much she wept, and then stood out before the governor and cried that she too was a servant of the Crucified. She was imprisoned with them, and Procopius, who had prayed always for her, rejoiced; she tended the women's wounds, being skilled in medicine, and he took her by night to bishop Leontius to be baptised. All of them were beheaded outside the city. Justus went home from his failure, fell into a fever and died that night.

After Justus came a harsher governor, Flavian, and Procopius was tried again. At the last a flame came out of the furnace they had heated and burnt the pagans standing round, while the fire turned to coolness for him, and the governor fled; and the citizens shouted for him to be destroyed before the whole city should be won over. He asked for time to pray, stood facing east with his hands and eyes raised and prayed for the city, for the people, for those in danger, for the weak, the orphans and the widows, and above all that the pagans should come quickly to the right faith and the Church of Christ grow and shine to the end of the age. A voice from heaven promised him what he asked and called him to the kingdom, and he bent his head gladly under the sword on the eighth day of July. Believers came by night, anointed his body with spices, wrapped it in a clean shroud and buried it with honour.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 8 July, and calls him great martyr. saint.gr says he lived and grew up at Jerusalem in the years when Diocletian was emperor of the Romans; that his father Christopher was a devout man, unlike his mother [Theodosia](/saints/theodosia-mother-of-procopius), who believed in the idols. After his father's death his mother took him to the emperor, who made him governor of the city of the Alexandrians and ordered him to hunt down and torture the Christians; and so he set out for Alexandria. On the road, the page says, lightning and thunder suddenly began to fall, and at the same time he heard a voice calling him by his name, threatening him with death because he was about to persecute the Christians and the True God with them. He begged the one who spoke to reveal himself further, so that he might see who it was; and then a cross of crystal appeared before him and a voice was heard saying, «Ἐγὼ εἶμαι ὁ Ἐσταυρωμένος Υἱὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ» — I am the crucified Son of God. After that wonder he believed and became a Christian; and a little later, returning from a victorious campaign, his mother tried to persuade him to sacrifice to the idols, understood that he had become a Christian, and betrayed him to the emperor.
