# Simon (19 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 19 Νοεμβρίου names «Όσιος Σίμων», glosses the name as a Hebrew word for one obedient to God and kind to men, and then says plainly where the commemoration comes from: he is unknown to the synaxaristae and to the Menaia, and his memory stands in the Paris Codex 1621, of the thirteenth century. For the narrative the page prints a passage in older Greek which it credits to the Hagiology of S. Eustratiades, page 425.

That passage says he was a monk from Calabria, of a great and marvellous monastery. Some of the brethren were once sent out on an errand by the sea, were seized by Turkish pirates and carried off to Africa. Simon went to buy the captive brothers back; and when he had found them and was asking after them in distress, a Hagarene came up and stretched out his hand to strike the holy man, and the hand withered. A second man came, and the same thing happened to him.

The others then took Simon to the ruler of the place and told him what had happened. The ruler was astounded, and begged the holy man to restore the soldiers' withered hands by prayer; and when that was done, he gave orders that the captive monks be carried home with honour. On the voyage, when the drinking water ran out, Simon's prayer turned the sea water sweet, which moved the unbelievers to wonder. He worked other miracles himself, the passage ends, and fell asleep in peace.
