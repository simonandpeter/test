# James (13 June)

saint.gr keeps him under a line that is itself the story: «Όσιος Ιάκωβος ο εξ απάτης τον διάβολον προσκυνήσας», the venerable James who by a deception did reverence to the devil. Its notice says that this venerable man renounced the world after distributing all his goods to the poor, and gave himself to the preaching of the divine word, to severe fasts and to vigils; and that the people, seeing him achieve so many virtues, praised him greatly. Instead of shutting his ears to those dangerous praises he forgot the words of Scripture, that God sets himself against the proud and that whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and so he fell into the exceedingly dangerous passion of pride.

The devil did not lose the chance. One night he appeared to James as an angel and told him that he fell short of the apostle Paul in nothing, and that for his great holiness God himself would visit him the next night. James, blinded by pride, believed it, and made ready with incense and candles and waited for God. Sure enough, in the silence of the night a noise was heard; James opened his door and bowed down. But when he lifted his head he saw the hideous face of Satan mocking him — and at once he made the sign of the cross, and the demon vanished.

In the morning he confessed his mishap with tears to a certain elder, who sent him to a coenobium. There he worked as a cook for seven years with much humility and obedience. Afterwards he shut himself for another seven years in his cell, where he did handwork and kept his rule with great reverence. Repenting thus sincerely of what he had done, he became a pattern of humility, and God rewarded him with the gift of working wonders. The page's own couplet puts the moral first: there is no worse passion than self-conceit, for by it the enemy deceived James.
