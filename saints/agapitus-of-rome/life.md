# Agapitus of Rome

saint.gr says he was the fifty-eighth in the line of the popes of Rome and was called to the pontifical throne in 535. He was of an ascetic cast, it says, and a man of great virtue. On account of the heresy of the monophysites he went to Constantinople, where he deposed the patriarch Anthimus I of Trebizond for the unsoundness of his opinions, and in a synod under his own presidency had the presbyter Menas made patriarch, a most devout and Orthodox clergyman. At Constantinople he was given a great reception by the emperor Justinian, by the clergy and by the people.

In the Synaxaristai, the page goes on, two stories of him are found. On his journey he found at a Greek harbour a man dumb from his birth and unable to walk; the sight was pitiable and the pope's heart had mercy on the sufferer. By his touch alone he gave him back the health of his feet, then put holy Communion in his mouth and loosed the bonds of his tongue as well. The other wonder he did at Constantinople, healing a blind man he met at the Golden Gate.

Pope Agapitus died in 536 at Constantinople, a year after his enthronement, and his body was buried in the church of the Holy Apostles, where his synaxis used to be kept. The page's couplet is a dying exchange: «Θνῄσκων τὶ κράζεις; Σῶτερ, ἠγάπησά σε. Ἀλλ' ἠγαπήθης, Ἀγαπητέ, καὶ πλέον» — dying, what do you cry? Saviour, I have loved you. But you were loved, Agapitus, and more.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and gives him three sentences, the first of them an errand the other page does not mention. He was sent, it says, by Theodotus, king of the Goths, to the emperor Justinian at Constantinople, to turn the emperor from his campaign against the Goths; on the road he healed a mute man and a blind man; at Constantinople he helped to establish Orthodoxy; and he died in 536. The year agrees exactly with the year this folder already held, so nothing was added and nothing displaced. The two reasons for the journey — an embassy for the Gothic king here, the monophysite heresy on the Greek page — both stand, and nothing was chosen between them.

**The Russian calendar adds the year he came to the throne and three things the other sources read for this folder do not have.** He was a zealous champion of Orthodoxy, it says, who earned universal respect by his devout life and was raised to the papal throne in 535. The Gothic king Theodoric the Great — the page's own attribution — sent him to Constantinople to negotiate a peace. On the road he met a man who was lame and dumb, and healed him of the lameness; and after the man had received the holy Mysteries he spoke. At Constantinople he healed a blind beggar.

A local council was sitting in the city at that time. He took part in it and zealously defended the Orthodox teaching against the heretic Severus, who taught that the body of the Lord Jesus Christ was subject to corruption as a human body is; and he died at Constantinople in 536.
