# Paul (Ansimov)

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 9 ноября ст. ст. carries eleven names on one line and he is the ninth of them: «Сщмчч. Парфения , еп. Ананьевского, Константина , Димитрия , Нестора , Феодора , Константина , Виктора , Илии , Павла пресвитеров, Иосифа диакона и прмч. Алексия (1937).» One year stands for all eleven, «Сщмчч.» heads the ten clergy and «прмч.» the monk who closes the line, and the grades it prints sort them into a bishop, eight presbyters, a deacon and a hieromonk. The name link behind his place supplies the calendar's own nominative with the surname the line leaves out, «Павел (Ансимов), протоиерей, сщмч.», and that is where the name form in this folder is read from.

**days.pravoslavie.ru gives this company nothing but that line.** Each of the eleven name pages carries a title, a list of days and no text at all, and not one of them has a `/Life/` link of any kind; the day page's hymns are the two martyrs' of 284–305, the venerable Matrona's, the Mother of God's before the Скоропослушница icon and the wonderworker of Aegina's, and nothing is sung under any of these eleven names. The life below is azbyka.ru's, read in the in-app browser because that host answers no fetcher, and the paragraphs are this corpus's own.

He was born on 24 August 1891 in the village of Chetyre Bugra in the Astrakhan country, the son of a priest. He finished the Astrakhan diocesan school in 1906 and the Astrakhan theological seminary in 1911, where he studied beside [Peter (Nikotin)](/saints/peter-nikotin), afterwards ordained priest, with whom he kept up a friendship all his life. In 1912 he married the daughter of a priest who was rector of one of the Astrakhan churches and was himself ordained priest; and wanting still to deepen his theology he entered the Kazan Theological Academy and finished it in 1918, in the ruin of the state and the beginning of the persecution.

In 1921 he was invited to serve in the church of the Dormition at the Cossack village of Ladozhskaya on the Kuban. Famine had come to the southern provinces and the priest's family had much to bear before they reached it: there were times when they came to a village and his wife stood begging at the church before they moved on. In 1923 the church at Ladozhskaya was seized by the Renovationists and soon closed, and he had to leave; his father-in-law wrote from Moscow that there was a place for a regent in a church at Cherkizovo, and from 1924 he served there as regent.

In 1925 a priest's place fell vacant at the church of the Entry on Vvedenskaya square in Moscow and he was appointed to it. For several years the godless, using the workers of an electric-lamp factory, pressed for the liquidation of that church on the ground that they needed a club and a club could be made of it; in 1929 they succeeded, and the church was plundered and its building given over. In 1930 he was appointed to the church of St Nicholas in Pokrovskoye, which had a community of sisters from the closed Moscow convents attached to it.

On 23 June 1930 he was arrested for having organised that sisterhood and shut in the Butyrka prison. An officer of the OGPU offered him his freedom if he would leave the service of the Church — «мы вас возьмем счетоводом, молодые нам нужны, а вы грамотный, молодой» — and when he refused he was badly beaten. On 11 August the investigator concluded that the charge had not been borne out, and on 12 August he was released and went back to his church; for zealous and blameless service he was raised archpriest.

**The second arrest was an examination exercise.** On the night of 28 December 1930 two cadets whose supervisor at the OGPU had told them to arrest some priest, search him and perform the legal acts needed, took him for their specimen: they searched the flat thoroughly, opened the icon cases and turned the icons over, and then told him to say goodbye to his family. He was taken to the Butyrka prison. The ground for it was an informer's note of another priest's talk in which his name happened to be mentioned. He was released on 18 January 1931, and standing before the icons at home he said: «Я теперь понял, что Господь дает мне некоторое время еще послужить».

That year the church of St Nicholas in Pokrovskoye was closed and its destruction begun. Workmen drove up and set about taking the cross off the dome; they threw ropes over it and rocked it, and for a long while it would not give, and the rain that began to fall enraged them because now they had to get wet on the roof. At last they wrenched it out, and it hung swinging upside down with the raindrops running off it like tears before it fell. The building was afterwards made into a bread factory.

From 1932 to 1935 he served in the church of the Resurrection at the Semyonovskoye cemetery until it was closed, and then in the church of the Nativity of Christ at Izmailovo. Many young people helped him there willingly, and he wrote them small notes of comfort; one of them, to a girl of the parish in 1936, prays that the communion of the Holy Mysteries may renew her soul and make it clean and young, and signs itself «Грешный молитвенник и доброжелатель протоиерей Павел Ансимов». Asked by parishioners what happiness was, in a time when everything before their eyes was falling, he said: «Счастье – это способность человека радоваться всему, что дает ему Господь».

In 1937 informers reported that he allowed anti-Soviet readings in his sermons. The indictment made him the head of a widely branched counter-revolutionary fascist group of churchmen in the city of Moscow and charged him with supplying its members with Nilus's forgery of the Protocols and with spreading false rumours of a persecution of religion in the USSR. He was arrested on 2 November 1937 and shut in the Butyrka prison. He said his attitude to the Soviet power was loyal, denied having held secret services and baptisms in private flats, and closed with «Все предъявленные мне обвинения я отрицаю».

On 19 November 1937 a troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on 21 November 1937 and buried in an unmarked common grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow. **The calendar keeps him on 9 ноября ст. ст., which is the civil 22 November, and his life has him shot on 21 November 1937** — the day before, 8 ноября old style. The difference is one day and not the thirteen of the style conversion, so it is neither a second feast nor an arithmetic slip that could be read away: five of the eleven died on the 22nd and six on the 21st, and the calendar holds all eleven on the one day. The attestation's day is the day page that listed him, as the brief requires, and the death above is the date his own life gives.

**He is given no relation to the ten others the line names.** Four of them — the company of Semipalatinsk — are tied to one another by their own pages and to nobody else; of the six that are not, five lives end at Butovo on 21 November 1937 and no one of them names another, under sentences passed by troikas sitting on different days over different cases. A shared line, a shared class, a shared year and even a shared grave are not a company unless a page sentences the men together, and none of these pages does.
