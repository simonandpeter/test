# Luke of Mytilene

Luke was born at Adrianople in Thrace. His father died when he was six, which threw his mother into poverty, and she entrusted the boy to a merchant of Zagora who was to raise him and teach him his trade. Luke went with the merchant to Russia on a trading journey and then settled with him in Constantinople, where the merchant kept a shop. There, still a boy, he quarrelled with a Muslim lad and beat him in front of the boy's mother's house; when other Muslims ran after him to punish him he shouted that they should let him go and he would become a Muslim, and they stopped.

A wealthy Muslim then adopted him and provided for him, but in time his conscience began to trouble him. He asked his old master for help, and the Russian embassy sent its deputy to visit the aga who had adopted him and ask for the boy back. The aga answered that Luke had become a Muslim of his own will, unforced, and could not be given back; and fearing a further inquiry he had him circumcised by force. The ambassador said he could do nothing unless the boy fled the house.

Within days Luke escaped to Galata, where his old master and a servant of the embassy took him at once, made him put off his Muslim clothes for Christian ones, and put him aboard a ship for Smyrna; from there he reached the island of Thera, where he stayed a while. Falling ill, he went to a spiritual father and disclosed his whole past, and was counselled to go to the Holy Mountain, where he could be out of danger and could see to the saving of his soul.

He went, and was received first at the Great Lavra to serve in the guest-house for a time. At Iviron he told the abbot that he had never been received back into the Orthodox Church, and was sent to the Skete of St John the Forerunner, where the fathers received him back to the Orthodox faith. He returned to Iviron, and then went to Stavronikita, where he was blessed to wear the monastic habit. Finding no settled place in any of the monasteries, he went out into the world, found no peace there either, and came back to Athos.

At the Skete of St Anne the elder Bessarion received him with much love, and Luke told him of his fall and of his struggle and longing to confess Christ. The elder first tried to discourage him, setting the torments and the death before him and urging him rather to be saved by the monastic way; but Luke insisted, and was sent to a confessor of the skete, Father Ananias, who praised his courage and his desire, showed him plainly what he would have to bear, and — since he was resolved — put him to prepare himself and to be tried for a season in hard spiritual struggle, with the elder Bessarion's prayers.

Later, with his confessor's blessing, he went to the island of Lesbos, to the village of Pamphila, where he received a piece of the bloodied shirt of the new martyr [Theodore](/saints/theodore-hadji-of-mytilene), and then to the island's chief town. There he stood before the kadi and said that at thirteen he had been deceived into their faith, being a child and unable to tell truth from falsehood; that now, come to the age of understanding, he had learned their religion was darkness, and he renounced it before them and confessed the Christian faith he had held before.

The kadi said he was sorry for him and warned him of torments he had never heard of. Luke answered that he had thought beforehand of every torment he could be put to, and had come anyway. In prison he was seen by the metropolitan, who had been summoned to the town with several priests and the elders because a Muslim family had secretly left the island to become Orthodox; Luke bowed his head and asked his blessing, at which the Muslims guarding him fell on him and beat him without mercy. The metropolitan afterwards ordered prayers in every church of the island for «the servant of God Luke», and sent him Holy Communion in prison by a man he trusted.

On Sunday, 23 March, he was brought out of prison and tempted a last time to go over to Islam. Refusing, he was hanged in the Greeks' market-place of Mytilene. He was nineteen. His body was kept on the gallows three days, and the martyr seemed to be sleeping, giving off a sweet fragrance that everyone passing by could smell. Then it was taken down and thrown into the sea, so that the Orthodox should not have it; but the sea cast it ashore, the Christians buried it in secret, and his grave became a spring of miracles and healings.
