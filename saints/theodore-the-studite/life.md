# Theodore the Studite

There was at Constantinople a man of good family and rich, named Photinus, whose wife was called Theoctista, and both were believing and God-fearing. They bore this blessed Theodore, baptised him after the Christian custom, brought him up with care and gave him to the learning of letters. Those were the years of the heresy that fought the holy icons, while the unjust emperor Constantine Copronymus reigned and there was a great persecution of the faithful; and for that reason Photinus, leaving the city and the rank he held from the emperor, took counsel with his wife and gave away all they had, renounced the world, and both took the monastic life and laboured well in it to the end.

Theodore, having learned the Greek wisdom, became an orator and a chosen philosopher, disputing with the heterodox for the true faith; and he was so skilled in the divine Scriptures and in the dogmas that the heretics could never get the better of him.

When Copronymus the ill-believing emperor died, his son Leo came after him and followed his father's bad faith; but he did not reign long, and soon lost both the empire and his life. After him Irene his wife came to the throne together with her son Constantine. She being of the true belief — as her name, which means peace, declares — brought peace to the Church and stilled the disorder: she called a multitude of venerable Fathers and, together with the most holy Patriarch [Tarasius](/saints/tarasius-of-constantinople), gathered at Nicaea the Seventh Council of the whole world, which cast out the teachings of the unbelieving and decreed again that the divine icons should be honoured and venerated as before.

Three hundred and sixty Fathers were gathered there, and among them the consecrated Plato, who had lived before that on Mount Olympus, and whose memory, the life says, is kept on the fifth day of April. He was Theodore's uncle on his mother's side, and Theodore his nephew; and Plato had the Spirit of God living in him and was of use to all, being skilled in the divine Scriptures and pleasant in speech.

When the Council was over, Plato took with him the blessed Theodore, and with him two of his brothers, Joseph and Euthymius, who wished to take the monastic life; and going with them he came to a place called Sakkoudion, a place very beautiful and well suited to those who wished to live in stillness.

The life doxologia.ro prints is a very long one and closes with the wonders at his grave. A demoniac came to the tomb, and the venerable father appeared to him in sleep and blessed him, and he woke free of the enemy's torment. A man who had eaten poisoned food and was near death took oil from the lamp burning by the tomb and poured it into his mouth, and the deadly poison came out of him, and he was whole from then on. Another, very ill in the stomach, had only to look at the saint's icon and call on his name to be healed; and another, held fast by a terror so that he could no longer speak but fled from everyone in fright, was brought to the tomb and anointed with the oil and was delivered of that affliction at once, and his senses being whole again he gave thanks to God and to His servant.

The life also tells how a man who had been deceived by certain monks against Theodore turned them out of his house in dishonour as the cause of his fault, and afterwards held the venerable one in more faith than before, reading with love the writings and the hymns Theodore had composed, and begging him to forgive that fault. The Romanian life names no year anywhere in what it prints.
