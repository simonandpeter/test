# Menelaus of Menat

saint.gr says Menelaus was born at Presinie in France about the year 700, of a very distinguished noble family. From very early on he gave sign that he would follow the road Jesus had shown, and that brought him many obstacles: when he came of age his father pressed him to take up his formal place in society as the prince he was and to marry a woman of blue blood. The young Menelaus answered boldly that he never wanted to marry, and because of the constant quarrels with his father he was forced, with a breaking heart, to give way to his father's worldly plans.

After hard prayer, and so as not to fall dishonourably, he took counsel with two of his closest and most trusted friends, Savvininos and Konstas, and the three of them dared it together and left the paternal yoke. Guided by divine providence they abandoned their city and came to the ruined, ancient and famous monastery of Menas, or of Saint Menas. When they had settled well into that place of stillness they were visited one day by a young monk, Theophritos, who proposed that they go and put themselves under the spiritual direction of Saint Eudus; and so they joined his company.

After a good while these young-old hesychasts had won a great name, and it carried so far that one day his mother, her sister and his former betrothed arrived at the monastery gates. At first the saint believed they had come to change his mind — but they asked him with longing to lead them into the godly life of Christ and to make them nuns. After that a great number of novices came and put themselves under him, wanting the road of hesychastic ascesis, and the small ruined monastery of Menat was restored; in its place, the page says, stands the towering abbey and lavra that would take the saint's name, since he is reckoned its second founder and renewer.

He foresaw that his end stood at the door, prepared the brothers and fathers of the monastery with holy counsels, proposed his successor, and in a few days departed to Christ. The page then lists five of the many wonders he worked as abbot while still alive: a man called Robert, from Turonius, cured of a bad paralysis at a liturgy; a woman born at Carantonio who had lost her sight and saw as soon as she met him; a man blind from birth, Karaldis, a nobleman of Paris, who saw after the elder made clay of the monastery's holy water and signed him with the cross; a dead child brought back to life at his burning entreaty; and a demon cast out with a single blow, after the example of his spiritual patron the venerable Benedict. The page gives no year for his death.
