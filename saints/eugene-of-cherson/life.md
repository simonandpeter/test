# Eugene of Cherson

Eugene is one of the bishops of Cherson whom the Romanian calendar keeps together on the seventh of March. The life doxologia prints for the seven tells his part briefly. After [Basileus](/saints/basileus-of-cherson), the first of them, had been killed at Cherson by a mob that the Jews of the city had stirred up against the Christians, one of his disciples put in for the night with his ship on the coast of the Hellespont, and found three bishops there labouring at the good news of Christ: Eugene, [Elpidius](/saints/elpidius-of-cherson) and [Agathodorus](/saints/agathodorus-of-cherson).

The three went with him to Cherson and took up the work that Basileus had left. But the devil armed the Jews and the Greeks of the city against them as he had armed them against Basileus: a crowd gathered, seized the holy bishops, dragged them bound along the road and beat them with sticks and with stones, and so they died.

doxologia gives no year for their coming or their death, tells nothing of where any of the three was born, and prints for them the same troparion it prints for their fellow bishops of Cherson.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, inside the company of seven, and gives him one sentence. saint.gr says that after the martyr's death of St Basileus the bishops Agathodorus, Elpidius and Eugene were sent to Cherson as heralds of the Gospel and of the faith, and that they too were killed by the unbelieving. It gives no year for any of the three, and the distich it prints over them names none of them singly: a fellow-striving three, it says, speaking the Prophet's word, I gave my back to scourges. The company's apolytikion and kontakion put his name first among the seven.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day among the seven bishops of Cherson, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the seven of them one entry. All of them were bishops in Cherson at different times, it says, and all of them suffered at the hands of unbelievers — whether of Jews, or of Greeks, or of Scythians — except Aetherius alone, who died in peace. All of them went out into those wild parts as missionaries, sent by the patriarch of Jerusalem to carry the light of the Gospel; all were tormented, and all suffered for the sake of their Lord.

Of him and of two others the entry says the same thing in one breath: Eugene, Elpidius and Agathodorus were beaten with staves and with stones until they gave their souls to God.

The Russian church keeps all seven of them on this day, on one line — «Священномучеников, в Херсонесе епископствовавших: Василия , Ефрема , Капитона , Евгения , Еферия , Елпидия и Агафодора (IV).» — and its life is the fullest account of the company any of the four calendars read has given. They carried the gospel, it says, into the lands of the northern Black Sea from the Danube to the Dnieper, the Crimea among them, and sealed their apostolate with a martyr's death in the fourth century at Херсонес Таврический. The page sets them in a history: the faith had reached the Crimea, anciently Таврида and subject to the Roman emperors, long before the baptism of Владимир; its beginning there was the preaching of the apostle Андрей Первозванный; and its spread was helped against their will by its own enemies, since the emperors used the place for state criminals, which in the first three centuries meant confessors of Christ — «в правление Траяна (98–117) был сослан на заточение в Инкерманские каменоломни святой Климент, епископ Римский», who went on preaching in the Inkerman quarries and was martyred there. The pagans of the Crimea resisted hard and many preachers died in the struggle.

The episcopal see at Херсонес was founded at the beginning of the fourth century, in a turning time when the city, a base for Roman soldiers, was passing into dependence on Byzantium. In the reign of Diocletian, which the page gives as 284 to 305, and in the year 300 — «то есть еще до начала гонения, которое император начал с 303 года», before the persecution the emperor began in 303 — the patriarch Ермон of Jerusalem, whose years the same sentence gives as 303 to 313, sent many bishops out into various countries for the preaching of the gospel. **The two numbers do not agree and nothing is done about it here**: a patriarch whose reign the page dates from 303 cannot have sent anybody in 300, and both figures are the page's own.

He is one of three who came together, and **the Russian page dates all three to one day and one year**. A year after the death of Василий, hearing of it, three of his fellow labourers — the bishops Евгений, Елпидий and Агафодор — left their preaching in the country of the Hellespont and came to Херсонес to carry on his holy work. They laid out much labour for the saving of human souls, the page says, and all three shared the lot of their predecessor: «беснующиеся язычники также побили их камнями 7 марта 311 года» — the raging pagans stoned them too, on 7 March 311. That is the whole of what the life gives the three of them separately, and it gives them nothing apart from one another.

The preaching of the hieromartyrs, the page ends, established the faith at Херсонес: from the beginning of the fifth century the city was a spiritual centre from which Christianity spread north towards Rus, from the ninth the Russians were settling in it, modern excavation has found more than fifty churches there of the fifth to fourteenth centuries, and in 987 Владимир was baptised in it. «Этот древний город явился колыбелью христианства для Руси» — this ancient city was the cradle of Christianity for Rus.
