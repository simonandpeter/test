# Atticus of Constantinople

saint.gr says Atticus was born at Sebastea in Armenia and was educated there. When he came to Constantinople he became a cleric of the archbishopric. His learning was middling, the page says, but his self-respect and his labour in study, so that he might be able to preach, were great; in his life he was ascetic and at the same time sociable.

He succeeded Arsacius, who died in October 405, in the archbishopric of Constantinople, and his election was at the end of 405 or the beginning of 406. Under him the church of Holy Wisdom, burnt in 404, was dedicated again in 415; and in 421 he baptised the famous Athenais, daughter of the Athenian sophist Leontius, renaming her Eudocia, and then blessed her marriage to the emperor Theodosius II. He was a great benefactor of the poor and worked against superstitions, and by his moderation and goodness he won back many schismatics and heretics. In 422 he wrote [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom) into the diptychs of the Church.

For all his moderation, the page ends, Atticus behaved very strictly towards the heresy of the Messalians, who kept monastic settlements in Asia Minor and Syria and boasted that they achieved moral perfection without need of the Law — going so far as to despise work and live on alms, saying that they were regularly granted divine visions, and scorning the holy mysteries and the traditions of the Church. Atticus died in peace in 425, having been patriarch twenty years.
