# Agnes of Rome

Agnes was born at Rome towards the end of the third century into a patrician family that had been Christian for more than a century and a half, serving the altar or dying as martyrs. The page insists on how well she is attested: Ambrose of Milan composed a hymn for her, «Agnes beatae virgini», and preached on her feast; Jerome and Augustine name her; and the Spanish poet Prudentius sang her in the fourteenth hymn of his Peristephanon, published in 405. She was martyred at the beginning of the fourth century, thirteen years old, in Diocletian's persecution.

How she died is told two ways, and the page says so. Pope Damasus, following a Greek tradition, has her burned at the stake; Ambrose and Prudentius, whom the page takes as the likelier, say that she was pressed to sacrifice to Vesta, refused, was exposed to the jeering of frivolous men and then beheaded at a stroke of the sword. Augustine plays on her name: agnes in Latin is a lamb and in Greek the pure one, and in heaven, he says, she was repaid for her meekness and her purity.

The Passio the page follows adds the story. The prefect's son asked her in marriage and sent her gifts; she answered that she was betrothed already to a Bridegroom whose beauty outshone the sun, and would not be moved. He denounced her as a Christian, and she came of her own accord before the prefect Symphronius, who could find no fetters small enough for a child's hands. Sent to a house of prostitution to be shamed, she was covered by her own hair, which God made grow over her whole body; an angel filled the place with light, and a man who came near her fell dead at her feet.

Condemned then as a sorceress, she was glad, and said that the Bridegroom's servants had come for her and her lamp was ready. She knelt, and the executioner's hands shook until the prefect shouted at him. She was buried in the catacombs on the Via Nomentana that carry her name.
