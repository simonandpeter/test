# Acepsimas of Naeson

In the parts of Persia, in the city called Naeson, there was a faithful bishop named Acepsimas, who from early childhood to old age did every kind of good work and kept his life blameless. Later, though he was eighty years old, he did not weaken in his labour, but struggled greatly with fasting, with prayers and with diligent care for the flock entrusted to him. He showed the greatest struggle of all near his end, when he endured manfully to the shedding of his blood for his Lord, for whom he laid down his soul eagerly after heavy and long torments.

Some years before his martyrdom there was this prophecy of his suffering. Once, as he sat in the house, a child of his was filled with a prophetic spirit and kissed him on the head and said, "Blessed is this head, for it will receive torments for Christ." He rejoiced at the prophecy and said, "Be it to me, my son, as you have said." A friend of his was sitting beside him at the time, the bishop of a nearby city, who heard the child's words, smiled, and asked the child to tell him something about himself too; and the child, inspired by God, answered, "You will not see your city, but turning back towards it you will die on the road in a village called Etradat." The child's prophecy was fulfilled for both bishops: one died on the road in the village the child had foretold, and the head of Acepsimas was counted worthy of a martyr's crown.

Shapur, the ill-believing emperor of the Persians, raised a great persecution against the Christians of his land. In the very days of Christ's saving Passion he sent an order into every part of his dominion that all who confessed Christ in Persia should be killed; and a multitude of Christians were killed without sparing through the villages and the cities, for the servants of the idols and the magi grew the more savage against them and searched eagerly for those in hiding, dragging them out of houses and hidden places and killing them with cruel torments, while others of the faithful gave themselves up of their own accord and poured out their blood eagerly.

Many of those in the royal palaces were killed for Christ, and with them Azad the eunuch, whom the emperor loved greatly, a true servant of Jesus Christ and a confessor of his most holy name. Learning of the death of his beloved eunuch Azad, the emperor was very sorry for him, and stopped the killing of the people at large, but ordered that only the preachers and the teachers of the Christian faith should be sought out for death. The order being published everywhere, the chiefs of the magi and the sorcerers went eagerly through all Persia looking for the Christian bishops and priests and killing them with every kind of torment.

So the bishop Acepsimas was taken, and soon after him the presbyter Joseph, who was seventy years old, and likewise Aithalas the deacon; both of them, the life says, just and holy men, whose zeal for God was like the zeal of the prophet Elijah, whose look was honourable and whose word was like a two-edged sword cutting through unbelief. They were brought before the chief of the magi, who looked at them with pride and terrible eyes and said, "You lost race! Why do you go round the simple people and deceive them with your unjust teachings?" The presbyter answered that they did not deceive the people but turned them from deceit to the true God, who is Lord and Maker of the sun and of the fire and of all creation, visible and invisible, and that a teaching about the one just God is not unjust.

After long beating Joseph was bound with two iron chains and thrown into the prison beside Acepsimas, where they encouraged one another for the easing of their pains and comforted themselves with the hope of God's mercy. Then Aithalas the deacon was left bound before the chief of the magi, and asked whether he would worship the great sun and taste of the blood of the sacrifices or stay stiff and unbending like the others; and he answered that it was the work of his questioner's unclean grey hairs to drink blood and to have for a god what God had made, and that he would not honour the creature above the Creator. His hands were bound under his knees, a great beam put through the binding and six soldiers set to jump on him and trample him, so that his joints came apart, and then he was beaten with thorned staves until his flesh was broken.

Five days later the saints were brought out of prison to the place called paradise, where the temple of the fire-god stood that the pagan Persians honoured, and the chief of the magi asked whether they would stay in their madness or become one with him. They answered that they were now in the mind they had had from the beginning, and would remain in it to the end, and that neither threats nor torments nor gifts would change their good confession. So he found a new torture: they were bound with ropes under the arms and about the thighs and the shins, and a beam was put through the ropes and turned, so that their bones cracked and those standing far off heard it; and they were tortured so from the third hour to the sixth, and were then carried back like sacks and thrown into the prison.

The judge ordered the guards to let nobody in to them, and that whoever brought them food or drink or clothing should be killed at once. So the sufferers of Christ spent three years in that prison in great want, failing from hunger and thirst and the rotting of their wounds and bearing pain beyond measure, until even the guards were moved at the sight of it and often wept, but were afraid to bring them any relief for fear of their lord; only in secret one or another of them sometimes gave them a piece of bread or a little water.

After three years Shapur came there, and with him Ardasabor, the greatest lord over all the magi of Persia. Hearing of the martyrs in the prison he had them brought before him, and when they came out they were like reeds shaken by the wind, so weak had they grown; unable to walk, they were carried by others, and their look was a marvel, their faces like those of the dead who lie in graves, their eyes sunk into their heads and their bones covered with skin only. "Are you Christians?" he asked; and they answered that they were, and believed in the living God and worshipped him. He set before them the state their faith had brought them to and advised them to worship the most shining sun, or die a cruel death; and with one mouth they renounced his impiety and confessed the true God, who made not the sun only but all creation, and showed themselves ready to die for the Maker of all.

After many exhortations, when Ardasabor saw that he could not bend them to his will, he ordered thirty soldiers first to beat Acepsimas with raw thongs on the back and on the belly, while the magus shouted, "Obey the emperor's command and you will live!" And the saint, as long as he was able, answered, "I am striving to finish the will of my Lord with all the strength I have received from him"; then he weakened and fell silent. After that they cut off his holy head, and the prophecy of that God-inspired child was fulfilled, who had kissed him on the head and said, "Blessed is this head, for it will receive torment for Christ."

His body they carried out into the road and threw to the dogs, and a guard stood off at a distance watching that the Christians should not steal it. But after three days, when the guards had gone off somewhere, the Christians took that treasure of great price and buried it with honour. Doxologia's page for him ends there; the sufferings of the two who were condemned with him, the presbyter [Joseph](/saints/joseph-presbyter-of-persia) and the deacon [Aithalas](/saints/aithalas-the-deacon), it tells on their own pages.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them on the same day, 3 November, and divides them by rank and by the manner of their deaths. Acepsimas was a bishop, saint.gr says, Joseph a presbyter and Aithalas a deacon, and all three were martyred for Christ when Shapur II was king of Persia, about the year 330. Acepsimas gave up his last breath after being beaten hard with thorny rods of pomegranate wood; Joseph, after his flesh had been torn, suffered a martyr's death by stoning; and Aithalas was cruelly whipped and then hung up head downwards until he too gave up his blessed soul. The epigram sets the same division in two lines — they kill Acepsimas by the rods, and Acepsimas's friends by the stones — and the apolytikion the page prints calls the three of them pillars of the Church who pulled down the arrogance of the fire-worshippers.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps the three of them on 3 ноября ст. ст., under the typikon's lowest mark — «Cовершается служба, не отмеченная в Типиконе никаким знаком», a service marked in the Typikon with no sign at all — and tells the whole passion in one paragraph. Two of its particulars are not in the longer account above. He was brought to **Arbela** for his trial, after Shapur's order went out to hunt down and execute the Christian clergy; and the judge who put the sacrifice to him there was **Ardakh, a priest of the god of the sun**. His age at his arrest is eighty in both accounts, and the Russian has him beheaded, which is the Romanian reading and not the Greek.

The Russian account is also where the by-names in this folder's Russian name form come from. Its line calls him only «еп.», bishop; the name page behind the line calls him of Naeson, of Anis in brackets, of Arbela and of Persia, and the first of those is the see this folder already carried. The day page prints two Slavonic hymns under the line, a troparion which is the common of martyrs and names none of the three, and a kontakion which names him and his fellow-sufferers; both are in this folder now, and the kontakion is the Slavonic of the Greek one it already held. The century the line gives, «(IV)», contains the about-330 this folder already carried, so no date was changed.

**The Russian calendar’s 10 апреля ст. ст. keeps three men it says were taken with him**, and this folder gains its first `related` rows from it. The Сретенский calendar’s line for that day is «Мчч. Иакова пресвитера, Азадана и Авдикия диаконов, Персидских (ок. 380).», and the life behind it says of the three that «Они были взяты вместе с епископом Акепсимом (память 3 ноября)» — they were taken together with the bishop Acepsimas, whose memory is 3 November, which is the day this folder is kept on by three calendars. The three are [James of Pharatha, companion of Azas](/saints/james-of-pharatha), [Azas, companion of James of Pharatha](/saints/azas-the-deacon) and [Abdicius of Persia](/saints/abdicius-of-persia).

**The bishop is named there without a link and this corpus holds two Acepsimases**, so the rows are a reading: the page calls him a bishop, sets the deaths in Persia and puts his day on 3 ноября, and this folder is the bishop of Naeson in Persia where `acepsimas-3-november` is of Antioch and Syrian and carries no office. Nothing else here is changed — row, day, `names` and `dates` are untouched. **The three are dated between 332 and 380 and this folder is dated 376, which is the one thing that tells against the Greek end of their interval**, since a company taken with him cannot have been taken forty-four years before.
