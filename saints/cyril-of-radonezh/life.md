# Cyril of Radonezh, father of Sergius

He has a day in the calendar because of his son, and the calendar says so in the line itself: «Прпп. схимонаха Кирилла и схимонахини Марии, родителей прп. Сергия Радонежского». Cyril was in the service first of Constantine II Borisovich of Rostov and then of Constantine III Vasilievich, one of the men closest to them, and went with them more than once to the Golden Horde. He had the means his position called for, and, living in a village by the plain manners of the time, did not hold himself above ordinary farm work.

The life of [Sergius](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) is where the page goes for what is known of them. Before the son was born, it says, [Maria](/saints/maria-of-radonezh) and the others at the Liturgy heard the child cry out three times — before the Gospel, during the Cherubic hymn, and when the priest said «Святая святым». Taking this as a great mercy, the two of them vowed the child to God as Hannah had vowed Samuel. He was born and named Bartholomew, and astonished everyone by fasting from the first: on Wednesdays and Fridays he would not take his mother's milk, nor on any day when she had eaten meat, so she gave up meat altogether.

They kept the church's rules strictly, helped the poor, and were particular about the apostle's charge not to forget hospitality to strangers, «тем бо не ведяще неции странноприяша Ангелы»; they taught their children never to let a travelling monk or a tired stranger pass the house unasked. The page admits that no detailed account of their life has come down, and quotes Metropolitan Platon to the effect that the fruit of the tree praises it better than any eloquence could.

About 1328 they moved from Rostov to Radonezh. Three versts away stood the Khotkovo monastery of the Protection, at that time housing both men and women, and in old age — by a custom that took in commoners, princes and boyars alike — they wished for the angelic habit themselves, the spirit of the monastic life having passed from the son to his parents. They went there to spend what was left in repentance, and did not labour long in the new rank: in 1337 they departed in peace. On 3 April 1992 the Council of Bishops glorified them both for the whole Church, six centuries of veneration having gone before it.

*After the Russian church calendar's [28 September](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260928.html) and its [life of the two of them](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life6670.htm); read 4 October 2026.*
