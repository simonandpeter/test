# David of Hermopolis, the former robber

David, before he entered the monastery, was the leader of a band of robbers in Egypt, in the desert of Hermopolis, and committed many murders and other crimes. Reaching old age he thought over his life and was horrified at his deeds; leaving the band he went to a monastery and asked the abbot to receive him among the brethren for repentance. The abbot refused, explaining that their life was very hard and beyond his strength; David insisted, and at last told the abbot that he was the notorious robber David — and that if the monastery’s doors were not opened to him for repentance he would go back to his old life, ruin the monastery and kill the monks. The abbot let him in, and to everyone’s astonishment David became an exemplary monk, surpassing all the brethren in the harshness of his labours. After a time the Lord sent the Archangel Gabriel to tell him he was forgiven; in his humility David could not believe that the Lord had so soon forgiven so great a sinner, and the Archangel told him that for his little faith he would be dumb — David begged that his speech be left him — the Sretensky calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. The Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, says the Archangel gave him the power of wonderworking, and that he reposed in peace in the sixth century, having by many and long labours been made worthy of the kingdom of God.
