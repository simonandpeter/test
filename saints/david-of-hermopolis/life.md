# David of Hermopolis, the former robber

David, before he entered the monastery, was the leader of a band of
robbers in Egypt, in the desert of Hermopolis, and committed many murders
and other crimes. Reaching old age he thought over his life and was
horrified at his deeds; leaving the band he went to a monastery and asked
the abbot to receive him among the brethren for repentance. The abbot
refused, explaining that their life was very hard and beyond his strength;
David insisted, and at last told the abbot that he was the notorious
robber David — and that if the monastery’s doors were not opened to him
for repentance he would go back to his old life, ruin the monastery and
kill the monks. The abbot let him in, and to everyone’s astonishment David
became an exemplary monk, surpassing all the brethren in the harshness of
his labours. After a time the Lord sent the Archangel Gabriel to tell him
he was forgiven; in his humility David could not believe that the Lord had
so soon forgiven so great a sinner, and the Archangel told him that for
his little faith he would be dumb. David begged that his speech be left
him for prayer, for the reading of his rule and for taking part in the
church services, and that was granted him — the rest of the time he stayed
dumb. At the end of his life he received from God the gift of wonders,
healed many sick and drove evil spirits out of the possessed, and having
lived so for many years he went to the Lord, in the sixth century. The
Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, says the Archangel gave him the power
of wonderworking, and that he reposed in peace having by many and long
labours been made worthy of the kingdom of God.
