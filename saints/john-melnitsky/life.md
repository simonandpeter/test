# John (Melnitsky)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him thirteenth of fifteen on 21 октября ст. ст., the second of the two deacons in a line of eleven priests, two deacons and two monks of 1937. He was born in 1878 in the hamlet of Sheteyevo in the Udomlya uyezd of Tver province, the son of a peasant, Pyotr Melnitsky. In 1899 he finished the third class of the Tver seminary and had to leave his studies to help his father on the land, but the thought of giving himself wholly to God did not leave him, and he petitioned the diocesan bishop to be taken into the Church's service.

On 5 May 1907 he was appointed acting psalm-reader at the church of the village of Vorotilovo in the Bezhetsk uyezd, and confirmed in the post on 19 June 1908. On 21 November of that year he was ordained deacon to the same church. All that while he taught in the Vorotilovo parish school and taught the Law of God both there and in the Novinskaya zemstvo school, and was commended more than once by his superiors, as the award papers put it, for gratuitous and diligent teaching of the Law of God. His service in church and school went on until 1918.

In the summer of 1918 he was transferred to the church of the village of Ilovo in the Vyshny Volochok uyezd, where he served until the persecution of 1929, when the state began destroying the clergy alongside the peasants who were then most of the country's people — and for those peasants, the calendar's life says, the church and the word of God were the one remaining source of real enlightenment. In 1930 his household was taken from him, which had been the support of a wife and six children, the youngest of them seven and five years old, and on 9 March 1930 he was sentenced for unpaid taxes to two years in a corrective labour camp.

Released from the Zavidovo labour colony on 20 March 1932, he petitioned the archbishop for a place in the diocese, and in 1933 was given the village of Taltsy, where his family followed him. He served there until the new persecution came. He was arrested immediately after the feast of the Protection, on 15 October 1937, and interrogated the next day. The chairman of the Stalin collective farm and a local Komsomol member testified at length: that at a feast in July he had told the people that however hard they worked in the collective farms their life would not improve, and that they should remember God, come to church on the feast and rest, since it is sinful to work on a feast; that in consequence the whole farm had lost two days at the height of the field work.

They said he had spoken in the same way in August, calling the people to gather round the church, with the same loss of two days in the harvest, and that collective farmers had come in for those feasts from three village soviets and not from his own alone; and that at a village meeting on 23 September he had said the Soviet power fleeces the country people with every kind of tax and self-assessment when they can barely live as it is.

Whether the witnesses were setting out what they had heard themselves, the calendar's life says, or whether their answers had been put into their ears by the investigator, is hard to say. On the same day he questioned the deacon, who answered that he had carried on no counter-revolutionary activity and would not admit guilt; that he had happened to say to individuals that life in the collective farms was poor and hungry, and that if bad conditions had been made for us it might be so that we should live better afterwards, and that they should not work on feast days; and that in conversation he had called people to church, saying that if they wanted their church not to be closed they must come to it as often as they could. He had been at the September meeting, he said, but had gone home before the end of it and had made no speech.

The investigation closed the following day, the papers went to the NKVD troika, and he was moved to the prison at Ostashkov. On 1 November the troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 3 November 1937. He was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000. The day page prints troparia and kontakia for other saints it heads and nothing under his name.
