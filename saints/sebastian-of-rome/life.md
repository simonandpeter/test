# Sebastian of Rome

doxologia.ro prints a very long life of him, and what is written here is read out of its opening and its close. Sebastian was born in the city of Narbonne and grew up and learned his letters at Milan. He was so pleasing and so loved by the heathen emperors Diocletian and Maximian that they made him commander of their nearest soldiers and ordered him always to be before them, for he was a brave and able man, full of wisdom, true in his words, just in his judgements and far-seeing in counsel. In what was entrusted to him and in his service he was faithful and manly, marked in goodness and choice in all his ways; the soldiers honoured him as a father, and everyone in the palace loved him.

Every day he served Christ diligently, praying and keeping his holy commandments, and he did it secretly, so that the heathen emperors should not learn of his faith — not out of fear of torments and not out of love of his wealth, but only to hide the soldier of Christ under the cloak of an earthly office so that he could help the Christians, who in those days were much driven out and tormented. Those he could not ask for and free from their bonds and torments, or bring any relief to, he strengthened with useful words, that they should give up their souls manfully for their Lord, by whose blood they had been bought, and pour out their blood looking to the everlasting rewards that come after the passing torments here.

Strengthening, as the Scripture says, the weak hands and the loosened knees of the faithful, he taught those who were faint of soul and doubted and feared the torments, and made them manly and woke them to the struggle. Seeing many of them finish their course before him and keep their faith unhurt in heavy torments and go to receive the everlasting crowns, he was set alight himself with the desire to suffer for Christ, and waited until the Lord should show him a fit time for it; and the time came in which he could no longer hide the flame of divine love and zeal that burned within him, and he showed everyone plainly who he was, for the light would not stay hidden in the dark any longer.

The occasion was this. Two honoured and well-born men, Marcellinus and Mark, brothers born together, had been taken confessing Christ and long kept in bonds; and Sebastian often went to them and comforted them, speaking of the holy faith and of what is useful, counselling them to renounce the swiftly passing deceits of the world and not to fear the passing torments. They listened to his sound counsel, bore the wounds their tormentors laid on them manfully and were unshaken in the faith, and were condemned to be beheaded. But an order was given that if at the hour when they bent their heads under the sword they should be willing to sacrifice to the gods, they should be released to their houses, to their parents, their families and their children, for their parents were still living: their father was called Tranquillinus and their mother Maria, and both were old. Their parents and all their household begged Agrestius Chromatius, prefect of the city of Rome, to sweeten the days of their life by thirty days, in which they might counsel them to bow to the gods, for the parents, the wives and all their kin were in the darkness of the heathen unbelief; and the time being given, the parents, the wives, the children, the kinsfolk and the friends surrounded them with much lamenting and begged them to have mercy on themselves and on these, and to do what the emperors wished.

The life then follows at length what came of that plea, and of the many the confessor won; and it ends with his own two deaths. Diocletian, angered, ordered him taken outside the city, bound naked to a post in the middle of a field and shot through with arrows; and set there like a target, a crowd of soldiers shot his honoured body, closing round him on this side and that until his whole body was full of arrows, and then, thinking him dead, went away and left him bound and full of arrows. When the wife of the holy martyr Castulus, whose name was Irene, went by night to take the body of Sebastian and bury it, she found him alive and carried him to her house; and in a few days he was healed of his wounds and became altogether well.

The Christians gathered to him secretly and begged him to leave Rome, as other Christians had done, so as not to fall into the heathens' hands again. But he made his prayer, went and stood on the steps of the palace of the Elagabali, and when the emperors passed by he cried out to them: the priests of your gods trouble you with their unclean sorceries, giving you lying reports of the Christians, as though they were enemies of the empire of the Romans; but see how much good the Christians do you, for they pray in their prayers for this city, and do not cease to pray for your empire and for the health of all the Roman soldiers.

Diocletian looked at him and said: are you Sebastian, whom I lately ordered to be killed with arrows? And the saint answered: for this my Lord Jesus Christ was pleased to raise me, that I should be alive before you and be a witness before the whole people of your injustice, for with an unjust judgement you have raised a persecution against the Christians, the servants of Christ. Then Diocletian ordered him seized and taken to the hippodrome; and there the sufferer, glorifying Christ with a loud voice and reproaching the idols and the error of the Romans, was beaten with rods until he died, and passed with the voice of joy to Christ, the giver of crowns.

The heathen threw his holy body by night into a deep pit of filth, so that the Christians should not find it and take it; but the saint appeared in a vision to a right-believing woman, Lucina, and said: go to the pit of filth that is near the Circus, and you will find my body hanging on nails; take it and carry it to the catacombs, and let it be buried at the door of the cave, beside the graves of the apostles. That blessed woman took her servants and went at midnight to the pit that had been shown her, took the martyr's body reverently and buried it with honour in the place she had been told. doxologia.ro gives no year anywhere in what was read, and this folder gives none; its Tropar tab for him is a recording in Greek with no text, and the day's sung troparion, on another page, is for him and for Zoe together.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr begins him in a different city. It has Sebastian born at Mediolanum in Italy in 250 to 256, brought up by his parents with great Christian care; and because he was also of a distinguished family he drew the favour of the emperor Carinus, who quickly advanced him as a soldier, and afterwards Diocletian made him head of the first regiment of the praetorians. A charitable soul, the page says, he was from that place many times a protector of the poor and of the suffering Christians, and he helped readily in the needs of the Roman Church, for which pope Gaius awarded him the title of defender of the Church.

When the persecution against the Christians began, a group of Christians was arrested; and Sebastian, in order to hearten them at the hour they were being tried, declared before the general astonishment of everyone that he was a Christian himself. Diocletian ordered his death, and Sebastian was not long in falling, pierced in the chest by an arrow. A pious widow, Lucina, took up his body — and found that he was still breathing. She nursed him, and after a few days Sebastian had his health back. But he sought Diocletian out again, met him and rebuked him for his cruelty; and then the emperor gave the order and they scourged him to death.

The same day's calendar keeps the ten of his company by name, and saint.gr says that they all suffered in Diocletian's persecution together with him: [Nicostratus](/saints/nicostratus-18-december) and his wife [Zoe](/saints/zoe-18-december), [Tranquillinus](/saints/tranquillinus) and his wife [Marcia](/saints/marcia-wife-of-tranquillinus), [Claudius](/saints/claudius-18-december), [Tiburtius](/saints/tiburtius-18-december), [Castor](/saints/castor-18-december), [Castulus](/saints/castulus-18-december) and the two brothers [Marcus](/saints/marcus-son-of-tranquillinus) and [Marcellinus](/saints/marcellinus-son-of-tranquillinus). Of Tranquillinus and Marcia the page says that they were pagans who went to the prison to turn their two sons from the faith, and that in the end, with Sebastian standing by them, they became Christians themselves.
