# Romulus, martyred under Trajan, with many soldiers

Romulus lived in the reign of Trajan and was an official close to the emperor, a commander — praepositus, the Greek synaxarion calls him, head of the imperial household, the Prologue. While the emperor was in the East to put down the peoples who had risen against Rome — Iberians, Sarmatians, Arabs — in 107 and again in 115, he made a count of his troops and found in them up to eleven thousand Christians, whom he at once sent in disgrace into exile in Armenia; Romulus — who, the Greek says, had himself at first been a persecutor, sent by Trajan to Gaul to make the Christian soldiers there worship the idols, and failing — reproached the emperor for godlessness and folly in reducing his army in time of war, and openly confessed himself a Christian. Enraged, Trajan had him beaten without mercy and then beheaded; the exiled Christian soldiers were put to death in Armenia by various executions — ten thousand of them crucified, the Prologue says, the rest worn out by other torments. The Russian calendar keeps him with many martyrs on 6 September; the Greek counts the eleven thousand with Eudoxius and his company in one entry.
