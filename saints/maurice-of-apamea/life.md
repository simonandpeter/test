# Maurice of Apamea, with 70 soldiers

Maximian the emperor, thinking that by his service to the gods of lying name he would win help and a greater raising of his empire, ordered everyone under his rule to sacrifice to the idols; and he was not content, as earlier emperors had been, to write to the governors of cities and countries about the honour of the idols, but went about the world himself, serving the devils' altars in earnest, hoping that wherever he came he might bring back to his own bad faith all who had left error for the knowledge of Christ.

With that in mind he left Rome, came into the eastern parts, and entered Apamea, a city of Syria. There the servants of the idols came to him and accused the Christians: we tell your unconquered power, they said, that all among us honour the great ancestral gods with fear, above all father Zeus and wonderful Apollo, and that these guard and defend your empire; but Maurice and the seventy soldiers of yours who are with him, puffed up with your honours and enriched with your great gifts, count the gods as nothing and set themselves against your great and unconquered empire, being deceived by the Christian error. Maximian, hearing it, was angrier than a burning furnace, above all because such soldiers were found in his own companies.

On a set day he had a judgement seat prepared at the place called Amaxic, at the city's northern gate, so that the whole people might watch; and the whole city came, of every age, so that not even the maidens stayed at home. The seventy soldiers of Christ were brought there with Maurice their commander at their head. The emperor reproached him: we had expected that men fed on our bounty and given the first honours should follow our love and bring the disobedient back to a sound mind, and instead we hear you do the opposite. Maurice answered that the wrong done to their senseless gods was preparing the crown of victory for them before the true God, who made heaven, earth and the sea and all that is in them; unclean devils and deaf, blind, senseless idols were not to be called gods.

The emperor had Maurice separated from the seventy and tried to coax them. Theodore and Philip answered for all of them: we are so far from error, tyrant, that we wish to deliver even you from what we have been delivered from; we abhor your unclean faith and lay down our temporal soldiering to be soldiers of God, the King of hosts. When Maximian told Philip, an old man, to be a good counsellor to his fellows, Philip answered that he would not be a bad counsellor to men who had willingly been pierced with the fear of God, for it is written: woe to him through whom the offence comes.

So their belts and their soldiers' clothes were taken from them, and the emperor told them to see from what honour they had fallen. God in heaven, they said, will clothe us with the robes and belts of His own incorruptible and eternal glory. They were put in prison in chains, and there they agreed together to prepare their souls by prayer, and prayed that the Holy Spirit would put into their mouths what they should answer. After three days they were set before him again, at the same gate, with the whole city watching.

Then the tyrant saw a young man among them and asked his name and his people. I am called Photinus, he answered, and my name is from the true light; I am a soldier of my Christ, who shamed your father Satan; by race I am a Roman, and by body and by spirit I am the son of the most illustrious Maurice, born of him and taught the knowledge of God by him. Told to spare his youth and sacrifice to great Zeus, he said he was wiser than the old men, believing in the Lord whom they neither believed nor knew.

Maximian had them stripped and beaten with ox sinews until their bones were broken and the ground was red with the blood; and when the servants tired he asked whether they had learned how easily he could take their lives. They answered that as he did not know the love and light of Christ, being at peace with the darkness of the devil's error, so they did not feel the torments he gave them, and he should invent newer and heavier ones. He had a great fire built to throw them into; but the martyrs did not wait for the servants, and went into the middle of the flame themselves as into water, and walked in the fire unharmed, reviling the tyrant and his gods, so that everyone wondered.

Then he had them bound to the frames and torn with iron claws, and they bore that too, until the people wondered at the emperor's madness. Maurice laughed at him: Photinus, this young man, has broken the power of your tyrant's empire by all he has borne; how will you beat our whole camp, being beaten by one boy? Maximian, grinding his teeth, had Photinus killed at once with the sword before his father's eyes, meaning to fill Maurice with grief; and Maurice was filled instead with joy and gladness at his son's martyr's end, and told the tyrant he had granted their desire by sending Photinus on ahead to Christ.

Seeing that they endured everything, the emperor took counsel with his suite how to destroy them so as to frighten all the Christians in the world; and one of them, a root of malice, said: it is now the season of summer, for the month is July, and outside the city to the west there is a meadow between two streams and marshes where the mosquitoes, gadflies, wasps and hornets are now so thick that a man can hardly pass and cannot stay an hour. Have them taken there and bound to the trees and smeared with honey, so that the flies may eat them, and they may learn that our unconquered and eternal gods are not to be reviled.

So it was done, and there they commended their souls to God, praying to be settled with all His saints, since they had loved Him and desired Him with their whole soul and had given themselves to death for Him; and so they gave up their holy souls. Maximian, hearing that they were dead, was still troubled at them, and ordered their honoured heads struck off and their bodies left unburied in the meadow and the wood, for the birds and the beasts. Some faithful brethren came by night, with fear and trembling, gathered the relics that had been thrown there and buried them in the earth. Doxologia names Maximian and the month of July but prints no year, and the page carries no troparion.

The Greek calendar names him on 1 Ιουλίου as well, and saint.gr's entry for that day gives him no life — it prints the distich and then sends the reader to its page for 27 Δεκεμβρίου, where the biography is. The distich is the one thing it keeps of him there, and it is the honey: «O Mαυρίκιος γυμνός εγχρισθείς μέλι, / Kρίνει μελισσών ηδύ τας τρώσεις μέλι» — Maurice, smeared naked with honey, judges the stings of the bees sweet as honey. It is the same torment the Romanian life above describes, which is what makes the Greek entry his.

The same page carries a refusal of its own. «Σημείωση: Εδώ ορισμένοι Συναξαριστές αναφέρουν και τη μνήμη κάποιας Αγίας Απολλωνίας. Τέτοιο όνομα όμως δεν βρίσκουμε πουθενά» — some synaxaria, it notes, keep the memory of a certain St Apollonia here as well, but no such name is to be found anywhere. saint.gr prints the report and declines it, so no folder is made for her.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day with his seventy. In the time of the emperor Maximian, the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović says, he was the commander of the local troops at Apamea in Syria, and the pagans denounced him to the emperor as a Christian and a sower of the Christian faith among the soldiers; the emperor came himself and led the investigation, and seventy Christian soldiers were brought before him with their commander, one of them the commander's own son, Photinus.

Neither flattery nor threats could shake them. To the threats they answered that there is no fear in the right-minded and powerful soul of those who love the Lord; when their soldiers' belts and clothes were stripped off them, that their God would clothe them in incorruptible clothes and belts and in eternal glory; and when the emperor reproached them for despising the honour he had given them, that his honour was dishonour, because he had forgotten the God who gave him his imperial power. Then the emperor had the son beheaded before his father's eyes to frighten him and the rest, and the father said only that his tormentor had granted their wish and sent Photinus on ahead of them, a soldier of Christ.

The sentence was the most inhuman death the Prologue records of this day: they were taken to a marsh, stripped, tied to trees and smeared with honey, so that mosquitoes, wasps and hornets should eat them. They gave up their souls to God in those torments only on the tenth day. Christians took their bodies secretly and buried them honourably. It dates their suffering about the year 305.
