# Athanasius I of Constantinople

saint.gr's calendar for 28 Οκτωβρίου keeps «Όσιος Αθανάσιος Α' Πατριάρχης Κωνσταντινούπολης». He was born at Adrianople in Thrace, the page says, or by other sources at Androusa in Messenia about the year 1235, of devout parents named George and Euphrosyne. His first name was Alexios, and he was a man of great self-restraint and sobriety. He embraced the monastic life young, and lived as an ascetic on the Holy Mountain and in the monastery of Ganos in Thrace.

As patriarch he was marked out by the severity of his standards, not only in the monastic life but in the pastoral work of the clergy and above all of the bishops. His attempt to bring order to undisciplined wandering monks, and his insistence that bishops resident in Constantinople return to their provinces, raised the sharpest opposition; his bold efforts, which would have given radical answers to the Church's serious problems, came to nothing because they ran against the general tendencies of his age.

To this far-seeing patriarch, the page says, are owed the Novels issued by the emperor Andronikos II Palaiologos, whose reign it dates 1282 to 1328, as his own valuable letters to the emperor show. The bishops and monks touched by those strict measures became his personal enemies, gave themselves to slandering him, and forced him to resign twice; the page sets the names of Gregory of Cyprus and of John XII against his two patriarchates, with the years 1289 to 1293 and 1304 to 1311, and its wording does not make plain which years belong to which man.

He then withdrew from the throne and lived as a monk in some monastery, most likely the one he had founded himself at Xerolophos, and there died peacefully at a hundred years old, about 1323. His life was written by John Kalothetos, a contemporary of Gregory Palamas, and put into plainer Greek by Agapios Landos in the «Νέος Παράδεισος».

The page is careful about his relics. They were carried from Constantinople to Venice in 1455, after the City fell to the Turks, by the Venetian shipowner Domenico Zottarello, who took them for the relics of Athanasius the Great of Alexandria — and as his they are honoured by the Venetians to this day. In 1705 the skull was destroyed by fire and replaced with a gilded head; in 1807 the relic was moved to the monastery of San Zaccaria, where it is kept now. A rib is at the monastery of Pantokrator on the Holy Mountain, and fragments at Esphigmenou and in the church of Saints Constantine and Helen at Androusa in Messenia. No hymn is printed, and the page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου».

The Russian calendar keeps him on 24 октября ст. ст., four days before the Greek one, and its life page says as much in its own heading. Its telling runs the same road with different stations. He was Alexis in the world and came of Adrianople; he left home in his youth for Thessalonica, was tonsured in a monastery there with the name Acacius, and went on to the Holy Mountain, to the brotherhood of the Esphigmenou house, where he served three years in the refectory. In his labours he was given the gift of tears, and his virtues won him the goodwill of the whole brotherhood.

Fearing their praise, he left the Mountain for the holy places of Jerusalem and then for the mountain of Patra, where he lived long as a hermit, and afterwards for the mountain of Auxentius and then for the monastery of the blessed Lazarus on the mountain of Galanteia, where he was clothed in the great angelic habit with the name Athanasius, received holy orders and became ecclesiarch. There he was granted a revelation: from the crucifix he heard the voice of the Lord calling him to the pastoral office.

Ten years later he went back to the Holy Mountain for the sake of silence and prayer, returned to Galanteia because of troubles there, and found no solitude in it either, for crowds came to him for direction, so that a women's monastery grew up on the spot. The see of Constantinople was then empty after the disturbances of a former patriarch's time, and in 1289 a council of hierarchs and clergy chose him for it unanimously on the pious emperor's proposal.

He set about the work with zeal and did much to strengthen the Church, and the severity of his reproofs made him enemies among the influential clergy, so that in 1293 he had to leave the see and withdraw to his monastery. In 1303 the patriarchal staff was given him again and he bore it worthily seven years more; in 1308 he appointed the metropolitan of Kyiv and all Russia. In 1311, unwilling to be the cause of a disturbance in the Church, he gave up its government again and went to his house for good. **Towards the end of his life he saw Christ a second time, and was reproached by Him for not having carried his pastoral duty through to the end**; weeping, he repented of his faint-heartedness and received forgiveness and the gift of wonderworking. He died at the age of a hundred, and the day page carries his troparion, for which it prints no tone, and his kontakion in глас 2.
