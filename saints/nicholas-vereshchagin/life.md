# Nicholas (Vereshchagin)

He was born on 10 December 1893 in the village of Glebovo in the Tver province, the son of a clergyman named Michael Vereshchagin, was educated at the Tver seminary, and was ordained priest at a time when the persecutions after the revolution had already begun. He served at Glebovo, in his father's own church.

The case against him was made in the summer of 1937 by an officer of the Staritsa NKVD, who was collecting information on the clergy to be arrested and destroyed and found a peasant of Glebovo willing to bear false witness. The peasant's statement is in the calendar's own life of him, and what it alleges is a conversation at a wall: collective farmers had come to take stone from the church fence, and Vereshchagin — the witness says, in his presence — told them they were taking it for nothing, because the collapse of Soviet power was coming soon, the unbelievers would be deported and their leaders all destroyed. Two further charges were tacked on: that he wandered the collective farms agitating, saying that life in them was the old serfdom over again, where a believer could go to church only by the master's leave; and that as churchwarden he had taken in an unknown person, a Pole, hostile but cunning and never open about it.

He was arrested on 20 September and imprisoned at Rzhev, and the interrogation the same day was short because he would not bear false witness against himself. He admitted the one thing that was true — that the stone had been taken in July — and denied the agitation, three times, in almost the same words. «Нет, виновным себя не признаю и больше показать ничего не могу»: no, I do not admit guilt, and I can say nothing further.

The NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot on 15 October. He was shot two days later, on 17 October 1937.
