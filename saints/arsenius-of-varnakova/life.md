# Arsenius of Varnakova

He was born in the first half of the eleventh century, saint.gr says, most probably in the village of Karya in the prefecture of Phokis; and he is the founder of the monastery of the Panagia of Varnakova, in Doris of Phokis, which he raised in the year 1077. The apolytikion the page prints calls him a lover of stillness, the founder of Varnakova, a bright lamp of piety and an adornment of those who live unwedded, and the kontakion calls him the most holy ascetic of that place, cleansed by the streams of his tears.

His hermitage survives to this day, the page says, in a place hard of access and very beautiful north-west of Varnakova. It has been made into a small church so that it may stay for ever a place of prayer. The sanctuary lies at the back of the cave — fifteen steps down to the holy table — while from the mouth of the cave outwards the nave proper is built. In a wonderful manner, all through the celebration of the Liturgy, holy water drips from the roof of the cave. Outside it and to the right of the church is a small cistern, which most likely gathered the rainwater. The view is astonishing and the landscape solemn: a place of quiet, of calm and of lifting up of the soul.

Arsenius of Varnakova fell asleep in the year 1111. Part of his relics are kept in the monastery of Varnakova in the district of Nafpaktos. The page gives him no see, no journey and no disciples, and beyond the founding, the hermitage and the two years it says nothing more of his life.
