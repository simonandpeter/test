# Eustratius of Arabraca

doxologia.ro prints one life for the five great martyrs of 13 decembrie, and this is it. Under Diocletian and Maximian the Roman power was full of the worship of the idols, and word came to the two emperors that Greater Armenia and Cappadocia were resisting their orders; so the persecutors they sent killed a great multitude of every age. Eustratius, a man of note in the city of Satala, first among his fellows in birth and in office — for he was of the rank of the commanders — was grieved in soul at what was being done to the Christians every day, and prayed and fasted for them; he wished also to enter the struggle of the saints and be counted worthy of their martyrdom, but, weighing the many torments and the cruelty of the tormentor, he was afraid.

He tested himself: he gave his belt to a trusted servant to carry to the priest, to see whether his confession would be received. In those days [Auxentius](/saints/auxentius-companion-of-eustratius) was arrested with other Christians, questioned before the tribunal, tortured and thrown into prison in chains. When Lysias sat in judgement in the middle of the city, Eustratius went into the prison first and asked all those bound there for Christ to pray for him, because he too would share their struggle that same day; and they knelt and prayed, and followed him out.

Standing before the judge with the company of soldiers, he confessed; and Lysias, astonished at so unlooked-for a boldness, had him hoisted in the air by ropes and torn. He answered that the torments were doing him good against the tormentor's will, and that he knew himself now to be a temple of God and of the Holy Spirit. When the sores vanished from his body before the eyes of the crowd and [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-satala) spoke out for him, the tyrant, saying he had no more time for questioning, had them both loaded with chains and shut up with the other Christians, to be kept for another hearing.

Lysias moved them by night, and after two days they came to Arabraca, the home town of Eustratius and [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-satala). As the saints drew near, a citizen of the place, [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius), a simple and unlettered man, was sent out by his own wife — who asked him why he did not go the same road and choose the same portion — and, having kissed his sons and taken leave of her, and commended himself to a rich and honourable citizen named Mucaror, he ran after the saints, declared himself, and was bound with them and thrown into the common prison.

Then the tyrant gave sentence: [Auxentius](/saints/auxentius-companion-of-eustratius), who after many torments still stood by what he had chosen, was to die by the sword and to be denied burial. [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius), brought out next, asked Eustratius what he should answer, being a plain man and no scholar, and was told to say only, unchangeably, «I am a Christian, a servant of Christ» — which he said, and nothing else, to every question about his name, his trade, his country and his faith; so his ankles were bored through, he was hung head downwards and pricked all over with red-hot spits and burned, and gave up his spirit thanking God. [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-satala), brought out naked after him, called the judge a vessel of Satan, and had his tongue cut out, both hands struck off with the sword and his shins broken with staves, and so died too.

[Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) was a soldier, and was found out when Lysias took in his hand the cross that hung at his breast and questioned him. At Nicopolis and then at Sebaste the governor Agricolaus took over the case, with a letter from Lysias, and Eustratius argued with him out of Plato and out of the Scriptures on the justice and the incomprehensibility of God and on why God the Word took our sufferings upon himself, until Agricolaus fell silent for a long while and could not answer.

Then the governor had an iron bed brought and heated red; and [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius), hearing it, leapt onto it of his own accord, signed himself with the Cross, stretched himself out on the fire and cried, «Lord Jesus Christ, into your hands I give up my spirit», and died, and Eustratius answered Amen.

Eustratius was taken back to prison, where he wrote out what he had to say, kept the day fasting and the night in prayer, and was visited by the bishop of Sebaste; he asked the bishop to serve the holy Sacrifice at the third hour of the morning, and communicated. When Agricolaus saw in the morning that nothing would move him, he had a furnace heated.

Eustratius prayed, made the sign of the Cross of Christ, and went into the furnace singing, «Lord Jesus Christ, into your hands I give up my spirit»; and so he gave up his spirit in peace, and the fire did not harm his body nor touch a hair of his head. He was made perfect on the thirteenth day of the month of December, and the bishop of Sebaste took his relics and those of [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) and did what was written in the martyr's own account.

Of the five, Eustratius is the officer of Satala who tested his own courage before he confessed, argued with the governor at Sebaste, was the last to die and died in the furnace; doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mare Mucenic Eustratie» and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps the five on the same day, and saint.gr makes Eustratius the first of them. It says they suffered in the hard persecution of the Christians under Diocletian, and that Eustratius, who was a senior officer, was arrested by Lysias the duke; Lysias tortured him in the most frightful way and then sent him on to Agricolas the eparch. That man, famous for his cruelty towards Christians, made Eustratius walk in iron shoes that had sharp nails inside them, and then finished him by throwing him into the fire. The page's kontakion calls him more eloquent than the orators, and its apolytikion names him the wise one among the five.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the five of them on the same menologion day under one heading, and says that these five brave men shone out like five bright stars in the black time of the Christ-fighting emperors Diocletian and Maximian. It gives him a post and a birthplace, and the two cities are the ones this corpus has used to tell two of the five apart: he was a Roman commander in the city of Satalion, it says, and he and [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius) were both from the city of Aravrakin. Neither reading is chosen here and nothing is renamed on it.

The imperial governors Lysias and Agricola first tortured [Auxentius](/saints/auxentius-companion-of-eustratius), as a priest. Seeing the innocent suffering of the Christians, he gave himself up to Lysias of his own accord and declared that he was a Christian; and while he was being tortured [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-satala) stepped before the judge and cried out, Lysias, I too am a Christian. When they were led with the other martyrs through Aravrakin, Mardarius saw them from the roof of his own house, took leave of his wife and his two small children and ran after them.

Auxentius was beheaded, Eugene and Mardarius died under torture, [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) breathed his last on red-hot iron, and he himself died in a fiery furnace. Before that death, the book says, Saint [Blaise](/saints/blaise-of-sebaste) communed him in the prison, and it sends its reader to its own 11 February for him. Their relics were afterwards taken to Constantinople and buried in the church of their own name, the Holy Five. In that church they were seen alive; and there has remained from him a beautiful prayer which is read at the Saturday midnight office, of which this book prints the first words only.
