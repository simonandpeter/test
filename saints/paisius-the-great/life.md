# Paisius the Great

Paisius was of Egypt, born of believing parents rich in good Christian habits, who had seven children like themselves and enough wealth to help those in need, for the more they gave away to the poor the more God multiplied to them. When their father died the whole care of the children fell on their mother, and Paisius, the youngest of them all, gave her the most anxiety. An angel of the Lord appeared to her one night and told her not to grieve as though she cared for them alone and God did not, but to dedicate to God one son through whom his holy name would be glorified.

She answered that all her children were God's and he might take which he would; and the angel took Paisius by the hand and said, "This one is well pleasing to the Lord." She objected that he was not worthy of God's service, being so young, and that one of the elder sons would do better; and the angel answered that the power of God is shown in the weak, and that the least of them was the one God had chosen.

Growing up in the fear of God he was wounded with divine love and desired the monastic life; and when he came of an age to work the commandments he went into the desert of Scetis, to the divine Pambo, who had the gift of foresight. He first finished well the labours of obedience and patience, doing eagerly everything his spiritual father laid on him, and then took up harsher suffering, wanting to reach the measure of the perfect; and Pambo, seeing him reach for the higher works, guided him carefully.

He wore his body down with fasting and vigil, and kept it subject to the will of the soul. He began to fast the whole week and eat only on Saturday, and his food was a little bread and salt; on the other days, instead of feeding on bread that can be felt, he was gladdened by the bread of the mind, the word of God. He often read and pondered the prophecy of Jeremiah, who appeared to him more than once. Then to the first week he added a second, and fasted two weeks together, eating on the Saturday his usual little bread and salt; and the most wonderful thing was that nobody knew of this life, equal to the angels', except God who sees what is hidden.

The envious enemy, unable to come near him, tried to rob him of his poverty, and so of grace, by moving a rich man to bring him a load of silver and gold. God showed his servant the trick, and when the man came and asked where Paisius was, the saint asked him what he wanted with him, and turned the gift away. The page carries other things of him: an elder whose disobedient disciple had died unrepentant begged God for the man's soul, was twice refused, and was sent at last to make Paisius his intercessor; and once, after twenty-one days of fasting, Christ himself appeared to him and said, "My chosen Paisius, thou sufferest much evil for me," and he answered that it was no great thing, since the Lord's own goodness gave him the strength.

When the report of him had gone through the whole world, [Poemen the Great](/saints/poemen-the-great), then still a young man, longed to see him and asked the elder Paul, a friend of Paisius who often visited him, to take him. Paul went in alone, and when Paisius asked after Poemen and heard that he had stayed outside for shyness, he had him brought in, embraced him and blessed him, and said to Paul: "Remember that this young man will save the souls of many, and through him many will be counted worthy of paradise, for the hand of the Lord is seen with him." At the end the page tells how the abbot Isidore's men searched that desert for the relics of the divine Paul and found them, and how the two fathers, Paul and Paisius, steered the boat that carried them safe to Pisidia, where Isidore laid them in the monastery he had built, and the sick and the possessed who came near them were healed. The account is signed by a humble John.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 19 June, and heads his page «(300 - ;)». The venerable Paisius, it says, came from Egypt and was born in the year 300, of very rich but also devout parents; there were seven children and Paisius was the youngest. He was left fatherless young, and his loving mother brought him up according to the commands of the Gospel. While still young he went into the desert to Abba Pambo, famous for his virtue, and with that experienced spiritual father for guide acquired many divine virtues.

When Pambo died, Paisius withdrew to the western part of the desert and settled there, and crowds came out to him to ask the way of salvation and to hear spiritual and soul-profiting words from his mouth. When he had reached deep old age many of the brethren begged him to leave the desert and come down to the nearest town, so that more people might profit by his holy words; which was done, and many were given the chance to learn the way of salvation from his God-inspired speech. The venerable Paul was his disciple. Having profited many, he died at a very advanced age and was buried in the desert where he had practised his ascesis; and after some years father Isidore took up his holy relics and carried them to Pisidia, where he laid them in his monastery there.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 19 June, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep him on, and it gives the year of death that neither of them had: he passed over into eternity, it says, in the year 400, where the Greek page said only that he died at a very advanced age. It calls him an Egyptian by birth and by language, has his mother vow him to God’s service after a vision in a dream, and says that his teacher received him as a disciple and as fellow-disciple of John Kolovos, who also wrote the life of Paisius — and the Romanian account above this paragraph is signed by a humble John, which this book does not say is the same man, and neither does this paragraph.

By a great grace from God, it says, he had a particular gift of abstinence from food: often he did not taste bread for fifteen days, more often for a week, and once, on John Kolovos’s testimony, he lived seventy days tasting nothing at all. The prophet whose book he loved above others and read most often appeared to him more than once; angels of God appeared to him often; and the Lord Christ himself appeared and said to him, Peace to thee, my beloved servant.

He had a great struggle with the spirits of malice, which showed themselves to him sometimes as they are and sometimes in the form of angels of light, and this servant of God was never once deceived or beguiled by them. He was clear-sighted, and a wonderworker famous throughout Egypt. And this book names the man who took up his relics and carried them to his own monastery and buried them honourably, giving him the see that the other two books left off him — a name the attestation note records and this paragraph does not, because the folder it matches may not take a relation off an identification made in another man’s life.
