# Ludmila of Bohemia

Ludmila was born probably in 856 to noble but pagan parents — her father the count Melnik, a rich man among the great officials of the land, the Romanian Lives say — and at sixteen married the ruler of the Czechs, Bořivoj. They became Christians in the days when the two great missionaries of the Slavs, Methodius and Cyril, were in Bohemia, and the two rulers, with warm Christian zeal, built many churches and called priests for the full christening of the people they governed. But Bořivoj died at only thirty-six, and the young widow, mother of three boys and a girl, gave her whole life to Christ and the Church. When later her grandson Wenceslas took the rule, her daughter-in-law Drahomíra tried to bring back paganism, and Ludmila hindered her in every way, so that Drahomíra swore to take revenge on her even by death; to escape, Ludmila withdrew to a castle near the Bohemian town of Tetín, and there Drahomíra’s men found her and killed her by strangling, in 917, aged sixty-two. Later her grandson Wenceslas carried her holy relics to Prague, where they are kept to this day. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 16 September.
