# Ludmila of Bohemia

Ludmila was born probably in 856 to noble but pagan parents — her father the count Melnik, a rich man among the great officials of the land, the Romanian Lives say — and at sixteen married the ruler of the Czechs, Bořivoj. They became Christians in the days when the two great missionaries of the Slavs, Methodius and Cyril, were in Bohemia, and the two rulers, with warm Christian zeal, built many churches and called priests for the full christening of the people they governed. But Bořivoj died at only thirty-six, and the young widow, mother of three boys and a girl, gave her whole life to Christ and the Church. When later her grandson Wenceslas took the rule, her daughter-in-law Drahomíra tried to bring back paganism, and Ludmila hindered her in every way, so that Drahomíra swore to take revenge on her even by death; to escape, Ludmila withdrew to a castle near the Bohemian town of Tetín, and there Drahomíra’s men found her and killed her by strangling, in 917, aged sixty-two. Later her grandson Wenceslas carried her holy relics to Prague, where they are kept to this day. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 16 September.

The Russian calendar keeps her on 16 September old style, with a troparion, and dates her death to 927. Its life says that she and Bořivoj were baptized by Methodius, archbishop of Moravia, and then built churches and brought priests to serve in them. Bořivoj died young, at thirty-six, and Ludmila, a widow, lived strictly and went on caring for the Church through the thirty-three-year rule of her son Vratislav, whose wife was Drahomíra and whose son was Wenceslas. When Vratislav died and Wenceslas came to the throne at eighteen, Drahomíra began to bring back pagan ways; Ludmila opposed her and withdrew to Tetín, and there two nobles sent by Drahomíra came into the house as she prayed and killed her. She was buried at Tetín by the town wall, many healings came from her grave, and Prince Wenceslas carried her body to Prague, to the church of St George.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, calls her the grandmother of [Wenceslas](/saints/wenceslas-of-czechia), king of the Czechs, and says that her daughter-in-law hated her and sent men who strangled her; that Wenceslas buried her in St George’s at Prague, where many wonders came from her relics; that she suffered at Tetín in 927; and that Wenceslas, a great zealot of the Orthodox faith, was killed by his brother Boleslav. The two calendars’ 927 stands beside the Greek synaxarion’s 917.
