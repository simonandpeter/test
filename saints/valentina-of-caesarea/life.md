# Valentina of Caesarea

Doxologia prints one short life for the three martyrs of this day. Saint [Ennatha](/saints/ennatha-of-gaza) was from the region of Gaza, and Valentina from Caesarea. When the governor Firmilian was sitting in judgement, the brave virgin Ennatha was brought before him, asked whether she would deny Christ, confessed that Christ is God, and was cruelly tortured for it.

Then Valentina, who was a virgin also, could not bear to watch the inhumanity and cruelty of what was being done, and was filled with boldness. When she was ordered to sacrifice to the idols — for there was an altar standing there beside the judgement seat — she struck it with her feet and threw it down together with the fire that was on it. At that the tyrant was enraged and tortured her cruelly as well; and when he left off torturing them he sentenced them both to be burned in the fire. After them [Paul](/saints/paul-with-valentina-and-ennatha) was brought to suffer.

That is the whole of the life the page gives her. It prints no year and no emperor. Its «Tropar» tab holds a sung video whose only words are an invocation, «Sfântă Muceniță Valentina, roagă-te lui Dumnezeu pentru noi!», and not a troparion with its tone, so no hymn is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, with [Ennatha](/saints/ennatha-of-gaza) and [Paul](/saints/paul-with-valentina-and-ennatha), on one page. Valentina was from Caesarea in Palestine, saint.gr says, and Ennatha from Gaza; and it was watching Ennatha's torture that undid her — her honest soul could not bear to keep silent, so she stepped forward decisively and remonstrated with the governor Firmilian, and then had the same fate as Ennatha. At that moment a young man named Paul came forward and branded Firmilian's crime, and the governor in his rage ordered Paul beheaded and the two women burnt alive. The page's distich for the two women makes the flame a betrothal: «Εἰς ἀρραβῶνα παρθένοις κόραις δύο, Ὁ νυμφίος δίδωσι θάρσος πρὸς φλόγα» — the bridegroom gives the two virgin girls courage before the flame as their betrothal gift.
