# Polycarp of the Caves

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 24 July and gives him three sentences, of which the first is a list of his virtues set inside the book’s own quotation marks: he had love towards God and towards his neighbours, joy because of a conscience without reproach, peace because of the conquest of all the passions, patience in temptations and in troubles, mildness in being subject to everyone, mercy towards the poor, faith without doubt in carrying out the commandments, truth in the keeping of vows, meekness in not knowing anger, and self-restraint — and the list ends with an and-so-forth.

He governed the Lavra of the Kyiv Caves so well, the entry goes on, that after his death no worthy successor could be found for him among the monks — for those who were worthy would not out of humility accept the abbacy — so that the brethren were forced to take a secular priest, Basil, for abbot. Saint Polycarp reposed in the Lord in the year 1182.

The entry gives him no birth year, no family and no country, and tells nothing of his life before the abbacy; the by-name in its heading is the whole of what places him.

*After the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 24. јул ст. ст. — [the day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-08-06&prolog=1); read 7 October 2026.*
