# Philip the Deacon

Philip was from Caesarea in Palestine. He married, and had four daughters who were prophetesses. Chosen deacon by the apostles, he was ordained together with Stephen the first martyr and archdeacon, and served the Christians with all diligence, caring for the poor and for the widows. After Stephen was killed and persecution broke out against the Church in Jerusalem, all were scattered through the country of Judaea and Samaria except the twelve apostles; and Philip went to Samaria, where he preached Christ.

The whole people with one soul attended to his words, for he confirmed his preaching with many wonders, healing every sickness and driving out the evil spirits, which cried with a loud voice as they came out of men. There was great joy in that city because of Philip, the life says, for by his coming those who received his preaching and believed in the true Messiah were healed not only of bodily sufferings but of the sufferings of the soul.

There was in Samaria a famous magician named Simon, at whose works all the country marvelled and whom all honoured as a great man. Seeing the great wonders of God that were worked there through Philip’s prayers, and hearing his good tidings of the kingdom of God and of the name of Jesus Christ, he believed and was baptised with the rest.

Then an angel of the Lord appeared to Philip and commanded him to go south, along the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza. Going, he found an Ethiopian man, the eunuch of Candace queen of the Ethiopians, who had come to Jerusalem to worship and was returning to Gaza, which he governed; sitting in his chariot, he was reading the books of the prophets. Philip taught him faith in our Lord Jesus Christ and baptised him.

After the baptism he was caught away by an angel and set down at Azotus, and there he went about the cities preaching the word of God. Afterwards he came again to Caesarea, where his house was, and lived in it with his four daughters, who were virgins and prophetesses. Then he returned once more to Jerusalem, where the apostles ordained him bishop and sent him to Tralles in Asia to preach. There he turned many to Christ and baptised them, and working great wonders he departed to the Lord in deep old age.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 11 October, and says where he came from. saint.gr makes Philip a man of Caesarea in Palestine, a deacon among the seven deacons of the first Church at Jerusalem — it cites Acts 6 — and a married man with four daughters endowed with the gift of prophecy, at Acts 21.8–9. He did not stay in Jerusalem, the page says, but went to Samaria and preached the Gospel there as a genuine apostle of Jesus Christ; and there, by his preaching, he baptised Simon the magician a Christian. The page adds that part of his skull is kept at the monastery of Saint John the Theologian on Patmos.

The Russian calendar keeps him at the head of its own 11 October, «Ап. Филиппа , единого от семи диаконов (I)», under the first of the day's two plain-service icons, and its index page calls him one of the Seventy where the day line calls him one of the seven deacons. The site reaches two lives for him and they agree on the shape. He was born at Caesarea in Palestine, and the first adds that he was married and had children.

After the descent of the Holy Spirit the twelve appointed him to the diaconate in the Church of Jerusalem and gave him, with the other six, charge of the offerings of the faithful and the care of the widows, the orphans and the destitute; the senior of the seven first deacons was the archdeacon [Stephen](/saints/stephen-the-first-martyr). When the persecution began and the Jews stoned Stephen, Philip left Jerusalem for Samaria and preached Christianity there with success. Among those he converted was the famous sorcerer Simon, who, the page quotes from Acts, having been baptised did not leave Philip's side.

At the direction of an angel of the Lord he went out to the road that joins Jerusalem to Gaza and met there the official of the queen of Ethiopia, whom he also brought to Christ. The second life keeps what the first leaves out: that after that nobleman's baptism the angel caught him away to Azotus, and that he went through all the cities preaching the word of God until he came to Caesarea, where he stayed a while in his own house before returning to Jerusalem.

He preached tirelessly in many countries of the Near East about Palestine, and at Jerusalem the apostles ordained him bishop and sent him — to Lydia, says the first life, where he baptised many; to Tralles in Lydia, says the second, where he turned many to Christ and worked a multitude of wonders. Both say he died in deep old age, and neither gives a year; the day line's «(I)» is the only date on the Russian pages. The two lives also call his kinsfolk nothing, where the Russian life of the two women martyrs of this same day names them relatives of the apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle).

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 11 October and calls him an apostle in its heading, but the entry is this Philip’s and not the Philip of the Twelve: born at Caesarea in Palestine, married, and the father of four daughters, all four given the gift of prophecy by God and all four vowed virgins for Christ’s sake. When the holy apostles chose the deacons, Philip was chosen beside [Stephen](/saints/stephen-the-first-martyr) and the rest, and he served the poor and the widows with great diligence.

When the persecution of Christians arose at Jerusalem he withdrew into Samaria and preached the Gospel there, bearing witness to it by many wonders, by the casting out of demons and the healing of the sick. Simon Magus was baptised at the sight of them, and so was [the eunuch of queen Candace](/saints/the-eunuch-of-queen-candace). After that an angel of God carried him in an instant and invisibly to Azotus, where he taught and preached and turned many to Christ. He was appointed bishop in Tralles, and ended in peace in deep old age.
