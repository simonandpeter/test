# Philip the Deacon

Philip was from Caesarea in Palestine. He married, and had four daughters who were prophetesses. Chosen deacon by the apostles, he was ordained together with Stephen the first martyr and archdeacon, and served the Christians with all diligence, caring for the poor and for the widows. After Stephen was killed and persecution broke out against the Church in Jerusalem, all were scattered through the country of Judaea and Samaria except the twelve apostles; and Philip went to Samaria, where he preached Christ.

The whole people with one soul attended to his words, for he confirmed his preaching with many wonders, healing every sickness and driving out the evil spirits, which cried with a loud voice as they came out of men. There was great joy in that city because of Philip, the life says, for by his coming those who received his preaching and believed in the true Messiah were healed not only of bodily sufferings but of the sufferings of the soul.

There was in Samaria a famous magician named Simon, at whose works all the country marvelled and whom all honoured as a great man. Seeing the great wonders of God that were worked there through Philip’s prayers, and hearing his good tidings of the kingdom of God and of the name of Jesus Christ, he believed and was baptised with the rest.

Then an angel of the Lord appeared to Philip and commanded him to go south, along the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza. Going, he found an Ethiopian man, the eunuch of Candace queen of the Ethiopians, who had come to Jerusalem to worship and was returning to Gaza, which he governed; sitting in his chariot, he was reading the books of the prophets. Philip taught him faith in our Lord Jesus Christ and baptised him.

After the baptism he was caught away by an angel and set down at Azotus, and there he went about the cities preaching the word of God. Afterwards he came again to Caesarea, where his house was, and lived in it with his four daughters, who were virgins and prophetesses. Then he returned once more to Jerusalem, where the apostles ordained him bishop and sent him to Tralles in Asia to preach. There he turned many to Christ and baptised them, and working great wonders he departed to the Lord in deep old age.
