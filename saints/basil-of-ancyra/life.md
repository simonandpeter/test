# Basil of Ancyra

Basil was a priest of the Church of Ancyra in Galatia, and gave himself wholly to teaching men the Christian truth and turning them back from the devil's road. He preached continually that a cruel time had come and that the captains of the ranks of hell are of many kinds: Satan has his own servants, some of them in sheep's clothing and inwardly ravening wolves, who sit beside the road of this short life to hunt souls to ruin, and the time itself shows their craft and their snatching.

Without fear he said: I show you all the road that leads to salvation in Christ God, and I convict and reprove the wandering of the unclean. Those who leave the eternal and true God and run to blind, deaf and dumb idols will inherit the flame of the unquenchable fire that comes out of their gods; and we who love Christ must trample down the devil's deceits and the dances of the idol-feasts, and flee the hateful hinderers, strengthened by the help of our Lord Jesus Christ. So he went about the city day after day, confirming each man in the faith.

He was a priest in the time of Eudoxius the Arian, patriarch of Constantinople, who at the Arian council in the city of Constantine forbade him to serve the holy things because of his Orthodoxy; but afterwards two hundred and thirty bishops gathered in Palestine commanded him to serve them again boldly, since, holding the right faith and living a life pleasing to God, he preached the word of faith blamelessly and turned many from error. When every Orthodox Christian soul was being persecuted, he was denounced to the emperor Constantius, son of Constantine the Great, as stirring up the people — and in truth he had taught many to believe rightly, being firm and steadfast in the faith and the ordinances of the fathers.

When Julian the Apostate took the empire and became a destroyer of men's souls, setting his mad laws for the unclean sacrifices before them, Basil stood against it. The count who tried him at the last reproached him with having refused to submit, with having insulted the emperor and with having put the count himself in danger, and said he would pay him what he deserved with torments that would take him quickly out of this life. He had iron spits heated and Basil's body pierced with them, on the back and on the belly.

So tormented, Basil fell to the ground and prayed with a great voice: My light, Christ; my hope, Jesus; calm haven of the storm-tossed; I thank Thee, Lord, God of my fathers, that Thou hast brought my soul out of the lowest hell and hast kept Thy glorious name in me, so that, finishing my course in chains, I may share the eternal rest, according to the promises made to my fathers by Thee, the Great High Priest, Jesus Christ our Lord. Now receive my spirit in peace, abiding unchanged in this confession. And as the burning spits pierced him through, he fell asleep sweetly and gave up his spirit into the hands of God.

He died, the page says, on the twenty-eighth day of January; and after Julian the Apostate was killed and swiftly destroyed, the much-suffering body of the martyr was honoured by the Christians on the twenty-second day of March, and on that day his memory was set. His brave suffering, it ends, confirmed all the Christians in the true faith in Jesus Christ.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, gives him a year and turns the last part of the story on the emperor himself. saint.gr places him in the years of Julian the Apostate (360–363) and says he was denounced to the eparch Saturninus for making light of Julian's measures against the Church and accusing them. Ordered by the eparch to declare publicly that he denied Jesus Christ, the priest smiled at the demand and declared that the whole of his life belonged to the Gospel and to the Saviour of souls; and when Saturninus threatened him with harsh torture if he would not deny Christ, he answered — the page quotes him — how could you have thought that I would deny Christ, when even the last of the laymen of my parish who believe in him is ready to pour out his blood for our holy faith? Saturninus then tortured him and put him in prison.

Some days later Julian passed through Ancyra, was told about the presbyter Basil and ordered him brought before him; but he found the Christian priest's faith stronger still, and gave the order by which they put him to death in a martyr's way. So Basil was martyred in the year 362. Its couplet turns on the manner of it — the prophet said that a whole belly feels pain, but the martyr did not say that a pierced one does; Basil was pierced with skewers on the twenty-second — and the page names a portion of his relic at the monastery of Boura in Megalopolis.
