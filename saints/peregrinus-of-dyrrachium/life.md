# Peregrinus, martyred at Dyrrachium

saint.gr's calendar for 7 Ιουλίου keeps seven men in one line, «Άγιοι Περεγρίνος, Λουκιανός, Πομπηίος, Ησύχιος, Παππίας, Σατορνίνος και Γερμανός», and tells them together. The page says these saints came from Italy and lived in the years of the emperor Trajan, in the year 98. When the persecution against the Christians broke out they went aboard a ship and sailed away to the city of Dyrrachium.

There they saw the martyrdom of [Astius](/saints/astius-of-dyrrachium), bishop of Dyrrachium, whom the same calendar keeps on the day before; and because they called him blessed they were arrested. Having confessed that they were Christians, they were thrown into the Adriatic sea by order of the proconsul Agricola, and so they took the unfading crown of martyrdom. Their holy relics, the page says, came out of the sea and were buried in the sand.

Seventy years later the relics were made known to the bishop of Alexandria, who took them up, buried them with honours and built a small church in their name. The page gives no age, no rank and no separate manner of death for any one of the seven, and it prints no hymn under their line — only the synaxarion couplet: «Συν έξ κατήλθεν εις βυθόν Περεγρίνος, / Aθλήσεως εκείθεν αγρεύσων στέφος» — Peregrinus went down into the deep with six others, to fish up the crown of the contest from there.
