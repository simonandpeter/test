# Arsenius of Komel

Arsenius of Komel was born at Moscow, of the noble family of the Sakharusovs. In his youth he took the tonsure at the Trinity-Sergius monastery, where he copied books — a Gospel copied by him in 1506 is known — and in 1525–1527 he was abbot of the Trinity monastery, often withdrawing to the lonely Makhrishche monastery; the grand prince Vasily, visiting, was astonished to see the abbot of a rich house in old patched clothes, and the brethren explained that Arsenius longed to go into the desert. Going with his cell-attendant into the Komel forests fifty versts from Vologda he made a great wooden cross and set out through the forest with it on his shoulders to find the place of the future hermitage; crossing a bog he stumbled under the weight of the cross and fell, and a ray from heaven lit him at that moment and made him stop there — he planted the cross and built the first cell. Local hunters killed his attendant and forced him away to the Shilegon forest, where monks gathered to him and then people fleeing a Tatar raid; seeking silence he wished for a wilder place, and in 1530 the grand prince gave him a charter for land in the Komel forest on the river Kokhtysh, where with his disciple Gerasim he began to clear the forest, taming the beasts by prayer; when a few monks had gathered to him he built a church of the Placing of the Robe of the Mother of God, and visiting the Shilegon hermitage taught the peasants who had settled round the monastery to keep the feasts and Sundays holy — once when they disobeyed and worked on a feast a sudden wind scattered all the sheaves — he passed his life in fasting, prayer and constant labour and died on 24 August 1550; his life, written soon after, burned in the fire at the Komel monastery in 1596 and was restored in brief from the surviving notes and his posthumous wonders by the monk John. A hundred years after his death the abbot Joasaph built a stone church of the Placing of the Robe in the monastery, whose two chapels showed the spiritual tie of teacher and disciple — the left dedicated to Sergius of Radonezh, the right to Arsenius of Komel. The Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, tells it in a sentence: of a great family of Moscow, a monk in his youth at the house of Sergius of Radonezh, chosen abbot as an exemplary monk, he longed for prayerful solitude and went into the Komel forest, where he laboured until death, fighting bravely with great temptations of the demons, and reposed in 1550. The Russian calendar keeps him with a troparion and kontakion.
