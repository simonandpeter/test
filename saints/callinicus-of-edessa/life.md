# Callinicus of Edessa

On 23 June 2020 the Ecumenical Patriarchate decided the canonisation of Callinicus, Metropolitan of Edessa, Pella and Almopia (1919–1984), and his commemoration each year on 8 August, the day of his repose. The account doxologia.ro prints is a long testimony by a spiritual son of his who lived beside him from his student years until the hour of his death, and who has written three books about him: a first, The Witness of a Life, immediately after that death in 1984; a second and much larger one, The Adornment of the Church, in 1998; and a third in 2015.

When he was tonsured a monk in 1957 he chose the name Callinicus — in Greek, the good victor — not because any bishop before him had borne it, as often happens in church life, but because he wanted to walk in the steps of the martyrs and finish the good fight as a victor. All his life he loved the martyrs and spoke of them with fire. His spiritual son first met him at Agrinio, when he was Protosynkellos of the Holy Metropolis of Aetolia and Akarnania, and was struck by his ascetic manner of life, by the joy on his face and by his living preaching, above all his gift for speaking with the young.

In time he was elected Bishop and Metropolitan of Edessa, Pella and Almopia. The hierarch who ordained him called him in his address simple, temperate, struggling, unfeigned, and praised his rare virtues: his faith, his holiness and purity, his unquenched love of God and his exemplary poverty and freedom from possessions. Having frequented the Holy Mountain as a student, his disciple found in him so much of the Athonite fathers that he felt him to be an Athonite bishop.

After many years of pastoral labour he was struck down by cancer, a tumour on the brain, and his illness lasted seven months and was painful. He kept the remembrance of death always, and used to say that the one verb we need to know and to conjugate is the verb to depart. He glorified God unceasingly for the illness and prayed that God's will be done; he thanked those who served him and humbled himself beyond measure. Sophrony of Essex, who visited him in the hospital in London, bore witness to the deep watchfulness that marks a true hesychast. I do not mind the illness, he said; let me be a saint even with one leg or one eye. [Paisios the Athonite](/saints/paisios-the-athonite) called him a holy bishop, and said he had never until then seen another bishop like Callinicus.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 8 August, and saint.gr fills in the life the Romanian notice summarised. It gives his name in the world, Dimitrios Poulos, and his birth at the village of Sitaralona of Agrinio on 26 January 1919, to pious parents, George and Aikaterini, with a priest grandfather and a presbytera grandmother who shaped his upbringing. He took a first-class degree in theology at Athens in 1942, served as secretary of the metropolis of Aetolia and Acarnania, and spent three years from 1946 as a preacher attached to a mountain commando company.

He was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Myrtia on 23 November 1957 and took the name Callinicus, was ordained deacon the next day by his own brother, then bishop of Didymoteicho, and priest on 1 December of the same year. On 24 June 1967, after twenty-five years' service in his home metropolis, he was elected Metropolitan of Edessa and Pella, and he reposed on 8 August 1984 after a seven-month illness, with twenty-nine bishops at his funeral. At his own wish he was buried in the public cemetery of Edessa, to lie near the people he had served. The Holy Synod of the Ecumenical Patriarchate numbered him among the saints on 23 June 2020, and saint.gr prints the communiqué whole.
