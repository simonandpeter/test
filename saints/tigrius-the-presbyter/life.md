# Tigrius

saint.gr's calendar for 12 Ιανουαρίου keeps two men in one line, «Άγιοι Τίγριος ο Πρεσβύτερος και Ευτρόπιος» — Tigrius the presbyter and [Eutropius](/saints/eutropius-companion-of-tigrius) — and the page behind the line has one sentence for both of them: «Οι Άγιοι Τίγριος και Ευτρόπιος έζησαν κατά τους χρόνους του βασιλέως Αρκαδίου (395 - 408 μ.Χ.). Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο των Αγίων.»

That is: they lived in the years of the emperor Arcadius, whom the page dates 395 to 408, and we have no details of the lives of these saints. So what the calendar holds of Tigrius is a name, the rank of presbyter, a reign, a companion and a day. It prints no city, no manner of death, and no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 16 June, where the Greek calendar keeps him and his companion on 12 January, and it gives the life saint.gr said it did not have. These two, it says, were of the clergy of [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom). When malicious men drove Chrysostom out of Constantinople the cathedral church caught fire, and the flame rose from it and fell on the houses of his persecutors. The people saw the finger of God in it; his enemies laid the fire at the door of his adherents, and many of those men suffered for it — among them Tigrius the presbyter and [Eutropius](/saints/eutropius-companion-of-tigrius) the reader.

In his youth Tigrius was the slave of a rich man, who had him castrated. Freed from that slavery, he gave himself wholly to the service of the Church, and in that service he shone like a bright lamp. The book sets four words of its own about him in quotation marks: meek, humble, merciful and hospitable. The city governor — an unbaptised Greek named Optatus, who raised the hunt against Chrysostom’s people with particular malice — put him to great tortures, and then sent him into exile in Mesopotamia, where he died in prison. This book gives no year for any of it, and the reign the Greek page named is left to stand as the only bracket there is.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps the two of them on one page (life1324) and one line. It says that they were contemporaries of «святитель Иоанн Златоуст» and of his clergy, and that this presbyter was a meek and merciful pastor. When the archbishop was driven out of Constantinople in 404 the two of them were seized as his adherents and charged with setting fire to the churches and the buildings of his opponents. He was put to the torture, beaten with straps, and exiled to Mesopotamia, where he died in confinement.
