# Herman of Alaska

Doxologia's life says he preached Orthodoxy in Alaska together with nine other Russian missionaries, and calls him the first Orthodox saint of the American continent. He was born near Moscow, the page says, around the year 1757; at sixteen he entered the monastery of Saint Sergius and the Holy Trinity, and lived in one of its dependencies near the Gulf of Finland. There he was healed of a wound under his chin after praying before an icon of the Mother of God: in his sleep she touched him, and when he woke the wound had closed.

After nearly six years at that dependency he went to the monastery of Valaam, near Lake Ladoga, and continued his life of prayer and asceticism in that old home of Orthodox monasticism. In 1793 the Synod of the Russian Church resolved to send a mission to christen the inhabitants of Alaska, which had become Russian territory at the end of the eighteenth century and whose people, mostly Aleuts, were pagan; Father Herman was one of the ten sent.

He wrote in a letter: «Domnul să fie lăudat! Am botezat mai bine de șapte mii de americani și am săvârșit mai bine de două mii de cununii… Îi iubim și ei ne iubesc pe noi; sunt buni, însă săraci. Își doresc atât de mult să se boteze, încât și-au distrus și și-au ars idolii» — more than seven thousand baptised and more than two thousand marriages blessed, and a people so eager for baptism that they burned their idols.

A description from 1819 gives him of middling height, delicate build, with mild bluish eyes. His bed was a bench covered with a sealskin, his pillow two bricks and a plank his blanket; he ate with measure, slept little, prayed much and worked hard. He bore with the weaknesses of others and did not press them to his own austerity, and he was gentle with the wild animals: the birds and squirrels were his companions, and the wild bear ate from his hand.

As he grew in age and in holiness he was granted the sight of angels and of evil spirits, received power over the elements and the gift of prophecy. On the night he fell asleep in the Lord, the people of the island of Afognak saw a pillar of light above New Valaam and fell on their knees crying «Sfântul nostru a plecat de la noi» — our saint has left us; and in another village, that same night, they saw something like a human form rising from New Valaam towards heaven.

He left the Aleuts no buildings, the page ends, no churches, schools or hospitals, but built living churches and faith in their souls; and today all the Aleuts of Alaska are Orthodox and have priests from among themselves. The page gives no year for his death.

The Greek calendar keeps him too, on 9 August, and adds nothing to the life. saint.gr's whole notice is a disclaimer, «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Ρώσου Αγίου» — we have no details of the life of the Russian saint — with a head line giving his years as 1756 to 1836 and a note that he is counted among all the saints of Siberia. The 1756 of that head line is a year earlier than the «în jurul anului 1757» the Romanian page gives above, and neither page argues for its figure, so both are left standing.
