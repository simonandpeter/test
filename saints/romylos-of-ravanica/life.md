# Romylos of Ravanica

The life of Romylos is in manuscript 154 of the skete of Kafsokalyvia, written by his disciple Gregory. He was born about 1300 in the Danubian city of Vidin to devout parents, a Greek father and a Bulgarian mother, and named Raiko at holy baptism; from childhood he showed a longing for learning, and his teachers, admiring his wisdom and good sense, called him the child-elder. Grown up, to avoid the marriage his parents were planning he fled secretly to a monastery in the province of Tarnovo, where after the canonical probation he was tonsured in the small schema with the name Romanus and served with great reverence in the church as sacristan. In those same years Gregory of Sinai, kept on 6 April, left the Holy Mountain with his disciples and settled at Paroria, today’s Strandzha, on the Byzantine-Bulgarian border, and when Romanus heard of this teacher of noetic prayer and the hesychast life he asked the abbot’s blessing to put himself under his guidance, taking with him another brother, Hilarion. Gregory received them with great joy and, as Romanus was strong, of powerful constitution, gave him the heaviest and most laborious services, which he — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here. The Romanian Lives, by Father Makarios of Simonopetra, say the virtues Romanus had gained as a second nature and his constant occupation with prayer made him worthy of much grace; the Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 18 September.
