# Paul of Ptolemais

Paul and his sister [Juliana](/saints/juliana-of-ptolemais) were put to death under the emperor Aurelian (270–275) in the Phoenician city of Ptolemais. When the emperor came to the city, Paul, standing among those who met him, made the sign of the cross, and was noticed; he was seized and thrown into prison, and when he was brought to trial the next day he confessed his faith in Christ openly and boldly, and was cruelly tortured. Juliana, seeing her brother suffer, began to accuse the emperor before everyone of injustice and cruelty, and was tortured too. They were beaten, torn with iron hooks and burned on red-hot grills, and their wonderful endurance could not be broken; three of the soldiers who tortured them, struck by the greatness of their spirit, believed in Christ — Quadratus, Acacius and Stratonicus — and died with them.

doxologia.ro keeps the two on 4 martie and prints a longer life of them. It says that Aurelian had given his order against the Christians throughout the world, and that he came through Assyria into Phoenicia and entered Ptolemais himself to force them to sacrifice; that Paul, seeing him come in, signed his face with the cross and said to his sister, «Îndrăznește, sora mea, și nu te teme» — take courage, sister, and do not be afraid, for a great trial is coming upon the Christians. After they were put to the sword their bodies were left unburied outside the city with soldiers watching from a distance, and for seven days and nights the dogs would not touch them, the wolves that came sat beside them as guards, and the carrion birds flew over without settling; when the guard was withdrawn by night the Christians came and buried them with honour.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives the executioners names. saint.gr makes Paul and [Juliana](/saints/juliana-of-ptolemais) brother and sister, living in the years of the emperor Aurelian; their parents brought them up in the whole life of the Christian faith, fit not only to believe and to be adorned by an upright life but to carry their cross on their shoulder and to be ready and willing for every sacrifice of self-denial in Christ. When their father and mother left this life the two kept to the road God had marked for them: Paul was eminent among the young men, to whom he often became a teacher by his clear knowledge and his pure life, and Juliana the same among the young girls, so that brother and sister became two of the brightest ornaments of the Christian Church in Ptolemais. That drew the hatred of the idolaters, who denounced the pair to the emperor Aurelian when he once passed through their city.

Aurelian could not shake their faith either, and ordered them hard tortured; and while the executioners [Stratonicus](/saints/stratonicus-the-executioner), [Codratus](/saints/codratus-the-executioner) and [Acacius](/saints/acacius-the-executioner) were torturing them they marvelled at the courage of their souls, confessed Christ and were beheaded on the spot. At the last the new executioners burnt the flesh of the two with lighted torches, and seeing that the brother and sister stayed unmoved in their faith, beheaded them. The page adds that their memory is kept again on the seventeenth of August, which is the day this folder's Russian row already holds.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day with his sister. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the pair four sentences: brother and sister of Ptolemais in Phoenicia, frightfully tortured for Christ by the emperor Aurelian and beheaded at the last. At their torments, it says, many wonders were shown, and many of the heathen who saw them turned to the faith, so that some of those too were beheaded in the year 273 and received their crowns. It names none of them, and the three executioners the other calendars name on this day are not in it.
