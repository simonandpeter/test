# Juventinus the Soldier

Juventinus and [Maximus](/saints/maximus-the-soldier) were soldiers — bodyguards of the emperor Julian the Apostate, under whom they suffered. Once at Antioch Julian decided to defile the Christians by sprinkling all the food sold in the markets with the blood of idol-sacrifices; the two openly condemned the emperor’s act and boldly rebuked him for his apostasy from the Christian faith — at a soldiers’ feast, the Prologue says, which Chrysostom tells in a homily in their praise, and when men of the emperor came to the prison to turn them from the true faith, saying how many of their comrades had denied Christ, they answered like knights: that is just why we must stand bravely and offer ourselves as a sacrifice for their apostasy. Both, after merciless beatings, were put to death in prison at the impious emperor’s order, about 361–363 — beheaded in the dark of night, the Prologue says; their relics were found and proved wonderworking.
