# Erasmus of the Kyiv Caves

The whole of what the Russian calendar gives of Erasmus is a letter quoted in his life: what [Simon of Vladimir](/saints/simon-of-vladimir), the bishop who died in 1226, wrote of him to a friend in the monastic life. There was in the Caves monastery, Simon writes, a black-robed monk called Erasmus, who gained the inheritance of glory in the kingdom of heaven because he spent everything he had on the adorning of the church of the Caves: he overlaid a great many icons with metal, and they stood above the altar still in Simon's own day.

Then came his temptation. When he had made himself poor the brethren began to think little of him, and the spirit of malice put it into his mind that there had been no profit in spending his goods on the church and that he would have done better to spend them on the needy. Erasmus did not weigh the thought; he fell into despondency and began to live carelessly. God, who is generous and merciful, saved him for the sake of his one virtue by sending him a heavy illness, and he lay seven days speechless with his eyes shut, barely breathing.

On the eighth day the brethren came, and hearing how hard he breathed they said: woe to the soul of this brother, who has lived in sloth; it sees something and is troubled, and will not come out of the body. Then Erasmus suddenly stood up as though he were well, and said: fathers and brethren, I am indeed a sinner, and an unrepentant one, as you said; but our venerable fathers [Anthony](/saints/anthony-of-the-caves) and [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) have appeared to me now and have said that they prayed for me, and that the Lord has given me time to repent.

And afterwards, he said, I saw the most pure Mother of God with her Son Christ God in her arms, and she said to me: Erasmus, since thou hast adorned my church with icons, I also will adorn thee in the kingdom of my Son; rise, repent, put on the angelic habit, and on the third day I shall take thee to myself, as one who has loved the beauty of my house. Saying this, Erasmus began to confess his sins before them all without shame, then went into the church and was tonsured into the great schema, and on the third day he died, about the year 1160. He was buried in the Near Caves.

The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion for him, both of them about that spending of his goods on the icons and about the repentance at the end of it, and his life page names a second memory on 28 сентября with the venerable fathers of the Near Caves.
