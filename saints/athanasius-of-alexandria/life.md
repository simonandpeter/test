# Athanasius of Alexandria

Athanasius attended the Council of Nicaea in 325 as a deacon, and spent the next
forty-five years defending its formula against the Arian party that dominated
imperial policy for much of that time. Made bishop of Alexandria in 328, he was
exiled five times under four emperors and spent roughly seventeen of his
episcopal years away from his see — once hiding among the desert monks, whose
way of life he did more than anyone to publicise.

His *Life of Anthony*, written soon after
[Anthony's](/saints/anthony-the-great) death in 356, became one of the most
widely read texts of late antiquity and carried Egyptian monasticism into Latin
Europe. His Festal Letter of 367 lists the twenty-seven books of the New
Testament as they now stand, the earliest surviving list to do so.

He is venerated in the Chalcedonian and non-Chalcedonian churches alike, which
is less common than it sounds: he died in 373, nearly eighty years before the
division that separates them.
