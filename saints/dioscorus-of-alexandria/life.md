# Dioscorus of Alexandria

Successor to Cyril in the see of Alexandria in 444, Dioscorus presided over the
council held at Ephesus in 449 which reinstated the archimandrite Eutyches and
deposed his opponents. Pope Leo called that assembly a *latrocinium*, a den of
robbers, and the name stuck in Western usage. Two years later the Council of
Chalcedon deposed Dioscorus in turn — formally for procedural and disciplinary
offences rather than for heresy, a distinction his defenders have pressed ever
since. He was exiled to Gangra in Paphlagonia and died there in 454.

Chalcedon is the fracture that separates the Oriental Orthodox churches from the
Chalcedonian ones, and Dioscorus stands on the far side of it. The Coptic church
counts him among its patriarchs and venerates him as a confessor for the exile;
Rome and Constantinople record a deposition.

His case is the mirror of [Nestorius](/saints/nestorius). The two were condemned
by opposite parties, twenty years apart, and each is honoured by a communion that
the other's accusers belong to. Neither can be represented by a single row
saying whether he is a saint.
