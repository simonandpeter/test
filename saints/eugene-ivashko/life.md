# Eugene (Ivashko)

He was born on 26 August 1899, in the Minsk diocese, into the family of a priest named Alexander Ivashko. He finished eight classes of the Minsk seminary in 1920, taught in a school of that province for two and a half years, and in August 1922 passed the examinations the diocesan commission required of a man who would be ordained. The metropolitan of Minsk ordained him priest on 1 April 1924 to the church at Pavlovichi in the Bobruisk district, and from then on he was moved and moved again — to Loshnitsa in 1926, to Yuryevichi in 1927, and in that year arrested by the authorities and sentenced to three years.

In 1930 he was sent to the village of Pogromnoye in the Buzuluk district, and had barely begun to serve when the church was closed; he was moved to Medvetskoye in the same district. On 19 February 1931 his wife Antonina died and left him with two children, a daughter of five and a son of one year and eleven months, and his mother's sister came to help him bring them up. In 1932 he was sent to Voskresenskoye in the Rzhev district, and in the same year to the church of the Dormition in the town of Murom.

He served at Murom nine months, and when he left the parishioners gave him a written address, which the calendar prints whole. It tells him that his nine months there had won the hearts of all of them; that despite his youth he had kept the services with such zeal that two bishops had given him three awards in that short time; that he was an example to the clergy; and that they all knew how hard and sorrowful his own life was and how patiently and without complaint he carried the cross sent him. It ends: bitter and heavy it is for us to part with you for ever, but the memory of you as a deeply honoured pastor will live in our hearts always.

He went on to Zavidovo and then to Spas-Bereza in the Olenino district, where the bishop made him dean. The diocese was in unceasing war with the renovationists, most of whom did the will of the OGPU and the NKVD and were kept by them; and the bishop gave him a letter he had written in 1929 to a priest who was going over to them, to copy out and use in that fight. In the summer of 1937 the authorities demanded the church be shut on the pretext that the building was unsafe, knowing how far the peasants had been ruined by state levies and loans and reckoning that the money could not be raised. He put it to his parishioners plainly — if you do not give for the repair now, they will close it and you will have nowhere to pray — and the money came in, the calendar says, as only money for a church ever does come in, and the church was saved.

That summer the district NKVD began calling in the people of Spas-Bereza and the villages round it and taking statements meant to prove him guilty. The indictment was drawn up on 5 October 1937 on the evidence of what the page calls duty witnesses, and he was arrested and shut in the prison at Zubtsov on 22 October. The last interrogation was on 5 November. The investigator had not written any of the earlier ones down, hoping to break him first by asking him to pity the children who would be left orphans — the daughter was twelve and the son eight — and when that failed he put the charges on paper one by one: hostility to the Soviet power over the elections; agitation against the loan and for the overthrow of the government; praise of the enemies of the people and blame of Stalin; telling believers not to go out to work on Sundays and feasts. To each of the four he gave the same answer, and the record keeps it four times over: in this I do not admit myself guilty.

He was thirty-eight. On 9 November a troika of the NKVD ordered him shot, and he was shot on 11 November 1937, which is the civil date of this menologion day. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia by the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
