# Sergius (Krestnikov)

Sergius, in the world Sergei Alexandrovich Krestnikov, was born on 1 July 1893 in Moscow. Having finished school he entered the Moscow Pokrovsky monastery in 1905 and sang in the choir there until 1915; then for two years he sang in the choirs of Moscow churches, and having by that time made his final choice he entered the Trinity-[Sergius](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) Lavra as a novice in 1918, and lived there until the house was closed in 1922.

After that he sang in the choir of the Paraskeva church at Sergiev Posad, and in 1928 became reader at the church in the Kukuyevo cemetery, where the elder of the Zosimova hermitage, the hieroschemamonk [Alexis (Soloviev)](/saints/alexis-of-zosimova), lay buried. There the novice Sergius was reader and watchman and chairman of the church council at once.

In 1935 the NKVD arrested the former inhabitants of the Lavra who were living in lodgings about the Posad, and the priests serving in the town's churches. Sergius was arrested on 21 October 1935 and shut in the Butyrka prison in Moscow, and the questioning of witnesses and of the accused began. Some of them deposed that he had said the clergy were being tormented with taxes and that the Soviet power had set itself to liquidate every Orthodox church in Russia, and the deposition put into his mouth a further sentence blaming the demolitions on a people, which is not repeated here: the same case charged him with reading and circulating antisemitic literature, and the words come to us only through evidence collected to convict him.

The investigator asked what the monastics talked about among themselves. Of religious and spiritual matters, he answered: some of them held that the times of the antichrist had already come and that the persecution for religious conviction was growing fiercer for that reason, but he thought otherwise — that what they were all living through was the time before the antichrist's coming, and not the thing itself. Asked how he had reacted to the closing of the churches, he said that the Soviet power closed churches without the consent of the faithful and did wrong in it, and exiled the clergy for nothing and deprived them of a normal life.

Asked whether he had voiced his discontent to those about him, he said he was discontented with the Soviet power because it closed churches by force and without the consent of the faithful and exiled clergy and faithful for nothing. Asked whether he admitted the charge, he said he was anti-Soviet in his mind, because the Soviet power had organised a persecution of religion, exiled believers for nothing, and closed churches without their consent — and he said it in the same words a third time.

With that the questioning ended, and on 5 January 1936 the investigation itself was closed. He was charged with having been a member of a counter-revolutionary grouping, with spreading false counter-revolutionary rumours of a persecution of believers in the USSR, with glorifying for counter-revolutionary ends the grave of «старца Алексея», the elder Alexis, with putting about false rumours of miraculous healings at that grave, and with reading and circulating counter-revolutionary antisemitic literature.

On 8 January 1936 a Special Conference of the NKVD gave him five years in corrective labour camps and he was sent to Ukhtpechlag, to the town of Chibyu. He came into the camps at the time when the persecution rose again and did not pass over those who were already in camps and prisons: on 21 January 1938 he was arrested a second time, charged with anti-Soviet agitation, and shut in the investigation block at Chibyu. Asked whether he admitted the charge, he said he did not: he had told the prisoners that life in the camp was hard, and he had nothing more to say.

On 15 March 1938 an NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot. The novice Sergius Krestnikov was shot on 25 April 1938 and buried in a common grave nobody marked. The Sretensky calendar's own line gives none of this: it reads «Прмч. Сергия (1938).», a baptismal name, a rank and a year, and the index entry behind it adds the surname, the rank of novice, and one other day, the synaxis of the new martyrs.
