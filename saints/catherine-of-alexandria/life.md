# Catherine of Alexandria

She was the daughter of Konstos, governor of Alexandria under Maximian. Living in the capital of Hellenistic learning, and possessed of a rare beauty and a rarer intellect, she was given an excellent education in the works of the greatest philosophers and teachers of antiquity. Young men of the worthiest families of the empire sought her hand and she would have none of them, telling her parents that she would marry only someone who surpassed her in nobility, wealth, comeliness and wisdom.

Her mother, a secret Christian, sent her to her own spiritual father, an elder living in a cave outside the city. He listened, and told her he knew of one who surpassed her in everything: whose countenance is more radiant than the shining of the sun, by whose wisdom all creation is governed, whose riches are given to all the nations of the world and never diminish, and whose compassion is unequalled.

The description woke in her an ardent desire to see him. Do as I tell you, the elder said, and you will gaze upon that countenance; and at parting he gave her an icon of the Theotokos with the Child on her arm, and told her to pray with faith to the Queen of Heaven.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 25 November, and sets out her learning. saint.gr makes her the child of a family of nobles of Alexandria, «θυγάτηρ βασιλίσκου τινός ονομαζομένου Kώνστου», the daughter of a petty king named Konstos, and dates her martyrdom to the beginning of the fourth century. She was exceedingly clever and eager to learn: at eighteen she already had the Greek, Roman and Latin literature and philosophy — Homer, the Latin poet Virgil, the physicians Asclepius, Hippocrates and Galen, Aristotle and Plato, the philosophers Philistion and Eusebius, the great magicians Jannes and Jambres, Dionysius and the Sibyl and others — and she was perfectly trained besides in the doctrines of the Christian faith. When the persecution of the Christians was being carried on under Maxentius, son of Maximian, Catherine was not afraid but spread it abroad with boldness that Jesus Christ is the only true God; for which the eparch of the region arrested her, and tried by argument to make her deny her faith. Finding her words too strong for him, he called a public debate with the worthiest rhetors of Alexandria, and Catherine silenced them — and not only that: some of those who had argued with her were persuaded and embraced the Christian faith, and the same calendar keeps them on this day as [the hundred and fifty rhetors](/saints/hundred-and-fifty-rhetors), and [Basilissa](/saints/basilissa-wife-of-maxentius), Maxentius's wife, with them. Faced with that, the eparch ordered her tortured hard, in the hope that she would bend and deny Christ; she stayed unshaken, and at the last she was beheaded at his order.

The Охридски пролог makes her the daughter of the emperor Costus, who after her father's death lived with her mother at Alexandria. Her mother was a secret Christian, and through her own spiritual father she brought Catherine to the faith of Christ as well. In a vision Catherine received a ring from the Lord Jesus himself as a token of her betrothal to him, and that ring, the book says, stands on her hand to this day. She was greatly gifted by God and very well schooled in Greek philosophy, in medicine, in rhetoric and in logic, and she was besides of unusual bodily beauty.

When the wicked emperor Maxentius was offering sacrifice to the idols himself and ordering others to do the same, Catherine came boldly before him and exposed the error of his idolatry. Seeing her stronger than himself in wisdom and knowledge, the emperor called fifty of the wisest men to dispute with her about the faith and put her to shame; but she outwitted them and shamed them instead. The enraged emperor ordered all fifty of the wise men burned in the fire — and before their death, at Catherine's prayer, every one of them confessed the name of Christ and declared himself a Christian. While the martyr was in prison she brought the emperor's own commander [Porphyrius](/saints/porphyrius-the-stratelates) with two hundred soldiers to the true faith, and the empress herself, [Augusta-Basilissa](/saints/basilissa-wife-of-maxentius); and all of them suffered for Christ.

At her own torture an angel of God appeared to her, stopped the wheel on which the holy maiden was being tortured and broke it; and after that the Lord Christ himself appeared and comforted her. After many torments she was beheaded with the sword in her eighteenth year, on the twenty-fourth of November in the year 310, and milk ran from her body instead of blood. Her wonderworking relics rest on Sinai.
