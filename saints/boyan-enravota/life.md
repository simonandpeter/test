# Boyan Enravota

Boyan, surnamed Enravota, was the son of Omurtag, king of Bulgaria from 816 to 831, at a time when the Bulgarians were still in the darkness of idolatry. Although he was the first-born, the power did not pass to him at his father's death but to his younger brother Malamir. From the beginning of the ninth century, that is from the start of the reign of the war-loving Krum, 803 to 814, Christians had been persecuted in Bulgaria, and particularly in the districts that had been taken from the Byzantines.

When his brother had the rule in hand he wished to be sure that Boyan had not embraced the Christian faith, and so invited him to take part in a sacrificial banquet of the idolaters. At the saint's refusal to sacrifice to the idols, the king ordered him put to death by beheading. It was about the year 833.

On the testimony of Theophylact, archbishop of Ochrid from 1090 to 1126, saint.gr says, the prince prophesied at the moment of his martyrdom: «Αυτή η πίστη, για την οποία σήμερα πεθαίνω, θα διαδοθεί στην χώρα των Βουλγάρων» — this faith, for which I die today, will spread through the land of the Bulgarians; you try in vain to destroy it by my death. The sign of the Cross will be everywhere. Churches pure and undefiled will be raised to the honour of the true God, and priests pure and undefiled will serve him; your idols and your altars will be destroyed as though they had never been. Three years later Malamir died, and in the year 865 Boyan's descendant was baptised a Christian and declared the Orthodox faith the faith of the state.
