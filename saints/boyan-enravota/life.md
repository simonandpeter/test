# Boyan Enravota

Boyan, surnamed Enravota, was the son of Omurtag, king of Bulgaria from 816 to 831, at a time when the Bulgarians were still in the darkness of idolatry. Although he was the first-born, the power did not pass to him at his father's death but to his younger brother Malamir. From the beginning of the ninth century, that is from the start of the reign of the war-loving Krum, 803 to 814, Christians had been persecuted in Bulgaria, and particularly in the districts that had been taken from the Byzantines.

When his brother had the rule in hand he wished to be sure that Boyan had not embraced the Christian faith, and so invited him to take part in a sacrificial banquet of the idolaters. At the saint's refusal to sacrifice to the idols, the king ordered him put to death by beheading. It was about the year 833.

On the testimony of Theophylact, archbishop of Ochrid from 1090 to 1126, saint.gr says, the prince prophesied at the moment of his martyrdom: «Αυτή η πίστη, για την οποία σήμερα πεθαίνω, θα διαδοθεί στην χώρα των Βουλγάρων» — this faith, for which I die today, will spread through the land of the Bulgarians; you try in vain to destroy it by my death. The sign of the Cross will be everywhere. Churches pure and undefiled will be raised to the honour of the true God, and priests pure and undefiled will serve him; your idols and your altars will be destroyed as though they had never been. Three years later Malamir died, and in the year 865 Boyan's descendant was baptised a Christian and declared the Orthodox faith the faith of the state.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and names his house. He was the son of Krutogon and the grandson of Gruboš. He confessed the faith of Christ while his brother Milomir was a heathen; and at his brother's order he was beheaded for the true faith, in 827.

The Russian calendar's life fills out in full what the Greek notice gives in outline, and puts his death about 830 where the Greek page puts it about 833. When his pagan brother Маломир, also called Владимир, came to the Bulgarian throne, Boyan asked him to release a Christian named Киннамон, held in prison for years because he had refused to take part in a pagan sacrifice under the prince before him.

The request was granted, and Boyan, seeing how far the man was wasted, asked him what he had suffered for. In a long conversation Киннамон set out the errors of paganism and the need of the faith of Christ for salvation, and ended: «Без Христа Иисуса нет света для ума, нет жизни для души» — without Christ Jesus there is no light for the mind and no life for the soul. Boyan received holy baptism with joy and gave himself to prayer, to fasting and to the thought of God.

When Маломир learned of his brother's conversion he demanded that he renounce Christ and return to the idols. Boyan answered that he loathed the pagan idols and honoured Christ the true God, and that nobody could part him from the love of Christ; and his brother condemned him to death. Before he was killed with the sword he foretold that the faith he was dying for would spread through the whole Bulgarian land, that churches would be raised to the true God, and that his brother would get nothing by his cruelty and would soon die himself.

Маломир died shortly afterwards without an heir, and the throne passed to the elder brother Пресиян, whose son baptised the Bulgarian people and so fulfilled the second half of the prophecy.
