# Dositheus (Kolchenkov)

Dositheus — Demetrius Kolchenkov in the world — was born in 1811 in the
village of Snagost in Kursk province, of devout parents, and first appears
in the Glinsk hermitage’s service lists in 1840, binding books and
attending on the confessor, the hieromonk Porphyry, and then on the abbot
Eustratius; in 1843 the consistory formally entered him among the novices,
with the testimonial “of good conduct, diligent at church, greatly inclined
to monasticism, zealous and unquestioning in obedience”. In 1849 he was
tonsured with the name Dositheus and made sacristan, an obedience he kept
for twenty-six years to his death with entire zeal, reverence and fear of
God, sparing neither strength nor the time he might have spent in his cell
to keep the church clean and in order; two years later, for his modesty,
meekness and obedience, he was put forward for the diaconate, and, not
wanting the rank, for humility’s sake pretended to be barely literate and
was refused. For that the enemy of salvation raised a violent war against
him: first regret at the refused rank, then all the charms of the world,
and at last impure fleshly lust — five years of struggle, weariness of
spirit and irritation, in which he grew pale, sickly and gloomy and was
ready to go back to the world, only a faint voice of conscience holding
him within the walls; the brethren and the abbot comforted and encouraged
him, urged him to conquer by patience and not to leave, and set a church
prayer for him, and the Lord heard it: the temptations gave way, peace
settled in his heart, and he resolved once for all to stay.

From then his life turned sharp about. Talkative before, he became wholly
silent, answering by signs and opening his mouth for two or three words
only in extreme need; having loved cleanliness, neatness and comfort, he
now showed complete contempt for them all, so as to fight the enemy better
and buy off eternal torment by a little suffering in time: he never
whitewashed his cell, never swept it, left whatever fell where it lay, had
a broken table, chair and bed and rags for bedding, and let dirt, soot,
cobwebs and countless insects fill it without a single remedy against
them; his one window was smeared with whitewash against curious eyes, and
he let no one in, not even the abbot — father, I am whitewashing the cell,
there is nothing to see, he told Innocent at the door, and the abbot
passed on. When he died and the brethren went in they found in a corner of
the holed floor a great heap of rubbish, remains of bread and food, rags
and worn-out linen, rotting for years in a stench that made the cell
impossible to enter — yet while he lived neither he nor his clothes gave
off any smell at all. He wore his clothes until they fell to pieces and
never changed his linen until it rotted off him — naked I was born and
naked I shall die, he said, and so it was, for he died rich in complete
non-possession. Not to break his solitude he did not go to the refectory
but took his several plain dishes in one wooden bowl, sour borshch, fish
soup and kasha mixed together — I am ill, he told the astonished brethren,
and I shall cook it all again in my cell — and in his last years ate once
a day, at about eight in the evening, and very little, again pleading an
illness that would not let him eat sooner and covering with the excuse his
labour of abstinence; the leavings he threw on the floor for the mice and
rats, and sometimes took ash from the kitchen, and like the penitent king
David ate his bread with ashes and mingled his drink with weeping,
crucifying the flesh with its passions and desires so as to enter the
kingdom through many sorrows. For his long service in churches dedicated
to the Queen of heaven he was granted to see the most holy Virgin in the
altar, and he heard the angels’ singing at the death of the elder
[Theodotus](/saints/theodotus-levchenko). The ancient soul-destroyer
stirred a storm of thoughts against him, set the boys of the nearby
villages to hound him with their jeers, and found willing helpers among
some of the brethren to vex him, and only his whole humility bore it all
without harm to his soul; those close to him noticed in him the gifts of
foresight and healing — walking to the river with a hieromonk from his own
village he said, two novices were wrestling and one threw the other into
the water and he nearly drowned, and at the river they found it so; and a
fellow-sacristan, overcome by charcoal fumes in his cell and unable to
rise, heard him say, go to church, your obedience is waiting for you, and
the fumes passed at once and the fever he had had before never came back.
He died on 5 November 1874 in his sixty-fourth year, leaving this vale of
tears for the eternal joy of heaven, and on the fortieth day appeared in a
dream to the brethren’s cook, who asked whether he had received mercy from
God — by the mercy of the Mother of God I have, he answered, and vanished.
The life is from the Glinsk Paterikon of the schema-archimandrite John
(Maslov); the Council of Bishops of 2017 entered him in the calendar of
the Russian Church, which keeps him on 9 September of the old style, 22
September, in the synaxis of the Glinsk fathers.
