# Gregory of Derkoi

saint.gr says that Saint Gregory was born in the village of Zoubanta in Achaia and studied at Dimitsana, at Nafplio and at the Great School of the Nation in Constantinople. His education and his character drew the regard of the Ecumenical Patriarch Sophronios II, who in February 1777 consecrated him metropolitan of Lacedaemonia in succession to another national martyr, the metropolitan Ananias Lampardis.

In 1790 he managed to escape arrest by the pasha of Tripoli and to get to Constantinople. Unable to return to Lacedaemonia, he was moved by the Ecumenical Patriarchate to the metropolis of Vidin, which he held from 1791 to 1801, and where he became a friend of Pazvantoğlu, who revolted against the sultan in 1797. Under the patriarchate of Kallinikos V he was translated to the metropolis of Derkoi. He was among the first members of the Philiki Etaireia, and offered many and great services to the Church and to the nation.

After Alexander Ypsilantis crossed into Moldo-Wallachia, on 9 March 1821, Gregory was imprisoned together with the metropolitans [Joseph of Thessalonica](/saints/joseph-of-thessalonica-1821), Ioannikios of Tirnovo and [Dorotheos of Adrianople](/saints/dorotheos-of-adrianople). After the Revolution broke out in the Peloponnese, on 23 to 25 March 1821, and the fighting that followed, the killing of the imprisoned hierarchs was ordered. This page says the other three were hanged at Arnavutköy, where Joseph's own page distributes them between three places; for Gregory the gallows had been set up in the courtyard of his own house at Therapeia in Constantinople. Gregory prayed, blessed the noose of his gallows and put it round his neck, made the sign of the Cross, and so ended his earthly life, this martyr of Christ and of the nation, the great Gregory of Derkoi, on 3 June 1821.
