# Aithalas the Deacon

Aithalas was a deacon in Persia in the fourth century, and suffered with the
bishop Acepsimas and the presbyter Joseph under King Shapur II. Acepsimas
headed the Church in the Persian city of Naesson, loved by his flock for his
ascetic life and unwearied pastoral labour; when Shapur ordered the
Christian clergy hunted down and killed, the bishop, then eighty years old,
was seized and brought to Arbela before Ardakh, the priest of the sun-god,
refused to sacrifice to the Persian gods, was cruelly beaten and thrown into
prison, and the next day the seventy-year-old presbyter Joseph and the
deacon Aithalas were thrown in after him, after the heaviest tortures. Three
years they were held in chains, starved and parched, until the king came to
the temple of the fire-god near Arbela and wished to see them; worn out and
covered with festering sores, they stood before him and again refused to
worship his gods, confessing their faith in Christ. The bishop was beheaded;
the presbyter and the deacon were sent into the city to be stoned — Joseph’s
stoning lasted hours, a guard was set so that the Christians should not take
the body, and on the fourth night a great storm passed over the city,
lightning killed the guard, the wind scattered the stones and the body was
gone; Aithalas was taken to the village of Patrias and stoned there, and the
Christians buried his body secretly, and a tree grew on his grave whose
fruit brought healing. That was in 380. The Russian calendar keeps the three
on 3 November and Aithalas alone on 1 September of the old style, 14
September, which is the day this entry stands under; the Greek synaxarion
keeps him with Ammoun on 2 September.
