# Aithalas the Deacon

Aithalas was a deacon in Persia in the fourth century, and suffered with the
bishop Acepsimas and the presbyter Joseph under King Shapur II. Acepsimas
headed the Church in the Persian city of Naesson, loved by his flock for his
ascetic life and unwearied pastoral labour; when Shapur ordered the
Christian clergy hunted down and killed, the bishop, then eighty years old,
was seized and brought to Arbela before Ardakh, the priest of the sun-god,
refused to sacrifice to the Persian gods, was cruelly beaten and thrown into
prison, and the next day the seventy-year-old presbyter Joseph and the
deacon Aithalas were thrown in after him, after the heaviest tortures.

Three years they were held in chains, starved and parched, until the king
came to the temple of the fire-god near Arbela and wished to see them; worn
out and covered with festering sores, they stood before him and again
refused to worship his gods, confessing their faith in Christ. The bishop
was beheaded; the presbyter and the deacon were sent into the city to be
stoned — Joseph’s stoning lasted hours, a guard was set so that the
Christians should not take the body, and on the fourth night a great storm
passed over the city, lightning killed the guard, the wind scattered the
stones and the body was gone; Aithalas was taken to the village of Patrias
and stoned there, and the Christians buried his body secretly, and a tree
grew on his grave whose fruit brought healing. That was in 380. The Russian
calendar keeps the three on 3 November and Aithalas alone on 1 September of
the old style, 14 September, which is the day this entry stands under; the
Greek synaxarion keeps him with Ammoun on 2 September.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 3 November with the bishop [Acepsimas](/saints/acepsimas-of-naeson) and the presbyter [Joseph](/saints/joseph-presbyter-of-persia), and doxologia.ro prints the three men's shared passion at length. Left bound before the chief of the magi after Joseph had been thrown into the prison, Aithalas was asked whether he would worship the great sun and taste of the blood of the sacrifices; he answered that it was the work of his questioner's unclean grey hairs to drink blood and to have for a god what God had made, and that he would not honour the creature above the Creator. His hands were bound under his knees, a great beam put through the binding, and six soldiers set to jump on him and trample him until his joints came apart; then he was beaten with thorned staves until his flesh was broken, and carried into the prison like a log.

Brought out with the others after three years before Ardasabor, the chief of all the magi of Persia, he heard the bishop beheaded and the presbyter beaten senseless, and was then offered honour and gifts from the emperor's hands if he would not go the way those two madmen had gone. He answered that it would be a great shame before heaven and earth if he did not travel the same road his fathers had travelled before him: if they, old in years and weak in body, had suffered so manfully, how should he, younger in years and stronger in body, fear these passing torments? He did not fear death for Christ, who had died for him. The judge had him beaten harder than the first two, and he called the man a foul dog and said that such torments frighten only the small-souled.

Ardasabor sent him and Joseph to Arbela with his friend Adesh, to be stoned by Christians rather than cut down with the sword, so that the teachers of the Christians should die at Christian hands; and they were carried there on animals like lifeless wood. In the filthy prison at Arbela the holy woman [Sinandulia](/saints/snandulia-the-persian) bribed the guards, took them to her house by night, washed and anointed their wounds and bound them up. Six months later their wounds were a little healed and they could stand, and only Aithalas's hands were like relics.

Under the new judge Zvrot he was hung up and beaten long without sparing, crying without ceasing, "I am a Christian!" A Manichaean hung up after him bore the first blows and then cursed the Manichaeans, renounced his faith and promised to worship the Persian gods; and Aithalas, hearing it, mocked him — he had barely tasted torment and had already denied his creed — and blessed Christ who strengthens his own in the cruellest torments and makes them unconquered in the right faith. For that he was beaten again with thorned rose switches until he seemed dead, and was laid outside like a corpse; a magus who was merciful by nature covered his naked body with a clean cloth and was himself beaten without mercy for it. Feeling that he still breathed, they dragged him by the feet into the prison.

Both were sentenced at the last to be stoned by Christians, and doxologia's account then follows Joseph to the pit, the ring of Christians forced to throw, Sinandulia's refusal, the crushing of his head and the disappearance of his body on the fourth day. There the page stops: it does not tell how Aithalas himself died.
