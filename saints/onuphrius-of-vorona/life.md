# Onuphrius of Vorona

Among the many fathers who sanctified the land of Romania by their high
spiritual life is Onuphrius, the greatest hermit of the Botoșani country, a
dweller of the Sihăstria Voronei skete, of whose life very little is known,
since he wished to be known to God and not to men. From notes on the
manuscripts he and the hieroschemamonk Nicholas, who lived with him, copied,
he was born about 1700 in Russia, probably into a noble family, since he
came to be governor of a district; longing for the hermit’s life and kindled
with divine zeal, he left family, rank and country about 1749 and came to
Moldavia, drawn by the fame of the great hermits hidden from the world in
the depths of the forests, and went round many monasteries gathering
soul-building counsel like a bee and learning from each father the work
pleasing to God, reading the Scriptures and useful books, which he later
copied with zeal and patience in a beautiful hand. So he lived fifteen
years, growing in spirit beside chosen fathers such as Basil of Poiana
Mărului and Paisius Velichkovsky, who found him at Dragomirna about 1763 on
his coming from Athos; a close spiritual friendship grew between them, each
guiding the other in the perfect prayer of the heart, Paisius being his
confessor, and after they parted their bond went on by letter. Hearing of
other Romanian and Russian hermits in the forests of Vorona he entered the
Sihăstria Voronei skete in 1764 and withdrew into the wilderness; for
twenty-five years he lived in a cave on the bank of the Vorona brook in
harsh ascesis, in humility, abstinence, silence and unceasing prayer, eating
once a day after sunset and passing the night in vigil, dozing only two or
three hours in a chair, as the hieromonk Nicholas, who also lived in the
forests of Vorona, testifies. He took the great schema from the hands of
his confessor, the elder Paisius, at Dragomirna in 1774, and went back to
his cave, and there, calling without rest on the name of the sweetest Jesus
until every passion of the flesh was quenched, he was counted worthy of the
sight of the divine light and the dew of the Holy Spirit, and was blessed
by God with the gift of discerning thoughts, so that monks and lay people
alike sought him out and marvelled at his gentleness and wisdom; he set many
on the good road, scattered the snares of the enemy, and worked wonders in
his lifetime.

He was told of his end beforehand. On the night of Pascha, when he wished to
receive the Body and Blood of the Saviour one last time, an angel appeared
to the priest Nicholas Gheorghiu of the nearby village of Tudora, told him
to take the holy gifts and go to God’s servant Onuphrius, and led him to
the cave, where he found the hermit very weak; he asked to be carried to
the skete, and a few days later, on 29 March 1789, fell asleep in the Lord,
and was buried by a few monks in the orchard at the root of an apple tree.
Not long after, wonders began at the grave that proved his holiness — many
saw candles burning and heard voices singing there, and three monks lost in
the forests at nightfall were led by a strong light to his cave — and in the
winter of 1846 the prince Michael Sturdza, out hunting, found a great fair
fruit at the root of the apple tree that stood over the grave, took it to
his daughter, who was ill with epilepsy, and she was healed; wondering, he
came back to Vorona, learned the hermit’s life, and had his bones taken up
and set in a reliquary in the sanctuary of the monastery church. Ten years
later, after Onuphrius himself appeared in a dream to the archimandrite
Joseph Vasiliu, abbot of Vorona, asking to be taken to the Sihăstria, the
reliquary was placed in a niche of the narthex of the Annunciation church
there, where it lies to this day, and where many come to venerate him and
find his help. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 9 September.
