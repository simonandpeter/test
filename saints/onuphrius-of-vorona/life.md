# Onuphrius of Vorona

Among the many fathers who sanctified the land of Romania by their high spiritual life is Onuphrius, the greatest hermit of the Botoșani country, a dweller of the Sihăstria Voronei skete, of whose life very little is known, since he wished to be known to God and not to men. From notes on the manuscripts he and the hieroschemamonk Nicholas, who lived with him, copied, he was born about 1700 in Russia, probably into a noble family, since he came to be governor of a district; longing for the hermit’s life and kindled with divine zeal he left family, rank and country about 1749 and came to Moldavia, drawn by the fame of great hermits hidden from the world in the depths of the forests, and went round many monasteries gathering soul-building counsel like a bee and learning from each father the work pleasing to God, reading the Scriptures and useful books, which he later copied with zeal and patience in a beautiful hand. So he lived fifteen years, growing in spirit beside chosen fathers such as Basil of Poiana Mărului and Paisius Velichkovsky, who found him at Dragomirna about 1763 on his coming from Athos; a close spiritual friendship grew between them, each guiding the other in the perfect prayer of the heart, Paisius being his confessor, and after they parted their bond continued by letter. Hearing of other Romanian and Russian hermits in the forests of Vorona he entered the Sihăstria Voronei skete in 1764 and withdrew into the wilderness for stillness; for twenty-five years he lived in a cave on the bank of the Vorona brook in harsh ascesis, in humility, abstinence, silence and unceasing prayer, eating once a day after sunset and passing the night in vigil, dozing only two or three hours — the Romanian life runs on past the point read here.
