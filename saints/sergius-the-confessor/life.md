# Sergius the Confessor

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 13 mai reads «Sfântul Cuvios Serghie Mărturisitorul», and the page kept under his name carries no Viață tab and no Tropar tab — only the epistle and gospel appointed for the day, Ephesians 6:10–17 and Luke 12:8–12, which are the readings of a confessor. The calendar gives no country, no century, and no account of what he confessed or before whom; this page says no more than the calendar says.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day and puts him in his family. saint.gr says he came of a distinguished and devout house, that he was the father of the holy [Photius](/saints/photius-of-constantinople) — honoured, it notes, on 6 February — and the brother of the patriarch [Tarasius](/saints/tarasius-of-constantinople), who held the throne from 784 to 806. The iconoclast emperor Theophilos, who reigned from 829 to 842, did not scruple to take him into the persecutions he made against monks and heads of households alike, and Sergius was denounced as a fervent supporter of the holy icons.

Brought before the emperor, he was worked on every way to change his mind, and in vain: he said steadily that he could not please his earthly king by betraying the heavenly one. The emperor confiscated the whole of his property and imprisoned him, and then exiled him with his wife Irene and his children. Poor now, all of them, they endured many hardships, and they did not repent of it but were glad to be tried and to suffer for the sake of Orthodoxy. Death found Sergius far from his wife and children, and he died in the conviction that this was the death he ought to have, and that his family were happy, having something to show in the way of sufferings for the love of Christ the Saviour.
