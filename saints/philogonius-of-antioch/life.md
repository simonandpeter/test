# Philogonius of Antioch

saint.gr's calendar for 20 December keeps the venerable Philogonius, whom its page makes bishop of the great and resplendent city of Antioch. He lived, it says, in the years of Constantine the Great. From his childhood he gave himself to the learning of the sacred letters and so dedicated himself to God; and it was his eloquence, together with his unquestioned piety and uprightness, that made the Christians of Antioch raise him to the episcopate of their city.

Before that he had been an advocate, and the page says he practised the profession by the rule of his own feeling for his fellow men: whenever he could, he defended those of them who had been wronged and who were powerless before their idolatrous oppressors. For what it says next the page cites John Chrysostom — that Philogonius, bishop of Antioch, was a married man and had a daughter when he was ordained, and that his family did not keep him from carrying out a great work of charity and from giving himself to his flock.

He gave up his spirit to the Lord in peace in the year 324, or, as the same sentence allows, in 323. The page carries two lines of verse above the life and prints no hymn for him.
