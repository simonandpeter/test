# Gelasius the Mime

saint.gr says Gelasius was a mime — an actor — by trade, and that when he was ordered to make a mockery of the baptism of the Christians, as the Porphyrius who came of the mimes had been, he was baptised in earnest and was perfected by the sword. That is the whole of the entry: a trade, an order, a baptism and a beheading.

Its couplet turns on his name, which is laughter: about to be enlightened, you laugh — and it is error you are laughing at; and once washed, Gelasius, your head is struck off. «Φώτισμα μέλλων ἐκγελᾶν, γελᾷς πλάνην, Πλυθεὶς δὲ Γελάσιε, ἐκτέμνῃ κάραν». The page gives no year, no emperor, no city, no relics and no hymn; and the Porphyrius it sends the reader to it puts on 4 November, which is not the day this corpus keeps its own mime Porphyrius on, so no link is made here.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 27 Φεβρουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/02/27/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3590/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
