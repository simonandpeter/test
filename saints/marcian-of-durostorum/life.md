# Marcian of Durostorum

Marcian and [Nicander](/saints/nicander-of-durostorum) were soldiers in the Roman army, in the legions of the Danube, and did their military service at Durostorum in the Roman province of Moesia Inferior. In the course of a war the Roman Empire fought against the Persians, in the year 298, the emperors of the time, Diocletian and Galerius, decided to put out of the Roman army all those who were Christians, holding that such men did not fight bravely enough to defend the borders or the integrity of the empire.

So it was in the city of Durostorum that these two soldiers confessed our Saviour Jesus Christ and suffered a martyr's death for him. When it was found that they were Christians, and when they acknowledged it themselves, they were thrown into prison and urged to deny Christ and to sacrifice to the gods. They refused those urgings to apostasy and were put to several torments; but they did not give up the Christian faith, standing firm in it and confessing Jesus Christ still. So, by imperial order, on 8 June 298 their heads were cut off.

That is the whole of what the calendar prints for the two of them, and it prints no troparion. It distinguishes neither man from the other, and gives neither their country nor their age. The calendar's line names Nicander first and Marcian second; the life it prints names Marcian first.
