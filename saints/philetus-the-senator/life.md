# Philetus the Senator

Philetus and the Christians who were with him lived in the time of the emperor Hadrian, and worshipped God every day. He was seized and brought before the emperor, who, unable to withstand the martyr's wisdom, handed him over to one of his close servants; and they tied him to a pillar and beat him with swords, after which the emperor ordered him thrown into prison. As he was being led there, his wife [Lydia](/saints/lydia-wife-of-philetus) and his first three sons joined him, of whom Cronides held the office of commentarisius.

That night, as they were singing in the prison, an angel appeared to them and strengthened them to suffer to the end. In the morning the martyrs were brought before the emperor and at once thrown into great vessels in which oil mixed with resin was boiling; but the vessels went cold immediately. Seeing the miracle, the fourth son, who held the rank of duke and had been until then in the emperor's retinue, got into one of the vessels of hot oil himself, saying: «Lord, God of the Christians, help me!»

The emperor left Rome for Illyricum, full of threat and anger, and as he went he gave orders that the vessels of oil and resin be heated for seven days and the saints put into them again. The order was carried out, and the saints were left unharmed. When he came back from his journey and learned all this the emperor was ashamed; and the saints, after some time which they spent in prayer, departed this life.

doxologia.ro's page for his wife names their four sons — Macedon, Theoprepius, Cronides the commentarisius and Amphilochius the duke — and calls him «Filet Sincliticul», Philetus of the senate. The calendar gives no year for them, and no troparion, and says only that they suffered under Hadrian.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and names his whole household and the two officers who tried it: «Άγιοι Φιλητός ο Συγκλητικός, Λυδία σύζυγος αυτού, Θεοπρέπιος και Μακεδόνας τα τέκνα αυτών, Αμφιλόχιος ο Δούκας και Κρονίδης ο κομενταρήσιος οι Μάρτυρες». saint.gr sets the persecution in the year 125, under the emperor Hadrian. Philetus, his wife [Lydia](/saints/lydia-wife-of-philetus) and their two sons [Theoprepius](/saints/theoprepius-son-of-philetus) and [Macedon](/saints/macedon-son-of-philetus) were seized and required to deny Christ, and parents and children alike held to their confession; and because their accusers could not stand against the wisdom of Philetus, the family was handed over to [Amphilochius](/saints/amphilochius-the-duke), who was duke in Slavonia. He hung Philetus and Lydia on a tree and flayed them, at which [Cronides](/saints/cronides-the-commentarisius) the commentarisius believed in Christ and was imprisoned with them. In the night, as the saints sang and prayed, angels came and gave them courage; the next day the duke had them thrown into a bronze cauldron of oil and resin, which went cold at the sign of the Cross, so that he too believed and cast himself in. The page ends the account by saying that when the emperor learned the saints had been kept safe and sound he set them free, and that they ended their lives praying — and its couplet for the couple agrees: «Ὥσπερ Φιλητοῦ καὶ Λυδίας σάρξ μία, Οὕτως ἓν αὐτῶν καὶ μετ' εἰρήνης τέλος», as Philetus and Lydia were one flesh, so one also was their end, and that in peace.

The Russian calendar keeps the company four days earlier in the menologion than the Greek, on the twenty-third of March, old style, and tells the whole of it in one reading. Philetus was a man of rank at the court of the emperor Hadrian, and for confessing Christ openly he came before the court with his wife and their two sons. By Hadrian’s own order the family was sent into Illyria, to the commander there, to be put to the torture; and he had them hung on a tree and their bodies scraped with knives.

They were then shut up in prison together with the governor of the prisons, who had come to believe. In the night an angel stood before them and strengthened them for what was coming. The next day the martyrs were lowered into a cauldron of boiling oil, and the oil went cold in an instant and they were unhurt. The commander was so struck by it that he believed in Christ and stepped into the boiling oil himself, praying, Lord Jesus Christ, help me — and lived.

The same torture was tried again when Hadrian himself came into Illyria. The holy martyrs were thrown into the boiling oil again and again and by the power of God stayed alive, and the emperor went back to Rome shamed, while they gave thanks and glorified God and gave up their souls to him in prayer. The calendar sets the whole of it inside Hadrian’s reign and gives no closer year than that.

This calendar makes him a «знатный сановник» at the court itself and its name page calls him a senator, which is the word the corpus had already chosen for him off the Greek.
