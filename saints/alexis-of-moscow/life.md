# Alexis of Moscow

Alexis, the wonderworker of Russia, was of a boyar family. His parents were called Theodore and Maria and were from the parts of Sever, from the city of Chernigov, and he was born in the days of the great prince John Danilovich, who stood godfather to him at holy baptism and gave him the name Eleutherius. When he came to a fitting age his parents put him to his lessons; and at twelve, walking in the wilderness where he was setting snares to catch birds, he fell asleep and heard a voice saying to him: «Alexis, why dost thou labour in vain? Behold, I will make thee a hunter of men.» He woke and saw nobody, and from that hour he pondered what such a vision should mean.

Loving God from his youth, he left his parents and marriage, and went to the monastery of the Holy Theophany in the city of Moscow, where he was tonsured by the abbot Stephen, brother of [Sergius](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) the wonderworker; at his tonsure they gave him the name Alexis, the name the voice had called him in the dream. He was twenty then, and he remained in the monastic order until he was forty, serving God with fasting, with all-night vigil and with unceasing prayer, so that many marvelled at his labour.

He was famous and praised by all for his amended life, and the great prince of Moscow Simeon Ivanovich and the metropolitan Theognostus loved him greatly, so that for his many good works they made him bishop in the great city of Vladimir. When Theognostus died, the great prince John Ivanovich, who had taken the rule after his brother Simeon, chose Alexis by the judgement of a council and sent him to Constantinople to be consecrated by the most holy patriarch Philotheus, who set him metropolitan of Kyiv and of all Russia. Returning and taking up that great governance of the Church, he added labour upon labour, so that he was a light and a pattern to the flock in word, in life, in love, in spirit, in faith and in purity.

At that time the pagan emperor Verdevir reigned in Scythia, who had killed his twelve brothers, a cruel and most unmerciful man who wanted war against the Christians and whom everyone feared. At the request of the great prince John, Alexis went to Verdevir's camp, calmed his anger with gentle words, obtained peace for the Christians, and came back to Moscow, to his see — for the metropolitan's throne had been moved there by Saint [Peter](/saints/peter-of-moscow), metropolitan of Kyiv, because of the frequent wars and barbarian invasions. He then raised a church in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, of the Image not made with hands, and founded beside it a monastery with a common life, entrusting the abbacy to Andronicus, a disciple of Saint Sergius; and he raised many other churches in other cities and places.

The honour paid him went out not only among the faithful but among the unbelieving Hagarenes who had not heard of Christ. Their pagan emperor Amurat had an empress who had been blind three years; hearing of Alexis and of what God does through his prayer, he sent to the great prince Demetrius Ivanovich and asked him to send the man of God to pray for her and give her sight, saying that if she were healed by his prayer there would be peace between them, and that if he were not sent he would plunder the land with sword and fire.

Alexis, receiving that letter, thought the thing hard and beyond his own strength; but at prince Demetrius's entreaty he consented to go into the land of the Hagarenes, and first made prayer with all his clergy in the great cathedral church of the Most Pure Theotokos. As he finished his prayers a candle at the tomb of Saint Peter the wonderworker lit by itself, and all of them saw it; and from that Alexis took assurance of God's help preparing his way. He took a little piece of the wax of that self-kindled candle, made a small candle of it, and set out with the consecrated clergy, putting his hope in God without doubting.

Before he reached the Hagarenes' city, the empress saw in a vision the bishop of God, Alexis, coming with all his priests in hierarchical vestments; waking, she ordered costly vestments made ready at once for the bishop and the priests, as she had seen them in the dream. The emperor of the Hagarenes met him with great honour and brought him into his palace; and the bishop of God, beginning the singing, had the candle made from that wax lighted, and after much prayer sprinkled the blind empress with holy water, and from that hour she saw well. The emperor with his nobles and all who were there marvelled greatly at the wonder and raised praises to God, and he honoured Alexis and those with him and let them go home in peace with many gifts.

Coming back from there, Alexis raised a church of stone within the city of Moscow in the name of the holy Archangel Michael, in memory of his honoured and glorious miracle that was once worked at Chonae. Then he founded a monastery which is still called «the Miracle», and ordered that he should be laid in that church after his death. Having done many other wonderful things to the glory of God, and shepherded the rational flock of Christ, he drew near to his blessed end in deep old age.

Knowing his departure to God, he served the divine service and communicated of the Holy Mysteries; then, giving peace to the prince and the people in a last kiss to all, he went to the Lord in the year 6886 from the creation — 1378 after Christ — on the twelfth day of February. He had spent twenty-four years in the episcopate, and his age was eighty-five. They buried him with honour in the church of the holy Archangel Michael which he had built, within the precinct of the Annunciation of the Most Pure Virgin Theotokos. After many years his holy and much-healing relics were found whole, and the vestments uncorrupted, as though put on yesterday; and he was afterwards brought into the church built in his name, from which healings still flow as from a spring. The page prints no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, under the line «Όσιος Αλέξιος ο Θαυματουργός Αρχιεπίσκοπος Μόσχας», and saint.gr's life adds two things worth having. It says that he learned Greek in order to read the New Testament in the original, and was thereby able to set the Slavonic text against the Greek and correct the inaccuracies of the various translators and copyists — the new Slavonic edition of the Gospel made straight from the Greek being, in its words, an invaluable text of Russian national literature. And it tells what came of healing Taidula, the wife of the Tatar khan, who had written to the prince Symeon that «heaven refuses nothing to your priest's petitions»: asked how he wished to be thanked, Alexis asked instead that the Russians be relieved of the heaviest of the taxes they paid the khan. The khan also gave him a piece of ground in the Kremlin, where the monastery of the Miracle was later built, and a precious ring. His incorrupt relic, the page adds, is in the patriarchal church of the Theophany at Moscow.

**The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 5 October with the synaxis of the hierarchs of Moscow**, seventeen names in one line under one polyeleos service, marked «(Моск. Свт.)» and not expanded: Peter, Theognostus, Alexis, Cyprian, Photius, Jonah, Gerontius, Joasaph, Macarius, Philip, Job, Hermogenes, Tikhon, Peter, Philaret, Innocent and Macarius — two and a half centuries of metropolitans, then the patriarchs, then the metropolitans of Moscow and Kolomna, ending with a man shot in 1937. He is the third of them. His own Russian row keeps 12 February, and the synaxis is recorded in it rather than given a row of its own, because one row per church is the schema.
