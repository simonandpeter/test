# Anthony the New (1 December)

Of the venerable Anthony the New the Evergetinos reports, saint.gr says, that he became a monk and lived an ascetic life; and that in order to labour harder still he left the life of stillness and went to a coenobitic monastery at Kios in Bithynia, where he put himself under the abbot and worked at the hardest work of the house. Having shown much obedience and much patience, and become a pattern of ascetic humility, he died in peace.

Nikodemos the Hagiorite, whom the page quotes at length, fills that out. Anthony had held a lordly office before he became a monk, and after many years of stillness and labours beyond nature he was reading John of the Ladder's discourse on obedience and found at the end of it the words: whoever, sitting in stillness, has come to know his own weakness, and goes and sells himself into obedience, that man, being blind, has looked up to Christ without labour. On that he left his stillness and gave himself to the coenobium at Kios.

The abbot set him first to serve in the church, that being the heaviest service; when he had kept at it a while he asked for a heavier, and was handed to the chief worker of the vineyards to prune them, and because he knew nothing of such work he cut the fingers of his hands many times over. He stayed at it through the digging and the vintage and was then given the refectory. His clothes were torn to pieces and his shoes worn through, so that he was stiff with cold and the soles of his feet split on the cold marble; the abbot gave him neither clothes nor shoes, trying his patience.

Worn out at last, he said to God in the hidden lips of his heart: look, Lord, on my humiliation and my labour, and forgive all my sins. And one night he saw in his sleep a glorious man holding a balance, with all the sins of his youth in the left scale, and in the right the axe with which he had grubbed the wild grass out of the monastery's fields; and the axe weighed that side down and scattered the kinds of his sins. Then the man said: behold, the Lord has accepted your labours and forgiven your sins.

The abbot, seeing his patience over so many years and that he had settled in his mind to bear every toilsome and grievous work of the house with a great soul, called him aside and said: God repay your wage, father, for the souls you have profited by your coming to us and by your life according to God; for the brethren under me have gained from nothing else so much as from your God-sent presence and your perfect obedience. And he gave him clothes and shoes and whatever else was needed, and from then on, whatever he saw was wanted, he would go secretly and lay it at the place of his bed, where Anthony found it on returning and used it for the body's need.
