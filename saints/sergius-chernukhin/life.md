# Sergius (Chernukhin)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him for 27 октября ст. ст. in four words — «Прмч. Сергия (1942).» — and the name link behind the line adds the surname and the office the line leaves out: «Сергий (Чернухин), игумен, прмч.», an abbot of that name, one of the new martyrs of the Russian church in the Soviet years. It carries no life for him. A second Russian calendar, azbyka.ru, carries a long one, compiled by Abbot Damaskin (Orlovsky) out of the state and patriarchal archives it lists, and the rest of this is from there.

He was born Simeon, on 3 February 1879, in the village of Semyonovka in the Buzuluk district of the Samara province, the son of a peasant named Savely Chernukhin. In 1900 he went as a novice to the monastery of St Daniel at Moscow, was enrolled there formally on 10 January 1907, was tonsured on 15 May 1910 with the name Sergius and set to the choir. He was ordained hierodeacon in 1911 and hieromonk in 1915, given the pectoral cross in 1920, and raised to abbot in 1923, serving in the Trinity cathedral and the church of the Protection of the Mother of God in his monastery, and after those were closed in the church of the Resurrection beside it.

The mass arrests of clergy and monastics came to Moscow in December 1930. The OGPU searched his flat on the 28th, and he was questioned on 13 January 1931. What the investigator wanted was his attitude to the Soviet power. He did not deny that the Church was then under great persecution, but said that all of it was sent for our sins, and that as for the authorities, every authority is from God and must be obeyed. That religious account of events did not satisfy them, and on 8 February 1931 he was sentenced to three years' exile in the Northern territory — Arkhangelsk, then the little town of Pinega, then the village of Kuloy, where he stayed out the term.

He came back to Moscow on 28 February 1934 and was not allowed to live there, having been in exile; so he settled at Kashira, and then moved to the village of Nikolskoye-Golovinskoye in the Shakhovskoy district when he was appointed to the church of St Nicholas there. He served in it until the mass arrests of 1937. The witnesses against him, on 24 and 25 November that year, were the outgoing and incoming chairmen of the village soviet, who said he had refused to subscribe to the state loan and had told them so in words the record keeps: get away from me, you Herods, I will not pay the Soviet power one kopeck.

He was arrested on 1 December 1937, shut in the prison at Volokolamsk and questioned the next day. The investigator said it was known to the inquiry that he had carried on counter-revolutionary agitation among the population, and twice he answered that he had carried on none, nowhere and never. The questioning was finished that same day, and on 3 December an NKVD troika gave him ten years in a labour camp. He reached the Amur region on 5 February 1938, was moved to the Bushuy camp on 30 December 1940 and to the Svobodny camp on 1 March 1941, and died there on 9 November 1942 — the civil date of this menologion day — and was buried in an unmarked grave.

The Сретенский line's «Прмч.» is venerable-martyr and its link's «игумен» is the office, and both are kept. Neither calendar prints a hymn under his name: the day page's eleven troparia and kontakia belong to the three older saints it heads and to a prince whose relics it remembers.
