# Barlaam of Antioch

Doxologia's life gives him as a man of Antioch in Syria, old in years. He was taken for confessing the name of Jesus Christ and brought to judgement before the pagan governor, and being pressed to sacrifice to the idols and refusing, he suffered many wounds and torments for Christ his Lord. First he was beaten without mercy with ox sinews, then torn with iron claws.

After that the pagans took him into the idols' temple, and there, at the tormentor's command, his hand was stretched out over the altar where a fire was burning, and burning coals with incense were laid in it — in the hope that the martyr, unable to bear holding live coals in his hand, would throw them with the incense before the idols, and so seem to have sacrificed.

He held the burning fire in his hand and showed himself stronger than brass and iron; he held it so long that his fingers burned through and fell to the ground with the coals, and he never moved his hand nor threw them with the incense onto the altar before the idols. So the sufferer and strong soldier of Christ showed himself manful and unconquered, and in a good confession gave up his soul into the hands of the Lord. Both the divine Chrysostom and Basil the Great, the page adds, honoured him with words of praise. It gives no year.
