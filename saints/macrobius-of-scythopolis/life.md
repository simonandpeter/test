# Macrobius of Scythopolis

saint.gr names six men together for this day and tells one story for all of them. [Aphrodisius](/saints/aphrodisius-of-scythopolis), [Misdas](/saints/misdas), Macrobius, [Valerian](/saints/valerian-4-may), [Leontius](/saints/leontius-of-scythopolis) and [Antoninus](/saints/antoninus-of-scythopolis) were martyrs, it says, and with them others suffered at Scythopolis in Syria. Nothing on the page gives their country, their trades, their years or the emperor under whom they died.

These saints presented themselves of their own accord before the ruler of their country and reproached him most sharply for his inhuman doings against the Christians. Then they smashed the altars of the idols to pieces, and so provoked the fury of the idolaters, who set on them with stones and clubs and killed them all. The Christians took up their bodies, buried them with honours, and raised a church over them; and their synaxis used to be kept at the apostoleion of James the Brother of God, which stands inside the venerable house of the Mother of God at the Chalkoprateia.

The page glosses his name as it glosses the others, «(μακρύς + βίος) = αυτός που ζει πολλά χρόνια», the one who lives many years, which the story does not let him do. His single line of his own is the couplet he shares with Valerian: fire, raised above the heads of two martyrs, fixed a contender's roof over them. The verses say fire and the story says stones and clubs, and saint.gr prints both without choosing.
