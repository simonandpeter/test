# Raphael of Šišatovac

George Momčilović was born on 23 April 1875 at Deronje in Bačka, the third
and last child of the Serbian peasants Velimir and Persida Momčilović. He
finished the elementary school of his native village, and at twelve his
father took him to the monastery of Kovilj, where he spent some years in the
monastery school; then he moved to the monastery of Bođani, where as a
novice he studied icon painting. In 1897 the igumen of Kovilj, Miron
(Đorđević), moved with the twenty-two-year-old George to the brotherhood of
Manasija, where he was soon tonsured with the name Raphael after the
venerable Raphael of Banat, a monk of Hilandar, ordained deacon at once and
hieromonk the next year. The new metropolitan of Serbia and the Holy Synod
sent him to Russia, where he studied icon painting and served at the Serbian
metochion in Moscow; three years later he came home, the cold of Russia
having weakened a health already shaken, went for a year to Italy to perfect
his painting, and from Rome returned to Belgrade — for a time at Rakovica,
two years at Bukovo, and probably in 1908 back at Kovilj. In 1935 he was
received into the brotherhood of Šišatovac on Fruška Gora; little is known
of that period of his life, and more has been written about his icons than
about him, his mastery drawing the notice of art historians. About three
years after he came he was chosen its superior, and in those three years he
painted a great number of icons, several iconostases and a remarkable
gallery of portraits of the Church’s leading men, among them Patriarch
Gabriel of Serbia, who was himself to suffer in the war, with the future
church of St Sava on Vračar behind him — a building the igumen cared for
especially, giving to it all the income from the sale of his works, which he
priced with a monk’s modesty. So, the Serbian account says, the Lord granted
that he should be glorified with all the new martyrs of Serbia under the
vaults of that very church.

A list drawn up by the Holy Synod in March 1958 from what its archive then
held, of the priests and bishops of the Serbian Church killed by the Ustaše
in the so-called Independent State of Croatia from April 1941 to the end of
1944, tells his death under number 173 of the Syrmian martyrology. On 25
August 1941 the police officer Nikola Buntak burst into the monastery with
several Ustaše and arrested the igumen and three monks, Demetrius, Herman
and Theophan, and took them to Slavonska Požega; in the train they tore out
his beard and beat him, making him sing the songs of the Serbian Chetniks.
At the station they waited until nine at night for the train from Slavonski
Brod bringing four hundred more arrested Serbs from the districts of
Bosanski Brod and Derventa; the Ustaše bound the Serbs in fours, put Father
Raphael and his three monks at the head of the column and led them through
the town, beating them so that the old men, the igumen among them, stumbled
and fell, which only enraged the guards further — come, Chetniks, sing your
song, Holy God! Then they were brought into the yard of the hospital, where
a camp had been set up, lined up and beaten one by one, and after the
beating made, half alive, to run to the barracks; the igumen, exhausted and
torn, lost consciousness and fell, and those running behind him had to run
over his body. Beaten past recognition, he was put in the camp hospital, and
after four days moved to Serbia, to a Belgrade hospital, where it was found
that five ribs were broken and there was much internal bleeding; on 3
September 1941 he died of his injuries. He was buried in a Belgrade cemetery
or in one of the mass graves whose place was not then established; only the
Lord knows where his body lies. In his life he painted icons and pictures
and held his gift humbly, and most of all he laboured to paint the icon of
God, the true monastic likeness, in himself; that chief work of his life he
finished by a martyr’s death for Christ, for the precious Cross and the
Orthodox faith. The Serbian calendar keeps him on 21 August of the old
style, 3 September, and the Russian calendar the same day.
