# Leontius (24 April)

Leontius is one of eight martyrs saint.gr prints together on 24 April, and the whole of what it says of them it says of all eight at once. They were martyred in the year 303, it says, when they came to the Christian faith through the wonders which the great martyr [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) performed — George whose own feast the calendar had kept the day before.

Having confessed Christ they were arrested and held in prison. Afterwards, by order of Diocletian, whose reign the page dates 284 to 305, they were hung up and their flesh was scraped, and at the last they were beheaded, all of them taking so the unfading crown of martyrdom.

That is the whole account. The page heads Leontius «(; - 303)» like the rest and gives him no country, no city, no rank and no relic; its couplet is a piece of arithmetic on their number and nothing about them, and it prints no hymn. The seven others, as the calendar's line names them, are [Danabos](/saints/danabos), [Demetrius](/saints/demetrius-24-april), [Eusebius](/saints/eusebius-24-april), [Longinus](/saints/longinus-24-april), [Nestabus](/saints/nestabus-24-april), [Neon](/saints/neon-24-april) and [Christopher](/saints/christopher-24-april).

The Serbian church keeps the four on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović names all four in its heading — „Св. муч. Евсевије, Неон, Леонтије и Лонгин.“ — and tells them in two sentences. These four, it says, were the soldier comrades of [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer); seeing his brave endurance and his wonders, these admirable soldiers became Christians, and for that they were beheaded.

That is the whole of the entry: [Eusebius](/saints/eusebius-24-april), [Neon](/saints/neon-24-april), Leontius and [Longinus](/saints/longinus-24-april), their trade, their reason and their death, with **no year, no city and no order of execution**. Third of the four as the heading prints them is Leontius. The 303 this folder already held is saint.gr's, which heads each of the eight it keeps on this day «(; - 303)»; the Prologue dates nobody here, so **no date was added and none changed**.
