# Capito of Cherson

Capito was sent to Cherson in place of [Aetherius](/saints/aetherius-of-cherson), who had died on his way back from the emperor, the Christians of the city having asked Constantine the Great for another bishop. The people there demanded a sign of him, as they had of the others: that he should go into a burning furnace, as the three children once went into the furnace of Babylon, so that the unbelieving people might be convinced.

Hoping in God, Capito agreed to what they asked, and ordered a great furnace to be built for the purpose. When it had been heated fiercely, the bishop, with all the people watching, prayed long; the deacon cried out with a loud voice, Let us attend; and the bishop went into the furnace and stood in that flame for an hour, praying with his hands stretched out to heaven, and took no hurt from it. Then, carrying burning coals in his phelonion, he came out to the people unharmed; the fire had not touched his vestments, and the phelonion full of live coals had not caught alight.

Great wonder and fear came upon them all, and with one voice they cried that the God of the Christians is the one God, great and strong, who kept his servant unburnt in the furnace. The whole city of Cherson and that country received the Christian faith, being convinced by the wonder; and the report of it was carried to Constantine the Great, and to the first Council of the whole world at Nicaea, the three hundred and eighteen holy fathers, who all glorified God and marvelled at the bishop's great faith and boldness.

Some years later, as Capito was sailing from Cherson to Constantinople, a great storm rose and the ship was driven by the waves to the mouth of the river Dnieper. There were godless and unbelieving men in that place, and having taken out everyone who was in the ship and plundered everything, they drowned the bishop of God, Capito, and him alone, in the water. So he ended as a martyr, on the twenty-first day of December; but his memory is reckoned with the bishops of Cherson before him, who suffered on the seventh day of March.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives him the company's one unbloody wonder. saint.gr says the Christians of Cherson asked Constantine the Great to send them another hierarch, and Capito was sent; and that the idolaters asked him for a sign, so that they might see which was the true faith. He put on his hierarchical vestments, made the sign of the Cross and walked into a lit furnace; he stayed in it a good while and came out unharmed and unburnt, and the idolaters who saw it were baptised. The notice ends there: this page tells neither how nor when he died, and its distich says only that Capito lifted up his hands in prayer and, lifting up his feet, passed over to God.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day among the seven bishops of Cherson, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the seven of them one entry. All of them were bishops in Cherson at different times, it says, and all of them suffered at the hands of unbelievers — whether of Jews, or of Greeks, or of Scythians — except Aetherius alone, who died in peace. All of them went out into those wild parts as missionaries, sent by the patriarch of Jerusalem to carry the light of the Gospel; all were tormented, and all suffered for the sake of their Lord.

He is the last of them and the one the entry spends most of itself on. When Capito was sent as bishop, the wild Scythians asked him for a sign before they would believe, and proposed it themselves: that he go into a fiery furnace, and if he did not burn they would all believe in Christ. With warm prayer and hope in God he put the hierarch's omophorion upon himself, made the sign of the cross, and went into the glowing furnace, holding his heart and his thoughts raised up to God. He stood in the flame about an hour and came out whole, without any hurt either to his body or to his clothing.

Then they all cried out that God is one, the Christian God, great and mighty, who had kept His servant in the fiery furnace; and the whole city and the whole country round about were baptised. Of this wonder, the Prologue says, much was spoken at the Council of Nicaea, and all glorified God and praised the firm faith of Saint Capito. And it happened to Capito that pagan Scythians caught him on the road at the river Dnieper and drowned him in the river.

The Russian church keeps all seven of them on this day, on one line — «Священномучеников, в Херсонесе епископствовавших: Василия , Ефрема , Капитона , Евгения , Еферия , Елпидия и Агафодора (IV).» — and its life is the fullest account of the company any of the four calendars read has given. They carried the gospel, it says, into the lands of the northern Black Sea from the Danube to the Dnieper, the Crimea among them, and sealed their apostolate with a martyr's death in the fourth century at Херсонес Таврический. The page sets them in a history: the faith had reached the Crimea, anciently Таврида and subject to the Roman emperors, long before the baptism of Владимир; its beginning there was the preaching of the apostle Андрей Первозванный; and its spread was helped against their will by its own enemies, since the emperors used the place for state criminals, which in the first three centuries meant confessors of Christ — «в правление Траяна (98–117) был сослан на заточение в Инкерманские каменоломни святой Климент, епископ Римский», who went on preaching in the Inkerman quarries and was martyred there. The pagans of the Crimea resisted hard and many preachers died in the struggle.

The episcopal see at Херсонес was founded at the beginning of the fourth century, in a turning time when the city, a base for Roman soldiers, was passing into dependence on Byzantium. In the reign of Diocletian, which the page gives as 284 to 305, and in the year 300 — «то есть еще до начала гонения, которое император начал с 303 года», before the persecution the emperor began in 303 — the patriarch Ермон of Jerusalem, whose years the same sentence gives as 303 to 313, sent many bishops out into various countries for the preaching of the gospel. **The two numbers do not agree and nothing is done about it here**: a patriarch whose reign the page dates from 303 cannot have sent anybody in 300, and both figures are the page's own.

**His is the one death on this day whose own date the calendar prints and then sets aside, and it is the one date of the seven that the folders' existing bounds cannot hold.** In the place of Еферий the emperor Константин sent a bishop Капитон to Херсонес. The Christians met him with joy, but the pagans demanded a sign of the new bishop before they would believe in the God he preached. Putting all his hope in the Lord, «святой Капитон в святительском облачении вошел в разведенный костер, долго молился в огне и вышел из него невредимым, набрав в свою фелонь горячих угольев» — in his episcopal vestments he walked into a kindled fire, prayed a long while in it, and came out unharmed with a lapful of hot coals gathered into his phelonion; at which a multitude of the unbelieving were convinced of the power of the God of the Christians. The miracle and the bishop's great faith were announced to Константин and to the holy fathers at the First Ecumenical Council at Nicaea, which the page dates 325. Some years after that he set out for Constantinople on business, and his ship was driven by a storm into the mouth of the Dnieper; the local pagans seized it and drowned everyone aboard, himself among them. **«Это произошло 21 декабря»** — it happened on 21 December, and «память святого епископа Церковь установила отмечать с прочими херсонесскими священномучениками, 7 марта», the Church appointed his memory to be kept with the rest of the Cherson hieromartyrs on 7 March. So the feast on this folder is the company's day and not his own, as the calendar itself explains, and no row is written for 21 December; the mouth of the Dnieper is a stretch of water and no `locations` is written for it.

The preaching of the hieromartyrs, the page ends, established the faith at Херсонес: from the beginning of the fifth century the city was a spiritual centre from which Christianity spread north towards Rus, from the ninth the Russians were settling in it, modern excavation has found more than fifty churches there of the fifth to fourteenth centuries, and in 987 Владимир was baptised in it. «Этот древний город явился колыбелью христианства для Руси» — this ancient city was the cradle of Christianity for Rus.
