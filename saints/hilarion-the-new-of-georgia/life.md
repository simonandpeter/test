# Hilarion the New of Georgia

The blessed elder Hilarion the New, called in the world Iese Kanchaveli, was born in the year 1776 in the village of Losiantkhevi in Shorapani, in the jurisdiction of the city of Kutaisi. His parents, Khakhuli and Mariam, children of an old aristocratic family, were very devout and honoured the Great Martyr George in a particular way; and for their faith God gave them a boy whom at his baptism they named Iese.

When the child was six, the hierodeacon Stephen — his mother's brother and a hermit of the monastery of Tabakini in Georgia — took him from his father's house and brought him to his own hermitage in the mountains. Hearing afterwards that a seminary had opened at Tbilisi, Iese set out for the city, calling on the way on Athanasius, bishop of Nikozi, for his blessing; and the bishop, seeing the young man's gifts, advised him to go home: «My son, thou wilt learn far more in the desert than thou couldst ever learn in a classroom. Go home, and the Lord, when he has taught thee prayer, will lead thee on a way that will serve thy people and the Church.»

Iese returned to his family, and his father took him to Kutaisi to be brought up at the court of the Imeretian king. King Solomon II soon recognised that the young man was distinguished above all the others by his piety, and made him his spiritual counsellor and personal instructor; at the king's proposal Iese married the princess Mariam, and soon after his marriage the humble nobleman was ordained priest and appointed confessor of the court church. Barely two years later the princess Mariam died and left brother Iese a widower. The page then tells at length of the politics of the Imeretian kingdom and of his years afterwards, which are not set down here because they were not read.

Its close tells this. In Russia the elder published twelve volumes in the Georgian language, without giving the name of their author. Saint Hilarion fell asleep at the monastery of Saint Panteleimon, in a cell of the Great Martyr George, on 14 February 1864. Though he was gravely ill, he went on thanking the Lord sincerely to his last day: «Glory to God!» he said. «I wished for martyrdom, and God did not grant it to me. Instead he sent me a sickness which will be equal to the merit of martyrdom, if I am able to bear it.»

Before his death he asked his disciple, Father Sava, to bury his body in secret; but the circumstances were such that his place of burial was revealed in time. In 1867, during the vigil for the feast of the Ascension, a group of monks opened Saint Hilarion's burial vault and immediately smelt a sweet fragrance coming from his body; and at that moment one of the monks saw a shining crown of light blazing like the sun above Father Hilarion's cell. The Holy Synod of the Georgian Orthodox Church canonised the hieroschemamonk Hilarion (Kanchaveli) on 17 October 2002, and, to distinguish him from Saint Hilarion the Georgian, who is kept on 19 November, named him «Ilarion Kartveli, Akhali» — Hilarion the New, the Georgian. The page carries no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and calls him Hilarion the Iberite, for Iviron. saint.gr says the Holy Synod of the Patriarchate of Georgia numbered him among the saints on 17 October 2002, and that his life ran through the upheavals of the nineteenth century — the breaking up of the kingdom of Georgia, its annexation to Russia, and the Greek revolution of 1821. He was born in the kingdom of Imereti in 1776 and was called Iesse in the world; as a child he was shaped by his mother's brother, the hierodeacon Stephen, a great ascetic and a recluse, and stayed with him some twelve years. Fulfilling his father's wish he served king Solomon II of Imereti, living an ascetic life in the palace, married and was ordained priest of the court; and when his wife Maria died two years later he gave himself to God. He followed Solomon into exile, supported the queen in Moscow after the king's death, refused a bishopric and slipped away, and came by way of the desert, Odessa and Constantinople to the Holy Mountain in 1819, changing his costly clothes for a beggar's rags. He went first to Iviron and then to Dionysiou, where in 1821 he took the great habit and the name Hilarion, and where he was made confessor of the monastery when it came out that he had been a priest and the court's spiritual father. Wanting martyrdom, he agreed to appear before the Turkish governor of Thessalonica in the abbot's place, and was saved with the other monasteries' representatives in a way the page calls wonderful. He then shut himself up for three years in the tower of the New Skete, was tried hard there by the demons and broke off the enclosure, worked afterwards on the Georgian library at Iviron and brought out an anthology called «Φωτισμένος», and fell asleep in the Lord on 14 February 1864.
