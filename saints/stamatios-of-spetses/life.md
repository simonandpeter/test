# Stamatios of Spetses

Stamatios came from Spetses, and was the elder of two brothers; their father was called Theodore Ginis and their mother Anezo. In 1822 he set out for Constantinople with his brother [John](/saints/john-of-spetses) and five other sailors who lived by trade, [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-of-spetses) their fellow-contender among them. A heavy sea forced them to run aground opposite Chios, on a beach of the Asia Minor shore called Çeşme.

Ashore, and afraid of the Turks, they trusted their lives to a Christian and paid him to find materials for mending their damaged boat. He betrayed them to the agha and brought soldiers down on them. Two of the seven were killed and two got away by sea; the Ottomans took the two brothers and the old captain Nicholas. The pasha questioned them, had Nicholas beheaded on the plain outside the castle, and the brothers imprisoned.

For seven days together the Turks worked on the two of them to make them Muslims and got nowhere. The martyrs found a way to send their written confession secretly to the Metropolitan of Chios, who sent back his blessing; and the bishop contrived, by a woman, to have the immaculate Mysteries brought to them. Led out before the pasha they declared their faith again, and going to the death they cried to the crowd, «Χριστιανοί είμεθα, για τον Χριστό πηγαίνουμε στον θάνατο» — we are Christians, for Christ we are going to death. Stamatios was beheaded at eighteen years old.
