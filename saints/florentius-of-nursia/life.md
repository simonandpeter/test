# Florentius of Nursia

[Eutychius](/saints/eutychius-of-nursia) and Florentius were monks who laboured in Italy in the sixth century, in the region of Nursia. Eutychius turned many to God by his teaching; when the abbot of the neighbouring monastery died he was begged to take charge and agreed, but went on caring for the old place of his labours, where his companion Florentius remained.

Florentius worked wonders in his lifetime — he tamed a bear, which served him, pastured the sheep, carried water and did the elder’s other bidding; four monks, envying his fame, killed the bear, and the saint foretold God’s punishment on the killers, which came as he said, for they were struck with sickness — and seeing it Florentius grieved bitterly, calling himself the murderer of those monks. Eutychius worked no wonders in his life, but after his death healings were given from the garments he left, and in drought they were carried over the fields and God sent rain, as in 1492. Eutychius died on 23 May 540 and Florentius on 1 June 547.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them on the same day and names the place of their asceticism. saint.gr says they lived in the sixth century and practised their asceticism in a monastery of the region of Valcastoria, near Nursia in Italy, and that they fell asleep in peace in the year 540 — one year for both, where the Russian calendar the corpus read first gives that year to [Eutychius](/saints/eutychius-of-nursia) and 547 to him. It adds where the memory of them comes from: [Gregory the Dialogist](/saints/gregory-the-dialogist), whom it sends to 12 March, praised their virtues and their wonders in his discourses. The page gives them no birthplace and no account of the wonders themselves, and prints no hymn to them.
