# Nicholas of Pskov

saint.gr's calendar for 28 Φεβρουαρίου names him with the city that distinguishes him — its line reads «Άγιος Νικόλαος του Πσκωφ» — and the page standing under it gives one sentence: Nicholas came from Russia and was a fool for Christ; he lived in the city of Pskov in the years of the reign of Tsar Ivan the Terrible; and he fell asleep in peace in the year 1576.

That is the whole of the entry, apart from the relics: his body, the page says, is in the cathedral church of the Holy Trinity at Pskov in Russia. It gives him no year of birth — its heading leaves the first of his two dates a question mark — no account of his folly for Christ, no couplet and no hymn; and what stands above the sentence is only saint.gr's standing gloss on the name Nicholas, which belongs to the name and not to this man.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him a single sentence: he lived the life of a fool in the city of Pskov in the time of the tsar Ivan the Terrible, and fell asleep on 28 February 1576 — the day it keeps him on and the year the folder already holds.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 28 Φεβρουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/02/28/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1933/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026; and the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 28. фебруар ст. ст. — [the Serbian day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-03-13&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
