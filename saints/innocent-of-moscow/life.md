# Innocent of Moscow

Innocent, in the world John Evseyevich Popov-Veniaminov, was born on 26 August 1797 in the village of Anginskoye in the diocese of Irkutsk, into the family of a church reader; the Greek calendar names his parents Eusebius and Thekla. He could read as a small boy and read the Apostle in church at seven, and at the Irkutsk seminary, as its best pupil, was given the surname Veniaminov in memory of Archbishop Benjamin of Irkutsk. He married Catherine, a priest’s daughter, was ordained deacon in 1817 and priest in 1821 — on 18 May by the Russian life, 28 May by the Greek.

From 1823 he gave forty-five years to the missions of Kamchatka, the Aleutian Islands, North America, Yakutia and the Khabarovsk region, travelling in harsh country and at great risk. He baptised tens of thousands, built churches with schools beside them and taught in them himself, and his skill in many crafts served him. He studied the languages and ways of the peoples he preached to — in ten years on Unalaska, the Greek calendar says, not one native remained a pagan — made an alphabet and grammar of the Aleut language and translated the Catechism, the Gospel and many prayers into it; his *Indication of the Way into the Kingdom of Heaven* (1833) went through more than forty editions, and in 1859 the Yakuts first heard the Word of God and the services in their own tongue through his labours.

After his wife’s death Metropolitan Philaret of Moscow tonsured him a monk in November 1840 with the name Innocent, for Saint Innocent of Irkutsk, and in December he was made bishop of Kamchatka, the Kurils and the Aleutians, with his see at Sitka; he became archbishop in 1850. In 1868 — on 5 January by the Russian life, 25 May by the Greek — he succeeded Philaret as metropolitan of Moscow, founded the Missionary Society, made the Moscow Protection monastery a missionary house and in 1870 set up the Japanese Orthodox Mission under Archimandrite Nicholas (Kasatkin), later Saint Nicholas of Japan. He died on Great Saturday, 31 March 1879, and was buried at the Trinity-Sergius Lavra; the Russian Church glorified him on 6 October 1977, and keeps him on the day of his death and the day of his glorification. The Sretensky calendar’s longer life of him was not read.
