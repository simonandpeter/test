# Eustalia

Eustalia and Sopatra lived in the time of the emperor Maurice, which saint.gr puts at about the year 584. Eustalia came of Rome, of Christian parents who were particularly well off, so that after their death she had a considerable fortune, and she spent it on works of charity. Having served in Rome the girls who needed help because they had fallen into sin, she went on to Constantinople.

There she became known for her benefactions and for her spiritual learning, and many young women came to visit her at the monastery that gave her hospitality. One of those was the daughter of Maurice, [Sopatra](/saints/sopatra), who chose the monastic life over the riches of the palace and became a nun beside Eustalia. Eustalia went to the Lord first, and Sopatra carried on her work worthily to the end of her own life.

The page gives her no year of death and no burial, and prints no hymn for the pair. Her distich plays on her name as a robing: «Ὅλη καλὴ σὺ πρὸς Θεὸν χωρεῖς Λόγον, / Στολαῖς σταλεῖσα ψυχικαῖς Εὐστολία» — wholly fair, you go to God the Word, Eustolia, arrayed in the robes of the soul.

The Russian calendar's joint life for the two women gives this folder its first date and its first story. Eustolia was a native of Rome who came to Constantinople and entered one of the women's monasteries there, and by a virtuous and strict monastic life won the love and the honour of the sisters; not only nuns but many people of the world came to her for counsel and comfort. She met the emperor's daughter [Sopatra](/saints/sopatra), who was drawn to the monastic life, in the church of the Mother of God at Blachernae, and after that conversation the princess resolved to leave the world for good and give her will wholly over to Eustolia as her teacher. Out of the palace her father had given her a monastery grew up by degrees, known for the strictness of its rule. Eustolia died in 610 and Sopatra about fifteen years after her.
