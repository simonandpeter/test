# Anthony the Great

Born to a prosperous Coptic family in middle Egypt, Anthony gave away his
inheritance in his late teens after hearing the gospel counsel to sell all he
had, and withdrew to the desert margins of his village. He moved progressively
further into the wilderness — to the tombs, then to an abandoned fort at Pispir,
and finally to the mountain near the Red Sea where his monastery still stands.

He did not invent Christian asceticism, and he founded no order. What made him
the figure later tradition calls the father of monasticism was the crowd of
imitators who followed him into the desert, and the *Life* written shortly after
his death by [Athanasius of Alexandria](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria), which
carried his example across the Greek and Latin worlds within a generation.

He is said to have died at 105, having twice left the desert: once to support
the confessors during the persecution under Maximinus, and once to preach
against Arianism in Alexandria. He asked to be buried in an unmarked grave.
