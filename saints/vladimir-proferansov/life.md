# Vladimir (Proferansov)

He was born on 29 June 1874 in Moscow, the son of a priest, Alexander Proferansov. In 1898 he finished the Moscow theological seminary and went to work as a teacher, and in 1902 he was appointed psalmist to the church of St George on the Lubyanka. From 1905 he was a full member of the Moscow society for public readings and libraries, and in 1907 he had a letter of thanks from the Holy Synod for diligent work in the field of popular education; medals followed in 1909 and 1914.

From 1915 he acted as churchwarden at the same church; in 1916 he was ordained deacon to it, and in 1920 priest. In 1917 he was made clerk to the parish board of trustees, from 1918 he worked with the Moscow diocesan council, and from 1923 he was secretary to the patriarch; in 1924 he was given a pectoral cross for his work in the chancery of the church administration. His standing as a priest who knew both the patriarch and the deputy locum tenens well, and the nearness of his church to the OGPU building on the Lubyanka, led the OGPU to decide to recruit him.

On 9 January 1932 he was called in for questioning and offered collaboration. He agreed to it neither under persuasion nor under threat, and the investigator had to content himself with a signed undertaking to keep the conversation absolutely secret from everyone on pain of the highest penalty. On 8 February he was arrested and shut in the Butyrka prison in Moscow, and questioned the next day. Asked what he could say about gathering anti-Soviet churchmen around himself, he said he had organised no group; asked whether illegal meetings of the church’s active members had been held under his leadership, he said there had been no such meetings and almost no meetings at all; asked how often he commemorated those imprisoned for counter-revolutionary activity, he said he could not say how often, but whenever notes were handed in for prisoners he always commemorated them; asked whether he had told the faithful that Soviet power would soon end and a tsar would rule Russia, he said he had never spoken against Soviet power.

Questioned again on 25 February after several days of threats, he held out calmly and courageously, seeming absent from the interrogation, and said he did not admit himself guilty of anti-Soviet agitation or of gathering an anti-Soviet element around him. On 14 March 1932 a special conference of the OGPU collegium sentenced him to three years’ exile at Semipalatinsk. There he came to know a priest, [Constantine (Nekrasov)](/saints/constantine-nekrasov), who is the second presbyter in the same line of this calendar, and who it seems invited him to come to the town of Mozhaysk when his term was out, knowing that men who had been in exile were forbidden Moscow.

He came back from exile in 1935 and settled at Mozhaysk, a seriously sick man. The authorities again offered to make him an informer, promising him a good parish in exchange, and he refused again; and for that they would not let him serve. His own house in Moscow, where his wife Maria Petrovna lived, he could visit only secretly and never for more than a day. On 5 December 1937 he was arrested and imprisoned at Mozhaysk, charged with carrying on hidden counter-revolutionary work among the people around him. Asked what he knew of counter-revolutionary activity among the clergy, he said he knew nothing of any; asked what counter-revolutionary activity he was carrying on, he said he had carried on none and was carrying on none. On 9 December 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 15 December 1937 and buried in an unmarked common grave. He was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia in August 2000.

The two pages give him two grades and this folder records the fuller one. The name page titles him «иерей», a priest; the life calls him протоиерей throughout from 1932 onward and the day line puts him among «пресвитеров». He stands fifth of the eleven presbyters in that line, and the day page carries no hymn under his name.

His name page lists one other day, «26 января (Новомуч.)», the synaxis of the new martyrs and confessors of the Russian Church. One row per church is the schema, so that day is recorded here and he does not render on it.
