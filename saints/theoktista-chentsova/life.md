# Theoktista (Chentsova)

Theoktista was born on 21 January 1880 at Kaluga, the daughter of a Kaluga merchant who dealt in ironmongery, and she finished the town school. In September 1937 the authorities arrested her as an active believer and charged her with counter-revolutionary activity, which she did not admit. The calendar prints a piece of the interrogation, and what the investigator had against her was her flat.

He put it to her that she had furnished her flat with icons and held secret loud prayer meetings in it. She answered that there were indeed very many icons in her flat, about twenty, and that they had once been in the corridor as well until the other tenants objected and she moved them into the rooms — but that she held no prayer meetings and no loud services there. He put it to her that she had given suppers after church at which anti-Soviet talk went on; she answered that she had given no suppers and had carried on no such talk. He told her the investigation had established that she belonged to a counter-revolutionary church-monarchist organisation, and she answered that she did not admit herself guilty of it.

On 19 November 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced her to ten years in a corrective labour camp. She died in one of the Karaganda camps on 16 February 1942 and was buried in an unmarked grave, and was numbered among the saints of the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
