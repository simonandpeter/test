# Melasippus

Melasippus and his wife Cassine were of the city of Ancyra in Asia Minor, saint.gr says, and lived in the years of the emperor Julian the Apostate, which the page puts at 361. They were true Christians, and brought up their son Antonius with great care, so that he became a worthy member of society and a tried labourer of the Gospel. When the duke Agrippinus learned of the faith of the household he ordered the three of them arrested.

He tried to break the parents by telling them that their child's life depended on them, and that if they denied the Lord he would spare their son; and he pressed Antonius in the same way, saying that he could save his parents. But they did not fear a martyr's death, the page says, and their hearts were ready to go to the Lord. Hearing them praise Jesus in spite of his threats, Agrippinus grew angry and ordered them tortured. Melasippus and Cassine he put to death hanged upon a wooden frame.

Their distich sets their endurance against their flesh: «Οὐ σάρκες ἦσαν, ἀλλ' ἄσαρκός τις φύσις. / Ὁ Μελάσιππος πρὸς ξέσεις καὶ Κασίνη» — they were not flesh, but some fleshless nature, Melasippus and Cassine under the scrapings. Kept with him are [Cassine](/saints/cassine), their son [Antonius](/saints/antonius-son-of-melasippus) and [the Forty Children](/saints/forty-children) who believed at his son's deliverance. saint.gr prints no hymn for the entry.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps the three of them together on the same day the Greek calendar does. Its line for 7 ноября ст. ст. is «Мчч. Меласиппа и Касинии и сына их Антонина (363).», and the name link behind «Меласиппа» gives the calendar's own nominative, «Меласипп Анкирский, мч.».

The life behind the three name links is four sentences and is the same for all three of them. They suffered in the reign of the emperor Julian the Apostate, in the city of Ancyra, in 363. Melasippus and Cassine, torn with iron hooks and disfigured, died under the torture. Their son, the boy Antoninus, whom the persecutor made stand and watch his parents' torments, spat in the face of the God-forsaking emperor; for that he was put to the cruellest tortures of all, in which he stayed unhurt, and was then beheaded.

Forty young men who saw that the Lord was keeping his confessor Antoninus and that the tortures did him no harm believed in Christ, confessed their faith openly and received a martyr's end — [the Forty Children](/saints/forty-children) this corpus keeps from the Greek line for this same day. **The Russian line does not name them**: it stops at the three, printing «Мчч. Меласиппа и Касинии и сына их Антонина (363).» and nothing more, so no Russian row was written on that folder and the forty are reported instead.

**The page puts Ancyra in Phrygia, and the two calendars do not give the same year.** Its words are «в городе Анкире во Фригии», where the pages this folder was written from put Ancyra in Galatia; the page does not reconcile the two and nothing is chosen here. The year is the second disagreement: saint.gr gave 361, the year of Julian's accession, and this page gives 363 on its line and in its life, so the death above now reads 361 or 363.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 7 Νοεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/11/07/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2934/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 7 ноября ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261107.html) and [the three martyrs' life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life2497.htm), read 4 October 2026.*
