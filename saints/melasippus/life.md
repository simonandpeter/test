# Melasippus

Melasippus and his wife Cassine were of the city of Ancyra in Asia Minor, saint.gr says, and lived in the years of the emperor Julian the Apostate, which the page puts at 361. They were true Christians, and brought up their son Antonius with great care, so that he became a worthy member of society and a tried labourer of the Gospel. When the duke Agrippinus learned of the faith of the household he ordered the three of them arrested.

He tried to break the parents by telling them that their child's life depended on them, and that if they denied the Lord he would spare their son; and he pressed Antonius in the same way, saying that he could save his parents. But they did not fear a martyr's death, the page says, and their hearts were ready to go to the Lord. Hearing them praise Jesus in spite of his threats, Agrippinus grew angry and ordered them tortured. Melasippus and Cassine he put to death hanged upon a wooden frame.

Their distich sets their endurance against their flesh: «Οὐ σάρκες ἦσαν, ἀλλ' ἄσαρκός τις φύσις. / Ὁ Μελάσιππος πρὸς ξέσεις καὶ Κασίνη» — they were not flesh, but some fleshless nature, Melasippus and Cassine under the scrapings. Kept with him are [Cassine](/saints/cassine), their son [Antonius](/saints/antonius-son-of-melasippus) and [the Forty Children](/saints/forty-children) who believed at his son's deliverance. saint.gr prints no hymn for the entry.
