# Pachomius of Chios

Pachomius was born in 1840 in the village of Elaia on the island of Chios, and his name at birth was Panagiotis Arelias. The page then speaks of the massacre of the Greek population of Chios by the Turks in 1822, after which the islanders, made poor and wretched, went looking for a better life in Constantinople, then a great centre of trade; it puts the two statements in that order, and gives no further account of how he came to the city.

At Constantinople the young Panagiotis found himself one day in prison for the killing of a Turkish rival, although he had acted in his own defence. In the endless hours of despair and remorse in the prison, and in the fear of being put to death, he took refuge and comfort in his Orthodox faith, and prayed continually to the Mother of God to free him from the sentence of death and from the prison so that he might live a life of repentance.

When he had been released, two priests advised him to seek his repentance by going to Jerusalem. There, at the age of twenty-two, he was tonsured a monk with the name Pachomius in the Holy Lavra of Saint Sabbas in the Holy Land, and lived in asceticism, deep prayer, fasting and study, repenting fully of his crime and gaining the knowledge of heavenly things and the virtues.

He returned to Chios in 1865 and founded the monastery of the Holy Fathers on Mount Provateon, and the monastery of the Holy Emperors Constantine and Helen at Frangovounio. He did not cease to study the holy books, and set up a great library in his monastery. He passed to the Lord on 14 October 1905. By the life and example of this recent saint, the page says, we learn that we can rise even from the heaviest sins, drawing down God’s mercy by sincere repentance.

He was a pattern of life to [Nektarios](/saints/nektarios-of-aegina) and to [Anthimos of Chios](/saints/anthimos-of-chios). In 1886, at twenty, Anastasie — the future Nektarios — went to Chios as a schoolmaster, and used to visit the newly founded monastery of the Holy Fathers with the elder Pachomius, his spiritual father; after seven years he entered the monastery under his direction, and three years later was tonsured at Nea Moni with the name Lazarus, and a year after that ordained deacon with the name Nektarios. Pachomius and a wealthy local benefactor then persuaded the young monk to finish his schooling at Athens.

In 1888, at nineteen, Argyrios — the future Anthimos — visited the monastery of the Holy Fathers and received the elder Pachomius’s blessing to live a monastic life when he should go home again, since his poor parents and his village needed his help. After a time he withdrew to the monastery and was tonsured, taking the name Anthimos, which Pachomius gave him. The page prints no troparion for Pachomius.
