# Cyrus of Constantinople

saint.gr says Cyrus was a monk in a monastery of Pontus opposite Amastris. There the emperor Justinian II met him, and Cyrus assured him prophetically that he would win back his throne. When that came about the emperor remembered the words, and in the year 705 called him to Constantinople to take the patriarchal throne; his patriarchate lasted until 711. The page gives no year for his birth or his death, and none is supplied here.

Those years, saint.gr says, were troubled in Church and state by the heresies of Monophysitism and Monotheletism, although the Fourth and the Sixth Ecumenical Councils had condemned them. Their adherents were very many in Syria, Egypt, Mesopotamia, Armenia and Persia, and had helped the Arabs take those countries from Byzantium; and while their recovery was impossible, a party — chiefly of heretics, the page says — insisted it could be done if Byzantium revoked the decisions of those two councils.

That, saint.gr says, is unhappily what the emperor Philippicus did. Cyrus resisted the imperial order strongly, and the emperor deposed him for it and shut him up in the monastery of Chora. There he passed the last years of his life with an untroubled conscience, having scorned the impious emperor's threat, accepted his persecution and been ready to accept death as well. His synaxis, the page adds, was kept at the monastery of Chora, and at the church of Holy Wisdom if his memory fell on a Sunday.
