# Antonina of Nicaea

Antonina suffered at Nicaea in a persecution under the emperor Maximian, whom doxologia dates 284–305. She was horribly tortured and then thrown into prison, and Maximian could not bring her to renounce Christ and bow down to the idols. While the torments were going on, the life says, the angels of the Lord defended the martyr and put fear into the men who were killing her.

Even when they stretched her on a bed made red-hot with fire she was left unhurt by the power of the Lord. At the last, after a great many torments, they sewed her into a sack and drowned her in a lake. doxologia's page has no more than this to say, and prints no hymn for her.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and adds the city and the manner of her death. saint.gr says she came of Nicaea in Bithynia and lived in the years of the emperor Maximian, and that being a Christian she showed herself to Maximian when he came up to Nicaea and confessed Christ with a bold judgment. For that she was tortured and shut in prison; and when they brought her out they pressed her by force to deny Christ, and she would not, but stayed firm in her faith. Then the executioners hung her up and flayed her ribs, and afterwards laid her on a red-hot gridiron, from which the page says she came away unharmed. At the last they put her in a sack and threw her into the lake of Nicaea, and there she found the death of a martyr. The same page adds that her memory is kept again on the twelfth of June; the corpus keeps one feast to a church, so that second day stands as a note on the Greek row and she renders on the first of March only.

The Serbian church keeps her on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives her four sentences: born at Nicaea, arrested for the faith of Christ and fiercely tortured, and at the last sewn into a sack and drowned in a lake. It dates that 302 — a year inside the reign this folder's interval already holds, and narrower than it, which is a reading left for the author rather than written over a sourced interval. But God saved her soul, the Prologue closes, and glorified her for ever among the angels in heaven and among the faithful on earth.
