# Werburgh

Werburgh — saint.gr prints five Latin-letter spellings of the name beside the Greek — is the patron of Chester, and was abbess in the monasteries of Weedon, Trentham, Hanbury, Minster in Sheppey and Ely. She was born in Staffordshire in the seventh century. Her mother was St Ermenilda, daughter of Ercombert king of Kent and of St Sexburga, and her father was Wulfhere, the first Christian king of Mercia.

She inherited her mother's temper and her gifts. Being much sought after in marriage for her beauty and her grace, and not wishing to marry, she entered at last, with her father's consent, the abbey of Ely, which her aunt St Etheldreda had founded and which the Greek calendar keeps on 23 June. After her father's death in 673 she and her mother became nuns in a monastery at Ely, where she was her mother's subordinate and rose to such a height of virtue that her uncle king Ethelred appointed her head of the women's monasteries of the kingdom.

She worked many miracles, the page says, and was given the gifts of healing and of prophecy. One of the best known of them is that by a single command she drove off a flock of wild geese that were ruining the corn at Weedon, and no wild goose was ever seen in that district again. Having foreseen her death, she fell asleep peacefully in the community at Trentham, leaving instructions that her body be buried at Hanbury.

Nine years after her repose king Kenred took up her relics incorrupt. In 869, during the Danish invasion, the body dissolved in a wonderful manner. The page prints no hymn for her and no verses.
