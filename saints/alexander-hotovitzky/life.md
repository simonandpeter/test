# Alexander Hotovitzky

Alexander Hotovitzky was born on 11 February 1872 in the town of Kremenets, in the devout family of the archpriest Alexander, who was rector of the Volhynia theological seminary. He was given a good Christian upbringing by his parents, who breathed into him the love of the Orthodox Church and of the people of God, and he studied at the Volhynia seminary and at the theological academy of Saint Petersburg.

When he had finished the academy he was sent as a missionary to the diocese of the Aleutians and North America, and appointed reader at the newly founded Orthodox church of Saint Nicholas in New York City. After his marriage to Maria Scherbuhina, a graduate of the Pavlovsk institute in Saint Petersburg, he was ordained deacon, and soon after, on 25 February 1896, ordained priest by Bishop Nicholas Ziorov of the Aleutians, in the cathedral at San Francisco.

A week later he returned to New York to serve at Saint Nicholas. In the years that followed he took part in many missionary works and had an essential part in the founding of many Orthodox parishes; by his own initiative and active part a new cathedral of Saint Nicholas was built to replace the small parish church in New York City, and he travelled much in the United States and in Russia itself to gather funds for its building. After eighteen years he was recalled to Russia, on 26 February 1914.

In Russia he helped Patriarch [Tikhon](/saints/tikhon-of-moscow) in the administration of the diocese of Moscow. On 4 September 1924 the State Political Directorate recommended the administrative exile of thirteen clergy and church leaders, he among them; after a further interrogation he was exiled for three years to the Turukhan region of Siberia. In the summer of 1937 he was arrested again, condemned to death and shot on 19 August 1937, and was buried in the Donskoye cemetery in Moscow.
