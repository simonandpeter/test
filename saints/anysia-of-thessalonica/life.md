# Anysia of Thessalonica

In the days of the persecutor Maximian there was a maiden named Anysia, born in the famous city of Thessalonica of rich and believing parents, who taught her the fear of the Lord; and she grew in good works faster than in years, for her parents fed her more with the teachings of the right faith than with milk. When her childhood years had passed her decency showed itself beyond hope, and she was taught the reading of the better books; and being wise she understood everything easily, and bore fruit worthy of her teaching.

Full of the Spirit of God and of angelic ways, her face truly shone with a chosen beauty; but she did not go out of her house, and hid the beauty of her youth in her chamber. Often she would say to herself, with sorrow: «O deceiving life of youth, which either harms others or is harmed by them! Old age is good — but woe is me, for the length of time fills me with grief, since it keeps me so long apart from the things of heaven». That maiden never ceased to call Christ to her help, bending her knees and watering the ground with her tears.

When her parents passed to the Lord she became heir of her father's property, and considered what she would do with the wealth she had inherited; for she had a great treasury of gold and silver, most splendid houses, many herds of cattle, men servants and women servants, an unnumbered multitude of clothes of great price, ornaments of gold and every kind of pearl and precious stone, and she shone with the greatness of her riches.

Seeing all this, she said: «How shall we be saved in the midst of so much earthly wealth? How shall we tread on the dragon's head, and how shall we put off his deceit as one puts off a garment? But I know what I shall do: against the dragon I shall use the dragon's own craft». The middle of the page — some thirteen thousand characters of it — was not read; what it prints at the end is her death.

Bearing herself with courage and not letting the insult pass, she spat in the face of the man who had seized her, saying: «May my Jesus Christ rebuke you, devil». And he, filled with fury and unable to bear the hearing of the name of Christ, drew his sword and struck her in the side and ran her through. The holy maiden fell at once to the ground and, with the pouring out of her blood, gave up her holy soul into the hands of Christ her God, whom she had loved and desired every day of her life, longing to die for him.

The people who saw her dead passed by and wept, on the one hand for the sacrifice of her youth and for so cruel a death, and on the other murmured against that godless imperial order, which had given an unjust judgement, to destroy an innocent people. Christians took her honoured relics and cared for them with honour, and buried her about two stadia from the gate called the Cassandreotic, on the left side of the public road, where afterwards they built a house of prayer over her. All this was done while Maximian reigned.

doxologia.ro's line for the day names her «Sfânta Muceniță Anisia fecioara» and gives no year, and the page the site keeps for her troparion is an audio recording of the hymn in Greek and prints no text.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 30 December, and gives the year the Romanian line withheld: saint.gr puts her in the reign of Diocletian and heads her entry «(; - 298)». She was of Thessalonica, it says, the daughter of devout and very rich parents; and when they died she was left mistress of herself. Neither the wealth she inherited went to her head nor her orphanhood carried her away, but with prudence and self-restraint she tried always to learn «τι εστίν ευάρεστον τω Κυρίω» — what is well-pleasing to the Lord.

That piety of hers made her known to the idolaters. Once, as she was going to church, a pagan soldier met her, seized her violently, dragged her to the altars of the idols and pressed her to sacrifice to the gods. Anysia confessed that she believed in the one true God, Jesus Christ, and that it was he she strove to please every day. The soldier, enraged, began to blaspheme God; and then Anysia spat in his face. Shamed, he drew his sword and ran it through her side, and so she took the unfading crown of martyrdom.

The Охридски пролог for the Julian 30 December tells the same death and adds the life that went before it. Anysia was born at Thessalonica of notable and wealthy parents and brought up in the faith of Christ, and she was orphaned early; from then on she gave herself wholly, in her own house, to the thought of God and to prayer. Kindled with the love of Christ she would often say: „О лажни животе младих, јер или саблажњаваш, или се саблажњаваш“ — O deceitful life of the young, for either you give offence or you take it; better is old age, and yet sorrow takes me for the length of time that divides me from the things of heaven.

Then she sold her whole estate and gave it to the destitute, and lived by the work of her own hands. She kept a strict fast, slept very little, and poured out tears without ceasing at prayer; and when sleep began to master her she would say to herself, „Опасно је спавати када непријатељ мој бди“ — it is dangerous to sleep when my enemy is awake.

This book puts the edict at its sharpest: the wicked emperor Maximian proclaimed that any man might kill a Christian wherever he saw or met him, without trial and without sentence. The day the holy girl went out into the street to go to church was a heathen feast of the sun. The soldier who saw how fair she was came up to her with an unclean desire and asked her name; she crossed herself and answered, „Слушкиња сам Христова, и идем у цркву“ — I am the handmaid of Christ, and I am going to church. When he came closer and began to talk senselessly she pushed him off and spat in his face, and he struck her with his sword under the ribs and ran her through. She suffered in 298, the Christians buried her honourably, and a church was raised over her grave.
