# Susanna, mother of Nina

Susanna was born in Cappadocia, where many Georgians then lived, and was the sister of a bishop of Jerusalem whom some, the page says, identify with Juvenal. Their parents died young and the two children were left orphaned and without support, so they thought to go to Jerusalem. They set out in faith in God, not minding the long journey before them, and when they reached the Holy City they found refuge in the Church of the Resurrection.

In a short time her brother was appointed steward of the Holy Sepulchre, while Susanna entered the service of the pious Sara-Niophora of Bethlehem, who was a deaconess there. It was that deaconess, the life says, who counselled the bishop to give his sister in marriage to [Zabulon](/saints/zabulon-father-of-nina), the famous and God-fearing commander of armies who had helped the Franks to know Christ. The counsel seemed good to him; the marriage was made, and the two went home to Cappadocia, where their daughter [Nina](/saints/nina-of-georgia), the enlightener of Georgia, was born.

When Nina was twelve the family went up to Jerusalem, and there Zabulon left them for the desert of the Jordan. Susanna was then appointed deaconess by her brother the bishop, in the holy Church of the Resurrection, to care for the poor and sick women; and Nina was given into the hands of the aged Niophora, a woman full of piety, to be brought up. The page gives Susanna no year and no account of her death, and names the book it is drawing on: the Georgian Paterikon.
