# Michael the Presbyter (4 September)

Michael Bogorodsky was born on 19 September 1878 in the village of Molokovo
in Tver province; his father Nicholas was clerk on a landowner’s estate.
After the Tver seminary he was ordained and served in the churches of the
Tver diocese, and for blameless fulfilment of his pastoral duties Patriarch
Tikhon gave him the pectoral cross and the rank of archpriest in April
1922. The godless state fought the church without mercy, charging some
clergy and laity with political crimes and others with non-payment of
impossible taxes: any official could demand a huge sum of any priest, and
since there was usually nowhere to get it, the priest was arrested and the
church closed with him. His zeal was soon noticed. In January 1930 he was
arrested and charged with having “evaded in December 1929 the repeated
grain-procurement tasks set him by the commission”, sentenced to a year and
a fine of four thousand roubles — and since he had no such money, nor even
the smaller tax, his property was inventoried, and, to take a conscientious
and respected pastor from his people, he was banished for five years from
Moscow province “in view of his social danger to the locality”. Freed after
a year, he came back to the Tver diocese, and the hieromartyr Archbishop
Thaddeus sent him to the Skorbyashchenskaya church at Selets in the
Maksatikha district as dean of the eleventh deanery; in April 1932 he was
given the palitsa for his zeal. Like many priests who had been inside he
was careful and kept off politics, but when the new persecution began no
prudence could save anyone from arrest. The NKVD called frightened false
witnesses, who could say nothing against him and whose every word was
written up as evidence: that at the end of July 1937, asked by the church
fence how he was living, he had said, not badly — the believers are
increasing, the pull to the church grows and grows; that he had said there
was respect for priests before the revolution, that the new constitution
gave a priest the right to serve and the same rights as everyone, and that
he would never give up his priesthood. He was arrested on 8 August 1937,
the church papers taken at the search, and special notice paid to his
blessing to be confessor of the deanery’s clergy and their families — so
the first question was what organisational work Archbishop Thaddeus had
given him. In 1933 to 1936, he answered, I kept the register and heard the
confessions of the clergy and their families in my deanery. Told that he
had carried on counter-revolutionary agitation since his exile, he said he
had never done such work anywhere — I sometimes said that before there was
respect for us priests, and now they laugh at us and despise us. Told that
he had criticised Pravda’s reports on Spain for printing only the rebels’
losses, he said he could say nothing to that, but he knew the course of
events in Spain, since he read Pravda regularly. That was the end of the
interrogation. On 13 September the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot,
and he was shot a few days later, on 17 September 1937 — 4 September of the
old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him, with the bishops Gregory
and Sergius and their company. The Jubilee Council of Bishops of August
2000 numbered him among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia.
