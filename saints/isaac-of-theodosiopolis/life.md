# Isaac of Theodosiopolis

Isaac and [Joseph](/saints/joseph-of-theodosiopolis) were brothers, born at Theodosiopolis, also called Karnu, the present Erzurum. Their father was a Muslim of rank and their mother a good and devout Christian, who secretly brought up these two sons, and an elder one whose name is not known, in the Christian faith. When they were grown men, Joseph alone of the three being married, the brothers wished to leave their Muslim father so as to confess faith in Christ without hindrance, and wrote to the emperor Nicephorus I (802–811) asking leave to move to Constantinople and enter service at his court. The answer was favourable. The eldest soon set out, but Joseph and Isaac were held on the order of the emir of Theodosiopolis.

Asked why they were moving to Constantinople, the brothers answered, to the astonishment of all present, their father among them, that they had been Christians from birth and were fleeing the ungodly so as to confess their faith freely. No persuasion or threat moved them. The emir called a council of his nobles and sentenced them to death, and at the place of execution they knelt, prayed to the Lord, and were beheaded, in 808. At night a pillar of extraordinary light came down over the unburied bodies and stood above them, and the next day the Muslims, struck by the sign, asked the Christians of the city to bury them; a church of the Holy Trinity was later built over the grave.

The Russian calendar calls them the Georgian brothers and marks the feast as Georgian; its life calls them the martyrs of Theodosiopolis.
