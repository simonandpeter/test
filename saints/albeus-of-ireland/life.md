# Albeus (Ailbe) of Ireland

Albeus — Ailbe — is honoured as the chief patron of Munster, one of the four
provinces of Ireland. He was converted by certain Britons and had travelled
to Rome before Patrick came among the Irish; after his return he became the
disciple and fellow-labourer of that apostle of his country, who ordained
him first archbishop of Munster, and he fixed his see at Emly, since removed
to Cashel. With such authority did he deliver the dictates of eternal wisdom
to a rude people, with such force did he set forth the holiness of the
divine law by word and example, and so plain were the miracles that
confirmed what he preached, that the doctrine made its way easily to his
hearers’ hearts; he brought over an incredible multitude to the faith of
Christ and breathed into many the perfect spirit of the Gospel, having a
wonderful art of making men not only Christians but saints. King Aengus gave
him the isle of Aran, and he founded there a great monastery so famous for
the holiness of its inmates that the island was long called Aran of the
Saints; the rule he drew up for them was still extant in old Irish in
Ussher’s day. Though zeal for God’s honour and charity for souls kept him in
the world, he was always careful, by habitual recollection and frequent
retreats, to nourish in his own soul the pure love of heavenly things and to
live in a very familiar and intimate acquaintance with himself. In his old
age he longed to give the care of his flock to others and prepare himself in
holy solitude for his great change, and begged leave to withdraw to Thule,
the farthest country toward the north that the ancients knew — Shetland, or
Iceland, or some part of Greenland — but the king guarded the ports to
prevent his flight, and he died amid the labours of his charge, in 525 by
the annals of Ulster and Inisfallen or in 541 by the Four Masters; Butler
notes that even the first date would make him a hundred and sixty-five years
old, and that the chronologers have probably confounded him with an Albeus
of Seanchua who died in 545. Emly was plundered in 1122 and his mitre and
chief relics scattered or burnt; the see was joined to Cashel in 1568, and
Emly dwindled to a village. The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 12 September
with the line that it has no details of his life, and Butler keeps him the
same day.

*After Alban Butler’s Lives of the Saints (1866), volume IX, September — [St. Albeus, Bishop and Confessor in Ireland](https://www.bartleby.com/lit-hub/lives-of-the-saints/volume-ix-september/st-albeus-bishop-and-confessor-in-ireland), read 6 September 2026; the Greek synaxarion’s line (saint.gr, 12 September) was all this entry had until then.*
