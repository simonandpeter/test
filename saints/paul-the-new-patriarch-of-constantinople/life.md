# Paul the New

Paul, called the New, patriarch of Constantinople, is kept on 30 August with
two other patriarchs,
[Alexander](/saints/alexander-patriarch-of-constantinople) and John, and the
calendars are not sure which Paul he is. Some, the Greek synaxarion says,
take him for Paul III, patriarch from 687 to 693, who presided over the
council called the Quinisext; others for Paul IV, a Cypriot who shone,
Theophanes says, in word and deed, came to the throne in 780, resigned at
the end of August 784 and withdrew to the monastery of Floros, where he
lived as a simple monk only two or three months after his resignation — a
zealot of Orthodoxy, noted for his almsgiving, and before the twelfth
century his memory was kept on 2 September. The Prologue, which the Serbian
calendar follows, tells it of the fourth Paul: he governed the Church for
five years and eight months in the years of the iconoclasm and gave up the
throne, secretly taking the schema, to repent of having at first gone along
with the iconoclasts; he was the predecessor of the great Tarasius, and
reposed in 784 in the reign of Irene and Constantine. The Sretensky
calendar’s joint life of the three patriarchs says of Alexander, John the
Faster and Paul that they lived at different times but each had to face the
heretics who distorted the teaching of the Church. The synaxarion’s verse
for him: leaving the courses of this life, Paul found a pause from its
pains.
