# Carpus of Thyatira

Carpus and Papylas were born at Pergamum of right-believing parents, and the page calls them great pillars and foundations of the Church. Both gave their bodies only what was needed, and so little even of that, it says, that they differed from the angels in body alone and by their great abstinence scarcely seemed to have a body at all. Rising by such a life to the height of the virtues, they were counted worthy to be trusted with the governing of the Church: Carpus was chosen bishop and preached the mysteries of the right faith to those in Thyatira, and [Papylas](/saints/papylas-the-deacon) was honoured by him with the rank of deacon.

Their fame went out through all the countries round about, for a good work cannot be hidden, and crowds ran to them, listened eagerly to their teaching and came near to the right faith. The devil found servants of his malice who slandered them to the pagan emperor Decius, and the proconsul Valerie ordered everyone in that country to come without delay to the place of sacrifice. The two were not in the assembly, for they were offering elsewhere the pure sacrifice of prayer; they were sought out and brought before him.

As they answered him the earth shook and every idol those pagans honoured broke and scattered like dust; but Valerie, who should have wondered at the power of God, grew only more set. Ashamed to begin with the heavy torments, because of their good birth and their gentleness, he had iron chains put on their necks and had them led naked through the streets, and then tried soft words. They answered that age is no warrant for a faith, since malice too is old and is not to be honoured for its age, and spoke to him of the God who has no beginning and no end and who brought all things out of nothing.

He gave their property to their accusers, tied them to horses and had them driven to Sardis, so that they covered the road from Thyatira in a single day and were dragged when the horses outran them. Their servant [Agathodorus](/saints/agathodorus-servant-of-carpus-and-papylas) suffered with them, and at Sardis was stretched out and beaten with ox sinews without mercy until he died. When the judge called his death the fitting end of a fool, the martyrs called it blessed and said they desired the same.

Carpus was then stripped, hung up and beaten with rods of thorn until the flesh was torn from him; other tormentors burned his ribs with candles and strewed salt in his wounds. He told Valerie that his counsel led from light to darkness and from life to death, and that he grieved more over the judge’s ruin than the judge did over his sufferings. Their thighs were cut with a razor, and they bore it magnifying God.

Beasts were brought and loosed on them. A she-bear showed nothing of her nature but lay down and embraced their feet as if honouring them; a lion was tamed as quickly, and, roaring, spoke with a human voice and rebuked the tormentors for their savagery, and they stopped their ears and called it the martyrs’ sorcery. Then iron shoes full of sharp nails were put on the saints and they were made to run in them.

Valerie heated a furnace until it glowed and threw them into it, and [Agathonica](/saints/agathonica-sister-of-papylas), the sister of Papylas, went into the flame with them, wishing to share the same struggle and desiring to die for Christ. The furnace did not burn them but was cooled by the power of Christ, for a great rain came down and the fire went out; and coming out unhurt they were taken back to prison, where they sang to God as in a church.

The judge, not knowing what more to do, was ashamed and beaten, and gave his last sentence: that their heads be cut off. They were led out to the place of killing rejoicing, and lifting their eyes and their hands to heaven they prayed not only for themselves but for those who were killing them; and so they ended their struggle, dying by the sword. The faithful took up their bodies in secret, which had been thrown out without honour and left unregarded, and buried them with honour. The page names Decius as the emperor and Valerie as the judge, and gives no year, and none is supplied here.
