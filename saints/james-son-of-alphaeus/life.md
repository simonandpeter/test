# James, son of Alphaeus

James was the son of Alphaeus and the brother of Matthew the publican and evangelist. The Romanian life says the Lord, choosing men without malice and upright for the apostolic office and sending them to preach the Gospel in all the world, chose this James and numbered him as worthy among the apostles. He was one of the twelve, an eyewitness and disciple of Christ, a preacher of the Mysteries and a follower of his steps.

When the Holy Spirit came down on them in tongues of fire he went out to many nations and peoples to preach Christ, to guide, and to bring back those who had wandered. The life says he burned the thorns of unbelief like fire, broke the idols and scattered their temples, healed many sicknesses, drove the unclean spirits out of men, and brought a multitude to the Lord; from which he won a new name, the divine seed, because he sowed and made to grow the right faith in the hearts of men.

Going about many countries he sowed that heavenly seed and gathered the ears of men's salvation, and finished his course after Christ as a follower of his sufferings: he was nailed to a cross and gave up his soul to God. The life ends by saying that this divine seed was gathered with a hundredfold of fruit into the heavenly granary.

The Greek calendar names him again on 30 Ιουνίου, where saint.gr keeps the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles. The page lists all twelve whom the Lord first chose, Judas Iscariot excepted and the twelfth place filled by the apostle elected in the upper room on the eve of Pentecost, and it says plainly that it sketches each man's life at his own feast and not here. That roll includes him, though the first of the three apolytikia the page prints runs through a different twelve — Peter and Paul with Luke and Mark, and without him — so that hymn is not taken here. What the synaxis is for, the page says, is the reminder of the unity the twelve had among themselves and of the manner of life that did so much for the world's rebirth in Christ; and the duty it draws from that is that Christians should move in their tracks with the same warm zeal for spreading the Gospel.

The Russian calendar keeps him at the head of its own 9 October, «Ап. Иакова Алфеева (I)», and gives him the day's rank: the first of the two icons the day carries is «Совершается служба с полиелеем», a service with the polyeleos, and it stands immediately before his line and before nobody else's. His index page keeps him here and on 30 June with the Twelve, which is the day the Greek calendar keeps him on.

The site reaches two lives for him and they agree. He was one of the Twelve and the brother of [Matthew](/saints/matthew-the-apostle) the Evangelist, who had been a tax-collector before the Lord called him; he was called by the Lord himself into the number of the Twelve and sent out with the rest to preach. After the descent of the Holy Spirit he preached first in Judaea, then went with [Andrew the First-Called](/saints/andrew-the-first-called) to Edessa, and spread the Gospel in Gaza, in Eleutheropolis and in the places bordering them, turning many to the way of salvation. From there he went into Egypt, and at Ostracine — a town on the coast at the Palestinian border, the longer of the two lives explains — he was crucified.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him at the head of the Julian 9 October and is the fourth book to tell this life, and almost nothing in it is new. The son of Alphaeus and one of the twelve great apostles; own brother of the apostle and evangelist [Matthew](/saints/matthew-the-apostle); a true witness of the words and wonders of the Lord, and of his passion, his resurrection and his ascension. After the descent of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost the preaching of the Gospel at Eleutheropolis and the places about it fell to his lot, and afterwards Egypt, where he suffered for his Saviour.

He carried the saving news of the incarnate God the Word with great power, the book says, both in word and in deed, pulling down idolatry, driving the demons out of men and healing every ailment and every sickness in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ; and his labour was crowned with great success, for many of the heathen believed, churches were founded and set in order, and priests and bishops appointed. He suffered in Egypt, in the city of Ostracine, crucified by the heathen.
