# The Holy Infant (18 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 18 November keeps «Άγιο Νήπιο», a holy infant, and dates it 304 — the year it gives the Romanos of the same day. Its whole notice is one scene. When Saint [Romanos](/saints/romanus-the-deacon-of-caesarea) said to the governor Asklepiades that even the children know that the idols are not gods, the governor asked an infant which god it believed in. This holy infant answered miraculously, with clear articulation of voice: Christ. And it was beheaded at once.

The calendar gives the child no name, no parents, no city and no age, and does not say whether it was a boy or a girl; «Νήπιο» is neuter in Greek, and the page leaves it so. It prints no hymn. The two-line verse over the entry puts the child where the Church puts it: an infant that gained the bosom of Abraham by the sword was seen seated with the infants of Bethlehem.

**This calendar gives the child a name.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 18 November on one line with the deacon, «Мчч. Романа диакона и отрока Варула (303).», the name link behind his place in it gives «Варул Антиохийский, отрок, мч.», and the life page they share is titled for the two of them. What it tells is the scene saint.gr prints without a name: the deacon, under torture, saw a Christian child in the crowd and told the governor Asklepiades that the young boy had more understanding than he had; the governor had the child brought, and to every question Varul confessed the faith in Christ, the one true God, firmly and without fear.

Angered, Asklepiades commanded that he be cruelly beaten and then beheaded. Before his death the holy boy asked his mother, who was present at the execution, to give him drink, and his mother entreated him to bear everything through for the Lord Jesus Christ; she herself laid her son’s head upon the block, and after the execution she buried him. The identification rests on the day, on the companion the two calendars name alike, on the governor, and on the manner of the death — not on a name, since the Greek gives none. Where they differ this corpus resolves nothing: saint.gr has an infant, «Άγιο Νήπιο», neuter and unnamed, and this calendar an «отрок», a boy, with a name and a mother. The folder’s display name and its silence about sex are left as the Greek reading made them.

The Охридски пролог names him. Its heading for the Julian 18 November is „Св. муч. Роман и Варул.“ — the holy martyrs Romanus and Barulas — and its body says that [Romanus](/saints/romanus-the-deacon-of-caesarea), under the lash at Antioch, saw a child whose name was Barulas and told the governor Asclepiades that this little child was more sensible than he in his age, for it knew the true God and he did not. The governor began questioning the child about the faith, and the child declared his faith in Christ the Lord as the one true God against the false idols; so Asclepiades had him cut down with the sword, and Romanus strangled in prison, and both of them inherited the kingdom of Christ.

That is the whole of what this book adds, and the name is the part of it that matters here: the Greek calendar which gave this folder its display name prints only «Άγιο Νήπιο», the Holy Infant, and this book agrees with the Russian calendar in calling him Barulas. It gives him no age beyond a child, no city of his own, and no year of his own — only that his beheading and the deacon's strangling were in the same hour, which that calendar puts in 303.
