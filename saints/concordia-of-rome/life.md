# Concordia of Rome

Concordia was the nurse of [Hippolytus](/saints/hippolytus-of-rome), keeper of the prisons in Rome, and was baptised with his whole household when Hippolytus came to Christ through the martyr [Lawrence](/saints/lawrence-of-rome). When Hippolytus was arrested for burying Lawrence’s body and would not sacrifice, the emperor had her beaten to death with leaden rods and the rest of the household beheaded before his eyes, before Hippolytus himself was dragged to death by wild horses, on 13 August 258. Her body was thrown into a sewer in Rome; after a time [Irenaeus](/saints/irenaeus-of-rome) and [Abundius](/saints/abundius-of-rome) learned from a soldier where it lay and buried it beside Hippolytus, and died for it. The Prologue gives her the household’s answer to the emperor: we would rather die honourably with our master in the faith of Christ than live dishonourably with you — and says she was killed first, and the other eighteen after her, before Hippolytus’s eyes.
