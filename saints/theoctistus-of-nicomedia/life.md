# Theoctistus of Nicomedia

He was a soldier, and the whole of what is recorded of him happens in one sentence at the end of somebody else's life. [Cyprian of Antioch](/saints/cyprian-of-antioch), once the chief pagan priest of that city, and [Justina of Antioch](/saints/justina-of-antioch), the virgin whose fasting and prayer had broken every spell he sent against her, were seized in the persecution under Diocletian, taken to Nicomedia and, after savage torments, beheaded. Theoctistus was on duty. Seeing the two of them suffer for nothing he had done, he declared himself a Christian and was executed beside them.

The Russian calendar prints the three together on 2 October with one year for all of them, 304, and the life on the same site states it outright: the three were put to death at Nicomedia in that year. The service is a six-stichera one, which the page says with an icon rather than a word.

**The Greek and the Romanian calendars both keep a Theoctistus on 3 October**, one day later, and the Greek synaxarion says of him only that he was beheaded, with no year and no place. That folder exists here already. Whether the two are one man was not settled on this reading and so was not assumed: the corpus holds them apart, and whoever closes the question should do it from a source that names both days.
