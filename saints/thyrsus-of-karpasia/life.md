# Thyrsus of Karpasia

Thyrsus is one of the three hierarchs of Karpasia, and in the Christian world, saint.gr says, he is better known by the popular name Ais Therissos, a much respected and much loved saint. Unfortunately, it goes on, the information we have from his synaxarion and his service is very poor and limited, and tells us few things about his life and his work; some even doubt whether he was a hierarch or a venerable or a martyr, because there is another saint of the same name who lived in the reign of the emperor Decius and suffered in the Hellespont.

When and where he was born, the page says, we do not know. From his service it learns that he was a most excellent nursling of the people of Karpasia and that from infancy he longed carefully for virtue, and from those words of the hymnographer it allows itself to take him for a man born in the beautiful province of Cyprus, Karpasia, and his parents for pious and virtuous people. In his village he will have learned what little letters there were in those years, but his regular and careful study of Scripture enriched his knowledge daily and cultivated his pure soul, so that in time small and great ran to him eagerly to hear the prudent words from his mouth.

When he reached the years of a young man he came into the ranks of the clergy and served his native Karpasia as deacon and as priest; and later, when the episcopal throne fell empty, he did not hesitate before everybody's entreaties to accept the call and take up the high office of bishop. His time as bishop coincided with a period of political disorders for Cyprus, when persecutions and pain weighed on hearts and what was needed was a strengthener, a consoler, a strong preacher; and such a man was found in him. By his visits to the villages and the countryside and by his fatherly manner he dispelled the pain and gave back to those hurt hearts consolation, hope and joy.

When Nikephoros Phokas freed Cyprus from the power of the Arabs in 965 and made it a province of the empire again, Thyrsus found a suitable successor for his see and withdrew to a cave on the northern shore of Karpasia. There, in his hermitage, crowds of the faithful visited him daily to hear his words and his counsels and to take his blessing, and there he stayed to the end. His death grieved everybody; his spiritual children buried the holy body with tears, and beside the cave in which he had lived as an ascetic they built a beautiful church, and kept his holy relics in a casket inside it.

The wonders he worked in life went on after his death, and the page tells one of them. A Christian of Karpasi suffered badly in his eyes, one of which had gone white inside and saw nothing. Having nothing else to do he remembered the saint, made ready what a liturgy needs, and asked the priest to come with him, serve, and give him communion. They went the evening before, and the sick man passed nearly the whole night awake on his knees, praying; towards dawn the pains softened and he slept a little; in the morning the liturgy was served and he received communion. Walking home, near the bridge outside Karpasi, he had a wish to take off the kerchief with which his eye was bound — and the eye was completely well and saw perfectly.

Thousands of the faithful visit the church of his name every year to ask his grace with reverence. His holy water heals pain of the eyes and diseases of the skin; and it is believed that the sick, after washing with it, must rinse with sea water, and that if the water is carried away it loses its healing property.
