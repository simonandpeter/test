# Aquilina (7 April)

Doxologia.ro's calendar for 7 aprilie names her and gives no life. The line is «Sfânta Muceniță Achilina», and her own page on the site carries no life tab and no troparion — only the name, the date, and the readings appointed for her day, the labourers together with Christ from the second letter to the Corinthians and the Canaanite woman from Matthew. No country, no city, no year, no emperor and no manner of death is given.

She is not Aquilina of Zagliveri, the new-martyr near Thessalonica whom the corpus keeps on 27 septembrie with a life of her own. She is also not named as the mother of Calliopius, who stands on the same Romanian day: that mother is called Theoclia in the life doxologia prints for him, and the calendar keeps the two women on separate lines. Who she is beyond a martyr's name on this day, the source does not say.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and joins her to a company the Romanian one does not. saint.gr's own sentence for her is as short as doxologia's: «Η Αγία Ακυλίνα μαρτύρησε αφού την έδεσαν οπισθάγκωνα και έκαψαν την κοιλιακή της χώρα» — she was martyred after they bound her hands behind her back and burnt her belly, which is what the couplet over the entry says too. It glosses the name from Ἀκύλας, from the Latin acula, a small needle, one sharp and useful as a needle, or else from aquila, an eagle.

In place of a life the page prints the notice it says stands in Laura Codex 170 for [Rufinus](/saints/rufinus-the-deacon), Aquilina and [the two hundred martyrs](/saints/two-hundred-martyrs-of-sinope) together — the same notice that stands on all three of their pages. It sets them under the lawless Decius, whose reign it gives as 249 to 251, gathered and agreed with one another to hold to the faith in Christ; it makes them the soldiers the emperor sent to arrest [Christopher](/saints/christopher), who cried out at the saint's wonders that great is the God of the Christians and him alone do they worship; and it ends with the emperor, boiling over with rage, ordering their flesh cut limb from limb with the sword before his eyes. Nothing on the Greek page connects her to the mother of Calliopius, and nothing on it says where she suffered.

**The Russian church calendar keeps the three of them on the Julian 7 April**, in one line, and it is the first calendar read for two of these folders to do anything but list them: «Мчч. Руфина диакона, Акилины и с ними 200 воинов (ок. 310).» — the martyrs Rufinus the deacon, Aquilina, and two hundred soldiers with them, about the year 310. The two name links give «Руфин, диакон, сщмч.» and «Акилина Младшая, мц.» — Aquilina **the Younger**, which separates her from the «Акилина» of Byblos the corpus keeps in June and which no other book read here printed — and the two hundred have no name link at all, the figure standing in the line as plain text.

The whole of the life is one sentence long and it is the story the Greek pages left out: the three suffered about 310 in the city of Sinope on the Black Sea under the emperor the page dates 305 to 311; when the deacon was put in prison for confessing the Christian faith, Aquilina cared for him and was taken into custody for it; and in the prison, by their wonders, the two of them turned the two hundred soldiers to Christ, and all of them together were beheaded with the sword.

Three things in that sentence are new to these folders and one is refused. The city is new: the Greek entry for the two hundred names Sinope and the entries for the deacon and the woman do not. The reason she was arrested is new, and it is the whole of her story — she was taken for looking after a prisoner. The wonders in the prison are new, and they are why the two hundred are counted with these two rather than beside them. **The year is recorded and not written onto two of the three folders**: `rufinus-the-deacon` holds «249–251» off the Greek page, which puts the three under Decius where this page puts them under Maximian, and the two cannot both be right; that widening is a reading this batch does not make, because it would mean a span of sixty years on a folder whose own Greek page states a reign. The two books are set side by side here and the question is left for the author.

The other two of the company as this calendar names them are [Rufinus](/saints/rufinus-the-deacon) and [the two hundred soldiers](/saints/two-hundred-martyrs-of-sinope); the line counts all three together and the life tells one story for the three.
