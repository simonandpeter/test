# Kyria of Caesarea

saint.gr's calendar for 6 Ιουνίου names five virgins on one line and on one page — Martha, Maria, Kyria (or Kyra), Vareria (or Valeria) and Markia — and says they came from Caesarea of Palestine. Having been taught the Christian faith by a devout Christian, they afterwards received holy baptism; the page gives no year for it and names no emperor.

From then on, the page says, they all sat together in one house and spent their lives in fasting, vigil and prayer, that the faith of the Christians might shine out over the whole world. But some betrayed them to the ruler of Caesarea, and because they would not consent to sacrifice to the idols they were tortured so hard that one after another they gave up their holy souls to Christ, the giver of the crown. The couplet the page prints sets them beside the parable: the Word of God spoke of five prudent maidens, foretelling these five virgins.

The other four of the five are [Martha](/saints/martha-of-caesarea), [Maria](/saints/maria-of-caesarea), [Marcia](/saints/marcia-of-caesarea) and [Valeria](/saints/valeria-martyr-6-june).

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 7 June, where both the other calendars keep the company on the 6th — and not on its own Julian 6 June, which prints three other entries and does not name her. Its heading names three of the five, her and [Valeria](/saints/valeria-martyr-6-june) and [Maria](/saints/maria-of-caesarea), and its account is the same story the Greek page tells in the same order: all three were of Caesarea in Palestine; when the persecution of the Christians came they withdrew out of the city into a hut; and there they prayed to God unceasingly, fasting and weeping, that the faith of Christ might spread over the whole world and that the persecution of the Church might cease.

Then, on somebody’s slander, they were brought to trial, tortured, and died of their torments in 304 — the only year any book read for this folder has given, and the folder now records it. So these glorious virgins were crowned with the martyr’s crown, the entry ends. Two of the five the Greek page names are not in this heading, and nothing here is written about them.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps the three of them together on 7 июня — «Мцц. Калерии (Валерии), Кириакии и Марии в Кесарии Палестинской (IV).» — and the reading behind that line tells the same story in four sentences: that they were women of Caesarea in Palestine in the persecution under Диоклетиан, who reigned from 284 to 305; that having been taught the Christian faith they left the pagan way of living, withdrew into a solitary place and passed their lives in prayer, asking the Lord that the persecutions should cease and the faith of Christ shine over all the world; and that the governor pressed them to bow to the idols, and they confessed Christ fearlessly, and were given over to torture, and died under it. This calendar's «(IV)» contains the year 304 this folder already records.
