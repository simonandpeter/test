# Domnina (12 October)

saint.gr's calendar for 12 October keeps «Αγία Δομνίνη», and gives her a short notice. It says she was counted worthy of a martyr's death under Diocletian, in 286, and that the eparch Lysias arrested her and threatened her savagely, on the idea that her woman's nature would not dare to expose itself to the tortures — the more so, the page adds, since she was also suffering from a long and heavy illness.

But he was very wide of the mark. Domnina showed the greatest and most heroic courage: she stayed steadfast in the confession of Christ and despised the scourges with which they beat her flesh hard and savagely. Afterwards they broke her bones and her joints, and so she gave up her holy soul to God.

That is the whole of the calendar's notice. It names no city and no country, gives her no rank and no family, and prints no hymn for her; beside the life it sets a two-line verse saying that Domnina, though her limbs were being pulled from their sockets, was not denying the true faith, and a note that the name Domna comes from the Latin dominus and means the lady, the mistress.

The Russian calendar keeps her on the same day and gives her the same year and the same magistrate, and is a little fuller about both ends of the story. She lived, it says, in the Cilician country, and she suffered for the confession of Christianity in 286; by order of the ruler Lysias she was beaten a long while and burned with fire, and then, torn all over, she was thrown into prison, and it was there that she died. Its index of names heads her «Домника (Домнина) Аназаровская (Киликийская)» — the only place either calendar names Anazarbus, and the only place the second form of her name is printed.
