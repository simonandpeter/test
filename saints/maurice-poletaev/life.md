# Maurice (Poletaev)

Maurice, baptised Michael, was born at Kronstadt in 1880 and studied at the St Petersburg theological schools and, it seems, the life says, at the academy as well. Ordained priest, he chose distant Turkestan, a decision that the family tradition says was not made without [John of Kronstadt](/saints/john-of-kronstadt), who blessed him for service in Central Asia and knew him personally, not only as a man of Kronstadt: the young priest’s wife was a niece of Father John’s wife. At the ancient city of Merv he was made rector of the church of the Protection of the Theotokos, built by the Russian soldiers serving in Turkestan at the very beginning of the century, and as a military priest he had the care of the large Russian garrison there.

After the revolution he was arrested again and again and forbidden to serve, and not wanting his wife and son to share his cup he left them and went on foot to Russia. At the Trinity-Sergius Lavra he took monastic vows with the name Maurice and in time became an archimandrite, and later he was sent to the parish of Yuryev-Polsky in the Vladimir diocese. Arrested there on 8 September 1928 on suspicion of counter-revolutionary activity, he was exiled to Oryol.

From 1930 he was rector of the church of All Saints at the Kukuyevo cemetery in Zagorsk, now Sergiev Posad, where the brethren of the closed Lavra were cared for, and [Basil (Kondratiev)](/saints/basil-kondratiev) was churchwarden of the church where he served. He was arrested on 21 October 1935 and held in the Butyrka prison in Moscow, sentenced by the NKVD’s special council on 8 January 1936 to three years of camps, and sent to the Karaganda camp, where a new case was opened against him in September 1937. On 28 September he was sentenced to be shot — and shot that day, the short life says. Where he was buried is not known.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 21 September old style with the martyr Basil, of 1937, and azbyka.ru keeps him also with the saints of Radonezh and of the Central Asian metropolitan district. It prints a troparion and a kontakion for the two in Church Slavonic, given here with the site’s English; the kontakion sings of Father John’s blessing, his care for soldiers, and his service in the church of All Saints under the shelter of Saint Sergius.
