# Andrew of Totma

saint.gr's calendar for 10 Οκτωβρίου names Andrew of Totma and gives him three lines. He was born, the page says, in 1638; he was a fool for Christ — «δια Χριστόν σαλός» — and he fell asleep in 1673. Then it says plainly «Δεν έχουμε άλλες λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Αγίου»: we have no further details of the life of the saint. The head of the page repeats the two years as «(1638 - 1673)».

So nothing is written here of where he was born, of the town of Totma beyond its name, of what his folly took the form of, or of how he died, because the source read for this folder says none of it. The page prints no hymn for him. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.

The Russian calendar keeps him on its own 10 October, «Блж. Андрея , Христа ради юродивого, Тотемского (1673)», and agrees with the Greek page on both of his years. Its life adds the places. He was born in 1638 in the village of Ust-Totemskoye and left the world while still a boy; by the blessing of Stephen, abbot of the Resurrection monastery at Galich, he took on the hard labour of folly for Christ's sake. He settled in the town of Totma on the bank of the river Sukhona, by the church of the Resurrection of Christ.

Winter and summer he went barefoot in torn clothes, eating only bread and water and so little of either as to keep himself barely from dying of hunger, and praying day and night. Whatever was given him he gave away to the poor. For those labours, the page says, he was counted worthy of the gift of wonderworking. One winter a blind man named Azhibokai came to him offering a large sum of money and asking to be healed, and the fool ran away; so Azhibokai washed his eyes with the snow the blessed one had been standing on, and saw at once.

The time of his death was revealed to him. He confessed, received the Holy Mysteries and went peacefully to God. Over his grave was raised the belfry of the church of the holy martyr [Andrew Stratelates](/saints/andrew-stratelates), whose name he bore and whose memory the page puts on 19 August, and many healings were witnessed at his coffin.
