# Dometius of Phrygia

saint.gr's calendar for 23 Μαρτίου keeps a martyr Dometius who came from the country of Phrygia and lived in the years of Julian the Apostate (361–363). Seeing that many were denying Christ and bowing down to the idols, the page says, he was troubled and grew indignant; and one day, while a horse-race was being held and sacrifice was being offered to the idols, he could bear it no longer.

Longing with a divine zeal to bear witness to the Name of God, he stood in the middle of the theatre and with a loud voice cursed the apostate Julian, spat upon all who worshipped the idols and mocked the false gods. For that he was arrested and suffered many tortures; and at the last, because he would not deny Christ, they cut off his holy head with the sword.

That is the whole of what the page prints. Its couplet turns on the executioner rather than on him — this sword-bearer, looking on at something dreadful, rages to bring the blade down on Dometius. It gives him no city in Phrygia, no year of his own, no governor by name and no relics, and prints no hymn; nothing has been taken from any other source and nothing supplied from memory.
