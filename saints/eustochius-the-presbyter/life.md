# Eustochius

saint.gr says that Eustochius the presbyter, Gaius — or Gaianus — his nephew, and Gaius's children Lollia — or Loulo — Proba and Urbanus — or Urbasius — came from the city of the Ousades, and that they lived in the days of the emperors Maximian and Agrippa, in the year 300. Eustochius was at first a priest of the idols; but when he saw the manly spirit of the Christians in the face of the torments, he came to Eudoxius the bishop of Antioch, was baptised a Christian and was ordained presbyter.

Afterwards he went to Lystra in Lycaonia, where he found his kinsman Gaius with his three children, Lollia, Proba and Urbanus, and baptised them Christians. When he was arrested by the pagans he confessed before the governor that he was a Christian, and they hung him upon a piece of wood and tore his flesh from him.

Then he was taken, with Gaius and Gaius's children, to Agrippinus — or, as others have it, Agrippa — the governor of Ancyra, where all of them refused to deny Christ. They were all tortured with frightful and barbarous torments, and because they stayed firm in their faith they were beheaded at the last, and so went up crowned to heaven. The page gives each of the five the year 300 and no year of birth, prints no hymn under their line, and carries a couplet for each of them out of the synaxaria.

The day's line names all five of them — Eustochius, [Gaius, nephew of Eustochius](/saints/gaius-nephew-of-eustochius), and his children [Lollia, daughter of Gaius](/saints/lollia-daughter-of-gaius), [Proba, daughter of Gaius](/saints/proba-daughter-of-gaius) and [Urbanus, son of Gaius](/saints/urbanus-son-of-gaius) — so the entry is one folder for each, and nothing in it is a heading over people it does not name.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the Julian 23 June, the same menologion day the Greek calendar keeps them on, under one heading that names two men and then says, and others with them. It begins further back than the Greek page does: Eustochius was a priest of the idols in the time of the emperor Maximian, and seeing the manly spirit of the Christian martyrs he threw off the pagan religion and was baptised — and it was the bishop of Antioch, Eudoxius, who baptised him himself. It says nothing of his being ordained presbyter, which the Greek account above gives him.

After that, this book says, he brought his kinsfolk over to Christianity little by little; and his kinsman [Gaius, nephew of Eustochius](/saints/gaius-nephew-of-eustochius) was baptised together with three children, [Proba, daughter of Gaius](/saints/proba-daughter-of-gaius), [Lollia, daughter of Gaius](/saints/lollia-daughter-of-gaius) and [Urbanus, son of Gaius](/saints/urbanus-son-of-gaius) — whom it calls a kinsman and not a nephew, and the three children it does not call his at all. All these, it ends, and still others besides them, were brought before the court, tortured and beheaded for the faith in Christ the Lord at Lystra, and their souls passed over into the immortal kingdom of Christ. Where the Greek account has the five taken on to the governor at Ancyra and beheaded there, this one has them die at Lystra, and names no governor. It gives no year.
