# Hellius of Egypt

Hellius was given to a monastery as a child, the reading says, and brought up in piety, abstinence and chastity; and when he was grown he withdrew into the Egyptian desert, where by unbroken labour he reached a high perfection in the spiritual life. He had the gift of discernment and knew the thoughts and the dispositions of the monks who spoke with him.

Great faith, simplicity of soul and deep humility let him command wild animals, and the reading gives two instances. Carrying a heavy load to a desert house and much wearied, he prayed, called a wild ass to him and laid the load on it; the ass took it obediently to the place and was let go back into the desert. Another time, needing to cross a river and having no boat, he called a crocodile out of the water and crossed safely standing on its back.

A young novice of a house he visited asked to be taken with him into the depth of the desert. Hellius warned him of the great labours and temptations that overtake every hermit, but the novice kept asking and he took him. On the first night the boy ran in terror to Hellius's cave, frightened by dreadful visions; Hellius comforted him and led him back, signed the cave with the cross and told him not to fear, for the terrors would not come again. The novice believed him and stayed, and afterwards reached such perfection that, like his teacher, he was granted food from an angel at the hour of need.

In deep old age Hellius passed over in peace to the heavenly mansions.
