# Theodora of Thessalonica

The Greek calendar’s 29 August refers to 5 April, where the main entry stands.

A model of pure and humble soul among the young women of Thessalonica, Theodora lived a holy life from childhood; the world and its pleasures did not interest her, and she belonged wholly to Christ. That longing brought her to the monastic life, where with prayer, vigil and study of the word of God she forged herself further; with the money from selling her handiwork she fed the hungry, and with the sisters she lived in peace, meekness and long-suffering. The abbess held her in such esteem that at her own death she asked to be buried beside her, and tradition says that when the nuns opened the grave they found Theodora’s body incorrupt.

The synaxarion then weighs another entry, quoted by Eustratiades from a Lavra codex, of a Theodora of the same city who had lived as a harlot, repented, gave her goods to the poor and spent five years in a deep pit in fasting and nakedness before an angel told her she was forgiven — perhaps another woman of the same name, it says; and it notes recent study identifying the Theodora of 5 April with Theodora the Myrrh-streaming of Aegina, kept on 3 or 29 August.

Doxologia's life for 5 aprilie, which is the day the Greek entry pointed to, fills in what came before the convent. She was born on the island of Aegina to right-believing Christian parents, Anthony and Chrysantha, whose own faith stood out at a time when the Church was troubled by the iconoclast heresy in the reign of Michael Balbus; the heresy did not touch that good pair, who shone in it like a light in darkness. Grown, Theodora was given in marriage and bore a daughter; then a barbarian invasion moved the household to Thessalonica, where she brought her daughter up and made her a bride of Christ. When her husband died she entered the convent of virgins where her daughter was, and was tonsured there.

The Romanian life tells the wonder at the grave in the same way and adds what followed. Some years after her death the abbess of that house died and was laid in a grave beside her; and when Theodora's grave was opened, everyone standing there saw her move, after so many years, as though she were alive, and make a little room for the abbess who was being laid down beside her — giving the wider place to her own guide, and showing her humility after death. They were all struck with awe and cried, Lord, have mercy. Fragrant and healing myrrh flowed from her relics, and many were healed by being anointed with it: demons were driven out, the blind saw, and every infirmity was cured.
