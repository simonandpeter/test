# Athanasius of Attaleia

saint.gr says Athanasius was born at Attaleia and lived at Smyrna, where he kept company with Ottomans who mocked him continually for his faith. Though he was unlettered, the page says, he answered their mockery so aptly that they were often left with nothing to say. One day he was heard saying in Turkish the words «Δόξα τῷ Θεῷ», glory to God — «Λάϊ λαλά», as the page gives the Turkish.

He was arrested by the same men on the charge that by saying it he had confessed faith in Mohammed, and so he was brought before the judge. Because he would not give way to the demand of the Islamic court that he become a Muslim, he was beheaded on 7 January 1700 at Smyrna, after days of dreadful tortures. Three days afterwards, with the judge's leave, Christians took up his holy relic and buried it with honours in the church of Saint Paraskeve.

The page's two-line verse turns on his name: this is not the Athanasius of old, it says, but a new one, and a martyr of the Lord. saint.gr counts him among the new martyrs who bore witness after the fall of Constantinople, and prints no hymn for him.
