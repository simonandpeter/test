# Nicodemus of Veroia

saint.gr's calendar for 24 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Νικόδημος ο Οσιομάρτυρας από την Βέροια». He came of a rich and lordly family of Veroia, the page says, lived in the years of the emperor Andronicus II Palaiologos, and practised his asceticism at the monastery of Philokalles in Thessalonica. He used to help prostitutes escape from their sin, and for that reason he was regularly to be found in the dens where they worked.

The monks of his own monastery did not understand what he was cultivating and treated him as a drunkard and a fornicator. The pimps of the women envied him for his spiritual work and knifed him; carried back to the monastery covered in blood, he was not let in, and he breathed his last before its gates. His body was thrown into a foul-smelling place.

Ten years later myrrh welled up from it, and then his holiness was recognised. When the disciple of Saint Gregory Palamas, the venerable Philotheos Kokkinos, was abbot of that monastery, he wrote his life. The successive destructions of those places saved neither his relic nor even the exact site of the monastery of Philokalles; and his memory, after his struggles were drawn back out of oblivion, was placed on 24 November by the metropolitan of Veroia, Pavlos Giannikopoulos, the anniversary of that bishop's own consecration.
