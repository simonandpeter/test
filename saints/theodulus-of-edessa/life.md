# Theodulus of Edessa

Theodulus was a patrician in the years of Theodosius the Great, which saint.gr dates 379 to 395, and he was a man famous for his good sense, his virtue and the modesty of his life. For that he was advanced by the emperor Theodosius to the dignity of eparch, which is the office his epithet remembers. But seeing the injustices of the powerful he was disgusted, and having resigned his office he lived as a private man.

Two years after his marriage his wife died, and he went to Edessa, having first divided his great property among the poor and among charitable foundations. There, when he was in the forty-second year of his age, he climbed up on a pillar and in the most adverse conditions kept a hard asceticism. So, with asceticism and prayer, having lived another forty years on the pillar, he died in peace.

The page gives no year for that death and no account of what he said or did on the pillar, and it prints no hymn for him. Its couplet is the one play it makes on the office he gave up: Theodulus prudently gave back an eparchy of earth for the eparchies of the heavens.
