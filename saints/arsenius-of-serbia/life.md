# Arsenius of Serbia

saint.gr's calendar for 28 Οκτωβρίου names «Άγιος Αρσένιος Αρχιεπίσκοπος Σερβίας», and the page behind that line says one thing only: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Αγίου» — we have no details of the saint's life. So the Greek calendar keeps an archbishop of Serbia on this day and tells nothing of him but his see and his rank, and nothing else on the page expands either.

There is no year on it, no birthplace, no account of his election, his rule or his death, and no hymn. The entry carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands, bare as it is. The Serbian calendar, which would be the one to say more, has not been read here, and its row on this folder stays undocumented.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the same day, names him by his numeral — «Свт. Арсения I, архиеп. Сербского (1266)» — and adds the year the Greek page did not. Its two short lives agree that he spent the greater part of his life as a monk in the monastery of Žiča, that he was consecrated archbishop of Serbia in 1233 for the strictness of his ascetic life, and that he died in peace in 1266 with his relics resting in the Peć house he founded. They do not agree how long he governed: one says thirty years of wise rule, the other thirty-three, and they also spell the monastery and his resting place differently. Nothing is chosen between them. His days there are two, this one and the Serbian 30 August, which is the synaxis of the Serbian saints.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 28 October and tells the whole of his career, which the Greek page this folder was written from does not. He was born in Srem, and calls him the great hierarch of the Serbian church and the successor of [Sava](/saints/sava-of-serbia). He was tonsured while still young and gave himself sincerely and wholeheartedly to the struggle for the salvation of his soul; then, hearing of Sava’s remarkable person and of what he was doing, he went to him at Žiča. Sava received him kindly and enrolled him in the brotherhood there, and seeing the rare virtues in him soon made him abbot of the house.

When the Hungarians attacked the Serbian lands, Sava sent him south to look for a place for the archbishopric more sheltered than Žiča. He chose Peć, and built there a monastery and a church to the holy apostles, which was afterwards called the church of the Lord’s Ascension. Before his second departure for Jerusalem Sava appointed him his own successor on the archiepiscopal throne; and when Sava died at Trnovo on the way home, it was Arsenius who pressed King [Vladislav](/saints/vladislav-of-serbia) to have the body brought back to the Serbian land.

He governed the church wisely for thirty years and reposed in the Lord on 28 October 1266 — the year this folder already recorded from the Russian calendar, here with the day of the month added. The entry ends at the altar wall of his church at Peć, where the inscription still stands, and quotes it in its own Church Slavonic spelling: O Lord our God, give heed, visit and bless this temple — and remember me also, the sinner Arsenius. He is buried there, in the church at Peć.
