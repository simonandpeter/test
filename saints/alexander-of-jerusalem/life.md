# Alexander of Jerusalem

Alexander lived at the end of the second century and the beginning of the third. doxologia's page calls him a hieromartyr, and says he was a disciple of the great teacher and writer of the Church, Clement of Alexandria, whom he names «my teacher and benefactor» in a letter to Origen quoted by Eusebius of Caesarea. He was first bishop of Flavias in Cappadocia, and was afterwards appointed co-administrator beside Narcissus, bishop of Jerusalem, who was then a hundred and sixteen years old.

Under the emperor Alexander Severus he was imprisoned for his faith. Released, he went to Jerusalem to venerate the holy places, and while he was there the old bishop Narcissus asked him to stay and help him govern the see — a thing extremely rare in the ancient Church, the page says, which was accepted and put into effect with the consent of all the bishops of Palestine. When Narcissus died in 212 Alexander succeeded him at once, and led the Church of Jerusalem for thirty-eight years.

In those years he founded the first public library in Jerusalem, stocked with works of Christian theology. He gave Origen leave to preach in church although Origen was then still a layman, and, having to justify the decision, said that such permissions had been given elsewhere and that Origen himself had been given it before, when he was very young; it is said that the two of them had studied together at the catechetical school in Alexandria. He ordained Origen priest in the year 230.

Old as he was, he was arrested between 249 and 251 with other bishops in the persecution under Decius, and taken to Cappadocia. Of his imprisonment it was said that «the glory of his grey hairs, together with his great holiness, wove him a double crown in captivity». His life mentions that he suffered many tortures and survived them; that when wild beasts were brought into the arena to devour him, some licked his feet and others lay down on the sand. Worn out by his sufferings, he died in prison in the year 251.

Eusebius of Caesarea knew several of the letters he sent to the Antinoites, a Christian people of Egypt, and quotes from one of them; another known letter is addressed to the Antiochenes, a third to Origen, and a fourth was written together with Theoctistus of Caesarea to Demetrius of Alexandria.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 12 December, and adds the see he held before Jerusalem and the library he built there. saint.gr has Alexander living from the end of the second century to the middle of the third, and makes him a pupil of Clement of Alexandria; he distinguished himself in his studies and was marked out in the preaching of the Gospel, and later was shown to be bishop of Flaviada in Cappadocia.

His work there made him brighter still; and because [Narcissus](/saints/narcissus-of-jerusalem), bishop of Jerusalem, was very old — a hundred and sixteen, the page says — he took Alexander as assistant bishop and at the same time as his successor.

When Narcissus had died a martyr's death, then, Alexander took the episcopal throne of Jerusalem. From that place he contended for the faith, enlightened and built up the people, and took care at the same time for the founding of a library at Jerusalem, in which he gathered many ecclesiastical and theological writings to help the studies of the younger clergy.

In 251, in the persecution of the Church under Decius, he was arrested by the governor of Palestine at Caesarea, who required the faithful bishop to deny Christ; and he not only refused but remarked with courage and force on the error of the idolaters. He was then given as food to the beasts, after having first undergone hard tortures; the lions came up to him without harming him at all, but he died of the wounds of the torments. His memory, the page adds, is repeated also on 16 May.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, agrees with the Romanian calendar on the year of his death and adds a year neither it nor the Greek page had. He was first bishop of Cappadocia; and in the persecution of Severus, in the year 203, he was thrown into prison and exiled. Afterwards he received the throne of Jerusalem. He founded the famous library of Jerusalem, which Eusebius used in writing his Church History.

In the days of Decius he was tortured in various ways and thrown before the beasts; and having come out alive and untouched by them, he was thrown into prison, where he ended his earthly life and went over to the Lord in 251.
