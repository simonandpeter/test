# Mardarius (Isaev)

**He is the second of the three the line puts together, and the only one of them who was shot.** days.pravoslavie.ru heads him on name page 9227 and carries no life; the life here is azbyka.ru’s, out of игумен Дамаскин (Орловский)’s «Жития новомучеников и исповедников Российских ХХ века. Март». He was born on 4 January 1886 in the village Савостино of the Старицкий уезд in the Tver country, into a peasant family, and was christened Михаил. He finished the village school and lived with his parents until he was twenty-four.

In 1921 he entered the Желтиков monastery at Tver. In 1922 he was cell-attendant to a priest who would afterwards be a bishop, served as psalm-reader in the cathedral at Рыбинск and as subdeacon to a bishop there, and was tonsured with the name Мардарий; in 1924 he was ordained hierodeacon and in 1925 hieromonk, and sent to the church of the village Федорицкое in the Мологский district. From 1929 he served at Деревеньки in the Угличский district. Arriving at the parish he looked for no comfort: he made himself a cell in a little bath-house on the edge of the village that was not meant to be lived in.

In the Yaroslavl diocese he came close to the archbishop of Углич, Серафим (Самойлович), who had in effect governed that diocese from 1924 to 1926 while its metropolitan was under arrest; in him, says the life, a peasant’s son who had chosen the service of God and of His Church found an ascetic of his own mind.

A priest whom the secret police later called in to describe him said what kind of monk he was: «монаха-аскета, строго исполняющего предписания монашеской жизни, преданного церковности, весьма аккуратного и истово совершающего богослужения», and his services drew crowds. He preached no sermons, the same account says, but gave his direction at confession, and his confessions were so long that no other priest round him came near it — «В этой исповеди причина его популярности». Many priests were already under arrest, and he helped them with money through their relatives; his own parish was large enough that he wanted for nothing and gave away freely. The believers loved him for a meek and humble temper.

When the Углич vicariate was put under another bishop after the archbishop’s arrest in 1928, he would not accept the appointment, holding it made to please the godless persecutors, and went on commemorating the imprisoned archbishop as his own. Word of it travelled, and people began coming to Деревеньки from fifty kilometres off, from the towns as well as the villages, so that a village church with almost daily services kept in the monastic manner was always full. Seeing the crowds, he began to expect arrest, and kept himself careful and quiet so as to give nobody a handle.

He was arrested in February 1933. At the interrogations he said that many came to him from the villages and from the towns; that he had talked with peasants but never against the collective farms; that his confessions ran five or ten minutes and were heard before the service, and that in them he called people, as a priest, to faith in God and a life by the commandments. He confirmed that he was indeed of the imprisoned archbishop’s mind. On 11 June 1933 he was taken to the prison at Углич; the believers went after him and petitioned for his release, and on 13 July the officer agreed to free him on his written undertaking to leave the district. He went to Рыбинск.

In the summer of 1935 he was appointed to the church of the village Юрьевское in the Мышкинский district, and there began the full monastic services again — which, in the hopelessness of the life about them, the life says, stayed the one food that kept human souls up. It could not last. On 9 January 1937 the NKVD arrested him and shut him in the Углич prison, and the interrogations ran for months. «Я это категорически отрицаю», he answered to the charge of anti-Soviet agitation, and to the charge of keeping up connections with churchmen outside his parish he answered that he lived a closed life, went nowhere and knew nobody, and met the believers only in church and only as a priest must.

The last interrogation was on 28 April. He would not admit to wrecking the collective farm’s work by his services: the believers came of their own will, and he had urged nobody to leave work for church, and neither the farm nor the soviet had complained of it at the time. He denied driving an official and a farm chairman out of the church enclosure, and told what had happened instead — two men he did not know had stood a long while at the sanctuary window during the service, distracting him, and he sent the psalm-reader to ask them to move away. In June the investigation closed, and on 9 July he was given the indictment to read: he had carried on anti-Soviet agitation and spread church literature of anti-Soviet content.

On 10 December 1937 the Special Collegium of the regional court heard him. He said he understood perfectly what he was charged with and did not admit it: he had spread no literature and agitated against nothing. Several witnesses had not come, and the court put the hearing off to a next sitting; by then he had been nearly a year under investigation in the Углич prison. In the second half of 1937 the extrajudicial troikas were set up, and his case was handed to one. On 17 March 1938 a troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot. He was shot the next day, 18 March 1938, and buried in a common unmarked grave.
