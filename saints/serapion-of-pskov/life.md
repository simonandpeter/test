# Serapion of Pskov

Serapion was born at Yuriev — Tartu — when it lay under German rule and
Orthodoxy was being pressed out of it; his parents kept to the Russian church
of St Nicholas there, and he knew Scripture well enough to stand up for the
faith more than once. When they tried to convert him by force he left for the
wilderness of Tolva near Pskov, where Euphrosynus, kept on 15 May, had begun
his life of prayer, and put himself under him. He was tempted early: wanting
the solitary life at once, he went off without a blessing, hurt his leg
badly, and came back repentant. He took the great schema and lived
fifty-five years beside Euphrosynus under a vow of silence, a model to the
brethren who gathered there, for whom the elder built a church of the Three
Hierarchs and wrote a skete rule. He kept the vow of poverty so strictly
that the copyist of his life called him an unburied corpse; he bore every
insult by blaming himself alone and asking the insulter’s pardon; and he
held that the twelve psalms sung alone in a cell do not equal one “Lord,
have mercy” sung in church. He died on 8 September 1480, the Nativity of the
Mother of God, and is kept the day before so as not to fall on the feast.

The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 7 September and again on 15 May, and says
it has no information about his life; the Russian calendar keeps him the
same day of its own reckoning.

entry is the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 7 Σεπτεμβρίου —
[the entry](https://www.saint.gr/1607/saint.aspx); read 5 September 2026.*
