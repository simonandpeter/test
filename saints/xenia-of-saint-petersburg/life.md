# Xenia of Saint Petersburg

Xenia Grigorievna Petrova was married to a colonel of the imperial army who was also a cantor at court, and lived the full and worldly life of the capital's aristocracy. Her husband's unexpected death, when she was only twenty-six, threw her into great distress and made her see how empty every earthly tie is; and from then she decided to free herself of everything that bound her to the earth and to go towards the kingdom of heaven by the narrowest and hardest road, the folly for Christ.

Her whole manner of life changed. Her relations, seeing her give away her goods in alms, thought that mourning had taken her wits. She put on her husband's military coat and would answer to nothing but the dead man's name. Without a roof, barefoot, dressed summer and winter in the same patched rags, she walked the streets of the poor quarters and let the boys mock and laugh at her meekly, after the pattern of Christ in his Passion. What alms were given her out of pity she gave away on the spot to the poor; she ate rarely, when she visited some family she knew, and she spent her nights in a field outside the city, on her knees in prayer until the sun rose.

The devout people of the city began to see, little by little, that this strange conduct hid a holy life; that her riddling or figurative words were not without wisdom, and often held the foretelling of things to come. God's blessing seemed to go with her wherever she went: when she came into a shop, the day's takings visibly grew; when a driver took her into his cab, he found many fares; when she kissed a sick child, the child recovered at once. The pity shown her turned before long into honour throughout the whole city, whose true guardian angel she became.

Having carried the cross of folly willingly for the love of Christ for forty-five years, she fell asleep in the Lord at the age of seventy-one, between 1794 and 1806. Her grave was honoured more and more from the first and became a true place of pilgrimage; the page says that for two centuries the wonders, healings, prophecies and appearances there have not stopped, that people crowd to it for the strong help of her prayers, counting her the defender of the city, and that each one takes away a little earth from the grave and oil from the lamp that burns over it without ceasing. doxologia.ro prints the life over the name of Hieromonk Makarios of Simonos Petra, from the fifth volume of his Synaxarion in Romanian.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and heads her page «(1730 - ;)». saint.gr fills out what the Romanian page left bare: it names her husband Andrew Feodorovich Petrov, a colonel and first cantor at the imperial court, who fell dead at a ball while drinking with his friends when she was twenty-six and without having confessed or communed, which is what she feared for him ever afterwards; it says she then vanished from Saint Petersburg for eight years, believed spent under elders in the school of Paisius Velichkovsky's disciples; and that when her relatives took her to court over the property she was giving away, the judge found her sound in mind. Her relics lie in her tomb in the Smolensk cemetery, and she was glorified in 1978 and again by the Patriarchate of Moscow in 1988.
