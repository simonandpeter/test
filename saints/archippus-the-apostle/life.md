# Archippus

Archippus, one of the seventy apostles, was bishop at Colossae, a city of Phrygia, after Epaphras, on the testimony of Saint Ambrose; and this is why the Apostle Paul calls him his fellow soldier in the letter to Philemon. Philemon was a citizen of note in Colossae, and Apphia, John Chrysostom says, was his wife. Believing in Christ, Philemon made his house a church, for all the faithful of Colossae gathered at his house and the divine services were done there as in a church.

Philemon was afterwards a bishop in the apostolate. In the days of the apostles, the life explains, some bishops had a see and others had none but went about through cities and countries; these last were called apostolic bishops, since they were sent out on the apostolate to preach. Philemon was set in such a bishopric and was numbered among the seventy apostles, going through the cities of Phrygia and others preaching the word of God; and it is written of him that he was bishop in Gaza as well.

Apphia ordered the church at Colossae, serving God day and night in fasting and prayer, giving rest to the saints who laboured at the good news of Christ, and feeding the poor, the ruined and the stranger; so that her house was not only a church but a place for strangers, a hospital and a shelter for everyone who had nowhere to lay his head.

Once, while the feast of the unclean Artemis was being kept at Colossae, Archippus with those who were there and with Philemon gathered all the faithful into the house Apphia ordered, and they were offering their accustomed prayers to God and doing the holy service. The idolaters, who hated the faithful, knew that all the Christians were gathered in Philemon's house, fell on them without warning and drove out the flock of Christ, beating some and killing others; and Archippus, Philemon and Apphia they seized and brought to Artocles, the chief man of the city of Ephesus, who ordered them all tortured.

First they were stretched on the ground and dragged, and beaten with staves without mercy; then each was buried apart in the earth up to the hips and stoned. Philemon and Apphia they killed with the stones; but Archippus, after the beating, they left alive for the children to mock. The children gathered and pierced the saint with knives. So the apostolic three went up to heaven, the life says, before the throne of the Holy, life-giving and undivided Trinity. Doxologia gives no year for any of them.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and tells both his calling and his death. He came, saint.gr says, from Colossae, where the Apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) came to know him and brought him to the faith of Christ; and his zeal and his whole readiness and piety quickly showed the disciple Archippus to be a fellow-soldier of his glorious teacher, which is the very word Paul uses of him in his letter to [Philemon](/saints/philemon-the-apostle) — and he names Archippus in the letter to the Colossians too. In the carrying out of his divine apostolate he was seized by order of the eparch Androcles; and having refused to sacrifice to the idols he was stripped and buried to the waist in a pit. Because he went on confessing Christ with a loud voice, they pricked him with needles, front and back, in the neck, the armpits, the sides, the eyes, the ears, the mouth and the head; and still he persisted in confessing Christ, Jesus being his prayer and his hymn. Then a thick and heavy stoning made an end of his life, and to the crown of the apostle was added the crown of the martyr. Its couplets keep to the stones: longing for the cornerstone, Archippus was threshed with stones by his longing for it; and on the nineteenth Archippus died by hurled stones. Its oikos names [Apphia](/saints/apphia-wife-of-philemon) beside him, as one who knew him well for a far-shining torch lit from the first light.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, in one entry with his two companions. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović has him one of the Seventy, whom the apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) mentions in the letters to the Colossians and to Philemon and calls his fellow soldier. At Colossae, it says, the centre of Christianity was in the house of [Philemon](/saints/philemon-the-apostle), where the Christians gathered for prayer and which Paul called, writing to him, your house church.

At the time of some feast of the pagan goddess Artemis all the faithful at Colossae were gathered in that house at prayer as usual. The pagans learned of the meeting, rushed in and seized every Christian there, and took the three of them — Philemon, Archippus and [Apphia](/saints/apphia-wife-of-philemon) — as the leaders: first to the rod, and then buried to the waist in the earth and beaten with stones. Philemon and Apphia they killed that way. Archippus they pulled out of the hole barely alive and left to the children for sport, and the children stabbed him all over with knives; and so, the Prologue says, Paul's fellow soldier too finished the course of his earthly way well.

**The Russian calendar keeps him twice, and says so in its own words.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar prints the three of them on 19 февраля ст. ст. and again on 22 ноября ст. ст. — «Апп. от 70-ти Филимона и Архиппа и мц. равноап. Апфии (I).» — and the second of the two lives it hangs behind their names opens by calling the November day «вторая память 22 ноября», the second commemoration. This folder carries a row for each of the two days.

The two Russian lives agree that he was cut to pieces with knives and disagree about where. The first has the enraged crowd beat him and cut him during a pagan festival, when it burst into the Christian church in the middle of the service, so that he died on the road to the judge before any sentence was passed. The second has all three handed over to the judgement of the city governor «Артоклиса» for preaching faith in Christ, and him savagely cut with knives there. The second page also makes him bishop of Colossae, where the first gives him no see at all and only has him living in the city; and it is that page which calls the November day the second commemoration. Both set the death under Nero, 54–68.
