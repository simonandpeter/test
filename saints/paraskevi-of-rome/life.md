# Paraskevi of Rome

Paraskevi, the venerable martyr of Christ, was born in a village within the borders of old Rome, of Christian parents named Agathon and Politeia. They kept the Lord's commandments without slackening, but had no children, and prayed God earnestly to give them some; and God, who does the will of those who fear Him, heard them and gave them this daughter. She was born on the sixth day of the week, and they named her at holy baptism Paraskevi, after the name of the day on which she was born, for the sixth day is called in Greek paraschevi, that is, Friday.

Weaned while she was still small, she gave herself to God, and her mother taught her all the mysteries of the Christian faith. She was always and unceasingly at prayer in the church, and learning the holy Scriptures she read the sacred books continually. When her parents died she shared out among the poor all the goods that were left to her, and then, being tonsured and clothed in the monastic habit, she went out preaching the name of the true God and of our Lord Jesus Christ, and turned many of the Greeks to the knowledge of God.

In those times Antoninus reigned at Rome, and certain Jews accused her to him, saying that a woman named Paraskevi was preaching Jesus, the son of Mary, whom their fathers had nailed to the cross. The emperor had her brought before him, and when he saw her he wondered at her understanding and her beauty, and said that if she would submit to him and sacrifice to the gods she would be made heir to many gifts, and if not he would give her over to cruel torments; and she answered that it should not be hers to deny her God. What was done to her under him is passed over here; the page's account of it is longer than this.

Afterwards, faced with a dragon, she breathed upon it and made the sign of the Cross over it, and the beast hissed loudly and split in two and was seen no more; and the governor and all who were with him, seeing it, believed in God. She went back into the city and preached everywhere, and turned many to the true knowledge of God.

Then she came into another city, where the governor was a man named Tarasius, who, hearing of her, had her brought before his court. Questioned about the faith, she said that she was a Christian and confessed Christ the true God; and for that a brazen cauldron was set out, full of oil, pitch and lead, and a fire was made under it, and while it was boiling hard the governor ordered her to be thrown in. But the fire was put out by the coming of an angel of God, the cauldron cooled, and the saint was unharmed. The tyrant brought many and various torments against her and could not shake her strong faith; and at the last he cut off her head with the sword, and her soul went to the eternal dwellings.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 26 July, and calls her venerable-martyr as the Romanian line does. saint.gr says Paraskevi was born at Rome in the years of the emperor Antoninus, whose reign it dates from 138 to 160, the daughter of the devout Christians Agathon and Politeia, who took care of her Christian upbringing as they had promised God if he gave them a child; and because the child was born on a Friday she was given that name. After her parents died she shared out her whole property among the poor and took up missionary work at Rome and the country round it, preaching the word of Christ. Her activity provoked the idolatrous emperor Antoninus, who arrested her and promised her material goods if she would sacrifice to the idols.

Seeing her stay firm in her faith, he put her to the torture of the red-hot helmet, which she endured with patience, and then ordered her set in a cauldron of boiling oil and pitch. When he saw the saint unharmed and could not explain how she had stayed untouched, he brought his own face close to the cauldron to test whether it was truly hot, and was blinded on the spot. The saint by her prayer gave Antoninus back his sight, and the result was that he believed in Christ, or by others that he stopped the persecutions against the Christians; at any rate he set Paraskevi free, and she went on preaching the Gospel in other places until she reached Greece. At Tempe an idolatrous ruler put her to horrible tortures, which she bore with patience, and she was perfected by death through beheading. The Greek page adds that she is the patron of Komotini, of Chalkida and of Lavrio.
