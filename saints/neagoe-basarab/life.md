# Neagoe Basarab

Born probably in 1481 or 1482, of the Basarabs on his father's side and the Craiovești on his mother's, Neagoe had for his spiritual school the monastery of Bistrița in Oltenia, his great-grandparents' foundation - and for his teacher, by God's providence, no less than Niphon, the former Ecumenical Patriarch, then withdrawn there. From Niphon he learned the mystery of hesychast prayer, watchfulness of soul, and the learning of Christ; and when he came to the throne of Wallachia in 1512 he ruled as he had learned - encouraging trade and the crafts, keeping peace where he could, attempting even to mediate between Orthodox and Catholic Christendom and to build, in Stephen the Great's line, a coalition of the eastern lands against Ottoman expansion, though Wallachia remained a vassal through his reign.

In 1517 he sought and obtained from the Ecumenical Patriarchate the canonization of his own teacher Niphon, and on the reliquary at Dionysiou the Romanian ruler is painted bowing to his saint. He gave generously to the Orthodox monasteries of Wallachia and all the Balkan lands, and in his reign the monastery of Curtea de Argeș was built, where his own relics now rest. To his son he left the Teachings of Neagoe Basarab to his son Theodosie - the first Romanian creation of universal standing, as the Synod's own tomos calls it, a testament spiritual, pedagogical and encyclopedic at once. The Romanian Church canonized him in 2008.
