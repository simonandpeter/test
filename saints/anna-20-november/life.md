# Anna (20 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 20 Νοεμβρίου prints ten names in one line: «Αγίες παρθένες Θέκλα, Βαουθά, Δενάχις, Τεντούς, Μάμα, Μαλοχία, Άννα, Νάνα, Άστη και Μαλάχ». They were ascetic Christian women of Persia, the page says. The king of the Persians ordered them to worship the fire, and because they would not accept it they were all cut to pieces by the swords of the soldiers.

The page spells the third of them «Δενάχις (ή Δινάχ)», and its couplet names only the first three — Thecla, Bauthas, Denachis, virgins; the contest of the sword showed them virgin athletes. Its heading blocks gloss two of the ten names in the way that site glosses any name, by listing famous bearers: under ΘΕΚΛΑ it prints «Κόρη του Αυτοκράτορα Θεόφιλου», the daughter of the emperor Theophilos, and under ΑΝΝΑ the Hebrew Hannah. Neither gloss is a statement about the woman in this line, and nothing of either is recorded here.

The page gives them no city, no year, no king by name and no hymn, and prints no relics for them.

This folder is Anna, named seventh in the line, beside [Thecla](/saints/thecla-20-november), [Bauthas](/saints/bauthas), [Denachis](/saints/denachis), [Tentous](/saints/tentous), [Mama](/saints/mama), [Malochia](/saints/malochia), [Nana](/saints/nana-20-november), [Aste](/saints/aste), [Malach](/saints/malach).

**The Russian calendar keeps the whole Persian company on one line and one life page.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar prints for the Julian 20 November a single clause, «Сщмчч. Нирсы еп. и Иосифа, ученика его, Иоанна, Саверия, Исакия и Ипатия, епископов Персидских; мч. Азата скопца, Сасония, Феклы, Анны и иных многих мужей и жен, в Персиде пострадавших (343).», and behind every name in it the same life page stands, life2574, titled for all of them together. saint.gr prints the same day as four separate groups with four separate lives; the Russian page is the first source read here that puts them in one company. Its whole account of them is that they «были казнены во время гонения на христиан в Персии при царе Сапоре II» — that they were put to death during the persecution of Christians in Persia under king Shapur II — and then one short sentence apiece on the manner of each death.

She closes the life’s last sentence with Сасоний and Фекла: «Пытки, мучения и смерть за Христа претерпели также мученики Сасоний, Фекла, Анна и многие другие мужчины и женщины (+ 343)». The clause gives her no rank of her own beyond the «мч.» that governs the second half of it; «мц.» and the virginity are the name page’s, and both were already here from the Greek.
