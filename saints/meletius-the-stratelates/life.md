# Meletius the Stratelates

saint.gr says they were martyred in the years of the emperor Elagabalus, who reigned from 218 to 222. Meletius, the leader, as the page puts it, of this whole line of martyrs, was serving as a soldier together with two other saints, [Stephen](/saints/stephen-companion-of-meletius) and [John](/saints/john-companion-of-meletius). They were denounced for believing in Christ and ordered to deny him; and because they did not do it they were put to dreadful torments. In the end Meletius they hanged, and Stephen and John they beheaded.

But [Serapion the Egyptian](/saints/serapion-martyr-24-may) the idolater, when he saw the holy Meletius break the idols of the temple by his prayer, believed; and [Callinicus](/saints/callinicus-the-magus) the magus, when he too saw that Meletius had suffered nothing from the deadly poison they had given him to drink, believed as well. Both of them, having confessed Christ steadfastly, were beheaded.

At the sight of their martyrdom others believed and gave their lives for Christ: twelve komites and tribunes, whom the day's line names as [Festus](/saints/festus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Faustus](/saints/faustus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Marcellus](/saints/marcellus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Meletius](/saints/meletius-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Sergius](/saints/sergius-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Marcellinus](/saints/marcellinus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Felix](/saints/felix-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Photinus](/saints/photinus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Theodoriscus](/saints/theodoriscus-of-the-twelve-tribunes), [Mercurius](/saints/mercurius-of-the-twelve-tribunes) and [Didymus](/saints/didymus-of-the-twelve-tribunes); three women, [Marciane](/saints/marciana-martyr-24-may), [Palladia](/saints/palladia-companion-of-meletius) and [Susanna](/saints/susanna-companion-of-meletius); and two infants, [Cyriacus](/saints/cyriacus-the-infant) and [Christian](/saints/christian-the-infant). After them followed a multitude of martyrs whose number, the page says, reaches eleven thousand two hundred and eight. Not one of that multitude is named, so not one of them is a folder here.

The manner of each death comes from the couplets the synaxarion sets over them, which the page prints one by one. Of Meletius: they call the pine bitter, but to him who was hung upon it it was a kind of Hymettian honey. Of Callinicus: beheaded, you exchange the magus for a contender, and you were not called Fair-Victor for nothing. Of Stephen and John: with John, Stephen bows his head to the sword for longing after the unfading crowns. Of Serapion: the martyr, not sacrificing to carved things, has his neck cut through by a worshipper of carved things. Of the twelve tribunes: perishable were the ranks of tribune and count, and out of the fire they found the rank that does not perish. Of the two infants: the sword took away Cyriacus and Christian, Christian servants of the Lord. Of the three women: Marciane, Susanna and Palladia are killed with wood for not sacrificing to Pallas.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day, counts his company and dates it a century earlier. He was accused of the destruction of a heathen temple, it says, in the time of the emperor Antoninus; and being nailed to a tree, Meletius gave up his holy soul. With him suffered also many soldiers who had been under his command and would not deny Christ their Lord. The heading numbers them at one thousand two hundred and eighteen soldiers with their wives and children, and names none of them.

All of them suffered honourably, the Prologue says, in the second century, and passed over into the kingdom of Christ God. The Greek page puts their suffering between the years 218 and 222, which is a hundred years later, so the two books are left to disagree and the folder now holds the wider reading rather than either narrower one.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 24 мая ст. ст., at the head of a company it numbers at 1218 besides those it names. In the reign of the Roman emperor Antoninus Heliogabalus, which it dates 218 to 222, he was the military commander of the province of Galatia. He was a Christian and prayed earnestly that the Lord would put an end to the pagan error; and the demons that lived in the pagan shrines, terrified by his prayer, went into dogs, which by their howling began to put the inhabitants of the province in fear. With his soldiers he destroyed the maddened dogs and pulled down the shrines, and was seized and brought for judgement before the governor Максимиан. For refusing to sacrifice to the idols he was put to the torture, and died without ceasing to confess his faith in Christ.

The tribunes of his regiment, Стефан and Иоанн, were beheaded for confessing Christ the true God. The rest of the soldiers of the regiment, who also declared themselves Christians, were put to the sword with their wives and children; 1218 people perished in the torments, and by some sources, the calendar says, eleven thousand. Феодор and Фавст, with many others, were burned. Of the women and children who suffered, the calendar names Маркиана, Сосанна, Палладия and the infants Кириак and Христиан; and of the soldiers it names the twelve tribunes, Фавст, Фист, Маркелл, Феодор, Мелетий, Сергий, Маркеллин, Филикс, Фотин, Феодориск, Меркурий and Дидим. One Мелетий and one Фавст and one Феодор stand both in the line’s own list of seven and in that list of twelve, and the calendar does not say whether it means the same men twice.
