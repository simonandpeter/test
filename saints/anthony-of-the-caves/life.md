# Anthony of the Kyiv Caves

Anthony of the Kyiv Caves was born in 983 at Lyubech near Chernihiv. Having
the fear of God from his youth he wished to put on the monastic habit, and
when he came of age he went wandering, and reaching Athos burned with the
desire to imitate the labours of its holy dwellers; tonsured there, the
young monk pleased God in everything, and growing on the way of virtue
excelled above all in submission and obedience, so that all the monks
rejoiced to see his holy life. Foreseeing in him a great ascetic to come,
the igumen, at God’s prompting, sent him home: Anthony, it is time for you
to guide others too in the holy life — return to your Russian land, and the
blessing of the Holy Mountain be on you; from you a multitude of monks will
come. Back in Rus’ he went round the monasteries of the Kyiv country and
found nowhere the strict life he had known on Athos. By God’s providence, on
one of the Kyiv hills on the steep bank of the Dnieper, which reminded him
of his beloved Athos, in the forest near the village of Berestovo, he found
a cave dug by the priest Hilarion, later metropolitan of Kyiv, and there he
laboured in prayer, fasting, vigil and work, eating a little every other
day and sometimes not for a week. People began to come to him for a
blessing and counsel, and some to stay with him for good; among the first
was Nikon, who in 1032 tonsured the newcomer Theodosius. His holy life lit
the whole Russian land with the beauty of the monastic struggle; he
received those who sought monasticism with love, taught them how to follow
Christ, and had Nikon tonsure them, and when twelve brethren had gathered
they dug a great cave together with a church and cells in it. Then he set
Barlaam over them as igumen and withdrew, dug himself a new cave and shut
himself in it — and monks soon settled round that too, and so the Near and
the Far cave monasteries were born, and over the Far cave the monks built a
small wooden church of the Dormition. When Prince Izyaslav took Barlaam away
to the St Demetrius monastery, the meek and humble
[Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) was chosen igumen with
Anthony’s blessing and the brethren’s consent — a hundred of them by then —
and Izyaslav gave the monks the hill, on which a great church and cells were
built with a palisade round them: the glorious monastery called Pechersky,
of the Caves, because it was founded over them. Many monasteries, the
chronicler remarks, are built by the wealth of tsars and boyars, but none
can compare with those built by the prayers of the saints, their tears,
their fasting and their vigils; Anthony had no gold, and by his labours
raised a house like no other, the first spiritual centre of Rus’. For his
holy life God gave him the gifts of foresight and wonders, shown above all
in the building of the great church of the Caves: the Mother of God herself
appeared to him and to Theodosius in the church at Blachernae, to which
they were carried in rapture without leaving the monastery, and gave them
gold, with which they hired the master masons whom the Queen of Heaven sent
into the Russian land to build her church at the Caves; and at that
appearance she foretold Anthony’s death, which came in his ninetieth year,
on 7 May 1073. His relics, by God’s dispensation, remain hidden. The
Russian calendar keeps him on 2 September (Julian) with Theodosius, the
two founders of monasticism in the Russian land.
