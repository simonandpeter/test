# Anthony of the Kyiv Caves

Anthony of the Kyiv Caves was born in 983 at Lyubech near Chernihiv. Having
the fear of God from his youth he wished to put on the monastic habit, and
when he came of age he went wandering, and reaching Athos burned with the
desire to imitate the labours of its holy dwellers; tonsured there, the
young monk pleased God in everything, and growing on the way of virtue
excelled above all in submission and obedience, so that all the monks
rejoiced to see his holy life.

Foreseeing in him a great ascetic to come, the igumen, at God’s prompting,
sent him home: Anthony, it is time for you to guide others too in the holy
life — return to your Russian land, and the blessing of the Holy Mountain be
on you; from you a multitude of monks will come. Back in Rus’ he went round
the monasteries of the Kyiv country and found nowhere the strict life he had
known on Athos.

By God’s providence, on one of the Kyiv hills on the steep bank of the
Dnieper, which reminded him of his beloved Athos, in the forest near the
village of Berestovo, he found a cave dug by the priest Hilarion, later
metropolitan of Kyiv, and there he laboured in prayer, fasting, vigil and
work, eating a little every other day and sometimes not for a week. People
began to come to him for a blessing and counsel, and some to stay with him
for good; among the first was Nikon, who in 1032 tonsured the newcomer
Theodosius.

His holy life lit the whole Russian land with the beauty of the monastic
struggle; he received those who sought monasticism with love, taught them
how to follow Christ, and had Nikon tonsure them, and when twelve brethren
had gathered they dug a great cave together with a church and cells in it.
Then he set Barlaam over them as igumen and withdrew, dug himself a new cave
and shut himself in it — and monks soon settled round that too, and so the
Near and the Far cave monasteries were born, and over the Far cave the monks
built a small wooden church of the Dormition.

When Prince Izyaslav took Barlaam away to the St Demetrius monastery, the
meek and humble [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) was
chosen igumen with Anthony’s blessing and the brethren’s consent — a hundred
of them by then — and Izyaslav gave the monks the hill, on which a great
church and cells were built with a palisade round them: the glorious
monastery called Pechersky, of the Caves, because it was founded over them.

Many monasteries, the chronicler remarks, are built by the wealth of tsars
and boyars, but none can compare with those built by the prayers of the
saints, their tears, their fasting and their vigils; Anthony had no gold,
and by his labours raised a house like no other, the first spiritual centre
of Rus’. For his holy life God gave him the gifts of foresight and wonders,
shown above all in the building of the great church of the Caves: the Mother
of God herself appeared to him and to Theodosius in the church at
Blachernae, to which they were carried in rapture without leaving the
monastery, and gave them gold, with which they hired the master masons whom
the Queen of Heaven sent into the Russian land to build her church at the
Caves; and at that appearance she foretold Anthony’s death, which came in
his ninetieth year, on 7 May 1073. His relics, by God’s dispensation, remain
hidden. The Russian calendar keeps him on 2 September (Julian) with
Theodosius, the two founders of monasticism in the Russian land.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 10 iulie and prints there a short life taken from an Athonite book, Moses the Athonite's The Saints of the Holy Mountain. It calls him the founder and father of monasticism in Russia, and has his abbot Theoctistus, who tonsured him, send him away by a divine revelation: it is God's will, my son, that you go to Rus to carry the blessing of Mount Athos. He went, was driven back to Athos by men's malice, and stilled himself in a cave near Esphigmenou which is kept to this day with a church in his honour; then his elder sent him a second time, saying that the blessing of the Holy Mountain would be upon him there and that many monks would come out of him.

Finding in no monastery of that country the hard struggle of the Athonites, the page says, he wandered until he ended in a cave near Kyiv and prayed, Lord, establish me in this place, that the blessing of the Holy Mountain and of the abbot who tonsured me may remain upon it. Crowds came for his blessing; some stayed as monks, Varlaam was chosen abbot and Anthony withdrew out of sight; and when the brethren had multiplied under Theodosius and asked his blessing to found a monastery he gave it gladly, and the house dedicated to the Mother of God became the mother of Russian monasticism after the Athonite rule.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 July, and names him by the monastery of his tonsure: «Όσιος Αντώνιος ο Ρώσος ο Εσφιγμενίτης», Anthony the Russian, the Esphigmenite. saint.gr says he was born in 983 and lived in the days of the emperor Romanos Argyropoulos and of Vladimir the pious ruler of Russia; that in 1012 he came to the Holy Mountain, where he was tonsured a monk in the monastery of Esphigmenou. He then returned to Russia and became the father of the monastic order of the Russians; but because Svyatopolk declared a persecution against the monks, he went back to the Holy Mountain, to his elder Theoktistos. When order returned to Russia under Yaroslav he came back, made many monks, and built the wonderful Pechersk monastery, where he fell asleep in peace at the age of ninety. The page adds that his relics lie buried in the Lavra of the Caves at Kyiv.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same 10 July. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads him with no rank word at all — the name and the by-name and nothing else — and calls him the founder and father of monasticism in Russia. It says he was born in the small place of Lyubech near Chernihiv, left his homeland early and went to the Holy Mountain, where he was tonsured and struggled in the monastery of Esfigmenou; and that it was according to a heavenly revelation that the abbot there sent him back to Russia, to make monks of its people.

He chose a cave by Kyiv. When those who wanted the monastic life gathered round him he set [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) over them as abbot and himself stayed in the cave as a keeper of silence, and by God’s blessing the monastery grew and became the mother of Russian monasticism. The Prologue says he suffered much malice from men and from demons and overcame it all by his meekness, and that he had a great gift of seeing into things and of healing the sick. It puts his repose in 1073, in the ninetieth year of his life, and says he left his spiritual nursery to bear good fruit for the Orthodox people of Russia through the centuries.
