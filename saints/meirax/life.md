# Meirax

saint.gr's calendar for 11 December keeps the holy Meirax, and says that his life is like that of the prodigal of the Gospel. He was born at Tenese in Egypt, the page says, and brought up by his parents in Christian piety; but the allurements of youth drew him into prodigality, and worse still, his acquaintance with the Agarenes made him change his faith. His parents learned of it and wept bitterly, but they did not despair and set their hope on God in prayer; and God showed his mercy for the sake of the devout parents.

The son who had denied his religion grew sick of the life of sin; and as the phoenix comes forward out of the flames, so his old faith came forward out of the ruins of his soul. Repentance came into his heart, and one day he appeared before his parents, fell at their feet, asked their forgiveness and said that he wished to come back to the Christian faith. They received him with joy, kissed him and glorified God.

But Meirax's soul was not content with that restoration. He went to the emir, made the sign of the cross and cried out that he had come back to the true faith. The emir gave the order and they scourged him hard with bull's sinews until his flesh was torn; then they beheaded him. His head the Christians bought and buried with honour, and his body the Agarenes put into a boat and threw into the sea. The couplet set over the entry warns the reader: everyone who sees the reputation of those who have denied Christ, let him not chatter against them foolishly.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and is the first source to tell this folder anything: the Greek line was his name and the words “and a most profitable narrative”, with no year, no country and no story. He was an Egyptian, it says. He was misled by a Muslim emir and accepted Islam.

Afterwards he repented. He went into the mosque carrying a cross, declared himself a Christian, and called on the Muslims to leave the lie and receive the truth; and for that he was tortured and beheaded, about the year 640. The book says about, and not a year, so nothing narrower is claimed here.
