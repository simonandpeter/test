# Isaac the Recluse of the Kyiv Caves

The venerable Isaac, whom saint.gr also calls Toroptsanin, was born in Russia of rich and notable parents, and his chief occupation was trade. At some point his good mind considered that everything human is vain; so he shared out his whole property to the poor and came to the cave of [Anthony of the Kyiv Caves](/saints/anthony-of-the-caves), whose memory the page notes is kept on 10 July, longing to give himself to the Lord. There he was tonsured a monk and began his hard spiritual struggle, and with Anthony's blessing he shut himself up in the depth of the Caves, in the darkest cell.

He prayed to the Lord without ceasing, with tears, not knowing when it was night and when day. He had no mattress and never lay down to sleep; he slept a very little, sitting on a stump. He ate only a little of the prosphora, and drank a little water, which Anthony brought him himself.

Isaac was tried hard by the temptation of vainglory, but the mercy of God and the love of the fathers kept him whole and unharmed. Having lived in hard ascesis and fasting, and having gone forward in obedience and in simplicity, he fell asleep in peace in a holy manner; and the abbot John and the brethren buried his holy relic with great honour in the underground place where the venerable relics of the other holy ascetics of the Lavra also rest.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović puts him in the time of the monastery's holy founders. He came to the Caves a rich merchant, left everything and shared it out among the poor, and gave himself to the strictest struggle in a walled-up cell, where [Anthony](/saints/anthony-of-the-caves) himself passed him a single prosphora through a small opening, and that only every other day.

Then he was deceived by the demons, who appeared to him in the light of angels: he bowed to them, and after that to Satan himself, believing it was Christ. He fell ill of it and lay two years, the Prologue says, after which he recovered and became a more careful and more experienced ascetic, and towards the end was given abundant grace for it. He fell asleep in 1090.
