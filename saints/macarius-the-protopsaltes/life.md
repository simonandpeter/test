# Macarius the Protopsaltes

Macarius the Protopsaltes — a translator and composer of church chant and a
zealous patriot, the Romanian life says, and at the same time a humble monk
advanced in the spiritual life, a good servant of the Church of Christ and a
distinguished speaker — had a great part in spreading psaltic music in the
Romanian lands, translating the church chants from the Greek, fitting them
to the singing of the Romanian people and making them easy for all to
understand. He was born in 1763 and grew up in a devout family of three
children, two of whom took the monastic life young — he and his sister, the
nun Justina Perieţeanu, later abbess of Viforâta. From childhood he strove
to learn psaltic music; he studied for a time at Odessa, where he learned
Greek, then returned to Bucharest as the pupil of Fr Macarius the Teacher
and Fr Dionysius, both preachers at the princely court, servers of the
metropolitan cathedral and disciples of Paisius of Neamț. The spiritual bond
with the archimandrite Macarius the Teacher, a zealous monk practised in the
work of prayer and devoted to studying and translating the fathers, planted
in him a longing for the Holy Mountain, and there the young singer received
at his tonsure the name Macarius, following his spiritual father worthily in
the labours of the monastic life and the translating of the church’s songs.

After a time he came back to Bucharest and was ordained hieromonk for the
metropolitan cathedral; for his deep theological knowledge and his gifts as
a speaker he was made preacher of the princely court in 1804–1805. From 1805
to 1808 he studied at Iași with Peter of Byzantium, protopsaltes of the
Ecumenical Patriarchate, then returned to Bucharest and was given the abbacy
of the Golgotha monastery near Târgoviște, where he refounded the
monastery’s old school and served as teacher of chant and preacher. In 1817
he returned to Bucharest to study the new musical notation with Peter of
Ephesus, and two years later Metropolitan Dionysius Lupu refounded the music
school of the metropolis of Ungro-Wallachia and set him at its head; seeing
his Greek and his psaltic learning, the metropolitan gave him the obedience
of translating and adapting the church’s songs into Romanian in the new
notation, so that what was sung in church might be understood by the people,
and he laboured at it all his life. Some Greeks, however, would not have
books of chant in Romanian and hindered him from printing his work in
Wallachia; he tried Sibiu, then Budapest, and met Greek opposition there
too, and went to Vienna, where in 1823, with great difficulty, he printed
the Theoreticon, the Heirmologion and the Anastasimatarion, the first
psaltic books printed in Romanian. Through his labour and his gift as a
teacher and enlightener of the young, after only ten years of the Bucharest
school many had mastered the craft, and his pupils opened music schools in
all the great towns of the metropolis, so that in that short time, by his
prayer and toil, the services of churches where until then chant in Greek
was heard, understood by very few, came to be sung in Romanian. In 1827 he
printed at Bucharest the second volume of the Anthology, the psaltic chants
for the matins of the year.

When Gregory the Teacher was exiled from the metropolitan throne of
Wallachia on 10 February 1829, Macarius, close to him, withdrew to Iași,
where Metropolitan Benjamin Costachi of Moldavia set him to open a school of
psaltic music on the Bucharest model and gave him the abbacy of Bârnova in
the district of Iași; meeting opposition, he withdrew to Neamț, where he
translated many chants and founded a music school. When Gregory was recalled
to Wallachia on 22 August 1833, Macarius returned too at the metropolitan’s
bidding and was sent to Căldărușani to see to the printing of the Lives of
the Saints for the year, which he did with great zeal, founding music
schools for monks at Căldărușani and Țigănești; at Căldărușani he met the
schemamonk Nectarius the Protopsaltes, the great hesychast who later lived
at the Romanian skete of Prodromou on Athos, and hearing how beautifully he
sang encouraged him to multiply his talent. On 6 February 1836, with the
blessing and help of Bishop Chesarie Căpățână, he printed the Lamentations
of the Lord; that summer he fell ill and was bedridden, and chose to live
his last days at Viforâta, where his sister the abbess Justina cared for him
with great love; and he went to the Lord on 30 August 1836, after a short
suffering. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 31 August, marked as a feast
of the day, with a troparion and a kontakion.
