# Baptos, companion of Porphyrius

Baptos — or Dauktos, saint.gr gives both forms — and [Porphyrius](/saints/porphyrius-companion-of-baptos) were among the soldiers who, at the prefect's order, tortured [Charalampus](/saints/charalampus-of-magnesia) of Magnesia. But because there was a pious disposition in their hearts, the page says, God brought them onto the road of salvation: the moral brightness that shone out of the martyr lit up their souls and made them believe in the truth of Christ.

So they threw down the instruments of torture, fell at the saint's feet and asked him for forgiveness. That open act of theirs on the Christian faith's behalf roused the prefect's fury against them, and they were beheaded on the spot, and so took the eternal crown of martyrdom. The distich over the two plays on both their names at once: «Πορφύριος καὶ Βάπτος ἐκ κοινοῦ ξίφους, Ἀθλήσεως βάπτουσι κοινὴν πορφύραν» — Porphyrius and Baptos, from one sword, dye one purple of contest.
