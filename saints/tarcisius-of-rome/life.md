# Tarcisius of Rome

saint.gr says that the holy martyr Tarsizios was martyred at Rome at a very young age, in the time when Valerian and Gallienus were emperors, which it dates to the year 257. He was a disciple of the holy hieromartyr Stephen, bishop of Rome, whose memory the Church keeps on 3 August. From a small age Tarsizios helped the priests at the liturgical assemblies, and for that reason the martyrologies name him an acolyte, which means a follower and helper of the priests in their work.

The event he is remembered for runs, in the page's telling, as follows. A persecution had broken out against the Church in those years, and one day the Precious Gifts, the Body and Blood of Christ, were entrusted to Tarsizios to be carried to the Christians who had been arrested and were under sentence of death in the prisons. On the road, however, idolaters surrounded him and insistently demanded that he hand over what he was holding at his breast. Tarsizios refused stoutly, and then they beat him with sticks until he died.

But when they stooped to take what Tarsizios was holding, the page goes on, they found to their terror that the Precious Gifts had miraculously vanished, and that in the young saint's hands there was only the cloth that had covered them. The Christians reverently took up his holy relic and carried it to the cemetery, the catacomb, of the tomb of Saint Callistus at Rome, where the place of his grave is shown to this day. Later Damasus, bishop of Rome, set over his tomb the epigram the page quotes: while holy Tarsizios was carrying the mysteries of Christ, someone with a profane hand went to defile them; but he chose to give up his life rather than give the divine pearls to the dogs.

The page rests that account on the Roman martyrology, naming the Acta Sanctorum for August, volume three, and says his holy relics are now in the Roman Catholic church of Saint Silvester in Capite at Rome. It adds that today, at the church of the Holy Unmercenaries at Karavas in the metropolis of Piraeus, a festal synaxis in honour of Saint Tarsizios is kept by the whole Youth Movement of that metropolis on the first Saturday after the feast of the Meeting of the Lord.
