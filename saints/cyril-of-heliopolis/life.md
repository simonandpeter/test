# Cyril of Heliopolis

doxologia.ro tells of Cyril the deacon in the same notice as [Mark of Arethusa](/saints/mark-of-arethusa), and tells it in the words of Theodoret: who can speak without tears of the wickedness the pagans did in Phoenicia? For in the city of Heliopolis, which has the Lebanon for its border, that hateful people remembered Cyril the deacon, who in the reign of Constantine, burning with divine zeal, had broken many of the idols honoured in that city.

They did not only kill him: they cut his belly into pieces, and in their anger dared to bite his inward parts with their teeth. But the deed was not hidden from the all-knowing God, and they took a fitting punishment for their wickedness; for all who dared to do it fell dead, every one. First they lost their tongues, which were wounded and rotted in the mouth and fell away, and at the last all of them were blinded in their eyes; and by such disasters the power of the true and right faith was shown.

The same passage tells what was done at Ascalon and Gaza, the cities of Palestine: they cut open the bellies first of the men adorned with the honour of the priesthood, and then of the women and virgins consecrated to God, filled them with barley and threw them to the pigs to eat. So inhuman a torment they inflicted. The crowns of victory were made ready for the holy martyrs in the kingdom of Christ, and everlasting punishment in hell for the tormentors.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, under the emperor's name and with his own city left out. saint.gr heads him «(; - 363)» and says that under Julian the Apostate, in 363, the deacon Cyril shone out as the boast of the Church of Phoenicia; because he stood unmoved in the Christian confession and preached against the idols, he stirred up the fury of the idolaters, who with swords opened his belly so that his entrails were poured out. By the same death, the page adds, a number of virgin women at Ascalon and at Gaza and some men in holy orders ended their lives, and their memory is kept with his on this day — they are [the virgins and priests of Ascalon and Gaza](/saints/virgins-and-priests-of-ascalon-and-gaza). Its couplet turns the death into a verse of the Psalms: «Γαστὴρ Κυρίλλου Λευΐτου διὰ ξίφους, Ὡσεὶ πάχος γῆς, εἶπε Δαυΐδ, ἐρράγη» — the belly of Cyril the Levite was broken open by the sword, as David said, like the thickness of the earth. The page names Phoenicia and not Heliopolis, and Heliopolis is a city of Phoenicia; that is why the Greek row stands on this folder and no second one was made. Beside him it keeps [Mark](/saints/mark-of-arethusa), bishop of Arethusa, and the one apolytikion it prints is for the whole company.
