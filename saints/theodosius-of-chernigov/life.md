# Theodosius of Chernigov

Theodosius was born in the early 1630s in Podolia, of the old noble line of the Polonitsky-Uglitsky; his father Nicetas was a priest, and the name he was given at baptism has not come down. He studied at the Kiev Brotherhood College at the Epiphany monastery in its great years, when it was the centre of the Orthodox defence against the Jesuits and the Uniates, and the friendships he made there - Lazar Baranovich among them - shaped the rest of his life. He took the schema young, served as ecclesiarch of the Kiev Caves, then as abbot of the Korsun and Vydubitsky monasteries, which he restored from Uniate hands and made ordered and prayerful houses.

In 1692 he was consecrated Archbishop of Chernigov, having long been the effective helper of his aged predecessor. His rule was short and gentle: the life remembers his mildness, his strict justice, and his deep compassion toward everyone who came to him - not only the Orthodox. He died on 5 February 1696 and was buried in the Boris and Gleb cathedral in Chernigov, in a crypt built for him behind the right kliros.

His veneration grew at once around the crypt, and in 1896 - two hundred years after his repose - his relics were uncovered and translated, and he was glorified. The Russian calendar keeps that uncovering on 9 September of the old style, which is the day this entry stands on; his repose it keeps on 5 February.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 5 February, the day of his repose, where the Russian calendar the corpus read keeps the uncovering of his relics on 9 September. Its own line is only «Όσιος Θεοδόσιος εκ Ρωσίας», Theodosius out of Russia, but the page behind it is unmistakably him: father Nicetas, mother Maria, the Kiev academy, abbot of Korsun in 1662 and then spiritual guide of the ancient Kiev monastery of Vydubychi, abbot of Yelets at Chernigov in 1688, chosen by the ailing archbishop Lazar as his successor and elected archbishop of Chernigov on 11 September 1692, reposed in 1696 and laid in the monastery of Boris and Gleb at Chernigov, where his relics became a source of healings. The page opens by calling him «Ο Όσιος Θεοδόσιος του Ούγκλιχ», Theodosius of Uglich, which fits nothing else on the page and is presumably its rendering of the family name Polonitsky-Uglitsky; and it says that at Kiev he strove to imitate the lives of the venerable Antony and Theodosius of the Caves, and that he often withdrew to the little skete of Mikhaylovshchina for solitude and quiet.
