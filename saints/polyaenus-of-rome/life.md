# Polyaenus of Rome

Polyaenus was one of three Roman martyrs — [Hermas](/saints/hermas-of-rome),
[Serapion](/saints/serapion-of-rome) and Polyaenus — who suffered for
Christ in the second century. They were known, the Orthodox Church in
America’s life says, for their zeal in spreading the faith and answering the
pagans’ arguments; arrested and brought before the authorities, they held to
their confession, were beaten without mercy and thrown into a dark and foul
prison, where hunger and hardship did nothing to shake them. Taken out and
bound with ropes, they were dragged by the mob over rough ground full of
sharp stones, and died of it — striking against the rocks and ledges, the
Sretensky calendar’s life says, in narrow and impassable places — their
flesh torn and their heads covered in wounds, and their souls untouched. The
Russian calendar keeps the three on 18 August (Julian).

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 18 August with his two companions, and doxologia.ro prints one life for all three. It says Polyaenus, [Serapion](/saints/serapion-of-rome) and [Hermas](/saints/hermas-of-rome) came from the city of Rome and showed themselves unyielding in confessing and preaching the right faith, rising up with strength against the error of idolatry. For this they were denounced to the chief men of the city, and would not deny Christ. They were first cruelly beaten, then thrown into a dark and foul prison and left in want and much suffering; and since they remained unmoved in their hope, they were brought out and dragged by the crowd, bound with ropes, over ground full of sharp stones, until their bodies were broken and covered with wounds and blood, and their souls, untouched by the pain, rose to Christ.
