# Juvenal of Jerusalem

Juvenal was Patriarch of Jerusalem between the years 420 and 458, in the days of the great saints of the desert: [Euthymius the Great](/saints/euthymius-the-great), whom the Romanian calendar keeps on 20 January, [Symeon the Stylite](/saints/symeon-the-stylite), kept on 1 September, and Gerasimus of the Jordan, kept on 4 March. During his years as shepherd the Eastern Church was troubled by mistaken and dangerous teachings, which he fought with much pastoral zeal, protecting the flock of Christ steadfastly.

He took part in the Third Ecumenical Council, gathered in the city of Ephesus in the year 431, and in the Fourth Ecumenical Council at Chalcedon in the year 451.

Doxologia's page for him carries no life and no troparion, only that notice, and it is set down here whole; the page gives neither the year of his birth nor the year of his death.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same 2 July and puts his place in church history higher than the Romanian notice does. saint.gr says that on the likeliest account he was patriarch twenty-eight unbroken years and was the contemporary of the emperors Theodosius the Less, Pulcheria, Marcian and Leo I; that at the Third Ecumenical Council at Ephesus his part was of the most active, because he was a man not only of great zeal but of eloquence and learning; and that he wrote on the Dormition of the Theotokos and on her Translation.

It adds the act that ties him to this very day in the calendar: it was Juvenal who sent the Theotokos's burial swaddling-bands to the emperor Marcian, and they were laid up in the church at Blachernae — the deposition that saint.gr keeps as the first entry of 2 Ιουλίου, immediately above his own. The page dates his death, on the word of Dositheos, to the year 457, which falls inside the interval already recorded here, and the distich over the entry points at his grave: «Οὗ μνῆμα θεῖον ἡ Παλαιστίνη φέρει» — whose divine tomb Palestine bears.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 2 July, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep him on, and it tells a story neither of them has. It begins where the Romanian notice begins, with the company he belonged to — a contemporary of the great lights of the Orthodox Church, it says, of [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-the-great), of a Theodosius, of [Gerasimus](/saints/gerasimus-of-the-jordan), of [Symeon the Stylite](/saints/symeon-the-stylite) and others. It gives him the year the other two books withhold: in deep old age he presented himself to the Lord in the year 458, where the Greek page says 457 on the word of Dositheos, so the folder now reads 457 or 458, widened to hold both books.

It also names what he fought at the two Councils, where the Romanian notice records only that he was there. At Ephesus it was Nestorius, who called the Theotokos the bearer of Christ rather than the bearer of God; at Chalcedon, Eutyches and Dioscorus, who taught that in Christ there was one nature only, the divine, without the human. After the victory of Orthodoxy at both he returned to his throne at Jerusalem. But if the heresies were condemned, this book says, the heretics were not rooted out.

By the intrigue and violence of a Theodosius, a friend of Dioscorus, Juvenal was driven from his throne, and that Theodosius set himself up in his place. The empress Eudocia, widowed and then settled in Jerusalem, helped the usurper at first; being unsteady and hasty, she went at last to the stylite to ask him where the truth was, and he showed her every heretical teaching for what it was and directed her to hold what the Councils had established. She obeyed, repented, and turned against the false patriarch. Then a letter came from the emperor to the commander Athanasius to drive the usurper out and bring Juvenal back, which he shortly did; and he governed the church of Jerusalem as its hierarch thirty-eight years in all. In his time, the book ends, the keeping of the Nativity on 25 December was established.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 2 июля ст. ст. — «Свт. Иувеналия , архиеп. Иерусалимского (ок. 458).» — the same menologion day as the three calendars this folder already carries, and with about 458 for the year, which is where its floruit ends. That calendar grades him archbishop in the line and patriarch on his name page, and sets him on this day only. Under his name it prints a troparion which has him succeed to the throne of the Brother of God, calls him the praiseworthy hierarch of the holy city, and has him set forth the incarnation of the Son of God together with the God-wise fathers at Chalcedon.
