# Sergius (Uklonsky)

Sergius Mikhailovich Uklonsky was born on 5 May 1864, the son of Michael
Uklonsky, priest of the church of Tesovo. He finished the Bethany
seminary, was ordained priest in 1888 to the church of Rassudovo in the
Podolsk district, and at the start of the century served at the Protection
church of Kulikovo in the Dmitrov district; in 1921 the hieromartyr
Seraphim (Zvezdinsky), bishop of Dmitrov, raised him to archpriest and
made him dean of the eighth district. After the bishop’s arrest and the
Renovationists’ seizure of church power he did all a dean could to
withstand the pressure of the Living Church: for failing to appear at a
meeting of deans called by the Renovationist Moscow diocesan
administration he was dismissed from the deanery on 6 March 1923, with a
note in his record, and answered by calling a meeting of the clergy of
his district, which resolved not to accept the Renovationist changes nor
the Renovationist dean; on 17 April the Renovationist administration “put
on record the improper and unlawful action of the archpriest Uklonsky”
and warned that if he went on acting as dean he would be removed from his
place. In 1924 he moved to the church of Cherneyevo in the Dmitrov
district, and in the early thirties was forced to leave for Tver province,
serving at Aleshenka near Bologoye and then at Novoye — renamed Sverdlovo —
on the Volga; on 29 January 1931, at sixty-seven, he was tried under
article 61 for non-payment of tax and given a year of forced labour, and
about the same time his son, back from exile, died. By 1937 he looked, the
file says, a worn-out old man with comic gestures and grimaces. On 26
February 1937 he was moved to the church of Mshentsy, at Vasilievsky Mokh
in the Korytnovo rural soviet of the Bologoye district of Tver, then
Kalinin, region. He urged his parishioners to pray, confess, fast and
baptise their children, went from house to house talking of the faith and
baptising, and people came to his services from the neighbouring villages
and from further off, nuns of the closed convents among them; on the local
feast of the Ninth Friday the clergy of the churches round and other
guests gathered, and it was a solemn day. With Pascha coming he asked the
nuns to go round the village and get the people’s agreement to his
visiting them on the feast, so that with their signatures he could ask the
rural soviet’s leave — a priest in his seventies, so hungry that he was
ready to go from door to door for alms. The chairman of the soviet wrote a
report, and on 2 September 1937 he was arrested; by resolution of the
troika of the NKVD for the Kalinin region the seventy-four-year-old
archpriest was sentenced to be shot, and the sentence was carried out on
19 September 1937. The jubilee Council of Bishops of 2000 numbered him
among the hieromartyrs and confessors of the Russian Church; the Russian
calendar keeps him on 9 September of the old style, 22 September, and in
the synaxes of the saints of Tver and Moscow, and the life is from the
church of his native Tesovo.
