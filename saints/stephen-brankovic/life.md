# Stephen Branković

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 10 декабря in a line that names three of one family: «Блж. Иоанна (1503) и родителей его блжж. Стефана (1446) и Ангелины, Бранковичей, правителей Сербских.» — the blessed [John](/saints/john-the-despot) and his parents the blessed Stephen and [Angelina](/saints/angelina-of-serbia), Brankovići, rulers of Serbia. Two readings stand behind the line, and both are short.

The fuller of them has the life of this Serbian ruler and his family full of reverses and calamities. After the Turks took Serbia in 1457, it says, Stephen — the middle son of the man then ruling Serbia, of a gentle temper and excellently read in Holy Scripture — went to the Turkish capital after his sister, who had been given to the sultan as a wife. But learning that the Turks had burnt the monastery of Mileševa with fanatical cruelty, he stood up to defend Serbia from the conquerors. When he married Angelina, daughter of an Albanian prince, the Turks threatened him and his family with destruction; and with his wife and three children he had to hide, first in Albania and then in Italy, where he died.

The other reading calls him Stephen the Blind, and says he was blinded at Edirne together with his brother Gregory, having lived for a time with that sister at the court of the sultan; that he began to rule the Serbs in 1458 and soon had to flee to Albania, where he married; that he raised two sons in exile; and that he died in Italy, blind and unhappy, but in perfect devotion to God, and that God kept his relics incorrupt and fragrant. His son [Maximus](/saints/maximus-of-ungrovlachia), it adds, was made archbishop in Wallachia and founded at the end of his life the monastery of Krušedol, where his relics rest with his parents’ and his brother’s. Angelina carried her husband’s body to Kupinovo.

That son is a folder the corpus already keeps, and it is the corpus that identifies the two rather than this reader: the life of [Maximus](/saints/maximus-of-ungrovlachia) opens by calling him the son of the ruler of the Serbs, Saint Stephen Branković the Blind, whom the Greek calendar honours on 10 December, and of Saint Angelina — and it says his name in the world was George, which this calendar does not. No Greek row is written here on the strength of that sentence; saint.gr was not read for this day.

The day line closes his name with the year 1446, which no reading of the three repeats and which neither of them can be made to hold; nothing further is set down here about when he died.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 10 декабря ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261210.html) and its two readings of him, [the one](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life3098.htm) and [the other](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life6930.htm); read 13 October 2026.*
