# Babylas of Nicomedia, teacher, with 84 children

Babylas and his eighty-four pupils suffered at Nicomedia for confessing
Christianity in the reign of Maximian — later, the Greek synaxarion notes,
than the bishop of Antioch of the same name kept the same day. The emperor,
staying at Nicomedia, raised a persecution, and the old man Babylas, who
taught children Christian piety, was denounced to him like many other
believers; brought before the emperor he was given to many torments for
confessing the true God, and through his sufferings kept crying to God: I
thank you, Lord, that you have made me, old and weak, young and strong.
After a stoning they put chains on his bleeding body and sent him to
prison, and then his pupils were brought before the emperor. Neither gentle
persuasion nor the promise of gifts broke the children’s Christian
conviction, and two of them, Ammonius and Donatus, declared firmly: we are
Christians, and we do not sacrifice to dumb and deaf demons. Enraged by so
unexpected and so firm an answer from children, the emperor ordered them
first beaten, then put to death by the sword together with their teacher;
and on the way to the execution Babylas sang his thanksgiving to God —
behold, I and the children whom God has given me. With spiritual joy they
received the martyr’s end, first Babylas and then all his eighty-four
pupils.
