# Agathonica, sister of Papylas

Agathonica was the sister of [Papylas](/saints/papylas-the-deacon), the deacon of [Carpus](/saints/carpus-of-thyatira), bishop of Thyatira. Under the pagan emperor Decius the two were slandered for refusing the idols, and the proconsul Valerie wore out every torment upon them: iron chains on their necks, a naked procession through the streets, the horses that dragged them from Thyatira to Sardis, the rods of thorn, candles at their ribs, salt strewn in their wounds, and the razor at their thighs.

Beasts were brought and loosed on them and would not touch them. A she-bear showed nothing of her nature but lay down and embraced their feet; a lion was tamed as quickly and, roaring, spoke with a human voice and rebuked the tormentors for their savagery, and they stopped their ears and called it sorcery. Then iron shoes full of sharp nails were put on the martyrs and they were made to run in them.

Valerie made a fire and heated a furnace until it glowed, and threw the saints into it; and into that flame Agathonica went with them of her own accord, wishing to be a partaker of the same struggles and desiring to die for Christ. The furnace did not burn them but was cooled by the power of Christ, for a great rain came down and the fire went out. They came out unhurt and were taken back to the prison, where they sang to God as in a church.

The judge, not knowing what more to do, was ashamed and beaten, and gave his last sentence: that their heads be cut off. Agathonica was led out with them to the place of killing, and they rejoiced, and lifting their eyes and their hands to heaven they prayed not only for themselves but for those who were killing them; and so they ended their struggle, dying by the sword. Their bodies, thrown out without honour, were taken up in secret by the faithful and buried with honour. The page names Decius and Valerie and gives no year, and none is supplied here.

The Greek calendar keeps the four of them on the same day, 13 October, and saint.gr sets out who each of them was. All four had Pergamon for their country, it says, and they were martyred when Decius was emperor, a most harsh persecutor of the Christians. Carpus, thoroughly schooled in letters, most devout and with many services to the Church behind him, had become bishop of Thyateira; Papylus, who had studied medicine and gave his services without pay, became a deacon and Carpus's immediate fellow-worker; and Agathodorus, a chosen and faithful soul, was a servant in the bishop's house at Thyateira. When the proconsul Valerius arrested the three of them they confessed Christ before him with boldness, and he told them that Christians were superstitious, incapable, without higher feelings, and that educated men like themselves ought to renounce them at once. Carpus answered that charge in the words of the Apostle Paul — «κοπιῶμεν ἐργαζόμενοι ταῖς ἰδίαις χερσὶ λοιδορούμενοι εὐλογοῦμεν, διωκόμενοι ἀνεχόμεθα, βλασφημούμενοι παρακαλοῦμεν» — we labour with our own hands; when we are reviled we bless, when we are persecuted we bear it, when we are slandered we answer with sweet and comforting words. Exasperated by the answer, Valerius tortured them and then beheaded them all, together with Agathonica, the sister of Papylus. The page records that portions of the relics of Papylus and of Carpus are kept at the monasteries of Stavronikita on the Holy Mountain and of the Holy Cross on Samos.

The Russian calendar keeps the four of them on 13 October too, and gives the year the other two calendars do not: 251, under Decius. She and Agathodorus followed of their own will, it says, when Carpus and Papylas were tied to horses and dragged from Thyatira to the neighbouring city of Sardis; and at Sardis she was beheaded with the two of them, while Agathodorus was beaten to death with ox sinews. The Russian does not say she was Papylas's sister — that is the Greek's and the Romanian's — and names her for Pergamum instead.
