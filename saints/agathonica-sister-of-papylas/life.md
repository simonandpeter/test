# Agathonica, sister of Papylas

Agathonica was the sister of [Papylas](/saints/papylas-the-deacon), the deacon of [Carpus](/saints/carpus-of-thyatira), bishop of Thyatira. Under the pagan emperor Decius the two were slandered for refusing the idols, and the proconsul Valerie wore out every torment upon them: iron chains on their necks, a naked procession through the streets, the horses that dragged them from Thyatira to Sardis, the rods of thorn, candles at their ribs, salt strewn in their wounds, and the razor at their thighs.

Beasts were brought and loosed on them and would not touch them. A she-bear showed nothing of her nature but lay down and embraced their feet; a lion was tamed as quickly and, roaring, spoke with a human voice and rebuked the tormentors for their savagery, and they stopped their ears and called it sorcery. Then iron shoes full of sharp nails were put on the martyrs and they were made to run in them.

Valerie made a fire and heated a furnace until it glowed, and threw the saints into it; and into that flame Agathonica went with them of her own accord, wishing to be a partaker of the same struggles and desiring to die for Christ. The furnace did not burn them but was cooled by the power of Christ, for a great rain came down and the fire went out. They came out unhurt and were taken back to the prison, where they sang to God as in a church.

The judge, not knowing what more to do, was ashamed and beaten, and gave his last sentence: that their heads be cut off. Agathonica was led out with them to the place of killing, and they rejoiced, and lifting their eyes and their hands to heaven they prayed not only for themselves but for those who were killing them; and so they ended their struggle, dying by the sword. Their bodies, thrown out without honour, were taken up in secret by the faithful and buried with honour. The page names Decius and Valerie and gives no year, and none is supplied here.
