# Adrian (17 April)

doxologia.ro's calendar for 17 April names him and prints one short notice out of the Menaion for April, and that notice is the whole of what the page has. This martyr Adrian, it says, was one of those who had at some time been seized and put into various prisons; and when they were brought out, at a time when the Greeks were in their error and sacrificing to false gods, he too was pressed to come up to the altar and offer incense.

He would not do it. Strong of body and brave, he overturned the altar instead, spilled the offerings that lay upon it and scattered the fire. By this he moved the governor to fury and kindled the anger of the idolaters; he was seized, beaten without mercy, and thrown into a furnace burning with fire, and so, the notice ends, he was made perfect in peace.

The page gives no year, names no city, and says nothing of his birth or his manner of life; it names the Menaion for April as its source. He is not the Adrian of Nicomedia whom this calendar keeps on 26 August, nor the second Adrian of that same day.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and calls him Adrian the New. saint.gr says he was imprisoned by the idolaters because he refused to leave the faith of Christ, and there was put to many deprivations and torments to break his spirit. When they thought his steadiness must have given way at last, they brought him out of the prison and ordered him to offer sacrifice to the gods of the idols. Adrian not only refused: being near the altar, and enraged at the governor's insistence and at the abuse he was uttering against Christ, he ran and threw the altar down, with the fire and the victims that were on it.

The governor, astonished at what Adrian had done, gave the signal for a savage attack on him, and blow after blow with iron bars and stones wherever they could reach him left him covered in blood, with his face and his head full of wounds. But the fury of the idolaters was not satisfied, the page says; they lit a furnace and threw him into its flames, and there the fearless martyr found his martyr's end. Its couplet has the fire speak for him: «Ἂν οὐκ ἔγνως τὶς ἐστιν ὁ φλογὸς μέσον, Γνώσῃ λαλοῦντος, Ἀδριανέ, καρτέρει» — if you did not know who it is in the midst of the flame, you shall know when he speaks; hold out, Adrian.

**The Russian calendar gives this folder its year, its city and the whole of the story, where the Greek gave a line.** Two readings stand under his name, the shorter inside the longer, and they agree. He suffered for Christ in the persecution of the Christians in 251, the shorter says: for refusing to sacrifice to the pagan gods and for destroying a pagan altar he was cruelly tortured and then burned in an enormous furnace.

The longer sets it in the reign of the emperor Decius, which it dates 249 to 251, and tells how it happened. He had been shut in prison; and at a pagan feast all the imprisoned Christians were let out so that they might sacrifice to the idols. He was ordered to throw fragrant resin on the altar, and instead he threw down the offerings lying on it, scattered the fire and destroyed the altar. The pagans fell on him in a fury, beat him with staves and with iron rods, pelted him with stones, and then threw him into a white-hot furnace. The calendar's own name for him carries the city the lines do not: Corinth.
