# Eupsychius of Caesarea (under Hadrian)

Eupsychius belongs to the devout glories of Caesarea in Cappadocia. His father Dionysius died, but he was not left an orphan: catechised in the Christian faith and baptised, he became a subject and blessed son of the heavenly Father, gave all he had to the poor, lived in great simplicity and devoted himself to the service of the salvation of souls. For this, under the emperor Hadrian (117–138), he was hunted and condemned: first they tore his sides and threw him bleeding into prison, where an angel of the Lord healed his wounds, and at last, unable to change his faith, they beheaded him, and instead of blood milk and water came from his wounds. Stout-souled, the verse plays on his name, he went to the sword rejoicing that he sacrificed his soul to its maker.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, makes him the son of a senator named Dionysius. Beaten and scraped, he was thrown half-dead into prison, where an angel healed him; set free, he gave away all he had, part to the poor and part to the men who had informed on him. Taken again, he was scraped until he died, under Hadrian, and milk and water ran from his wounds. The Prologue’s reflection for the day takes that gift as its lesson: he had so far overcome anger that he counted his accusers his benefactors.
