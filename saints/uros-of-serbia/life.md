# Uroš of Serbia

The Ohrid Prologue gives him one paragraph and it is the whole of what this folder has. He was the son of the emperor Dušan, and he reigned in the hard time of the breaking-up of the Serbian empire. Meek, devout and mild, he would not bring the unbridled magnates to heel by force; and the most unbridled of them was Vukašin, who was the death of the good emperor. So Uroš suffered as a martyr on 2 December 1367, in the thirty-first year of his life. Killed by men, says the book, he was glorified by God.

His wonderworking relics lay in the monastery of Jazak in Fruška Gora. From there, in the course of the Second World War and in the year 1942, they were carried to Belgrade and laid in the cathedral church beside the bodies of [Lazar of Serbia](/saints/lazar-of-serbia) and the despot [Stephen Štiljanović](/saints/stephen-stiljanovic). In the days of this good emperor the monastery of Saint Naum was built on Lake Ohrid, over the relics of [Naum of Ohrid](/saints/naum-of-ohrid); his magnate Grgur built it.

That is all. The book gives him no year of birth, names no city of his reign and no battle, says nothing of how he was killed beyond who killed him, and gives him neither wife nor child. Where it calls him «цар», this corpus’s own reading of that word is the rank it keeps for a crowned ruler; the see is not recorded here, because no form of words for a Serbian emperor exists in this site’s four languages.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the Julian 2 December and gives him a short life with dates the Prologue does not. He was the son of the tsar Dušan Nemanjić and was born in 1337; in 1346 he was crowned to the kingdom. Dušan asked the hand of the French king’s daughter for his son, but the pope of Rome insisted that the princess should not change her Latin confession; Dušan would not have a Roman Catholic in the family, and so Uroš married the daughter of Vlad, prince of Wallachia.

On his father’s death in 1355 he became an independent and active ruler of Serbia. He was faithful to the Lord, says this page, gave widows and orphans a father’s peace, quieted quarrels and established peace, had mercy on the poor and defended the wronged.

For the sake of peace in Serbia and to save his own life he was forced to withdraw to his kinsman, prince Lazar. The throne was seized at once by Vukašin, his uncle, whom the fear of a rival would not let rest; and through his own sister, the saint’s mother, Vukašin called his nephew to him at Skopje as though for a reconciliation. He met him with honours, as a tsar, and invited him to the hunt; and when Stephen, tired with hunting, got down from his horse at a well and bent to draw water, Vukašin struck him a mortal blow on the head with a mace. That was in 1367.
