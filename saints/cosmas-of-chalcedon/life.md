# Cosmas of Chalcedon

Cosmas, the hierarch and confessor of Christ, was of the city of Constantine. Loving Christ from his childhood, he left this empty world and was tonsured a monk; and having cleansed himself by fasting and the other virtues he became, the page says, a dwelling place of the Holy Spirit, and was set as bishop in Chalcedon in the days of the iconoclasts. There he showed many labours in the help of the right faith.

The heretical emperors pressed him to renounce the veneration of the holy icons, and he would not listen to them; for that he was driven out of his see and bore many trials. Called back and pressed again with afflictions to join himself to their heresy, he refused them a second time, and was thrown into troubles without number. He struggled, the page says, together with the venerable Auxentius; and having been tried and tormented by the iconoclasts he came to his end and departed to the Lord.

The notice is short and is the whole of what doxologia.ro prints. It gives no year for his birth, his consecration or his death, names no emperor, and carries no troparion.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day as the Romanian and tells the same story in fewer words, with three things the Romanian notice did not give. It names the emperor the Romanian left anonymous: «Лев Армянин», whose reign it dates 813 to 820, and whose decree that the holy icons be taken out of the churches is the order he would not obey. It gives his country as Constantinople, where he went into a monastery while still young and took the monastic tonsure, before being ordained to the see of Chalcedon. And it closes with a pair of years, «(+ ок. 815 - 820)», which is the first date any source read for this folder has offered and which is now this folder’s `death`.

The rest it says as the Romanian does. The iconoclasts tried many times to draw him to their side and could not; he was driven from his cathedra and sent into confinement; when he was brought back from exile he went on defending the veneration of the icons, and the unceasing persecutions weakened him in body while he stayed firm in spirit. The fellow-struggler the Romanian page calls «cuviosul Auxentie» is named here too, and he now has a folder of his own — [Auxentius](/saints/auxentius-companion-of-cosmas) — written off this same Russian page, which is the only one of the three calendars to give him a name link of his own.
