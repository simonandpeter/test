# Martha (Milyukova) of Diveyevo

Martha — Maria Semyonovna Milyukova in the world — was born on 10 (23)
February 1810 to a peasant family of the village of Pogiblovo, today
Malinovka, in the Ardatov district of Nizhny Novgorod province; the
Milyukovs, a family of righteous and God-pleasing life, were close to the
elder Seraphim of Sarov, and besides Maria there were two elder children,
a sister Praskovya and a brother John. The village and those round it
were crown villages, belonging not to a landowner but to the treasury,
their land cut separately and poor, the black earth having been seized by
the neighbouring landowners; the peasants lived very poorly, and the
children worked hard from childhood in the fields and with the cattle. By
Seraphim’s blessing Praskovya had entered the community of Mother
Alexandra, the first founder of Diveyevo, and was of high spiritual life;
and when Maria was thirteen she came with her sister to Fr Seraphim for
the first time, on 21 November 1823, the Entry of the Mother of God into
the Temple — she “tagged along”, Praskovya said — and the great elder,
foreseeing that the girl was a chosen vessel of God’s grace, would not let
her go home but bade her stay in the community, then under the eldress
Xenia Mikhailovna Kocheulova, whom Seraphim called a pillar of fire from
earth to heaven. From those early years this extraordinary, angel-like
child of God led an ascetic life surpassing in severity even the sisters
famed for their strictness, the superior first of all; unceasing prayer
was her food, she answered only necessary questions with a heavenly
meekness, and was almost a silent; and Seraphim loved her with an
especial tenderness, letting her into all his revelations, the future
glory of the monastery and other great spiritual mysteries, charging her
to tell no one until the time — which she kept sacredly whatever the
sisters and her family begged — and she came back from him shining with
unspeakable joy. Soon after her entry the Queen of Heaven willed to found
beside the Kazan community a new one, with which the monastery promised
to Mother Alexandra began: from 1825 the sisters, and then Xenia herself,
went to Seraphim for blessing, but she would not change the heavy Sarov
rule that weighed on him and on the sisters, and on 25 November 1825, the
feast of Clement of Rome and Peter of Alexandria, making his way along the
Sarovka to his far hermitage, Seraphim saw the Mother of God with the
apostles Peter and John behind her; striking the earth with her staff so
that a spring of bright water boiled up, she told him not to abandon the
command of her servant Agatha, the nun Alexandra, to leave Xenia and her
sisters, and to build her promised monastery on another place in Diveyevo
that she would show him, taking eight sisters from Xenia’s community,
whom she named. Two weeks later, on 9 December 1825, Maria and another
sister, Praskovya Stepanovna, came to Seraphim, who took them to the far
hermitage, gave them two lit wax candles and set Maria to the right of
the crucifix on the wall and Praskovya to the left, and they stood so more
than an hour while he prayed between them; then he kissed the crucifix
and had them pray and kiss it too — the mysterious prayer with which,
before the founding of the new community, he consecrated the sisters the
Mother of God had chosen for her particular service. For four years she
laboured with Seraphim and the sisters in the building of the new
community, cutting posts and timber for the mill the Mother of God had
blessed them to build on the site, carrying stones for the church of the
Nativity of the Mother of God, grinding flour and bearing other
obediences, never leaving the prayer of the heart, silently lifting her
burning spirit to the Lord. Given by God the rare gift of pure and
unceasing prayer, she was guided in everything by Seraphim himself, and
her obedience was absolute: asked once by her sister about some Sarov
monk she wondered, childlike, what monks looked like — like Father? —
and to Praskovya’s astonishment, since she went to Sarov so often, said
humbly that she saw and knew nothing: Fr Seraphim had told her never to
look at them, and she tied her kerchief over her eyes so as to see only
the road under her feet. Such was this child-ascetic, who lived six years
in the community and at nineteen went quietly and peacefully to the Lord
on 21 August 1829. Knowing in spirit the hour of her death Seraphim
suddenly wept, and told his neighbour Fr Paul with great grief that
Maria had gone, and he was so sorry for her that, see, he could not stop
weeping; he gave her from himself a round oak coffin, hollowed out, for
which Praskovya came with the sister Akulina Vasilyevna — you will be own
sisters now, he told them, joining their hands, and I your father, who
begot you in the spirit; and Maria is the schema-nun Martha — I gave her
the schema; she has everything, schema and mantle and my kamilavka, and in
all that lay her; do not grieve, her soul is in the Kingdom of Heaven
near the Holy Trinity at God’s throne, and all your family will be saved
through her. He gave twenty-five roubles for the funeral, twenty-five in
copper to give three kopecks to everyone at the burial, sisters and
laity, two towels for the altar, a bundle of yellow candles to burn day
and night for the forty days, a rouble candle for the coffin and half a
pood of white candles; and by his blessing she was laid in the coffin in
two shifts, a cotton cassock girt with a black woollen band, the black
schema with white crosses and the long mantle, a green velvet cap
embroidered with gold, his own kamilavka over it, a big dark-blue fringed
kerchief, and a leather prayer-rope in her hands — all things he had given
her with his own hands, bidding her always wear them to communion, as she
did every great feast and all four fasts. Everyone who came to him those
days he sent to Diveyevo to her funeral — the sisters working at Satis
(quick, quick, my joys, to Diveyevo: a great servant of God, Maria, has
gone to the Lord), the sisters gathering berries in the Sarov forest,
Sarov monks and a whole crowd of people, saying that whoever was at her
burial would receive remission of sins, and bidding the lay girls and
sisters dress, comb their hair and fall at her coffin. At the funeral her
sister Praskovya plainly saw in the royal doors the Queen of Heaven and
Maria standing in the air, cried out in rapture through the whole church,
Queen, do not forsake us!, and began from that day to play the fool for
Christ and prophesy, giving away the clothes she wore, while the demons
in the possessed shrieked and clamoured; the Lord and the Queen of Heaven,
Seraphim said when Akulina told him, wished to glorify our mother Martha
and lady Maria — and had poor Seraphim been at her burial, many would
have been healed by her spirit. To her brother John, who had come from the
funeral to ask whether Praskovya would recover, he asked twice whether he
was truly Maria’s brother, thought long, and grew so radiant that John
had to shield his eyes: what mercy she has been granted by the Lord — in
the Kingdom of Heaven at God’s throne, near the Queen of Heaven, she
stands with the holy virgins, an intercessor for all your family; she is
the schema-nun Martha, I tonsured her; whenever you are at Diveyevo never
pass by, but fall at her grave and say, our lady and mother Martha,
remember us at God’s throne in the Kingdom of Heaven — and talked with him
some three hours; and he had the sister Xenia Putkova, who kept his
lists for commemoration, write Maria down as a nun, for by her deeds and
poor Seraphim’s prayers she had been found worthy of the schema there —
pray for her, all of you, as the schema-nun Martha. Tall and attractive,
with a long fair fresh face, blue eyes and thick light-brown brows and
hair, she was buried with her hair loose, to the left of Mother
Alexandra. Little of her survives in the eldresses’ stories: Seraphim told
one of them how zealous she was for labour — when the church of the
Nativity was building the girls carried stones themselves, two or three
each, and she would take five or six and, with prayer on her lips,
silently lift her burning spirit to the Lord, and soon, with a sick
stomach, went to God; and Praskovya Stepanovna told how, sent by him with
Maria and two horses for timber and having shifted his four thin logs onto
one horse and a thick one onto the other against his arrangement, they saw
the horse fall and begin to die, begged his pardon on their knees, put the
logs back as he had laid them, and the horse sprang up and ran so that
they could hardly catch it. Her words came down through the sisters,
written by the eldress Justina, later the nun Hilaria, from a page found
in the cell of the schema-nun Margarita Lakhtionova: leading her to the
Kazan church she said, foreseeing her early death, remember, this church
will be ours and no priests will live here; the parish church will be
built elsewhere, and here, as Fr Seraphim says, will be a Lavra, and where
the Ditch is, a coenobium; all this place is hallowed by the labours of
Mother Agatha Semyonovna, and what a cathedral it will be, my joy, like
the one at Jerusalem, and the present church will go inside it and remain
only as a kernel — and Seraphim had the ground on both sides of the
Nativity church fenced: here are the footsteps of the Queen of Heaven,
this earth is holy, the Mother of God walked round her church; do not walk
on it, nor let even a beast, and weed the grass and carry it into the
monastery, the grass is holy. Seraphim loved her above all, the same
eldress said, and told her much of the monastery, mostly forbidding her
to repeat it, but some things he charged her to remember and pass on: the
cemetery church would be of the Transfiguration, and an altar of All
Saints made earlier still — fulfilled in the All Saints chapel of 1847
and the Transfiguration church of 1855; and of the monastery’s straitened
means: poor Seraphim could make you rich, but it is not profitable; I
could turn ashes to gold, and will not; with you much will not multiply
and little will not diminish; in the last time you will have abundance in
everything, but then will be the end of everything. The nineteen-year-old
schema-nun Martha was appointed, in Seraphim’s words, superior over the
Diveyevo orphans in the Kingdom of Heaven, in the Mother of God’s
monastery — the Lord has twelve apostles, the Queen of Heaven twelve
virgins, so I have twelve of you, and as the Lord chose the martyr
Catherine for his bride, so of the twelve I have chosen Maria for mine in
the life to come, and there she will be eldest over you; and he said her
relics would in time rest openly in the monastery, for she had so pleased
the Lord as to be granted incorruption — see, mother, how important
obedience is: Maria, silent as she was, broke my command a little out of
joy, loving the monastery, and told a little, and for that alone, when her
relics are uncovered, her lips alone will be found decayed. Her sister
Praskovya was for a time head of the Mill community, and in the troubled
year 1862 took up the fool’s life, blessed for it by Seraphim — you, my
joy, are above me — saw the Mother of God with him and was three times
commanded to set right the affairs of her monastery and rebuke wrong,
which she fearlessly did, the bishop first, foretelling the restoration
of justice, and died in peace on 1 (14) June 1862, the Ascension, after
unction, communion and the prayers for the departing; their brother John
died a monk of Sarov, its gate-keeper, who as a lay peasant had often
worked for Seraphim and always repeated his words — whoever wrongs my
orphan girls will be greatly punished by the Lord, and whoever defends
and helps them will receive great mercy; happy is everyone who spends a
day and a night with poor Seraphim at Diveyevo, for the Mother of God
visits Diveyevo every day. Three of his daughters entered Diveyevo, one
of them, Helen, marrying Seraphim’s spiritual friend Motovilov and
becoming the monastery’s benefactress and “great lady”, as Seraphim had
called her as a child, the only person at his burial in 1833 who lived to
his glorification in 1903; she died at Diveyevo in 1910, secretly
tonsured before death, and many Milyukov women were sisters of the
monastery until its closing in 1927. After the monastery’s revival the
Lord marked her day wonderfully: the Transfiguration cathedral, built in
1917, never consecrated and ruined under the Soviets, was returned in
1991 and restored until 1998, and its consecration fell, unplanned, on
the day of her blessed death. A portrait of her painted by the sisters
right after her death was kept until the monastery’s dispersal in 1927,
and Capitolina Lyashevskaya, later the nun Maria, painted a life-icon with
scenes of her carrying bricks up the rising Nativity church, Seraphim
giving her the schema, Seraphim praying with her and Praskovya with lit
candles for Diveyevo, her soul rising to God’s throne, the Queen of Heaven
and the schema-nun in the vision in church, and the three holy graves;
the portrait’s whereabouts are unknown and the icon is abroad. In 2000 she
was numbered among the locally venerated saints of the Nizhny Novgorod
diocese, and her relics rest in the church of the Nativity of the
Theotokos at the Seraphim-Diveyevo monastery; the Russian calendar keeps
her on 21 August (Julian) and in the synaxes of the saints of Diveyevo and
Nizhny Novgorod.
