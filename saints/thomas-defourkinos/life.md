# Thomas Defourkinos

saint.gr's calendar for 10 December keeps the venerable Thomas Defourkinos, who lived in the ninth century and whose country lay at the foot of the mountain called Kyminaion. He was brought up as a Christian from childhood, the page says, and kept his faith and his purity through his growing up; and what the calendar tells of him it tells twice, once in a short notice of its own and once at length out of the Synaxaristes of Saint Nicodemus the Hagiorite.

Moved by a holy longing, Thomas visited many monasteries and carried away much from the great patterns of piety he found in them. Later he became a monk himself, and used to teach the Gospel with zeal, together with other monks, in towns and villages during the Lents. The bishop there valued his great virtues so highly that when a magnate of Constantinople built a monastery near the river Sangarios he appointed Thomas its abbot; and the choice was abundantly justified, for Thomas governed the house in good order and was a pattern of the monastic life to his fellow monks. He increased its means in particular, so as to help those who took refuge in it.

God counted him worthy of the gift of foresight too. Once, the page says, he wrote an answer to the emperor Leo the Wise without opening the letter that had been sent him. Later Thomas withdrew to some quiet hermitage, and there death found him in deep old age but in a continual spiritual flowering and vigour. The couplet over his name says that Thomas, having made God his unbreakable foundation, proved stronger than the machinery of the demons.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and agrees with the Greek page on the century: he reposed in the Lord in deep old age in the ninth century. Its four lines are mostly epithets — a great faster, a conqueror of demons, and a man who saw into what was hidden — and then one scene, which the Greek page has too and which this book tells more bluntly: the emperor Leo the Wise wrote him a letter, and he answered it without opening it.

Nothing was added to this folder's ranks on the strength of those epithets, and no second interval was built out of a century it already carried.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps him on the same menologion day as the Greek calendar and prints a reading of him. He was born in Bithynia, it says, loved the monastic life from his youth and went off to one of the monasteries round about. By the time a Byzantine dignitary founded a monastery on the river Sangarius he was already an experienced monk, and the brethren chose him as the superior of the new house; from there he withdrew into the desert, where he struggled a long while as a hermit and endured many of the devil’s snares in his solitude. The Lord glorified him with the gift of healings and of foresight.

Once the emperor Leo VI the Wise, whose years the page gives as 886 to 911, came to the monastery to ask his counsel. Not finding him there, the emperor sent his envoy on to him with a letter; and scarcely had the messenger come near the old man’s dwelling when he brought out to him a sealed answer that settled the emperor’s doubts. No account of his repose has survived, the page ends — which is why the pair of years the day line gives him is written here as a floruit and not as a death.
