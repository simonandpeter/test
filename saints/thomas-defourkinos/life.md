# Thomas Defourkinos

saint.gr's calendar for 10 December keeps the venerable Thomas Defourkinos, who lived in the ninth century and whose country lay at the foot of the mountain called Kyminaion. He was brought up as a Christian from childhood, the page says, and kept his faith and his purity through his growing up; and what the calendar tells of him it tells twice, once in a short notice of its own and once at length out of the Synaxaristes of Saint Nicodemus the Hagiorite.

Moved by a holy longing, Thomas visited many monasteries and carried away much from the great patterns of piety he found in them. Later he became a monk himself, and used to teach the Gospel with zeal, together with other monks, in towns and villages during the Lents. The bishop there valued his great virtues so highly that when a magnate of Constantinople built a monastery near the river Sangarios he appointed Thomas its abbot; and the choice was abundantly justified, for Thomas governed the house in good order and was a pattern of the monastic life to his fellow monks. He increased its means in particular, so as to help those who took refuge in it.

God counted him worthy of the gift of foresight too. Once, the page says, he wrote an answer to the emperor Leo the Wise without opening the letter that had been sent him. Later Thomas withdrew to some quiet hermitage, and there death found him in deep old age but in a continual spiritual flowering and vigour. The couplet over his name says that Thomas, having made God his unbreakable foundation, proved stronger than the machinery of the demons.
