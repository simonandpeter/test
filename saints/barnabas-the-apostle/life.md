# Barnabas

Barnabas was one of the seventy apostles. His name before his apostleship was Joseph, and he was born on the island of Cyprus of Levite parents of the Jewish nation — the tribe from which the great prophets of God, Moses, Aaron and Samuel, had come. His forebears had crossed to Cyprus because of the wars in Palestine. His parents were very rich, and had a village of their own near Jerusalem, well supplied with plantings and with the fruits of the earth and adorned with fine buildings, for their house was there; since the days when the prophet Isaiah wrote *Blessed is he who has his seed in Sion and his house in Jerusalem*, Jews living in distant lands had striven to have their houses in Jerusalem.

They named the boy Joseph and gave him to his letters; and when he came of age they sent him to Jerusalem to the famous Gamaliel, the renowned teacher of that time, to learn perfectly the understanding of the Hebrew books and the whole law of God. There was another pupil with Joseph under the same teacher, of his own age, named Saul, who was afterwards called Paul; and both went forward in understanding, in the wisdom of Scripture and in good works. Every day, morning and evening, Joseph went to the temple of Solomon and prayed earnestly, passing the days of his youth in fasting and in great self-restraint, keeping carefully the whole wisdom of his virginity, fleeing the company of disorderly young men and always taking heed to himself, learning in the law of the Lord day and night.

In that time the Lord Jesus Christ, thirty years after his incarnation, began to show himself to the world and went up from Galilee to Jerusalem, teaching in the temple and working wonders. The young Joseph, seeing Christ and hearing the teachings that came from his most holy mouth, was humbled in heart and wondered at the wonders done by him; he saw him heal the paralytic at the pool with a word, and, seeing other wonderful works of his, was set alight with the love of his heart towards him. Coming near, he threw himself at the Saviour's feet and begged to be blessed and received as a disciple; and the Lord, who sees the secrets of men's hearts, blessed him with love and did not forbid him to follow.

He ran first to the house of his aunt, Mary the mother of John who was afterwards called Mark, and said to her, "Come and see him whom our fathers longed to see." The Lord, seeing that woman's faith, went into her house and blessed her and all who were in it, and was entertained by her with honour; and from that time, whenever the Lord went up to Jerusalem, he lodged with his disciples in Mary's house. When the Lord chose seventy others, besides the twelve, to send them two by two before his face into every city and place, this Joseph was numbered among them from the beginning, and was named Barnabas by the apostles, that is, "son of consolation" — as the sons of Zebedee were called sons of thunder, so he was so named because his apostolic labours were to bring much joy to the elect of God.

After the Lord's ascension the apostles lived in common at Jerusalem, as is written of them: the people who had believed were of one soul and one heart, and none said that anything of his goods was his own, but all things were common; and as many as were owners of villages and of houses sold them and brought the price and laid it at the apostles' feet. Then Joseph, whom the apostles called Barnabas, sold that village near Jerusalem which had come to him from his parents, brought the price and laid it before the apostles' feet, keeping back nothing; for he wished to grow rich in God, in whom he did grow rich, as it is witnessed of him that he was a good man and full of the Holy Spirit and of faith.

He often met with Saul and disputed with him out of the holy Scripture about the Lord Christ, wishing to bring him to the holy faith; but Saul, a great zealot for the traditions of the fathers, mocked Barnabas as a man deceived and blasphemed against Christ. After the Jews had killed the holy protomartyr Stephen, Saul began to persecute the Church, going into the houses of the faithful and seizing men and women and throwing them into prison; and Barnabas wept for him and prayed to God, stretching out his pure hands, that the eyes of Saul's soul might be lit up and that he might know the truth, for he longed to have in the Christian faith this friend who had been his companion at Gamaliel's teaching.

Those prayers and tears were not in vain. When the time of the divine mercy came, Saul turned to Christ, called by the Lord's voice as he went to Damascus; the wolf was changed into a sheep and Christ's blasphemer began to magnify his name. When Saul returned to Jerusalem and tried to join himself to the disciples of Christ, all were afraid of him and did not believe that he too was a disciple; but Barnabas met him and said, "O Saul, how long wilt thou be the blasphemer of the great name of Jesus Christ and the persecutor of his faithful servants?" Saul fell at his feet in tears, confessing Christ the only-begotten Son of God, of one essence with the Father and without beginning; and Barnabas wondered and wept for joy, embraced and kissed him, and took him by the hand and led him to the apostles, saying: "Behold, he who persecuted us is now ours; he who set himself against us now obeys our Lord. Behold, I set before you this gentle lamb, who was a cruel beast."

When the faith began to spread at Antioch in Syria, the apostles sent Barnabas there to see those who were there and to strengthen the newly believing; and seeing the grace of God he rejoiced and consoled them all with the word of the Lord. Seeing the disciples multiplying daily while the teachers were few, he left Antioch for a time and went to Tarsus to look for his friend Saul, and finding him brought him to Antioch, where both laboured a whole year at turning human souls to Christ, and where the disciples were first called Christians. Afterwards, when a great famine came according to the prophecy of Agabus, who was himself one of the seventy, the faithful of Antioch gathered much alms and sent it to the elders by the hand of Barnabas and Saul.

The two went down first to Seleucia, then sailed to Cyprus and to Salamis, and went through the whole island as far as Paphos preaching the word of God, having with them as their servant John, the son of Mary, surnamed Mark; at Paphos they found the Jewish sorcerer and false prophet Elymas sitting beside the proconsul Sergius, a wise man. Crossing Perga they came to Antioch in Pisidia, and being driven out from there shook the dust from their feet and went to Iconium, where Jews and pagans wished to stone them; and fleeing to the cities of Lycaonia, to Lystra and Derbe, they set on his feet and made whole a man lame from his mother's womb who had never walked, at which the people, thinking them gods, wished to sacrifice to them and called Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes.

Barnabas afterwards came to Cyprus, his own country, and suffered many labours there until a great people had turned to Christ; and when the holy faith had increased in Cyprus he went to Rome, where, as some tell, he first preached Christ, and then founded and established the see of the bishopric at Milan. Then he returned again to Cyprus, and while he was preaching Christ in the city of Salamis certain Jews came from Syria who set themselves against him and stirred up the people, saying that all he said was against God and the law of Moses, and blasphemed his honoured name with many insults.

Barnabas had with him the Gospel of Matthew, written by his own hand, and he charged Mark to bury it with him. Then, giving Mark his kinsman a last kiss, he went alone to the Jewish assembly; and when he began to tell them of Christ out of the books of the prophets, the Jews who had come from Syria rose up against him, and stirring up others they laid their murderous hands on him, brought him out of the city on the western side and there stoned him to death; then they lit a fire and threw the body of the holy apostle into it. Mark and a few other brethren went secretly and found the apostle's body whole and unharmed by the fire, and buried it in a cave about five stadia from the city, laying the holy Gospel on his breast as he had commanded; then Mark went to seek Paul, and finding him at Ephesus told him of Barnabas's end, and Paul wept for him and kept Mark beside him.

After the killing the Jews of Salamis raised a great persecution against the Christians, and from that time the place where the relics lay was not known. But after many years God was pleased to glorify that place, and great wonders began to be done there; a sick man stopping there by chance was made whole, and then another, and others hearing of it went on purpose. At last the apostle appeared in a vision to Anthimus, archbishop of Cyprus, named himself — "I am Barnabas, the disciple of our Lord Jesus Christ, who was sent by the Holy Spirit together with the chosen vessel, the holy apostle Paul, to preach the word among the nations" — and gave him for a sign the place five stadia west of the city which the people called "the place of health".

They dug under the tree, as the apostle had said, and found a cave stopped with stones; and taking away the stones they saw the coffin and smelt a great and most pleasant fragrance, and uncovering it found the relics of the holy apostle whole and untouched by corruption, and the Gospel laid on his breast. Many wonders were done then. Anthimus, not daring to move the relics from that place, sealed the coffin with lead, set an order of spiritual service there for the customary psalmody by day and by night, and went himself to Constantinople, where he stood in the council and answered his opponents as the apostle had taught him in the vision, and told the emperor and the patriarch and all the people of the finding.

The emperor Zeno rejoiced that so priceless a spiritual treasure had been found in the days of his reign, and at once confirmed that the island of Cyprus should not be under the rule of the patriarch of Antioch but should be governed freely by its own archbishop, who was to be appointed by its own bishops; and this freedom was given to Cyprus for the sake of the apostle's relics, and the first see of Cyprus was called an apostolic see, as the patriarchal sees were. The emperor asked for the Gospel found on the apostle's breast, and adorned it with gold and precious stones and set it in the imperial church of his palace, and gave the archbishop much gold to build a fair church in the place where the relics were found; and the archbishop, having built it, laid the relics in the holy altar on the right-hand side, and appointed the feast of the holy apostle Barnabas on the eleventh day of June, the day on which his relics were found.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 11 June, and saint.gr prints him there together with the apostle [Bartholomew](/saints/bartholomew-the-apostle), whose own principal feast the site also puts on that day. Its account gives no year. It says that Barnabas — who was also called Joses — was a Jewish Levite from Cyprus, and that in the days of the apostles he lived in Jerusalem; that being the chief co-worker of the apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), whom the page sends the reader to find on 29 Ιουνίου, he preached the Gospel of Christ in Jerusalem, in Rome, in Alexandria and in his native Cyprus. There he was stoned by the Jews and the idolaters, and afterwards they burned him, at Salamis in Cyprus. His holy relics were gathered up by the apostle and evangelist [Mark](/saints/mark-the-evangelist), whom the page places on 25 Απριλίου, and laid inside a cave; and an old tradition, the page adds, holds that Barnabas was buried with the Gospel according to Matthew, which he had copied with his own hands and had tried to make known «πάσει τη κτίσει», to the whole creation. He was, it says, a great and glorious figure of the Church, marked out for the ardour of his faith and for his gift in preaching the divine word; and both apostles served in the best way the fulfilment of the Lord's prophecy that the Gospel must first be preached among all nations.
