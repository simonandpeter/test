# Tryphon of Vyatka

saint.gr's calendar for 8 Οκτωβρίου names Tryphon of Vyatka and then says, in one sentence, that nothing is known: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Ρώσου Αγίου» — we have no details of the life of the Russian saint. The head of the page writes his dates as «(; - 1612)», with the year of his birth unknown, and that year of death is the whole of what the site records about him.

The page names no monastery, no office and no manner of death, and prints no hymn. The only other saint it sets beside him is Prokopios of Vyatka, the fool for Christ, whose page it links as a neighbour; saint.gr says nothing that joins the two beyond the city, so nothing of Prokopios is read into this folder. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.

saint.gr gave this folder a name, a day and the year 1612. Everything else on it is the Russian calendar's 8 October, «Прп. Трифона , архим. Вятского, чудотворца (1612)», and the two lives its index page reaches. He came of devout parents in Arkhangelsk province — the second life names the village, Malaya Nemnyushka in the Pinega district. When his parents wanted to marry him he left home in secret, feeling from his youth a calling to the monastic life, and went to Veliky Ustyug, where he lodged with a parish priest and kept strict fast and prayer. He lived afterwards in the little town of Orlets beside a church, bearing cold and hunger, and from there went to the Pyskor monastery on the river Kama, where he was tonsured by the abbot Barlaam.

The twenty-two-year-old monk missed no service and carried the heavy obedience of the bakehouse. When he fell gravely ill [Nicholas of Myra](/saints/nicholas-of-myra) appeared to him, healed him, and strengthened him in his struggle; the second life adds his guardian angel to the vision and says that from then on he laboured still harder and received the gift of wonderworking from God. The two lives give different reasons for his leaving: the first says he went in search of solitude, the second that some of the brethren envied his fame and that he withdrew to avoid discord.

He settled at the mouth of the river Mulyanka, on the place where the city of Perm now stands, and there turned the pagan Ostyaks and Voguls to Christianity; the second life says he destroyed a fir tree they honoured as a god. Then he went to the river Chusovaya and founded a monastery there in honour of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos. In 1580 he came to the town of Khlynov in Vyatka province, founded a Dormition monastery there too, and was made archimandrite. He wore a hair shirt and heavy chains on his body, and gave all his strength to bringing the light of the faith to those who had lost their way. The second life says he travelled to Moscow several times on the business of his houses, and that at Kazan, in conversation with the metropolitan [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-moscow), he foretold him the patriarchate and a martyr's death.

Before his end he wrote a testament to the brethren, and the first life prints it. He calls himself coarse and worse than all, says that God and his most pure Mother allowed so poor a man to have charge of their house, and begs them for God's sake to keep spiritual love among themselves, because without it no virtue is complete before God. He sets the Lord's word to the disciples that they love one another beside the Apostle's that they bear one another's burdens; he tells them not to judge one another before God, in church or in cell, alone or among the brethren; to say their cell prayers in fear, and on no account to miss the singing in church — to run to God's church for the spiritual singing even if there is work to do. Give God's to God first, he says, and then do the rest. He went to the Lord in deep old age in 1612 and was buried in the Vyatka house he had founded.
