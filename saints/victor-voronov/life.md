# Victor (Voronov)

He is the third of the six presbyters the Сретенский monastery’s calendar names on one line for the Julian 16 November: «Сщмчч. Иоанна , Николая , Виктора , Василия , Макария и Михаила пресвитеров (1937).» Victor Ivanovich Voronov was born on 10 March 1889 in the town of Vyshny Volochok in the province of Tver, and in 1923 he was ordained priest to one of the churches of that town. In the persecution at the end of the twenties he was arrested and sentenced to five years’ imprisonment for not paying his tax; he appealed, and because he was poor to an extreme and in fact owned nothing at all, the sentence was quashed.

In 1930 he went to serve in the church of the village of Zaborovye in the Yesenovsky district, and moved there with his wife Alexandra Fyodorovna and their two sons, five years old and three. In 1936 he was arrested again and charged with keeping a record of the births and deaths of his parishioners; that time he was fined. On 15 November 1937 he was arrested once more and shut in the prison of Vyshny Volochok, and the investigator questioned him the day after the arrest.

He said when he had come to the district and that he had served as a priest from 1923 until the day of his arrest. Told the investigation had material that he had carried on counter-revolutionary agitation against the existing order, he said he had not and would not admit it. Told twice over that there was material and witnesses to an attempted anti-Soviet demonstration in 1937, he said twice that there had been no such attempt on his part. Told he had spread provocative rumours of a war soon to come and the perishing of the Soviet power, he denied that too.

The last question was whether he admitted going round the houses performing rites without the village soviet’s permission. He answered that to perform rites in the houses of the faithful he did not consider the village soviet’s permission to be required. On 27 November the troika of the НКВД sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 29 November 1937. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the jubilee council of bishops of August 2000, for the veneration of the whole church.
