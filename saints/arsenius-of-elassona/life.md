# Arsenius of Elassona

saint.gr says Arsenius was born in Eurytania in the year 1548, of devout and God-loving parents, Theodore and Christodoule. His writings, his knowledge of languages and his years as a teacher show that he was very well educated; where he learnt his letters, the page says outright, is not known to us. In 1574 he was elected bishop of «Δημονίκου και Ελασσώνος» and succeeded Damaskinos II, who had held the see from 1570; in the same year he received the visit of the ecumenical patriarch Jeremias II the Tranos, who was making a tour of Roumeli and the Peloponnese and was lodged at the monastery of the Olympiotissa.

That a secret school was running at the Olympiotissa from about 1580 makes the page think he took a lively interest in the elementary schooling of the young of his diocese: in agreement and in co-operation with the abbot and the monks he organised that school, and urged and encouraged the Greek children of Elassona who had a zeal for letters to come up to the monastery and learn there to read and write upon the sacred texts.

In 1588 he went with Hierotheos, metropolitan of Monemvasia, to accompany Jeremias II to Moscow, where on 26 January 1589 they proclaimed and installed the metropolitan Job as patriarch of Vladimir, Moscow, all Russia and all the northern lands. Arsenius described the ceremony and the celebrations of Job's enthronement in verses in the vernacular, under the title «Κόποι και διατριβή του ταπεινού Αρχιεπισκόπου Αρσενίου», the labours and the sojourn of the humble archbishop Arsenius. He never went back to the diocese of Elassona, but stayed in Russia for good, and there fell asleep in peace in the year 1625.

Of his life and work in Russia, the page says, it is known that he taught in the Greek community school of Lviv in Ruthenia, where there was a numerous and flourishing Greek colony, mostly of merchants, among them the Cretan wine merchant Constantine Korniaktos. Later he was administrator of the province of Suzdal, from where he corresponded often with Meletios Pigas, patriarch of Alexandria. In all those years in Russia he did not forget the country he came from or the monasteries where he had probably passed his youth: he gave many books, icons and sacred vessels to the monastery of Tatarna and to the Meteora. His body was buried in the metropolitan church of Suzdal, and its captions say it lies today in a shrine inside that church.
