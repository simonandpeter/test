# Panagis Basias

saint.gr says he was born at Lixouri in Kephallonia in 1801, the son of devout and notable parents, Michael Typaldos-Basias and Reggina Dellaporta. He learned Italian, French and Latin and was trained in philosophy and theology; while still young he was set apart as a reader, and at the beginning of his career he was appointed a schoolmaster of letters and exercised the office of teacher. But taking fire from the radical preaching of Kosmas Flamiatos and Eusebios Panas, who held that the English protectors of the Seven Islands were in truth tyrants plotting against the Orthodox mind of the islanders, he left the public school and gave lessons at home instead, keeping on with his mission.

At twenty, after his father's death, having a native bent for it and moved by the person of the island's patron the great ascetic Saint Gerasimos and of his neighbour, another great ascetic, Saint Anthimos, he left everything and went to Xeroskopelo, a small island in the lower Livatho of Vlachernai and a place to which the English exiled clergy; the famous Zakynthian cleric Nikolaos Kantounis was in exile there in those days. He did not stay long, bent by the entreaties of his widowed mother and his unprotected sister, and so he came back into the world a monk; but the whole of his life, the page says, proved a continual ascetic contest and a consistent keeping-watch over the monastic ideas and resolutions he had made.

In 1836 he was ordained deacon and presbyter under the name Paisios by Parthenios Makris, archbishop of Kephallonia. He sought no parish place; he usually served in the chapel of Saint Spyridon at Platys Gialos, where crowds of the faithful gathered to hear his warm preaching. He was, the page says, the personification of almsgiving and a warm support of the weak, and he received from God the gift of prophecy, foretelling what was to come upon persons, families and the community more widely, as the submission for his canonisation puts it.

In 1867 his house was thrown down by the terrible earthquakes of Palliki, and from then on he was lodged in the house of his cousin John Geroulanos, father of the notable surgeon Marinos Geroulanos. Because of the fame that spread from his many wonders, and to avoid the dreadful reef of the spiritual life, accursed self-regard, he took refuge in the known method of the great ascetics and feigned madness, and so he is counted in the company of the saints who were fools for Christ.

For five years he was worn down bedridden, and sick as he was he went on blessing, making peace, guiding and counselling the Christians who visited him night and day. There he received the visit of the new archbishop Germanos Kalligas, to whom he foretold his raising to the archiepiscopal throne of Athens. The venerable Panagis fell asleep in 1888, and at the public funeral three days later Germanos Kalligas, metropolitan of Kephallonia, pronounced a famous eulogy. His relics were translated on 6 June 1976, and he was enrolled among the saints by the Ecumenical Patriarchate on 4 February 1986; they are kept in a silver case in the church of Saint Spyridon at Lixouri, where his tomb is.
