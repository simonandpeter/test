# Paul (Moiseev)

Paul, in the world Ivan Yakovlevich Moiseev, was born on 16 February 1849 in the village of Dresvishchi in the Gryazovets district of Vologda province, to a peasant family. He taught himself to read from the Psalter, loved the lives of the saints and kept away from village games. He chose monastic life past thirty: on 19 March 1880 he became a novice of the Pavlo-Obnorsky monastery near his birthplace, took the habit there on 9 October with the name Paul, and was ordained hierodeacon on 16 November and hieromonk on 24 June 1881. In 1887 he governed that monastery for half a year and was its treasurer.

From 1890 he was at Ustyug, first as superior of the Nikolo-Prilutsky monastery and then as dean of the monasteries of the Veliky Ustyug vicariate; Bishop Gabriel of Veliky Ustyug made him hegumen on 24 June 1898, and on 13 March 1902 he became superior of the Koryazhma monastery of St Nicholas, where he served until his death and was a firm support to a brotherhood beset by disorder. The decree separating the Church from the state troubled him, and the retired archimandrite [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-sobolev) came from Solvychegodsk to talk it over with him.

After October 1917 a commandant sent by the new authorities lived in the Koryazhma monastery. On 23 September 1918 an illiterate villager told the Solvychegodsk Cheka that suspicious people were hiding in the monastery, and on 30 September the military commissar and members of the commission came there, demanded that its administration hand over every hiding place and the money, and recorded the refusal as resistance to the Soviet power. The commandant wrote all the answers on one sheet: Paul said no to every question and that nothing more was in the monastery; [Nicodemus](/saints/nicodemus-shchapkov) admitted nothing; [Seraphim](/saints/seraphim-kulakov) said he had been in charge of nothing; and [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-sobolev) said he took no part in the monastery’s affairs and lived apart.

The commission counted Seraphim, who had no part in the monastery’s business, and the retired Theodosius among its administration, and ordered the four shot as ringleaders of a hostile band. No money was found, and without waiting for the sentence to be confirmed they were shot at two in the afternoon of 30 September 1918, on a deserted island in the Vychegda on the road from the monastery: Theodosius in his seventy-seventh year, Paul in his seventieth, Nicodemus at sixty-five and Seraphim at forty-five.

The Russian calendar keeps the four on 17 September old style.
