# Phlegon (Pongilsky)

Phlegon was born on 28 March 1871 in the village of Karyaevo in the Uglich district of the Yaroslavl province, the son of a priest, Nicholas Pongilsky. He finished the Yaroslavl theological seminary and became a teacher in the parish school at Smolenskoye, in Krasny Bor in the Yaroslavl district. In 1895 he was ordained priest to the church at Vasilyevskoye in Yukhti in the Uglich district, and two years later was moved to the church at Zhelezny Borok in the Yaroslavl district.

In 1905 he was transferred to the church of Saint Nicholas in Yaroslavl and began to teach the Law of God in several of the city's schools. During the First World War he served as priest to the 663rd infantry regiment and carried out a pastor's duties in the military hospitals. His zeal made him widely known in Yaroslavl, and the parishioners of the church of the great martyr [Demetrius of Thessalonica](/saints/demetrius-the-myrrhstreamer) asked Archbishop [Agathangel (Preobrazhensky)](/saints/agathangel-preobrazhensky) to appoint him to them; the bishop granted their request and from 1915 he served in that church. That same year he was given a gold pectoral cross, and some years later he was raised archpriest and made one of the deans of the city.

He was arrested on 7 September 1929, when the persecution grew and several dozen clergy and laypeople of the Yaroslavl diocese were taken at once. The occasion was the correspondence of the Yaroslavl clergy about Metropolitan Sergius's declaration, and the conduct of the archbishop Sergius had set over Yaroslavl, Paul (Borisovsky), who had pointedly served on New Year's eve by the new civil calendar — something not then done in that diocese, and which the faithful read as an intention to go the way of the Renovationists in pleasing the godless.

As a dean long trusted by the Yaroslavl bishops, it was through him that the exiled vicar of the diocese, Bishop [Benjamin (Voskresensky)](/saints/benjamin-voskresensky) of Tutayev, kept up his correspondence with Archbishop Paul. The charge was anti-Soviet activity, which amounted to his having discussed with other priests the life of the Church and her position in a state openly set on destroying her. Questioned, he said he did not admit the charge: he was a dean, but he had taken part in no illegal meetings, and the letters he had received from Bishop Benjamin were addressed to him only to be passed on.

On 3 January 1930 the Collegium of the OGPU sentenced thirty-three accused clergy and laypeople of the Yaroslavl and Ivanovo dioceses, and Phlegon was given three years' exile to the Northern Krai. He was released on 10 November 1932. Wanting to be near his family, who were then living at Novogireyevo outside Moscow, but barred as a former political prisoner from settling close to the city, he took a house on the boundary of the Moscow and Vladimir regions and found work on the railway.

In 1936 he was a weighman at Khrapunovo station in the Noginsk district and was dismissed at the demand of the passport office as a man who had served a sentence. He settled at Petushki, in one flat with priests banished beyond the hundred-kilometre zone, and took work as a watchman at a railway fuel yard. He was arrested again on 4 December 1937 and shut in the Taganka prison in Moscow, and was questioned once, the day of his arrest and no other.

The investigator put it to him that he had voiced counter-revolutionary views in September and again in October of that year, and that his activity was proved by a number of witnesses. He answered each time that he had carried on no counter-revolutionary agitation, that he had voiced no such views and could not have voiced them, and that he did not admit himself guilty. The investigation was closed the day after his arrest, and on 9 December 1937 an NKVD troika gave him ten years in a corrective labour camp.

Archpriest Phlegon Pongilsky died on 23 April 1938 in one of the camps of the Novosibirsk region and was buried in a grave nobody marked. The Sretensky calendar's own line for the day gives none of this: it reads «Сщмч. Флегонта пресвитера (1938).», and the index entry behind it adds the surname, the rank of archpriest, and one other day, the synaxis of the new martyrs. The day page prints three hymns and not one of them under his name.
