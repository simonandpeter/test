# Theodora, companion of Didymus

saint.gr says Theodora and Didymus took the martyr's crown in the fiercest persecution the Church knew, under Diocletian. At Alexandria Theodora was arrested by the eparch Eustratius; and because she confessed her faith in Christ courageously, they beat her and put her in prison. The same was done again some days later, and in vain: the Christian virgin stayed unshaken in her confession, and so brought the governor's raw obstinacy to despair.

Then, to take his revenge on the modest virgin, he shut her up in a brothel so that her body should be defiled. As soon as [Didymus](/saints/didymus-companion-of-theodora), a man of rank at Alexandria, heard of it, he decided to take the risk of freeing Theodora from the shame that threatened her. He put on his uniform, went to the brothel and asked to see Theodora privately; and taking the advantage of the darkness, he dressed her in his own clothes and so made her escape easy.

When Eustratius learnt what had happened he boiled with rage, and ordered Didymus beheaded and his body afterwards thrown into the fire. But Theodora would not take her own safety selfishly. She presented herself to the eparch and reproached him sternly for the killing of Didymus; and he, in his anger, ordered her thrown into the flames as well. The page gives no year, names no relics and prints no hymn under either of them.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her and Didymus on the Julian 27 May, not on the 5 April the Greek calendar keeps them, and it is the first book read for them to give a year. In the days of the wicked emperor Maximian, it says — where the Greek page puts the martyrdom under Diocletian — there was at Alexandria a virgin Theodora, of noble birth and upbringing. As a Christian she was brought to trial before the heathen, and after long examinations for the faith of Christ the tormenting ruler ordered her thrown into a brothel.

Saint Theodora prayed to God earnestly to save her from defilement; and while she was praying, a soldier named [Didymus](/saints/didymus-companion-of-theodora) came in to her and told her that he too was a servant of Christ. He dressed her in his soldier’s clothes and himself in her woman’s clothes, and let her go out, and stayed behind in the brothel himself. He was taken afterwards and brought before the judge, confessed that he was a Christian and that he had saved Theodora, and said that he was ready to die for Christ.

When he was led out to execution Theodora came running to him and cried: though you saved my honour, I did not ask you to save me from death; yield me the martyr’s death. Beloved sister, Saint Didymus said to her, do not hinder me from dying for Christ and washing my sins away with my blood. Hearing the quarrel, the heathen condemned them both; both were beheaded and their bodies burned in the fire. They suffered honourably, the Prologue says, in the year 304 at Alexandria. The Greek page has her burnt alive after reproaching the eparch, and the two accounts are left as two.
