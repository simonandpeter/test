# John Kalfas

saint.gr says John was born and brought up in Galata, and worked as a joiner's foreman — a kalfas, which is what the calendar calls him — in the Sultan's palace, doing work at the same time for other Turkish officials. He once took a Turkish boy into his service, the nephew of an Agha from Anatolia, to teach him the trade; and in some conversation with him John spoke ill of the Muslim religion and praised the Christian one.

The boy denounced his master for proselytising him. John was seized and taken, beaten as he went, before the judge, on a charge of insulting the Muslim religion; he was pitilessly whipped and thrown into prison. For all the persistent efforts of the Turks he went on confessing Christ steadily. By the Vizier's order he was handed after many tortures to the eparch, who took him to the place of execution near the Auction House and beheaded him there on 26 February 1575, a Sunday.

The page gives him no year of birth — its heading leaves the first of his two dates a question mark — and names no relics; its couplet tells the reader not to count John's wounds but rather the dwellings the heaven within holds, and it prints no hymn. What it links beside him is the synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered after the taking of Constantinople, which is a company and not a person and is not kept here.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him five sentences and glosses his by-name: he was born at Galata in Constantinople and was a builder, a mason, by trade, which is what Kalfa means. His earnest confession of the Christian faith put him wrong with the Turks, who began forcing him to turn Muslim, and he answered bravely that he would not deny his sweet Jesus Christ — in him he believed, him he served, him he confessed.

After heavy tortures they beheaded him, the Prologue says, on 26 February 1575 at Constantinople, which is the day and the year the folder already holds.
