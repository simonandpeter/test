# Blaise the Cowherd

Blaise came from Caesarea in Cappadocia and was born of rich and charitable parents. The family's business took him away from Caesarea for a while, and when the persecution of the Christians came the idolaters hunted for him as a Christian and could not find him. His mother, who longed for him to be safe, advised him to take the road of flight; but he refused. So many others were bearing witness — why should he run away?

So he came forward willingly and gave himself up to his pursuers, whom he then entertained and waited on as though they had been his benefactors. They arrested him, whipped him, and threw him into a boiling cauldron, where he stayed five days and took no hurt at all; the grace of God kept him whole. Many soldiers who saw the wonder believed in Christ, and he baptised them with the water of the cauldron.

When the governor heard of it he sent other soldiers to get Blaise out of the cauldron, and they too believed when they saw it. Then the governor came himself to look at the saint in the boiled water, and, thinking that it must have cooled, asked for some of it to wash his eyes. The moment he did so he went blind and died on the spot. Blaise was left free, visited his family, and then gave up his soul to God; and those who happened to be there at his end saw his blessed soul come out of his mouth like a white and shining dove and fly up to heaven.
