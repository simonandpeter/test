# Mercurius of Smolensk

saint.gr's calendar for 24 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Μερκούριος «ὁ ἐν Σμολένσκῃ»» — Mercurius the one at Smolensk — and then says the whole of what it has in a single sentence: «Δεν υπάρχουν λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Αγίου», there are no details of the life of the saint. The city in his epithet is everything the calendar gives.

Besides the line the page prints only its usual gloss on the name, from the Latin Mercurius, the one like Hermes. It sets no couplet over him; it gives him no country, no century, no year, no rank beyond the holy, no manner of death, no companions, no relics and no hymn. The same listing keeps a second Mercurius of that day, the faster of the Lavra of the Caves, on his own page.

**The Russian calendar has the life saint.gr said in words it did not have**, and keeps him on the same menologion number, 24 ноября ст. ст. It hangs two readings behind his name and they agree in nearly everything. He was a soldier of princely descent; the second reading makes him a Slav, probably from Moravia, who left his own country for Rus out of zeal for the true faith and served in the army of the prince of Smolensk. Brought up in Orthodoxy, he kept an ascetic life in secret: a strict faster, a virgin, and every night, unknown to anyone, he sang thanksgiving to the Lord.

In 1239 the Tatar host of Batu, which had already taken many Russian cities, came up and stood twenty-five versts from Smolensk at Долгомостье, threatening the city and its holy things. A sacristan praying at night in the cathedral before the wonderworking image of the Mother of God heard a voice from the icon telling him to go and find the soldier and say: *Mercurius, go out to the battle, the Lady calls you.* The soldier came to the cathedral and heard the voice of the most pure Virgin himself, sending him out against the enemy and promising him help from heaven, the crown of victory and everlasting blessedness — which was the very thing he had long been asking for.

That same night he went to the Tatar camp. The second reading has him fight and kill the giant who led the host before taking on the host itself; both have him call on the name of the Lord and of the Theotokos and destroy a multitude, while the Tatars saw with horror that lightning-like men and a radiant Woman were fighting on his side, and fled. He was killed in the fighting — by the son of the giant he had killed, the second reading says. The people of Smolensk buried him with honour in the cathedral of the Dormition. He appeared afterwards to the same sacristan and bade him hang his weapons over the tomb, promising the city help in all its sorrows and wars; his sandals are kept in the cathedral still.
