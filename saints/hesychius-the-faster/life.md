# Hesychius the Faster

The Ohrid Prologue says Hesychius was born near Bursa in the eighth century, and that he withdrew to a mountain called Maionis, which had an ill name for apparitions of demons. There he made himself a hut and a little church in honour of the holy Apostle Andrew, and he fenced in a garden with his own hands and worked it, so that he could live by his own labour. By his prayer he worked wonders; and he foretold that after his death there would be a women's monastery on that spot.

A month before the end he was shown the day and the hour of his death. At midnight on the day he had foretold, people saw his hut lit up with an uncommon light, and when they came to it they found him dead. He was buried in the church of Saint Andrew, and later Theophylact, bishop of Amasea, carried him into the city of Amasea. He made a peaceful end and passed over into the kingdom of his Lord in the year 790.

That is the whole of the entry, and the Prologue is the only source read for him. It does not say how he came to the mountain, gives no name to the monastery he foretold, names no disciple, and prints no hymn.
