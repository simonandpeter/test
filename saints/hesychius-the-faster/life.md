# Hesychius the Faster

The Ohrid Prologue says Hesychius was born near Bursa in the eighth century, and that he withdrew to a mountain called Maionis, which had an ill name for apparitions of demons. There he made himself a hut and a little church in honour of the holy Apostle Andrew, and he fenced in a garden with his own hands and worked it, so that he could live by his own labour. By his prayer he worked wonders; and he foretold that after his death there would be a women's monastery on that spot.

A month before the end he was shown the day and the hour of his death. At midnight on the day he had foretold, people saw his hut lit up with an uncommon light, and when they came to it they found him dead. He was buried in the church of Saint Andrew, and later Theophylact, bishop of Amasea, carried him into the city of Amasea. He made a peaceful end and passed over into the kingdom of his Lord in the year 790.

That is the whole of the entry, and the Prologue is the only source read for him. It does not say how he came to the mountain, gives no name to the monastery he foretold, names no disciple, and prints no hymn.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, and this is the first full account of him the folder has held: it was written off the Охридски пролог alone, and its Romanian and Greek rows are still undocumented. He was born in the seaside town of Адриния in Bithynia and brought up from his youth in piety; he left his father's house and took up the ascetic life in a desert place on the mountain of Маионис. Threatened there by demons and by the wild beasts and the robbers who lived in that place, he wanted more solitude rather than less, and settled there for good: he built himself a cell, dug a garden, and lived on what his own labour grew.

After a time disciples began to gather to him, and at a spring of water found not far off in the valley he built a church in the name of the holy apostle Андрей Первозванный. The gift of wonders came to him while he still lived. A girl possessed by a demon was brought to him, and her parents fell at his feet and begged his prayers; he prayed for her and the demons went out of her. **And then he turned to the parents and prophesied that on the spot where their daughter had been healed a women's monastery would stand** — which the calendar says came to pass.

Thirty days before his end an angel appeared to him and foretold it, and he received the news with joy. Before his blessed repose he called his disciples and taught them a long while; and at midnight a heavenly light suddenly shone about his cell and the places round it, and with the words «В руце Твои, Господи, предаю дух мой» he went to the Lord. A women's monastery was afterwards built on the place of his labours, as he had foretold. **His relics were moved, and that is not his death**: buried in the church of the apostle he had raised, they were carried in 792 by Феофилакт, bishop of Amasea, into the city of Amasea in Asia Minor. The line's «ок. 790» is the year this folder already recorded for his repose, so no date moves, and the 792 of the translation is not entered as one.
