# Rabbulas of Samosata

saint.gr says Rabbulas was born at Samosata in Syria and lived under the emperors Zeno the Isaurian, whose reigns it gives as 474 to 475 and 476 to 491, Anastasius the First, 491 to 518, and Justinian the First, 527 to 565. He was educated by a teacher named Barypsabas, and besides the rest of his learning and formation he learned the Syriac tongue as well.

Then he became a monk and shut himself away in the mountains and the caves, to give himself to prayer and ascesis, and he lived as John the Baptist and the prophet Elijah had. Afterwards he came to Phoenicia, where he founded a coenobium, and later, with the help of the emperor Zeno and of John bishop of Beirut, he built a monastery that was raised into a missionary centre for the spreading of the word of God among the idolaters.

When the emperor Zeno died, Anastasius Dikoros succeeded him, and with the new sovereign's help Rabbulas built at Constantinople a new monastery, which is called the monastery of Rabbulas. When he had passed his eightieth year, in the first years of the reign of Justinian, he finished his life in peace, whispering the Lord's word: come to me, all you who labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Its couplet says that Rabbulas, having made the counsels of the demons ineffectual, went away from this world by the counsel of God. The page gives no year for his birth or his death and prints no hymn.
