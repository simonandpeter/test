# Basil the Confessor

He lived in the days of Leo the Isaurian, the fighter against the icons. Leaving the world and the things of the world he became a monk and lived the monastic life in good works, as was fitting; and when the honouring of the holy icons was thrown out he set himself firmly against those who fought them. For that they took him and tormented him much, but he did not submit and did not agree with their heresy, preaching the truth to the death, and having the divine [Procopius](/saints/procopius-of-decapolis) to help him.

Basil was torn with iron, as Procopius was, over the whole of his body and about the neck, and thrown into prison, where he lay bound a very long while, until the lawless emperor perished from the earth. When that tyrant died, Basil and Procopius and the other confessors were set free; and they lived in the same hermit labours as before, bringing many to the right faith and guiding them to a better life. After many years, and a life that pleased God, his blessed end came, and he went rejoicing, in prayers and thanksgiving, to the God he had loved from his youth.
