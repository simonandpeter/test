# Basil the Confessor

He lived in the days of Leo the Isaurian, the fighter against the icons. Leaving the world and the things of the world he became a monk and lived the monastic life in good works, as was fitting; and when the honouring of the holy icons was thrown out he set himself firmly against those who fought them. For that they took him and tormented him much, but he did not submit and did not agree with their heresy, preaching the truth to the death, and having the divine [Procopius](/saints/procopius-of-decapolis) to help him.

Basil was torn with iron, as Procopius was, over the whole of his body and about the neck, and thrown into prison, where he lay bound a very long while, until the lawless emperor perished from the earth. When that tyrant died, Basil and Procopius and the other confessors were set free; and they lived in the same hermit labours as before, bringing many to the right faith and guiding them to a better life. After many years, and a life that pleased God, his blessed end came, and he went rejoicing, in prayers and thanksgiving, to the God he had loved from his youth.

The page gives no year, and its troparion tab carries a Greek recording and no text.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and names his elder. saint.gr says Basil the Confessor lived and worked under the empire of the iconoclast Leo III the Isaurian, and that from an early age he left the life of the world to give himself to the teaching of the Gospel and to asceticism, living at first in a hermitage and feeding his spirit and his soul with the gifts of faith and love. He became the disciple and the obedient son of [Procopius the Decapolite](/saints/procopius-of-decapolis), whom the calendar keeps the day before. When circumstances called him, he answered with wonderful readiness and defended Orthodoxy with courage and outspokenness; he was harshly persecuted for his unbending resistance and his brave advocacy, and was imprisoned and put to many tortures.

When the tyrant Leo died, Basil was set free and went back to his hermitage to carry on his monastic labours. A great contender of the Church, the page says, he was continually in arms for the strengthening of the Orthodox faith, the enlightening of heretics, the settling of the faithful and the repentance of sinners; and so contending in holiness he fell asleep in peace. Its couplets say that Basil, carrying Christ in his soul, laid down his soul for the sake of Christ's shadow — the icon — and that on the eighth they hid Basil under the earth for an image's sake.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, the day after his teacher's, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him four sentences entirely in that teacher's company: the companion and fellow sufferer of [Procopius of Decapolis](/saints/procopius-of-decapolis), whom he followed faithfully in peace and in persecution. He bore many torments from the iconoclasts; and when the iconoclasts came to nothing, by God's providence, he went back with Procopius to their own monastery, where he struggled a long time in fasting and prayer and ended peacefully — in 747, by the Prologue's reckoning.
