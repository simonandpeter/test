# Kerykos of Aprus

saint.gr's calendar for 27 Μαρτίου names the venerable Kerykos «ὁ ἐν Ἄπρῳ», the one at Aprus, and the page behind the line is a single sentence: he practised ascesis in Thrace, near the city of Aprus, and fell asleep in peace. There is no year, no monastery, no teacher, no disciple, no wonder, no relic and no hymn. Aprus was a city of Thrace on the road between Constantinople and the Hellespont, and the epithet is how the calendar tells this Kerykos from every other.

What the page adds is a couplet addressed to him in the second person, which is unusual enough to be worth keeping: «Ἐγώ, Πάτερ Κήρικε, καὶ τεθνηκότα, Κἂν καρδία φέρειν σε, κἂν γλώττῃ θέλω» — I, Father Kerykos, would carry you even in death, whether in my heart or on my tongue. The couplet spells the name Κήρικε where the heading spells it Κήρυκος, and the page glosses it «ΚΗΡΥΚΟΣ (από την λέξη κήρυξ) = ο κήρυκας», from the word for a herald.

That gloss is about the word, not the man, and nothing else on the page is about him at all. The folder is deliberately thin: one sentence of life is what the source has, and the corpus keeps a quite different saint of a near-identical name, Cyricus the son of Julitta, whose spelling is Κύρικος and whose day is in July.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 27 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/27/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1267/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
