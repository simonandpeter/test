# Quadratus

Quadratus was one of the Seventy Apostles. He preached the word of God in Athens and in Magnesia, where he was also bishop, and the life the Romanian synaxarion keeps for him is built around one image: Sirach's morning star among the clouds. The clouds are the darkness of the pagan cities; Quadratus shone in it, overturned the unclean sacrifices, broke the idols, and emptied the temples by his prayer - and as the morning star brings the sun up behind it, so he brought the great light of the faith into souls that had sat in the shadow of death.

The darkness, the life says, always hates the light. Seeing their idols trodden down, the idolaters raised a persecution against him and would have stoned him as another Stephen; struck with the stones, he was kept alive by the grace of Christ. Then they bound him and set out to kill him by hunger, keeping bread from the man who had been feeding their own souls - and it is they, the life observes, who were starving, with the famine that is not of bread but of hearing the word of the Lord. Unable to kill by hunger a man fed from above, they put him at last to many torments, and he finished the contest of martyrdom in the reign of the emperor Hadrian.

His body was laid in Magnesia, and pours out healings on the infirm.
