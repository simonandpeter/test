# Euthymius of Dimitsana

This new martyr of Christ was born at Dimitsana in the Peloponnese, the son of Panagiotes and Maria, and was baptised Eleutherius; he was the youngest brother of George, Christos, John and Katerina. When he had finished school at Dimitsana he and John went to Constantinople to enter the Patriarchal Academy, and later to Iași in Romania, where their father and brothers had business. Before long Eleutherius decided to go to Mount Athos to become a monk.

The war between Russia and Turkey stopped him at Bucharest, where he lodged with the French consul and then with an employee of the Russian one, and there he gave himself to a life of pleasure and let the thought of monasticism go. When the fighting quieted he set out again for Athos, travelling with some Muslims; and on that road he left Orthodoxy for the religion of Islam, was circumcised, and took the name Reschid. Not long after, his conscience began to torment him for having forsaken Christ, and his companions, seeing the change in him, restricted his movements and watched him closely.

One day he was seen wearing a cross and was denounced to the master of the house, Rais Efendi, who did not punish him at once — which drew the others' envy — explaining that it was too early for Eleutherius to have given up all his Christian habits. When the household travelled to Adrianople, Eleutherius claimed to have letters for the metropolitan Cyril, who was serving vespers there and who sent another man to collect them; to that man he said he wanted Christian clothes, and the man grew suspicious and drove him away. Back in Constantinople, Rais Efendi tried to hold him with costly gifts, and he prayed God to help him escape.

At the first chance he fled and found a priest from the Peloponnese living near the Patriarchate, told him everything, and begged his help to get away; the priest, afraid of the consequences if they were caught, refused, and only gave him directions and let him go. With help from the Russian embassy, Eleutherius took ship for Mount Athos, and at the Great Lavra he was received back into the Church and tonsured a monk with the name Euthymius.

He read the New Martyrologion of Saint Nicodemus of the Holy Mountain, and, stirred by the examples in it, longed with his whole being to wash away the sin of his apostasy with the blood of martyrdom. He went to Constantinople with a monk named Gregory and arrived on 19 March 1814. On Palm Sunday he communed, put on Muslim dress and went to the house of the grand vizier, Rusud Pasha; holding palm leaves in his hand he declared himself an Orthodox Christian ready to die for Christ, spoke against the Muslim religion and trampled the turban from his own head, so that in the vizier's eyes he seemed drunk or mad.

He convinced the vizier that he was neither, and was thrown into a dark cell in chains. Brought out after some hours, he was coaxed with flattery and promises of goods to stay a Muslim, and answered boldly that their religion rests on lies, and that he would never deny Christ again though they tortured him and killed him. He was beaten and shut up again, and three hours later asked whether he had changed his mind or was still stubborn; and he said there is only one true faith, that of the Orthodox Christians, and how should he believe in Mohammed, their false prophet?

The vizier saw he could not be turned, and ordered him beheaded. When they moved to bind his hands he asked to receive death with his arms free, and they let him walk unbound to the place of execution; he went joyfully and without fear, the cross in his right hand and the palm branches in his left. There he turned to the east and prayed, thanking God for counting him worthy of martyrdom for His name, and prayed for his family and friends. Then he kissed the cross he held, knelt and bowed his head. The executioner struck hard with the sword and failed to take off his head, struck again, and, seeing that he could not, cut the martyr's throat with a knife.

He was killed at midday on 22 March 1814, in Constantinople. His head is kept in the Russian monastery of Saint Panteleimon on Mount Athos. Doxologia's own page for him carries the feast line «1 Mai», while its calendar for 22 martie — the day of his death — is where this folder's Romanian attestation was read. The 1 mai day has since been read: it names him, «Sfântul Cuvios Mucenic Eftimie», beside the other two new-martyrs of the Holy Mountain, and serves this same life, so it is a second Romanian commemoration; it is recorded in the note on his feast.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and follows the whole road twice over. saint.gr gives him his name in the world, Eleutherius, and a rich family of Dimitsana; he was schooled in that town and went on, with his brother John, to the Patriarchal Academy of Constantinople, and then to Iaşi in Romania where his father was living with his elder brothers George and Christos. He decided to come to the Holy Mountain to be tonsured, and being unable to do it for the conditions of the time went to Bucharest instead, where he stayed first with the French consul and then with a senior Russian official; later he attached himself to some Turks, and on the journey to Constantinople he denied Christ, was made a Muslim and was named Resit.

Immediately after his circumcision, the page says, remorse of conscience took him and he sought to come back to the faith of his fathers. He came to Constantinople, and from there, helped by the Russian embassy, reached the Holy Mountain, where at the Lavra he met the patriarch St Gregory V, confessed his sins to him and found protection and consolation from him. He went about many sketes and monasteries confessing his apostasy and living in prayer, asceticism and fasting, and was tonsured a monk with the name Euthymius.

Seen off by many monks of the Holy Mountain, he reached Galata in Constantinople on 19 March 1814 with a monk named Gregory. Having communed of the immaculate Mysteries on Palm Sunday he took off the monastic habit, dressed in Turkish clothes, made a prostration to the monk Gregory who had come with him, and set out on the road to martyrdom. He presented himself before the vizier Rusut Pasha and, having trodden the Turkish fez underfoot in front of him, confessed Jesus Christ true God and declared that he denied the religion of Mohammed. By the vizier's order he was thrown into prison and harshly tortured, and when he was brought before the ruler a second time he gave way neither to the flatteries nor to the threats; with a steady mind and a brave soul he went on confessing the name of the Lord, and so he was beheaded at Constantinople on 22 March 1814. His countrymen, to honour him, built a church at Dimitsana in his name together with that of the patriarch Gregory V.
