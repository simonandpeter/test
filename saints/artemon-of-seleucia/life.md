# Artemon of Seleucia

Artemon's country was Seleucia, the city of Pisidia, where he was born and raised and lived an honourable life in the days of the holy Apostles, who were then lighting the world with the good tidings of Christ. When the Apostle Paul came to that city, Artemon was a lamp among all its citizens, shining with good works, and it was not fitting that he should be hidden under a bushel.

Finding him established in the faith of Christ and full of divine wisdom, the Apostle Paul set him as shepherd and teacher of the people, ordaining him the first bishop in Seleucia of Pisidia. He guided the flock entrusted to him well and in a manner pleasing to God, and was a harbour of salvation to all, a provider for widows and the poor, a physician of souls and bodies, and a worker of miracles; and having spent all the days of his life well and in God's good pleasure, he ended it in deep old age.

doxologia.ro's calendar and life give him no year, and no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and tells the same beginning. saint.gr places him in the apostolic times, when the Gospel of Christ was being spread for the first time among the idolatrous societies: [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), the Apostle of the Nations, came to Seleucia of Pisidia, preached the word of God and drew chosen souls who became the first Church of that city, and among the newly enlightened Artemon shone most, whom Paul raised to be its bishop as well. Through his episcopate, the page says, he justified the preference and the hopes Paul had rested in him: he cultivated the teaching, cared for the poor and the widows, and was an example to the faithful in word and in work, their father and their helper, their boast and their consolation. He died in peace and his whole flock mourned him as one household, because he had put into practice what Paul said — be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity. Its couplet says that Artemon, having thrown off the flesh as a shell and having nothing of earth, sets out for heaven, and that he received Eden about the twenty-fourth.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and tells the whole of him in three sentences. He was born and brought up at Seleucia. When the apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) came to that city he saw Artemon, strengthened him further still in the faith of Christ, and appointed him bishop of the city. Artemon governed the rational flock of Christ with love and zeal, was a physician of the souls and the bodies of men, and passed over into eternity in deep old age.

That is all it gives: no year, no century and no reign, so this folder still holds no date. The Greek page puts his Seleucia in Pisidia, which the Prologue does not name, and the Prologue's phrase about his being a physician is a figure for a bishop's care rather than a trade, so nothing was taken from it into this folder's recorded data.
