# Irene the Empress, the nun Xenia

Irene was the wife of the emperor John II Komnenos — Kalo-John, the Prologue calls him — who reigned from 1118 to 1143, and she took the name Xenia in the monastic life.

Besides her monastic struggles and her many good works she is remembered above all as the founder of the monastery of Christ Pantokrator, the Almighty, in Constantinople, one of the most celebrated and most beautiful monasteries of the city. The Prologue adds that Stefan of Dečani, the Serbian king, later lived his monastic life there.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same menologion day, and where the Prologue gives a line saint.gr gives the life. She lived in the twelfth century, it says, a beautiful and virtuous girl; Alexios Komnenos noticed it and married her to his son John, who was called Kaloioannes, the good John, for his many virtues. As empress she spent open-handedly on works of mercy, and went by herself into poor huts to bring not money only but the higher strengthening and consolation of hope in Christ; she built homes for the aged and hostels besides, and left them large sums for their safe and easy keeping.

Then great griefs were tried on her. Her husband died on a campaign in Syria in 1143, and later two of her four children went the same way. Irene sought relief from her sorrows in the monastic life; and having taken the consent of her son the emperor Manuel she withdrew to the monastery of the Pantokrator, where she was tonsured and renamed Xene. Death found her there, and she was buried in great simplicity, as she herself had wished — for shortly before she died she was saying that the empress Irene had died long ago, and that nothing was left any more but the nun Xene. The couplet the page prints makes the Almighty give her a monastery above as the foundress of her own monastery below.
