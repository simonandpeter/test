# Nicholas Svyatosha

He was the great-grandson of the great prince Yaroslav the Wise and the son of David Svyatoslavich, prince of Chernigov, who died in 1123 and whom some sources, the calendar notes, call a saint. Svyatosha himself was prince of Lutsk, with a wife and children; one of his daughters later married the Novgorod prince Vsevolod-Gabriel, who died in 1138. On 17 February 1106 he left his household and was tonsured in the monastery of the Kyiv Caves — the first of the Russian princes to become a monk, and he bore patiently the reproaches of his brothers for choosing the life of an obedient who answers back to nobody.

He took the obediences of the house with great humility. For three years he worked in the kitchen, chopping its wood and carrying its water; for the three years after that he was the monastery's gatekeeper. He planted a garden by his cell, and out of his own means built a church of the Holy Trinity in the monastery and a hospital church dedicated to Saint Nicholas, his own patron in heaven.

His physician, Peter, argued with the prince that an obedience of that kind would ruin his health. Then the physician himself fell ill, and was healed only at the prayers of the saint; after that Peter took the monastic habit himself. Having passed through the several obediences, Nicholas laid on himself a vow of silence. Money that came to him he spent on the adorning of the church, or on books — for he loved reading — or gave to the poor.

He was a diligent peacemaker: in 1142 he reconciled the princes of Chernigov with the great prince Vsevolod. Soon after his death his own brother, prince Izyaslav, fell gravely ill; the abbot of the monastery sent the sick man the saint's hair shirt, and Izyaslav put it on and was healed. His relics lie in the Near caves, the caves of [Anthony of the Caves](/saints/anthony-of-the-caves), and the calendar keeps him there with the fathers of that company on 28 September as well as on this day.
