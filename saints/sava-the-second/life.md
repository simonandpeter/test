# Sava II

saint.gr names him and gives him two sentences. Its calendar for 8 February prints «Άγιος Σάββας Αρχιεπίσκοπος Σερβίας», and the page behind that line numbers him: Saint Sava the Second, archbishop of Serbia, fell asleep in peace in the year 1271. Of his birth it gives nothing, not even a year — its own heading leaves that date a question mark — and it gives no account of his rule of the Serbian church, no hymn and no couplet.

The one thing it adds is where he lies: his body, it says, is at the Patriarchate of Peć in Kosovo, and incorrupt. That, the number and the day are the whole of the entry.

He is not [Sava of Serbia](/saints/sava-of-serbia), the first archbishop and the son of Stephen Nemanja, whom the Greek calendar keeps on 14 January and this corpus keeps with him; and he is not [Sava III](/saints/sava-the-third) either, whom the Romanian calendar keeps on 26 July. The number on this page and the year 1271 are what set him apart from both, and nothing on the Greek page joins him to either.

The Serbian church keeps him on the Julian 8 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue — the first Serbian source read for him — gives the family the Greek page did not. He was the son of King [Stefan the First-Crowned](/saints/stefan-the-first-crowned) and the nephew of [Sava of Serbia](/saints/sava-of-serbia), the first archbishop; and before his tonsure his name was Predislav. Following the example of his great uncle, Predislav became a monk and gave himself eagerly to the ascetic life, and when he was chosen archbishop of Serbia after [Arsenius](/saints/arsenius-of-serbia) he took the name Sava II. He governed the church, the Prologue says, with great devotion and love.

Two further things come only from this source. His relics lie in the monastery at Peć — no other calendar read for him says where they are. And his year of repose is 1268, which makes three years from three sources: 1268 here, «(1269)» on the Russian calendar's line, and 1271 on the Greek page. None of the three says how it reached its figure, so his recorded death is now the span across all three and no one of them is preferred. The verses the Prologue sets under the day begin with young Predislav weeping, and his mother the queen asking him where his gaze is wandering: into the face of his uncle, holy Sava.
