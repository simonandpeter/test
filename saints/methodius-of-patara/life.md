# Methodius of Patara

Methodius gave himself to God from his youth, doxologia's life says, and showed himself a clean vessel and a dwelling of the Holy Spirit. By the divine election he was counted worthy of the rank of a bishop and took the pastorate of the reasonable sheep of the city of Patara, which is in the parts of Lycia. There he shepherded his flock well and in the love of God, enlightening the fullness of the Church with gentle words.

In that time the heresy of Origen had risen and was deceiving many. Seeing it grow strong, the hierarch of Christ stood up against it like a chosen shepherd and burned it with his words as with a divine fire, undoing all the darkness and fog of the heresy by his most wise teaching and by the divine gift. His God-inspired words shone out over the whole world like lightning, and the report of him went out into all the earth like the sound of a trumpet.

The unseen enemy could not bear his boldness or his great labours for the Church of Christ and for the right faith, and armed against him the enemies that are seen, his own servants, the Greeks who worshipped idols, urging them to kill by torments the servant of God, who before his martyrdom had already clothed himself in the life-giving mortification of the body. So the pagans took the hierarch of Christ and cut off his head with the sword, and he passed to the better life: he who had served the Lamb of God was himself brought as a living sacrifice, slain like a lamb.

For this he was adorned with a double crown, being a brave defender of the right faith: he fought against the heresies, and before the Greeks he confessed Christ with boldness to the shedding of his blood and to a martyr's end. The life places his suffering in the reign of Aurelian and prints no year, so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 20 June. saint.gr says that his rare virtue and his great learning raised him to the episcopal throne of Patara, that his enlightening word together with his holy example fed the souls of his flock, and that he was a sleepless guard of Orthodox doctrine, resolved all his life «φυλάσσειν τὰ δόγματα τὰ κεκριμένα ὑπὸ τῶν ἀποστόλων», to keep the teachings the apostles had judged. After a systematic struggle he greatly reduced the followers of Origen's mistaken opinions in his province; the few who remained contrived, in revenge, to place a servant of their own beside him, and one day when the bishop had fallen ill that man killed him with a knife. As he was being stabbed Methodius heard the murderer say «this is how the Origenists kill», and he smiled faintly and let his head fall, content, giving up his spirit to the Lord, glad to fall a victim of his duty to keep the Church's doctrines right. The page then sets against its own account the opinion of S. Eustratiades, that he flourished at the turn of the third and fourth centuries, that he was bishop not of Patara but of Olympus in Lycia and afterwards of Tyre — or, by another opinion, of Philippi in Macedonia — that the error came from his dialogue on the resurrection being set at Patara, and that he was a distinguished hierarch and a Platonist philosopher, a fierce opponent of the Origenists and a capable poet, as his surviving *Symposium of the Ten Virgins, or On Chastity* shows, who suffered a martyr's death at Chalcis in Syria in the persecution under Diocletian. It adds that a portion of his relics is at the monastery of St Panteleimon on Athos.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 20 June, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep him on, and it gives in six lines the year that neither of them gave. From his youth, it says, he gave himself to the ascetic struggle, and like a city set on a hill he was seen and called to the episcopate in the city of Patara in Lycia. A learned and eloquent hierarch, he wrote against the heresy of Origen, and his God-inspired words shone out over the whole world like lightning — which is the Romanian page’s own sentence, in Serbian, so that two books now say it alike.

Then the pagans rose against him, tortured him, and cut him down at Chalcis in Syria in the year 311. That is the first year of any kind the corpus has for him, and it comes with a place the Greek page had printed only as the correction it set against its own account of a murder by a planted servant. Here Chalcis in Syria is the book’s own telling, and the year is attached to it. The Romanian page’s reign of Aurelian is not widened against it and is not reconciled with it: a reign is no source for a year, and the two accounts are left standing side by side, as they are above.

The Russian calendar's own account of him is headed «Усекновение главы святому Мефодию Патарскому», the cutting off of his head, and it adds two things to what is already written here. It says what he left behind him: works in defence of Christianity against the pagans, an exposition of the Orthodox dogmas against the heresy of Origen, moral teachings, and commentaries on the Scriptures. And it says how he died — seized by the pagans, confessing the faith in Christ the Saviour firmly before them, and condemned to death by beheading. It dates that to 312, where this folder records 311 off another calendar; the year was left as it stood and both readings are now on the page.
