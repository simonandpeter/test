# Peter (Grigoriev)

Peter Fyodorovich Grigoriev was born in May 1895 at Oboyan in Kursk
province, the son of a personal nobleman and hereditary citizen of the town
who served as observer of the Kursk-Oryol forestry. He finished the Kursk
seminary in 1916 and was at the theological academy in Petrograd when the
February revolution came, and broke off his studies to go home to his
mother; in 1919 he became psalmist of the Smolensk church at Oboyan and was
ordained priest the same year. He was married to Zinaida Ivanovna, born in
1893, and they had a daughter Olga, born in 1928. In 1930 he lost his civil
rights, was arrested in February for systematic anti-Soviet and
anti-collective-farm agitation, and was released in June for want of
evidence, and went on serving at the Smolensk church; on the night of 17 to
18 October 1931 he was arrested at home, a hundred and fifty-eight church
books being seized, and on 16 April 1932 the OGPU troika of the Central
Black Earth region sentenced him to ten years in the camps as leader of a
church-monarchist counter-revolutionary organisation, one of thirty-six
accused, charged also with wide connexions among the clergy and monastics
and with collecting money for the imprisoned archbishop Damian of Kursk —
the indictment said he held illegal meetings, sent members into the villages
to collect, forwarded the money to the archbishop, and received his
directives and instructions through the warden of the cemetery church and a
priest. He was held in strict isolation and admitted nothing; the same year,
with the same thirty-five, he was charged again in the group case of the
Zealots of the Church, and in May 1932 reached the White Sea–Baltic canal at
the Soslovets halt of the Murmansk railway. He was in the Belbaltlag until
January 1937, marked in his file “keep in strict isolation, not to be
recruited”; in spite of every prohibition he kept in touch with the other
imprisoned priests and laity, taught them, found means to communicate them
and to bless water, and served Pascha. In July 1933 his term was cut by a
year and eight months, and in August a new case was opened on him; in 1935
and 1936 the priests were separated into a group called “Orthodox clergy”
and settled apart near Koma station, and he lived alone thirty kilometres
from the station, coming in only for provisions. In January 1937 he was
moved to the Volga camp in Yaroslavl region and worked as draughtsman to the
chief of works of the seventh section, characterised as an idler and
disorganiser who kept no camp discipline and took no part in cultural work.
In September 1937 the troika of the Yaroslavl NKVD sentenced him to death in
the group case of the priests [Gleb Apukhtin](/saints/gleb-apukhtin), Peter
Grigoriev and others: that in the camp he had led a church-monarchist
organisation, agitated systematically, spread leaflets, and with other
prisoners including the priests Istomin and Ilyin illegally organised the
collective reading of prayers and kept the rites — communion, confession and
the rest. At the interrogations he held astonishingly firm, naming no one he
knew, and to the charge of counter-revolution said, I am a Christian, I
believe in God and I preach the Christian confession, and denied everything
laid to him. He was shot on 23 September 1937 in the Volga camp in the
Uglich district of Yaroslavl region, at the isolator of the third section.
Rehabilitated on 8 August 1989, he was glorified by the Council of Bishops
of 13 to 16 August 2000 at the Yaroslavl diocese’s proposal, and is kept on
10 September of the old style, 23 September, the day of his death, and in
the synaxes of the Rostov-Yaroslavl and the Kursk saints.
