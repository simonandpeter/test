# Gregory the Hesychast of Athos

saint.gr says this Gregory lived at the beginning of the fourteenth century and came from Constantinople. Most devout, he was known at the same time for his theological and philosophical learning. He was the teacher of [Gregory Palamas](/saints/gregory-palamas), and to many others besides he handed on the waters of the philosophy of watchfulness.

He went to the Holy Mountain and chose a skete near the monastery of the Lavra. That skete was not long in becoming a common meeting place for the more devout souls: Gregory readily untied their difficulties, gave them knowledge, enlightened their minds and held up those who were shaken, giving them valuable directions for staying steady in the faith and making their hearts holy. Death found him busy with these works, pleasing to God, in the year 1310.

He keeps his feast also with the rest of the Athonite Fathers on the second Sunday of Matthew, the page says, where he is named in the second troparion of the third ode of the canon of their common service — a moveable day, which is not recorded here. The page gives him no family and no monastic name before Gregory, names no relics, and prints no hymn of his own; its couplet says only «Tον Γρηγόριον νήψεως τον εργάτην, / Λόγοις γεραίρω και γαρ ούτως ην θέμις» — with words I honour Gregory, the workman of watchfulness, for so it was right.

He is not the Gregory of Sinai whom the same calendar keeps on the same day, and whom the same day page lists separately with a long life of his own.
