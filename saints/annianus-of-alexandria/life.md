# Annianus of Alexandria

Annianus was the disciple of [Mark the Evangelist](/saints/mark-the-evangelist) and his successor on the episcopal throne of Alexandria, and — saint.gr quotes Eusebius of Caesarea for it — he was «ανήρ θεοφιλής και κατά πάντα θαυμάσιος», a man dear to God and in every way admirable. According to the hagiological reports, it says, he practised as a pagan the trade of a shoemaker at Alexandria.

When Mark disembarked from his ship in that city he applied to Annianus to mend his broken shoes; and Annianus, at his work, wounded his own left hand with the tool. After that had happened the apostle asked him to believe in God so as to be healed; and at once he made clay from his spittle and anointed Annianus's hand with it, calling on the Name of the Lord, and the shoemaker's hand was made well. After the wonder Annianus was baptized by the apostle Mark, and succeeded him on the throne of Alexandria.

The page gives three readings of the year of his death and chooses none. He fell asleep in peace in the year 82; by another testimony he ended in the time of the emperor Domitian, that is in 85; but another tradition of eastern origin says that he shepherded the Church of Alexandria eighteen years and fell asleep in peace in 86. His relics, it adds, are in the Roman Catholic church of St Thomas at Venice. It prints no hymn.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells the meeting in its own words and with its own details. When [Mark](/saints/mark-the-evangelist) stepped out of the ship onto dry land at Alexandria, his shoe split on one foot; and seeing a cobbler there, he gave it to him to mend. Sewing, the cobbler drove the awl through his own left hand, so that the blood ran and he cried aloud with the pain. Then the apostle of God mixed dust with his own spittle, anointed the wounded hand with it, and all at once the hand was whole.

The cobbler marvelled at the wonder and asked Mark into his house; and hearing his preaching, Annianus — for that, the book says, was his name — was baptized, and all his household with him. He showed so much devoutness and so much zeal in the work of God that Mark ordained him bishop, and this holy man became the second bishop in the Church of Alexandria. **The Prologue gives no year**, so the three readings of his death this folder already carries stand untouched.
