# Ananias, companion of Simeon of Persia

The bishop of Seleucia-Ctesiphon suffered in the persecution the Persian king Shapur II, whose reign the Russian calendar dates 310 to 381, raised against the Christians. He had been slandered as being in league with the Greek state and as acting against the king. In 344 the king issued a decree laying a heavy tax on the Christians, and when some of them refused to pay it — which was presented as a revolt — he opened a cruel persecution. [Simeon](/saints/simeon-of-persia) was brought to judgement in iron fetters as a supposed enemy of the Persian kingdom, and with him two presbyters, [Audelas](/saints/audelas-companion-of-simeon) and Ananias.

The bishop did not so much as bow to the king. Asked why he had not paid him the honours due, he answered that he had bowed to his rank before, but that now, having been brought there to deny his God and fall away from his faith, it was not fitting for him to bow. The king pressed him to worship the sun and threatened, if he would not, to wipe Christianity out of the country altogether; and when neither persuasion nor threat moved him he was led away to prison.

On the road the eunuch [Husdazad](/saints/gothazat-companion-of-simeon), who had been the king's own tutor, saw the bishop, stood up and bowed to him; but the bishop turned away from him and reproached him for having worshipped the sun out of fear of the king, being a Christian. The eunuch repented with his whole heart, changed his costly clothes for coarse ones and sat weeping bitterly at the palace doors: woe is me, how shall I stand before my God, whom I have denied? Even Simeon turned away from me. The king heard that his beloved old teacher was grieving and asked him what had happened, and he said openly that he repented bitterly of his apostasy and would worship the sun no more, but only the one true God. The king marvelled at the sudden resolution of the old man and coaxed him not to dishonour the gods their fathers had worshipped; but he would not be shifted, and was condemned to death. His one request was that heralds should tell the whole city he was dying not for any crime against the king but as a Christian, and the king granted it.

The bishop learned of the eunuch's end and thanked the Lord with tears. Called before the king a second time, he again refused to worship the pagan gods and confessed his faith in Christ; and the angry king ordered every Christian then in the prison beheaded before his eyes. They went to the execution without fear, blessed by the bishop, and laid their own heads under the sword. So Audelas, the bishop's companion, was beheaded. When the turn came to the presbyter Ananias he suddenly began to tremble, and one of the nobles, [Phousik](/saints/phousik-companion-of-simeon), a secret Christian, was afraid that he would deny Christ and cried aloud: do not be afraid, old man; close your eyes and you will see at once the divine Light of our Lord Jesus Christ. With that cry he gave himself away. The king ordered his tongue torn out and his skin flayed from him, and with him his daughter [Askitrea](/saints/askitrea-daughter-of-phousik) was tortured to death.

Last of all the bishop came to the executioner and laid his head on the block with a prayer, on 13 April 344. The killing went on through the whole of Pascha week, to the twenty-third of April, and the eunuch [Azat](/saints/azat-the-eunuch), a dignitary close to the king, was martyred in it too. The sources, the page says, record a thousand martyrs, then another hundred, and another hundred and fifty. The day's line names six of the company and closes with «и иных многих», and many others, who are not named and have no folder here.

The calendar's own line and the heading of its life both call him a presbyter and a hieromartyr, and that, with the moment of his trembling and the cry that steadied him, is the whole of what it says of him by himself: no birthplace, no years, no see and no word of his own. The Greek page the rest of this company was written from names only the one presbyter and leaves him out altogether, so this folder stands on the Russian calendar alone.
