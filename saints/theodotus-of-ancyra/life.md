# Theodotus of Ancyra

The life of Theodotus that doxologia prints is signed by Saint Nilus, who says he lived with the martyr from the beginning, was with him in the bonds of the prison, and writes only what he saw with his own eyes. Some say, Nilus allows, that Theodotus lived at first no differently from anyone else, that he was lawfully married and kept an inn for profit; let each say what he will, he writes, and the last struggle of his martyrdom adorned the whole of his earlier life.

Before his martyrdom he did much good. Married though he was, he fought against the desires of the flesh and subdued the body to the spirit, fulfilling the apostle's word that those who have wives should be as though they had none, until he could teach others purity and sober wisdom. He had abstinence and fasting as the beginning of all good things and armed himself with them as with a shield; he counted the mortifying of the body a fitting pleasure for a Christian; and he overcame the desire of riches by giving away everything he had to the poor. What was most to be wondered at in him was that, pretending to live as an innkeeper, he was carrying on a great spiritual trade, winning many souls for God and working secretly like an apostle under that appearance.

When the governor Theotecnus came to the borders of Ancyra, the report of his coming alone so terrified the faithful that the churches were emptied at once and the hidden places and the mountains were full of fugitives. In the city were seven virgins, grown old in chastity and the fear of God, who had betrothed themselves in maiden purity to Christ; the eldest of them, the blessed Thecusa, was Theodotus's aunt. The governor put them to many torments and could not force them to the idolatry, and then gave them over to some debauched young men to be defiled. Thecusa took hold of the feet of the boldest, threw back the covering from her head to show him her grey hairs, and asked him to think of his own mother; and the youths were put to shame, wept, and left the virgins untouched.

They were drowned afterwards in the lake, with stones at their necks, for refusing to serve the idols at the washing of them; and Theodotus, who had been praying for them in fear that one might weaken, took counsel with Polychronius and Theocharides how to get their bodies out of the water and give them burial. His aunt Thecusa appeared to him in a vision and reproached his sleep. The three went out at night; the soldiers guarding the lake fled at the sight of an armed man shining like fire, whom the life names as the martyr Sosander, and a wind emptied the water to the far bank so that the bodies were seen. They cut the ropes from their necks with sickles, carried them away in carts and buried them beside the church of the holy patriarchs. Their names, the life says, were Thecusa, Alexandra, Claudia, Phaina, Euphrasia, Matrona and Julia, and they suffered on the eighteenth day of the month of May.

In the morning the city learned that the bodies had been taken. Christians were seized everywhere and examined, and Polychronius — Theodotus's own kinsman and friend, the betrayer the saints had warned him of — saw the sword over his head and told the truth. The pagans dug up the relics of the virgins and burned them, and looked for Theodotus; and when he heard of it that evening he determined to give himself up. He came of his own accord to the judgement and to the torments, and bore them, the soldiers said afterwards, as though he had been of brass or of iron; and he was beheaded in a field outside the city, before a great crowd of men and women.

He had told the brethren that when the priest Fronto came from the village of Malos with his ring as a token they should give him the body if they could take it secretly. Fronto came, sat down with the guards, gave them his own wine to drink, and heard from them the whole story; and while they drank, the body was taken. He went off as though looking for his donkey and did not come back. The donkey, led by an angel, stopped at the very place where Theodotus had once eaten with Fronto and praised it as a fit place to receive martyrs' relics, and would go no further until its master came; and there Fronto, with some faithful brethren, buried the martyr with honour and afterwards built a church in his name.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day, twice over: once on the page of the virgin martyrs of Ancyra, where he is the last of nine head names, and once in a listing of his own. He was the nephew of the holy martyr [Thecusa](/saints/thecusa-of-ancyra), saint.gr says, and he was martyred at Ancyra in the year 304, in the years of the emperor Maximian, who reigned from 285 to 305. When the governor Theoteknos had drowned the women in the lake, Theodotus drew their holy relics out by night and buried them reverently; and when the tyrant learnt of it he seized him and beheaded him after many tortures, and gave the relics he had buried to the fire. It counts it further to his praise that he was the head of a household and left a widow and orphans behind him. The page sends the reader to 7 June as well, which is the second Romanian day this folder already records, and it calls him neither innkeeper nor bishop.

The Russian calendar keeps him on a day neither of the other two does. Its line for 7 ноября ст. ст. is «Мч. Феодота корчемника (303).» — Theodotus the innkeeper — and the name link behind it gives «Мученик Феодот Анкирский». The name page lists three days, «18 мая , 7 июня , 7 ноября»: 18 мая is the day doxologia.ro and saint.gr keep him on, and is, his own long life says, the day the seven virgins died; 7 июня is the day of his own death in that same life. The day page that listed him here is 7 ноября, so that is this row's feast and the other two days are a note on it.

Two lives stand behind his name page. The shorter says he lived at Ancyra in Galatia in the third century and was marked by particular kindness and responsiveness; at the height of the persecution under Diocletian (284–305) he supplied the Christians with all they needed and gave them shelter in his own house, where the services were secretly held. He visited the imprisoned, bought them out, and buried with reverence the bodies of martyrs thrown out to be eaten by beasts. Refusing to sacrifice to the idols and reproving the pagan errors, he confessed Christ and was cut down with the sword; a storm that rose up stopped the burning of his body and it was given Christian burial.

The longer life fills the trade in. He was «корчемником», kept his own inn, and was married; already then he kept purity and chastity, trained himself in abstinence and subdued the flesh to the spirit by fasting and prayer. His conversation brought Jews and pagans to the Christian faith and sinners to repentance, and he received from the Lord the gift of healing and cured the sick by laying his hands on them. In the famine he kept the homeless in his inn and fed them, hid the hunted, and gave out of his own stores whatever the ruined churches needed for the Liturgy; when the churches of Ancyra were shut and ruined, the Liturgy began to be served in his inn.

Knowing that the same contest was before him, he told the priest «Фронтон» that martyrs' relics would shortly be brought to a place the two of them had chosen together, and gave him his ring in witness of it. The eldest of the seven virgins drowned for Christ at that time was his own aunt, [Thecusa](/saints/thecusa-of-ancyra); it was he who went to the lake at night and took up the seven bodies for burial. «Полихроний», breaking under torture, named him as the man who had done it.

He was shown the instruments of torture and promised great honours and riches to deny Christ. He glorified the Lord instead and confessed his faith; the pagans put him to long torments and the power of God kept him alive, and he was taken back to prison.

The next morning the governor tried again, understood that his courage could not be shaken, and ordered his head struck off. A storm stopped the soldiers burning the body, and they stayed to guard it from a tent. The priest «Фронтон» was passing on the road nearby, leading an ass loaded with wine from his own vineyard, and the ass fell by the place where the body lay; the soldiers helped to raise it and told him whose body they were guarding. He laid the relics on the ass, brought them to the place Theodotus had chosen for his burial, and gave them to the earth with honour, and afterwards raised a church there. The page names the author of the account: «Нил», a contemporary and fellow-struggler who saw his death.

**The two lives disagree about the water, and the day line disagrees with the life about the year.** The shorter says the seven women he drew out and buried were «утопленных в море», drowned in the sea; the longer drowns them in the lake at Ancyra, a heavy stone tied at each one's neck. And the line prints «(303)» where the long life has him die «7 июня 303 или 304 года». Both readings of the year stand in the date above and neither is chosen; the two waters are recorded here and nothing is chosen there either.
