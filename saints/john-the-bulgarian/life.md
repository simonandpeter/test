# John the Bulgarian

John was born in Bulgaria in 1765. While he was still a child he spent much of his time among Muslims and, little by little, gave up Christ and followed the Muslim faith. Later, when he was sixteen, repenting bitterly of his fault, he fled — as so many others did — to the Holy Mountain, where he entered the Great Lavra and stayed three years, serving an elder whose hand was withered.

But the peace and quiet of Athos could not drive off the sadness that lay on his heart because he had denied Jesus Christ. So, with a blessing from his spiritual father, he decided to go to Constantinople and confess Christ publicly, and to pour out his blood to wash away the sin of apostasy of which he felt himself guilty.

The young monk dressed himself as a Turk, which a Christian is not permitted to do, and on reaching Constantinople went straight to the church of Holy Wisdom, turned into a mosque by Mehmed the Conqueror after the taking of Constantinople in 1453. In front of the Muslims there he made the sign of the cross and began to say Christian prayers; then he confessed that he had been born a Christian, had fallen into error and had gone away from the faith of Christ, and said aloud that he wished at that moment to renounce the false Muslim faith and return to Christ.

Hearing these words the Turks went mad with rage, seized John and took him before the kadi, who tried to persuade the young Christian to renounce his faith in Christ and come back to the Muslim faith. John answered: «Fără Hristos, nu există mântuire» — without Christ there is no salvation. The furious Hagarenes dragged the saint into the courtyard of Holy Wisdom and there cut off his head; so he received the crown of martyrdom on 5 March 1784, at the age of nineteen.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and tells the fall the martyrdom answered. saint.gr says the new martyr John came of Bulgaria and was handsome in appearance and lettered; that he once became entangled in some adventure and denied Christ, and that feeling the stings of remorse for that apostasy he left Bulgaria and came to the Holy Mountain, where he made obedience to a monk of the Lavra of St Athanasius who was crippled in one hand. At eighteen he left the Holy Mountain and came to Constantinople, put on Turkish clothes and went into the church of the Holy Wisdom of God, which had been turned into a mosque; and there, in front of the Turks, he made the sign of the cross over himself and venerated in the church as a Christian does. The Turks seized him at once and pressed him to deny Christ, and John confessed with a spiritual courage his faith in the piety of his fathers; so they beheaded him in the courtyard of the church of the Wisdom of God, in the year 1784.
