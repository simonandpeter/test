# John of Ustyug

saint.gr says John was born in the fifteenth century in the village of Pukhovo, in the country of Ustyug, of devout and God-loving parents, Savva and Maria. From his childhood he was marked out for the austerity of his way of life and for the strictness with which he kept the fasts: on Wednesdays and Fridays he ate nothing at all but a little bread, and drank a little water.

After his father's death his mother went to live in the monastery of the Holy Trinity at Orletsk and became a nun. The young John began his own ascetic life with silence and with folly for Christ's sake, and passed the rest of his days in unceasing prayer, living in a hut at Ustyug.

He fell asleep in peace in the year 1494 and was buried near the cathedral church of the Dormition of the Mother of God. That is the whole of what the page prints: no wonder, no saying of his, and no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and prints his heading with no life under it at all — „Свети Јован Јуродиви, Устјужски чудотворац“, and then the next entry. The page was read to be sure of it, and the silence is the page’s own and not a fault of reading. So what the Serbian calendar adds is the line itself, and the line calls him two things: a fool for Christ’s sake, which the Greek page also says, and the wonderworker of Ustyug, which it does not. No year is given here, and the year this folder holds is still the Greek page’s alone.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 29 мая ст. ст. and gives him a short life. He came of the village of Pukhovo, near Ustyug, and from his young years was marked by a strict fasting life: on Wednesdays and Fridays he ate nothing at all, on the other days he lived on bread and water, and he passed the nights without sleep, in prayer. Moving into the city of Ustyug he took on himself the labour of folly for Christ: by day he went about in torn rags and barefoot in every season of the year, resting sometimes on a heap of dung and bearing the mockery and the insults humbly, and by night he prayed.

In his own lifetime he was granted the gift of wonders. One winter he stirred the burning coals in an oven with his hands and, crossing himself, lay down on them and took no hurt. He healed Мария, the wife of the Ustyug prince Феодор Красный, of a fever by a word. He died in peace in the Lord at eighteen years old, on 29 мая 1494 — and no year of birth has been reckoned here from that age.

He became famous above all, the calendar says, for his help against invasion: it is to his prayers that it credits the victory of the Russian army over the Polish and Lithuanian invaders at Dmitrov in 1610. The first of the calendar’s two readings is headed for him and runs under the title of the two fools for Christ of that city together.
