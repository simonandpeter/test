# Samuel the Egyptian

saint.gr's calendar for 16 February prints one line for the whole company, «Άγιος Πάμφιλος και οι συν αυτώ Μάρτυρες» — Pamphilus and the martyrs with him — and the page behind it names the twelve of them one by one: [Pamphilus](/saints/pamphilus-of-caesarea), Daniel, Elias, Isaiah, Theodulus, Jeremiah, Julian, [Valens](/saints/valens-the-deacon), [Paul](/saints/paul-of-jamnia), Porphyrius, Samuel and [Seleucus](/saints/seleucus-of-cappadocia). They were martyred under the emperor Diocletian, whose reign the page gives as 284 to 305; they came from various places, but the love and the faith of Christ joined them. Working at Caesarea in Palestine they confessed Christ before the eparch Firmilianus, who spent every effort on persuading them to deny their faith in Christ, and they stayed fixed immovably in the piety of their fathers; so Firmilianus gave the order that they should be put to death after being tortured first.

Of the twelve, nine were beheaded with the sword, and Samuel was one of them. He is one of the five the page marks as Egyptians — [Daniel](/saints/daniel-the-egyptian), [Elias](/saints/elias-the-egyptian), [Isaiah](/saints/isaiah-the-egyptian) and [Jeremiah](/saints/jeremiah-the-egyptian) beside him — and over those five it prints a couplet of its own: the callings of prophets and the deaths of martyrs, it says, are the boast of five beheaded martyrs. The page gives him no homeland within Egypt, no age, no office and no separate hymn; what it keeps of him is his name, his epithet, his company, the eparch, the city and the sword.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day, in one entry for the whole company: the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads it „Светих дванаест мученика.“ — the holy twelve martyrs who suffered in the time of the emperor Diocletian — and then names all twelve in its body. He is one of five brothers, in body and in spirit, natives of Egypt, who were coming home from their sentence in the mines of Cilicia when they said at the gate of Caesarea that they were Christians and were brought before the court for it. Asked their names, the Prologue has them answer that they had thrown away the pagan names their mother gave them and called themselves Elias, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Samuel and Daniel; asked where they were from, that they were from the Jerusalem above. All of them were beheaded.
