# Palladius (Popov)

Palladius Ivanovich Popov was born on 27 January 1879 in the village of
Ustye in the Nikolsk district of Vologda province, a priest’s son. He
finished the Archangel seminary in 1902, studied from 1903 to 1905 at the
Kazan veterinary institute — a training that was of use to him afterwards —
and from 1906 to 1913 taught at the two-class school at Sizyabsk in the
Pechora district of Archangel province, now in Komi; in 1906 and 1907 he
belonged to the Socialist Revolutionary party. He married Lydia Stepanovna,
from the village of Lasta, and they had no children. In 1913 Bishop
Nathanael (Troitsky) of Archangel ordained him priest for the church of the
Meeting of the Lord at Gam in the same district, now the Izhma district of
Komi, where he taught the law of God and ran the parish school, from 1914
taught at the two-class school at Mokhcha, and in 1917 and 1918 taught at
the Mokhcha teachers’ courses and the Gam village school; after 1918 he
lived at Gam as its priest, and the parishioners remembered Father Pal, as
they called him, as a good and kind man. In 1933 he was charged under
article 59.12 and not convicted. He was arrested at Gam on 31 July 1937 and
held in the Izhma prison, charged with having, since the publication of the
new constitution, systematically agitated among the collective farmers
against the Soviet power, interpreted the constitution to them and to the
independent farmers in a distorted way, and awaited war and the fall of the
Soviet power; on 21 September 1937 the troika of the Komi NKVD sentenced him
to death under article 58.10, and he was shot at Izhma between 22 and 25
September 1937. Numbered among the new martyrs by the Holy Synod on 6
October 2001, he is kept on 10 September of the old style, 23 September, and
in the synaxis of the Komi saints.
