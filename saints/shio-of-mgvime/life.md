# Shio of Mgvime

Shio was born at Antioch in Syria at the beginning of the sixth century, of noble and wealthy parents who had no other children; he was the sole heir of their great fortune and the only comfort of their old age, and they brought him up with much gentleness and care, planting in him the seeds of virtue and of piety, which he willingly received. Once he had learned to read he gave himself without ceasing to the study of the Scriptures, carrying the New Testament and the Psalter always with him, and at sixteen he understood and expounded the word of God so wisely that many were astonished.

His parents, seeing him study without rest, grew uneasy and often urged him to take some rest; sometimes they went so far as to take the holy books away from him, which filled him with sorrow, and he would ask them in tears why they would not let him learn the law of God, for those books were his great treasure. He coaxed them with tears and entreaties, and took up the God-inspired texts again with joy.

He became one of the twelve disciples whom [John of Zedazeni](/saints/john-of-zedazeni) chose out of his Syrian community and brought into Georgia, in obedience to a vision in which the Mother of God told him to take twelve worthy men to strengthen the faith in her newly founded Church. In the life of his teacher he is named in the opening list as Shio of Mgvime and called afterwards Sisoe. When the archbishop advised that each of the twelve take a helper, all agreed except Shio, who, being a lover of the hermit's life, asked instead for a blessing to live alone. He was sent into Kartli, westward, and that life calls him «pildă de lacrimi și plâns dumnezeiesc» — a pattern of tears and of godly weeping; when their teacher came later to visit, it was Shio who went out at the head of the brothers of his monastery to meet him, kissed his elder and led him into the church of the Most Holy Mother of God, and sent a monk to tell bishop [Jesse](/saints/jesse-of-tsilkani) of the visit.

The long middle of his own life, from his coming to Georgia to his enclosure in the cave at Mgvime, was not read through here. At the end, the brothers told the abbot that he was dying, and he at once ordered a priest to take the Holy Mysteries and go to the cave, and sent another brother to tell John of Zedazeni that his disciple's repose was expected; and the next day John came to Mgvime to lead the soul of the blessed Sisoe to the heavenly dwellings. On 9 May, having communicated for the last time and received his spiritual father's blessing, he raised his eyes to heaven and said: Lord, into thy hands I commend my spirit. The brothers wrapped him in his own worn garment and buried him in the cave, as he had asked. His memory, the page says, is kept every year on 9 mai, the day of his blessed repose, and also on the Thursday of Cheesefare week.

The Greek calendar keeps him too, but not on the Romanian day and not with a life. saint.gr's 4 February prints «Όσιοι Ευάγριος και Σίος εκ Γεωργίας», the venerable Evagrius and Shio out of Georgia, and the page behind the line carries no account of either man — only a note on what the name Ευάγριος means, and then «Βλέπε 4 Ιανουαρίου», see 4 January. So three things are recorded here and no more: that the Greek calendar names him, that it names him with [Evagrius](/saints/evagrius-of-georgia), and that its own page refers its reader to a second day it keeps them on. The 4 January Greek page was not read, and the Greek adds nothing to what doxologia already told of his life.
