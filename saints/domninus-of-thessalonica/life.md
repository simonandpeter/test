# Domninus of Thessalonica

The Greek synaxarion says Domninus lived at the end of the third century, about 288, and came from Thessalonica. When Maximian was building a palace in that city, Domninus was arrested as a Christian and a preacher of piety and brought before the emperor, who asked how he dared confess another god than the one the emperor worshipped, and told him to sacrifice to the idols if he wished to live. He would not, and the emperor had his body torn, and Domninus laughed at the tyrant as he suffered. Then he was led out of the city and his legs were broken; with his legs cut off in the cruellest way he lived seven whole days without food, and then, giving thanks to God, gave up his soul. The entry keeps no hymn for him.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 1 October and dates him «ок. 284-305» — Diocletian's reign rather than a year, where saint.gr settles on about 288. The wider reading contains the narrower, so his date here is still the Greek one and the reign is recorded in the Russian row. Its index of names heads him «Домнин Солунский (Фессалоникийский), мч.», naming Thessalonica twice, once in the Slavonic form and once in the Greek.
