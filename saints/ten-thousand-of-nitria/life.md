# The Ten Thousand of Nitria

saint.gr's whole notice of them is one short paragraph, and it begins with the ending: the holy Ten Thousand — «Μύριοι» — venerable of Nitria were put to death by fire by Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, because he bore a grudge against Isidore the Presbyter. Above it the page sets a distich: «Έκτεινε εις σπήλαια πυρ τους μυρίους, Σπήλαιον οικήσαντος οικέτας Λόγου» — fire killed the ten thousand in their caves, the servants of the Word who himself dwelt in a cave.

These ascetics, the page says, lived in caves and huts of Nitria in Egypt, and their chief work was the keeping of the commandments of God; they lived with fasting, with vigils and with prayer, and were about ten thousand in number. Because the holy Isidore, the first among them, was contending with Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, over certain church questions that Theophilus opposed, and because Isidore had the backing of the many monks behind him and was calling Theophilus to account, Theophilus, to take revenge on Isidore, sent men of his own secretly and burned all those fathers alive inside the sketes.

That is the whole of the account. The page gives no century and no year, names no member of the company but Isidore, and prints no hymn to them.
