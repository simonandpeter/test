# John (Bogoyavlensky)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 11 декабря and gives him a life, a long one. He was born on 12 February 1892 in the village of Vetrino in the Yaroslavl province, the son of a priest named Dmitry Bogoyavlensky and his wife Lyudmila. He finished the Yaroslavl seminary in 1914 and did not want to follow his father: he wished for a secular path and for work in the zemstvo institutions. But because he had finished the seminary in the second class the zemstvo would not take him, and he had to settle for a post as a teacher in a parish school.

That work did not satisfy him either, and in the autumn of 1916 he entered the law faculty of the Demidov lyceum at Yaroslavl, where he studied two years, until 1918, when the authorities turned the lyceum into a state university and he did not wish to go on there. In 1918 famine began in Yaroslavl, as it did in many of the industrial towns of Russia, and he began looking for a schoolmaster’s place in some small town; and in 1919 he went to Vesyegonsk, where he joined a Soviet school of the second level as a teacher of history, geography, the history of socialism and political economy. For all his earlier wish for a secular path, the page says, he was a man of deep faith, which he did not hide, and he went often to church.

The page is long and only its two ends were read, so what followed — his ordination, which the calendar’s own name page attests by calling him a priest, and his arrest, and how he died — is not set down here. Its last pages are the record of an interrogation. He is charged with systematic counter-revolutionary agitation aimed at wrecking the economic and political campaigns in the collective farms, and told that the investigation requires truthful testimony from him; and to every charge put to him — that he had instructed a nun to agitate that Soviet power was penal servitude and the collective farms would fall apart, that he had praised enemies of the people in a church watchman’s hut, that he had told the farmers of a village that they worked day and night for coppers and all of it would go to the state — he answers that he does not confirm it, that the witnesses’ testimony is false, and that he carried on no anti-Soviet agitation.

The calendar gives his year as 1941. The day page carries four hymns and not one is under his name.
