# Dasius of Durostorum

saint.gr's calendar for 20 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Δάσιος που μαρτύρησε στο Δορύστολο» — Dasius, who was martyred at Dorostolon. He lived towards the end of the third century, the page says, in the time of Maximian, and received the crown of martyrdom in that city, which was built near the river Istros, the Danube. The couplet set over the notice plays on the sword: Dasius, contending by the sword, was to find an athlete's crown out of the sword.

He belonged to the military ranks, and a great faith in Christ held him; and for that reason he was denounced to his commanding officer. The officer tried first to tempt him with promises, and when he saw that he had not managed it he threatened him with torture. The saint stayed faithful to the Lord all the same. So his commander gave the order for his torture, and at the end of it for his beheading.

The page adds where his relics are: they lie in the Roman Catholic church of Saint Cyriacus at Ancona in Italy. It gives him no rank beyond the soldiering, no year of birth and no hymn.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 20 November on a line of his own, «Мч. Дасия (284-305).», and its life page makes the bracket a reign and not a year: he «жил в III веке в городе Доростоле на Дунае», lived in the third century in the city of Durostorum on the Danube, and he was beheaded after cruel torments by the command of «императоров Диоклитиана (284 - 305) и Максимиана (284 - 305)» — the numbers belong to the two emperors, who are each given the same span, and the line has lifted them out and set them after his name as if they were his.

The story the page tells is the one the Greek page tells, and it tells it at greater length. The townsmen were preparing for the feast of the heathen god Saturn; by custom, thirty days before the feast the heathen chose a beautiful youth, clothed him in sumptuous garments and paid him royal honours, and on the day of the feast offered him in sacrifice to the god. The choice of his fellow citizens fell on him, because there was no youth in the city more beautiful than he. Learning of it, he said: «Если мне суждено умереть, то лучше умереть за Христа как христианину» — if it is appointed me to die, better to die for Christ as a Christian. He confessed his faith in Christ openly before the citizens, reproved their impiety and their errors, and turned many of them to Christ; and for that he was put to the torments and beheaded.
