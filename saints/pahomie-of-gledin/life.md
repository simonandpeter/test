# Pahomie of Gledin

Pahomie's parents, the priest Eftimie and the presbytera Ana, took care to give him a good education. The commemoration book of the skete of Pocrov says of him that from his youth he loved Christ and longed for the hermit's life, having a burning love towards God; and that, obeying the Saviour's holy command that whoever would come after Him must deny himself, take up his cross and follow Him, he left house, parents, brothers, kinsmen and friends and followed Christ in the humble monastic life, entering as a brother at the monastery of Neamț.

He was ordained priest and chosen great ecclesiarch, and in 1702 the brotherhood of Neamț elected him their guide. He was abbot there only until the spring of 1704. Between 1704 and 1706 he was on pilgrimage to the Kyiv Caves Lavra, and it was then that he met [Demetrius, metropolitan of Rostov](/saints/demetrius-of-rostov). Returning to Moldavia, he withdrew to a place of quiet near the monastery of Neamț and refused to take up the abbacy again; and on 18 December 1706 he was elected bishop of Roman.

He was consecrated on 18 January 1707 and shepherded that see until 10 April 1714, when he withdrew from it. In 1717 he set out for Kyiv once more, and spent the rest of his life there in prayer and in the labours of a scholar. He died at the Caves Lavra in 1724. His canonisation was approved by the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church at its session of 14–15 November 2006, which set 14 April as the day of his commemoration.

The page keeps something of his own hand. Writing of his people he gives thanks that they had sat in the darkness of ignorance and in the shadow of the law and of incomprehension, and that now the sun of righteousness, the right faith, had shone — «dau laudă lui Dumnezeu, dau numelui Domnului nostru Iisus Hristos tot neamul nostru românesc». And on the margin of a book called *Cheia înțelesului* he wrote: «Cetește cuvintele cu minte deșteaptă și cu candela și vei pricepe tainele lui Dumnezeu toate» — read the words with a wakeful mind and with the lamp lit, and you will understand all the mysteries of God. Reading, for Pahomie, was both work for the enlightenment of his people and a spiritual exercise, and his learning and his spiritual influence reached beyond the see of Roman, the skete of Pocrov and the monastery of Neamț into the monastic and scholarly life of Moldavia.
