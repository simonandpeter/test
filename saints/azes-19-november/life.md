# Azes (19 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 19 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Άζης ο Θαυματουργός» — Azes the wonderworker — and gives him the one full notice on which the shorter lines of that day all lean. He lived in the years of the emperor Diocletian, about 289 by the page's own reckoning, and came from the country of the Isaurians. He was a soldier by trade, but he left the military life and lived in the desert, and there he worked many wonders.

Hunters who had seen him denounced him, and a detachment was sent out to arrest him. Deep in that country the soldiers grew thirsty and could find no water anywhere. Azes prayed, and water sprang up in front of them, and they drank; and that wonder was enough to make many of the detachment believe in Christ.

Brought at last before the eparch Aquilinus, he confessed Christ with courage. They bound him to a wheel and lit a fire beneath it, but the flame rose and then went out all at once, and the sight of it drew [the eparch's own wife and daughter](/saints/wife-and-daughter-of-aquilinus) to Christianity. To be rid of Azes for good, Aquilinus gave the order and he was beheaded.

The same calendar keeps on the same day [the hundred and fifty soldier martyrs](/saints/hundred-and-fifty-soldier-martyrs) who believed through him and were put to the sword, and [twelve soldier martyrs](/saints/twelve-soldier-martyrs-19-november) more, who it thinks were perhaps of the same time. The page prints him no hymn and no relics.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 19 Νοεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/11/19/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2904/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
