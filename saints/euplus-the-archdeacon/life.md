# Euplus the Archdeacon

The archdeacon Euplus suffered in 304 under Diocletian and Maximian. He served in the Sicilian city of Catania and, always carrying the Gospel with him, preached Christ fearlessly to the pagans. Once, as he was reading and explaining the Gospel to a crowd, he was seized and brought before Calvisianus, the governor of the city; he confessed himself a Christian and denounced the idols, and was tortured and thrown wounded into prison, where he spent seven days in prayer and the Lord opened a spring of water for him.

Brought a second time before the judge, strengthened and joyful, he confessed Christ again and rebuked the judge for shedding innocent blood; the judge ordered his ears torn off and his head cut off. As he was led to execution the Gospel was hung about his neck, and asking time to pray he began again to read it and explain it to the people, and many of the pagans believed; then the soldiers took him and beheaded him. The Prologue tells it the same way, with the book hung about his neck and the martyr mocking the torturer — these torments are a cobweb to me — and says that his wonderworking relics lie in a village near Naples.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 11 august and prints the trial at length. Diocletian and Maximian sent into Sicily an officer named Pentagur, who had the whole people of Catania called together and gave the governor Calvisian power to torture and kill anyone found naming Christ. A servant told Calvisian of a man named Euplus who carried a book about the streets, went into the houses and taught the people that the God of the Christians is great; the soldiers found him in a poor house reading the Gospel to those who listened, bound his hands behind him and brought him with the book to judgement. Asked whether he was the reviler of the gods, he answered by asking who the gods were that he should honour them.

After the tearing of his ears with iron hooks and a last refusal to sacrifice, he was condemned to the sword, and asked and was given time to pray. At the place of death he turned to the crowd that had followed, opened the Gospel he carried, read out the wonders of Christ and taught them the knowledge of the true God, and many of the unbelieving were enlightened. Then he prayed, and a voice came from above: Blessed are you, Euplus, my good and faithful servant; enter into the joy of your Lord. He bent his head under the sword, and the faithful took his head and body and buried them in a known place, where many healings were worked at his grave.
