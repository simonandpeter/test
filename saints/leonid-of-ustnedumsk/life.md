# Leonid of Ustnedumsk

The calendar prints two readings of Leonid and they do not agree, so both are given here. The first says he was born in 1551 in the Novgorod region, in the Shchekinsky settlement of the Poshensky district, to a villager named Philip and a mother Catherine; the second says he lived in the Poshekhonsky district of the Vologda country and worked the land. Both say that at about fifty he saw in a dream the Mother of God, who bade him go to the Morzhevskaya hermitage of Saint Nicholas on the Dvina, take from there the icon of the Mother of God the Directress, and build a church for it on the river Luza by the Turin hill.

Taking the revelation for an ordinary dream, he did not dare obey it. He went instead to the Kozheozersky monastery and was tonsured there — in 1603, says the first reading, which gives him a year in that house where the second gives about three — and then to Solovki, where he worked in the bakery for three years. The dream returning, he went to the Morzhevskaya hermitage, spent a year at its labours and then told its superior his vision; the second reading names the superior Korniliy. Receiving the icon, he settled near the Turin hill in a hut woven of brushwood, but the people there drove him out and he moved up the river into a marsh, where with one villager's help he cut a cell and set a chapel beside it.

He went to Rostov for an antimension and a blessing to build a church, and was ordained hieromonk; the readings name two different metropolitans, Varlaam in the first and Filaret in the second. In 1608 he raised a wooden church of the Entry of the Mother of God and set the Directress in it. The marshes around the house made it unfit to live in, so he dug a channel from the Luza to the Black lake and called it the river Do-not-think: for while he was digging it a snake stung him, and the thought of the poison's end frightened him, until he put his hope in God and resolved not to think of the danger, and was unharmed. He dug two more channels, from the Black lake to the Holy and from the Holy to the Black stream.

Even so the spring flood of the Luza drowned the house, and towards the end of his life he moved its buildings to a cape above the Black lake, where a church was consecrated in 1652 — on 25 May that year, says the first reading, by Arseny, archimandrite of the Archangel monastery at Ustyug. He died on 17 July 1654, in his hundred and fifth year by the first reading and in his hundredth by the second, and was buried in the monastery church, where his heavy hair shirt was long kept.
