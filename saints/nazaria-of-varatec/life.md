# Nazaria of Văratec

After the sudden death of her husband and of her two children, Nazaria crossed the mountains into Wallachia and entered the community of the skete of Scânteia on the Măgura of Odobești, in the county of Vrancea, where as a rasophore she was given the name Natalia. Later, at the skete of Bontești in the commune of Cârligele, she was tonsured to the monastic life with the name Nazaria. Both sketes stood under the spiritual guidance of Saint Basil of Poiana Mărului, a teacher of hesychast prayer and of the winning of the virtues through struggle, humility and discernment.

After several years under his guidance she went into Moldavia and settled, seeking stillness and spiritual labour, at the women's hermitage of Gura Carpănului, near the skete of Pocrov. In those sketes there was the same zeal for living by the instructions of the Fathers that Saint Pachomius of Gledin, bishop of Roman and founder of Pocrov, had left to the monks. She received the great schema from the hands of the elder Paisius of Neamț, and in those hermit places she met the spiritual father Joseph.

With the blessing of Paisius and Joseph she went in 1779 to Durău, where she lived eight years and where several nuns gathered under her guidance. In 1788 she was called to be abbess of the skete of Văratec, founded at that time out of the elder Paisius's wish to bring together in one place the nuns who were living as hermits in those parts, so that they might be kept from scandal and from other dangers. The news that a women's monastery had been founded drew many women who desired a holy life.

In 1803 Veniamin Costache became metropolitan of Moldavia and took particular care of the community at Văratec and of the neighbouring one at Agapia, giving them a rule for the good order of their spiritual and material life. At the request of the abbess Nazaria and of the metropolitan, on 25 June 1808 the Divan of Moldavia confirmed Văratec's perpetual possession of the land the council of the monastery of Neamț had given it with the elder Paisius's blessing.

She set herself to raise a new church of stone in place of the wooden one, and it was built in her time as abbess and consecrated in 1812. At the metropolitan's wish she also sent nuns to set the monastery of Războieni in order, where she is entered in the diptychs as its first abbess. Weighed down with the years of her labours, she passed to eternal life on 18 December 1814, and was buried in the narthex of the church of the Dormition of the Mother of God, her own foundation, on the left side beneath the choir loft.

In 2014, during works in the great church of the monastery, her bones were laid in the monastery ossuary beneath the altar of that same church. To this day her memory, as the first abbess, is for the community of Văratec a witness of love and unity in Christ. The Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church canonised her on 1 July 2025 under the title Saint Nazaria of Văratec, with her feast on 17 August; she is commemorated with [Olympias](/saints/olympias-of-varatec) and Elizabeth of Văratec on the same day.
