# John (Smirnov)

John was born on 20 March 1873 in the village of Vysokoye in the
Mikhailov district of Ryazan province, the son of the psalmist Alexis
Smirnov, all five of whose sons became priests. He finished the Ryazan
seminary and in 1904 was ordained priest to the cathedral of St Nicholas
at Zaraysk, where he settled with his family in a wooden house within the
town kremlin; the cathedral then held the famous wonderworking icon of
Nicholas of Zaraysk, glorified by many wonders, which drew pilgrims from
every end of Russia and the generous gifts of the great saint’s grateful
venerators, and kept ancient manuscript and early printed service books
and silver and gold vessels, the offerings of grand princes, great
commanders and boyars. In the warm months he served in the summer
cathedral of St Nicholas, in the cold in the winter church of John the
Forerunner, with up to a thousand worshippers at the great services; a
zealous pastor, he was made rector of the cathedral, archpriest, dean of
the churches of the Zaraysk district, and in 1930 given the mitre. In
1922 many valuables and icons were seized from the town’s churches, and
some were saved from destruction: at the suggestion of the director of
the local museum and church-council member I. P. Perlov part of the
precious plate and the ancient manuscript and printed books were handed
to the museum for safe keeping. In 1928 the authorities closed the
cathedral and the church of the Forerunner; he was appointed to the
Transfiguration church in the same town and began to work for the
cathedral’s reopening, collecting the faithful’s signatures to a petition
that it be left a working church — which the authorities cast, in their
indictment, as a group of churchmen, former people and traders spreading
counter-revolutionary rumours of the Soviet power’s imminent fall and
preparing a mass demonstration against the closing of the cathedral: the
question of closing it had been discussed at all the factory meetings,
the workers had asked the higher power to give it them as a club, the
All-Russian Central Executive Committee had granted it, and an
insignificant backward part of the workers had grown discontented — which
the churchmen had used to agitate for keeping the cathedral, the priest
Smirnov calling the church council several times and, at his initiative,
the community in May 1928 giving away free portraits of Nicholas the
Wonderworker with a note of the icon’s age and how long it had been in
the cathedral, and in the summer of 1928 carrying the cathedral icon round
all the villages of the old Zaraysk district with services at which he
said, the wonderworking icon of Nicholas is holy, it must be honoured, it
works wonders — as he said repeatedly in his sermons; and when in February
1929 the committee decided the cathedral’s closure finally in the
workers’ favour, calling a meeting of about a hundred members of the
community, announcing the decision, and urging all to agitate among the
workers and peasants to defend the cathedral and give their signatures —
the more signatures, the better our chances, for the higher power will
reckon with it and leave us the cathedral — so that the community of some
hundred and fifty grew several times over. In February 1930 the OGPU
arrested him with ten members of the community and put them in the prison
at Kolomna; the questions about his attitude to the Soviet power and the
closing of the cathedral were so framed that any answer could be used
against him, and he answered as evasively as he could: since June 1904 I
have lived at Zaraysk without a break and serve at the Transfiguration
church; in 1917 and since I have joined no party current and taken no part
in their work; I am of the clerical estate and have never owned anything
but household furniture and clothes; in religion I belong to the old
churchmen, the Tikhonites; to the seizure of church valuables in 1922 I
was indifferent, neither for their seizure nor for their being left; to
the closing of the cathedral at Zaraysk I was likewise indifferent; to the
Soviet measures I am sympathetic, since they are called for by the course
of history, and I have never gone against them and count them quite
necessary; I have never gone against the measures of the Soviet power nor
carried on counter-revolutionary agitation, and I admit no guilt in
anything. On 30 April 1930 the OGPU troika sentenced him to three years’
exile in the Northern Region. After his arrest the authorities first put
a museum display in the cathedral, then carried off the priceless plate
and ancient manuscripts and lodged the NKVD archive there, and turned the
church of the Forerunner into a cinema; his wife Zinaida Ivanovna and
five children were turned out of the church house and for a time wandered
the town with only temporary shelter, she begging and the children doing
any work to survive — until one morning she opened the door and found a
bundle of food on the step with no note, and the same the next day and the
next, until one of the daughters got steady work; they never learned who
their benefactor was, and she always held it to be the help of the holy
hierarch Nicholas. Back from exile he was made rector of the
Transfiguration church at Zaraysk. In 1937 the persecution grew, and at
the authorities’ demand secret informers gathered reports on priests
returned from exile: one wrote that he had said nothing printed in the
Soviet press could be believed, that the Soviet power lived only by deceit
or would long since have been overthrown, that they shouted the people
lived gaily and well while there was no sugar in the town shops, where in
the old days they had fed it to cattle, and that it needed infernal
patience to bear such torments — would the people never come to their
senses?; and that on 11 August at a so-called solemn service at the
Saviour church, with six priests gathered, he had said before the service:
the Soviet power is going back to the arrests of 1930; in Moscow they
have arrested Bishop John and many priests — there is your constitution,
there is your inviolability of the person; we must not fear repression but
stand firm at our post. In mid-August the investigator questioned the
false witnesses, who signed the testimony he had written, and on 20 August
1937 the NKVD arrested him and put him in a prison of the Kolomna
district, charged with voicing vile counter-revolutionary slander on the
Soviet power, calling on the people to vote against the communists at the
soviet elections, and voicing defeatist and terrorist sentiments. The
inquiry knows that you carried on counter-revolutionary work among the
people of Zaraysk, arguing that life in the Soviet Union is hard — the
believers themselves came to me repeatedly with complaints of their hard
life, and I explained to them that they bear all their torments and
burdens from themselves, and that the Lord God has sent them this to try
them as Orthodox; that on 11 August in the Saviour church you spoke of the
repressions in Moscow against the clergy in connection with the coming
elections — indeed, on 11 August I told the clergy present that arrests of
clergy had begun in Moscow, in particular of the archpriest Lebedev, and
that the arrests had begun again as in 1930; by this I wanted to warn the
clergy to be more careful; that you spread vile counter-revolutionary
slander on the Soviet press — no, that I deny; I spoke only of the
repressions against the clergy and the hard life of the people; will you
give frank testimony on the charge of counter-revolutionary agitation and
insurgent and terrorist sentiments? — no, I do not admit myself guilty of
it; I only told the Zaraysk clergy of the arrest of a number of priests
and maintained that the Soviet power and the communists had again begun
persecuting the Church and us, her servants. With that the interrogations
ended, and he was taken to an NKVD prison in Moscow; on 8 September the
NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot the next day, 9
September 1937 — 27 August of the old style, the day the Russian calendar
keeps him — and buried in an unknown common grave at the Butovo range
outside Moscow. The life was compiled by the igumen Damascene (Orlovsky);
the Russian calendar keeps him also in the synaxes of the saints of
Ryazan and Moscow and of the new martyrs of Butovo and Radonezh.
