# Cheremon of Egypt

Cheremon lived the ascetic life in Egypt, in the desert of Scetis, at the end of the fourth century or the first years of the fifth; his name is in the Lausiac History of Palladius and in the alphabetical Paterikon. His cave lay forty stades from the church and twelve from the spring of water. He died at his handiwork, more than a hundred years old. Theodore the Studite remembers him too, in the Lenten Triodion, in the sixth ode of the canon at matins on Cheesefare Saturday.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same menologion day and says the same two things. saint.gr's whole notice is that the venerable Chaeremon was an ascetic of the desert, and that he died in peace at the hour when he was working. It gives no country, no century, no year and no hymn. The couplet of the synaxarion printed above it turns his name into the farewell: «Λήξει βίου σου χαῖρε, Χαιρήμων μάκαρ. / Ἀρχὴν γὰρ εἶδες τῆς ἀμοιβῆς τῶν πόνων» — rejoice at the ending of your life, blessed Chaeremon, for you have seen the beginning of the reward of your labours.
