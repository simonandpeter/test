# Artemon of Laodicea

In the reign of Diocletian there was a great persecution of the Christians, and officers were sent through every city and country of the Roman dominion to sacrifice to the gods and to force the Christians to worship the idols, torturing and killing those who would not bend. Into the parts of Cappadocia came a count named Patricius to carry out the emperor's order. Before he arrived, Sisinius, bishop of Laodicea, hearing that the count was near, took his presbyter Artemon and some of the Christians, went by night into the temple of Artemis, broke all the idols there and burned them with fire.

Returning to the Christian church, which stood five stadia from the city, they kept their usual prayers, and the bishop strengthened the faithful: my children, we hear that the Antichrist is coming into the city meaning to kill the Christians; let nothing separate you from the love of Christ, neither fire nor sword nor beasts nor any kind of death, however terrible. When Patricius entered the city he first sacrificed in the temple of Apollo and held an inquiry, setting the imperial threats before the people, and for five days they were busy with questioning and with hunting Christians down.

Going then into the temple of Artemis to make his sacrifice and finding no idols, he asked where they were, and some of the pagans told him that the bishop Sisinius with his priest Artemon and other Christians had broken Artemis and the rest. He mounted at once and rode with armed soldiers towards the Christian church, meaning to shed the blood of the faithful and to cut Sisinius and Artemon in pieces; but when he came within about a stadium of it a great shivering took him and he fell, and such a fever burned in him that they carried him on a litter to a house nearby.

Artemon was afterwards taken and held in prison at Caesarea. A deacon named Philias came to him there and told him that it was a wild beast, speaking with a human voice, that had brought the bishop news of him, and that the bishop had sent to see whether it was true, for he grieved for him. Artemon answered: tell my master the holy bishop that I am taken to torture for Christ and sit in prison, and let him pray for me that I may overcome the cunning devices of this Christ-hating executioner and be counted worthy to share with my Lord Jesus Christ. They kissed one another and parted, the deacon to his own place and Artemon to his chains; and the bishop prayed with tears, asking the God who delivered Daniel from the lions and the Three Children from Nebuchadnezzar's furnace to deliver His servant.

Brought out for questioning, the count called him an old man and asked his name. «Mă numesc Artemon, rob al lui Hristos Dumnezeul meu» — I am called Artemon, the servant of Christ my God. Are you the one who broke the carved image of the great goddess? I am. Patricius mocked him, saying he had taken him for a servant of Artemis because his name was hers, and that she must have spared him for the likeness of the name; Artemon answered that he would overcome the count's tyranny too, by praying to his God, and that a dead and dumb idol had been easy enough to overthrow.

The account is a long one, and the end of it is this. The bishop of Caesarea, learning all that had happened — that many had been baptised by Artemon and that he was teaching the people the word of truth — came to him with the faithful, kissed him, prayed, and marked with a sign the place where the martyr had suffered, and afterwards built a church on it. Of the martyrs Artemon had baptised he ordained some priests and some deacons, and set Vitalis as bishop over the parts of Palestine. Artemon kissed the bishop and the people and went out to the part of Asia appointed him; caught up by an angel on the road, he was carried to the place he had been sent to, and there worked great wonders by the power of God and brought many to Him. Afterwards the unbelievers seized him and cut off his head.
