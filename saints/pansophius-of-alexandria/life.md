# Pansophius of Alexandria

Pansophius was of Alexandria, in the years of the emperor Decius, whom saint.gr puts at 250. His father was called Nilus and held the rank of proconsul. Pansophius studied Greek letters to a high degree, and the holy Scripture with them; and when his father died he distributed the estate — the page's account of what he did next is a little longer than what is set down here.

He was denounced to the Augustalis of Alexandria, who ordered him brought before him. There Pansophius, with the virtue and the great learning that marked him, broke down the errors of the idols one by one. Humiliated, the tyrant ordered him beaten to death, and so he gloriously received the crown of martyrdom.

The page prints no hymn for him, and gives no year for his death. Over him it sets a couplet that turns the beating into a gain: strike Pansophius the more, you deceivers, for so you weave him the more crowns.
