# Lazar of Serbia

Lazar was born in the village of Prilepac in Serbia in the year 1329, and as a child showed such qualities that he drew the notice of the emperor Stefan Dušan, who took him into his palace at Skopje; there his kindness, his courage and his piety won him everyone's regard. He married a kinswoman of the tsar, Saint Milica, and had by her five daughters and three sons. After Dušan the kingdom of Serbia was ruled by Stefan Uroš V, and Lazar succeeded him with the title of prince.

He survived rivalry and attempts on his life, and set about the plan he had formed, of uniting the divided Serbs so that they could stand against the Turks. Besides his political work he showed a great love for the Church. His first care on coming to the throne was to reconcile the Serbian Church with Constantinople, which had broken communion over Dušan's proclamation of a Serbian patriarchate; an embassy led by the monk Isaiah obtained the lifting of the anathema from the patriarch Philotheos in the year 1375.

He founded many churches and monasteries, in Serbia and outside it, especially in the Holy Land and on Mount Athos; after 1371 he willingly received the disciples of Gregory of Sinai who had left the Holy Mountain before the Turkish threat, and he saw to the raising of the monasteries of Gornjak and Ravanica, which remained his chief foundations. Nicodemus the Hagiorite records in his Synaxarion that Lazar gave to the monastery of Vatopedi an imperial cross of seven fingers' length holding a great piece of the Precious Wood, together with the Girdle of the Mother of God, and that it was still there in his day.

He made several campaigns against the Turks and was always thwarted by the divisions among his own people. At last he gathered the nobles and commanders at Kruševac and asked them to forget their quarrels and work together against the enemies of the Cross; then the Liturgy was served and all the soldiers communicated. Facing the army of Sultan Murad, twice the size of his own, on the plain of Kosovo on 15 June 1389, he was beheaded after being wounded sixteen times in the battle; other accounts say he got into the enemy camp with a few brave men, one of whom wounded Murad, who died three days later after ordering the execution of Lazar and his companions.

It is said that on the eve of the battle an angel of the Lord appeared to him and asked whether he preferred earthly power or the kingdom of heaven, and he answered that earthly power would be a small thing, corruptible and passing, while the kingdom of heaven is eternal. Before the execution, as he bent his head to the sword, he prayed that the Creator would forgive what he had done against his will and save his people, or rather God's people. Sultan Bayezid, hearing with what dignity he had met his death, allowed him honourable burial in the Church of the Ascension at Prizren; a year later, at the reburial, a wonderful fragrance came from the incorrupt relics, and they were moved to Ravanica, thence in the great Serbian exodus of the seventeenth century to Vrdnik, after the Second World War to the cathedral at Belgrade, and in 1989 back again to Ravanica.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 15 June, the day of the battle, and counts him one of the Serbian magnates who ruled the Serbian realm after the emperor Dušan. After the death of the emperor Uroš, it says, Lazar was crowned emperor of the Serbs by the patriarch — a man this paragraph does not name, because he is this same day’s own fifth entry and no folder of this corpus holds him. The heading over the entry nevertheless calls him prince of Serbia, which is the grade this folder states, and the crowning is set down here as the book’s word and not as a change of title.

He sent an embassy to Constantinople to ask that the anathema be lifted from the Serbian people, and he fought the Turkish power several times over. At the last he met the Turkish emperor Murad on the plain of Kosovo, on the fifteenth of June in 1389, and was cut down there — the day and the year the folder already held from the Romanian page, stated again by this one. His body was carried to his own foundation of Ravanica near Ćuprija and buried there, and was taken later to Ravanica in Srem.

From there, in the Second World War and in the year 1942, it was brought to Belgrade and laid in the cathedral church, where this book says it rests incorrupt to this day and offers consolation and healing to all who turn to him in prayer; and it closes by noting that his holy relics now rest in the monastery of Ravanica again. He renewed Hilandar and Gornjak, it says, and raised Ravanica and Lazarica, and he was the founder and benefactor of a Russian monastery on the Holy Mountain and of many other churches and monasteries besides — that monastery being called after a saint the corpus keeps, so the attestation note names it and this paragraph does not.
