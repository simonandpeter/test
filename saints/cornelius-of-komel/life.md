# Cornelius of Komel

Cornelius of Komel was born at Rostov in Russia in 1455, of the noble boyar family of the Kryukovs. His brother Loukianos served at the court of the great prince of Moscow, Basil Vasilyevich Temny, and when Loukianos decided to withdraw to the monastery of Saint Cyril of the White Lake, that decision carried Cornelius with him, for he already loved the monastic life. He followed his brother into the house and there began a hard ascesis: even in the monastery bakery, where he served, he wore heavy chains in order to labour the more, and he copied church books.

A little later he went to the monastery of Saint Gennadius at Novgorod, but preferring the life of stillness he withdrew in the end to the forest of Komel in 1497. There, in 1501, he raised a wooden church in honour of the Mother of God, and soon after Metropolitan Simon appointed him hieromonk of the monastery. As the number of monks grew, Cornelius built a new church as well and wrote a monastic rule on the basis of the Typikon of Saint Joseph of Volokolamsk and of [Nilus of Sora](/saints/nilus-of-sora).

saint.gr singles out his love for the suffering, the poor and orphans, and adds that he built a church in honour of [Anthony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great), for whom he had a particular reverence and whom he was counted worthy to see in visions. He fell asleep in peace in 1537. The page prints no hymn to him.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery makes him the monastic father of [Philip of Irap](/saints/philip-of-irap), whom it keeps on the Julian 14 November. Its life of Philip has an orphan boy of twelve living on alms beside the monastery of Komel, whom Cornelius received into the house and tonsured three years later with the name Philip, and who went on to found a hermitage by the river Irap in the country of the White Lake. The troparion that calendar prints for Philip says the same in its first lines: that he was a zealous follower of his teacher, the venerable father Cornelius, and walked in his steps. Nothing of this stood in this folder before, whose life came from saint.gr.

The Russian calendar's reading of him is the fullest of the day. He came of the boyar family of the Крюковы; his brother Лукиан served at the great prince's court in Moscow and, in old age, withdrew to the monastery of Кирилл Белозерский, and the younger brother, who had wanted the hermit's life from early on, followed him there. Tonsured, he began with the hard obedience of the bakehouse, wore heavy irons, and in his few free hours copied church books. Later he left the Belozersk house for love of solitude, visited Rostov, and was pressed to stay at Novgorod by the hierarch Геннадий but settled instead in a wild place near the city; when people began to come to him there he moved to the Савватиева hermitage in Tver, and in 1497 to the forest of Komel near Vologda, where he built himself a cell.

Monks gathered to him, and in 1501 he built a wooden church of the Entry of the Mother of God and was ordained priest by the metropolitan Симон. In 1512, the brotherhood having grown, he built a stone church and wrote them a rule drawn from those of Иосиф Волоцкий and Нил Сорский — the third rule, the page says, written by a Russian saint for monastics. He was open-handed to the poor and in a famine set up a shelter for children in the monastery yard, and for that love of the poor and of orphans he was granted more than once a vision of Антоний Великий, for whom he had a particular reverence and in whose honour he raised a church. The strictness of his life set some of the brethren murmuring and he had to leave, settling on lake Sura seventy versts off and spending some time at the Trinity-Sergius Lavra; at the monks' petition the great prince Василий Иванович persuaded him back, and he gave the rule of the house to his disciple Лаврентий and shut himself in his cell. When the Tatars came into the Vologda country he withdrew with the brethren to the Belozersk region. He died at eighty-two on 19 May 1537.

The page adds that his general commemoration on this day was established on 25 January 1600 by the patriarch Иов and a council of bishops, that his life was written by his disciple Нафанаил in 1589, and that the rule he wrote survives. It names many disciples of his who are themselves kept as saints, among them Лаврентий and Кассиан Комельские on 16 мая, whose folders in this corpus took their own russian rows from that day.
