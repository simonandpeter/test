# Cyril of the White Lake

Cyril was born in the Galich country of Kostroma of pious and God-loving parents, and tradition says, the page reports, that he had his divine calling from his mother's womb. From childhood he gave himself to asceticism and prayer, and received the call from God through a divine vision; so he left his father's house to live in the monastery of the Caves of Pskov. When his parents learned of their son's decision they took the monastic road themselves — first his mother, with the name Helen, and then his father, with the name Barsanuphius, whose spiritual guidance the abbot assigned to his own son Cyril.

As a monk he impressed the abbot, the venerable Cornelius whom the Greek calendar honours on 20 February, and the brothers, by his virtues, his obedience, his study of the holy Scriptures, his severe asceticism and his prayer. After his father's repose he asked the abbot's blessing to go out and practise asceticism in a desert place. Some twenty years of eremitic life in various ascetic places of the Russian land brought him at last to the outskirts of Moscow, Novgorod and Pskov, where he lived by prayer and fasting.

After a succession of divine signs and visions of the Mother of God he founded a monastery and raised two churches at the White Lake, and his holy manner of life drew new brothers around him; God endowed him with the gifts of wonderworking and of discernment. Having foreseen his end, he fell asleep in peace in the year 1532.

The page then says that the Church keeps his memory on 15 June and on 7 November — neither of them the day on which this same site prints him. The day recorded here is the day page that names him. No hymn is printed for him.
