# Hypatius the Healer

saint.gr's calendar for 31 Μαρτίου names the venerable Hypatius the healer, and the page behind the line is four short sentences about how he lived. He lived keeping a strict fast. At night he made his prayers and slept little; he ate a little bread and drank only water. He gave himself wholly to the service of the sick, and he received from God the gift of healing.

That is all of it. The page gives no century, no country, no monastery, no bishop, no teacher, no relic and no hymn, and it tells not one of the healings it credits him with; this folder therefore records no date and no place. What the source keeps of him is a rule of life and a charism, and that is what is kept here.

One caution belongs on the folder, because the Greek calendar sets two men of this name on one day. The other is Hypatius, bishop of Gangra, who sat at the First Ecumenical Council and was stoned to death by Novatianists in 326; he has his own entry, his own page and his own hymns on saint.gr, and nothing of his is here. This Hypatius is identified by his epithet and by his nursing of the sick, and by nothing else.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day as the Greek one and tells the four things the Greek page could not. Its two readings of him are nearly as short as saint.gr’s — one of them repeats the Greek account almost sentence for sentence: he was famous for the labour of severe fasting and of unceasing vigil in prayer, standing at prayer through the nights and sleeping very little, and eating only bread and water; he gave himself wholly to the service of the sick, and for that self-forgetting labour received from God the grace of wonderful healings, so that people in all kinds of sickness often run to his intercession. That is the same rule of life and the same charism and in the same order, which is what makes the two pages one man.

What the Russian adds is where, when, what his obedience was, and where he lies. He practised asceticism in the Kyiv Caves monastery in the fourteenth century; his obedience in the house was to nurse the sick; and his relics rest incorrupt in the Far Caves. The calendar keeps him a second time with the fathers of those Far Caves on 28 августа. The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion for him, which are below and which the Greek calendar printed none of; the kontakion turns on the one word the whole record of him hangs from — ever adding labours to labours, he was never found idle, and was fitly called the diligent one, taking his name from his works.
