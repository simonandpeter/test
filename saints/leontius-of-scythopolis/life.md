# Leontius of Scythopolis

saint.gr names six men together for this day and tells one story for all of them. [Aphrodisius](/saints/aphrodisius-of-scythopolis), [Misdas](/saints/misdas), [Macrobius](/saints/macrobius-of-scythopolis), [Valerian](/saints/valerian-4-may), Leontius and [Antoninus](/saints/antoninus-of-scythopolis) were martyrs, it says, and with them others suffered at Scythopolis in Syria. Nothing on the page gives their country, their trades, their years or the emperor under whom they died.

These saints presented themselves of their own accord before the ruler of their country and reproached him most sharply for his inhuman doings against the Christians. Then they smashed the altars of the idols to pieces, and so provoked the fury of the idolaters, who set on them with stones and clubs and killed them all. The Christians took up their bodies, buried them with honours, and raised a church over them; and their synaxis used to be kept at the apostoleion of James the Brother of God, which stands inside the venerable house of the Mother of God at the Chalkoprateia.

His couplet is the strangest thing on the page. It is headed for Leontius, Antoninus and a Meles, and it says that Leontius and two fellow-contenders ran to the sword like lions; but the day's listing names six men and not seven, the notice gives six head lines and not seven, and no Meles appears anywhere else. Whether that is a seventh martyr the verses remember, or a word doing some other work, saint.gr does not say, and nothing is claimed for him here.
