# Felicitas, with her seven sons

The Ohrid Prologue keeps a martyr Felicitas and her seven sons on this day, and gives the eight of them a single short paragraph. She was a Christian, it says, and in the time of the emperor Antoninus, in the year 164, she was condemned to death together with all seven of her sons. Of her city, her house, her husband, and the names and the ages of the seven, the entry says nothing whatever; it counts them and leaves them unnamed.

What it does keep is her one prayer. She asked God for this only: that she should not be put to death before her sons, so that she might be there to give them courage while they were tortured and killed, and that they should not deny Christ under it. And so it fell out, the Prologue says, by God's own ordering.

„С радошћу је ова ненадмашна мајка испраћала једног по једног сина, док их није свих седам испратила и видела погубљење“ — with joy, it writes, this unsurpassed mother saw off one son after another, until she had seen off all seven and seen them put to death. Then she too received the martyr's death, giving thanks to God. The entry closes by saying that all of them suffered at Rome, and that their relics are there.

*After the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 25. јануар ст. ст. — [the day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-02-07&prolog=1); read 3 October 2026.*
