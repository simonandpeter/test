# Maximus the Greek

Maximus was the son of a rich Greek official of Arta in Epirus, and was well educated. In his youth he travelled a great deal and studied languages and the intellectual disciplines in Europe, at Paris, at Florence and at Venice. Coming home again he went to the Holy Mountain and was made a monk at Vatopedi, where he gave himself to the old manuscripts left there by the emperors Andronicus Palaeologus and John Cantacuzene, both of whom, the page notes, had become monks themselves.

Prince Basil III of Moscow, who reigned from 1505 to 1533, wanted an inventory made of the Greek manuscripts and books of his mother Sophia Palaeologina, and asked the Protos of the Holy Mountain, the abbot Simeon, for a translator. Maximus was chosen, and at Moscow he was set to turn the patristic and liturgical books into Slavonic, beginning with the annotated Psalter. Slavonic was not his own tongue, and the page says plainly that there are inconsistencies in what he made. Metropolitan Varlaam valued the work; his successor Daniel did not.

Daniel asked him for a Slavonic version of the Church History of Theodoret of Cyrus, and Maximus refused outright, saying that the letters of the heretic Arius in it could be a danger to the less learned. The breach did not stop his work: he wrote against Muslims, Latins and pagans, translated Chrysostom's commentaries on Matthew and John, and wrote books of his own. When Basil wished to put away his wife Solomonia because she had borne no children, Maximus sent him a work on the right faith telling him not to give way to the passions of the flesh, and for that he was imprisoned.

His mistranslations were then read as deliberate corruptions of the text. Prison was hard, but an angel appeared to him and said, «Îndură, Avvo!» — endure, Abba; through this passing pain you will be freed from eternal torment. On the wall he wrote in charcoal a canon to the Holy Spirit which is still read in church. He was seventy when the ban on him was lifted, and he spent his last years at the Trinity–St Sergius Lavra, translating the Psalter out of Greek with his servant and disciple Nil. He died on 21 January 1556 and was buried by the north-west wall of the church of the Holy Spirit there, where many miracles followed.
