# Vsevolod (Potyominsky)

Vsevolod Ioannovich Potyominsky was born on 30 March 1870 in the town of
Nikolsk in Vologda province, the son of John Potyominsky, sexton of the
Annunciation church of Lyamenga, and finished three classes of the Vologda
seminary. From 1890 he was deacon of the Simonovolomsky church in the
Ustyug district, and from 1900 of the Presentation church in the Nikolsk
district; from 1891 to 1900 he taught in the school at Simonovolomskaya
Pustyn in the Ustyug district. In 1903 Bishop Alexis (Sobolev) of Vologda
and Totma ordained him priest to the Nicholas church of Semukovo, where he
was also teacher of the Law of God in the people’s school, and from 1910
he taught in the primary school of Kyrs. He had a large library, with many
books on agronomy among them, opened a school in his village where he
taught from textbooks of his own, and had such standing with his
parishioners that several times they defended him from arrest. He was
tried in 1928, 1934 and 1936, deprived of the vote and turned out of his
house with his family. In the winter of 1937 the church at Semukovo was
closed; the building was meant for a club, and more than once the crosses
carved on its walls were attacked, but the crosses miraculously appeared
again. He could be arrested only by a trick, being summoned as if to a
christening; on 7 August 1937 he was put in the Syktyvkar prison, charged
with systematically carrying on counter-revolutionary activity against the
measures of the Soviet power, spreading in June 1937 provocative rumours of
war and the fall of the Soviet power, and preparing for the elections to
the soviets by putting up candidates from among counter-revolutionary
elements and agitating among the people for them. The case was conducted
with the grossest breaches — no right of defence, an unreasoned indictment
never confirmed by the prosecutor — and he did not admit guilt. By sentence
of the troika of the NKVD of the Komi republic of 16 September he was shot
on 19 September 1937 — 6 September of the old style, the day the Russian
calendar keeps him — near Verkhny Chov outside Syktyvkar, and by decision
of the Holy Synod of 6 October 2001 was included in the synaxis of the new
martyrs and confessors of Russia; the calendar keeps him also in the
synaxis of the saints of Komi, and its life follows the Orthodox
Encyclopaedia.
