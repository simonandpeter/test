# Peter (Titov)

Peter, his life says, was one of those who chose the service of God and of the Church knowingly, in a time of merciless persecution: it was plain to him when he chose it that a power professing militant godlessness with such fury would spare neither the Church nor her ministers, and the nearness of arrest, and of a violent death, only made him the readier to spend all his strength on God and on his flock. He was born in 1890 in the village of Titovskoye in the Vesyegonsk district of the province of Tver, the son of a psalmist, finished the Tver seminary, was made psalmist at Fyodorovskoye, and in 1924 was ordained priest to the church at Arkhangelskoye in the Kimry district.

On Sunday 9 February 1930 the service in that church ran from six in the morning until four in the afternoon, with four hundred people there by one account and six hundred by another, because every church around had been closed. Kimry communists stopping the night in the village made their own inquiry, went to the chairman of the village soviet and demanded that the priest be summoned to the OGPU to answer for it; one of them gave evidence against him on 11 February, and on 17 February he was arrested — the witnesses being questioned only afterwards, the verdict and the closing of the church having been settled in advance. He was sentenced to three years of exile for counter-revolutionary sermons.

Back from exile he lived at Fyodorovskoye, where his father-in-law was the priest, and served in that church as psalmist; in July 1937 he was given the church at Selikhovo. He was arrested on 27 October and held in the Tver prison. He admitted the old sentence and nothing else: no slander of the authorities, no wish for foreign intervention, no anti-Soviet speech at the burial of his father-in-law's daughter, who had herself served a term, and the selling of communion bread below cost to draw schoolchildren into the church was the warden's doing and not his. «В систематической антисоветской деятельности я виновным себя не признаю.» The investigation closed on 18 November, the troika sentenced him on 25 November, and he was shot on 27 November 1937. He was canonised for the whole church in August 2000.

This is the longest of the ten life pages, and the tool that fetched it cut some seventeen hundred characters out of its middle, keeping the beginning and the end. What stands above was read from the parts that came through whole; the cut fell in the record of the 1930 interrogation.

The day's line names twenty-seven new martyrs of 1937 together — twenty-three presbyters, a deacon, a hieromonk, a lay martyr and a woman — and the calendar carries a life page for ten of them, of whom he is one. The men of that line were tried separately, in different districts and different prisons, and no one of these lives names another of them, so no relation is recorded between them here.
