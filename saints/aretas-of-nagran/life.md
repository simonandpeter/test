# Aretas of Nagran

In the days when Justin reigned in the Greek land and Elesbaan in Ethiopia, both of them right-believing and good, there rose in Arabia — which was once called Saba and is now the land of the Homerites — a lawless king named Dunaan, a Jew, a blasphemer of the name of Jesus Christ and a great enemy of the faithful, whose counsellors and soldiers were Jews or else pagans who worshipped the sun, the moon and idols. He set himself to drive every Christian out of his kingdom.

Elesbaan of Ethiopia, hearing of the persecution, gathered his soldiers, made war on him and beat him, and made him tributary; but after a little Dunaan rose again, destroyed the garrisons Elesbaan had left, and ordered everywhere that Christians should either take the Jewish faith or be killed without mercy. In all his kingdom nobody dared confess Christ except in one city, Nagran, where the faith had been planted by the envoys Constantius the son of Constantine the Great sent to the people of Saba, who had taught them and built churches there.

Dunaan besieged Nagran, and when he had taken it by an oath he did not mean to keep, he summoned the honoured men and the heads of the city. Among them was blessed Aretas, greatest of them in age and in understanding, in office and in honour, ninety-five years old, a nobleman and voivode to whom the whole ordering of the city was entrusted; by his wise counsel and his able governing the citizens had held out against the enemy a long time and very bravely.

The Romanian life tells at length what followed: the questioning, the refusals, and the killing of the citizens with Aretas at their head; and it tells also of the noblewoman who answered the king to his face that she would not deny her God nor live among God's enemies in the dwellings of sinners, and who was led with her daughters bareheaded and with loosened hair through the streets for a mockery, and comforted the women who wept for her.

Elesbaan came again, took the city, killed the unbelievers of the palace with the queen, and sent word to the emperor Justin and to the bishop of Alexandria that the Lord had avenged Christian blood. The archbishop sent bishops and priests to the Homerites to teach and to baptise; Elesbaan rebuilt the church Dunaan had burnt in Nagran, adorned the tombs of the martyrs, set free all the Christians, and made the son of Saint Aretas voivode of the city.

Having set a good and right-believing man, Abramius, over the land, and having ordered the laws, Elesbaan went home, gave thanks, and sent his crown to Jerusalem. Then he left the world: he went out of the palace and the city by night, dressed like a poor man and known to nobody, and shut himself in a cell of a monastery near by, which he never left again. He lived on a dry biscuit every third day, and sometimes on figs and dates, and had in his cell nothing but a piece of felt, a wooden water vessel and a basket; wine and oil he never tasted. After fifteen years in the monastic order he departed in peace to the Lord.
