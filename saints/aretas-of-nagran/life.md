# Aretas of Nagran

In the days when Justin reigned in the Greek land and Elesbaan in Ethiopia, both of them right-believing and good, there rose in Arabia — which was once called Saba and is now the land of the Homerites — a lawless king named Dunaan, a Jew, a blasphemer of the name of Jesus Christ and a great enemy of the faithful, whose counsellors and soldiers were Jews or else pagans who worshipped the sun, the moon and idols. He set himself to drive every Christian out of his kingdom.

Elesbaan of Ethiopia, hearing of the persecution, gathered his soldiers, made war on him and beat him, and made him tributary; but after a little Dunaan rose again, destroyed the garrisons Elesbaan had left, and ordered everywhere that Christians should either take the Jewish faith or be killed without mercy. In all his kingdom nobody dared confess Christ except in one city, Nagran, where the faith had been planted by the envoys Constantius the son of Constantine the Great sent to the people of Saba, who had taught them and built churches there.

Dunaan besieged Nagran, and when he had taken it by an oath he did not mean to keep, he summoned the honoured men and the heads of the city. Among them was blessed Aretas, greatest of them in age and in understanding, in office and in honour, ninety-five years old, a nobleman and voivode to whom the whole ordering of the city was entrusted; by his wise counsel and his able governing the citizens had held out against the enemy a long time and very bravely.

The Romanian life tells at length what followed: the questioning, the refusals, and the killing of the citizens with Aretas at their head; and it tells also of the noblewoman who answered the king to his face that she would not deny her God nor live among God's enemies in the dwellings of sinners, and who was led with her daughters bareheaded and with loosened hair through the streets for a mockery, and comforted the women who wept for her.

Elesbaan came again, took the city, killed the unbelievers of the palace with the queen, and sent word to the emperor Justin and to the bishop of Alexandria that the Lord had avenged Christian blood. The archbishop sent bishops and priests to the Homerites to teach and to baptise; Elesbaan rebuilt the church Dunaan had burnt in Nagran, adorned the tombs of the martyrs, set free all the Christians, and made the son of Saint Aretas voivode of the city.

Having set a good and right-believing man, Abramius, over the land, and having ordered the laws, Elesbaan went home, gave thanks, and sent his crown to Jerusalem. Then he left the world: he went out of the palace and the city by night, dressed like a poor man and known to nobody, and shut himself in a cell of a monastery near by, which he never left again. He lived on a dry biscuit every third day, and sometimes on figs and dates, and had in his cell nothing but a piece of felt, a wooden water vessel and a basket; wine and oil he never tasted. After fifteen years in the monastic order he departed in peace to the Lord.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 24 October, and saint.gr sets out the politics behind the killing. He was one of the leading men of the city of Negra in Ethiopia, it says, in the time of Justin, of Elesbaan the Christian king of Ethiopia and of Dounaan the Jewish king of the Homerites. When Elesbaan had subdued Dounaan he set a garrison in the city; the Jewish king then revolted, killed the guards and marched against Negra, took it, and slaughtered many Christians — among them Arethas, who for all his old age was strengthening the Christians and urging them on to martyrdom. The page's couplet for the day says that Arethas, beheaded, brought to God many martyrs beheaded likewise; and its own notice for the holy woman and her infant says that [the two of them](/saints/holy-woman-and-her-infant) were martyred with him.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this same day, 24 октября ст. ст., and gives him an office the other two did not: the name link behind his line calls him «градоправитель», the governor of the city of Negran in Arabia, whose people were Christians. Its account of the siege is fuller than theirs. The Jewish king of Arabia, resolving to destroy Christianity in his country, issued a decree that all the followers of Christ be killed, and came against the city with a great army; his heralds proclaimed at the walls that he would leave alive only those who denied the crucified Galilean and His Cross, which they called «знамение проклятия», the sign of a curse.

Unable to take the city by force, he turned to deceit, and swore that he would not compel the Christians to become Jews but only lay a tribute on the city. **The citizens did not listen to their governor's advice**, believed the oath and opened the gates. Next day the king ordered a great fire lit and all the clergy of the city thrown into it, to frighten the rest: four hundred and twenty-seven men were burnt. The governor and the other elders were put in prison.

He then sent preachers through the city to turn the Christians to Judaism, and spoke himself with those brought out of the prison, saying: «Я не требую от вас, чтобы вы отреклись от Бога неба и земли, не хочу, чтобы вы поклонялись идолам, но желаю только, чтобы не верили вы в Иисуса Христа, потому что Распятый был человек, а не Бог» — I do not ask you to deny the God of heaven and earth or to worship idols, only not to believe in Jesus Christ, because the Crucified was a man and not God. The martyrs answered that Jesus is God the Word, the second person of the Holy Trinity, who took human flesh for the salvation of men; «Не отречемся от Христа, ибо Он для нас – Жизнь, а смерть за Него – приобретение». More than four thousand Christians of the city and the country round it — men, women, old people and children — were put to death.

The day page carries his troparion (глас 1) and kontakion (глас 4), both of them addressed to him and to those who suffered with him. [Syncletica](/saints/syncletica-of-nagran), whom the same day keeps with her two unnamed daughters, suffered in the same city under the same king, and the Ethiopian king who avenged this blood is on the day as well.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 24 October and counts the company: this holy martyr suffered for the faith of Christ with more than four thousand Christians — priests, monks and nuns, citizens, women and children. Aretas was governor of the city of Nagran in the land of the Homerites, southern Arabia, and he was ninety-five years old when he suffered, which is an age and not a year and changes nothing here.

The land of the Homerites was ruled then by a Jew named Dunaan, a wicked persecutor of Christians. Resolved to wipe the Christians out of his country altogether, he surrounded the Christian city of Nagran and sent word first that if they would not renounce Christ he would put them all to death. The citizens shut the gates, and Dunaan attacked the walls a long while without success. Then the lawless prince swore to them that he would do them no harm — they had only to open the gates so that he might come in and take the tribute they owed him, and he would withdraw at once.

The Christians believed him, and opened the gates. Then the perjured man called the elder Aretas to him with all the clergy and the other notable citizens, and cut them all down with the sword; and after that he made a terrible slaughter through the city. The Byzantine emperor Justin, hearing of it, was greatly grieved, and wrote to [Elesbaan of Ethiopia](/saints/elesbaan-of-ethiopia) to march on Dunaan with an army and avenge the innocent blood of the Christians. Elesbaan obeyed Justin, fell on the prince of the Homerites, defeated him, destroyed his whole army and put him to the sword.

By a revelation of God a pious man, Abramius, was made prince of the Homerites, and again by a revelation of God a Saint Gregory was made their archbishop. And at Nagran the Christians rebuilt the church of the Holy Trinity which Dunaan had burned, and raised a church to Saint Aretas and the other martyrs of Nagran. They suffered and received the martyr’s crown from the Lord in the year 523, which is the year this folder already held.
