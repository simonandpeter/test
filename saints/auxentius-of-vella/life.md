# Auxentius of Vella

Auxentius was born in 1690 in the province of Vella in Ioannina, saint.gr says, of devout parents. While still young he went to Constantinople and worked at the furriers' trade in the khan called Mahmut Pasha. Later he came to desire amusements and pleasures; he left his trade and took service on the royal ships, where he made merry with his Turkish companions.

Those same companions then slandered him, saying that he had denied Christ and had confessed their own religion. Frightened, Auxentius left the ships, bought himself a boat and worked as a boatman. But he repented of his earlier faults and wished to bear witness for Christ, and meeting by chance Gregory of Xeropotamou, synkellos of the Great Church, he confessed that longing to him.

Afterwards sailors of the fleet met him in the street, recognised him and led him before the judge. At the tribunal Auxentius confessed that he was a Christian, in spite of hard tortures, and they shut him in the prison of Pasha-Kapisi, where the synkellos Gregory visited him and urged him to stand bravely before the unbelieving. Questioned again, he held to it: «Εγώ χριστιανός γεννήθηκα και χριστιανός θέλω ν' αποθάνω» — I was born a Christian and a Christian I wish to die.

They condemned him to death by beheading, and beheaded him on 25 January 1720 at Constantinople. saint.gr says that his skull is kept at the monastery of Xeropotamou on the Holy Mountain, and that he is remembered among the new martyrs who suffered after the fall of Constantinople.
