# Kerkyra

Kerkyra lived in the first century and was the daughter of the king Kerkyllinos, and she came to believe in Christ from the tortures of the holy apostles [Jason](/saints/jason-of-tarsus) and [Sosipater](/saints/sosipater-of-iconium). She confessed Christ, sold all her jewellery, and gave the money to the poor. When her father learned of it and could not turn her mind, he handed her over to an Ethiopian to be corrupted; but the Ethiopian came to believe in Christ through her, and was put to death for it.

Kerkyra herself was tortured in many ways, and at the last they hung her up and killed her with arrows. The apostles' own page for the same day tells the story from their side: the torments they suffered from the eparch Capreanus moved the daughter of the ruler, Kerkyra, who embraced Christianity; and Kerkyllinos in the end repented, was catechized, baptized, and renamed Sebastianos. Her couplet says that the queen, pricked all over by the casts of the arrows, shone out of her wounds as out of pearls.

saint.gr gives no place for her and no year but the century, and it prints no hymn of hers. The Ethiopian whom the page does not name is very probably the Christodoulos the Ethiopian that the same day's listing keeps; but that page says of itself only that he died in the first century and that he is «πιθανόν», possibly, the same man as the one kept on 26 February, so nothing is written of him here.

The Serbian church keeps her on this day, where the Greek keeps her on 29 April, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells her end differently in two places. While the king of Corfu was putting the apostles [Jason](/saints/jason-of-tarsus) and [Sosipater](/saints/sosipater-of-iconium) to the question, his daughter watched the torments of God's men from a window; and learning why they were being tortured she declared herself a Christian too and gave away all her ornaments to the poor.

Her father, angry with his own daughter, shut her in a prison of her own; and when that did not turn her from Christ he gave the order and they set the prison on fire. The prison burned and the maiden stayed alive, and seeing the wonder many of the people were baptized. Then, in a fury, he had her tied to a tree and killed with arrows. **There is no Ethiopian in this account**, where the Greek notice has her handed to one to be corrupted and has him believe through her and die for it; and this book drowns her father at sea as he sailed to hunt down those who had believed, giving the name Sebastian to a new king who took the faith, where the Greek has the father himself repent and be renamed Sebastianos. Neither difference is resolved here. **The entry gives no year**, so the first century this folder already carried stands as it was.

The Russian calendar's reading of the island gives her the one thing the other books do not. When her father had her shut up alone and sent in to her a robber and fornicator named [Murin](/saints/murin-of-corfu) to dishonour the bride of Christ, a bear fell upon the man at the door of the prison; she heard the noise, drove the beast off in the Name of Christ, healed the robber's wounds by her prayer, and taught him the faith, so that he declared himself a Christian on the spot and was put to death for it. Her father then had the prison set on fire and she came out of it alive, and only after that was she hanged on a tree, choked with bitter smoke and shot through with arrows.
