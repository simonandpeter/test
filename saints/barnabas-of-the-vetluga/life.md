# Barnabas of the Vetluga

saint.gr's calendar for 11 Ιουνίου names him and gives no life. Its whole notice is one sentence: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέριες για τον βίο του Ρώσου μοναχού Βαρνάβα» — we have no details of the life of the Russian monk Barnabas. What the day's line adds is the river he is named for, «του ποταμού Βετολούγκα», the Vetluga; and the page's gloss on his name, which is Hebrew and means son of consolation.

So the Greek calendar keeps of him a name, a nation, a monastic habit and a river, and nothing more. There is no century on the page, no place of birth, no monastery, no manner of death, no synaxarion verse and no hymn. Two other Barnabases stand on the same Greek day — the apostle of Cyprus, and a wonderworker of Limassol whose entry the site itself marks as uncorroborated — and this monk is neither of them.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the same day and has a great deal more of him than a name and a river. He was born at Veliky Ustyug, and before he went into the wilderness he was a priest of one of that city's churches. The raids of the Cheremis drove him from his homeland, and in 1417 he settled on the bank of the Vetluga, on the Red Hill, where he lived alone for twenty-eight years, «Богу работая в псалмопении и молитвах, питаясь былием и вершием дубовым» — working for God in psalmody and prayers, feeding on herbs and the tops of oaks.

There was no human dwelling within fifty versts of that hill. The man who wrote his life says that the wild beasts came to him, «дивии звери, медведи многи», many bears, living close by his cell, and that he went about among them as among cattle, looking at them and taking comfort in them, and that they were gentle to him. People came to the hermit now and again «благословения ради», for the sake of a blessing, and to them he foretold that after his departure God would multiply the dwellings of men on the bank of the Vetluga, and that monks would live where he had lived.

He died in great old age on 11 June 1445, and both things he had foretold followed. Monks came «от разных стран», from various lands, to the place of his labours, and husbandmen came after them, and the people multiplied along that whole river as far as the Volga. On the Red Hill the monks built two churches, one in honour of the Holy Trinity and one over his grave «во имя святителя Николая Чудотворца», and founded the coenobitic monastery that took the name of the Варнавинская пустынь. The town of Варнавин grew up where the house had stood and its chief church became the city cathedral.

His life was written in 1639 by a monk of that house, «честнейшим иеромонахом Иосифом (Дядкиным)», who was afterwards head of the printing office in Moscow, and in the same year his relics were attested by order of the patriarch Иоасаф, so that the wonders worked at his grave might be certified. One other saint is named beside him in the reading, and only in passing: «по преданию», by tradition, in 1439, before he settled on the river Unzha, «преподобный Макарий (память 25 июля)» came here for instruction and teaching. The calendar gives nothing of that man but a forename and a day, so no relation is written from it.
