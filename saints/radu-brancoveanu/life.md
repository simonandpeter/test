# Radu Brâncoveanu

[Constantine Brâncoveanu](/saints/constantine-brancoveanu) ruled Wallachia for nearly twenty-five years from 1688, and doxologia.ro's life says he was born in 1654, descended on his father's side from the voivode Matei Basarab and on his mother's from Șerban Cantacuzino, in whose reign the whole of Scripture was first printed in Romanian. Orphaned young, he was raised by his uncle the stolnic Constantin Cantacuzino, the most learned boyar of the age, who set wise teachers over him.

His reign began with the founding of the great monastery at Hurezi, where he prepared his own place of rest, and the life says the churches and monasteries he raised or endowed across Wallachia are past counting. His giving did not stop at his own borders: it reached the Romanians of Moldavia and Transylvania, the Orthodox patriarchates, Christians in distress in Syria, the Caucasus and Arabia, and the holy places of the East — Jerusalem, Athos, Sinai and the monasteries of Greece and the Greek islands.

In Holy Week of 1714, after boyars had slandered him as plotting against the Turks, soldiers sent by Sultan Ahmed III took the prince with his sons and his sons-in-law to the Porte, to the grief of the whole city. Taking his leave he said that if these misfortunes came from God for his sins, God's will be done, and that if they were the fruit of human malice, God would judge. At the end he told his sons that with their blood they would wash away their sins.

The treasurer [Ianache Văcărescu](/saints/ianache-vacarescu) was beheaded first, then the eldest son [Constantin](/saints/constantin-son-of-brancoveanu), then [Ștefan](/saints/stefan-brancoveanu), then Radu. When the executioner came to the boy [Mateiaș](/saints/matei-brancoveanu), who was only twelve, he was afraid and cried out to the sultan that he would become a Muslim. His father answered that none of their blood had ever lost the faith, and that it were better to die a thousand deaths than throw away the faith of one's fathers to live a few more years on earth. The child took heart, laid his neck quietly on the block and said to the executioner: «Vreau să mor creștin. Lovește!»

The bodies were thrown into the sea by the pagans, gathered by merciful Christians and buried in secret on the island of Halki, in the church of a monastery of the Mother of God the prince had helped. His wife [Maria](/saints/maria-brancoveanu), by whom he had eleven children, was exiled with her daughters and her sons-in-law; in 1720 she brought his relics home to the church of Sfântul Gheorghe Nou in Bucharest, her own foundation. On 20–21 June 1992 the Holy Synod of the Romanian Church numbered the prince, his four sons and his kinsman Ianache among the saints, with 16 August for their day.

Radu was the third of the four sons named on the calendar's line, and he was beheaded after his brother Ștefan.

The Greek calendar keeps him **on 21 Μαΐου**, not on the 16 August this folder stands on, and it is the same company: saint.gr's page for that day is headed for Constantine the new martyr «και οι συν αυτώ μαρτυρήσαντες», and those martyred with him. Its account agrees with this one in almost everything and adds a few particulars. It has the prince orphaned of his father at a year old, in a rising of the people in February 1655; brought up by his mother and by his grandparents Păuna and Constantin Cantacuzino in the family house at Bucharest; taught Greek and Latin; left heir of the whole property after the death of his two brothers; married to Marica, niece of Antonie Vodă of Popești; and raised to the highest offices from 1678 by his uncle Șerban Cantacuzino. It gives Mustafa Agha the arrest on 24 March 1714, the Great Tuesday, and a bitter journey of three weeks to Constantinople; and it names the order in which the swords fell — the treasurer Ianache first, then the eldest son, then Ștefan, Radu and Matei. It adds that the heads were set on poles, carried through the streets of the City and left three days at the first gate of the Seraglio before they too were thrown into the sea, and that Christians took them out secretly at the urging of the Ecumenical Patriarchate and buried them on Halki, outside the church of the monastery of the Mother of God that the emperor John V Palaiologos had built. Marica brought the relics secretly back to Romania in 1720, in the reign of Nicholas Mavrocordat, to the church of Saint George the New.
