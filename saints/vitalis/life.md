# Vitalis

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 11 ianuarie reads «Sfântul Cuvios Vitalie» — the Venerable Vitalis — and his own page on that site carries the name, the date, and nothing else about him: no life, no troparion, no kontakion, no account of where he lived or when. What the page does print beside the name are the readings appointed for his commemoration, Galatians 5:22-26 and 6:1-2 on the fruit of the Spirit and the bearing of one another's burdens, and Matthew 11:27-30, the Lord's easy yoke and light burden, which are the readings the Romanian calendar gives a monastic saint.

So what is recorded here is the commemoration itself and the rank the calendar gives him. «Cuvios» is the Romanian word for a venerable monastic, and that single word is the whole of what this source says about his life. He is kept on 11 January, within the afterfeast of the Lord's Baptism. Nothing further is claimed for him here, because nothing further was read.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and is almost as bare as the Romanian one. saint.gr says he lived according to God, as a monk, at the beginning of the seventh century, and fell asleep in peace, and that «δεν υπάρχει ιδιαίτερο βιογραφικό υπόμνημα γι' αυτόν τον Όσιο» — there is no particular biographical memoir of this venerable man. What it adds, it adds as a separate thing and not as his life: that we know of a certain ascetic Vitalis spoken of in the life of [John the Merciful](/saints/john-the-merciful-of-alexandria), who was of the monastery of Abba Seridus, and who, when he went down to Alexandria to sell his handiwork, gave the money he earned to a harlot, begging her not to sin — as [Nicodemus the Hagiorite](/saints/nicodemus-the-hagiorite) relates. The page does not say that the two are one man, and neither does this.

*After doxologia.ro's calendar for 11 ianuarie — [the day](https://doxologia.ro/11-ianuarie) and [his page](https://doxologia.ro/sfantul-cuvios-vitalie); read 19 September 2026; and saint.gr's calendar for 11 Ιανουαρίου — [the Greek day](https://www.saint.gr/01/11/index.aspx) and [the Greek life](https://www.saint.gr/1086/saint.aspx), read 30 September 2026.*
