# Michael of Synada

As he was counted worthy to share a name with the great angel of the heavenly powers, the life says, so his own life was angelic: he took on himself virginal purity and the other good works. He was dedicated to God from his mother's arms and took up the monastic life while still young, together with Saint Theophylact, afterwards of Nicomedia, in the days of the Most Holy Patriarch Tarasius. Tarasius sent the two of them to a monastery built near the mouth of the Euxine Sea, where they laboured and grew in virtue and won boldness before God by their prayers.

Once at harvest time, in great heat and with no water, and weak with thirst, they prayed to God and made a dry copper vessel pour out water in plenty; for the Lord does the will of those who fear Him and hears their prayers. The page sets that wonder beside two older ones: the water God brought out of the rock in the desert for thirsty Israel, and the spring of living water that came from the dry jawbone of the ass when Samson was dying of thirst.

The good life of these fathers shone out like the ray of a star, and the Patriarch Tarasius judged them worthy of the high rank of the hierarchy. Theophylact he made metropolitan of Nicomedia; Michael he consecrated bishop and sent to Synada, and there Michael shepherded the flock of Christ well, by his word and by the pattern of his life.

When Tarasius had departed this life and Nicephorus had come after him to the throne of the Church of Constantinople, the storm of the heresy against the holy icons rose again, though the Seventh Ecumenical Council of the holy Fathers had put it under anathema. For the emperor Leo the Armenian, wounded by that heresy, raised a persecution against the Church of Christ, called the holy icons idols and threw them out, and tortured and destroyed those who venerated them in many kinds of torment. He drove Patriarch Nicephorus and the other right-believing bishops from their sees and set heretics of his own mind in their places.

Then Michael showed himself a renowned confessor of the right faith and a rebuker of the heretics' error. Strengthened and made wise by the grace of the Holy Spirit, he put down all the hatred of God's adversaries and stopped the mouths of those who blasphemed the divine images. Leo, whose name was the name of a beast, could not bear his hard tongue, and set the hierarch of Christ before an unjust tribunal.

Michael, neither afraid of the threats nor weakened in mind, cried out with a loud voice: I honour the holy icon of my Saviour Jesus Christ and of His Most Pure Virgin Mother and of the other saints, and I venerate them; and I take no care for your order and count it as nothing. Leo, confounded, filled with the anger of a beast, condemned the confessor of Christ to exile. Michael endured many bitter griefs and troubles, driven from place to place, until he came to the wideness of heaven and gained the eternal rest. So he finished his good course and was adorned with a double crown — set among the hierarchs as a hierarch and among the martyrs as a martyr. The page gives no year for his birth or his death.
