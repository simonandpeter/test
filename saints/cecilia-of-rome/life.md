# Cecilia of Rome

Cecilia was born at Rome of honourable and wealthy parents. Hearing the preaching of the Gospel she believed in Christ, and being wounded with love for him she resolved in her mind not to marry but to keep her virginity whole and consecrate it to Christ her Bridegroom. Against her will her parents betrothed her to a young man of good family who did not believe, Valerian by name, and he adorned her every day with costly clothes and gold; but under them she wore a rough hair garment, and prayed without ceasing that she might be kept free of the marriage bond.

On the wedding night she told him a secret: an angel of God guarded her virginity, unseen by him, and would kill him if he touched her. Valerian was afraid, and then asked to be shown the angel too. She answered that he could not see the angel of God while the uncleanness of his unbelief was on him, and sent him along the Appian Way, to the poor who lived beside it, to be taken to an old man named Urban, a bishop who hid from the persecutors among the graves and the caves and the houses of the poor.

The poor knew Cecilia well from her alms, and they brought him to Urban, who wept for joy and prayed that the young man's heart might be opened. At once there stood by them an old man in garments white as snow with a book in his hand, who bade Valerian read; and he read, written in letters of gold: *One Lord, one faith, one baptism; one God and Father of all, who is above all and through all and in us all.* Asked whether he believed it, Valerian cried out that nothing under heaven was more worthy of belief, and the one who had appeared became invisible.

Urban taught him and baptised him and sent him back to his betrothed, and Valerian found her praying, with the angel standing beside her in great brightness, holding two crowns woven of red roses and white lilies. The angel set one on her head and one on his, and told them to keep the crowns with a pure heart and an undefiled body, for they came from the paradise of God, never wither, never lose their fragrance, and nobody can see them but one who loves purity as they did.

Valerian knelt and asked that his brother Tiburtius, dearest to him of all in the world, might be turned to Christ as he had been. The angel said the request was pleasing to God, that Tiburtius would be saved through him as he himself had been saved through the virgin, and that the two brothers would go together into the contest of martyrdom. Soon after, Tiburtius came in, wondering at a fragrance of roses and lilies which he could smell but not explain, and which left him feeling wholly made new.

Cecilia opened her mouth and taught him the faith, showing him the emptiness of the pagan gods and the powerlessness of soulless idols, and speaking of the incarnation of the Son of God, his miracles, his willing passion and death for love of mankind. Tiburtius was changed from an unbeliever into a believer; when he feared to go to Urban, who had twice been sentenced to death, she spoke of the everlasting life and of the reward of the martyrs, and he asked his brother to take him to the bishop quickly.

Urban baptised him and kept him seven days to teach him fully. After his baptism Tiburtius often saw the holy angels and spoke with them, and with his brother he healed the sick, gave his goods to poor Christians, orphans and widows, redeemed prisoners and buried with honour the bodies of the martyrs who were then being killed. For that burying the prefect of the city, Almachius, had both brothers seized; they answered him about the things that seem to be and are not, refused to sacrifice, and were beaten and put to death.

Cecilia meanwhile had a great crowd of men and women in her house listening to her teaching, and she asked them aloud whether they believed that what she had said was true. With one mouth they answered that they believed and confessed Christ, whom she preached, to be the true God. Filled with joy she called the bishop Urban, who came to her house and baptised those who had believed, as many as four hundred souls, men and women; and her house became a church of Christ.

Then the prefect brought her to his unjust judgement and heard her confess the name of Christ boldly. When he said he had authority from the emperors to kill and to make alive, she answered that he lied: he should have said he could kill, for to make alive he could not. He ordered her taken to her own house and killed there in a heated furnace, and she was tormented with smoke and fire three days and three nights and was kept alive and cool by the grace of God.

Hearing that she was still living, Almachius ordered her beheaded where she lay. The executioner struck her three times across the neck and did not sever the head, and so left her and went away; the faithful gathered her blood with a sponge and with cloth. Cecilia lived three days more, speaking with good understanding and strengthening the faithful in the faith, and then prayed and gave up her spirit into the hands of God, and was buried with honour.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 22 November, and names her household with her. saint.gr says she lived in the third century, that her parents were idolaters and noble besides, and that Cecilia heard the teaching of Christ and asked to be baptised. A little while afterwards her parents married her to a young nobleman, [Valerian](/saints/valerian-husband-of-cecilia), who under her influence embraced the Christian faith himself, and with him his brother [Tiburtius](/saints/tiburtius-brother-of-valerian) came into the bosom of the Church. In the persecution under Diocletian the three went on declaring their faith and went on with their work of charity, and for that they were arrested and beheaded. The same calendar keeps on the same day [Maximus](/saints/maximus-the-capitularius), the keeper of a prison at Rome, who it says came to believe through Valerian and Cecilia and was scourged to death. The relics of Valerian and of Tiburtius both lie, the page adds, in the Roman Catholic basilica of Saint Cecilia at Rome.
