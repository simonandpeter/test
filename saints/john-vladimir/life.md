# John Vladimir

saint.gr says he was born in the tenth century at Vladimir in Bulgaria, the son of Neeman, who was the son of Symeon the first king of Ochrid, and of Anna, both of them most devout Orthodox Christians. From childhood he was brought up in the discipline and admonition of the Lord by his parents and by Nicholas, bishop of Ochrid. Samuel-Stephen, tsar of the Bulgarians, wanting to bring him into subjection, put him in prison; and there an angel of the Lord appeared to him and disclosed that his martyr's end was not far off.

As part of his diplomacy the tsar married him to his daughter Korsara, but the saint kept his virginity. When he had become an independent ruler of the Serbs he gave himself with greater zeal to spreading and settling the Orthodox faith, appointing teachers and preachers for that purpose and founding monasteries, churches and hospitals at the same time. Among the houses he founded was a chapel standing in a wood, to which he went every day to pray. He was gentle, just, brave and devout, and out of love for God he helped the Church in her work against the heretics, and against the Bogomils above all.

His abstinence from any bodily union with the queen his wife, and his daily absences for prayer, bred in her the suspicion that he had to do with other women. On that account she slandered him to her brother, who resolved to kill him. When Samuel-Stephen died his son Radomir was crowned tsar, and the new tsar's twin brother, John Vladislav, misled the saint and invited him to visit; and on that visit, in 1015, John was murdered by treachery. After his death his wife went to live in a monastery, where she built up a church; and the saint, saint.gr says, went on after his martyrdom benefiting those who ran to him in faith and healing the sick.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 22 May and is the only book read for him that tells his house and his reign. He was of a princely family, out of Zahumlje; his grandfather was called Hvalimir and his father Petrislav. As a ruler he was wise, merciful, meek, virgin and brave; he prayed to God earnestly, and of his own will built churches and helped them. But he had hard struggles both within and without — within against heretics and Bogomils, and without against the invaders, the emperor Samuel and the emperor Basil.

Samuel took him captive by a trick and threw him into prison. While he lay there an angel of God appeared to him and foretold that he would soon be freed from the prison, but that he would end as a martyr all the same. Coming to know him better, Samuel came to love him, and gave him his daughter Kosara to wife. When Samuel died his son Radomir reigned; but Radomir’s cousin Vladislav killed Radomir, and then called Vladimir to him by a trick and cut him down too, in 1015.

The relics of this holy king and martyr rest incorrupt in his monastery near Elbasan, the Prologue says, and through the centuries numberless wonders have happened at them, and happen to this day. At the monastery of Saint Naum, in 1925, a church was raised to this crowned martyr as the founder of that famous house.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 22 мая ст. ст. and gives him two short readings. The first says that he came of a princely family and was a wise ruler, merciful, meek, chaste and brave; that he was a fervent man of prayer and a devout builder and endower of churches; and that he fought the Bogomil heretics within his country and invaders from outside it. He was taken prisoner, and in the prison received word from an angel that he would be set free from his confinement but would suffer a martyr’s death. In 1015 he was beheaded, and his incorrupt and wonderworking relics lie in his own monastery.

The second reading sets his birth in the tenth century and says he was brought up in piety from childhood, and when he came to manhood governed his possessions, Illyria and Dalmatia, wisely, keeping the holy faith in purity. He was married to Косара, the daughter of the Bulgarian tsar Самуил. Called to the Bulgarian tsar Иоанн-Владислав under the pretext of negotiations, he was treacherously killed by him on 22 мая 1015, at the entrance of a church. His devout wife shut herself up in the church her husband had built and died there, never leaving the church to the end of her life.

The two readings do not agree where his relics lie, the one saying his own monastery «близ Аблазона» and the other «близ Элбосани», and neither has been chosen here. Nor do they agree about his grade: the day line and the second reading call him martyr, and the title of the first calls him great-martyr. His pages list a second day beside this one, «30 августа (Серб.)», which the calendar marks as the Serbian local commemoration.
