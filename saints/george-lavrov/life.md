# George (Lavrov)

days.pravoslavie.ru’s calendar for 21 июня ст. ст. names him «Прп. Георгия исп (1932).» and gives him a life page of its own. In the world he was Герасим Дмитриевич Лавров, born on 28 February 1868 — the page writes the year with its own question mark, «1868 (1867?)» — in the Yelets country of the province of Oryol, of a devout and well-off peasant family. He finished three classes of school and no more. At twelve he came with his parents on pilgrimage to Optina and went up for the blessing of the elder «Амвросий», who took his head in his arms, blessed him, and said that he ought to stay there.

It was not until 1898, after his father’s death, that he became a novice of Optina; he was tonsured there in 1899 with the name Георгий and ordained hieromonk in 1902. The page records that he differed with the head of the skete, the elder «Варсонофий (Плиханков)», over the governing of it, and that the disagreement divided the monks of Optina — a thing, it says, until then unheard of there. From 1915 to 1918 he was superior of the Georgievsky monastery at Мещовск in the province of Kaluga.

When that house was closed in December 1918 he was accused of a secret conspiracy and of keeping arms, and taken from the prison at Мещовск with a party of other prisoners towards Kaluga, where they were to be shot. Before that he had a wonderful vision, after which he comforted them all and told them they would live; and in fact their wagon was coupled, for some reason, to another train, going to Moscow. In Moscow they were put in the Таганская prison, and while the enquiry was going on an amnesty came out in 1919 and the shooting was changed to five years of confinement.

Until 1921 he was in a solitary cell, and he was operated on in the prison. The prison doctor — the future secret bishop «Максим» — taught him some medical skills, and he was made an orderly, which gave him access to all sorts of people and to the cells of the condemned. He heard confessions and gave communion to those who wished it, and tried to comfort and hearten everybody; sometimes, the page says, he begged a dying man with tears to confess if he saw that he was going out of this life embittered.

In that prison he received the blessing to be an elder from a metropolitan. He was taken out of it on bail by the future hieromartyr «Феодор (Поздеевский)», who gave him a place to live and to serve in the Danilov monastery, which he headed; and there he received a great many people in a cell by the entrance of the Protection church. Little educated as he was, the intelligentsia and the young came to him. «Золотце, золотой мой, деточка» — my treasure, my golden one, little child — was how he spoke to people. He had the gift, the page says, of telling a sickness of the soul from a sickness of the spirit, and an archbishop of Tver was often with him.

During the disagreements that arose over a metropolitan’s declaration he urged the brotherhood not to bring new divisions into the Church, and stood with the deputy of the patriarchal locum tenens. In May 1928 he was arrested a second time, charged with having played the part of an elder in a Black-Hundred monastery and carried on anti-Soviet propaganda among those he served.

He was sentenced to three years of exile in a settlement in the Ural region, now in Kazakhstan. He lived there in hard conditions but was consoled by serving the liturgy in his little hut, and in exile he fell ill with cancer of the throat. After his release he settled at Nizhny Novgorod, where his spiritual children found him a room with difficulty, and on 21 июня — 4 July new style — 1932, after receiving the holy mysteries, he died, and was buried in the Бугровское cemetery there.

His relics were found in October 2000 and taken to the Danilov monastery in Moscow, and he was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the jubilee council of bishops in August 2000. One thing in the source has to be said plainly: the page’s own title calls him a venerable-martyr, while the day line and the name link grade him venerable and a confessor, and the page’s narrative has him dying of cancer in a room in Nizhny Novgorod. This folder follows the line and records a confessor.
