# Theodore, boyar of Michael of Chernigov

Theodore was a boyar of [Michael of Chernigov](/saints/michael-of-chernigov) and, the Russian calendar’s life says, his faithful friend and companion, who went with him to the Horde when the khan’s envoys told the prince to come and have his right to rule confirmed by the khan’s yarlyk. The second life on the same calendar says that Michael’s spiritual father, Bishop John, strengthened both men and gave them the Holy Gifts for the road, and that Theodore made the same promise as his prince, to die for Christ sooner than bow to idols. Arriving in 1246, they were ordered to pass through a fire and bow to the sun and the fire; both refused, and while their answer went to Batu they sang psalms and received the Gifts.

Michael was beaten and beheaded first. Then the Tatars flatteringly promised Theodore his martyred prince’s rank if he would perform the pagan rite, and he did not waver but followed his prince, and after the same savage tortures his head was cut off. Their bodies were thrown to the dogs and kept unharmed for several days until Christians buried them secretly, and the relics were later taken to Chernigov, then to Moscow, and in 1774 to the Archangel cathedral of the Kremlin. The life says that Theodore’s confession struck even his executioners, and that the khans, seeing Russians ready to die for Christ with joy, did not afterwards require of them in the Horde the direct performance of idolatrous rites.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 20 September old style with Michael as martyrs, confessors and wonderworkers; its day prints 1245 and both its lives 1246. The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, names the two together: Prince Michael went to the Tatar horde with his boyar Theodore at Batu’s call, and because they would not pass through the fire and bow to the idols they were beheaded in 1244.
