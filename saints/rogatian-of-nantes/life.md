# Rogatian of Nantes

saint.gr says the holy martyrs [Donatian](/saints/donatian-of-nantes) and Rogatian were brothers, of a noble Roman pagan family living in the city of Nantes in Gaul. Donatian was baptised a Christian and preached the Gospel of the Lord with zeal; but he was arrested in the years of the emperor Diocletian and brought before the governor Rictovarus, who called on him to sacrifice to the idols and save his life. The martyr refused with courage and confessed Christ, and his brother's example moved Rogatian to the same confession, though he had not had time to be baptised.

The ruler ordered them thrown into prison and cruelly tortured. Through the night the two brothers prayed fervently to our God and Lord, and the next day the torments began again: they pierced their heads with lances and at the last beheaded them, and Rogatian was baptised in the blood of his martyrdom. So, the page says, the holy martyrs received the unfading crown of glory and entered into the joy of their Lord.

Towards the end of the fifth century the Christians raised a church on the place where the martyrs were buried, and in 1145 their holy relics were translated to the cathedral of Nantes.
