# Basil of Mangazeya

saint.gr's calendar for 22 Μαρτίου keeps a Russian hieromartyr and wonderworker whom it calls bishop of Mangazeya in Siberia, and it gives him three sentences. The holy hieromartyr Basil was born in the town of Yaroslavl in Russia in the year 1583; he was elected bishop of Mangazeya in Siberia, and was martyred in the year 1602. The page heads him «(1583 - 1602)», which puts nineteen years between the two figures.

It adds two commemorations of the relic rather than of the man: the Church keeps the finding of his relics on 6 Ιουνίου, and their translation from Mangazeya to Turukhansk on 10 Μαΐου. Neither is a person and neither is kept here. Beside him the page sets the synaxis of all the saints of Siberia, which is a company and not a folder either.

That is the whole of what has been read. saint.gr gives no account of his election, no account of his arrest or of the manner of his death, no persecutor, and no wonder, though it calls him a wonderworker; it prints neither a couplet nor a hymn under his name. The rank and the see are recorded here in the page's own words, and nothing about them has been supplied or corrected from any other source or from memory.

The Russian calendar, which is his own, tells a different story from the Greek one and makes him not a bishop but a boy of nineteen. He was the son of a devout but poor man of Yaroslavl named Theodore, and was taken while still young by a rich Yaroslavl merchant to manage the selling of his goods at Mangazeya beyond the Arctic circle, one of the first Russian towns of Siberia. He kept the commandments strictly, and his honesty was plain to everyone from his earliest years; gentleness and humility were his adornment, and his love of prayer made him leave the business of the day and go to church whenever the services were sung.

One Pascha, while he was at the matins of the feast, thieves robbed his master’s shop. He was called to answer for it and would not leave the church until the service was ended, for all his master’s calling; and the master, who loved money, suspected him of a part in the theft and beat and abused him when he came back. The boy answered him, truly I took nothing of thine. Then the master took him to the town governor, who put him to fresh and heavy torture; and the merchant, losing patience at his silence, struck him in a rage with a bundle of warehouse keys, and of that blow he died.

His body was laid in a coffin and put into the ground without Christian burial, in a place where the wet comes up out of the earth; and forty-seven years later God was pleased to bring it up out of the earth and to glorify it with many wonders. He is the first saint glorified on Siberian soil. The readings say he helped travellers and fur-hunters who had lost their way or were in danger, healed palsy and blindness and other sicknesses, healed children at their mothers’ prayers and kept the faint-hearted from killing themselves, and that the surviving copies of his life, of the seventeenth to the nineteenth centuries, are full of such things. The day page carries six hymns under his name, three troparia and three kontakia, and all six are here.
