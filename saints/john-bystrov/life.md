# John (Bystrov)

John Bystrov was born in the village of Semyany in 1888 or 1889. His parents had only daughters and wanted a son, and his mother vowed that a boy would be given to the Lord. He finished the gymnasium and became a teacher in his home village, where the building of the church and the zeal of its priest, [John (Flerov)](/saints/john-flerov), moved him. He was soon known as a gifted teacher, with a fine future before him, and the vow was forgotten, until he grew more and more troubled by his unfaithfulness to God; at twenty-seven he told his mother that he must keep her promise, and that he wanted to. He married, was ordained and served at Sakany in the Nizhny Novgorod diocese. He and his wife had eight children, and he was strict and fair with them and they loved him. Persecuted, he had to change parishes several times; his last was the church at Arapovo.

His name as a teacher protected him for a while, but in the late 1930s arrest drew near. Some officials urged him to leave the church and go back to teaching, promising that his priesthood would be forgotten and he would be made a school director; his wife begged him with tears, for the children’s sake, to do as they said. He answered meekly that the Lord would not abandon them and would see all the children into the world. He was arrested on 11 September 1938 — the Chekists came during the service, did not dare to break it off, and waited outside for him — and held in the Nizhny Novgorod prison. He died, the life says, as many priests of that diocese arrested in 1937 and 1938 died: they were taken out to the middle of the Volga opposite the town of Bor, pushed bound into the water one by one and watched, and any who came up were drowned.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 21 September old style; azbyka.ru keeps him also with the saints of Nizhny Novgorod, and prints no hymns for him.
