# Boris (Kozlov)

**The one layman of the five, and the only name on this day the calendar calls a plain martyr.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar closes its line of sixteen with him — «и мч. Бориса (1937)» — where it has called the four before him «прмчч.», and carries no life for him; the life here is the article azbyka.ru prints for the civil 5 декабря out of fond.ru, which it hangs behind all five pages of this company. He was born on 23 July 1886 in the village of Слепцово in the Козельск district of the province of Калуга, son of a peasant, Антон Козлов. Like many peasant children then he went to St Petersburg to look for work and got a place at the Путиловский works; but the working world was then godless and morally hard for a religious village boy, so he left the works and from 1909 to 1911 was a labouring guest in one of the monasteries of St Petersburg, afterwards served a landowner at Малоярославец, worked at Калуга, and in 1920 came home to his own village and took up farming.

**He was made church warden in 1926 and the authorities broke him with a firewood quota.** In that year Борис Антонович was elected warden of the church of the Protection in the village of Покровское, not far from Слепцово. Probably, the article says, he carried out his duties with great zeal, and so began to irritate the godless, who, wanting the church closed and ruined, set him a quota for the getting of firewood which he could not meet; the court sentenced him to a year of forced labour and a fine of three hundred roubles. When the punishment was over he settled in Козельск and took work as a stableman at the town hospital, where he also lived, giving all his free time to the church and singing in the choir of the Благовещенская church. His nearest friends and fellows in mind were the monks of Оптина Пустынь living in Козельск.

**The witnesses against him are quoted twice and both say the same thing.** He was arrested with the monks on 28 October 1937 — two days before them — and shut in the prison at Сухиничи. The witnesses testified that being hostilely disposed to the Soviet power he had argued in August 1937 «что Бог спасет людей от варваров, которые закрывают церкви», that God would save people from the barbarians who were closing the churches, and that he visited the church secretly on his days off, where he met the monks; and a man who lodged with him said that he often went to the monastic gathering in the church but hid it all from outsiders, read church books at home and argued that only God would deliver people from their torments, and called on them to believe in God.

**He was asked about a watchman and about a gathering, and gave the plainest answers of the five.** Told that the investigation had it that he had organised monks at the hospital, got a monk taken on as watchman and so caused discontent among the staff, and carried on counter-revolutionary propaganda, he answered that a monk had indeed been taken on as watchman of the hospital at Козельск, that he did not know who had recommended him and that the steward had taken him on, and that «пропаганду против советской власти я не проводил и ничего по этому вопросу показать не могу». Told that he had secretly visited the gathering of the monks of the Optina monastery and that the truth was required, he answered: «Сборища монахов я не посещал, но церковь я посещал и пел на клиросе вместе с монахинями» — he had not gone to any gathering of monks, but he had gone to church and had sung on the kliros together with the nuns.

**Five men of one house, one lodging and one case.** The article is headed for all of them — «Преподобномученики Евтихий (Диденко), Авенир (Синицын), Савва (Суслов), Марк (Махров) и мученик Борис Козлов» — and begins with what 1937 was: the last period of the destruction of the Russian Church by a government of militant godless men, which reached the monks of Оптина still at liberty, living after the closing of their house in the town of Козельск, the nearest town to the monastery they loved. Three of them — Евтихий, Авенир and Савва — lodged in one house, each in his own cell with his own icons; Марк lodged elsewhere in the town and Борис at the town hospital where he worked. All four monks were arrested on 30 October 1937 and shut in the prison at Сухиничи; Борис had been taken two days earlier, on 28 October.

**The evidence against them is their neighbour and their landlady, and it is candid.** On 4 November the standing witnesses were questioned. The neighbour said the landlady kept the monks «на квартире специально, потому что они молились Богу», that they paid twelve roubles between them for the lodging while he, a working man, paid eighteen alone, that the four of them «ничем полезным не занимались, ходили по домам, распространяли церковный дурман», went to the church in a group and lived in a group in one lodging each with his own cell fitted out with icons — and that in October, when a pre-election meeting was held in the school building, the monks were invited and all of them flatly refused to go, which told on the other citizens. The landlady said they lived in her house, each in his own cell, and «монашествовали»; that one of them had argued in her presence «что раньше в монастыре жилось лучше, чем теперь при советской власти»; and that another had said «теперь всех сажают в тюрьму, потому что будут выборы советов и уже арестованы попы».

**The troika sentenced all five on 21 November 1937, and they were shot a fortnight later.** The article closes with the one sentence that makes this day their own: it pleased the Lord that on the very day of the feast of the Entry of the Most Holy Theotokos, 4 December, they should spend the day in prayer, in prison but still on earth, and that they were shot «на следующий день, 5 декабря 1937 года» — on the next day, 5 December 1937 — and buried in a common unmarked grave. 5 December 1937 is the Julian 22 ноября, the day both calendars keep them on; the ten other names of the same line were shot on 4 December, which is the 21st.
