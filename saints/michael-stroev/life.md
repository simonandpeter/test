# Michael (Stroev)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him second of the two people of 1938 it puts on one line for the Julian 3 March and grades him мч., a martyr and a layman. Michael Stepanovich Stroev was born on 6 September 1876 in the town of Voskresensk in the Zvenigorod district of the province of Moscow — the town that is now Istra — the son of Stepan Stroev, a former serf painter attached to the New Jerusalem monastery. Every grandfather and great-grandfather of his father’s had been a painter. Left without a father at nine, he began at twelve to help his grandfather, the artist, who became his first teacher in painting.

In 1902 he married Anna Grigoryevna Grigoryeva, a worker in a textile factory, and in 1904 he entered the evening courses of the Stroganov school. In 1909 he founded the first Volunteer Fire Society in the district and became in time a life member of it. In 1916 he was called up to the Astrakhan reserve regiment, quartered at Morshansk in the province of Tambov; coming home at the end of the war he was made head of the Voskresensk district fire brigade, and took the provincial fire courses while he served, and was afterwards moved for his health and his age to the command of the volunteer factory brigade.

Having been used to work from his earliest years he taught his children to work too, reminding them often of the proverb «Труд кормит — а лень портит», labour feeds and idleness spoils. A man of deep faith, in the time of the persecution by the godless power he was warden of the Trinity church in the village of Troitskoye, bearing that heavy and, in those days, dangerous obedience without a murmur.

In the thirties the chairman of the village soviet sold the schoolhouse to a Soviet professor for a country villa and proposed that the school move into the wooden church building — put up more than three hundred years before and therefore reckoned an architectural monument even in a godless time. The faithful would not have it and asked him to make the complaint to the authorities. The case was looked into, the chairman was obliged to cancel the sale of the school as unlawful, and so the children stayed in their old building and the church was kept whole for the service.

The persecution of 1937 spared nobody. In November both priests of the Trinity church, its deacon and its warden were arrested together and shut up in the Taganka prison in Moscow. Told that one of the priests’ evidence convicted him as a member of a counter-revolutionary group, he said he was no member of one; the evidence was read to him and he denied it; asked whether he knew the other priest and the deacon and on what terms he was with them, he said he knew them well and that their terms were good; and asked whether he went to their lodgings and they to his, he said that he had visited them in the course of his duty and that they came to him as well.

On 27 November 1937 the troika of the НКВД sentenced him to ten years in a corrective labour camp, and he was sent to work on a camp pig farm in Siberia. He died in confinement in the Suslovo section of Siblag on 16 March 1938 and was buried in an unmarked grave. The year on the calendar’s line is the year he died in the camp and not a year of execution: he was sentenced to ten years and not to death.

Two of the three men arrested with him are glorified, and azbyka.ru says so in its own footnotes: the priest it names is kept on 22 February by the old style and the 7 March by the new, and the deacon on 15 July old style and 28 July new. This corpus has no folder for either, nor for the second priest, so the company is written here in words and carries no related row; when the days that keep them are read, the rows are owed both ways and this is where they come from. Nothing on either host makes him and the novice of his own calendar line a company either: she was taken at a monastery in the Yegoryevsk district and he at a parish near Istra, and no page read names the two of them together.
