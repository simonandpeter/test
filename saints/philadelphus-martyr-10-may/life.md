# Philadelphus (10 May)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 10 mai reads «Sfântul Mucenic Filadelf», and the page it links for him carries neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab — only the feast line "10 Mai" and the readings appointed for the day. No country is named, no century is given, and nothing is told of what he did or how he ended.

The corpus already keeps a martyr Philadelphus, from the Greek synaxarion for 2 September; nothing read here connects the two, and the folder stands on the calendar's line alone.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and names his brothers and the manner of his death. The holy martyrs [Alpheus](/saints/alphaeus-martyr-10-may), [Cyprinus](/saints/cyprian-martyr-10-may) and Philadelphus were brothers, saint.gr says, sons of the ruler of the prefects Vitalius, of the country of the Vascones in southern Italy. Taught the Orthodox faith and baptised by a certain venerable man named Onesimus, they preached Christ, and were denounced for it; they were seized by one Anengelion under the emperor Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251, and sent bound to Rome. From there, after the first examination, they were carried to Puteoli to the governor Diomedes, who referred them to Tertyllus the ruler of Sicily; and when Tertyllus could not change their minds he condemned them to death. Alpheus was beheaded, his tongue cut out first; Cyprinus was fried in an enormous pan; and Philadelphus they burned upon a red-hot gridiron. Two couplets stand over him on the page. The first says that Philadelphus, or rather one should say Christ-loving, contended for love of Christ unto the fire of the gridiron; the second, that Philadelphus, brother-loving, found a divine crown by the sword together with his brothers — «Ὁ Φιλάδελφος φιλαδέλφως τῷ ξίφει, / Σὺν τοῖς ἀδελφοῖς θεῖον εὕρατο στέφος» — and those two accounts of how he died are the page's own and are not reconciled there. [Erasmus](/saints/erasmus-10-may) and [Onesimus](/saints/onesimus-10-may), whose page names these three, died with fourteen others on the same day.

The Ohrid Prologue, read for the Julian 10 May, keeps the three brothers under one heading and adds what the other two calendars did not. They were brothers born of one womb, sons of a certain prince Vitalis in southern Italy, wonderful in nobility and strong in the faith; they were judged for their faith in Christ and led from one judge to another, from one torturer to another; and they were taken over into Sicily and killed there. The book sets that in the time of the emperor Licinius, where the Greek page sets the arrest under Decius — sixty years and two reigns apart, and neither reading is chosen here, the Serbian giving no years for its reign at all.

Of his own death it says only that Philadelphus was burned upon an iron grid, which is the red-hot gridiron the Greek page gives, so the two agree. The brothers' incorrupt relics, this book adds, were found in the year 1517. And it closes by saying that these three brothers appeared to [Euthalia](/saints/euthalia-the-martyr), sending the reader to the second of March for her — which is the dream her own account already tells from the other side.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 10 мая ст. ст. in one line with four companions, «Мчч. Алфия, Филадельфа, Киприана, Онисима, Еразма и иных (251)», and gives the five of them a single life. It makes them men of Italy of the third century, and Филадельф one of three brothers, sons of Вителий the governor of Italy, with Алфий and Киприан; the three were enlightened in the faith of Christ and baptised by Онисим. When Christians were ordered sought out and tortured, the brothers were sent to Rome with Онисим, Еразм and fourteen others.

Онисим died at Rome of a heavy stone laid on his breast, and Еразм and the fourteen were beheaded there. The three brothers were sent on to Sicily and suffered at Месополь in the territory of Леонтины, and Филадельф was burnt upon an iron grid. The life dates this to 251, under the emperor Декий, though it has opened by setting the persecution under Ликиний; their relics were found at Леонтины in 1517. The calendar names these five and leaves the other fourteen unnamed.
