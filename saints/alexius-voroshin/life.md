# Alexius (Voroshin)

Alexius Voroshin, the blessed fool-for-Christ of Yelnat, was born in the early 1900s in the village of Kaurchikha in the Kostroma province, a church warden's son. On the point of betrothal, he asked his bride to keep away from the village youth gatherings where talk ran loose; she would not, and he reasoned that a bride who did not hear him would hear him less as a wife - and went instead to the Krivoezersk hermitage across the Volga for a year as a novice. Home again, he lived not in the family house but in the bath-house, then in a cell in the garden. After 1917 the peasants, trusting authority to be a holy thing, chose the man they respected as chairman of their village soviet; he prayed and attended services as before, and when a chairman was sent from the town a year later he withdrew entirely into his cell, to nine years of fasting and prayer.

In 1928 he took up the podvig of folly for Christ - living where he happened to be, in rags, giving away whatever he was given, and foretelling by his strange conduct the arrests, sicknesses and deaths that came to his neighbours, and the time when nearly every church in Russia would be closed, and war would come, and some would open again for a while. The authorities put him in the psychiatric hospital more than once; the doctors found him sane. In 1937, knowing of his arrest in advance, he came home to Kaurchikha, gave away his things and asked his family to bury him; next morning at Parfyonovo he was arrested and put in the Kineshma prison among the criminals, where he prayed constantly, barely slept, and gave his ration away. There being nothing to charge him with, the interrogators resorted to torture - he was stood barefoot on a red-hot stove - and, broken, he died in the prison hospital. His body was given to his relatives on the thirteenth day. His remains were uncovered in 1985 and rest now in the Holy Vvedensky convent in Ivanovo; he was glorified for the whole Church among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia in 2000.
