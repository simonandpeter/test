# Milos the Wonderworker

Milos — or Miles, as saint.gr's own text also spells him — was born in a village of the Persians, and when he had been baptised he learned the sacred letters. The page quotes Nikodemos of the Holy Mountain for his life. When he came of age and was to become a soldier of the king of the Persians, a fearful night vision kept him from that undertaking; and from then on he lived in virginity and ascesis, praying to God both for himself and for his whole people. After some years he left his native place and became a monk, and settling in that country where the prophet Daniel saw his visions when he was in Babylon, he was ordained bishop by the bishop Gedios.

The long account that follows ends with his passion. The sons of the king, having tortured him and his two disciples much, pressed them to sacrifice. The two disciples they tortured greatly and then killed; the old man they tortured over many days, and at the last they stood him in the middle, and one shooting arrows from in front and the other from behind, they killed him. It was then, while he was being shot, that he spoke to them the foretelling of their own death that the page reports.

The couplet of the synaxarion counts the three of them and distinguishes the deaths: «Ἔκτειναν ἐχθροὶ σὺν δυσὶ μύσταις Μίλον, / Σφαγὴ μὲν αὐτόν, τοὺς δὲ μύστας τοῖς λίθοις» — enemies killed Milos with two initiates, him by slaughter and the initiates with stones. Of the disciples the page's own note says that some sources give three and name them, so nothing of them is recorded here.
