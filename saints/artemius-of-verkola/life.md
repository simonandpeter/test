# Artemius of Verkola

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 20 октября ст. ст. as «Прав. отрока Артемия Веркольского (1545)» — the righteous boy Artemius of Verkola — and carries two lives for him, which agree on the beginning and differ about the rest. He was born in 1532 at Verkola in the Dvina country, into a devout peasant family, and both pages say that from childhood he was marked out by meekness, obedience and willingness to work.

At twelve, or at thirteen — the two pages count it differently — he was in the field when a storm came up, and he was killed by the lightning. The longer page puts it on 23 June 1545 and has his father in the field with him. Because he had died a sudden death, his neighbours read it as a judgement of God: they left him unsung, without the burial service, and laid him in the wood above the ground under brushwood and birch bark.

So he lay forgotten, for thirty-two years by the one account and twenty-eight by the other, until a light was seen over the place and his body was found uncorrupted; the first page dates the finding to 1577. The relics were carried into the church and healings followed. In the malignant fever that went through the Dvina country, a Verkola villager named Kalinnik took birch bark from the boy's coffin and hung it on the cross at the breast of his dying son, and the son recovered; and when icons of him came to be painted, the sick were healed by the shavings left from the boards.

A monastery was founded on the spot where the relics had been found, and they were laid there; the calendar calls it the Verkola monastery. The calendar's own two days for him are 23 июня, which the longer life makes the day he died, and this one.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 20 October, and is the second calendar to keep him at all — before today only the Russian did. He was born in 1532, it says, the son of Russian peasant parents, Cosmas and Apollinaria, from the village of Verkola in the Dvina district. From his fifth year he was marked off from the other children by an unusual piety and meekness.

When he was thirteen the boy was walking with his father through a great forest, and in a violent storm he died there; and his grieving father, who could not dig a grave, covered his body with branches and went away. Twenty-eight years later a man saw an unusual light in that forest, came to the light, and found Artemius’s body undecayed and incorrupt. Many sick people were healed the moment they touched it. His holy relics rest in a monastery near Pinega, in the province of Archangel.
