# Chariton, companion of Justin

Chariton is the first man doxologia's life names after Justin. When the idolaters had published through every land and city that decree against the Christians, that everyone who believes in Christ should be forced to sacrifice to the idols, there were seized the holy martyrs [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), Chariton, Euelpistus, Hierax, Paeon and [Valerian](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin), and a woman named Charito; and they were brought to Rome before Rusticus, the prefect of the city, and set on trial.

The prefect began with Justin, and the life gives their exchange at length: submit to the gods and to the emperors' commands, he said, and you will not be condemned; and Justin answered that no one who keeps the commands of Christ can ever be condemned. Asked what teaching he held, he said he had tried every philosophy and joined himself at last to the true teaching of the Christians: to know one God, the Maker of all things visible and invisible, and to confess our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God, foretold by the prophets, who is to come and judge the race of men.

Then Rusticus turned from Justin to the rest, and Chariton was the first he asked: are you also a Christian? With God's help, he answered, I too am a Christian. The woman Charito said that by the grace of God she was a Christian; Euelpistus, that he was a slave of the emperor but a Christian set free by Christ Himself; Hierax, that he honoured and worshipped the same God, and that his true father was Christ and his mother the faith, his earthly parents being dead, and that he had come out of Iconium in Phrygia. Paeon said he had his confession from his parents, and Valerian that he worshipped the one true God and despised theirs.

When the prefect threatened torments without mercy, Justin answered for them that they truly desired to endure torments for the Lord Jesus Christ and so be saved, and the others said the same, adding: do quickly what you mean to do, for we are Christians and will not sacrifice to idols. Rusticus gave sentence that those who would not sacrifice and would not obey should be beaten with rods and beheaded, as the laws of the Romans command. They were led out to the usual place, beaten, and cut down with the axe, and some of the faithful took their bodies secretly and buried them in a worthy place.

Doxologia prints this same life on the page of each of the men whose names its calendar carries for the day, and a single troparion for them all together with the philosopher Justin. Of the company the life names, only Justin, Chariton and Valerian have a line of their own in the calendar; Euelpistus, Hierax, Paeon and Charito are named here and have no folder. The life gives no year, and none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and names eight where the Romanian named four. These, saint.gr says, received the martyrs' crowns together with [Justin the philosopher and apologist](/saints/justin-the-philosopher), whom the same calendar keeps on this day, in the persecution against the Christians under Marcus Aurelius. They belonged to the brave phalanx of the Christians of Rome, where only genuine Christian courage could keep a man from being dismayed by the power of the idol-worship that the palace and the army upheld; and having confessed Christ repeatedly they were condemned to die by beheading, and all of them underwent it untroubled and joyful. The four the Greek adds are [Charito](/saints/charito) the virgin, [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june) and [Paion](/saints/paion). The distich the page prints over Chariton is shared with her: the divine Chariton and Charito the virgin, cut down, have the good fortune of divine graces.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the whole company in one heading of eight names: [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), Chariton, [Charito](/saints/charito), [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june), [Paeon](/saints/paion), [Valerian](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin) and [Justus](/saints/justus-1-june). All of them suffered at Rome, it says, in the time of Marcus Aurelius and of Rusticus the eparch. When Rusticus asked whether they thought that if they died for Christ they would receive a reward in heaven, Justin answered for them all: we do not think, we know. After that they were all beheaded, in the year 163, and passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ God.

He is named second, immediately after Justin, as he is in the Romanian life, and the year 163 is the first a book has given for him; this folder records it as the death. The Serbian spelling of his name is the same string this corpus already stores as a Russian form for another martyr, kept on 9 September, and the two are told apart by their days and their calendars rather than by their letters.
