# Stephen of Montenegro

Stephen was born in Montenegro, in Serbia, and saint.gr places him in the seventeenth century. From a young age he followed the monastic life, and became a monk in the monastery of Morača. Because of the Turks he was forced to leave the monastery and to live hidden among the shepherds of the district for seven years.

In 1660 he took up his ascetic life again in a cave, and inside it he lived in unceasing prayer for thirty years. Four years after his holy falling asleep the faithful found his honourable relic by the light that was lighting the cave in which he had come to rest. The page gives no year for his birth and none for his death, names no monastery of his own, and prints no hymn to him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 20 May and is the Serbian church’s own account of a Serbian saint. He was born, it says, in the Nikšić tribe, in the village of Župa, of poor but devout parents, Radoje and Jaćima. By tradition — the book marks the hedge itself — he struggled first in the monastery of Morača, where he was also abbot; the Turks drove him out from there, and he settled at Turman Rovački, at the place now called Ќelište. Afterwards he moved again and settled in Piperi, in a single cell, where he stayed until his death in laborious ascesis pleasing to God.

He fell asleep in peace in the Lord on the twentieth of May, 1697 — the day all three calendars keep him on. His relics rest in that place still, the Prologue says, and glorify with many wonders Christ God and Stephen, the man who pleased God. The Greek page tells the same life from the other end, with the monastery, the Turks and the cave but no family and no year: seven years hidden among the shepherds of the district, and from 1660 thirty years of unceasing prayer in a cave, whose light showed the faithful his honourable relic four years after he had come to rest in it.
