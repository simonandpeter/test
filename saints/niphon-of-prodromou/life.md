# Niphon of Prodromou

Niphon Ionescu is, doxologia says, the first and the true founder of the Romanian skete of Prodromou on Athos. He was born in the county of Vaslui of poor and very believing parents, and hearing of the famous abbot of the monastery of Horaița, the archimandrite Irinarh Roseti, he entered that brotherhood in 1826 and was tonsured by him with the name Nathanael. In 1840 he left for the Holy Mountain with the monk Nectarie from the same house; they struggled first at Koutloumousiou, then bought a cell in the skete of Saint Anne, and it was there that father Nathanael became a schemamonk with the name Niphon.

Many monks gathered round him, some of them from Horaița, and with his beloved friend Nectarie he bought a larger cell by the sea at the place called Kyrasha, on the estate of the Lavra, and moved there with his disciples. In 1850 the two came to Moldavia, were ordained priests, and asked the help of the metropolitan Sofronie Miclescu and of the prince Grigore Ghica at Iași for the building of a Romanian skete on Athos. With the three thousand five hundred gold pieces they were given, and with an intervention at the Lavra, that monastery's council recognised the cell of Vigla Ianicopoli as a Romanian skete; the abbots there before had been the venerable Justin the confessor and his disciple Patapie, between 1810 and 1825, but the cells they built had fallen into ruin.

In 1852 the hieroschemamonks Niphon and Nectarie laid the foundation of the skete's church, dedicated to the Baptism of the Lord; eight years later, in 1860, it was already painted. Under Niphon's leadership, with the great Romanian teacher and protopsaltis, the schemamonk Nectarie Crețu, among his community, the skete lived, the page says, the most peaceful age of harmony and spiritual flourishing in its whole history.

In 1870 father Niphon withdrew from the leadership and came back to the country, where he founded the skete of Bucium near Iași as a dependency of Prodromou. After some years he returned to the Holy Mountain and withdrew into stillness with his disciple, to a cave by the sea twenty minutes from the skete he had founded, with a little chapel beside it dedicated to the Nativity of the Lord, where he kept the daily rule and served the Liturgy on feasts.

There, in the cave between the mountain and the sea, he struggled another twenty years in fasting and prayer, glorifying God day and night; and in 1901 he gave up his soul into the Lord's hands, ninety-four years old. His cave is called the Cave of Father Niphon to this day, and his disciples buried his body beside it in a hidden place, as he had wished. Lord, number the venerable Niphon the hermit among the choir of the saints, the page ends; and it names its book, Archimandrite Ioanichie Bălan's Romanian Paterikon, Sihăstria Monastery, pages 472 to 474.
