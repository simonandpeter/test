# Demetrius (Smirnov)

Demetrius was born in 1868 in the village of Chernyaevo in the Dmitrov
district of Moscow province, the son of Basil Timofeevich Smirnov,
forty-seven years a priest. In 1883 he finished the Zvenigorod church
school and lived some years with his parents; in April 1889 he became
psalmist at the Kazan church in the village of Kotelniki in the Moscow
district, in November 1910 was moved as psalmist to the Blachernae church
at Vlakhernskoye-Kuzminki, and on 18 March 1912 was ordained deacon to
that church. For blameless service Patriarch Tikhon gave him a certificate
of blessing in 1920 and in 1924 the double orarion, and in March 1924 he
was appointed to the church of St Nicholas at Aksinyino in the Zvenigorod
district. On 21 September 1928 he was ordained priest for the Trinity
church of the village of Yershovo in the Zvenigorod district, in 1932 was
moved to the Trinity church of Troitskoye in the same district, and in
1937 was given the pectoral cross. Like every priest he was laid under a
heavy tax by the godless authorities, paid with difficulty; in 1937 he was
fined for missing a payment, and since the fine could not be paid either,
in February 1938 all his property was inventoried. In the summer of 1937
the authorities had decided to destroy the Russian Church physically:
persecution grew everywhere, clergy and laity were arrested all round and
sentenced without any guilt to be shot or to long terms in the camps, and
in October 1937 his own brother, the archpriest Vsevolod Smirnov, who
served at the Dormition church of Dermentsevo in the Volokolamsk district,
was arrested and shot a month later. On 15 March 1938 the investigators of
the Zvenigorod NKVD called several witnesses, who gave the testimony the
persecutors needed: the chairman of the collective farm at Troitskoye said
that as a minister of religion Smirnov had a corrupting effect on the
farm — during the spring sowing and the harvest and sowing campaigns he
gathered the farm people in church on the religious feasts and agitated
that God must be feared and loved and that the collective farms would give
them nothing good anyway; that on the feasts they went to church instead
of to work, wrecking the farm’s work and discipline; that in the summer of
1937 all the farm people had insistently demanded a moleben for rain,
that despite the village soviet’s ban Smirnov had held it in secret, all
of them were in church and no work was done on the farm all day, and he
had said in church: you see, there is no rain, everything is drying up,
because in your collective farm you have forgotten God; if you forget God
and the faith, you will die of hunger in your collective farm; and that
when the chairwoman of the village soviet came on 23 February 1938 to
inventory his property for the unpaid fine he had said, take what you
like, it is all the same to me now — the Soviet power is used to robbing.
On 22 March he was arrested and put in the cells at Zvenigorod, then in
the Sretenka prison in Moscow, and questioned the next day: with whom did
he keep up personal ties? — with his own brother, the archpriest Vsevolod
Smirnov, now arrested by the NKVD, and with no one else; you are charged
with counter-revolutionary activity and voicing terrorist sentiments
against the Soviet power and the leaders of the party, said the
investigator, reading out the false witnesses’ words — I do not admit
myself guilty of the charge. On 5 May three confrontations were held with
the false witnesses, who repeated their testimony to his face; asked
whether he admitted the facts they gave, he said: of all of them I confirm
only that I voiced discontent with the Soviet power, since it takes so
much tax from priests, and that people have forgotten God and the faith,
which is why they live badly, and that I went round the villages and
served molebens, and perhaps said something about the Soviet power, which
at my age I do not now remember; as to the inventory, I said that the
Soviet power lays on too much tax, beyond one’s income; as to the moleben
for rain, I served it, and told those present, you see there is no rain,
everything is drying up, because working in the collective farm you have
forgotten God, and if you forget the faith and God, then good will be
paid with good and bad with bad. The investigation ended that day, 5 May
1938, and on 9 August the special board of the NKVD sentenced him to five
years’ exile in Kazakhstan; evidently still in Moscow, he died on 13
September 1938 — 31 August of the old style, the day the Russian calendar
keeps him — and was buried in an unknown grave. The life was compiled by
the priest Maxim Maximov for the lives of the new martyrs of the Moscow
diocese.
