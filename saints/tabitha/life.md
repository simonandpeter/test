# Tabitha

doxologia.ro's calendar names Tabitha for 25 octombrie and gives her, in place of a life, a single sentence with a reference: «Sfânta Tavita (Căprioara) a fost înviată de Sfântul Apostol Petru la Iope (Faptele Apostolilor 9, 39-40) şi mai apoi cu pace s-a săvârşit» — Saint Tabitha (the Doe) was raised by the Apostle Peter at Joppa, and afterwards ended her life in peace.

That is all the page says, and it is worth saying what it does not. It does not retell the chapter it cites: nothing of the widows standing by weeping and showing the coats and garments she had made, nothing of Peter putting them all out and kneeling down, nothing of the word that raised her. It gives no year, no parentage, no place but Joppa, and no account of the peaceful death it reports.

The bracket in the calendar's own line is its gloss on her name: Căprioara, the doe, which is what Dorcas means. The page prints no troparion for her.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 25 October, and saint.gr begins her notice with the evangelist Luke's praise of her, that she was full of good works and of the alms she did. The word Tabitha is Syriac, it says, and is interpreted Dorcas, a gazelle, and this most pious and charitable Christian woman who lived at Joppa bore that name. From the Acts of the Apostles, at chapter nine, the page gathers that Dorcas was a skilled weaver who made tunics and garments, sold them, and out of the proceeds kept poor people, widows and orphans. When the apostle Peter, in the course of spreading the Gospel, had come to Lydda in Palestine, Dorcas fell ill and died; and when everything had been prepared for her funeral it became known that Peter was at Lydda, so two messengers begged him to come to Joppa. When he arrived they took him up to the upper room where she lay dead, and he, moved, said nothing but put everybody out, knelt and prayed earnestly, and then said, Tabitha, arise — and the dead woman rose. The news spread through all Joppa and many believed in Christ; and from then on Tabitha lived a good many years full of good works and alms, and death found her in deep old age, so that she went away in peace and with gladness.

The Russian calendar keeps her on 25 октября ст. ст. as well, calling her righteous where the Greek one calls her venerable, and it tells the one story that is hers. She was a virtuous and merciful woman of the Christian community at Joppa, and it happened that she fell gravely ill and died. The apostle Peter was preaching at that time not far off, at Lydda, and messengers were sent to him with an urgent request for help. When he came to Joppa she was already dead; he knelt and prayed earnestly to the Lord, then went up to the bed and called: «Тавифа, встань!» — Tabitha, arise. She rose up perfectly well.

The page gives the reference for all of it — Acts 9, 36 — and nothing beyond it: no parents, no trade, no year, and no second death. Its line and its first sentence put her in the first century, which is this folder's first dating.

The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion for her, and they are unusually long and unusually particular: Joppa keeping festival, the chief apostle speaking and the dead woman rising from the grave, the widows in their want crying out to him for her, and the name itself translated — «серну нарицаемую», the woman called a gazelle — beside a remembrance of the paralytic he had healed at Lydda.
