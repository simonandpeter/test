# Hermolaus of Nicomedia

Hermolaus the priest, with those who suffered together with him, the holy martyrs Hermippus and Hermocrates, was of the clergy of the church of Nicomedia. Hiding in a house, the three of them came out alive from among the twenty thousand holy martyrs who were burned in the church of Nicomedia by Maximian, and whose memory is kept on 28 December.

Being left of them all, these three servants of the Lord hid in one place and another for fear of the pagans; but where they could they taught the faithful the holy and right faith and turned them to Christ. And Hermolaus, seeing [Panteleimon the physician](/saints/panteleimon-the-healer) and speaking with him words inspired by God, made him a Christian. When Panteleimon was taken and brought to torture by that same pagan, Maximian the emperor of Rome, he was asked from whom he had learned the Christian faith, and being unable to lie he told him of Hermolaus, the Christian priest.

So the old man Hermolaus and the servants of Christ who served with him, Hermippus and Hermocrates, were seized and brought before the pagan court. Questioned, they confessed Christ the true God boldly and mocked the unclean idols and those who worshipped them, and for that they received from the torturer the sentence of death, and ended by the cutting of the sword. Of their suffering, the page says, more is written at length in the life of the Great Martyr Panteleimon, who is kept on 27 July.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 26 July, and keeps him with the two priests who were taken with him. saint.gr says all three belonged to the sacred clergy of the church at Nicomedia, and sets the year 305 against each of their names. When Saint Panteleimon was under examination and was asked by whom he had been taught the Christian faith, he answered that he had been taught it by the priest Hermolaus; that was enough for soldiers to be sent at once to arrest him, and his friends and fellow workers, the priests [Hermippus](/saints/hermippus-of-nicomedia) and [Hermocrates](/saints/hermocrates-of-nicomedia), followed him of their own will before the judge of Nicomedia. The judge examined Hermolaus first and then asked the other two what they wanted of him; they answered that they were soldiers of Hermolaus under the banner of Christ, and begged that all of them might have a death in common as they had had a brotherly life in common. The answer inflamed his anger instead of moving him, and he condemned all three. The Greek page adds a long list of his relics — portions at the monasteries of Pantokrator on the Holy Mountain, of Saint Nikanor at Grevena, of Leimonos on Lesbos and of Kykkos in Cyprus, at the Lavra of Saint Alexander Nevsky in Saint Petersburg and at the church of Saint George of the Greeks in Venice, and further parts at Venice, at the cathedral of Benevento in Campania and at Calci in the diocese of Pisa.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 26 July and opens with three words and no verb: a priest of Nicomedia. In the time of the emperor Maximian he too was among those two thousand martyrs whom the emperor condemned to be burned in the church and together with the church — and here the entry sends the reader to the 28 December. Hermolaus somehow escaped death on that occasion, together with two other priests, [Hermippus of Nicomedia](/saints/hermippus-of-nicomedia) and [Hermocrates of Nicomedia](/saints/hermocrates-of-nicomedia).

Hermolaus baptised [Panteleimon the Healer](/saints/panteleimon-the-healer), with whom he was brought together before the court, tortured, and at the last beheaded with the sword. With him suffered Hermippus and Hermocrates also, and all were crowned with the crown of victory and glory in the kingdom of Christ. They suffered honourably, the entry ends, about the year 304.
