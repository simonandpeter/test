# Anastasia of Rome, with Basilissa

Anastasia and [Basilissa](/saints/basilissa-of-rome) were of the great city of Rome, doxologia's notice says: well-born and rich, and disciples of the holy Apostles. After the Apostles' end the two women gathered up their holy relics by night and buried them, and for that they were denounced to the emperor Nero, who first put them in prison.

Brought out again and saying that they stood by their confession of Christ, they were hung up, and their breasts and their hands and their feet and their tongues were cut off; and at the last their heads were cut off with the sword.

That is the whole of what the Romanian calendar's life prints for her — four sentences shared with her companion. It gives no year beyond the name of Nero, no parentage, no burial place and no hymn; the page carries no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and fills the notice out. saint.gr says the two women were rich and of Rome, under Nero, whose reign it dates 54 to 68, and that they were an exception among their own people: they escaped the corruption and the estrangement that was flooding the upper ranks of Roman society, and lived in purity and virginity. They received the faith of Christ while they were young, and by the distributing of their goods became the support of the Christians of Rome. It is saint.gr and not the Romanian notice that says whose relics they buried — those of the chief Apostles Peter and Paul, after their martyrdom. A little later they were arrested on the charge of belonging to a band of Christian saboteurs; they confessed openly that they were Christians, and after various tortures were at last beheaded.

Its couplet has the pair as two ewe-lambs: «Ἀμνοῦ Θεοῦ σφάττουσιν ἀμνάδας δύο, Ἀναστασίαν καὶ Βασίλισσαν ἅμα» — they slay two ewe-lambs of the Lamb of God, Anastasia and Basilissa together. The page adds that her relics are kept in the Roman Catholic basilica of Santa Maria della Pace at Rome, and it prints no hymn for her.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the two women on the same day as the other two calendars and tells their story in four lines, and on one point it disagrees with both of them. It calls them two devout Roman women, and says that in the days of the emperor Nero they gathered the beheaded bodies **of the disciples of the apostles** and buried them honourably — where the Romanian notice has them gather the relics of the Apostles themselves, and the Greek page names those relics as the chief Apostles'. The disagreement is recorded and not settled here. For the rest it agrees: they were accused for that burying and thrown into prison, and after long torments — the entry's own parenthesis says their breasts and their tongue were cut off, and does not mention the hands and the feet the Romanian notice adds — they were beheaded. It prints no year, no parentage and no hymn.

**The Russian calendar gives the two women a year, which none of the other three sources does.** It keeps them on the same 15 April and serves two readings of them from the one index, the shorter inside the longer. They lived at Rome, both readings say, and were disciples of the apostles Peter and Paul, enlightened by them in the Christian faith; and out of Christian love they gathered up the bodies of the martyrs who had suffered for Christ and gave them honourable burial.

When this was reported to Nero, whose reign the page dates 54 to 68, they were shut in prison and cruelly tortured — beaten with the whip, torn with iron hooks, scorched with fire — and stayed unbent, confessing their faith in Christ the Saviour. By Nero's order they were beheaded, about the year 68. The Russian calendar is the one source read for this folder that prints her name second rather than first, and it prints no hymn under either name, the day's one hymn belonging to the three apostles the day is headed for.
