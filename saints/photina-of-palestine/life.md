# Photina of Palestine

A ship driven onto rocks in a storm broke to pieces, and of all who sailed in her one girl alone caught hold of a plank and came ashore on the sea rock where the hermit [Martinian of Caesarea](/saints/martinian-of-caesarea) was living in the open. He helped her up onto the rock, told her how he lived there and that a shipmaster came to him three times a year, blessed her, left her his bread and water and threw himself into the sea; dolphins carried him to land. The calendar calls her Фотиния and glosses the name in Russian as Светлана.

She stayed to keep the struggle alone. When the shipmaster came with bread and water for the hermit he found a woman on the rock instead; she told him everything, refused his offer to carry her to the city, and asked only that he come back with his wife and bring her men's clothing and wool for handwork. He did as she asked, and she lived on under the open sky by day and night, in heat and in cold, praising God for the life she had.

The page counts her prayers: twelve each day, and four and twenty times each night she stood up to pray, with a pound of bread serving her two days. After six years on the rock she died. Two months later the shipmaster and his wife came at the appointed time and found her asleep for ever, and they buried her in Caesarea in Palestine. The day's line keeps her with Zoe under one rank word and one century, and the calendar prints no hymn under her name.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 13 февраля ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260213.html) and [the life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life6904.htm); read 3 October 2026.*
