# Trophimus of Antioch

Trophimus was one of the three martyrs Trophimus, Sabbatius and Dorymedon, who were martyred under the emperor Probus and the governor of Antioch Heliodorus, in 278. When Trophimus and Sabbatius, finding themselves in Antioch, saw the manifold sinful orgies held in honour of Apollo — the soul-destroying festival of Apollo, the Romanian Lives say, at which the citizens as their custom was passed the whole day in sacrifices, drunkenness, dancing and every dissipation, and to which two strangers came from outside — they could not hold back and publicly condemned the sinful madness; quickly seized and brought to court, they boldly declared that they were Christians.

The governor Heliodorus had them scourged without mercy, so that their flesh was cut in pieces, and there Sabbatius breathed his last; Trophimus was taken to another, harsher governor, Dionysius Perennius, who flayed him with iron claws and threw him half-dead into prison. There a senator, Dorymedon, visited him, saw his martyrdom and was confirmed in the faith of Christ, and when the governor learned of it he tortured Dorymedon harshly — stripping him, the Romanian Lives say, hanging him up and burning his sides with red-hot iron spikes — and then threw the two as food to the beasts in the arena; but the hungry bear and the bloodthirsty leopard stood at their feet like tame lambs, and so did the maddened lion loosed after them, and when the executioners saw that the beasts would not touch them they beheaded them at once; the verses say Trophimus and Dorymedon, breathing one breath, received one end of life from the sword, and Sabbatius, scraped with sharp irons, reached as a martyr the divine sabbath-rest. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep the three on 19 September with a troparion and kontakion — God praised in Trinity glorified the trinity of martyrs.

The Russian calendar keeps the three on 19 September old style and prints 276; its life sets them in the reign of Probus (276–282) and tells it more briefly. At a pagan feast in Antioch, with sacrifices, wine and disorder, Trophimus and Sabbatius prayed that the Lord would set the misled on the way of salvation, and were seized and brought before the governor; Sabbatius died under torture, and Trophimus was sent to worse torments at Synnada before Dionysius, governor of Phrygia, walking three days in iron boots set with sharp nails, driven by mounted guards, and answering every torment only with the psalm: many are the afflictions of the righteous, and the Lord delivereth him out of them all.

The senator Dorymedon, a secret Christian, visited him in prison and washed and bound his wounds; when this came out he too was pressed to renounce Christ, and the two were thrown to the beasts, left untouched, and beheaded. The Russian troparion and kontakion are the ones the Romanian and Greek calendars sing.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, puts them in the third century under Probus, calls the governor of Antioch Atticus, and sets the unclean feast and sacrifices to Apollo at Daphne by Antioch; its Trophimus and Sabbatius are two eminent and honourable citizens who came to Antioch and were reported for keeping away. Asked his rank and country, Sabbatius answered that his rank and dignity, his homeland, glory and wealth were Christ the Son of God, ever living, by whose providence the universe stands and is governed, and he was beaten and scraped with iron until his bones showed through, and died so. Trophimus was sent to Dionysius in Phrygia, and there the senator Dorymedon served him in prison; thrown to the beasts, Dorymedon pulled the she-bear by the ears to make her tear him, and still she fawned on him, and at last Trophimus and Dorymedon were beheaded.
