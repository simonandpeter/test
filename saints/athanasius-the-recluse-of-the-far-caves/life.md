# Athanasius the Recluse of the Far Caves

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps two recluses of this name on the Julian 2 December and says so in one clause: one in the Near Caves, who died about 1176, and «другого Афанасия» — another Athanasius — in the Far Caves, in the thirteenth century. This folder is the second man. The calendar tells the two apart by the order of the words in their names and by nothing else: name page 303 reads «Преподобный Афанасий, затворник Печерский» and name page 304, which is his, «Преподобный Афанасий Печерский, затворник». Each has two life pages of his own, each with its own pair of days, and the day page prints a troparion for each separately.

What those pages say of him is short. He laboured in the Kyiv Caves monastery in the thirteenth century. Not wishing to see the vanity and the allurement of this world, he shut himself up in a cave so as to please God in silence, and there stood unceasingly at prayer. In many labours and spiritual struggles he gave up his spirit to God and was buried in the Far Caves, which are the caves of Theodosius; and the Lord glorified his own servant with the gift of healing.

The second of his two pages adds that his name is mentioned in the fourth ode of the common canon to the venerable fathers of the Far Caves, and that the «Сказание о житиях святых, в пещере преподобного Феодосия почивающих» — the account of the saints resting in the cave of Theodosius — says he needed no candle in the cave, because a light of heaven shone for him; and that to all who come to him with faith he gives healing. That is the whole of the record. Nothing is said of where he was born, of his family, of how long he lived or of the year he died.
