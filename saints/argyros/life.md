# Argyros

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 11 mai reads «Sfântul Mucenic Arghir», and the page kept under his name carries no Viață tab and no Tropar tab — only the scripture readings appointed for the day and an icon. The caption of that icon, a twentieth-century Greek lithograph in the Iaşi collection of the Synaxarion of the saints of the day, names him beside the hieromartyr of the same day: «Sfântul Sfințit Mucenic Mochie și Sfântul Nou Mucenic Arghiros». That he is called a new martyr is the whole of what the page says about when he lived; it says nothing of where he was from, nothing of how he died, and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and names his village, his parents and the occasion of his death. The holy new martyr Argyros was born in 1788 at Epanomi of Thessalonica, saint.gr says, to Asterios and Vasiliki, of the family of the Ntougioudis; and he came young to Thessalonica, where a tailor took him on as a servant. In those days a certain Christian from Sochos was shut up in the prison of the pasha of Thessalonica for some crime he had committed, and having nothing with which to pay the money the pasha demanded of him, he was threatened with hanging; and in the face of that threat of death the prisoner decided to change his religion. The Agarenes were delighted, and at once took him out of the prison and led him to a coffee-house in the place called Tachtakala, meaning to initiate him into the Muslim religion.

Argyros, who had learned of it, went into that coffee-house himself and began to reprove the man for his fault and at the same time to urge him to come back to the Orthodox faith. This conduct of his so provoked the Janissaries that they rushed on him and began to beat him so savagely that they would have killed him. The reader who wrote this paragraph did not read the middle of the Greek page, where the rest is told, and does not set it down. What the page's own verses say is that on the eleventh he endured the noose with a steadfast soul — «Ἑνδεκάτῃ Ἀργύριος τέτληκε βρόχον στεῤῥοψύχως» — and its hymns say the same, that by a death by hanging he was joined to the ranks of heaven. His skull, the page adds, is at the monastery of Saint Menas at Anthousa in Attica.
