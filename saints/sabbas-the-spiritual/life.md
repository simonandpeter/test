# Sabbas the Spiritual

Where Sabbas came from is unknown, saint.gr says. He lived the ascetic life in the holy monastery of the Honourable Forerunner on the island of Ioannina about the middle of the fifteenth century, and he was the spiritual guide of the venerable Nectarius and Theophanes the Apsarades, whom the Greek calendar honours on 17 May. He was the son of a noble family, and from an early age loved the poverty of Jesus Christ, and lived to his last breath as a humble and poor hermit.

Nobody ever saw him angry, the page says, or condemning anyone, or holding a grudge, while his heart overflowed with the humility of which he himself used to say: «Η ταπεινοφροσύνη είναι η πέτρα η στερεά και άρρηκτη επί της οποίας οικοδομείται η πνευματική ζωή» — humility is the firm and unbreakable rock on which the spiritual life is built.

His asceticism was very severe. Every evening he prayed until morning. His food was scant and plain; meat, fish and cheese he never accepted at his table, and he never let a drop of oil fall into his food. He fell asleep in peace in 1505, and when he gave up his soul to the Lord an ineffable fragrance welled from his honoured body. He was buried in the monastery of the Forerunner on the island of Ioannina; part of his foot is at the monastery of Varlaam of the Meteora, and a relic of his is kept at Rousanou of the Meteora. The page prints no hymn for him.
