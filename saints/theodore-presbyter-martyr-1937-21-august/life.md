# Theodore (Kallistov)

Theodore Stefanovich Kallistov was born on 17 April 1865 in the village of
Ust-Kera, or Arshinovka, in Penza province, the son of the deacon Stephen
Kallistov, and was baptised the next day in the Demetrius church there;
the parish registers show his forefathers in the male line clergy and
church servers back to the time of Peter, about 1722. After the Lomov
church school he entered the Penza seminary in August 1880 and finished
it in June 1886 with good and very good marks and excellent conduct, was
placed in the second rank of its pupils, and on 3 July 1886 was made
psalmist at Ust-Kera in the Nizhny Lomov district. On 12 October 1887 he
married Antonina Konstantinovna Vazerskaya, a graduate of the Penza
diocesan school and daughter of the priest Constantine Vazersky, a
well-known teacher of the Law of God and worker for the parish schools of
the province; the two had known each other from childhood, their fathers
and grandfathers having served together and been friends. On 25 October
1887 Bishop Anthony II ordained him priest to the Cosmas and Damian church
of Gumny in the Narovchat district; on 6 November he was moved to the
Saviour church of Alferyevo in the Insar district, on 5 January 1894 to the
Kazan church of Payevka in the Narovchat district, and on 26 April 1904,
with six girls and two boys already in the family, to the Archangel church
of Ivanovskaya Virga in the Nizhny Lomov district. From 1896 he was
assistant investigator in spiritual affairs; at Virga he taught the Law of
God in the Virga-Ivanovka school and the Virga-Sergievka college from 1904
to 1908 and in the Skripitsyn college from 1911, with the thanks of the
district school council for excellent results in 1907 and 1909, the
kamilavka in 1907 for zealous pastoral and parish service, the
archpastoral blessing in 1908 for his part in rebuilding the church, and on
3 February 1914 the order of St Anne of the third degree for his zealous
teaching of the Law of God — beside the Romanov tercentenary medal and
cross and the medal of the reign of Alexander III. By 1914 the Kallistovs
had fourteen children, four dead in infancy; the three eldest daughters
were married to priests and had gone with them to their parishes, the
younger girls went through the diocesan school, John was at the Tikhon
church school, and after the Second World War nearly all the surviving
daughters were teachers and the son Seraphim a teacher of mathematics and
a headmaster — their father’s gift passed on. On 7 March 1916 he was moved
to the cathedral church of the Life-giving Trinity at Troitsk in the
Krasnoslobodsk district; that December the daughter Katya died, and
Antonina, unable to bear the loss, died two years after her. The deaths of
daughter, wife and son-in-law did not break his faith: he went on serving
and supporting all who came to him, and in 1922, already rector, was
raised to archpriest.

His last years were full of sorrows. In the thirties the authorities did
everything to close the church at Troitsk, and he stood in their way. On
28 February 1930 he was detained by the OGPU and charged, among other
things, with compelling believers to take communion and with anti-Soviet
and anti-collective-farm agitation, which he did not admit; his
parishioners petitioned to have him released on their surety, but on 3
October 1930 the indictment was confirmed and the troika of the Middle
Volga OGPU exiled him, at sixty-five, to Western Siberia for three years
from the day of his arrest, and the church was closed. In 1933 he returned
to Troitsk and, by God’s providence, managed to open the church and resume
the services when almost every church in the region was shut; the working
church was a sore on the map of the republic, denunciations poured in from
officials of every rank and from those eager to prove themselves to the new
power, and in 1935 the church was closed again on the pretext of unpaid tax
on the building. He got it open once more and, to forestall another
closing, collected part of the tax the state demanded, but on yet another
denunciation, on 14 August 1937, in the great wave of repression in
Mordovia, he was arrested. At the inquiry he bore himself bravely, accused
no one, named no names and admitted no agitation. On 22–23 August 1937 the
troika of the NKVD of the Mordovian republic sat: Kalistov Fyodor
Stepanovich, born 1865, minister of a religious cult, archpriest, convicted
of counter-revolutionary activity, charged with active anti-Soviet
terrorist agitation and calling the population to struggle against the
Soviet power — resolved, to shoot. On 3 September 1937, between ten and
midnight, the sentence was carried out — 21 August of the old style, the
day the Russian calendar keeps him. He was rehabilitated under the law of
1991, and on 29 October 2019 the Holy Synod, on the report of Bishop
Pancratius of Troitsk, chairman of the commission for canonisation, and
the petition of Metropolitan Zinovy of Saransk, included his name in the
synaxis of the new martyrs and confessors of the Russian Church, fixed his
memory on 21 August / 3 September, the day of his death, declared his
remains, should they be found, holy relics, and ordered icons painted; the
Russian calendar keeps him also in the synaxis of the saints of Penza.
