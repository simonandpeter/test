# Marcellinus of Rome

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 7 iunie reads «Sfântul Marcelin, Episcopul Romei», Saint Marcellinus, bishop of Rome, and his page on the site has neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab. It carries the day's Epistle and Gospel — the Epistle to the Hebrews on the High Priest who is holy, guileless and undefiled, and the Gospel of the Good Shepherd, which are the readings appointed for a hierarch — and one article, titled «Sfântul Marcelin – drumul de la cădere la sfințenie», "the road from a fall to holiness".

That title is all the page says of his story, and a title is not a life; what the fall was, and when, this source does not say. So what is recorded here is his name, his see and his day: no year, no account of his episcopate or of his death, and no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 7 June, the menologion day the Romanian calendar keeps, and it answers the question this page could not. The Romanian source gave his name, his see and the title of an article about the road from a fall to holiness, and nothing about the fall. **This book says what the fall was in its second sentence**: Diocletian called him and threatened him with torture, and he sacrificed to the idols; and the emperor rewarded him for it with a precious robe.

Then he repented bitterly, and wept day and night over his denial of Christ, as the apostle Peter once did. A council of bishops was sitting at that time in Campania. The pope put on sackcloth and poured ashes on his head, went into the council, confessed his sin before them all and asked them to judge him; and the fathers told him to judge himself. Then he said: I deprive myself of the priestly rank, of which I am not worthy; and let my body not be buried after my death, but thrown to the dogs. And he laid a curse on anyone who should dare to bury it.

After that he went to Diocletian, threw the precious robe down in front of him, confessed his faith in Christ and reviled the idols. The enraged emperor had him tortured and then killed outside the city, together with three good men whom this corpus does not keep. Their bodies were buried at once and the pope’s lay thirty-six days. Then the apostle Peter appeared to the next pope and ordered him to bury the body, saying: he that humbleth himself shall be exalted. The entry gives no year for any of it, and none is recorded here.
