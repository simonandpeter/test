# Sabbas of Stagira

saint.gr says he was born at Stagira, about the end of the eighteenth century, and that out of love for the monastic life he took refuge in the monastery of Konstamonitou on the Holy Mountain and was trained there as a monk. The page explains his going to that house by the ties Konstamonitou had with Stagira and with Chalkidiki generally. In that monastery, which is honoured in the name of the protomartyr and archdeacon Stephen, he gave his life to God and went forward in virtue and devotion, being prepared, as the page puts it, to receive also the crown of martyrdom.

He was living there during the rising of Chalkidiki, in 1821, when he was called to take the road of martyrdom. saint.gr names its single source for what followed: the monk Dositheos the Lesbian of Konstamonitou, who wrote an account of the martyrdom of five venerable-martyrs of that monastery, and it refers the reader to 12 Ιουνίου for the others.

Dositheos's account, which the page quotes at length, says that the simple Sabbas the Stagirite foreknew the manner of his own death: leaving the coenobium, when he was sent out by the fathers to Ierissos, he told a brother of the house who was his friend what was to come. He had seen in a vision by night that his enemies pursued him and killed him, and that his soul flew up to heaven while his body lay on the earth. The foreknowledge proved true. Passing from there by the venerable monastery of Zographou, the blessed man was killed by the impious together with one layman, a servant of the monastery; both were men of Stagira.

The page adds that the first icon of the saint was painted by the iconographer Christos Karapalis and the service composed by the hymnographer Charalampos Bousias, and that the first festival in his honour was held in 1988, when the parish received that first icon in the presence of the metropolitan of Ierissos, the late Nikodemos.
