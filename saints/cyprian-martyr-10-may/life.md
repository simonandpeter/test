# Cyprian (10 May)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 10 mai reads «Sfântul Mucenic Ciprian», and the page it links for him carries neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab — only the feast line "10 Mai" and the readings appointed for the day. No country is named, no century is given, and nothing is told of what he did or how he ended.

The corpus already keeps six saints of this name — Cyprian of Antioch and Cyprian of Carthage among them — and none of them on 10 May; nothing read here connects this line to any of them, and the folder stands on the calendar's line alone.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and calls him by another form of the name. saint.gr's line has «Κυπρίνος», and its own parenthesis gives three more — or Cyril, or Cyrinus, or Cyprian — which is how the Romanian «Ciprian» and this Cyprinus are known to be one man. The holy martyrs [Alpheus](/saints/alphaeus-martyr-10-may), Cyprinus and [Philadelphus](/saints/philadelphus-martyr-10-may) were brothers, the page says, sons of the ruler of the prefects Vitalius, and came of the country of the Vascones in southern Italy. Taught the Orthodox faith and baptised by a certain venerable man named Onesimus, they preached Christ; they were denounced for it and seized by one Anengelion under the emperor Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251, and were sent bound to Rome. From there, after the first examination, they were taken to Puteoli to the governor Diomedes, who referred them to Tertyllus the ruler of Sicily; and Tertyllus, unable to change their minds, condemned them to death. Alpheus was beheaded after his tongue was cut out, Philadelphus was burned on a red-hot gridiron, and Cyprinus they fried in an enormous pan. The couplet over him plays on the fish his name carries: having eaten a piece of broiled fish before, O Saviour, receive gladly a carp out of the frying-pan — «Oπτού φαγών πριν Σώτερ ιχθύος μέρος, / Eκ τηγάνου Kυπρίνον ηδέως δέχου». The page of [Erasmus](/saints/erasmus-10-may) and [Onesimus](/saints/onesimus-10-may) adds that those two died with fourteen others together with these three.

The Ohrid Prologue, read for the Julian 10 May, keeps the three brothers under one heading and calls this one Кирин — which is the Κυρίνος of the Greek page's own parenthesis of four spellings, and so a fifth form of the one name rather than a fifth man. They were brothers born of one womb, it says, sons of a certain prince Vitalis in southern Italy, wonderful in nobility and strong in the faith; judged for their faith in Christ and led from one judge to another, from one torturer to another; taken over into Sicily, and killed there. The book sets that in the time of the emperor Licinius, where the Greek page sets the arrest under Decius — sixty years and two reigns apart, and neither reading is chosen here, the Serbian giving no years for its reign at all.

Of his own death it says only that Cyrinus died in the fire, where the Greek page has them fry him in an enormous pan: the same element put two ways. The brothers' incorrupt relics, this book adds, were found in the year 1517. And it closes by saying that these three brothers appeared to [Euthalia](/saints/euthalia-the-martyr), sending the reader to the second of March for her — which is the dream her own account already tells from the other side.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 10 мая ст. ст. in one line with four companions, «Мчч. Алфия, Филадельфа, Киприана, Онисима, Еразма и иных (251)», and gives the five of them a single life. It makes them men of Italy of the third century, and Киприан one of three brothers, sons of Вителий the governor of Italy, with Алфий and Филадельф; the three were enlightened in the faith of Christ and baptised by Онисим. When Christians were ordered sought out and tortured, the brothers were sent to Rome with Онисим, Еразм and fourteen others.

Онисим died at Rome of a heavy stone laid on his breast, and Еразм and the fourteen were beheaded there. The three brothers were sent on to Sicily and suffered at Месополь in the territory of Леонтины, and Киприан was burnt upon a pan. The life dates this to 251, under the emperor Декий, though it has opened by setting the persecution under Ликиний; their relics were found at Леонтины in 1517. The Russian calendar gives his forename three ways in one line — Киприан, Кирин and Кирилл — which is why this corpus keeps him under more than one spelling.
