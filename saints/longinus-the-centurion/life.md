# Longinus the Centurion

A centurion named Longinus, of Cappadocian stock, serving under Pilate, was set with his soldiers to attend the holy Passion and the crucifixion of Christ. Seeing the wonders that were done — the earthquake, the sun turned to darkness, the graves opened, the dead rising and the rocks split — he confessed that Christ is the Son of God, as the evangelist Matthew says: „And the centurion and they that watched Jesus with him, seeing the earthquake and the things that were done, feared greatly, saying, truly this was the Son of God”.

Some say of Longinus the centurion, the page adds, that it was he who pierced the side of the Lord Jesus Christ with the spear when He had died upon the cross, and that from the blood and water that flowed out he received healing for his sick eyes. When the life-giving body was buried, Pilate set him again with his soldiers to guard the tomb; and when the Lord rose in great glory and the angel came down from heaven and rolled away the stone, the guards shook for fear of him and became as dead men.

Then Longinus with two other of his soldiers believed in Christ altogether and became preachers of His resurrection, telling Pilate and the chief priests all that had happened. The chief priests took counsel with the elders and gave the soldiers much silver to hide the resurrection, saying they should tell men that the disciples had stolen Him away by night while they slept; but Longinus would not take the silver and would not hide the wonder. He confessed it, and his confession was true.

For that Pilate and the assembly of the Jews hated him, and turned upon him all the anger they had had against Christ, for he declared to everyone by his word that Christ is the true God, whose life-giving death and resurrection he had seen himself. He went home and filled all Cappadocia with his teaching and with his proved witness of the resurrection. The chief priests and elders, hearing of it, went to Pilate with many gifts and asked him to send word against Longinus to the Caesar at Rome — that he had thrown up his soldier’s office, turned from the Roman power and was troubling the people of Cappadocia by preaching another king.

Pilate took the gifts and wrote to the Caesar Tiberius, and the men he sent came into Cappadocia asking where Longinus was. Learning that he lived in his father’s village they went secretly, meaning to fall on him unawares, because they feared he might slip out of their hands. But Longinus, being full of the Spirit of God, knew what was coming, for the martyr’s crown prepared for him had been revealed to him, and he went out alone to meet them.

Hearing him, the soldiers were touched and at first did not believe that he was Longinus; and when they knew it for certain they were ashamed and did not wish to kill their benefactor. He pressed them to carry out their order, saying that they could not repay his love better than by sending him quickly to his Lord, whom he had long desired to see.

Then he put on white burial clothes, pointed with his hand to a hill nearby, and told his household to bury his body there and to lay with him the bodies of his two friends. Having prayed much and given the last kiss to everybody present, the three bowed their heads beneath the sword. The soldiers cut them off, took the head of Saint Longinus with them and went away; his household buried the bodies with honour in the place he had shown, and the head was carried to Jerusalem as proof, and thrown on a dung heap.

God, who keeps all the bones of those who please Him, kept the head whole in the refuse. A widow of Cappadocia had gone blind in both eyes and had sought help of the physicians a long while and found none, and resolved to go to Jerusalem and worship at the holy places; she took with her her firstborn son, and on the way the boy died. Weeping inconsolably, she saw Saint Longinus in a vision, who comforted her and told her all that had befallen him — how he had been at the Passion, the crucifixion, the burial and the resurrection, how he had preached Christ in Cappadocia and suffered for Him with his friends — and told her to go before the city and find his head under the refuse, for that was appointed for her healing.

The next night he appeared to her again in a great light, bringing her son with him, shining in wedding clothes, and holding the boy in a father’s embrace he said: see him for whom you grieve, see in what honour and glory he is, and be comforted, for God has numbered him among the heavenly ranks; take my head and your son’s body and lay them in one coffer, and weep for him no more. She rose, did so, and went home glorifying the Lord, saying that she had sought bodily eyes and had found the eyes of the soul as well, and that she had been in grief for her son’s death and now had him in heaven standing in glory before God.
