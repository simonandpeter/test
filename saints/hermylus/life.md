# Hermylus

Hermylus was a deacon, and had served Christ a long time before the day he was denounced. In the reign of Licinius (307-324), who was eager to please his unclean gods, a heavy order went out against all Christians: that they be hunted through the cities and the villages, and that whoever found one and told the emperor of him should be rewarded with great honour. So a soldier came to Licinius as he sat in judgment on a high place and said that there was a Christian there named Hermylus, who held the order of the diaconate by the rule of his religion, who had served Christ long, and who laughed at the gods and at the emperor and thought nothing of him.

Those who were sent found him at prayer with his hands raised to the Lord his God, and when he learned why they had come he was filled with joy and went with the soldiers, not dragged but running as a man goes to receive honour, and stood before the emperor with great boldness. Asked whether he was truly a Christian, he answered that he not only confessed it but was consecrated to the unseen God and served before him in the order of the diaconate. Told that he might as well be a deacon of the emperor's gods, he laughed and said the emperor must be deaf or mad: the idols were stone and wood, deaf and lifeless, the work of men's hands, fitter to be laughed at than bowed to.

He was beaten about the face with instruments of brass and shut in prison three days, singing as he went that the Lord was his helper. An angel came to him there and told him to speak and not be silent. Brought out again, he was stretched on the ground and beaten by six strong soldiers without mercy, and as he prayed a voice came from above: after three days he would be delivered from these dangers and would receive a great reward for his suffering. The torturers fell to the ground and could do nothing, and the emperor himself trembled, but would not know the power of God and sent the martyr back to prison. Later they stretched him naked and beat his belly with staves, and tore it open with eagles' claws.

The man set to guard him was [Stratonicus](/saints/stratonicus-companion-of-hermylus), who was secretly a Christian and his friend, and who wept to see him torn. He was denounced for weeping, confessed, was beaten until he fell silent, and was thrown into the same prison; and the two of them heard a voice from God saying that they had finished the course and kept the faith, and that the crown of righteousness was prepared for them, which they would take in the morning. Licinius sentenced Hermylus to be drowned in the Danube; and when Stratonicus refused to save himself and asked to die with his friend, the emperor gave him the same sentence.

As they were led down to the water the two sang together, "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will among men". The soldiers put them into nets and threw them into the deep of the river. On the third day their bodies were found on the bank, and the faithful buried them with honour some eighteen stadia from the city of Singidunum, which is Belgrade, laying both in one grave; so that as friends they should hold everything in common — the confession of Christ, the prison, the torments, the drowning, the burial of their bodies, and the glory in heaven.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day. saint.gr sets the two martyrs in the years of Licinius, emperor of the East, whom it dates 308 to 323, and says that Licinius — to please the idolaters, who disliked Constantine the Great — ordered a persecution of the Christians about 320 to 322. Hermylus, it adds, was a deacon in the order of the Church, and it was as a deacon that he was brought before the emperor. Its verses put their end in the Danube: that a basket served Hermylus and Stratonicus for a ship on their common voyage into the deep, and that on the thirteenth the Ister drowned Hermylus and his comrade.
