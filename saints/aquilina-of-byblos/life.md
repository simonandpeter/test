# Aquilina of Byblos

Aquilina was born in the city of Byblos in Palestine, among Christians who had learned the faith in Christ from the apostles. Her father was named Eutolmius; when the child was four months old her mother carried her to the bishop Eutalius, who signed her in the name of the Lord and made her a catechumen, and two months later enlightened her with holy baptism. Her father died when she was a year old, and her mother brought her up, teaching her at seven all the ways that belong to Christians.

In the seventh year of Diocletian's reign, when the girl was ten, a certain Volusian was set as proconsul over Palestine and began to persecute the faithful. Aquilina used to reason with the girls of her own age against the honour paid to idols, asking what good there was in gods who were themselves dead and powerless. One of the proconsul's servants, Nicodemus, overheard her and reported her, and she was taken in the second year of Volusian's proconsulship and the twelfth year of her life, and brought before the court.

Volusian tried her first with soft words, offering her life and the emperor's favour, and as many days as she liked to think it over; she asked for not one day and not one hour, saying that from infancy she had learned to worship one God. He then had her head bored through the ears with iron spits heated in the fire, so that the heat reached her brain and her brains ran from her nostrils with the blood, and she fell as though dead. Thinking her dead, he had her dragged outside the city for the dogs, as unworthy of burial. At midnight an angel touched her and raised her up sound, and told her to go and rebuke Volusian; the city gates opened of themselves, and she stood before him as he slept, and he woke in terror.

He condemned her to the sword. Giving thanks that her course had not been run in vain, she heard a voice from heaven calling her to receive what was prepared for her, and fell asleep before the executioner had raised his blade; he struck off the head of the dead girl all the same, and milk ran from the wound instead of blood. The Christians who were there anointed her body with costly spices, wrapped it in fine new linen and buried it with honour in Byblos, and many sick were healed at her grave.
