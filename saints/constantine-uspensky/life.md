# Constantine (Uspensky)

days.pravoslavie.ru names him first of seven on one line for 12 ноября ст. ст. and prints no life for him; the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru prints a long one. He was born on 9 April 1872 in the village of Alekseyevskoye in the Moscow province, the son of a psalmist, Vasily Uspensky. He finished the Moscow theological seminary in 1892 and was appointed a teacher at the Nikolo-Bogoyavlensky school, and soon married Anna, the daughter of the priest of the church of St Nicholas at Vasyutino-na-Mkhu in the Bogorodsk district.

In 1895 her father asked to be retired, and Constantine was ordained priest and appointed to his place. By tradition, the page says, a church had been founded on that spot «преподобным Сергием Радонежским» when he was looking for a solitary place to pray; the building standing there had gone up in 1787 in place of an ancient wooden one. He was made teacher of the faith at the Vasyutino parish school, and from 1902 head and teacher of the parish school of letters in the hamlet of Dalnyaya.

For diligent and blameless service he was given the nabedrennik in 1901, the violet skufia in 1906, the kamilavka in 1913, the gold pectoral cross in 1921; in 1925 he was raised archpriest, in 1929 given the palitsa, in 1932 the cross with ornaments. He served more than forty years in the one church. The local peasants were not well off and nobody then had a fruit garden; he planted an apple orchard by his own house, and at the Transfiguration the ripe fruit was carried to the church in great baskets and given out to the parishioners after the blessing. The apples out of the priest's garden were the village children's favourite treat.

When the persecution reached his parish a collective farm was set up at Vasyutino in the early thirties, and the school where he had once taught the Law of God became the place where the godless held their meetings. In December 1935 the headmistress arranged an antireligious lecture there at the hour of the service, and almost the whole village went to church to pray instead. In July 1936, at the invitation of the peasants of Alekseyevo, he served molebens in their houses for rain, and out of one house he was driven by force by a local unbeliever.

In 1936 the ringing of the bells was forbidden at Vasyutino and the godless began taking them down; young men of the place were found who did not fear the sacrilege, and the bells were thrown off. Soon afterwards a great fire broke out in the village and twenty-four houses burned to the ground. The flame reached the church. He came out on the church porch with an icon, and at the priest's prayers the fire stopped at the fence.

After the leadership of the country decided in the summer of 1937 on mass arrests and shootings of the clergy and the closing of churches, the NKVD began forcing some of the villagers to bear false witness. On 4 October 1937 the headmistress was questioned and said that whenever antireligious work was done in the village the Uspenskys contrived to appoint a service in church at that hour and so drew the people away from the lectures. On 6 November he and his son Boris were arrested and imprisoned at Noginsk.

The interrogator asked whether he had given out apples to the faithful in church in August, and he said he had. Asked why, he answered that it was the feast of the Transfiguration, and that by the church's own order apples are blessed in church on that day and given to the faithful. Asked what he had said while handing them out, he answered that he had handed them out in silence and said nothing; and when it was put to him that he had called the collective farm's apples the devil's, he said he had not said it. So the interrogator went through every charge the false witnesses had laid, and he rejected them all, as did his son.

Boris was born on 24 July 1895 at Karavayevo in the Vladimir province, the eldest of six children. He finished the theological seminary, was mobilised into the Red Army as a clerk when the world war turned into the civil war, and after demobilisation worked as a bookkeeper and in his last years lived with his father. On 12 November the troika sentenced the archpriest to be shot and his son to ten years in a labour camp. Constantine was shot on 25 November 1937 and buried in an unmarked common grave on the Butovo range near Moscow; Boris died in Siblag, in the Kemerovo region, on 15 December 1942, and azbyka's own footnote says his memory is kept on 2/15 December.

The day page prints seven men of 1937 on one line and carries no hymn under any of their names. They share a line and not a company: of the three read in this batch no two were sentenced by the same troika on the same date, and no two were held in the same prison, so no relation is written between them.
