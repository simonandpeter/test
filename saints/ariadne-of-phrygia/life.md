# Ariadne of Phrygia

Ariadne, though a slave, was higher and brighter than many ladies, slaves of the world’s vanities and earthly defilements. She lived in the years of the emperors Hadrian and Antoninus (117–139) and became a Christian in the city of the Promiseans — Prymnessus — in the theme of Phrygia Salutaris; when her master Tertyllus, one of the most powerful notables of the city, learned of it he pressed her to return to idolatry, but she held to her Christian confession, and it proved impossible to persuade her to sacrifice to the idols, even on the day her master’s son kept his birthday. Then they beat her hard and tortured her long, flaying her flesh with iron claws; to escape further pressure she left her master’s house, and as they pursued her she fell down a cliff and there found death — other synaxarion sources say the martyr herself begged God to open a rock and receive her in its cleft, and there, thanking God, she gave up her spirit, and angels of the Lord killed her pursuers with spears. A rock, split, saves Ariadne, the verse says, for Christ, the rock of life, sheltered her. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 18 September — a slave of one Tertill, the Romanian Lives say, a great man of the city of Promisia in Phrygia, under Hadrian.
