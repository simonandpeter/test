# Cyprian of Carthage

Cyprian was born around 200 at Carthage in North Africa, where his whole life and work passed. Thascius Cyprianus, son of a rich pagan senator, had an excellent secular education and became a brilliant orator, a teacher of rhetoric and philosophy in the school of Carthage, often pleading in the courts for his fellow citizens; he remembered later that he long remained in the deep darkness of night, far from the light of Truth, and spent his inheritance and his earnings on luxurious feasts that could not quiet his thirst for it. The Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, says that when he became a Christian he ceased to live with his wife and gave himself day and night to the study of Scripture and the perfecting of his character; for his uncommon virtues he was chosen presbyter and soon bishop — as merciful to Christians as he was hard on heretics; he wrote much under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, most forcefully against idolatry, Judaism and the heresy of Novatian, and beautifully on virginity, martyrdom, almsgiving, patience and the Lord’s Prayer. He suffered under Valerian and Gallienus in 258; before his death he prayed, blessed the people, and left twenty-four gold pieces for the executioner who was to behead him — unreachable greatness, the Prologue says, and the magnanimity of a true Christian. The Greek and Romanian lists for 31 August do not have him.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 13 September, under his own day, as the hieromartyr Cyprian, bishop of Carthage; its page for the day carries icons and texts of his and no life.
