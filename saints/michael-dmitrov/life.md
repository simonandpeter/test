# Michael (Dmitrov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him fourth of twenty-four and gives no life; the life here is the one azbyka.ru prints for the civil 2 декабря, out of pstgu.ru. He was born in 1873 in the village of Makkaveevo in the province of Ryazan, in the family of an archpriest and of an old priestly line. After the Ryazan seminary he taught in a parish school; he married in 1897 and was ordained priest. In 1905 he asked for his father’s place and was rector of the church of the Protection in his own village until 1913.

In 1913 he was made rector of the church of the Protection at Selishchi in the Kasimov district, and served there until his death. The church was small and wooden and the parish the poorest in the diocese, so poor that the diocese had to pay him a minimum itself; it was a few villages of thatched roofs and rushlights. He built a parish school and was its warden and law teacher, and beside it a roomy brick church; and he brought in congregational singing, moving the psalmist’s choir down from the ambo so that the people could stand by him and sing. His daughter Nina remembered him driving round the villages to gather anyone who would come to a practice.

Every Thursday he took part in the Kasimov clergy’s talks on pastoral work and the mission, and he went out to the neighbouring villages to speak with Baptists and sectarians, knowing the Scriptures very well and making long thematic extracts from them for his neighbours’ use. On the patronal feast of Selishchi the priests of Kasimov and the villages round came and served together — Sergius Pravdolyubov, Eugene Kharkov, Sergius Okunkov, Peter Uspensky, Basil Yastrebtsov and others, many of whom would also be martyred. The parish being poor he worked his own holding to feed his family, ploughed and sowed and reaped, milked the cow and kept bees, and before the service helped the widows and the weak of the village in secret.

He set up a fire service in the village with a watchtower and standing watches, took his turn at them, and when a fire broke out gathered every bucket he had and went to it, so that the peasants said a fire would soon be out if the priest came to put it out. He was arrested at the end of 1929 with many of the clergy of Kasimov; crowds of peasants came to the trial to stand up for him, and though he was convicted he was soon let out on the many petitions for a review. The searches and the summonses did not stop. He was arrested again in 1935; his wife Elizabeth was searched humiliatingly and died in 1936, and after her death he served every day for a year.

In 1937 he was given the right to wear the mitre. His eldest son asked him to go away to Moscow or somewhere else for a time until the worst years passed, and he refused firmly and said he could not leave his parish at such a time. On the night of Saturday to Sunday, 18 September 1937, after the all-night vigil, he was arrested and taken to the Kasimov prison, where the page says torture was used on him in full measure; he named nobody and admitted nothing. The troika of the Ryazan region sentenced him to be shot, and a man who shared his cell said he was not downcast and kept everyone else up. He was shot in the night of 1 to 2 December.
