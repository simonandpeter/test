# Basil of Kamenny

The calendar keeps him in six words and the two readings behind his name link are each a single paragraph, the shorter of them a cut of the longer. He lived in the fifteenth century and was a monk of the Spaso-Kamenny monastery, which stood on an island in Lake Kubenskoye, not far from Vologda. That island and that house are the whole of the geography the source gives him, and it gives him no parents, no abbot, no year and no death.

What it does describe is his shrine. By the reliquary of his relics, set up afterwards in the monastery church, there stood a full-length icon of him, heavy iron chains, and a cap made of bands of iron — the three objects by which the house remembered how he had lived. The church that reliquary stood in was dedicated to the Moscow fool for Christ of the same name, who died in 1557 and whose own memory the calendar keeps on this same day, a coincidence of name and day that this folder records and claims nothing by.

The longer reading adds one event. In 1774 the monastery burned, and everything in it was lost; the wooden reliquary and the image upon it came through whole.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 2 августа ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260802.html) and [the life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life6494.htm); read 13 October 2026.*
