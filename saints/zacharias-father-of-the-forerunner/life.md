# Zacharias, father of the Forerunner

Zacharias and [Elizabeth](/saints/elizabeth-mother-of-the-forerunner) were the parents of the Prophet, Forerunner and Baptist of the Lord, John. They were of the line of Aaron: Zacharias, son of Barachias, was a priest in the temple at Jerusalem — head of the priests of the course of Abijah, the Prologue says, which held the eighth turn of service, and not a simple priest but a high priest who entered the holy of holies, in the opinion of Chrysostom and other Fathers, the Greek synaxarion adds — and Elizabeth, daughter of Sobe, was the sister of Anna, the mother of the Theotokos. The righteous couple, walking blameless in all the commandments of the Lord (Luke 1:5–25), suffered childlessness, counted in Old Testament times a great punishment of God. One day, serving in the temple at the hour of incense, Zacharias received from an angel the news that his aged wife would bear him a son who would be great before the Lord and go before him in the spirit and power of Elijah; he doubted, and for his little faith was struck dumb — I am Gabriel, who stand before God, the angel said. When Elizabeth bore the son she declared, by the prompting of the Holy Spirit, that the child would be called John, though no one in their family had borne the name; asked, Zacharias wrote John on a tablet, and at once his speech came back and he prophesied of his son as the Forerunner of the Lord. When Herod heard from the Magi of the Messiah’s birth and resolved to kill every child under two in Bethlehem and round it, he knew well of John’s unusual birth and wanted him dead, fearing he was the King of the Jews; Elizabeth hid with the child — a year and a half old — in the hills, and seeing the pursuers cried, mountain of God, receive a mother with her child, and the mountain opened and hid them, a cave opening, the Prologue says, with water flowing and a fruitful palm growing by God’s power. Zacharias was then serving his course in the temple, and Herod’s soldiers, failing to learn from him where his son was, killed him at Herod’s order before the altar; his blood poured on the marble and hardened like stone as a witness of Herod’s crime. Forty days after his death blessed Elizabeth reposed too, and the child John remained in the desert, fed by an angel and kept by God’s providence until the day he was to appear at the Jordan.
