# Zosimas of Solovki

saint.gr calls him the great ascetic of the Russian north, abbot and founder of the coenobitic life on the island of Solovki. He was born in the province of Novgorod, in the village of Tolvui near Lake Onega, and from his childhood grew up in piety; after the death of his parents Gabriel and Barbara he shared out his property and was tonsured a monk. His longing for a desert place brought him to the shores of the White Sea and the delta of the river Sum, where he met the holy Germanus, honoured, the page says, on 30 July, who told him of a desert island on which he had spent six years with the holy [Sabbatius](/saints/sabbatius-of-solovki).

About the year 1436 Zosimas and Germanus crossed the sea and settled on the Solovki islands, and there Zosimas saw a vision of a beautiful church in the sky. The monks built cells with their own hands and began at the same time to till and sow the ground. Once, at the end of autumn, Germanus went to the mainland for provisions and the weather kept him from returning, so Zosimas stayed alone on the island the whole winter. He suffered many temptations in his wrestling against the demons, and was threatened even with death by hunger, until two strangers appeared in a wonderful way and supplied him with bread, flour and oil.

When many hermits had gathered on the island he built a wooden church of the Transfiguration of the Saviour and a refectory, and at his insistence an abbot was sent from the monastery of Novgorod to the new house with an antimension for the church. The abbots sent from Novgorod could not endure the conditions of the isolated island, and so the brethren chose Zosimas for their abbot. He set himself to the ordering of the monastery's inward life and brought in a strict coenobitic rule, and in 1465 he brought the relics of Sabbatius to Solovki from the river Vyg.

The monastery suffered from the nobles of Novgorod, who confiscated the monks' catches, and Zosimas had to go to Novgorod and ask the archbishop's protection. Marfa Boretskaya, who was rich and had influence, behaved insolently and had him thrown out, but repented later and asked him to dinner; and at that dinner he saw in a vision six of the leading nobles sitting at the table without their heads. He told the vision to his disciple Daniel and foretold a terrible death for them, and it was fulfilled in 1478, when the boyars were executed at the taking of Novgorod by Ivan III.

Shortly before his own repose he prepared his grave beneath the sanctuary of the church of the Transfiguration, and he fell asleep in peace in that same year, 1478. His relics and those of Sabbatius were moved on the eighth of August 1566 to the chapel dedicated to the two of them in the cathedral of the Transfiguration. The page adds that he is held to be the protector of hives and the guardian of bees, that the sick call on his grace to be healed, and that the many hospital churches dedicated to him bear witness to the healing power of his prayer before God.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day as the Greek calendar and gives the pair of them two sentences: that [Sabbatius](/saints/sabbatius-of-solovki) and Zosimas were the founders of the ascetic community on the Solovetsky island in the White Sea, and that many great saints were glorified in that community. Its year for Zosimas, 1478, agrees exactly with the year this folder already held, and its 1435 for Sabbatius with his; so nothing was added and nothing displaced. The Prologue names no church of the Transfiguration and no grave prepared beforehand, which is the Greek page's ending, and it keeps Sabbatius on a second day of its own besides this one, the twenty-seventh of September.

**This is his home calendar, and it is the only one of the four to print him a service or to tell his life at length.** He was born in the Novgorod diocese, it says, in the village of Tolvuya near Lake Onega, brought up in piety, and after the death of his parents Gabriel and Barbara he gave away his property and was tonsured. Looking for a solitary place he went to the shore of the White Sea, and at the mouth of the Suma he met Herman, who told him of a deserted island in the sea where he had lived six years with [Sabbatius](/saints/sabbatius-of-solovki).

About 1436 the hermits crossed safely to the Solovetsky islands, and God blessed the place of their settling with a vision granted to Zosimas of a beautiful church in the air. They built a cell and a fence with their own hands and began to till and sow the ground. Late one autumn Herman went to the mainland for supplies and could not get back through the weather, and Zosimas spent the whole winter alone on the island; he bore many temptations in the struggle with the demons, and when starvation threatened him two strangers appeared and left him a store of bread, flour and oil. In the spring Herman came back with a fisherman named Mark, bringing food and fishing tackle.

When several hermits had gathered he built them a small wooden church of the Transfiguration of the Lord with a refectory, and at his request an abbot was sent from Novgorod with an antimension for the chief church; and that was the beginning of the famous Solovetsky house. The superiors sent out from Novgorod could not endure the hardness of the life, and the brethren chose Zosimas abbot. He gave himself to the ordering of the house within and brought in a strict common life, and in 1465 he carried Sabbatius's relics to Solovki from the river Vyg. The house was oppressed by the Novgorod boyars, who took the monks' catches of fish; he had to go to Novgorod and seek the archbishop's protection, and on his advice he went round the boyars' houses asking them not to let the place be ruined. The powerful and wealthy Martha Boretskaya had him driven out with dishonour, then repented and invited him to dinner, during which he suddenly saw six of the greatest boyars sitting without their heads. He told the vision to his disciple Daniel and foretold the boyars a speedy death, which came true in 1478, when Novgorod was taken and the boyars were executed.

Not long before his end he made his own coffin, and he was buried in it behind the altar of the church of the Transfiguration, on 17 April 1478, which is the day the calendar keeps him on. A chapel was built over the grave afterwards, and on 8 August 1566 his relics and Sabbatius's were carried into a chapel consecrated in their honour in the Transfiguration cathedral. Many wonders were recorded of the two of them appearing to fishermen perishing in the sea; and the page adds that he is the patron of beekeeping and the keeper of bees, with the by-name of the bee-man, and that people turn to him often in illness, the many hospital churches dedicated to him witnessing to the healing power of his prayers.
