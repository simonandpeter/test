# Dositheia the Recluse of Kyiv

Only after she was buried did anyone find out that the recluse Dositheus of the Kitaev hermitage, whose counsel half of Kyiv had come for, had been a woman. The Russian calendar keeps her on 25 September under the name she was given at birth. She was born in 1721 at Ryazan into a gentry family, and her first schooling came from her grandmother Porphyria in Moscow, a nun of the Ascension monastery, who taught her to love the ascetic life.

When Porphyria took the great schema the girl went back to her parents' house and went on living there as though it were a monastery, because nothing in the world held her. Her parents were disturbed by it and wanted her married quickly. At fifteen she put on men's clothes and walked to the Trinity-Sergius Lavra, where she called herself Dositheus, a peasant's son. They took her as a novice and made her sacristan.

Three years later she slipped away so that her parents would not find her, and went to the Caves monastery at Kyiv, which would not have her because she had no passport. With nowhere else to go she settled in a cave near the Kitaev hermitage and lived there as a solitary. A monk left bread and water at the mouth of the cave; through Lent she shut herself in and ate the wild herbs that grew nearby.

Given wholly to God in asceticism and unceasing prayer, she was granted prophecy and the reading of hearts, and became known around Kyiv. Christians came in numbers to hear the «затворник Досифей» through a small window that did not even show his face. In 1744 the Empress Elizabeth Petrovna visited the recluse and asked that he be tonsured.

Some time before that a boy named Prokhor Moshnin had come to her — Seraphim of Sarov. Foreseeing what God had prepared for him, she blessed him and sent him on to Sarov, charging him to keep the Jesus prayer always on his lips and in his heart, so that the Holy Spirit would remain in him. Her own sister came once to ask the elder what had become of her sister; without naming herself, she answered that Dositheia was not to be looked for any more, because she was hiding in order to serve the Lord.

When the monastery lands were confiscated by imperial decree she took shelter in the Far Caves of the Kyiv Caves Lavra, and found no quiet there for the crowds who came asking things of her, so after four years she moved to a remote cell at Kitaev. Her last attendant and disciple was the monk Theophanes, whom she sent into Moldavia to Paisius of Neamț; he came back and stayed with her until she died, and then, as she had wished, went to Solovki to live as a solitary, where he did much to revive the prayer of the mind in Russia.

At fifty-five she felt that her hour had come and went round the brotherhood to take her leave. Back in her cell she spent the night over the psalms, and in the morning of 26 September 1776 they found her on her knees before the icon with a note in her hands: «Мое тело готово к погребению. Прошу вас, не трогайте его и похороните по обычаю».
