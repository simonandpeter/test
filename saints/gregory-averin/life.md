# Gregory Averin

Gregory Ivanovich Averin was born in 1889 to a peasant family in the Kostroma
province, finished a teachers' seminary in 1910 and taught in a parish school at
Kologriv. He chaired the district council in 1917, and in March 1918 was arrested as
a Socialist Revolutionary and charged with organising a peasant rising. He spent four
months in prison, and it was there that he became a convinced Christian; on release
he left the party.

He was ordained in 1921 and served in Kostroma as a celibate priest, then at
Ilinskoye until 1929, when he was arrested for anti-Soviet agitation and sentenced to
five years in the camps. He served again after his release, was arrested once more in
1935 and sent to a camp near Temirtau in Kazakhstan.

The account of him there is the part worth keeping: he made no attempt to go
unnoticed. Anyone meeting him could see at once that he was a priest. He prayed
openly in the barracks, talked with the prisoners, read them the prayers and the
Gospel aloud, and urged them not to give up their faith. He was arrested in the camp
in 1937 for religious work among the prisoners, spent seven days in the condemned
cell knowing his sentence, and was shot on 7 September — 20 September new style —
1937. He was glorified in 2000.
