# Vladimir (Vvedensky)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him fifth of fifteen on 21 октября ст. ст., in a line of eleven priests, two deacons and two monks of 1937. He was born on 14 July 1881 in the village of Pogost-Arkhangelsky in the Kalyazin uyezd of Tver province, the son of a priest, Dmitry Vvedensky. He finished the Tver seminary, was ordained priest, and served in the church of the village of Sknyatino in the same uyezd. The calendar's life gives nothing else of his years of ministry; what it records is the three weeks that ended them.

He was arrested on 7 October 1937 and held in the prison at Kashin, where he was interrogated over three days. A teacher from Sknyatino was called and reported a conversation of the previous January in the flat of the priest's son Mikhail: Fr Vladimir had argued for idealism against materialism, saying that at the ground of everything in the world lies the ideal, that is, God Himself, and that the Bolsheviks would come to understand this, since the new constitution already said that religious observance was not forbidden and the clergy were having their voting rights given back.

A station warehouse keeper who had known him nineteen years testified next, that he had said the collective farms were of no use to the peasant and would fall apart, that the newspapers' reports of the Spanish republican army's victories were the opposite of the truth, and that at confession in 1936 he had told children of pre-school age to come to church more often and read less Soviet literature. Asked whether he admitted conducting anti-Soviet agitation among the collective farmers, the priest answered that he had not and did not admit it; asked whom he had met on the bank of the Volga, he said he had talked with a man named Strogin about living and working, about the timber floating and about the harvest.

After interrogations of that kind the NKVD officer wrote an indictment admitting that the accused had not confessed but was "sufficiently exposed by the testimony of witnesses". On 1 November the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot two days later, on 3 November 1937. He was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops of the Russian Orthodox Church in August 2000. The day page prints troparia and kontakia for other saints it heads and nothing under his name.
