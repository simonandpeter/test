# Theophilus the Myrrh-streamer

Theophilus lived in the sixteenth century and came from Ziki, a place in the north of Greece. He had a very good education as a young man, doxologia says, but decided that it mattered more to give his life to God, cleansing himself of every passion that destroys the soul and seeking the virtue that fills a man with the grace of the Holy Spirit.

At the request of Niphon, patriarch of Constantinople, he travelled to Alexandria to find out whether the reports of the patriarch Joachim were true — that he could move mountains and drink poison without harm — and he looked into the matter and was able to see whether the stories were so. The page does not say what he found.

That obedience done, he went to struggle on the Holy Mountain: first to Vatopedi, then to Iviron, and at last to the cell of Saint Basil near Karyes, on land belonging to the Pantokrator monastery. He did not look for men's praise, but his fame became known throughout Athos and beyond it, for his holy life and his gifts could not be hidden and were made plain by the Lord; and when the archbishop of Thessalonica fell asleep, Theophilus was called to that throne and refused it out of humility.

In 1548, feeling his hour near, he ordered his disciple Isaac not to give him a splendid burial but to tie a rope round his feet, drag him out of the cell and throw his body into the stream nearby. He died on 8 July 1548, and Isaac, though he hesitated, obeyed him as he had always obeyed him in life. By God's will the relics were found later and laid in his cell, and after that a scent as of myrrh began to flow from the incorrupt body; he was afterwards canonised at the Pantokrator monastery.
