# Nicetas of Remesiana

Nicetas — his life writes the name Niceta and also Nichita — was born about the year 338 in the city of Remesiana, in the province of Dacia Mediterranea south of the Danube, today Bela Palanka in Serbia, into a family of Christian Daco-Romans. From childhood he learned to live in the spirit of the Gospel, and he studied in the high schools of his time, growing into a young man with a deep knowledge of many arts and sciences. Kindled with the desire for God, he gave his life to God's service for the salvation of men, and was therefore chosen and consecrated bishop of his native city, where he shepherded worthily between 366 and 414.

Though the Gospel had been preached to the Daco-Romans by the Apostles and their disciples, the unsettledness of the times and the inroads of the migrating peoples meant that the right faith needed new preachers full of courage and of the spirit of sacrifice. The missionary work of the hierarch of Remesiana reached from the two Dacias south of the Danube to the Daco-Romans north of it as well, and his preaching was addressed also to other, barbarian nations — the Goths, the Bessi and the Huns.

He was a true apostle, doxologia's life says, strengthening the Orthodox faith, bringing pagans to baptism and putting away superstitions from among the newly christened. To carry that mission on he ordained priests and deacons, built and consecrated churches and founded monastic communities. In parishes and monasteries alike he settled the reverent celebration of the divine Liturgy and of the holy services, and even of the night vigils, as a spiritual preparation on the eve of Sundays and of the great feasts.

In his missionary work he used the singing of the psalms, and also Christian hymns which he composed himself — among them the hymn Te Deum laudamus, «Pe Tine, Dumnezeule, Te lăudăm», written in a Latin everyone could understand and of great theological clarity. Of his writings, which show his right belief, his apostolic zeal and his fatherly care for his people, there have come down to us, whole or in part: six catecheses for those preparing for holy baptism, grouped under the title of the Little Books of Instruction, and the works On the Different Names of our Lord Jesus Christ, To a Consecrated Virgin, On the Vigils of the Servants of God, and On the Profit of Psalm-Singing.

He is known to have made at least two pilgrimages to the relics of Saint Felix in the city of Nola, in the Italian province of Campania, and on that occasion to have formed a warm and spiritual friendship with Saint Paulinus, bishop of that city, who dedicated to him two poems praising his missionary service and its divine zeal.

He fell asleep in the Lord a little after the year 414, closing a long pastorate rich in spiritual fruit, and honoured for his holiness by those who had known him and by their descendants after them. The mild, wise and humble figure of the hierarch of Remesiana stayed printed on the hearts of the Daco-Romans and was handed down across the centuries, and is beautifully drawn in some of the carols. He is honoured particularly by the Romanian communities in Serbia as a protector, since he lived in the time when the Romanian people was forming and strengthened their forebears in the right faith.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 24 June, the same menologion day the Romanian calendar keeps him on, and its whole entry is a page long. It calls him the friend and coeval of a Latin bishop whose own day it gives, and whose name is recorded on this folder’s Serbian attestation rather than here. It seems, the book says, that he was a Slav, and that as such he preached the Gospel among the Slavs in the Niš and Pirot region; the hedge is the book’s own and is kept. His see was the city of Remesiana, which some, it adds, understand to be Pirot.

What change Nicetas worked among the Slavs is best shown, it says, by the poem his friend made to him: What a change, and how happy a one. Mountains till then pathless and bloody now hide robbers turned into monks, the nurslings of peace; where the manners of wild beasts were, there is now the face of an angel; the righteous man hides in a cave where before the evildoer lived. Beside his missionary service, the entry ends, he wrote several books, six books on the faith among them, and a book on the fallen girl that moved many to repentance; and he reposed in the fifth century. That century is not written into his dates, which stay as the Romanian page left them, after 414, because closing an open interval at the end of the century would narrow it.
