# The Hundred and Fifty Rhetors

saint.gr's calendar for 25 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιοι Εκατόν πενήντα Ρήτορες» — a hundred and fifty rhetors — and its whole notice is one sentence: they believed in Christ through [Catherine](/saints/catherine-of-alexandria), whom the same calendar keeps on the same day, and were martyred by fire. The page names not one of the hundred and fifty, and so this is one entry and not a hundred and fifty.

Catherine's own page on that calendar says where they came from. When the eparch of the region found her words too strong for him, he called a public debate with the worthiest rhetors of Alexandria; and Catherine silenced them — and more than that, some of those who had argued with her were persuaded by what she said and embraced the Christian faith.

The couplet set over their line turns on the word for a faction: the factions these men once made for error became, when they died in the fire, the finding of their crowns. Beside them the same day keeps [Basilissa](/saints/basilissa-wife-of-maxentius), who the page says believed through Catherine too. It gives the rhetors no names, no year, no emperor and no hymn, and prints no relics for them.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 25 Νοεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/11/25/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1059/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
