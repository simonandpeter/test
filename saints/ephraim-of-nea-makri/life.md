# Ephraim of Nea Makri

Ephraim was born in Greece on 14 September 1384, on the feast of the Cross. His father died while he was small, and he and his six brothers were brought up by their devout mother alone. At fourteen he entered as a monk the monastery of the Most Holy Mother of God on the Hill of the Undefiled, in Attica, near the place now called Nea Makri.

On 14 September 1425 — again the feast of the Cross, and the day he turned forty-one, with twenty-seven years of hard monastic labour behind him — Turks attacked the monastery on that hill. The rest of the brotherhood were beheaded; Ephraim was taken captive. His martyrdom lasted eight months, in which he was tormented every day to make him leave the Orthodox faith and become a Muslim. He was hung head downwards, a burning brand was driven into his belly and his flesh was torn, and on Tuesday 5 May 1426, at nine in the morning, he gave up his soul into the hands of the Lord.

For more than five hundred years nothing whatever was known of him. On 3 January 1950 the nun Makaria found his relics by a revelation from God, and with them his life and his sufferings. In June 1971 he appeared in a dream to a nun of his own monastery on the Hill of the Undefiled and told her part of what he had endured: «Dacă ai ști cât am pătimit, chiar și la cap…» — if you knew how much I suffered, even in the head — and showed her with his hand that they had driven great nails through his head into a tree, and, crossing himself, that there had been very many of them.

So he is kept twice a year: on 3 January, the day his relics were found, and on 5 May, the day of his falling asleep. doxologia's troparion page for him is a Greek recording and prints no text.
