# Tatiana (Gribkova)

Tatiana was born in 1879 in the village of Shchukino in the Moscow
district of Moscow province, the daughter of the peasant Ivan Gribkov. In
1896 she entered the Kazan convent of Golovino, nine versts from Moscow —
founded in 1876, first as a community and from 1886 as a convent, with two
stone and two wooden churches, a hospital, an almshouse, an icon workshop
and a school, and nearly two hundred sisters. In 1925 the convent was
liquidated by the Soviet power, and she settled with her sister in her
native village. In the cruel persecutions of the Russian Church, whose
next wave fell in 1937, testimony against the novice Tatiana was made up
with the help of false witnesses: a twenty-five-year-old communist named
Kuznetsov, who rented a room in her house, denounced her on 13 May 1937
to the head of the Kuntsevo district NKVD, ending with a request — for
more concrete confirmation I can help you, but please do not tell her my
name, since I live in the same flat with her and it would lead to some
unpleasantness. Questioned on 30 May, he said he had lived in her house
since October 1935; that she was a former nun who worked nowhere, lived
with her sister, an active collective farmer, mostly on the rent of the
four families in her house, and quilted blankets; that a great many
people visited her, monastic folk, a woman who took literature from her,
local people, and that she herself went visiting in the evenings, above
all before the religious feasts, and before them went to Moscow to see
all sorts of priestly folk and her old convent acquaintances, evidently
well acquainted with the higher clergy — she knew Bishop Pitirim and
others, moved in circles where it was supposedly said in confidence that
Hitler would soon come, knew of the appointments of priests made at the
Synod in Moscow, and had said of Pitirim’s arrest that had they not taken
him at Easter every church would have been packed; that by all accounts
she had kept gold, having collected gold in the first years of the
revolution to help the tsar Nicholas fight for the “fatherland”; that she
had many religious books and pamphlets, was a hostile person, held
anti-Soviet talks, spread rumours of the coming of Antichrist and held
comrade Lenin the first Antichrist, called the collective farms serfdom —
saying in February 1937 that people had been herded in and deprived of
freedom and given no bread — held that the power would soon change and
religion triumph, and had said insulting things of the leaders of the
party and government, concluding that since none of our own Russians were
in power they must surely betray the Russian people. Even so she was not
arrested then; the arrest came on 9 August 1937, after Stalin’s order of 5
August for mass arrests, and she was put in the Novinskaya prison in
Moscow and questioned from the 10th: did you collect gold in 1917 to help
the tsar Nicholas? — no, never; the inquiry has established that various
ministers of religion visit your house, in particular from the former
convent — no, no one visits me, except that one nun, a relative of mine,
used to; that you spread religious literature among the people — no, I
gave no one religious literature to read; that you systematically carry on
anti-Soviet agitation and praise Hitler — that I deny entirely; I carried
on no anti-Soviet agitation; that you spread slander on the leaders of the
Soviet power — I deny it; I never spread any slander; the witness
Kuznetsov exposes you as having repeatedly voiced counter-revolutionary
thoughts about the Soviet power and its leadership in his presence — why
do you hide it from the inquiry? — I never voiced counter-revolutionary
thoughts in his presence; in general I hide nothing — what I did not say,
I deny; the inquiry has established that you carried on
counter-revolutionary agitation among the collective farmers; do you admit
guilt? — I do not admit myself guilty of counter-revolutionary agitation,
since I never carried it on. On 10 September 1937 the NKVD troika
sentenced her to be shot, and on 14 September 1937 — 1 September of the old
style, the day the Russian calendar keeps her — the novice of the Kazan
convent of Golovino was shot at the Butovo range outside Moscow and buried
in an unknown common grave. The life was compiled by the priest Maxim
Maximov; the Russian calendar keeps her also in the synaxis of the saints
of Moscow and of the new martyrs of Butovo.
