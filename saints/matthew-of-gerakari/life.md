# Matthew of Gerakari

Matthew was born about the year 1670 at Gerakari of Amari, in the diocese of Lampe and Sphakia, when Crete had been subjected to the Turkish conquerors, who were persecuting and torturing the Christians. His father was a priest, saint.gr says, and that helped the young saint to be brought up in the Christian values and virtues.

That was also, the page continues, the cause of what followed: the envious devil hated Matthew's virtues and led him into impiety, and those of the other religion chose him to marry an Ottoman woman, Aïshe, so that he would embrace Mohammedanism. After a little time, however, he understood his dreadful mistake, repented deeply, and returned to his faith and to his Church as a crypto-Christian.

He could not keep his faith hidden. Morning and evening he made the sign of the Cross, and his Ottoman wife, enraged at it, denounced her husband to the Turkish judge of the city of Rethymno, who called him to answer for himself. He stood before the barbarous judges with boldness and confessed his love for Christ outright, and the court sentenced him to death by beheading. He departed to the Lord on 18 August 1697, and that day has been the day of his memory ever since.

Pious Christians carried his martyred body to Gerakari where he was born and buried it with honours at a place which to this day is called «του Μαθιού το μνήμα», Matthew's grave; a church was raised to the martyr on the same ground. saint.gr calls him a boast of Crete — a man who, although he had changed his faith, repented and came back to Orthodoxy, and who will become in Crete the saint of the sympathy and the love of the people.
