# Job the Long-Suffering

Job came of the stock of Abraham, of the sons of Esau, the fifth from Abraham, and lived in the land of Uz, on one side of Arabia. He was the richest man of the east and greatly feared God: seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen, five hundred she-asses at pasture, and very many servants. He was true, blameless, upright and faithful, and kept away from every evil thing. He had seven sons and three daughters, and the sons, seeing their father give alms endlessly to the poor, did as he did, and served at table for the afflicted.

They feasted in turn, a day at each brother's house, and when the seven days were done Job would send to them and tell each to search his conscience closely in case he had offended the Lord in word or in thought; and he offered a sacrifice of cleansing for them, saying within himself that his children were young and might have sinned against God in their hearts. He feared God, the page says, not with the fear of a slave but with the fear that belongs to the love of a son.

On a day the angels of God came to stand before the Lord, and the devil came with them, and God asked him whether he had considered His servant Job, than whom there was no one like on earth. The devil answered: does Job honour God for nothing? Have You not hedged in all that is his? Stretch out Your hand and touch all that he has, and see whether he will not curse You to Your face. And the Lord gave all that was Job's into his hands, but told him not to touch the man — the devil, as the page insists, can do nothing without God's leave.

Then the messengers came one after another: fire fell from heaven and burned the sheep and the shepherds; the oxen and the asses were taken; and last, that his sons and daughters were eating and drinking at their eldest brother's when a great wind came out of the desert and struck the four corners of the house, and it fell on them and they all died. It broke his heart, and still he said no evil word. He rose and tore his clothes, cut off the hair of his head, poured dust over it, fell on his face and worshipped: naked I came out of my mother's womb and naked I shall go down into the grave; the Lord gave and the Lord has taken away; blessed be the name of the Lord.

The devil went back and asked for his body — skin for skin, and all that a man has he will give for his soul — and was given it, but not his soul. He struck Job with leprosy from head to foot, and Job went out of the city and sat on the dunghill, scraping his sores with a potsherd; and the beating did not last a day or a month or a year, but seven years and a half, while the worms ate him alive. Then, as with Adam in paradise, the devil worked through his wife, who asked him how long he would endure and say nothing against God; and Job answered her gently, seeing whose voice it was: have we received good at the hand of the Lord, and shall we not suffer evil?

Three friends came, each from his own country — Eliphaz of Teman, Bildad of the Shuhites and Zophar of the Minaeans, rich men who used to buy his cattle and his wool. Seeing him from far off they did not know him, and cried out and wept and tore their clothes and poured dust on their heads, and sat by him seven days and seven nights without a word. Then Eliphaz opened his mouth, and instead of comfort came the accusation: that God had cut him down like a barren tree, that he had held back the widow's due and the labourer's hire. Job answered mildly that they had come to wound him — he would rather have called the grave his mother and the worms his brothers and sisters than hear such things from his friends — and that he had been the father of orphans and the mother of widows, the eye of the blind and the ear of the deaf, the foot of the lame and the hand of the maimed, that the shearing of his lambs had warmed the shoulders of the poor, and that his door had never been shut to a stranger.

When the friends had gone, God appeared above him in cloud and whirlwind. Job had lain seven and a half years and was bones and a heart, wounded by his friends and reproached by his wife; and he rose up sound as a man of thirty, and God asked him where he had been when the earth was founded, where darkness dwells and where light has its place, and who set the bounds of the sea. And because he had waited with patience and had not spoken one evil word in all those griefs, God gave him a hundred and forty years more and doubled his goods, and told him he would live to see his fifth great-grandchild and would fall asleep full of days.

His herds became fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen and a thousand asses, and seven sons and three daughters were born to him again as before. The Lord doubled his cattle and did not double his children, so that no one should think the first children had perished as the cattle had: the cattle were lost altogether, but the dead children are not lost, and will be found at the resurrection of the righteous. There were no fairer maidens under heaven than his daughters, and he gave them an inheritance among their brothers. He lived two hundred and forty-eight years in all, saw his sons' sons to the fourth generation, and ended in deep old age.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and names what the Romanian line did not. He came of the land of Ausitis, saint.gr says, which lay between Judaea and Arabia, and he was the son of Zareth and of Basora; he was a prophet for forty years, and flourished about 1900 BC, or by others about 1400 BC. For all his fabulous wealth he was God-fearing, just, upright and blameless. By God's permission, to try him, he was tempted by Satan and lost his wealth and every person dear to him except his wife, and was himself struck with the heaviest form of leprosy, so that he went out of the city and passed the rest of his life in a cave, praying and scraping his sores for relief. Neither his wife's urgings nor his friends' were able to draw him away from God.

When his wife came to him and said in her anger that his memory had perished from the earth, that the children she had borne in pain were gone, that he sat on rotting refuse full of worms while she wandered from house to house like a servant, and that he should say a word against the Lord and die, he heard those bitter words with his usual meekness and answered her in great grief: why have you spoken so, like one of the foolish women? Having received so many good gifts from the hands of God, shall we not endure the calamities too? Only for a moment, when his three friends came to him — Eliphaz king of the Themanites, Baldad the tyrant of the Sauchaeans and Saphar king of the Minaeans, who kept him company seven days in silence — was his courage shaken, and at once his deep faith recovered it.

After seven years' endurance of that more than human trial God rewarded him and gave him back all the goods he had lost and the people he loved. He lived a hundred and forty years after it, and died in peace at the age of two hundred and forty, about the year 1650 before Christ, as a pattern of patience and perseverance; and his contests, the page says, are described at length in the book of the Old Testament that bears his name.

The Serbian church keeps him on this same menologion day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him a genealogy, a country, two parents, a second name and a span of years, almost none of which the other two calendars carry. He was a descendant of Esau, it says, and lived in Arabia about two thousand years before Christ. His father's name was Zaret and his mother's Vosora, and his own full name was Jovav. He was an honourable man and God-fearing and very rich.

**In the seventy-ninth year of his life** God let the heavy trials come upon him through Satan, as the book says it is all described in detail in the Book of Job. In one day he lost all his huge estate and his sons and his daughters. After that a grievous sickness fell on him, which covered his whole body with sores from the crown of his head to the soles of his feet, and he lay on a dunghill outside the city and wiped the pus from his sores with a piece of broken pot.

But Job did not murmur against God; he bore all the torments patiently to the end. So God gave him back his health and gave him much greater wealth than he had before, and there were born to him again seven sons and three daughters, as many as he had had before. **He lived two hundred and forty-eight years in all**, the Prologue says, glorifying and praising God the whole of that time. He is held to be the pattern of the patient bearing of every affliction God sends on us, and a figure beforehand of the suffering Lord Jesus.

**No year was taken from this page, and the two figures it gives are both recorded here rather than stored.** „Око две хиљаде година пре Христа“ — about two thousand years before Christ — is a round approximation of when he lived and not a death, and the Greek page this folder's `death` was read from already carries two flourishing dates that disagree with each other, about 1900 BC and, by others, 1400 BC, which that folder deliberately leaves in prose instead of storing. A third round approximation is not a bound, and putting it into `dates` would make the folder vaguer than its own sources already allow. The span of life is a second disagreement and is left standing the same way: this book gives **two hundred and forty-eight** years where the Greek page gives two hundred and forty.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day and gives him two lives. The shorter puts his birth in the twentieth century before Christ, in Arabia; the longer sets him two thousand to fifteen hundred years before the Nativity, in northern Arabia, in the country of Ausitis, in the land of Uz, and says that his life and his sufferings are written in the Bible, in the Book of Job. Both report the opinion that he was a nephew of «Авраам», a son of «Авраам»'s brother Нахор, and the longer marks it as an opinion and nothing more.

Both tell the same story. He was God-fearing and devout, turning from evil not in his deeds only but in his thoughts, and the Lord blessed him with great herds and great property. His seven sons and three daughters were at peace with one another and ate together in turn at each of their houses, and every seventh day their father offered sacrifice for them, saying: «Может быть, кто из них согрешил или похулил Бога в сердце своем» — perhaps one of them has sinned, or blasphemed God in his heart. For his justice and his honesty he was held in great honour by his townsmen.

When Satan appeared among the angels before the throne of God, he answered that this man feared God only because God guarded him; and the Lord gave all that was his into Satan's hands, forbidding him to touch the man himself. His goods went, and then his children; and he said: «Господь дал, Господь и взял. Да будет Имя Господне благословенно!» Satan was then allowed his body but not his soul, and struck him with leprosy from head to foot, so that he had to leave the company of men and sat outside the town on a heap of ashes, scraping his sores with a shard. His friends abandoned him; his wife, who went from house to house for her bread, thought he was being punished for hidden sins, and at last told him to curse God and die. He answered her: «Ты говоришь, как одна из безумных. Неужели доброе мы будем принимать от Бога, злого не будем принимать?»

Three friends came from far off to share his grief and spent it urging an innocent man to repent; he answered that he suffered not for sins but under a divine will no man can see through. Then God showed himself in a whirlwind and reproved him for trying to reach the secrets of the creation with his reason, and he repented with his whole heart — «я ничтожен, отрекаюсь и раскаиваюсь в прахе и пепле». The Lord told the three to ask him to offer sacrifice for them, saying that only his face would be accepted; he prayed for them, and his health and twice his former wealth were given back to him, with seven sons and three daughters in place of the dead. He lived a hundred and forty years more — two hundred and forty-eight in all, the longer life says — and saw his seed to the fourth generation.

That life closes by setting him as a figure of Christ, who came down to earth, suffered for the saving of men and was then glorified in his Resurrection; it quotes his own words in his leprosy, that he knows his Redeemer lives and that he will see God in his own flesh, and it ends with a sentence of «Иоанн Златоуст»: that there is no human misfortune this man did not bear, harder than any adamant, who met at once hunger and poverty and sickness and the loss of his children and the loss of his wealth, and after them his wife's cunning, his friends' insults and his servants' attacks, and in all of it proved tougher than any stone — and that before the Law and before Grace.
