# Glyceria of Novgorod

Glyceria of Novgorod lived in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, and she was the daughter of Panteleimon, a municipal officer of Novgorod. That is all saint.gr says of her life; she fell asleep in peace in the year 1522. The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day as the second-century martyr [Glyceria of Traianopolis](/saints/glyceria-of-traianopolis), who is another woman altogether and has a page of her own.

Her incorrupt relics, the page continues, were found on 14 July 1572 — the date is from the second Chronicle of Novgorod — near the stone church of Saints Florus and Laurus, and were buried there by Leonid, archbishop of Novgorod. During that burial of her holy relic, healings of the sick and wonders were worked. After the revolution of 1917 there is no information about what became of the relic.

The Russian church calendar keeps her on the same menologion day, 13 мая ст. ст., with the same year, and it gives her the father and the street the Greek page did not. She was the daughter of Пантелеймон, the elder of the Легощая street in Great Novgorod, and her two Russian pages agree that the scantness of what is known of her is itself a sign of how hidden her life and her labour were. The relics were found incorrupt behind the church of the holy martyrs Флор and Лавр, the Second Novgorod Chronicle saying «обретоша гроб верх земли и обретоша в гробе тело цало а не все»; an old woman called Настасия told the archbishop Леонид that she remembered the girl being carried to burial some fifty years before. The archbishop laid the relics solemnly in that church, and on the same day the four-year-old son of the clerk Богдан Суворов was healed at her coffin, and other healings followed; those healings were the ground of her canonisation.

The two pages do not agree on the year of the finding. The shorter puts it on 14 июля 1572; the fuller prints 14 июля 1522 and then quotes the chronicle under 1572 two sentences later, which makes its own figure a slip. Neither year is her repose, which the calendar and this folder both put at about 1522, and nothing here was changed. The fuller page adds that she is painted on an old icon of the wonderworkers of Novgorod, in the sixth of six rows of the saints of that land beneath a Sophia, the Wisdom of God; and that a chapel in her honour was built in the middle of the nineteenth century in the Тихвинская church of the Симонов monastery at Moscow. It ends by reading her as one of the wise virgins of the parable, who gathered oil for her lamp.
