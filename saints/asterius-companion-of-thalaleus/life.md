# Asterius, companion of Thalaleus

Alexander and Asterius were two of the soldiers set to torture the physician-martyr Thalaleus at Anazarbus in Cilicia, and saint.gr's whole account of them is the sentence in which they change sides: they were ordered to pierce Thalaleus's ankles, they came through him to believe in Christ, they confessed him, and they were beheaded. Thalaleus's own page tells the same thing from his side — that his patience under that horrible torture was so great that two of the soldiers who were torturing him, by name Alexander and Asterius, believed.

The page gives the two of them no country, no years and no burial place, and sets neither of them in a family. The governor who gave the order is Tiberian, and the emperor whose years Thalaleus's page names is Numerian, 283 to 284; but nothing on their own page repeats either date, so no years are recorded for them here.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 20 May inside the entry it heads for [Thalaleus](/saints/thalaleus-the-physician), and tells the whole of his story, which no other book read for him tells at all. When Thalaleus had confessed Christ before the judge, the judge ordered two executioners, Alexander and Asterius, to bore through the martyr’s knees with a drill, draw a rope through the drilled bones and hang him from a tree. But it was as though the unseen power of God took their sight: instead of Thalaleus they bored a plank, and hung the plank from the tree.

When the torturer learned of it he thought they had done it on purpose, and ordered them flogged. And in the middle of the flogging Alexander and Asterius cried out: as the Lord lives, from this hour we too are Christians, we believe in Christ, and we suffer for Him. Hearing it, the torturer had them cut down with the sword. The Prologue gives the year 284 for Thalaleus, who outlived them both and came to his own end after several further tortures, and it gives these two no year, so none is set down here. The heading names neither of them; the body names him second of the two.
