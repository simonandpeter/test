# Basil of Rostov

saint.gr says Basil was born on the seventh of December 1209, the son of Constantine Vsevolodovich, great prince of Rostov, and of the princess Agatha-Anna Mstislavovna. After his father's death his mother entered a monastery and became a nun, and fell asleep there on the twenty-fourth of January 1221. When in 1216 his father became great prince of Vladimir, he laid the duties of governing Rostov on the young prince Basil, who took them up with feelings of justice and love towards the people of Rostov; and when his father died, on the second of February 1218, his uncle St George, prince of Vladimir, became the boy's guide and counsellor.

In 1219 he took part in the battle the men of Vladimir and Suzdal gave against the Bulgars, and on the sixteenth of June 1223 in the battle against the Mongols on the river Kalka, where the Russians were beaten; Basil and his soldiers came back to Rostov. A few years later he married Maria, daughter of St [Michael of Chernigov](/saints/michael-of-chernigov), and had his first son, Boris. But the clouds, the page says, began to cover the sky of Russia. By 1236 the Tatars had taken a great part of the Russian land, and when their Khan offered St George peace, he answered: a glorious battle is better than a dishonourable peace.

On the third of February 1238 the Tatars encircled the city of Vladimir. The inhabitants took refuge in the protection of the Most Holy Theotokos and the bishop Mitrophanes strengthened the fighters, but the city fell on the seventh, and Mitrophanes died with thousands of women and children. On the fourth of March 1238 the Russians, led by the saints Basil and George, gave the final battle. The Tatars took Basil, and he told them, as the martyrs of the ancient Church did: you cannot take my faith in Christ from me. After that confession the barbarians hanged him in the forest of Shernsk, and the bishop Cyril reverently gathered the holy relics and buried them in the cathedral church of Rostov.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, and its calendar gives the family and the war at much greater length than the Greek notice does. He belonged to the Суздальские Мономашичи: his great grandfather Юрий Долгорукий, his grandfather the great prince Всеволод III Большое Гнездо, who died in 1212 and was the heir and continuer of his brother Андрей Боголюбский. He was the eldest of the nestlings of the Big Nest, born at Rostov where his father ruled, and Rostov was given him as an appanage of his own at eight years old; he was not yet ten when his father died, and his uncle Юрий, great prince of Vladimir, became his guide for the twenty years that followed.

The chroniclers praise his beauty, his bright and lordly look, his courage at the hunt, his liberality, his mind and his deep reading, and his mildness with the boyars: whoever served him and ate his bread and drank his cup, they say, could be no other prince's man. He married Мария, daughter of [Michael of Chernigov](/saints/michael-of-chernigov), in 1227 or 1228, and his eldest son Борис was born in 1231. Then the Tatars took the Bulgar land, Ryazan fell in December 1237 and Moscow after it, and on 3 February 1238 their army stood before Vladimir; on the seventh the city fell, and the Dormition cathedral was heaped with brushwood and burned with the bishop Митрофан and the great prince's whole household inside it.

On 4 March 1238 came the deciding battle on the river Сить. The Tatars encircled the Russian army by a sudden movement, few of the Russian soldiers came out of it alive, and the great prince was cut down. Василько was brought wounded to Batu's camp and pressed to follow the Nogai custom, to be at their will and to fight for them, and he refused the thought of treason to his country and his faith: «Никак не отведете меня от христианской веры». The chronicle says only «много мучивше его, смерти предаша, повергоша его в лесу Шернском», where the Greek page says he was hanged. He was not yet thirty.

A priest's son found the body in the Шернский forest and brought it to Rostov, where his wife, his children, the bishop Кирилл and the whole people met it with bitter weeping and buried him under the vaults of the cathedral church, beside the uncle who had died in the same battle; the people saw a mark of God's own mercy in the two being laid side by side. The Russian calendar is explicit that the Church honours the two of them as страстотерпцы, which is why passion bearer is added to this folder's types, and it names the Лаврентьевская летопись of 1377 as the fullest account of the two princes. Its life also carries a second day of his, 23 мая, marked as a Rostov commemoration, which the 23 May day page has not been read against and which is recorded here as a note and not as a feast.
