# Basil of Rostov

saint.gr says Basil was born on the seventh of December 1209, the son of Constantine Vsevolodovich, great prince of Rostov, and of the princess Agatha-Anna Mstislavovna. After his father's death his mother entered a monastery and became a nun, and fell asleep there on the twenty-fourth of January 1221. When in 1216 his father became great prince of Vladimir, he laid the duties of governing Rostov on the young prince Basil, who took them up with feelings of justice and love towards the people of Rostov; and when his father died, on the second of February 1218, his uncle St George, prince of Vladimir, became the boy's guide and counsellor.

In 1219 he took part in the battle the men of Vladimir and Suzdal gave against the Bulgars, and on the sixteenth of June 1223 in the battle against the Mongols on the river Kalka, where the Russians were beaten; Basil and his soldiers came back to Rostov. A few years later he married Maria, daughter of St [Michael of Chernigov](/saints/michael-of-chernigov), and had his first son, Boris. But the clouds, the page says, began to cover the sky of Russia. By 1236 the Tatars had taken a great part of the Russian land, and when their Khan offered St George peace, he answered: a glorious battle is better than a dishonourable peace.

On the third of February 1238 the Tatars encircled the city of Vladimir. The inhabitants took refuge in the protection of the Most Holy Theotokos and the bishop Mitrophanes strengthened the fighters, but the city fell on the seventh, and Mitrophanes died with thousands of women and children. On the fourth of March 1238 the Russians, led by the saints Basil and George, gave the final battle. The Tatars took Basil, and he told them, as the martyrs of the ancient Church did: you cannot take my faith in Christ from me. After that confession the barbarians hanged him in the forest of Shernsk, and the bishop Cyril reverently gathered the holy relics and buried them in the cathedral church of Rostov.
