# Maximus, companion of Terentius

Maximus stood with the forty who would not sacrifice when the edict of Decius came to Africa and the governor Fortunatianus called the people together and set the instruments of torture before them. When [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa) had answered for them all about the power of Christ crucified, the governor picked out four — Terentius, [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius), Maximus and [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius) — and had them shut in the innermost prison, keeping them for a second questioning while the rest were judged.

They would not yield at it. Iron was put on their necks, their hands and feet were bound, iron nails were strewn on the ground under them, and no Christian was let in to feed them. At midnight the prison filled with light; the Angel of the Lord stood over them, called them by their four names, broke their chains at a touch, and told them to rest and take the food Christ had sent them, and a table stood there covered with it.

The governor's last device was to loose serpents, asps and vipers into the cell. The guards who looked in through the roof saw the martyrs sitting and the angel standing and the creatures held off; and when the enchanters came in the morning to charm them out, the creatures fell on the enchanters and killed them instead. Fortunatianus, furious at finding the four unharmed, sentenced them to be beheaded, and they went to their death singing.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and enumerates it. saint.gr's line for 10 Απριλίου is «Άγιοι Αφρικανός, Θεόδωρος, Μάξιμος, Πομπήιος, Τερέντιος και άλλοι τεσσαράκοντα Μάρτυρες», and its page dates the martyrdom and names the authorities: «μαρτύρησαν το έτος 250 μ.Χ., επί αυτοκράτορα Δεκίου (249-251 μ.Χ.) και ηγεμόνος Φουρτουνιανού, στην Καρθαγένη της Αφρικής» — in the year 250, under the emperor Decius and the governor Fortunianus, at Carthage in Africa. The torments it tells are one set of torments for them all: they were beaten until their inward parts could be seen, their hips were pierced with red-hot nails and vinegar and salt were thrown on the wounds, they prayed and pulled down the wooden images, the altars and the temples of the idolaters, and at the last they were beheaded.

The page then prints a name and a gloss for each of the rest, and the names are largely those of the Greek past: Alexander, Anaxarchus, Anaximenes, Aristides, Demaratus, Democles, Demosthenes, Dionysius, Epaminondas, Eteocles, Zeno, Elias, Heracles, Isaiah, Hephaestion, Themistocles, Theophrastus, Theseus, Thomas, Isocrates, Luke, Macarius, Miltiades, Mnesarchus, Xenophon, Homer, Parmenion, Pelopidas, Pericles, Pindar, Polybius, Polynicus, Prometheus, Sophocles, Socrates, Timothy, Titus, Philopoemen, Phocion and Chronius. That list holds forty names, while the prose above it says «μαζί με άλλους τριάντα εννέα Χριστιανούς», another thirty-nine, and the day's line says forty others; the page reconciles none of the three counts. Δημᾶς, whom the Romanian calendar keeps in this company, is not in saint.gr's list at all.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same day and names him third of the four its heading enumerates, [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa), [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius), Maximus and [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius), with another thirty-six it does not name; and its account makes those four the men who were kept alive when the company was divided. Under Decius the governor of Africa ordered the whole people to sacrifice, and threatened every obstinate man with fearful torture; many fell away, but these forty stood unshaken and were given over to torment. The thirty-six the governor beheaded, after scourging them, scraping them and sprinkling salt on their open wounds; these four he threw into prison in heavy irons at the neck, the hands and the feet.

An angel of God appeared to them there, touched the irons so that they fell off, and set them an abundant table and fed them. They were brought out and tortured and shut up again; and when the governor's sorcerers gathered snakes and scorpions and shut them into the same room, the creatures huddled into a corner and lay three days without touching the servants of God, and on the third day, when the prison was opened, rushed at the sorcerers and bit them. At the last the four were sentenced to death, and went to the beheading singing psalms and praising God, who had counted them worthy of a martyr's death, in the year 250 — the year this folder already holds.
