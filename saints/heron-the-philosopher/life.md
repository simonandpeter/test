# Heron the Philosopher

Heron was a Christian philosopher. That is nearly the whole of what the
Ohrid Prologue records of him: Gregory the Theologian mentions him in his
writings, and he reposed in peace.

The Prologue gives neither dates nor a place, and neither does anyone else:
the Russian church calendar’s page says the time and place of his life are
unknown, that the Byzantine verse synaxaria keep Heron the Philosopher with
a couplet on 11 August (a Paris manuscript of 1301, for one) and the printed
Greek menaia and Nikodemos’s Synaxaristes on 10 August, and that these
sources say only that he scattered the errors of the pagan sages and died in
peace.

Delehaye supposed him to be the Heron to whom Gregory the Theologian’s
twenty-fifth oration is addressed — an Alexandrian, a Cynic philosopher of
ascetic life who became a Christian and refuted the errors of the pagans,
with martyrs for Christ in his family, who suffered for the Orthodox faith
from the Arians under the emperor Valens and the Arian patriarch Lucius
(375–378), contending for the truth, in Gregory’s words, by teaching,
exhorting, rousing, reproving and shaming commoners and rulers, in private
and in public, at every time and place, and was scourged and banished to the
desert.

But modern scholars follow Jerome, who took that Heron to be the same man as
Maximus the Cynic, who tried in 380 to displace Gregory from the see of
Constantinople — in which case the veneration of Heron-Maximus, and his
identification with the philosopher of the synaxaria, are both unlikely; the
page says so, and notes that he is not in the present Russian menologion.
The Serbian calendar keeps his memory on 10 August (Julian), in second place
after Lawrence the Archdeacon and Sixtus of Rome.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 August, and says the same two things this folder already says, with one addition. saint.gr's notice is: «Ο Άγιος Ήρων απεβίωσε ειρηνικά» — the holy Heron departed in peace — and then the identification, offered as a guess and not as a fact: perhaps he is the philosopher Heron to whom [Gregory the Theologian](/saints/gregory-the-theologian) addresses a whole oration. The page quotes that oration's opening, «Τὸν φιλόσοφον ἐπαινέσομαι, καὶ εἰ κάμνω τῷ σώματι· φιλόσοφον γάρ· ἐπαινέσομαι δὲ καὶ λίαν εἰκότως. Ὁ μὲν γὰρ φιλόσοφος, ἐγὼ δὲ σοφίας θεραπευτής» — I shall praise the philosopher, even though I am sick in body, for he is a philosopher; and I shall praise him with very good reason, for he is a philosopher while I am a servant of wisdom.

The page adds that some menologia set beside his memory that of a certain martyr Heraïs, and it gives no year, no country and no hymn.
