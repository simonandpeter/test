# Heron the Philosopher

Heron was a Christian philosopher. That is nearly the whole of what the
Ohrid Prologue records of him: Gregory the Theologian mentions him in his
writings, and he reposed in peace.

The Prologue gives neither dates nor a place, and neither does anyone else:
the Russian church calendar’s page says the time and place of his life are
unknown, that the Byzantine verse synaxaria keep Heron the Philosopher with
a couplet on 11 August (a Paris manuscript of 1301, for one) and the printed
Greek menaia and Nikodemos’s Synaxaristes on 10 August, and that these
sources say only that he scattered the errors of the pagan sages and died in
peace. Delehaye supposed him to be the Heron to whom Gregory the
Theologian’s twenty-fifth oration is addressed — an Alexandrian, a Cynic
philosopher of ascetic life who became a Christian and refuted the errors of
the pagans, with martyrs for Christ in his family, who suffered for the
Orthodox faith from the Arians under the emperor Valens and the Arian
patriarch Lucius (375–378), contending for the truth, in Gregory’s words, by
teaching, exhorting, rousing, reproving and shaming commoners and rulers, in
private and in public, at every time and place, and was scourged and
banished to the desert. But modern scholars follow Jerome, who took that
Heron to be the same man as Maximus the Cynic, who tried in 380 to displace
Gregory from the see of Constantinople — in which case the veneration of
Heron-Maximus, and his identification with the philosopher of the synaxaria,
are both unlikely; the page says so, and notes that he is not in the present
Russian menologion. The Serbian calendar keeps his memory on 10 August
(Julian), in second place after Lawrence the Archdeacon and Sixtus of Rome.
