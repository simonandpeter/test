# Gobron of Kakheti

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 17 November, «Мч. Гоброна, во св. Крещении Михаила, и с ним 133-х воинов (914) (Груз.).», and marks the line Georgian. He was Michael at his baptism, and came of a noble princely family; from his youth he was marked out by bravery and fearlessness, for which he was nicknamed Gobron, which the page says means in Arabic the manly and the brave.

In 914 the Arab commander Abdul-Qasim, having laid Armenia waste, took Tbilisi and laid siege to the fortress of Kveli, which Gobron and his soldiers were holding. After twenty-eight days of siege the Arabs broke the truce that had been concluded, forced their way into the fortress and took its steadfast defenders captive with Gobron at their head.

The Georgian king Adarnase II, who reigned from 881 to 923, ransomed many of the prisoners, but to the ransom of Gobron the Arabs would not agree. The emir offered him Islam and promised him freedom and riches, and was flatly refused. Then the 133 soldiers were destroyed before his eyes, having also refused to fall away from the faith in Christ. Dipping his finger in the blood of the martyrs, Gobron drew a cross on his forehead, and giving thanks to the Lord for the martyr’s crown sent down to him, took death meekly and without bitterness by the severing of his head on 17 November 914.

The author of the Martyrdom of Michael (Gobron), written between 914 and 918, the bishop Stephen of Tbeti, relates that the body of the saint and the bodies of his 133 soldiers were buried in a common pit, and that «Чудный свет почти каждую ночь освещал могилу святых мучеников, множество больных, приходивших на могилу святых, получали здравие» — a wondrous light lit the grave of the holy martyrs almost every night, and a multitude of the sick who came to it received health. The Georgian Church numbered him and his soldiers among the saints and set their memory on the day of the martyrdom.

The line and the life both count his soldiers and name none of them, so the company is one entry here and not a hundred and thirty-three folders, and it stands inside his. The page names Tbilisi, the fortress of Kveli and the pit, and gives no coordinate for any of them, so this folder carries no place.
