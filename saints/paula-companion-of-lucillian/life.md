# Paula, companion of Lucillian

Paula was born of believing parents, and from her youth her heart was kindled with the love of Christ; she kept her virginity for the deathless Bridegroom and strove to be found worthy of the heavenly chamber. Left an orphan with sufficient means of her own, she went round the prisons and visited those bound and suffering for Christ, buying the guards with gold so as to get in to the saints. Doxologia prints her life and the life of [Lucillian](/saints/lucillian) and the four young men with him as one text, on both their pages.

She served the servants of Christ, giving them all they needed out of her own goods: food and drink to those worn with hunger and thirst, clothing to the naked. She washed and wiped the martyrs' wounded bodies and their festering sores, laid healing plasters on them and wrapped them in clean linen; and kissing the wounds they had taken for Christ she begged them with tears to pray to God for her, that He would not deprive her of His mercy.

She stood by at their torments, praying secretly that God would strengthen His servants and give them patience and help to bear it to the end, for the glory of His holy name; and when the crowd had scattered she went to the place where they had been tormented, gathered up the blood they had shed on the ground, and kept it by her as a holy thing. When the old man and the four young men were taken to Byzantium to die, she followed and served them; and after the young men were beheaded she gathered their honoured bodies and buried them reverently.

Then she returned to Nicomedia and went on serving the martyrs with love, until the pagans knew her for a Christian and brought her to judgement before the same Silvanus. There, after many flattering promises and many threats, the count saw her unmoved and had her beaten long and without pity with sticks and rods; and when her body, not her spirit, gave way under the many wounds, an angel of the Lord appeared to her and healed her. Having her health again she was bolder and braver in her torments, and they struck her hard across the mouth for the mocking words she gave the torturer.

They threw her into prison, and brought her out again for examination and cast her into a fiercely burning furnace, and she came out of it unharmed: with her too, as with the martyrs before her, the power of God cooled the strength of the fire, so that the burning flame should not touch the bride of Christ. At last the torturer condemned her to death and sent her to Byzantium to take her punishment where Lucillian and the young men had ended.

When she came to the place where Lucillian suffered death on the cross, she gave thanks to God, who had counted her worthy of the martyr's crown and of the company of the saints; and having prayed earnestly to the Lord she laid her virgin head gladly under the sword. Being beheaded, she passed from things below to things above and entered the heavenly chamber. The life names Aurelian and Silvanus and prints no year, so none is recorded here; and the site's Tropar tab for her page is a recording, with no hymn text, so this folder carries none.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day, 3 June, and saint.gr dates them: «(; - 270)», in the years of the emperor Aurelian. Its account opens on the Lord's word that what is impossible for men is possible for God — for who, it asks, would have expected a man who spent almost his whole life inside idolatry, and was even a priest of it, to become a Christian? When Lucillian first heard Christian preaching in his native Nicomedia, divine grace made a real earthquake inside him: his idolatrous convictions, so deeply rooted in his soul, came down like towers of paper, his old eyes were opened, and with a young man's liveliness he proclaimed his faith in Christ and tried to bring other souls to him by preaching.

He was denounced for it to the count Livanios; he confessed Christ before him with courage; and the count, pressed by the pagan priests, who reckoned Lucillian a deserter from their religion, ordered him tortured. Then they threw him into a fire to be burned, and a heavy rain put the fire out; so they sent him to Byzantium, where he was counted worthy to bear witness by death on a cross. In the prison Lucillian had found four children, Claudius, Hypatius, Paul and Dionysius, imprisoned for the same cause, who were afterwards beheaded; and after the saint's death the virgin Paula took his holy relics and buried them, for which she too was seized, was cruelly tortured and at the last beheaded.

Of her the page says what doxologia says, that she was a virgin, that she buried Lucillian's relics and was taken for it; and its verse on her says that Paula, declaring with Paul that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy of the glory to come, is put to the sword.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the Julian 3 June, the menologion day both the other calendars keep, and its heading enumerates all six: [Lucillian](/saints/lucillian), [Claudius](/saints/claudius-companion-of-lucillian), [Hypatius](/saints/hypatius-companion-of-lucillian), [Paul](/saints/paul-companion-of-lucillian), [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-lucillian) and the virgin Paula. Its account is the shortest of the three, and it is the only one that brackets the suffering in years: it happened, the entry closes, in the time of the emperor Aurelian, between 270 and 275. The Greek page gave the reign and one year inside it, and the Romanian gave none at all, so the interval this folder records has been widened to the bracket this book prints rather than narrowed to the year the other did.

She is the sixth name in the heading, which calls her what both the earlier books call her, the virgin Paula. This book tells her part in two sentences and shifts the weight of both. She took the martyrs’ bodies openly, it says, and buried them honourably, and it was for that she was accused; and after her torments, beheaded, she received from the Lord two crowns, the crown of virginity and the crown of martyrdom. The long Romanian account of her service in the prisons, the gold she paid the guards, the blood she gathered from the ground, the angel who healed her and the furnace she came out of unharmed is not here, and neither is the count who sentenced her.
