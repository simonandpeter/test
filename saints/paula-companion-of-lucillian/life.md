# Paula, companion of Lucillian

Paula was born of believing parents, and from her youth her heart was kindled with the love of Christ; she kept her virginity for the deathless Bridegroom and strove to be found worthy of the heavenly chamber. Left an orphan with sufficient means of her own, she went round the prisons and visited those bound and suffering for Christ, buying the guards with gold so as to get in to the saints. Doxologia prints her life and the life of [Lucillian](/saints/lucillian) and the four young men with him as one text, on both their pages.

She served the servants of Christ, giving them all they needed out of her own goods: food and drink to those worn with hunger and thirst, clothing to the naked. She washed and wiped the martyrs' wounded bodies and their festering sores, laid healing plasters on them and wrapped them in clean linen; and kissing the wounds they had taken for Christ she begged them with tears to pray to God for her, that He would not deprive her of His mercy.

She stood by at their torments, praying secretly that God would strengthen His servants and give them patience and help to bear it to the end, for the glory of His holy name; and when the crowd had scattered she went to the place where they had been tormented, gathered up the blood they had shed on the ground, and kept it by her as a holy thing. When the old man and the four young men were taken to Byzantium to die, she followed and served them; and after the young men were beheaded she gathered their honoured bodies and buried them reverently.

Then she returned to Nicomedia and went on serving the martyrs with love, until the pagans knew her for a Christian and brought her to judgement before the same Silvanus. There, after many flattering promises and many threats, the count saw her unmoved and had her beaten long and without pity with sticks and rods; and when her body, not her spirit, gave way under the many wounds, an angel of the Lord appeared to her and healed her. Having her health again she was bolder and braver in her torments, and they struck her hard across the mouth for the mocking words she gave the torturer.

They threw her into prison, and brought her out again for examination and cast her into a fiercely burning furnace, and she came out of it unharmed: with her too, as with the martyrs before her, the power of God cooled the strength of the fire, so that the burning flame should not touch the bride of Christ. At last the torturer condemned her to death and sent her to Byzantium to take her punishment where Lucillian and the young men had ended.

When she came to the place where Lucillian suffered death on the cross, she gave thanks to God, who had counted her worthy of the martyr's crown and of the company of the saints; and having prayed earnestly to the Lord she laid her virgin head gladly under the sword. Being beheaded, she passed from things below to things above and entered the heavenly chamber. The life names Aurelian and Silvanus and prints no year, so none is recorded here; and the site's Tropar tab for her page is a recording, with no hymn text, so this folder carries none.
