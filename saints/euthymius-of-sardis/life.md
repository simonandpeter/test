# Euthymius of Sardis

Euthymius lived in the days of the emperors Constantine and Irene. He shone first as a star in the monastic life, and was then made a hierarch; and at the Seventh Council at Nicaea he crushed the heretics with power. When Nikephoros came up to the imperial throne and began to live lawlessly, the saint rebuked him, and the emperor banished him to the West together with other Orthodox bishops; and from then until his suffering as a confessor, for twenty-nine years, the blessed one did not see his metropolis again.

When the emperors changed and Leo, who bore the name of a beast, came to the throne, the saint was called back from banishment, and the emperor asked him whether he venerated the holy icons; and the saint, being full of zeal, laid the emperor under anathema. At this the emperor in his anger banished him again, to Ason. After Leo had been killed the new emperor called him back from Ason and required him to renounce the holy icons.

And Euthymius, in his zeal for Christ, said with a loud voice: «If anyone does not venerate our Lord Jesus Christ painted in an icon, let him be anathema». For this the saint was beaten, banished and shut in prison; and having been cruelly beaten with ox sinews he lived a little longer and gave up his soul into the hands of the Lord.

doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mucenic Eftimie, Episcopul Sardelor» and gives no year for his birth or his death; the page the site keeps for his troparion is titled for «Sfântul Ierarh Eftimie Mărturisitorul, Episcopul Sardei», and the hymn it prints is the common of a hierarch, with his name set in it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and prints for a life one long quotation from S. Eustratiades's Hagiology. He flourished under Constantine and Irene, it says, whose reign it dates 780 to 797; he was born in Lykaonia, studied at Alexandria, was tonsured a monk, and for his virtue and learning was raised to the throne of Sardis. He took part in the Seventh Ecumenical Council at Nicaea against the iconoclasts in 787, and signed the right belief of the Church there. The emperors trusted him with public missions; but under Nikephoros I an official at Sardis denounced him for tonsuring a girl that man wanted to marry, and he was exiled to the island of Pattalara. He returned in 814, was exiled again to Assos, and was recalled by Michael the Stammerer — whom he thundered at to his face: let anyone be anathema who does not venerate Christ circumscribed in an icon.

The Охридски пролог keeps him on 26. децембар ст. ст., „Свети Јевтимије Исповедник, епископ сардијски.“, and gives him three sentences. He was present at the Seventh Ecumenical Council. About thirty years he spent in exile for the honouring of the icons. Under the iconoclast emperor Theophilus he was beaten with ox sinews, and in that beating he ended as a martyr in the year 840, and received the crown of glory in heaven — the same year this folder already holds, read there off the Greek calendar.
