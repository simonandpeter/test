# Magdalena of Mălainița

Her father, Milenko Ilić, was a Serb, and her mother, Milca, a Romanian of the old Cătălinici family that had come from Transylvania. At her baptism, on 3/16 December, she was given the name Măndălina — Magdalena — which the page calls a sign of her calling to a life of humility and service. From her earliest childhood she showed love for prayer and for the Church, and although girls in those times were not encouraged to study, she learned to read, receiving from God the gift of wisdom and of an unshakeable faith.

At nineteen, on the day of the Meeting of the Lord, 2 February 1914, she was married into the neighbouring Romanian village of Mălainița. But the Lord’s will was otherwise: her husband Miloș fell in the First World War and left her a widow with a small child. She strengthened her faith then, the page says, and took to repeating the words «Vede Dumnezeu» — God sees — which became the seal of her soul.

During the Bulgarian occupation, from 1915 to 1918, the priests were arrested and killed and the church at Șciubic was left without pastors; and by the grace of Christ she kept the chanter’s stall of that church and kept the flame of the faith alight. In a time of spiritual darkness, when many turned to sorceresses because they could not understand the language of the services, she knew both Romanian and Serbian well and was a bridge between the two peoples and a comfort to the faithful. Through frost, storm and heat she walked five kilometres to the church to read and sing at the services, and the bishops, seeing her humility and her zeal, gave her leave to enter the altar, which was unusual for a woman in those days.

Living as a nun in the world, she fasted always, prayed at night and guided people to the law of God. Many did not know which were the feasts or the fasts, and she would tell them gently, saying each time, «Vede Dumnezeu». When she watched the sheep she recited from the holy books, and the place where she prayed was afterwards chosen for the building of a monastery. The page says she dreamed that she flew to Jerusalem, and that God fulfilled her wish and took her in spirit to the tomb of the Saviour.

On 15 October 1962 — 2 October by the old style — with a candle in her hand and crossing herself to the last moment, she passed to the Lord, and her face grew bright as though she had seen Christ coming for her soul. The funeral was served in the cathedral at Negotin with many priests and monks, and the people said of her that a saint had gone and that none like her had been born. Her relics are today in the church of the monastery of Mălainița, honoured by Romanian and Serbian faithful alike.

She was canonised by the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church in its session of 1 July 2025, under the title «Sfânta Magdalena de la Mălainița», with her commemoration on 15 October. The Tropar tab of her page is a video recording — her troparion sung by the Tronos psaltic group on 6 February 2026, at the general proclamation of the canonisation of sixteen Romanian women saints — and prints no text, so no hymn is recorded here.
