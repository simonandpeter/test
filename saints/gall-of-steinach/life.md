# Gall of Steinach

saint.gr's calendar for 16 October keeps «Άγιος Γάλλος», printing the Latin form beside the Greek, and makes him the best known of the disciples of Columbanus, whom it sends the reader to find on 23 November. He was born a little after 550 in Ireland, which was then, the page says, the third great centre of the Christian world after Rome and Constantinople; his parents, rich and devout aristocrats, sent him to study at the famous monastery of Bangor, where the great elders [Comgall](/saints/comgall-of-bangor) and Columbanus were then living. When Columbanus left on mission, Gall was one of the twelve monks who followed him.

In Gaul he shared both his labours for the Gospel and his persecutions. Together they went to Switzerland, where the devout priest Willemaros of Arbon, near Lake Constance, first gave them shelter; then they made cells in a deserted place in the valley of the Steinach and settled there, and turned many of the pagans of the district to the Christian faith. Once, after a sermon of theirs, they seized the bronze statues of the false gods, broke them and threw them into the lake; the more fanatical of the idolaters hunted the monks down and killed two of them.

When Columbanus left for Italy his faithful disciple wished to follow him, but a sudden and serious illness, which God permitted, forced Gall to stay in Switzerland and to carry on the mission there. He learned the language of the natives of Constantia and managed to baptise thousands of souls; and by his inspired preaching, his holy example and his striking wonders he did a great evangelical work, for which, the page says, he is justly reckoned the apostle of Switzerland.

His best-known wonder is the healing of the possessed daughter of the local ruler Gunzo, who afterwards became a nun at the monastery of Saint Peter of Metz, refusing marriage with a prince of Austrasia. After that the ruler and the Christians of Constantia begged the saint insistently to become their bishop; but out of modesty and humility he refused, and put forward for the office his virtuous disciple, the deacon John, at whose episcopal ordination he delivered a most spiritual address. He fell asleep in 646, and the humble little cells of the Steinach valley grew in time into the famous monastery of Saint Gall, which survived into the nineteenth century and brought forth great figures of letters and of piety.
