# Agathodorus, servant of Carpus and Papylas

Agathodorus was the servant of the martyrs [Carpus](/saints/carpus-of-thyatira), bishop of Thyatira, and [Papylas](/saints/papylas-the-deacon) his deacon. Under the pagan emperor Decius they were slandered for refusing the idols and brought before the proconsul Valerie; and when Valerie had given their property to their accusers, he tied the two of them to horses and had them driven to Sardis, so that they covered the road from Thyatira in a single day and were dragged when the horses outran them. Agathodorus followed behind and suffered with his masters.

At Sardis they took no rest, but spent the greater part of the night in prayer and in divine songs; and when they had slept a little, angels of God comforted them in a vision and strengthened them to bear what was coming. Waking, they told one another what they had seen, rejoiced greatly and thanked God for comforting them in their distress, and were kindled the more with the desire to suffer for Christ.

Valerie came to Sardis hoping that the road had broken them, and found their faces brighter than before, their spirits stronger and their hearts bolder. Despairing of overcoming them with torments he turned to guile and soft words and every kind of enticement, and the page says one might have seen a fox fighting with lions. Finding nothing gained, he gave Carpus and Papylas into the keeping of strong picked men, and took Agathodorus, who served the martyrs and became a perfect martyr himself, bound to them by love as a friend and a sharer in the same torments.

He was stretched out and ordered to be beaten with ox sinews without mercy. Judge and martyr contended, the page says: the judge wishing to overcome him by the multitude of the blows, and the martyr receiving them with such goodwill that he seemed unwilling to tire the men who beat him. Under that beating he died. Valerie then told Carpus and Papylas that their foolish servant had got the death his deeds deserved; they called the judge a madman, blessed Agathodorus’s death, and said they desired that same death for Christ with all their hearts. The page names Decius and Valerie and gives no year, and none is supplied here.
