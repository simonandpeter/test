# Alexie Kabaliuk the Carpathian

Alexie Kabaliuk was an Orthodox missionary in Bohemia and Slovakia at the end of the nineteenth century and the beginning of the twentieth. He was born on 1 September 1877 into the family of a woodcutter in Transcarpathia, and was named after Prince [Alexander Nevsky](/saints/alexander-nevsky). Young, he became a novice at the monastery of Kish-Baranya, but soon left to struggle on Mount Athos, at the monastery of Saint Panteleimon, and from there was sent to Russia, to the monastery of Saint Onuphrius at Yablochin.

He was tonsured a monk on 5 May 1910 and ordained hieromonk on 15 August of the same year. He returned then to Transcarpathia, where he preached the Orthodox teaching and came into conflict with the authorities: he was accused of treason and condemned to imprisonment in a concentration camp at the trial of Sighetu Marmației, which ran from 1913 to 1914.

On 17 August 1921 he was elected abbot of the monastery of Saint Nicholas at Iza, and in 1923 — the page adds a question mark and the year 1925 beside it — he was raised to the rank of archimandrite. He departed to the Lord on 2 December 1947 at the monastery of Dąbok, and was buried in the cemetery of the village of Iza in the district of Khust, in the Transcarpathian region of Ukraine. His relics were uncovered on 12 March 1999, and he was canonised on 21 November 2001.
