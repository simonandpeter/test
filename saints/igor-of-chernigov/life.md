# Igor of Chernigov

Igor Olgovich was a son of Oleg Svyatoslavich, called Gorislavich, and so of the Olgovichi, who in the middle of the twelfth century fought the Mstislavichi, the descendants of Vladimir Monomakh, for the throne of Kyiv; the Russian calendar’s life calls the two branches near kin, all great-grandsons of Yaroslav the Wise. It sets Igor beside his cousin, the venerable Nicholas Svyatosha, as two roads to holiness: Nicholas left his princely duties and spent almost forty years a monk, and Igor, drawn against his will into the struggle for Kyiv, had to atone for the inherited sin of the princes’ feuds by martyrdom. In 1138 his elder brother Vsevolod became grand prince of Kyiv and, pointing to Monomakh’s own example, declared that Kyiv would pass to Igor after him; the people of Kyiv, who did not love Vsevolod, would not be anyone’s inheritance.

When Vsevolod died on 1 August 1146 the Kyivans kissed the cross to Igor and he to them, but the boyars at once called in the Mstislavichi, and in the battle with Izyaslav Mstislavich outside the city the Kyivan troops went over to Izyaslav. Igor hid four days in the marshes, was taken, and on 13 August was put into a porub, a cold log pit without window or door; his reign had lasted two weeks. He fell so ill there that he was expected to die, and his enemies let him be cut out and tonsured into the schema at the Theodore monastery in Kyiv, where he recovered and stayed as a monk, in tears and prayer.

In 1147 the Kyiv veche resolved to take revenge on him. The metropolitan and clergy tried to stop it, and Izyaslav and above all his brother Vladimir tried to save him; but the crowd broke into the church during the Liturgy, seized Igor as he prayed before an icon of the Mother of God, and dragged him out. At the monastery gate Vladimir leapt from his horse, covered him with his princely cloak, begged them not to kill him, and got him into his mother’s courtyard; they broke the gates, pulled Igor down from the gallery and killed him on the lowest steps, dragged his body by the feet to the Tithe church and threw it down in the marketplace.

That evening, when it had been carried into the church of St Michael, all the candles over it lit of themselves. He was buried next morning in the monastery of St Simeon at the edge of Kyiv, and in 1150 his brother Svyatoslav Olgovich, prince of Chernigov, carried his relics to Chernigov and laid them in the cathedral of the Saviour. The icon he prayed before when he was seized, the Igor icon of the Mother of God, was kept in the Dormition church of the Kyiv Caves Lavra.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 September old style and on 5 June for the translation of his relics, with two troparia and two kontakia.
