# Cassine

Cassine was the wife of Melasippus and the mother of Antonius, of the city of Ancyra in Asia Minor, and saint.gr sets the three of them in the years of the emperor Julian the Apostate, which it puts at 361. Husband and wife were true Christians, the page says, and raised their son with great care into a worthy member of society and a tried labourer of the Gospel. When the duke Agrippinus learned what they believed, he had the household arrested.

He tried first to break the parents, telling them that their child's life hung on them and that if they would deny the Lord he would spare him; and he pressed the son in the same fashion. They did not fear a martyr's death, and their hearts, the page says, were ready to go to the Lord. Agrippinus, hearing them praise Jesus through his threats, ordered them tortured, and Cassine was put to death with her husband, hanged upon a wooden frame.

Their common distich says that they were not flesh at all: «Οὐ σάρκες ἦσαν, ἀλλ' ἄσαρκός τις φύσις. / Ὁ Μελάσιππος πρὸς ξέσεις καὶ Κασίνη». Kept with her are [Melasippus](/saints/melasippus), [Antonius](/saints/antonius-son-of-melasippus) and [the Forty Children](/saints/forty-children) who came to the faith when their son was not burned. The page prints no hymn for the entry.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps the three of them together on the same day the Greek calendar does. Its line for 7 ноября ст. ст. is «Мчч. Меласиппа и Касинии и сына их Антонина (363).», and the name link behind «Касинии» gives the calendar's own nominative, «Касиния Анкирская, мц.».

The life behind the three name links is four sentences and is the same for all three of them. They suffered in the reign of the emperor Julian the Apostate, in the city of Ancyra, in 363. Melasippus and Cassine, torn with iron hooks and disfigured, died under the torture. Their son, the boy Antoninus, whom the persecutor made stand and watch his parents' torments, spat in the face of the God-forsaking emperor; for that he was put to the cruellest tortures of all, in which he stayed unhurt, and was then beheaded.

Forty young men who saw that the Lord was keeping his confessor Antoninus and that the tortures did him no harm believed in Christ, confessed their faith openly and received a martyr's end — [the Forty Children](/saints/forty-children) this corpus keeps from the Greek line for this same day. **The Russian line does not name them**: it stops at the three, printing «Мчч. Меласиппа и Касинии и сына их Антонина (363).» and nothing more, so no Russian row was written on that folder and the forty are reported instead.

**The page puts Ancyra in Phrygia, and the two calendars do not give the same year.** Its words are «в городе Анкире во Фригии», where the pages this folder was written from put Ancyra in Galatia; the page does not reconcile the two and nothing is chosen here. The year is the second disagreement: saint.gr gave 361, the year of Julian's accession, and this page gives 363 on its line and in its life, so the death above now reads 361 or 363.
