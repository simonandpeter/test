# Cassine

Cassine was the wife of Melasippus and the mother of Antonius, of the city of Ancyra in Asia Minor, and saint.gr sets the three of them in the years of the emperor Julian the Apostate, which it puts at 361. Husband and wife were true Christians, the page says, and raised their son with great care into a worthy member of society and a tried labourer of the Gospel. When the duke Agrippinus learned what they believed, he had the household arrested.

He tried first to break the parents, telling them that their child's life hung on them and that if they would deny the Lord he would spare him; and he pressed the son in the same fashion. They did not fear a martyr's death, and their hearts, the page says, were ready to go to the Lord. Agrippinus, hearing them praise Jesus through his threats, ordered them tortured, and Cassine was put to death with her husband, hanged upon a wooden frame.

Their common distich says that they were not flesh at all: «Οὐ σάρκες ἦσαν, ἀλλ' ἄσαρκός τις φύσις. / Ὁ Μελάσιππος πρὸς ξέσεις καὶ Κασίνη». Kept with her are [Melasippus](/saints/melasippus), [Antonius](/saints/antonius-son-of-melasippus) and [the Forty Children](/saints/forty-children) who came to the faith when their son was not burned. The page prints no hymn for the entry.
