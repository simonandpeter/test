# Arianus of the Thebaid

doxologia.ro prints one life for the martyrs of 14 decembrie, and Arianus is the governor in its Egyptian half who ended as one of them. Under Diocletian the order went out again through all the provinces that everyone should share in the worship of the heathen on pain of death, and Arianus, the governor in the parts of the Thebaid, was eager to carry it out. At the city of the Antinoites he seized two leading Christians named Asclas and Leonidas, tortured them in many ways and destroyed them; then he had every Christian in the place arrested and the instruments of torture set before them.

He examined the thirty-seven who came forward rather than obey. He laughed at [Philemon](/saints/philemon-the-flute-player) the comedian when the face under [Apollonius](/saints/apollonius-the-reader)'s cloak was uncovered and proved to be his, taking it for a joke against the Christians; he called the cloud that came down and baptised him sorcery and a darkening of the eyes; and he had Apollonius kept in chains for worse torments. He had the ankles of both bored through and had them dragged by ropes through the whole city, and had Philemon hung on an olive tree and shot at.

When he came close to see why the arrows would not touch the martyr, an arrow came down out of the air with such force that it struck him in the right eye and blinded him at once. By that blinding, the life says, his soul was given light: before, with sound eyes, he had been blind in soul and without understanding, so that the powerless fought the Almighty; now, being blind, he knew the true God. Forced by the pain, he unbound the martyr and begged him to heal the eye.

Philemon would not heal him then, in case the kindness were taken for sorcery, but told him that his own end was near, and that afterwards the governor should come to his grave, take earth from it, lay it on the eye and call on the name of Christ. Arianus had the two beheaded; then, tormented by the eye, he went to Philemon's grave, took the earth and laid it on the sick eye and said: in your name, Jesus Christ, for whom these men chose death of their own will, I lay this earth on my eye, and if I see, then I too will confess that there is no other God but you.

He was healed twice over at once, in the eye and in the soul: with the eye he saw the sun, and with the soul he knew the justice of God, and he cried out for joy, I am a Christian, and confessed it before them all. He went from there and received holy Baptism with all his house, set free in peace the thirty-six Christians who were still held in the prisons for Christ, and came with two bishops and a great crowd to the martyrs' grave with clean linen and costly spices, and buried their bodies honourably; and afterwards he gave much alms to the poor.

Word came to Diocletian that Arianus had turned from a Greek into a Christian and would not sacrifice, and the emperor sent four servants to bring him. They pressed him to set out at once, but he begged leave to go first to the martyrs' grave, and gave them eighty gold pieces for it; and there he fell on his face and asked the martyrs to help him in the contest. Philemon's voice answered out of the grave: take courage, Arianus, and do not fear, for the Lord is calling you and weaving you the martyr's crown, and these servants will confess along with you and receive a great reward from Christ.

He wondered at it and went home, and being filled with the grace of God he knew the manner and the time of his end, and called his servants and foretold what was to come: go now with us to Alexandria, where I shall be brought before the emperor and shall finish the contest of confession, and shall be put in a sack and thrown into the sea; and on the eleventh day of this same month, at the sixth hour, go out to the shore, and when you find my body brought to land by dolphins, take it and lay it with the other martyrs.

At Alexandria Diocletian received him mildly, bathed with him before the temple of Apollo, and told him to sacrifice to the great god Apollo and then come cheerfully to supper. Arianus asked how he could leave the true God after so many wonders worked at the mere calling on the name of Christ, which were no empty tales but the plain truth, and of which his own eyes were the witnesses; and how he could sacrifice to a lifeless and unfeeling idol.

The emperor threw off his feigned mildness and had him bound with iron chains and a great stone tied to his feet, thrown into a very deep pit and covered over with earth and stones. He set his imperial throne on top of the filled pit and had the soldiers dance round it, saying: let us see whether his Christ will come and take him out of this pit. Then he went into the palace, and in his own room he saw the chain and the stone hanging above his bed, and Arianus lying in the bed.

The emperor was terrified and thought some enemy of his house had played a trick on him; but Arianus told him not to be troubled, that nobody had plotted against him — I am Arianus, governor of the Thebaid, over whom you said as you threw me into the pit, let us see whether his Christ will come and take him out; and behold, Christ has taken me out of the pit and told me to rest on your bed. Diocletian, hardly coming to himself for fear, filled the palace with his shouting about the sorcerer's craft, and ordered him put in a sack of sand, bound, and thrown into the depths of the sea.

Then the four servants who had seen the wonder took courage, remembering also what Philemon had said, and came to the emperor and reproached him: why do you condemn the just man, being yourself unjust, when he has done you no wrong? Christ is the true God who works these wonders. Bury him under mountains and hills, throw him into the depths of the sea, do what you will to his servant — Christ the true God is able to bring him out, as you have seen today; and we are ready to die with him for Christ. The first of them, Theoctistus, asked as his one gift that his goods be divided in two, half to the crown and half to widows and orphans.

The emperor mocked them and sentenced them with Arianus, each to be put in a sack of sand and thrown into the sea; and so Philemon's prophecy was fulfilled. As the soldiers threw them into the sea of Byzantium a very great dolphin appeared, lifted the five sacks on its back and, running like a swift ship, carried them to Alexandria, where Arianus's servants were waiting on the shore as he had foretold. They were frightened at the size of the creature and at how the sacks rode on its back without falling; and a voice came from God: take the relics of your master and the other four, and lay them with the relics of the saints Asclas and Leonidas.

The dolphin set the sacks on the shore and swam back into the sea, and the servants put the relics in a boat and travelled three days and three nights on the river. Off the metropolis of the Antinoites the boat would go no further, and all the boatmen were asleep, until a voice called twice to the steersman, Theodotius, and said that this was where God willed the relics to stay. He woke, saw the boat held as though it were tied, went into the city and told them everything; and the people ran out with torches and with incense, singing and praising the Lord, and buried them with reverence in the place God had appointed, and healings and other wonders were worked there. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mucenic Arian» and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 14 December too, and saint.gr gives the whole turn of his story. He was the governor of Thebes in Egypt in the years of the emperor Diocletian, which the page glosses as 290, and he arrested thirty-seven Christians. After [Philemon](/saints/philemon-the-flute-player) the pipe-player had confessed Christ in the place of [Apollonius](/saints/apollonius-the-reader), and had been baptised in a rain he prayed for, Arrianus tortured the two of them hard and then beheaded them; and Arrianus himself was struck blind.

But Philemon appeared to him in his sleep and told him to smear earth from his grave on his eyes and he would be healed, and so it happened. Then Arrianus believed in Christ, with four of his bodyguards — the four Protectors whom the day's calendar line counts with him and whom the page leaves unnamed — and later all of them together were martyred. The same day's calendar keeps [Hypatius](/saints/hypatius-of-the-thebaid), whose own page makes him one of the thirty-six martyrs of the Thebaid under this Arrianus.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars do, and it is the only one of them to tell his turn whole. In the days of Diocletian he was a judge tormenting the Christians bitterly in Egypt. He seized [Apollonius](/saints/apollonius-the-reader) and threatened him with torture; and when the pagan musician Apollonius had bribed to sacrifice in his place stood before the idols instead and came out shouting that he was a Christian, he laughed, thinking the man was mocking the Christians. He had [Philemon](/saints/philemon-the-flute-player) tortured horribly, and at the last he beheaded them both.

Then he became a Christian himself. His blind eye was healed by a wonder at Philemon's grave; and he was condemned to death by the emperor Diocletian and executed together with four soldiers, who also declared themselves Christians. Those four are the «and others» of the heading, and the book names none of them.
