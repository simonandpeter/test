# Hosea

The prophet Hosea was the son of Beeri of Belemoth, of the tribe of Issachar, a man who feared God. In that time many of the people of Israel had turned aside from God and forgotten His benefits and His wonders and were worshipping idols; but he served God, who made heaven and earth and who brought their fathers out of Egypt with a strong hand, and he taught the people who had strayed from the truth and turned them back by his wise counsels to the right faith of their forefathers.

Filled with the Holy Spirit, he prophesied much concerning Israel and concerning the coming of Christ and His resurrection, as is seen in his book; for he wrote down his prophetic words, fourteen chapters in all, which are reckoned in Holy Scripture as spoken through the Holy Spirit.

Hosea lived many years, the page says, eight hundred and twenty-two years before the birth of Christ; and his name is interpreted saviour, keeper or one who overshadows. He died in deep and honourable old age and was buried in peace in his own land. That is the whole of what doxologia.ro prints of him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 17 October, and gives his father and his kings. saint.gr says the prophet Hosea was the son of Beeri — or Beniah — from Galemoth, or Balethom, of the tribe of Issachar, and that he lived in the eighth century before Christ, under the kings of Judah Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah and under Jeroboam II of Israel; in the Old Testament he is the first of the twelve who are called the lesser prophets. He was a soul full of zeal for the divine Law, the page says, and so in his prophetic book he denounces the people of Israel outright for being defiled with idolatry. His symbolisms are reckoned hard to explain, but he shows his faith in Christ the Saviour most plainly; and the Lord himself used a great phrase of Hosea's to the Pharisees — I will have mercy and not sacrifice, and the knowledge of God rather than whole burnt offerings — while the Apostles Peter and Paul used phrases of his in their epistles. He is said to have lived seventy-five years, and then gave up his righteous soul to God.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same menologion day and puts him a century earlier than the Greek does. Its life says that the holy prophet Hosea came of the tribe of Issachar, and that he lived in the ninth century before the birth of Christ in the kingdom of Israel, a contemporary of the holy prophets Isaiah, Micah and Amos. Many of his tribesmen in that time had forgotten the true God and were worshipping idols; by his wise instruction he turned them back to the old piety, and rebuking the lawlessness of the people of Israel he announced to them great disasters from foreigners and the removal into the Assyrian captivity.

The same page lists, with the verses, the prophecies it reads as fulfilled in Christ. Almost a thousand years before the Saviour came, it says, the holy prophet by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit foretold that the Old Testament sacrifice would cease and the priesthood of Aaron be no more (Hosea 3.5), and that the true knowledge of God would spread over all the earth (Hosea 2.20). Of Christ he spoke too: that he would return out of Egypt (Hosea 11.1, compared with Matthew 2.15), that he would rise on the third day (Hosea 6, compared with 1 Corinthians 15.4), and that he would conquer death (Hosea 13.14, compared with 1 Corinthians 15.54–55).

His prophecies are among the books of Holy Scripture, the page ends, and his prophetic service lasted more than sixty years; in deep old age the God-inspired prophet died, having given his whole life to the fulfilling of God's will. The day line dates him 820 BC where the life says the ninth century and the Romanian calendar says 822 years before Christ; the Greek page says the eighth century, and nothing in any of them settles it, so his floruit stands here as the two centuries together.

The Serbian calendar keeps him on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue is the one page of the four that counts his book. It calls him the son of Beeri of the tribe of Issachar — the Greek page's parentage and the Russian page's tribe in one line — and says he lived and prophesied over 800 years before the birth of Christ, and that his God-inspired words are in his book, which has fourteen chapters. He rebuked Israel and Judah much for idolatry, it says, and foretold God's punishment for their sins, the ruin of Samaria and of Israel for their apostasy, but also God's mercy to the tribe of Judah; and he prophesied the abolition and ceasing of the Old Testament sacrifice, the coming of the Lord, and the riches of the gifts he would bring with him to the earth. He lived to a deep old age and fell asleep in peace.
