# Michael (Kosukhin), Presbyter, Hieromartyr (1937)

In the twentieth century bloody persecutions of the Russian Church began, and the authorities, bent on destroying the Church, were merciless to all — to young men barely of age and to deep old men. Michael was born on 7 January 1858 in the village of Yarenskoye in the Kalyazin district of Tver province, the son of the priest Alexis Kosukhin; he finished the Moscow seminary and was ordained priest, and in 1907 was appointed to the church of the village of Dymtsevo in the Bezhetsk district, where he served until his arrest. In the thirties his family were his brothers — one living in Poland, one a priest in the Tver region — and an unmarried daughter, who helped her aged father; until 1929 he had a holding of a horse, a cow and three desyatinas — the calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. After the revolution, despite his age and the persecutions, he went on serving and preaching; in 1937 the seventy-nine-year-old priest was arrested and charged with counter-revolutionary activity, and after interrogations the troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot, but he died in the prison hospital of his illnesses and the injuries he had received. The Russian calendar keeps him on 31 August (Julian).
