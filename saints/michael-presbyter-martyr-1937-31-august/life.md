# Michael (Kosukhin)

In the twentieth century bloody persecutions of the Russian Church began,
and the authorities, bent on destroying the Church, were merciless to all
— to young men barely of age and to deep old men. Michael was born on 7
January 1858 in the village of Yarenskoye in the Kalyazin district of Tver
province, the son of the priest Alexis Kosukhin; he finished the Moscow
seminary and was ordained priest, and in 1907 was appointed to the church
of the village of Dymtsevo in the Bezhetsk district, where he served until
his arrest. In the thirties his family were his brothers — one living in
Poland, one a priest in the Tver region — and an unmarried daughter, who
helped her aged father; until 1929 he had a holding of a horse, a cow,
three desyatinas of land and fifty hives of bees, but that year the whole
holding and property were seized and the house taken, and he and his
daughter settled in the church lodge. The authorities did not then arrest
or exile him, reckoning that a priest past seventy would soon die or be
unable to serve, and the church would close anyway; it did not happen —
he served and served, and his age and the nearness of death gave him only
more resolve and boldness, so that he missed no chance to preach, and for
that zeal for God and for the souls of the flock entrusted to him his
parishioners loved him and were always ready to come to his help. Seeing
his firmness the authorities began to persecute him: since the lodge he
lived in stood outside the church fence, the district and regional
executive committees resolved to evict him and take the building for a
veterinary clinic, and the chairman of the Dymtsevo village soviet came
again and again to demand that he leave, and every time he answered that
under no circumstances would he leave a church building. So on Sunday 25
March 1936 the chairman had the window-frames taken out of the lodge,
hoping that in the cold still outside the priest would leave of himself;
he did not, but after the Sunday service and sermon told the people that
since the village soviet had taken his frames out he asked them to ask the
authorities to give them back, the weather being still cold. About a
hundred and fifty were in church, and all went straight from the church
to the village soviet; the chairman, frightened by the crowd, locked every
door and hid, and the people, finding the doors locked and knowing he was
inside, walked round the house shouting to him to return the frames,
until, getting no sign of life, they had to disperse. The frames were not
returned, but he did not leave the lodge — I shall go nowhere from this
house, he told his people; I am old and afraid of nothing — and the local
authorities did not dare evict him by force, and he went on serving and
preaching with the same fearlessness; at Easter that year the bishop gave
him the pectoral cross for faithful service. Only a year later, with the
onset of the general persecution, he was arrested and put in the Bezhetsk
prison. He had just turned seventy-nine, was gravely ill and had not left
his bed for several months, moving only with his daughter’s help; the NKVD
men who came for him lifted him by the arms and legs, threw him into the
car and drove him to prison, where the doctor called to examine him
recorded myocarditis with swelling of the legs, no teeth at all, and
senile decrepitude, and pronounced him unfit for physical labour. What
sermons and instructions had he given the faithful touching the Soviet
power? — I preached to the faithful often, but said nothing about the
Soviet power or politics. In March this year you carried on anti-Soviet
agitation among the faithful and organised a mass demonstration of
believers demanding that the village soviet return your house; do you
confirm it? — on 25 March I did preach in church, and after the service I
told the faithful that the village soviet had taken the frames out of my
house and asked them to go and ask the soviet to give me the frames back,
since the weather was still cold; the faithful in church, about a hundred
to a hundred and fifty, all went to demand the frames of the chairman,
but the soviet did not return them. Do you admit guilt in the charge of
counter-revolutionary activity? — I do not admit myself guilty of the
charge. You deny your crime of counter-revolutionary activity, but the
inquiry and the witnesses have established exactly that you carried it
on; we demand truthful testimony — I repeat that I carried on no
counter-revolutionary activity, but preached sermons to the faithful. On
2 August the inquiry closed and the case went to the NKVD troika, which on
10 August sentenced him to be shot. Whether by the doings of the
investigator Golofast who questioned him or by the hard conditions of an
overcrowded cell, his left thigh was found broken; he could not walk and
could not have reached the place of execution, and on 16 August, by order
of the prison administration, he was moved to the Bezhetsk town hospital,
where, under sentence of death, he lived twenty-nine days and died on 13
September 1937 — 31 August of the old style, the day the Russian calendar
keeps him. The life was compiled by the igumen Damascene (Orlovsky); the
Russian calendar keeps him also in the synaxis of the saints of Tver.
