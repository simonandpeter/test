# Adrian of Nicomedia

Adrian, of Nicomedia, lived in the time of the emperor Maximian and was married to [Natalia](/saints/natalia-of-nicomedia). One day he saw twenty-three Christians ready to be martyred for their faith; at twenty-eight, struck by what he heard from them, he declared to the pagans that he too was a Christian. He was seized at once and shut in prison, where Natalia came to stand by him and tell him not to bend.

After many and terrible tortures he gave up his spirit. When the pagans tried to burn his body a violent downpour broke out and put out the fire; Natalia buried him, and a little later was buried beside him. The synaxarion names [the twenty-three](/saints/twenty-three-martyrs) who died with him, and adds [Atticus](/saints/atticus-the-martyr) and [Sisinius](/saints/sisinius-the-martyr) as perhaps arrested with him.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep them the same day, 26 August (Julian), and the Serbian prints their troparion. The Sretensky calendar’s life: Adrian and Natalia married young, only a year before their sufferings — thirteen months, the Prologue says — and lived at Nicomedia in Bithynia under Maximian (305–311), both of noble and rich family, Adrian head of the praetorium and a pagan, Natalia a secret Christian. Beginning the persecution the emperor promised rewards to those who pointed out Christians and brought them to trial; on one denunciation twenty-three Christians hiding in a cave near Nicomedia were taken, tortured, pressed to worship the idols and brought to the court to have their names and answers written down. Adrian, the head of the court, watching with what courage these men bore suffering for the faith and how firmly they confessed Christ, asked what reward they expected from their God for their torments; such a reward, they answered, as we cannot describe and your mind cannot grasp. Fired, he said to the scribes: write me down too, for I also am a Christian and will gladly die for Christ God. The scribes told the emperor, who summoned him — have you too gone mad, and want to perish? go, strike your name from the lists and sacrifice — and Adrian answered, I have not gone mad, I have come to my right mind; and Maximian had him imprisoned. Natalia, learning that her husband suffered for Christ, rejoiced, and hurried to the prison to strengthen him: blessed are you, my lord, that you have believed in Christ; you have gained a great treasure; spare nothing earthly, neither beauty nor youth — he was twenty-eight — nor riches; all earthly things are dust and corruption, and only faith and good works please God — the Sretensky calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. After long torments and imprisonment, the Prologue says, the emperor ordered an anvil brought into the prison and their legs and arms broken with a hammer, and Adrian with the twenty-three honourable men died in the heaviest torments; Natalia carried their relics to Constantinople and buried them there with honour, and after some days Adrian appeared to her all in light and beauty and called her to go to God too, and she gave her spirit to God in peace.
