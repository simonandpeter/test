# Nicholas (Karaulov)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 4 апреля ст. ст. in a line that carries two people who have nothing to do with each other, «Сщмч. Николая , еп. Вельского, прмц. Марии (1932).», and the name link behind his part of it, page 9152, gives him his see: «Святитель Николай (Караулов), епископ Вельский, сщмч.» The calendar has no life for him. azbyka.ru does.

He was born on 28 May 1871 in the village Томаш of the Кадниковский uyezd of Vologda province, the son of a priest. He finished the Vologda seminary in 1893 and was made psalm-reader at the George church in the city; he was ordained deacon that December, moved to the Saviour cathedral in 1894 and ordained priest to it in 1898. From 7 February 1901 he was rector of the Catherine church, and the whole of his pastoral work afterwards was bound up with it: he taught the law in a city school, gave to the fund for poor seminarians, gave towards a school building for the diocesan women’s college, and lectured at public religious readings.

azbyka keeps a long passage in his own words about a visit, not long before his wife’s death, to a hermitage elder whose cell he describes — the icon of the wonderworker of Myra in the corner, a plain sofa, a wooden table under a half-curtained window, and a crowd of all sorts in the passage waiting for a blessing. The elder looked at him a long time and said, in a particular tone, «Бедные, бедные, как вы живете…» Soon after that, he writes, he lost the wife he loved and was left with three small children; and the words stayed in his heart, because they were so plainly being fulfilled.

The persecution found him a priest well known in Vologda. He was arrested in 1921 for counter-revolutionary activity, sentenced to two years’ exile and sent to Pinega in Arkhangelsk province. He came back to a diocese in disorder: the ruling bishop had fallen away into the Renovationist schism, and the Orthodox bishop appointed in his place could neither live in the city nor govern. On 21 October 1923 the priest Nikolai Karaulov was tonsured, keeping his own name, consecrated bishop of Velsk and vicar of the Vologda diocese, and told to govern the diocese for the time being.

Only then, azbyka says, did he feel fully that a mitre under a merciless persecution is a heavy crown of thorns, and he asked to be let go: he wrote to the patriarch on 1 January 1924 that the clergy and laity of the Velsk uyezd were ill-disposed towards him as their bishop, that the powers given him were not enough in law, and that he was seriously ill. He refused the Arkhangelsk see when it was offered him too, and stayed in Vologda, serving in the church where he had once been rector.

By 1928 three bishops were living in the city, one of them retired, and serving in the two Orthodox churches the godless had left standing, both in cemeteries. On the great feasts they served together, and afterwards a priest or a layman would ask them to a festal meal where everyone spoke of what was happening — the pressure on the Church, the arrests, what the prisons and the camps were like for those who had been in them.

That is what the case was built on. On 10 May 1931 thirty-five people were arrested in Vologda and the Gryazovets district — seventeen clergy, seven monks and eleven laymen — and charged with visiting one another on church feasts and talking together. Questioned on 14 May, the bishop answered shortly: that he had no particular acquaintance, that he knew all the clergy of Vologda through his service and those he had studied with, and that he had no acquaintance out of town. Witnesses said what the meetings had been: that all the clergy might be arrested, that the bishops advised the priests to be careful, and that the metropolitan’s declaration was disapproved while his canonical authority was still held good.

The witnesses were not thought enough and an informer was put into the cell. What he reported azbyka prints at length, and it is the sharpest portrait of the man in the whole life: that a servant of the Church can only be a monarchist and will never give that up; that wherever and however hard the conditions, they will stay faithful to the covenants of the Church and the laws that were there before the revolution; that this one is for the Soviet power only because he is in the prison, and let him out and he will preach his beliefs with more zeal than before; and that he is the most popular bishop in the city, with three thousand households of believing acquaintance in it, respected by all the priests and by the men in the cell too.

On 14 December 1931 he was sentenced to three years’ exile in the Northern krai, and on 7 March 1932 the sentence was changed to the same term at the other end of the country, in Kazakhstan. He died in prison on 17 April 1932 and was buried in an unmarked grave. 17 April 1932 is the civil date of the Julian 4 April, so the calendar keeps him on the day he died; the line itself closes «(1932)».

No `office` is recorded. He was bishop of Velsk and this corpus mints no office key its five language packs cannot print, and «Bishop of Velsk» is not among them; the see is in the prose and in the name form’s note instead. azbyka lists three further days beside this one — the moveable synaxis of the New Martyrs on 7 February, the moveable synaxis of the Vologda saints on 11 July and the synaxis of the Arkhangelsk metropolia on 1 November — all three of them synaxes, and none written into the feast. Neither book prints a hymn under his name.
