# John (Sretensky)

In 1917 nine monks and novices lived at the Zilantov monastery on the edge of Kazan under the care of the archimandrite Sergius (Zaitsev). When in 1918 Kazan was freed by the White Czech troops under Colonel Kappel, two guns were set on the hill before the monastery gate and shelled the Bolshevik positions; on 28 August (10 September new style) the Czechs withdrew and the Bolsheviks burst into the suburb where the monastery stood, and the Red Army men lined up all the brethren against the wall of the monastery yard and shot them in volleys, then left the monastery and moved into the city. Only one survived, the aged hieromonk Joseph, who fell at the first volleys and was taken for dead; climbing out from under his brothers’ bodies he went into the city and found shelter at the monastery of John the Forerunner, and, deafened, dying a year later, told of his brothers’ martyrdom. Those shot were the archimandrite Sergius (Zaitsev, 1863–1918), the hieromonks Laurence (Nikitin, 1872–1918) and Seraphim (Kuzmin, 1870–1918), the hierodeacon Theodosius (Alexandrov, 1864–1918), the monks Leontius (Karyagin, 1870–1918) and Stephen, and the novices George (Timofeyev, 1880–1918), Sergius (Galin), Hilarion (Pravdin) and John (Sretensky); the funeral and burial of the martyrs was served by the archimandrite, later bishop — the Sretensky calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. The Russian calendar keeps the ten monk-martyrs of Kazan on 28 August (Julian).
