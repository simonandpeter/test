# Simeon of Pângărați

Simeon was born at the beginning of the fifteenth century in a village near Piatra Neamț, when the devout prince Alexander the Good ruled Moldavia. From youth he showed love and zeal for the things of God, chose the monastic life and entered the community of Bistrița monastery, where he laboured in obedience and grew in spirit, cutting off his passions and his own will; the longing for the silence of the desert being born in him, he took the abbot’s blessing and in 1432, in the days of Iliaș the voivode, Alexander’s son, withdrew with two disciples into hesychia on a rise of the left bank of the Pângărați brook, at the foot of the mountain called Păru, five kilometres from his monastery, and made himself a cell of fir logs in a glade among ancient forests, labouring with his disciples in unceasing prayer and heavenly thought. Growing much in the grace of the Spirit, a perfect hermit and skilled teacher of the prayer of the heart, he gathered disciples who loved silence, and his settlement was known for twenty-nine years as Simeon’s hermitage. Hearing of his labours and that he had no church to pray in with his disciples, the holy prince Stephen the Great gave him in 1461 money and help to raise a small wooden church, dedicated after a vision to the great martyr Demetrius the myrrh-streamer; finished the same year, it was consecrated on 26 October by Metropolitan Theoctist I, who ordained Simeon priest — and so he became the first founder and abbot of Pângărați monastery, called Simeon’s skete until 1508 — the Romanian life runs on past the point read here. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 7 September with [Amphilochius](/saints/amphilochius-of-pangarati), with a shared troparion that calls them the lights of the Carpathian forests and the secret counsellors of the voivodes.
