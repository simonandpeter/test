# Simeon of Pângărați

Simeon was born at the beginning of the fifteenth century in a village near
Piatra Neamț, when the devout prince Alexander the Good ruled Moldavia. From
youth he showed love and zeal for the things of God, chose the monastic
life and entered the community of Bistrița, where he laboured in obedience
and grew in spirit, cutting off his passions and his own will; and when the
longing for the silence of the desert was born in him he took the abbot’s
blessing and in 1432, in the days of Iliaș the voivode, Alexander’s son,
withdrew with two disciples into hesychia on a rise of the left bank of the
Pângărați brook, at the foot of the mountain called Păru, five kilometres
from his monastery, and made himself a cell of fir logs in a glade among
ancient forests, where he and his disciples laboured in unceasing prayer
and heavenly thought. Growing in the grace of the Spirit, a perfect hermit
and skilled teacher of the prayer of the heart, he gathered disciples who
loved silence, and his settlement was known for twenty-nine years as
Simeon’s hermitage. Hearing of his labours and that he had no church to
pray in with his disciples, the holy prince Stephen the Great gave him in
1461 money and help to raise a small wooden church, dedicated after a
vision to the great martyr Demetrius the myrrh-streamer; finished the same
year, it was consecrated on 26 October by Metropolitan Theoctist I, who
ordained Simeon priest, and so he became the first founder and abbot of
Pângărați, called Simeon’s skete until 1508. Many disciples who loved
Christ and silence gathered round him; he grew so far in ascesis, prayer
and the Spirit’s gift that he was counted worthy of the charisms of healing
and foresight, and the sick came to his cell and went away well by his
prayer, and villagers and even the boyars of the prince’s council came for
his blessing and a word of counsel, and Stephen himself loved him and often
asked his advice and his prayers for himself and for the country, always
threatened by the pagans. By God’s permission the Turks fell on Moldavia in
1476 and, for the people’s sins, defeated Stephen at Războieni; seeing the
danger, Simeon took his disciples, prayed for the prince and the country,
and crossed the Carpathians into Transylvania to the monastery of Cașiva,
where a little later, in the autumn of that same year, he passed in peace
to the heavenly dwellings and was buried with honour by his disciples.

His holiness shone after death through his relics, which Stephen himself
honoured: when the Turks had gone and peace was made, the hieromonk
Anastasius writes in his account of the building of Pângărați, the voivode
sent and had the holy relics brought in a precious reliquary and kept them
with honour in his treasury, took a part for a blessing and censed them
always with sweet spices and incense, for the strengthening of his own
faith and above all for the love and spiritual warmth he had had for him,
and buried the rest with holy fitness and honour in the fortress of
Suceava — towards the end of 1484, it seems, when his disciples are known
to have come back to the hermitage he had founded. The Romanian calendar
keeps him on 7 September with [Amphilochius](/saints/amphilochius-of-pangarati),
with a shared troparion that calls them the lights of the Carpathian
forests and the secret counsellors of the voivodes.
