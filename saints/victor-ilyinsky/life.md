# Victor (Ilyinsky)

Victor was born on 31 October 1872 in the village of Koy in the Sonkovo district of the province of Tver, the son of a deacon. He finished the teachers' courses at the district school in the province of Novgorod and came home to the village where his father, by then a priest, was serving. For ten years he taught in the parish school; from 1906 he served in the church as psalmist, and in 1912 he was ordained deacon to the same church.

He was first arrested in 1920, charged with concealing surplus grain, and given three months in a labour camp. In 1927 the local authorities opened a campaign to close the church at Koy; the deacon and his parishioners protested, some sixty women went to the village soviet and demanded that it stop, and the authorities gave way and shelved their plans for a time. After that the OGPU looked for a pretext. He was ordained priest to that church in 1930 and arrested at once for failing the grain-delivery plan — eight months in a camp; he was dekulakised the same year; and in 1934 he was arrested again for hiding church valuables and small change and given two years, from which he was released early in 1935 because the camp would have killed him.

In March 1936 the NKVD searched the church and took away the registers of births, marriages and deaths. He answered that he had kept them as his priestly duty required, on the instruction of his dean and of his archbishop [Thaddeus](/saints/thaddeus-of-tver), and that nobody had come to him for certificates. He was arrested on 12 November 1937 and held in the prison at Bezhetsk. At the interrogation of 20 November, where duty witnesses signed protocols the investigator had written in advance, he denied every charge: no anti-Soviet sermon, no attack on the candidates for the Supreme Soviet, no rumours of war. «Виновным себя в контрреволюционной деятельности не признаю». The troika sentenced him on 25 November and he was shot on 27 November 1937. He was canonised for the whole church in August 2000.

The day's line names twenty-seven new martyrs of 1937 together — twenty-three presbyters, a deacon, a hieromonk, a lay martyr and a woman — and the calendar carries a life page for ten of them, of whom he is one. The men of that line were tried separately, in different districts and different prisons, and no one of these lives names another of them, so no relation is recorded between them here.
