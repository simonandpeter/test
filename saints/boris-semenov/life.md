# Boris (Semenov)

Boris was born in 1900 at St Petersburg, the son of a typesetter in the printing house of a magazine. He studied to 1916 in a technical school attached to the state paper works and then worked there until 1920; when the works was evacuated to Moscow in 1920 the whole family moved with it. He was a clerk until 1922, lost the place in a mass lay-off, and went to study at a horticultural technical school. At that time he began to help in a Moscow church as an altar server, and there he met the bishop [Augustine (Belyaev)](/saints/augustine-belyaev) and became his cell-attendant and subdeacon.

When the bishop was exiled to Central Asia Boris followed him to Pendzhikent, worked in the fruit gardens there and helped him at the services he held in his room. When the bishop was given the see of Syzran he went to him there and served as subdeacon again, besides carrying out various church errands; on his journeys to Moscow the bishop sent letters with him to the metropolitan. In December 1930 the bishop ordained him deacon.

Arrested with his bishop, he answered the investigator that in his conviction the Soviet power was then oppressing the ministers of religion, because the clergy were taxed beyond what they could pay; that he had never had occasion to talk politics with the bishop and could not say what his views were on this or that measure; and that his own view of the collectivisation campaign was that collectivisation was acceptable to an Orthodox Christian only if it were not turned against his religious convictions, that is, if it did not aim at the oppression of religion.

On 28 October 1931 a special conference of the OGPU board sentenced the bishop and the deacon to three years in a concentration camp. The bishop was sent to one near the Lodeynoye Pole station in the Leningrad country; the deacon was sent to another camp, also near that station, and died in his imprisonment. No year is given, and the day's line closes the entry with the decade alone.
