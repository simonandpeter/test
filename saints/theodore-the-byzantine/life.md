# Theodore the Byzantine

Doxologia's page for Theodore the Byzantine carries no life tab and no troparion tab, only a single notice under his name. It says that he confessed Christ in the city of Mytilene, on the island of Lesbos in Greece, at the age of twenty-one, on 17 February in the year 1795, after he had been tortured by the Turks; and that his relics lie for veneration in the cathedral at Mytilene.

The rest of the page is photographs — of those relics, of the metropolitan cathedral at Mytilene, and of the treasures of the monastery of Dionysiou on Athos. There is no account of where he came from, of who took him, or of what he was asked, and this entry adds nothing to the notice.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and fills the life out. He came, saint.gr says, from Neochori of Constantinople; his parents were Hadji-Anastasios and Smaragdo, and his brothers Antonios and George, a reader of the Great Church and afterwards metropolitan of Adrianople under the name Gregory, from 1830 to 1840. Theodore was a painter and worked in the palaces. The Turks tried to draw him to the Muslim religion and succeeded; but he came to himself and repented of it, and so fled to Chios and stayed near a spiritual father, St [Macarius Notaras](/saints/macarius-notaras), studying every day the martyrdoms of the saints and books profitable and contrite, so that from day to day compunction and the longing for martyrdom grew in him. So he came to Mytilene, where before the authorities he confessed his faith in Christ and renounced the Mohammedan religion. He was seized, imprisoned and made to suffer beatings, kickings and other inhuman torments; they bound his feet in the stocks and put a heavy chain about his neck, wrapped his head with a rope and set two pieces of brick at his temples, and then tightened his head with a piece of wood so far that the balls of his eyes came out of their places — and the soldier of Christ kept saying: a Christian, a Christian, a Christian am I. On the Saturday morning he asked a Christian servant for pen and ink and wrote to the bishop to send him Holy Communion; and having communicated of the immaculate Mysteries he was led to the place of execution, to which he ran with great eagerness, and there suffered death by hanging in 1774, or by others in 1795. Three days later the Christians took his body and buried it on the south side of the church of the Panagia Chrysomalloussa, and in 1798 the relic was translated to the crypt of the metropolitan church of Mytilene, where the page says it lies incorrupt; it names him patron of Mytilene. Its couplet calls for a young hymn for Theodore the young, justly to be fitted to a contender of Christ.
