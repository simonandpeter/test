# Paul (Malinovsky)

Paul Vasilievich Malinovsky was born on 15 October 1874 to the deacon of
the Trinity church at Shelota in the Velsk district of Vologda province,
today the Verkhovazhye district of the Archangel region, Basil Petrovich
Malinovsky. His father died before he was a year old, and his mother
Alexandra Nikolaevna raised three sons alone; the two elder, Palladius
and Alexander, finished the Totma church school at the state’s expense,
and Paul entered the Vologda seminary at fourteen and finished it in
1894, and on 13 December 1894 was made supervisor of the Ust-Sysolsk
church school, a post he held to the end of 1897. On 14 November 1897
Alexis, bishop of Vologda and Totma, appointed him to a clerical post, and
on 28 January 1898 Gabriel, bishop of Veliky Ustyug, ordained him priest
to the Annunciation church of Ust-Vym; from 1898 he was head and
catechist of the Ust-Vym boys’ two-class parish school, and for diligence
in his work served in 1902–1905 as deputy for investigative and economic
affairs of the second deanery district of the Yarensk county, and in
1905–1917 as its dean. For zealous and useful service he was given the
nabedrennik on 29 March 1905, the skufia on 14 April 1910 and, by the
Holy Synod’s decision of 25 March 1917, the kamilavka, and wore the silver
medal of the church schools’ twenty-fifth anniversary; in 1906–1910 he
taught the Law of God at the Vogvazdino zemstvo school and in 1909–1914
headed the Ust-Vym girls’ parish school. Tall and stately, with a fine
velvet voice, kind and upright in daily life, he gave himself wholly to
the service of God and people. He married Athanasia, daughter of the
priest Paul Motokhov of the same Annunciation church, and they had
thirteen children, to whom their father was an example in all things —
restrained, calm, strict in measure, teaching them honesty, kindness and
justice; when his wife died all the cares of the large family fell on
him, and strong in spirit he raised them all to be decent people, while
parishioners came to him with their troubles and griefs and found words of
comfort and support, though he and his family knew want and privation all
those years. After 1918 he lived at Ust-Vym and until 1933 was rector of
the Annunciation church, and as dean headed the parishes loyal to
Patriarch Tikhon; in 1918 he was assessed for a contribution of five
hundred roubles, in 1921 prosecuted for not handing over church
valuables, in 1931–1932 listed by the OGPU as an anti-Soviet element who
had “grouped the monastics round himself and carries on anti-Soviet
activity through them”, and in 1936 charged under article 192 and
acquitted by the court. When the Annunciation church was closed in 1933
he served for a time at the Kazan church of Yb in the same parish, and
when that too was closed in June 1935 despite his efforts he had to live
by farming — but stayed faithful to his service and his flock, secretly
burying the dead, baptising infants and celebrating the other mysteries,
planting in his parishioners faith in goodness and love for Jesus Christ.
He said openly that the destruction of the Orthodox faith was ruinous for
Russia, which Orthodoxy had shaped for centuries, breeding the best moral
qualities and uniting Russians in the hardest moments of their history;
spoke unflatteringly of the collective-farm activists, lately the
village idlers, and of a collectivisation that was destroying the
age-old peasant traditions; and sheltered “kulaks” whom the Soviet power
had stripped of all they had earned by hard labour. He was arrested on 6
August 1937, together with the priest Nicholas Kirillov, and held in the
Syktyvkar NKVD prison, charged among other things with systematically
carrying on counter-revolutionary activity for the opening of the church
in the village of Kazansky Yb; on 23 August 1937 the troika of the Komi
NKVD sentenced him under article 58-10 to the highest penalty, and he was
shot at Syktyvkar — on 25 August 1937 by the short life, on 12 September
by the fuller one — and buried in an unknown grave. The Council of
Bishops of 13–16 August 2000 glorified him among the new martyrs and
confessors of Russia, and the Holy Synod entered him in their synaxis by a
decision of December 2001; the Russian calendar keeps him on 30 August
(Julian) and in the synaxis of the saints of Komi.
