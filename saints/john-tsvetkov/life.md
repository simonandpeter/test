# John (Tsvetkov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names six presbyters on one line for the Julian 16 November and his is the first of them: «Сщмчч. Иоанна , Николая , Виктора , Василия , Макария и Михаила пресвитеров (1937).» His life opens on the reason there was a priest of his kind at all: under the persecution there were fewer and fewer priests left who had been ordained before the revolution, and the bishops began to ordain devout laymen, so that the flock should not be left without the mysteries and the churches should not be closed. John Stepanovich Tsvetkov was born on 17 March 1878 in the town of Torzhok in the province of Tver, the son of a market gardener, Stephen Tsvetkov.

He finished the two-class model school attached to the Torzhok gymnasium. He married, and his wife soon left him; they had no children. At the beginning of the twenties he was ordained priest. In 1929 he was arrested, as many priests were in those years, charged with failing to deliver the state’s quota of farm produce, and sentenced to three years’ exile. On his return he was appointed to the church of the village of Perkhovo in the Udomlya district, and it was there that the persecution of 1937 found him.

On 17 November 1937 the chairman and the secretary of the village soviet and the chairman of the collective farm wrote out, at the demand of the НКВД, a character reference against him. They wrote that he drew the services out and preached at length; that he often called on his parishioners to behave decently and not to walk about or talk during the service; that he told believing parents they must bring their children to church, and that everyone, grown and child, should wear a cross and never take it off and should come to church on the great feasts and on Sundays. The reference concluded that all this was aimed at undermining the collective farms and overthrowing the Soviet power, and that he must be isolated from the masses as an enemy of the people.

Days later he was arrested and shut in the prison at Bezhetsk, and he was interrogated the same day. Told the investigation had evidence of anti-Soviet agitation, he answered that he had never carried any on. Asked where he had got the information that the Soviet power would not last long, he said he had never said it anywhere. Asked to what end he had frightened the faithful with punishment from God, he said he knew well that he would be hauled off for any anti-Soviet word, so he could not have said such a thing in church — and had not said it in private either. He would not admit the charge. On 27 November the troika of the НКВД sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 29 November 1937. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the jubilee council of bishops of August 2000, for the veneration of the whole church.
