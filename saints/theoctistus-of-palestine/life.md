# Theoctistus of Palestine

Theoctistus, the great ascetic, withdrew from the world to the lavra of Pharan six miles from Jerusalem, shut himself in a cell and gave himself to the severest labours; in those years Euthymius the Great settled in a neighbouring cell of the same lavra, and the common longing for asceticism bound the two closely — after the leavetaking of Theophany they went deeper into the desert for stricter struggle and came back to the lavra on Palm Sunday, and after the fifth year they withdrew for good to a cave in the desert. The fame of their virtue — the Greek synaxarion runs on — drew disciples, and the Romanian calendar says that Euthymius and Theoctistus founded a lavra for them, of which Theoctistus remained abbot while Euthymius withdrew to solitude; the Prologue adds that he was the abbot of Euthymius’s lavra, six miles from Jerusalem on the Jericho road, in everything Euthymius’s disciple, governing the monastery under his spiritual guidance to his ninetieth year, and reposed in the middle of the fifth century under Anastasius, patriarch of Jerusalem — 467, the calendars print.
