# Zacharias, son of Carion

Zacharias was the son of Carion the Egyptian, who left his children and his wife and became a monk. Though Zacharias was younger than most of the brethren of the skete, he was fuller of the gifts of the Holy Spirit than the older men, and felt his whole being kindled by the love of the grace of God. He was a great light of the Egyptian desert, and he passed to eternal life young.

Saint Macarius asked him who is a true monk, and Zacharias answered: the one who struggles daily to fulfil the commandments of God. Abba [Moses](/saints/moses-the-ethiopian) asked him what it means to be a monk, and Zacharias took off his monastic cap, trod it underfoot, and said: this is what it means to be a monk; no one can be a monk who does not let himself be crushed as this cap is crushed.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, under a bare line — «Όσιος Ζαχαρίας» — whose page links his father Carion beside him, and it tells the same two things the sayings tell. saint.gr says he excelled in the monastic life and was its boast, and that his humility above all was beyond compare, for the sake of which God adorned him with so great a spiritual pre-eminence that the famous abbas Macarius and [Moses](/saints/moses-the-ethiopian) themselves came near him with much reverence and always came to profit by his words, which were enlightened by the Holy Spirit. At the hour of his death, which came quiet and calm, the death of a true righteous man, many of the abbas stood about him as brothers; and in his last moments, while Zacharias was silent, he looked up somewhat mysteriously, and then abba Isidore looked up too and said, as if from some heavenly inspiration, «Εὐφραίνου, τέκνον μου Ζαχαρία, ὅτι ἠνεώχθησάν σοι αἱ πύλαι τῆς οὐρανῶν βασιλείας» — rejoice, my child Zacharias, for the gates of the kingdom of the heavens have been opened to thee. The couplet over him says that, made like to God in strength, he leaves the earth and becomes a co-heir with the Son of God.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and gives the sayings the other calendars did not. He was the son of Carion the Egyptian, who left his wife and children and became a monk; the father took Zacharias to himself because the mother could not feed him. Though younger than many of the elders in Scetis, he had been granted greater gifts of grace than many of them, and from the grace of God it felt to him as though his whole inside were burning.

Asked by a Saint Macarius who the true monk is, he answered that it is the man who constantly compels himself to the commandments of God. Asked by Abba [Moses](/saints/moses-the-ethiopian) what it means to be a monk, he took off his camelaucion, trod it underfoot, and said that unless a man is crushed like that he cannot be a monk. Both sayings are paraphrased here from Bishop Nikolai's text and not copied, and the Macarius of the first is given no by-name on the page, so he is left unidentified. Zacharias was a great light among the monks of the desert, and reposed in the Lord young. The entry gives no year, so this folder still holds none.
