# Achillius of Larissa

Doxologia's notice of Achillius is short. He lived, it says, in the time of the emperor Constantine the Great, and was born and brought up of right-believing parents, from whom he learned piety and the honouring of God together with worldly learning and the highest philosophy. Adorning himself with every good thing that is according to God, he was chosen archpriest of Larissa, which is in the second Thessaly, by all who dwelt in Greece.

He went to the great council at Nicaea, laboured and contended with the fathers to the end, and joined in deposing Arius and those with him and laying them under anathema. Then he returned to Larissa, where he pulled down many idol temples, drove demons out of men and worked many other wonders; and he ended his life in peace, after raising houses of prayer from the foundations and adorning them in every way.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day and fills in the years and the journeys. saint.gr says Achillius came from Cappadocia and lived in the days of Constantine the Great, whose reign it dates from 306 to 337; that the longing of his youth carried him first to the Holy Places and then to Rome, where he gave himself to the work of a preacher, teaching the divine word without ceasing in towns and villages and making nothing of hardship, abuse, persecution and distress; and that these great services raised him to be bishop of Larissa. From that place, the page says, he was the spiritual head and teacher who did what he said: he preached every day, helped widows, protected orphans, relieved the poor, defended the wronged, and was the sleepless guard of the faith and of the flock entrusted to him. He was distinguished at the First Ecumenical Council, held at Nicaea in 325 against Arius, and Constantine, valuing his virtues, gave him a large gift of money which, on his return to Larissa, he spent on building churches and on the care of the sick and the poor. When he foresaw his death he called all the priests of his diocese to him and gave them a father's counsel; the page's head line gives the year as 330, names him patron of Larissa, and says his relics lie in the metropolitan church of Larissa that bears his name.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 15 May, calls him a great hierarch and a wonderworker, and gives the wonder the other two books leave out. He was born in Cappadocia. At the First Ecumenical Council he shamed the heretics and drew great admiration by his learning and his holiness alike; and taking up a stone, he called out to the Arians: if Christ is a creature of God, as you say, then say the word and let oil flow out of this stone. The heretics kept silent and marvelled at the challenge. Then the saint spoke again: and if the Son of God is equal to the Father, as we believe, let oil flow out of this stone — and the oil did indeed flow, to the astonishment of them all.

He fell asleep in peace at Larissa in 330, the year the Greek calendar's head line gives too. Afterwards, the Prologue says, Samuel the emperor of the Macedonians, when he took Thessaly, carried the relics of Achillius to Prespa, to an island in the lake which was named after him and to this day is called Ahil, or Ail.

The Russian notice is short and agrees with the others in the main, with two things of its own. It sets him in the fourth century under the emperor whose reign the folder already names, has him made bishop of Larissa in Thessaly for the holiness and the learning he was famous for, and puts him at the First Ecumenical Council, where it says he rebuked the heretic Arius to his face. In his own city it has him planting the faith, pulling down the idol-houses and building and adorning churches; it gives him the gift of healing sickness and names the possessed in particular. He died in peace about 330. The notice then carries the relics further than the Greek and Serbian books do: from 978 they have lain in Bulgaria, at Prespa, in a place the page says is now the village of Ахили and is named for him.
