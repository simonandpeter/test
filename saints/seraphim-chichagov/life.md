# Seraphim (Chichagov)

Leonid Mikhailovich Chichagov was born on 9 January 1856 at St Petersburg, son of the artillery colonel Mikhail Nikiforovich Chichagov, in one of the best-known noble families of the province of Kostroma. He was baptised twenty days later in a church belonging to the military department, which the page calls symbolic of the whole of his life: like his forebears he began his service to God as service to the sovereign in the field.

Out of the Imperial Page Corps he went to the Balkan war of 1876 and 1877, was in almost every principal action of it, was commissioned on the field and decorated several times. Back in Petersburg he came to know the righteous priest of Kronstadt and became his spiritual child. In 1879 he married N. N. Dokhturova. He left the army in 1891 and in 1893, with that priest's blessing, was ordained, and served in several churches in Moscow.

His wife died in 1895. About then he began work on the chronicle of the Seraphim-Diveyevo convent, and in 1898 he was tonsured a monk under the name Seraphim; soon afterwards he was made superior of the Spaso-Evfimiev monastery at Suzdal. In 1903 the wonderworker of Sarov was glorified on a submission he had drawn up and with the emperor's active help, and he wrote the akathist to him. In 1905 he was bishop of Sukhumi, and from 1906 to 1912 archbishop in turn of Oryol, Kishinev and Tver. At the council of 1917 and 1918 he headed the department on monasteries and monasticism.

In 1917 the Renovationists drove him from his see. The patriarch appointed him to Warsaw, but he never reached it, because of the fighting there. In 1921, by then a metropolitan, he was exiled to the Arkhangelsk country, and he lived some years at the Voskresensky Fedorovsky monastery near Shuya. In 1928 he was brought back to the Church's business and given the see of Leningrad; in 1933 he was retired, and spent his last years outside Moscow.

In 1937 he was arrested and carried on a stretcher to the Таганская prison. On 11 December of that year he was shot at the range at Бутово outside Moscow. He painted icons, and the page says that one of them, an image of the Saviour, is in the church of the prophet Elijah in Moscow. The day page prints him a troparion and a kontakion, and the kontakion turns on the name he was given in religion and on the saint of Sarov whose glorification he worked for.

His name page lists one other day, «26 января (Новомуч.)», the synaxis of the new martyrs and confessors of the Russian Church. One row per church is the schema, so that day is recorded here and he does not render on it.
