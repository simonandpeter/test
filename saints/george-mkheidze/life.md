# George (Mkheidze)

George Mkheidze came of an impoverished princely family; feeling the call to
monasticism as a youth, he left the military school at St Petersburg and
lived for a time as a hermit, and was known for his mercy and freedom from
possessions — meeting a beggar he would take off his clothes and give them,
and if they were his last he dressed in sacking. Before Betania he lived in
the Khirsa monastery until it was closed in 1924, when he came to [John
(Maisuradze)](/saints/john-maisuradze) at Betania, the monastery of Queen
Tamar’s day that bears the name of Bethany near Jerusalem. Two of his
brothers were communists, one of them, Peter, Beria’s deputy; wishing to
turn the young monk from chastity they once locked him in a room with a
loose woman, and he stood the whole time in a corner with his face to the
wall reading prayers; both brothers died in 1938, the younger, John, by his
own hand, and Peter shot with his wife. Through the communist persecution
John and George kept Betania a monastery for many years, living within its
walls to their deaths — officially “watchmen of an architectural monument”
of a closed house, while the icons hung in the church, the lamps burned, the
services and processions went on, and people were baptised and married
there. John died in 1957; George took the great schema five years before his
own death with the name John, died in 1962, and is buried beside him at
Betania. The Georgian Church keeps the two confessors together on 21
September.
