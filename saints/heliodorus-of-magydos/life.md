# Heliodorus of Magydos

saint.gr's calendar for 19 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Ηλιόδωρος από τη Μαγιδώ της Παμφιλίας» and glosses his name — ἥλιος and δῶρον — as a gift of the sun. He suffered in the years of the emperor Aurelian, in 272 by the page's reckoning, at Magydos in Pamphylia, at a time when Aetius was the ruler of that city. The couplet set over the notice turns the gloss into the point of him: Heliodorus, looking at Christ the Sun, was shown to be above the darkness of the torments.

He was arrested, and when he would not be persuaded to sacrifice to the idols they first tore his sides with iron claws and then burnt the wounds with torches lit from resin. He came within very little of losing heart, the page says; but by prayer to the Lord his soul was made firm again.

A series of hard and horrible tortures followed, and the steadiness of Heliodorus's faith was not bent by them. In the end he was led outside the city, and there he suffered death by beheading. The page prints him no hymn and no relics.
