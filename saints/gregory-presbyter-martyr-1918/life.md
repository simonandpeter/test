# Gregory (Garyaev)

Gregory Simeonovich Garyaev was born on 29 January 1878 in the village of
Pyanteg in the Cherdyn district of Perm province, the son of the local
priest, Simeon Evangelovich Garyaev, rector of the Elijah church, and his
wife Anfisa Varlaamovna. In 1871 the village’s first school for peasant
children had opened in the archpriest’s own house, later a people’s
college, and there Gregory had his first schooling. In 1900 he finished
the Perm seminary; on 9 June that year Bishop Peter (Losev) of Perm and
Solikamsk made him a reader and psalmist of the Elijah church of Nikulino
in the Perm district, in August ordained him deacon in the Transfiguration
cathedral of Perm, and on 10 September priest, to the Peter and Paul
church of Kosmo-Damianskoye in the Solikamsk district; on 11 December
1903, at the parishioners’ petition and his own, he was moved to the
Saviour church of Solikamsk. A parish priest in a large one-priest parish,
he carried a long list of obediences with praiseworthy zeal: a permanent
member of the Solikamsk district branch of the diocesan school council
from 24 February 1904, its clerk for two years from February 1905, on its
school commission from March 1905 and its treasurer from March 1911; on
the audit committee of the Solikamsk church school in 1904–1906 and
1909–1911 and on its board in 1907–1908 and 1912–1916; teacher of the Law
of God at the Ust-Usolka zemstvo college from 1905 and at the Solikamsk
two-class boys’ college from 1906, and from 1914 the observer of its
teaching in the ministry schools of the deanery; a member of the deanery
council of the first district of Solikamsk in 1908–1910 and 1914–1916,
acting dean for three months in 1910, and the candidate to this and that
office a district’s clergy elect among themselves. He had the nabedrennik
on 15 March 1904 from Bishop John (Alexeyev), the skufia on 16 April 1907
from Bishop Nicanor (Nadezhdin) for his work in the church and the
schools, and the kamilavka on 6 May 1915.

In 1918, in the mass repressions in Perm province, he was arrested and
taken to Perm, and there on 8 September of the old style, the feast of the
Nativity of the Mother of God — 21 September 1918 — he was shot by Red Army
men. On 9 October the Izvestiya of the Perm provincial executive committee
printed a list of those shot “in answer to the attempt on members of the
Extraordinary Commission”, and at number 21 stands “Gorlyaev [Garyaev]
Semyon Grigorievich — priest — Black Hundred”; the preliminary inquiry of
the Perm district court’s investigator for the gravest cases, into “the
killing by resolution of the former Extraordinary Commission of a number
of persons of clerical rank”, has the testimony of Paul Bogoslovsky that
the priest Garyaev was shot, like the archpriest Alexander Shklyaev, at
the fifth verst of the Siberian highway. After the White army took Perm,
on 8 May 1919, eight bodies were dug out of a refuse dump at the fourth
verst of the highway, seven of them clergy, and taken to the city mortuary
for their families to know; his relatives knew him — his brother, the
grandson Gennady Denisov records, by the monogram G. G. S. embroidered on
his clothes, the face being unrecognisable from the beatings. On 13 May
the murdered — the archpriests Alexis Stabnikov and Alexander Shklyaev and
the priests Constantine Shirokinsky and Gregory Garyaev — were sung to
their rest by a council of clergy under Bishop Boris (Shipulin), acting
bishop of Perm and Kungur, and buried in the bishops’ cemetery by the
Transfiguration cathedral; the authorities later put a zoo there, and for
a time a camel’s cage stood over his grave by the zoo wall. There were
eight children. At his arrest he had time to charge his wife, Vera
Vasilievna, to keep the family together; in 1919 they went with the
retreating White army to Kurgan, and at Kungur she altered the children’s
registers, keeping their names and surname but writing another
birthplace, and the family lived in fear, hiding from pursuit; later she
taught, and then ran the school, at Torgovishche in the Suksun district.
The jubilee Council of Bishops of 2000 glorified him among the new martyrs
and confessors of Russia; the Russian calendar keeps him on 9 September of
the old style, 22 September, with the deacon of his church
[Alexander Ipatov](/saints/alexander-deacon-martyr-1918), shot the same
day, and in the synaxis of the saints of Perm.
