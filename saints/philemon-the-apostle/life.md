# Philemon

Philemon was a notable citizen of Colossae in Phrygia. Believing in Christ, he made his house a church: all the faithful of Colossae gathered at his house, and the divine services were celebrated there as in a church. Afterwards he was a bishop in the apostolate, for in the days of the Apostles some bishops had a see and others had none but went through different cities and lands; such men were called apostolic bishops, because they were sent out on apostolate to preach. His wife was [Apphia](/saints/apphia-wife-of-philemon), on the witness of Saint John Chrysostom.

Set in such a bishopric, Philemon was counted in the company of the seventy apostles, going through the cities of Phrygia and others and preaching the word of God; and it is written of him, doxologia's life says, that he was also bishop in Gaza. The Apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) wrote a letter to him, and in it calls [Archippus](/saints/archippus-the-apostle) — who was bishop in Colossae after Saint Epaphras, on the witness of Saint Ambrose — his fellow soldier.

Once, when the god-hated feast of the unclean Artemis was being kept at Colossae, Archippus with those who were there and with Philemon gathered all the faithful in the house that Apphia kept in order, and they offered their usual prayers to God and celebrated the holy service. The idolaters, who hated the faithful and knew that all the Christians were gathered in Philemon's house, fell on them without warning and drove out the flock of Christ, beating some and killing others.

Archippus, Philemon and Apphia they seized and brought to Artocles, the governor of the city of Ephesus, and at his order all three were tortured. First they were stretched on the ground and dragged and beaten with staves without mercy; then each was buried in the earth up to the hips and stoned. Philemon and Apphia were killed under the stones; Archippus they beat and left alive, to be mocked by the children, who gathered and pierced him through with their knives. So the apostolic three went to heaven, before the throne of the Most Holy and undivided Trinity.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 22 November, and sets him in his household. saint.gr makes the four of them disciples of the Apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), about the year 54, whom Paul names in the Epistle to Philemon. Philemon and his wife [Apphia](/saints/apphia-wife-of-philemon) were Christians in the city of Colossae with a developed sense of charity, who used their wealth readily for the relief of the poor and the sick and for the growth of the work of Christ, and they came to Christianity through Paul when he came to their city; and it is for Philemon's good works, the page says, that Paul writes «Χάριν ἔχομεν πολλὴν καὶ παράκλησιν ἐπὶ τὴν ἀγάπη σου ὅτι τὰ σπλάγχνα τῶν ἁγίων ἀναπέπαυται διὰ σοῦ, ἀδελφέ». Of [Archippus](/saints/archippus-the-apostle) it is said that he was a kinsman, perhaps even a son, of Philemon and Apphia, and Paul calls him a soldier in that letter for his great devotion to spreading the Gospel; [Onesimus](/saints/onesimus-the-apostle) was Philemon's servant, who ran away from him to Rome, fell into Paul's net there, and was sent back to Philemon a Christian, with Paul's plea that he be received not as a servant but as a brother. By the tradition, the page ends, all of them were martyred for the spreading of the Gospel.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, in one entry with [Archippus](/saints/archippus-the-apostle) and his wife. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović says that at Colossae the centre of Christianity was in his house, where the Christians gathered for prayer, and that writing to him the apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) called it your house church. In those days, it explains, the apostles ordained their disciples as bishops, some to a fixed place and some as missionaries travelling about from place to place; and he was one of these last.

At some feast of the pagan goddess Artemis the faithful were gathered in his house at prayer as usual when the pagans rushed in and seized them all, and took him, Archippus and [Apphia](/saints/apphia-wife-of-philemon) as the leaders: first the rod, then buried to the waist in the earth and beaten with stones. He and Apphia were killed that way.
