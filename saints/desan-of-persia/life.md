# Desan of Persia

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery heads the Julian 9 April with a company of two hundred and seventy-three and names three of them, Desan first: «Мчч. Дисана еп., Мариава пресвитера, @7[Авдиеса] и прочих 270-ти (362-364).» — the martyrs Disan the bishop, Mariab the presbyter, Audiesius and the other 270, about 362 to 364. The name link behind his place in that line, name page 636, gives the calendar’s own nominative with the epithet, the office and the rank the line withholds: «Святитель Дисан Персидский, епископ, сщмч.» It lists one day for him and only one, 9 апреля, and its single Жития link is the company’s.

That life is two sentences long and they are the whole of what this calendar records. The holy martyrs bishop Disan, the presbyter Mariab, Audiesius and the other 270 suffered martyrdom, it says, at the hands of the Persian king Shapur II; in captivity they refused to deny the Christian faith. azbyka.ru’s calendar for the same civil day carries that sentence word for word under a heading that spreads the epithet across all three names, «Мученики Дисан, епископ, Мариав, пресвитер, Авдиес Персидские и прочие 270». Neither page says how the two hundred and seventy are counted, or who they were.

What the Russian line compresses, the Greek entry the corpus already keeps under the name of [Audiesius, with the martyrs who suffered with him](/saints/audiesius-of-persia) tells at length, and it names this man. In the thirtieth year of his reign, saint.gr says, Shapur II overran the country it calls Byzate and put to death everyone who bore arms, leaving alive the women, the children, the old men, the bishop Heliodorus, the presbyters «Δησάν, Μαριάβ» and all their clergy. When Heliodorus understood that he was going to die he ordained the presbyter Desan bishop — which is the office the Russian name page gives him, and the only account read here of how he came by it.

As bishop he carried the Christian preaching further, to the Persians themselves. At that the chief magus Adephar accused him to the king, saying that he and all the Christians about him were blaspheming the Persian religion; and Shapur gave orders that they all be seized and made to worship the sun and the fire. Because they would not do it they were all beheaded, and only five of them, the page says, lost heart on the road and accepted the religion of the idols. The English form here follows the Greek «Δησάν», which is the form this corpus had already printed for him, where the Russian calendar spells him Дисан.

The company’s own life closes by sending the reader to another day. Among them, it says, was the martyr Ia, who is commemorated on 11 September — [Ias of Persia](/saints/ias-of-persia), whom this corpus keeps on that day and whose own Сретенский line prints the same bracket of years as this one. The one of the three named men who was already a folder here was the one the Greek calendar heads its entry with; this day’s reading makes folders of the other two, because this is the calendar that prints their names.
