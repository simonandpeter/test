# Symeon the Goldsmith of Trebizond

saint.gr says that Symeon came of Trebizond, but that he lived in Constantinople and followed the trade of a goldsmith there. He found himself one day in a brawl between Christians and Turkish Jews, as the page calls them, and was slandered as having supposedly knifed one of them. So he was arrested and shut in prison, where he stayed forty days. As soon as the health of the wounded man improved, Symeon was obliged to pay him two hundred and eighty piastres for his nursing.

Ten days after Symeon's release, the page goes on, the wounded man succumbed to his injuries in spite of having mended. Then Symeon was arrested again, and the judge pressed him to become a Mohammedan so as to escape the sentence of death. The martyr answered boldly: «Αν και μύριους θανάτους μου δώσετε, από την πίστη και την αγάπη του Ιησού Χριστού, τον Κύριο μου και Θεό μου δεν χωρίζω» — though you give me ten thousand deaths, from the faith and the love of Jesus Christ, my Lord and my God, I will not be parted.

Then, on 14 August 1653, they hanged him under a plane tree in Constantinople. The couplet printed above the notice makes the gallows his stair: «Tω Συμεών υπήρξε βαθμίς αγχόνη, / Δι' ης ανήλθεν Oυρανού εις το πλάτος» — the noose was a step for Symeon, by which he went up into the breadth of heaven. The account of this new martyr's passion, the page ends, was written by John Karyophylles. saint.gr prints no hymn to him and this folder records none.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 14 Αυγούστου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/08/14/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2270/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026.*
