# Stephen, founder of the monastery of Chenolakkos

Stephen came of a noble family — perhaps from Cappadocia, saint.gr allows — which brought him up in great devotion. When he was grown he wished to travel round the famous monastic centres, to see their life at close quarters and to complete his ecclesiastical learning there. The page's account of those travels is longer than what is set down here, and this life does not attempt it.

What it comes to is this: Stephen founded the monastery called that of Chenolakkos, which stood to the north-east of Triglia, near Moudania in Asia Minor, and many monks came to it, drawn by his virtue. There the venerable Stephen was marked out for his fatherly government and for the moral weight he carried with the monks; and having governed the monastery in a holy manner, he died in peace.

The page gives no year at all, and prints no hymn. The couplet it sets over him plays on the monastery's name: that for the divine Stephen, who built the house of Chenolakkos — the goose-pit — a pit was dug for a grave.
