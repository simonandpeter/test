# Silouan the Athonite

Simeon Antonov was born in 1866 to a Russian peasant family, one of seven children of a father the life remembers for deep faith, gentleness and good sense. At four he resolved that when he was grown he would "search for God over all the earth"; at nineteen, hearing of a living wonder-working saint, he understood he did not need to - "if there are saints, God is with us." The fire of that first conversion lasted three months and went out, and the strong, handsome young man lived as village youths live, once striking a man nearly to death - until a voice heard from the Mother of God in a dream turned him around for good. He served his military term in a guards engineering battalion in Petersburg, and in 1892, at twenty-six, entered the Russian monastery of St Panteleimon on Mount Athos.

There, praying one evening before the icon of the Mother of God, he received the unceasing prayer of the heart - and then, inexperienced, fell into torments that ended, after six months, in an hour of total despair. At Vespers in the chapel of the Prophet Elijah, at the words "Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy on me," he saw the living Christ beside the royal doors, and his whole being filled with the fire of grace. What followed was not rest but the hardest of roads: fifteen years of the grace withdrawing and returning, and then, in a terrible night of struggle, the answer he is remembered for. "The proud always suffer from demons," he heard; and when he asked what he must do, "Keep thy mind in hell, and despair not." He kept that word for the rest of his life - steward of a monastery of two thousand while praying without ceasing, weeping for "the whole Adam," writing down in secret the experience his fellow monks never suspected. The archimandrite Sophrony found him, and later published his notebooks.

His end was like his life. After eight days of illness, lucid, serene and sunk in prayer, he died so quietly that the neighbouring beds heard nothing, between one and two in the night of 24 September 1938, while Matins was being sung in the infirmary chapel. The Russian calendar keeps him this same day, at 11 September of the old style.
