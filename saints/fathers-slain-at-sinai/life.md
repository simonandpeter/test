# The Thirty-eight Fathers slain at Sinai

These holy fathers were living the holy monastic life in the caves of Mount Sinai, saint.gr says, in the fifth century after Christ. Their devout life was suddenly and savagely broken one day: bands of barbarians, raging against the Christian faith, appeared at the dwellings of the anchorites. At the sight the saints were at first shaken; but they collected themselves at once, and in the face of slaughter and of death they showed a wonderful courage and fearlessness.

Not one of them denied his faith. The barbarians cut them down inside their huts and their gardens, and they died praying, chanting hymns and doxologies and thanksgivings to God — so that, as the page puts it after the Apostle Paul, they fought the good fight of the faith, finished their course, and kept their faith unto death.

Two of them, Sabbas and Isaias, were saved from the murdering swords; it was they who buried the slain and told what had happened to them. The page adds that a good many of the names of these fathers may be found under the fourth day of Renewal Week in the synaxarion of the Pentecostarion — but it prints none of them here, and so this is one entry and not thirty-eight. The same slaughter fell on the [fathers in the desert of Raithu](/saints/fathers-slain-at-raithu), whom the same day keeps, and the one apolytikion serves them both.
