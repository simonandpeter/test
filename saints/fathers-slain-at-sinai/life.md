# The Thirty-eight Fathers slain at Sinai

These holy fathers were living the holy monastic life in the caves of Mount Sinai, saint.gr says, in the fifth century after Christ. Their devout life was suddenly and savagely broken one day: bands of barbarians, raging against the Christian faith, appeared at the dwellings of the anchorites. At the sight the saints were at first shaken; but they collected themselves at once, and in the face of slaughter and of death they showed a wonderful courage and fearlessness.

Not one of them denied his faith. The barbarians cut them down inside their huts and their gardens, and they died praying, chanting hymns and doxologies and thanksgivings to God — so that, as the page puts it after the Apostle Paul, they fought the good fight of the faith, finished their course, and kept their faith unto death.

Two of them, Sabbas and Isaias, were saved from the murdering swords; it was they who buried the slain and told what had happened to them. The page adds that a good many of the names of these fathers may be found under the fourth day of Renewal Week in the synaxarion of the Pentecostarion — but it prints none of them here, and so this is one entry and not thirty-eight. The same slaughter fell on the [fathers in the desert of Raithu](/saints/fathers-slain-at-raithu), whom the same day keeps, and the one apolytikion serves them both.

The Russian calendar keeps the two companies in one line on 14 января ст. ст., «Преподобных отец, в Синае и Раифе избиенных», and counts them differently from the Greek. Its life page says they struggled in the monasteries and caves of mount Sinai, where the ten commandments were given, and in the neighbouring desert of Raithu on the shore of the Red Sea, and that they suffered from the Saracens and the Blemmyes, nomad Arab tribes. The first slaying it puts at about the year 312, described by Ammonius, an Egyptian monk who saw forty fathers of Sinai die; at the same time, it says, the Arabs killed thirty-nine fathers at Raithu. A second slaying followed a hundred years later and was described by another eyewitness who escaped by a wonder, «преподобным Нилом Постником», whose own memory it keeps on 12 ноября.

The ascetics of Sinai and of Raithu led a life of particular strictness, the page says: all the week they stayed in their cells in silence, on the Saturday they gathered for the all-night vigil and on the Sunday they received the holy mysteries, and they ate nothing but dates and water. Many of them were famous for the gift of wonderworking. The seventeen names the Russian calendar prints — Исаия, Савва, Моисей, ученик его Моисей, Иеремия, Павел, Адам, Сергий, Домн, Прокл, Ипатий, Исаак, Макарий, Марк, Вениамин, Евсевий and Илия — are, in its own words, those «по именам в службе преподобным отцам упоминаются»: the names the service mentions, not a roll of the company. The fathers of Raithu are kept on the same day.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 14 Ιανουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/01/14/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/218/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 14 января ст. ст. — [the Russian day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260114.html) and [the Russian life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life215.htm), read 3 October 2026, which keeps the two companies in one line and numbers them forty and thirty-nine.*
