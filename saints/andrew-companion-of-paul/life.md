# Andrew, companion of Paul of Mesopotamia

saint.gr keeps eight martyrs on one page for 18 May, and says that all of them were martyred in the years of the emperor Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251. [Paul](/saints/paul-companion-of-andrew) and Andrew were from Mesopotamia and soldiers of Decius; and when they came to Athens they became soldiers of Christ, and together with [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-of-lampsacus) and [Christina](/saints/christina-martyr-18-may) all of them were stoned to death.

The page's couplet for the three men stoned reads: three men shatter with stones the firm stones of the faith, Paul, Dionysius, Andrew. Of the rest of the company it says that [Peter](/saints/peter-of-lampsacus) was of Lampsacus and was crushed with chains and staves when he would not sacrifice to Aphrodite, and that Heraclius, Paulinus and Benedimus were of Athens, where they fought against the error of the idols and of the philosophers who made war on the Christian religion, and were seized, tortured and beheaded. The company's own verse calls them an eightfold band of victors raised from earth to the city above.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 18 May in an entry of five, headed for [Peter](/saints/peter-of-lampsacus), [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-of-lampsacus), Andrew, [Paul](/saints/paul-companion-of-andrew) and [Christina](/saints/christina-martyr-18-may), and it gives each of them one word: Peter a fine young man, Dionysius a man of standing, Andrew and Paul soldiers, and Christina a virgin of sixteen years. All of them, it says, confessed Christ the Lord bravely and bore tortures and death for His name. 

It then tells the end of the one who did not. A certain Nicomachus, who was tortured with them, denied Christ in the middle of the tortures, and at once went out of his mind, and like a man possessed gnawed his own flesh and foamed at the mouth until he expired. The year, the Prologue says, was 250. The heading names him third of the five, and the soldier it calls him is the rank this folder already carries off the Greek page.

The Russian calendar names him fourth in its heading and third in its narrative. He was brought to judgement in the second group of the trial at Lampsacus, with his companion, with Дионисий, with Никомах who afterwards denied Christ, and with two soldiers fetched from Mesopotamia; all of them confessed Christ, refused to sacrifice, and were tortured. On the morning after, he stood before the governor again with two of the others, and for that confession the three were handed over to a crowd of pagans. They were tied by the feet, dragged to the place of execution and stoned to death; and the page says that while the stoning was going on the young girl of the company ran up to die with them, and was beheaded at the governor's order.
