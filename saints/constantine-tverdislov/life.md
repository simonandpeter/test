# Constantine (Tverdislov)

Constantine Nikolaevich Tverdislov was born on 1 May 1881 in the small town of Gorokhovets in Vladimir province, to Nicholas, a postal official, and Alexandra, parishioners of the Annunciation cathedral there; his surname, made from the Slavonic letters slovo and tverdo, marks, his life says, a family of the clergy. He finished the Murom church school in 1896 and the Vladimir seminary in June 1902 among its best students, and taught for two years at the school at Davydovo in the Pereslavl district. In June 1904 he married Helen Georgievskaya, a priest’s daughter, and they had three daughters, one of whom remembered him as uncommonly kind. On 1 August 1904 Bishop Nikon (Rozhdestvensky) of Murom ordained him priest to the Resurrection church at Yuryev-Polsky, where he later became rector, and he was remembered there as a zealous pastor, preacher and teacher of the gymnasium’s pupils.

After the revolution he was persecuted more than once. In 1928 he was arrested and sentenced to three years of exile, and when he came back he served at Vyazniki. In 1937 he was arrested again on a false charge, sentenced to death by the NKVD troika and shot on 1 October 1937. He was glorified in 2002.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 18 September old style.
