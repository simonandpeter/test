# Dodo of Gareji

Dodo was a disciple of David of Gareji, and came of the Andronikashvili family. From a small child he leaned towards the monastic life and was endowed with many virtues; a lover of poverty and of solitude, he lived as a hermit at Ninotsminda in the region of Kakheti in Georgia. When he heard of the high spiritual life of David, he came into the desert of Gareji and asked to stay beside him, and David received him with joy.

After a time, seeing his virtue, his temperance and his other gifts, David called him and said: Father Dodo, the Lord, who wills the salvation of all men, has been pleased that we should be in this desert region to become pleasing to Him by our ascetic labours. See how many devout souls have gathered here. For these brethren, who have left the world and their families for the love of God, we must make a monastery. Go up, then, with some of them onto the rocky mountain before us, and build there, cut into the rock, cells and a church, where you will glorify without ceasing the name of our Saviour Jesus Christ and honour His most pure Mother, the Virgin Mary.

Dodo did found on that mountain a monastery cut into the rock, where many hermits settled, and it was dedicated to Saint John the Baptist. He shut himself in a narrow cave with barely room for one man, and there day and night, winter and summer, in heat and in cold, he prayed with tears for the forgiveness of his sins, for the strengthening of the souls of his brethren, and for the strengthening of the true faith through the whole country.

Once David miraculously healed the son of prince Bubakar of Rustavi, and the grateful prince gave food and other necessities to the monks of Gareji; David took a part of what he sent and forwarded the rest to Dodo. He counselled Bubakar to be baptised by Dodo, and Dodo baptised Bubakar with joy, and his sons, and all his retinue. He lived to a great age in the monastery he had founded; his disciples buried him in the cave where he had struggled, and later a church was built over his grave.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 14 мая ст. ст. — three days off the 17 mai of the Romanian, which is a difference the two books simply have and not a conversion of style — and it gives him the one year anybody gives him. Its line reads «Прп. Додо Давидо-Гареджийского, Грузинского (596)», compounding his name with that of the founder of the desert he settled in, and marking him of Georgia. Behind that line there is no life page at all: the name link is a heading and a reference to an article on the same site, and this folder's life was not written from it.
