# Isaurus the Deacon

Isaurus and those with him were from Athens. Leaving his own country he came to a cave near Apollonia, and there he found [Innocent](/saints/innocent-of-apollonia), [Felix](/saints/felix-of-apollonia) and [Peregrinus](/saints/peregrinus-of-apollonia) and Hermias, and taught them not to give themselves to the things of this world that pass away; and they made the word good by the deed, keeping away from the talk and the company of their own kinsmen. Their relatives denounced them to Tripontius the eparch, who seized them and, unable to make them deny Christ, ordered them given over to torture.

Isaurus, the deacon of Christ, and those with him were handed over to Apollonius, the eparch's son, by whom they were tormented with fire and with water. Delivered from those torments in a wonderful way, they turned many to faith in Christ, among them the foremost men of the city, the brothers Rufus and Rufinus. Then they were condemned to death, and their heads were struck off.

Doxologia's calendar for 17 iunie gives Isaurus a line of his own, «Sfântul Mucenic Isavru», and sends it to the life the whole company shares. He is the Athenian who came to the cave and gathered the rest, and the deacon of that company.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 17 June, and saint.gr's heading for the company names six where doxologia's named four: «Άγιος Ίσαυρος και οι συν αυτώ Βασίλειος, Ιννοκέντιος, Ερμείας, Φιλήκας και Περεγρίνος» — Isaurus with [Basil](/saints/basil-companion-of-isaurus), Innocent, [Hermias](/saints/hermias-companion-of-isaurus), Felix, whom the Greek line spells Φιλήκας and the apolytikion Φήλιξ, and Peregrinus.

Its account adds that they all contended for Christ at Apollonia when Numerian was emperor, whom it dates 283 to 284; that the first three were Athenians who embraced the faith the Apostle Paul had preached to the ancient Athenians; that the eparch Tripontius tried by every means to make them sacrifice and failed, they confessing Christ crucified; that they came unharmed in a wonderful way out of a series of hard tortures and brought many to the faith; and that in the end they were beheaded.

The page prints one apolytikion for them, in the third tone, and it is a service of nine, naming Isaurus and Felix, Hermias and Peregrinus, Innocent, Manuel and Basil, Ismael and Sabel — the six of Apollonia with the three brothers out of Persia whom the same day keeps. It gives no kontakion and no year of death.
