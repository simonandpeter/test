# Angelis of Melambes

saint.gr's calendar for 28 Οκτωβρίου keeps four new martyrs together: «Άγιοι Νεομάρτυρες Αγγελής, Μανουήλ, Γεώργιος και Νικόλαος «ἐκ Μελάμπων Κρήτης»». All four were born in the village of Melambes in the district of Rethymno in Crete, the page says, of devout Christian parents. Angelis and [Manuel](/saints/manuel-of-melambes) were brothers by blood, sons of John Retzepis; [George](/saints/george-of-melambes) was the son of Constantine Retzepis, and [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-of-melambes) the son of another John Retzepis — all of them, that is, of one family.

They were rich men, the page goes on, distinguished for their courage, married and with children, and they enjoyed every privilege of the Mohammedans, because they passed themselves off as Turks. In 1821, however, they took the Christians' side and fought bravely for the freedom of their country. When they came home again and went to pay the appointed taxes to the Turks charged with collecting them, they were denounced as apostates from Islam to Mehmet Pasha of Rethymno.

They were arrested and taken bound to that city; and because they did not yield to their tyrants' flatteries but stayed firm in the faith, they were tortured in the most dreadful way. On 28 October 1824 they were beheaded in front of the Great Gate of Rethymno. Three of the four skulls are kept in the church of the Four Martyrs at Rethymno. The apolytikion the page prints names all four of them, and is copied into each of their folders.
