# Triphyllius of Leucosia

doxologia.ro's page for Triphyllius is five sentences long, and this is what they say. In his youth he studied law at Berytus in Phoenicia and practised as an advocate for a time, winning himself a name at it. He was a disciple of [Spyridon of Trimythous](/saints/spyridon-of-trimythous). After he was chosen bishop of Leucosia he took part in the Council of Sardica in the year 347, where he held to the right faith and took the part of [Athanasius the Great](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria). He passed to eternal life in the year 370. The page prints no troparion and no more of his life than that.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 13 June, and saint.gr names him for the older name of his city, «Επίσκοπος Λήδρας», while calling him in the same breath the first bishop of Leucosia and the patron of Nicosia. Its account is careful to mark where it is reasoning rather than reporting. The place of his birth, it says, is nowhere recorded; but because his name is bound up with Trimythous — Tremetousia, the country of his teacher [Spyridon](/saints/spyridon-of-trimythous), whom the page sends the reader to find on 12 Δεκεμβρίου — and with Leucosia, whose first bishop he was, it takes one of those two towns for his native place. His parents' names are likewise unknown, and the page infers that they must have been rich from the education they gave their child. After the elementary schooling he had in the island's two intellectual centres of the day, Salamis and Soloi, the young Triphyllios was sent by his parents to Beirut, then famous for its schools of law, and there he completed his studies in law and rhetoric. When he had finished he did not return at once to Cyprus but stayed a while at Beirut, practising as a lawyer and advocate with exceptional success, and from the first he had made his mark among the educated circles there by his rhetorical power and his wisdom, so that everyone admired the successful young man of learning and called his parents happy. He himself, the page says, looked on his successes with indifference; and those were years of persecutions and martyrdoms for the Christians, which he, though still an idolater, could not bear to see going on daily before him. The page also records that the saint's skull is kept at the monastery of Kykkos in Cyprus, and that of all the monuments once shown at his shrine time has preserved nothing.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 13 June, the same menologion day the folder’s other two rows carry, and gives his see as Leucosia in Cyprus, which is the reading the Romanian page gives and not the Ledra that the Greek page prints. It makes him a disciple of [Spyridon](/saints/spyridon-of-trimythous) and later his concelebrant on the island of Cyprus. He was a merciful man, pure in his thoughts, a virgin his whole life long, a living spring of tears, and a great faster. He ruled the flock of Christ well, and in his repose received a crown among the great hierarchs in heaven. This book gives the same year for his falling asleep as the Romanian page already gave, and says he went in peace.

The Russian calendar's life adds a good deal that the other three books do not. It has him born at Constantinople and educated at Верит — Beirut in Syria, as it glosses it — very learned and eloquent, who for all that chose himself a teacher who was neither bookish nor learned but a man of great holiness, [Spyridon](/saints/spyridon-of-trimythous). When the emperor Constantius II fell ill and got no help from his physicians he prayed, and saw in his sleep an angel showing him a company of hierarchs and pointing to two of them through whom alone he could be healed; he summoned the bishops of the empire, and the teacher came with his disciple. The sick man knew them at once, bowed to them and asked their prayers, and was made well when his teacher touched his head.

The same life tells two lessons the master gave the pupil. In the palace Triphyllius was taken with the beauty of the building, the emperor's majesty and the splendour of the court, and was asked what he was wondering at: does this glitter make the emperor more righteous? All of them, emperors and grandees, will die like the poorest beggar and stand at the judgement of God. Later, bishop of Leucosia by then and walking with his teacher through a country of vineyards and gardens called Паримна, he began to think of buying the land, and his thought was read aloud to him: our dwelling and our riches are in heaven.

The third lesson was public. At a council of the bishops of Cyprus the fathers asked him, as the learned and eloquent one, to preach to the people; speaking of the healing of the paralytic he put the word «ложе», couch, where the Gospel has «одр», bed. His teacher would not have the text loosely rendered, asked him whether he was better than the one who had said «одр» that he should be ashamed of his word, and walked out of the church. So, this book says, the pupil was kept from being proud of the gift of eloquence. He shepherded his flock wisely, built a monastery at Leucosia out of what came to him from his mother, and fell asleep in great old age about the year 370. At the beginning of the twelfth century the Russian pilgrim abbot Даниил saw his relics on Cyprus.
