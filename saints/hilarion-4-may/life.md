# Hilarion (4 May)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 4 mai reads «Sfântul Cuvios Ilarion» — Hilarion the venerable — with no epithet, no see and no country, and the page behind it carries only the day, the epistle to the Galatians on the fruit of the Spirit and the gospel of the easy yoke, the readings this calendar appoints for a monastic saint.

The rank is therefore the whole of what is known here: a monk, kept on this day. He is set down under the day because that is where the calendar prints him, and nothing more is claimed for him.

The Greek calendar keeps a venerable of this day and name, and saint.gr's notice is the first source here to say anything about him at all — though it prints the name as Ιλάριος, Hilarius, where the Romanian line has Ilarion. The warmth of his faith and the purity of his life showed him early to be a worthy worker of the Gospel, it says, and God granted him besides to work wonders: he healed paralytics and the lame, he broke a drought by his prayer, and he freed many possessed people of their scourge in the name of Jesus Christ.

For a time he withdrew to a desert place and lived in a hut, studying and praying and striving to make himself more spiritual, away from the noise of the world. Afterwards he appeared in society again and went on teaching and healing; and at the common demand he consented to be ordained a priest, and in that new sacred office he conscientiously carried out all the duties of a good presbyter. He was a father and a teacher, a friend of the truth, a worker of the Gospel, a pattern of spiritual speech and company and of a blameless life, and he rested in the Lord, busy with such works pleasing to God to the end of his days.

The page gives no century, no country and no monastery, and no year for any of it; its couplet says that he was cheerful by nature, as his name means, and that he works wonders laid in his grave.
