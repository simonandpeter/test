# Alexis (Benemansky)

**A schoolmaster, then thirty-three years a priest of Tver, and four times arrested.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him eighth of the sixteen it closes with 1937, among the «пресвитеров», and hangs a full life behind his name page. Алексий Константинович Бенеманский was born on 6 January 1881 in the village of Баранья Гора in the Новый Торжок district of the province of Tver, son of a priest, Константин Бенеманский. Out of the seminary he worked as a teacher in a primary school at Торжок. In 1904 he was ordained priest and served in the Tver convent of the Nativity of Christ, where, notwithstanding the authorities’ prohibition, he began teaching the Law of God on Mondays and Wednesdays. When the convent was closed he went to the church of the icon of the Mother of God of All the Sorrowing.

**The first exile was for the church valuables, and he spent it arguing with the Renovationists.** In the summer of 1919 he was elected a member of the diocesan council. During the civil war, in 1920, he was arrested and sent to rear-area labour. In 1922 the Bolsheviks began the campaign of seizing church valuables, and in the autumn of that year he was arrested again and sentenced by a commission of the NKVD to two years of exile in Turkestan. A wife and five children stayed behind. In exile he and three other exiled priests actively exposed the Renovationists, served apart from them, and openly commemorated the patriarch. In 1924 the Soviet government decided to release the clergy and laity sentenced in the trials over the church valuables.

**Back in Tver he fought the Renovationists again and built a parish library.** At the end of 1926 he returned, and there went on with the struggle against the Renovationists, who were at that moment reviving and, with the authorities behind them, preparing a council of their own. As rector of the church of All the Sorrowing he organised a library at it, understanding how much spiritual learning and spiritual books matter.

**The third arrest took him to Kazakhstan; the fourth to the pit.** On 2 (15) March 1932 he was arrested again and on 13 (26) March called to interrogation, where the investigator put his questions so that the priest’s answers would fit a charge drawn up in advance, of having created a counter-revolutionary organisation acting against the Soviet power. He kept strictly to church ground in his answers and did not admit himself guilty. In July 1932 he was sentenced to banishment to Kazakhstan for three years. When it was over he came home to Tver, and the town council refused him a document of registration, so that he had to go off the staff.

**He was accused in the same words as [Elijah (Gromoglasov)](/saints/elijah-gromoglasov).** In 1937 he was arrested for the fourth time, charged with taking part in a «контрреволюционной фашистско-монархической организации» headed by [Thaddeus of Tver](/saints/thaddeus-of-tver), the archbishop. He did not admit himself guilty. The indictment as officially drawn said that he, being an active participant in a counter-revolutionary church-monarchist group, recruited new members to it, set individual members to collect money among the believers for a secret monastery he was organising in the town of Калинин, and carried on active counter-revolutionary fascist agitation among the population — the life printing it with the original’s spelling kept. The life closes: «21 ноября (4 декабря н. ст.) 1937 года по приговору Тройки Н. К. В. Д. протоиерей Алексий Бенеманский был расстрелян. Мучители тайно похоронили тело священномученика. Место его захоронения неизвестно». Like Elijah he was canonised twice, as a locally venerated saint of Tver on 6 (19) September 1999 and at the Jubilee Council in August 2000.
