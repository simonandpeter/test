# Bede of Jarrow

saint.gr says he was born in Northumbria in England in 673 and entered the monastery of Jarrow at seven years old, where he studied and stayed for the whole of his life without ever going beyond its bounds. As his own writings and other testimonies show, he spent his life and all his energy on the study of the holy Scriptures, on the exact keeping of the canons of the monastic state, on teaching and on writing.

His theological works, chiefly commentaries, were deep and much valued, but the page says he became famous above all as a historian: his «Ιστορία της Εκκλησίας της Αγγλίας και του Αγγλικού λαού» is the most important source for the period of British and English church history before him. He also composed hymns and other poems, letters and homilies, and a «Μικρόν Μαρτυρολόγιον» with historical notes. He was the first, saint.gr says, to write in the English tongue, and beyond these gifts he was above all a man of God, reverent, humble, and pure in soul and body.

On the last day of his life, when he was altogether spent, one of his disciples reminded him that the last chapter of John was still to be interpreted. «Γράφε, τέκνον μου» — write, my child — he said, and began to dictate. When the chapter was finished he took leave of all the monks, gave each of them a small gift for a blessing, turned towards the church where he had prayed daily for sixty unbroken years, said the words «Δόξα τῷ Πατρὶ καὶ τῷ Υἱῷ καὶ τῷ Ἁγίῳ Πνεύματι», and gave up his spirit into the hands of God in 735. Part of his relics, the page adds, lies in the Roman Catholic cathedral of Durham.
