# Euschemon of Lampsakos

Euschemon, whom saint.gr's line also calls Euschemon in the longer form, was bishop of Lampsakos and lived in the years of the iconoclasts. Although the imperial orders laid the strictest penalties on those who defended the holy icons, he did his duty on their behalf with all his courage. He was denounced, exiled and imprisoned; but he bore the persecution with all the joy that the knowledge of fighting for the holy Church brought him.

The revered bishop was marked also, the page says, for his charitable feeling. The poor were relieved most fatherly by his care; and even in that exile of his, devout visitors used to make him presents of various sums of money, and he spent all of it on the destitute. So he was a genuine and a true bishop, a tender and heroic shepherd, and the Church justly numbered him among her holy confessors. The page gives him no year beyond the age of the iconoclasts, no relic and no hymn, and keeps for him two lines that play on his name, that Paul would have said of him before his death that he walked becomingly, as in the day.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and says in two sentences what saint.gr says in one. In the time of the war on the icons he bore persecution and imprisonment; and he reposed in the reign of the iconoclast emperor Theophilus, whose years the Prologue prints as 829 to 842. That reign is the only date either page gives him, and it is what this folder now records for his repose. There is no city of exile, no charge, no manner of release and no hymn.
