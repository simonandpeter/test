# Conon of Penthucla

saint.gr says Conon lived in the years of the emperor Justinian the First, 527 to 565, and came from Cilicia in Asia Minor. At a young age he became a monk at the monastery of Penthucla, which stood near the river Jordan. When Peter, patriarch of Jerusalem from 524 to 552, learned of his warm piety and his virtue, he gave him the ministry of baptising in the Jordan all who came there to receive holy baptism.

But when it fell to him to baptise a woman he was scandalised, being a man, and thought of leaving the community; and St John the Baptist appeared to him and said: be patient, elder, and I will lighten you of the warfare. One day, though, a Persian woman of great beauty came to be baptised, and he could not baptise her and anoint her naked, and the girl was left unbaptised. The archbishop was much distressed when he heard of it, and Conon took the road away. The honourable Forerunner appeared to him again and repeated those words of help; and Conon told him he was not going back, because he had promised help and had not given it. Then the Forerunner sealed him with the sign of the Cross and told him to return and doubt no more.

So the elder came back to the community, and the next day he anointed and baptised the young Persian woman without once considering that she was a woman. He lived twenty years after that, the page says, and reached the highest degree of dispassion, and died peacefully in a deep old age. Its couplet says: venerable Conon, if you know how to bear the wrestling, you had also a great reward from the Lord. No year and no hymn are given him.
