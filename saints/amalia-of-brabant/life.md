# Amalia of Brabant

saint.gr says the venerable Amalia — Amalberga in the Flemish tongue — lived in the seventh century in what was then Orthodox Belgium, in the province of Brabant, whose capital today is Brussels. She was the sister of Saint Pepin, duke of Brabant, count of Landen and chief courtier of the Frankish kings of the Carolingian line, or, the page says, according to others his niece. She married the count Witger, and was shown to be the mother of saints: Emebert bishop of Cambrai, Gudula, Reinelde the venerable martyr, and Pharaildis.

After a virtuous life in the world, marked out for its works of love and almsgiving, she became a nun at a great age in the monastery of Maubeuge, of the Benedictine order, and there fell asleep in a holy manner in the year 690. In iconography, the page adds, she is shown as an aged nun standing before a monastery. That is the whole of what it tells; it gives no year of birth, no account of her end beyond the year, and no wonders.
