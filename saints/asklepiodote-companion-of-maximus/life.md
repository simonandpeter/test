# Asklepiodote, companion of Maximus

saint.gr's calendar for 19 February prints the four of them on one line, «Άγιοι Μάξιμος, Θεόδοτος, Ησύχιος και Ασκληπιοδότη», and the page behind it gives one account for all four. The holy martyrs of Christ Maximus, Theodotus, Hesychius and Asklepiodote went about every city and every country preaching the Gospel with boldness, confessing that Christ is the true God and Lord of all and the maker of the world, while the idols are works of hands and things men have made out of stones and wood. For that they were seized by the idolaters and handed over to the ruler of the country, and were tortured, to make them deny Christ and worship the idols.

They would not be persuaded, so they were dragged in iron bonds from city to city and set to fight with the beasts; but the beasts did not dare come near them, and they were kept whole and unhurt. Then they were flayed with iron claws, and at the last a great furnace was lit and they were thrown into it, singing as they went and rejoicing; and giving thanks to the holy God they gave up their holy and blessed souls to the Lord and received the crown of martyrdom.

The page gives them no homeland, no city, no emperor, no century, no relics and no hymn. Its couplets divide them two ways: one for the three men, a fellow-contending company of three of the strongest, which plays the three of them against the Trinity and carries them as far as the cutting; and one for the woman alone, that Christ crowned Asklepiodote, whose head the sword cut off. The furnace of the account and the sword of that couplet are both the page's, and nothing here reconciles them. The companions on the line are [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-asklepiodote), [Theodotus](/saints/theodotus-companion-of-asklepiodote) and [Hesychius](/saints/hesychius-companion-of-asklepiodote).
