# Alexis (Krotenkov)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him on 5 апреля ст. ст. in four words, «Сщмч. Алексия пресвитера (1930).», and carries no life for him; the name link behind the line, page 14243, gives the surname the line leaves out, «Алексий (Кротенков), иерей, сщмч.», and lists beside this day only the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs on 26 января. azbyka.ru’s calendar for the same civil day has the life, and it is long: it is signed игумен Дамаскин (Орловский) and taken from «Жития новомучеников и исповедников Российских ХХ века. Апрель», Тверь 2006.

He was born in 1878 in the village of Неклюбка in the Верещагская volost of the Суражский uyezd of the province of Chernigov — now, the page’s footnote says, Неглюбка in the Ветковский district of the Gomel region of Belarus — the son of a Belarusian peasant, Пётр Кротенков. In 1902 he finished the teachers’ school at Присно and, having passed the examination, took a post as a teacher in the parish school of his own village. After the pastoral courses in Moscow he was ordained priest on 13 March 1911, to the church in the settlement Ушкевский in the Туринский uyezd of the province of Tobolsk.

In 1915 he was moved to the church in the village Нижне-Романовское of the Тобольский uyezd, and in 1929 to «храм во имя святителя Николая» in the village Ницинское of the Ирбитский okrug of the Ural oblast, where he served until the day of his arrest. The church there had been seized by the renovationists in 1925 and had come back to the Orthodox through the stubborn refusal of the villagers to have them. Besides the village the parish took in four hamlets, two thousand one hundred and sixty faithful in all; and short as his time there was, the page says, he showed himself a zealous pastor and preacher and earned the love of his parishioners.

At the end of 1929 the godless authorities resolved to close the church. Every obligatory payment had been made and one tax twice over, and the village soviet then demanded a further insurance payment of five hundred and twenty-four roubles. The sum was not easy for the parish, and the priest sent the churchwarden to the dean for advice; the dean sent him on to the Irbit finance department, remarking that the tax would have to be paid in any case. The warden also carried the rector’s own word: that even if the parishioners refused the tax as unjust and beyond them, he had decided not to give up the church, but to stay in it with some of them until the matter was cleared, and to guard it with all his strength from seizure.

On 8 January 1930 five hundred and twenty-five people came to the meeting in the church, in the presence of the chairman of the village soviet and the local policeman. The warden reported the repairs, the insurance paid twice, the third demand of five hundred and twenty-four roubles — and that the chairman had told him, that very minute, that the sum to pay was not five hundred and twenty-four but one thousand one hundred and ninety-six. The parishioners cried out asking where a third insurance had been found, and demanded a vote; and the meeting voted against it to a man. The chairman and the policeman left the church at once, and the priest said from the ambo: «Братья и сестры, завтра будет служба, приходите все обязательно».

The warden took the minutes to the village soviet and was arrested there on the spot. The priest and some of the parishioners stayed the night in the church. In the morning the chairman and the policeman came in with the warden as matins was ending, about two hundred present and most of them women, and before the liturgy could begin the policeman announced loudly that since a group of the faithful had refused the tax the church would be shut under two locks, one of the soviet’s and one of theirs, and sealed. The warden came out on the ambo and asked their forgiveness and said goodbye; the women surrounded him and took hold of him by the belt, and the cry went up: «Старосту не отдадим и церковь не отдадим». The policeman said that they were taking neither the church nor the warden, and went out with the chairman.

Then the priest came out on the ambo and told them they had just been witnesses of persecution themselves; that the blasphemers had wanted to close the church and would not succeed in tearing them from the faith; that a true Christian would never go into a commune nor consent to the closing of a church; and he asked everyone who was free to stay the night in the church, to lock it from inside and let nobody in until it was certain that the local and the central power had given up the closure. «До этого момента мы готовы умереть за веру Христову. Я лично из храма никуда не уйду — умру здесь.»

On 9 January the priest, the church council and about fifty parishioners stayed the night. The doors were barred from inside and a lookout was posted in the bell tower, and nobody slept that first night: molebens were served and the priest talked with his people. In one of those talks he said that many priests were then abandoning their churches and giving up the priesthood, and that he would not sell one hair of his head for a thousand roubles and would die for the church.

The dean, when the warden was sent to him again, advised that so many parishioners need not stay and that the council alone should; the chairman of the district executive committee came to the village and assured all who asked him that the church would not be closed. From then on only the priest and a small part of the parish kept watch, round the clock, and it was announced that if the godless tried to take the church by force the alarm bell would be rung.

On 16 January the warden was sent to Irbit again, to ask leave for the procession at Theophany and to tell the dean that the agitation was settling but that the priest would stay in the church, since he would be arrested the moment he came out and without his arrest they could not close it. The warden was arrested outside the executive committee: the authorities had already decided to allow no service on the feast, when the crowd would be large, and to take the priest before it. That evening an OGPU officer came to the priest’s wife in the guise of a soviet official from the okrug, questioned her with hypocritical sympathy, and let her understand that he could influence the case if he could speak with her husband.

The agent was so adroit that he convinced her entirely of his sincerity and of a peaceful outcome. In the dead of night she went to the church to ask her husband to come home and talk with the visiting official. «Меня арестуют, я это чувствую!» he answered. She argued hotly that nothing in the man’s talk could be suspected, and went on arguing with the arguments the officer had used on her; and seeing that she would not give way, he gave way himself. He prayed earnestly before the Athonite icon of the Mother of God that the Guide and Queen would herself go with him both in these present circumstances and on the road to eternal life. Then he came out of the church with his wife and went by the dark, empty street towards the house, and was arrested there by officers come from Irbit and shut in the Irbit prison.

The church was closed and the arrests began: sixteen people in all on the case, among them some who had nothing to do with the church. Under interrogation he admitted only that he had agreed, in view of the wish and the entreaties of the faithful, to stay the nights in the church, in the watchman’s room; of the rest he said that he did not acknowledge himself guilty, since he had carried on no agitation and preached no sermons against the soviet power. The indictment was drawn up on 25 March 1930 and said that kulaks, former punitive troops and White Guards had grouped themselves round the church council of the Ницинское church of the Tikhonite current with the task of active struggle against the soviet power, and that the organiser and inspirer of the group was the priest.

It was put to him as a charge besides that he had hung an Orthodox calendar in the church with a passage of Scripture copied out under it — the passage from the third book of Ezra about those who, having received freedom, despised the Most High and said in their heart, There is no God, though they knew that they were mortal. On 11 April 1930 a troika of the OGPU sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on 18 April 1930 and buried in a common unmarked grave. **18 April 1930 is the civil date of the Julian 5 April**, so this calendar keeps him on the day he died.
