# Matthew (Aloin)

days.pravoslavie.ru names him fourth of seven on one line for 12 ноября ст. ст. and prints no life for him; the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru prints a long one. He was born on 1 August 1879 in the village of Stenkino not far from Ryazan, the son of a priest, Alexander Aloin. He finished the Ryazan theological seminary, was ordained deacon in 1899 and priest in 1909, and in 1929 was raised archpriest.

He served in one diocese after another: first in the Chernigov diocese, in the village of Лысые, and then, having transferred to the Ryazan diocese, in the villages of Хавертово and Поляны. The last years of his life he served in the churches of the Kolomna district of the Moscow region — the church of St Nicholas in the village of Погост Старки, and from 1936, shortly before his martyrdom, the Dormition church in the village of Мячково.

In 1937 the Soviet authorities decided to close the Dormition church at Мячково and turn it into a grain store. A meeting of the citizens was called for the purpose, and the godless expected it to vote for closing the church as soon as possible; but the faithful who came to the meeting managed to save the church from closure.

In the summer of 1937 the godless began a merciless persecution of the Russian Orthodox Church, and in October the men of the Kolomna district branch of the NKVD decided to arrest him. Several inhabitants of Мячково were called to give evidence and signed the statements that were wanted. As proof of the priest's anti-Soviet activity they brought forward that he had spoken against the closing of churches and against the bringing up of the young in an atheist spirit, which he often talked of with the faithful in church and in the street, and that he was on good terms with those the Soviet authorities counted kulaks.

He was arrested on 31 October 1937 and imprisoned at Kolomna. Told that he was under arrest for active anti-Soviet work and required to give evidence, he said «Никакой антисоветской деятельности я не вел» — I carried on no anti-Soviet activity. Told that the investigation had material making him a member of a counter-revolutionary church-monarchist group and of a counter-revolutionary kulak group in the village, he answered that he had belonged to neither and had known nothing of their existence. The interrogator read out all the false witnesses' statements and asked once more, for the last time, for a candid answer, and he said that he denied the witnesses' evidence and had carried on no anti-Soviet agitation.

He was soon moved to the Taganka prison in Moscow and questioned again there on 13 November. Asked whether he admitted the charge of active counter-revolutionary agitation he said no; asked whether he confirmed his earlier answers he said yes; asked since when he had been a servant of religion, he answered that he had been a deacon from 1899 to 1909 and a priest ever since. That was the end of the questioning. The next day, 14 November 1937, the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 25 November 1937 on the Butovo range near Moscow and buried in an unmarked common grave.

The two hosts give him two grades and this folder records neither as an office. days.pravoslavie.ru's name page titles him «иерей», a priest; azbyka.ru calls him «протоиерей» throughout and says he was raised to that grade in 1929. The day page prints seven men of 1937 on one line and carries no hymn under any of their names, and he is given no relation to the other six: the line is a line and not a company, and nothing on either host puts his arrest, his prison or his troika with any of theirs. azbyka's own list of his days adds three synaxes — of the new martyrs who suffered at Butovo, of the saints of Ryazan on 23 июня, and of the saints of Moscow — which are synaxes and not second feasts, and he does not render on them.
