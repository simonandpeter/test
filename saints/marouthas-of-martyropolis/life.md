# Marouthas of Martyropolis

saint.gr keeps him with a company, under one line: «Όσιος Μαρουθάς ο επίσκοπος συν των Αγίων Μαρτύρων εν Μαρτυροπόλει» — the venerable Marouthas the bishop, together with the holy [martyrs in Martyropolis](/saints/martyrs-of-martyropolis). He lived, the page says, in the time of the emperor Theodosius the First, the Great, whose reign it gives as 379 to 395, and he was a bishop of the Church of Christ; it does not say of where.

The emperor, valuing his virtues, sent him as ambassador to Shapur the Third, king of the Persians, whose years the page gives as 383 to 388. There Marouthas freed the king's daughter from the evil spirit that had taken hold of her and was tormenting her; and that success gave him the courage to ask the king of Persia for the holy relics of the martyrs who had suffered in Persia. The king granted the request at once.

So Marouthas built a city in the martyrs' name — Martyropolis, today Miefercin, which stood in Greater Armenia near the river Nymphaeus — and laid up their relics in it as its treasure. He fell asleep in peace on the day on which the anniversary of the dedication of Martyropolis was kept. The page adds one thing more: that he wrote many accounts of the martyrdoms of the martyrs of his own time, in the Syriac tongue, published by Assemani. Its couplet on him plays on one word twice over: Marouthas having loved God exceedingly, exceedingly, he stands before God and rejoices exceedingly, exceedingly.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him four sentences: the bishop of the city of Tagrit in Mesopotamia, famous for his faith and his goodness. He softened the Persian king Yazdegerd's anger against the Christians, the Prologue says, begged from him the relics of four hundred martyrs in Persia, and founded a city of its own for them, Martyropolis, where he laid those holy relics. In that city he finished his own earthly course in 422.
