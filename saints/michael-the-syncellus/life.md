# Michael the Syncellus

saint.gr's calendar for 18 December keeps the venerable Michael the Syncellus and Confessor, born in Palestine in 761 — or in 763, according to others. His forebears were Persians, and his devout parents dedicated him to the service of God; Theodore, patriarch of Jerusalem, tonsured him a reader in the church of the Resurrection, and he was educated at Jerusalem in the best way there was. At twenty-five he became a monk at the monastery of Saint Sabbas, and after twelve years of ascetic labour he was ordained presbyter by the patriarch Thomas, at the proposal of the abbot of the monastery.

Two years later he withdrew from the monastery into a cell, where he gave himself much to prayer; and the abbot of the Lavra entrusted to Michael the spiritual formation of the two brothers Graptoi, [Theodore](/saints/theodore-brother-of-theophanes) and [Theophanes](/saints/theophanes-of-nicaea). But the patriarch Thomas, having need of him, moved him with his two disciples to Jerusalem and made him his syncellus and his counsellor. Later he sent him to Rome on certain church matters, and by his hand delivered letters as well to the then emperor Leo the Iconoclast, whom Thomas was rebuking harshly for the persecution of the icons.

Enraged at it, Leo threw the patriarchal embassy into prison, where Michael stayed for a good many years, until Leo's murder. But after Michael the Stammerer came to the throne in 820, and the venerable man had been let out for a little, he was arrested again and imprisoned, and this imprisonment was prolonged through the whole reign of Theophilus as well. He was released by Theodora and her son Michael and was given back to the Church as a Confessor, bearing in his body the marks of his contests.

After the deposition of the iconoclast patriarch John, the vote of everybody for the succession went his way. But Michael, eighty years old by then and ill, refused to take the throne, and asked that the monastery of Chora be granted him so that he might rest. There he passed the rest of his years in peace, and when he died they buried him near the graves of the confessors — [Germanus](/saints/germanus-of-constantinople) the Patriarch and Theodore the Graptos — in the year 846.
