# Auxentius, companion of Eustratius

doxologia.ro prints one life for the five great martyrs of 13 decembrie, and this is it. Under Diocletian and Maximian the Roman power was full of the worship of the idols, and word came to the two emperors that Greater Armenia and Cappadocia were resisting their orders; so the persecutors they sent killed a great multitude of every age. [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca), a man of note in the city of Satala, first among his fellows in birth and in office — for he was of the rank of the commanders — was grieved in soul at what was being done to the Christians every day, and prayed and fasted for them; he wished also to enter the struggle of the saints and be counted worthy of their martyrdom, but, weighing the many torments and the cruelty of the tormentor, he was afraid.

He tested himself: he gave his belt to a trusted servant to carry to the priest, to see whether his confession would be received. In those days Auxentius was arrested with other Christians, questioned before the tribunal, tortured and thrown into prison in chains. When Lysias sat in judgement in the middle of the city, [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) went into the prison first and asked all those bound there for Christ to pray for him, because he too would share their struggle that same day; and they knelt and prayed, and followed him out.

Standing before the judge with the company of soldiers, he confessed; and Lysias, astonished at so unlooked-for a boldness, had him hoisted in the air by ropes and torn. He answered that the torments were doing him good against the tormentor's will, and that he knew himself now to be a temple of God and of the Holy Spirit. When the sores vanished from his body before the eyes of the crowd and [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-satala) spoke out for him, the tyrant, saying he had no more time for questioning, had them both loaded with chains and shut up with the other Christians, to be kept for another hearing.

Lysias moved them by night, and after two days they came to Arabraca, the home town of [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) and [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-satala). As the saints drew near, a citizen of the place, [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius), a simple and unlettered man, was sent out by his own wife — who asked him why he did not go the same road and choose the same portion — and, having kissed his sons and taken leave of her, and commended himself to a rich and honourable citizen named Mucaror, he ran after the saints, declared himself, and was bound with them and thrown into the common prison.

Then the tyrant gave sentence: Auxentius, who after many torments still stood by what he had chosen, was to die by the sword and to be denied burial. [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius), brought out next, asked [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) what he should answer, being a plain man and no scholar, and was told to say only, unchangeably, «I am a Christian, a servant of Christ» — which he said, and nothing else, to every question about his name, his trade, his country and his faith; so his ankles were bored through, he was hung head downwards and pricked all over with red-hot spits and burned, and gave up his spirit thanking God. [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-satala), brought out naked after him, called the judge a vessel of Satan, and had his tongue cut out, both hands struck off with the sword and his shins broken with staves, and so died too.

[Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) was a soldier, and was found out when Lysias took in his hand the cross that hung at his breast and questioned him. At Nicopolis and then at Sebaste the governor Agricolaus took over the case, with a letter from Lysias, and [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) argued with him out of Plato and out of the Scriptures on the justice and the incomprehensibility of God and on why God the Word took our sufferings upon himself, until Agricolaus fell silent for a long while and could not answer.

Then the governor had an iron bed brought and heated red; and [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius), hearing it, leapt onto it of his own accord, signed himself with the Cross, stretched himself out on the fire and cried, «Lord Jesus Christ, into your hands I give up my spirit», and died, and [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) answered Amen.

[Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) was taken back to prison, where he wrote out what he had to say, kept the day fasting and the night in prayer, and was visited by the bishop of Sebaste; he asked the bishop to serve the holy Sacrifice at the third hour of the morning, and communicated. When Agricolaus saw in the morning that nothing would move him, he had a furnace heated.

[Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) prayed, made the sign of the Cross of Christ, and went into the furnace singing, «Lord Jesus Christ, into your hands I give up my spirit»; and so he gave up his spirit in peace, and the fire did not harm his body nor touch a hair of his head. He was made perfect on the thirteenth day of the month of December, and the bishop of Sebaste took his relics and those of [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) and did what was written in the martyr's own account.

Of the five, Auxentius is the one already in prison and already tortured when Eustratius came in to ask the bound Christians for their prayers, and the first of the five to be sentenced, beheaded and denied burial; doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mare Mucenic Auxentie» and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps the five on the same day, and saint.gr gives Auxentius the longest speech of them. He was a priest and a fellow-townsman of Eustratius, and the governor pressed him to change his faith with many enticing promises. The worthy minister of Christ answered him: there is no need for me to say much, Lysias; in this life I am Christ's and I shall be his until death. And if you give me countless beatings and wounds, and if you melt me with fire and iron, my Christ is almighty and his Cross unconquerable. Auxentius in himself is weak; but the mind of Auxentius the Christian you will never bend. Enraged at the answer, the governor had him beheaded at once.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the five of them on the same menologion day under one heading, and it is the only one of the three calendars to say what he was: «Аксентије свештеник», Auxentius the priest. That is why he suffered first. The imperial governors Lysias and Agricola tortured him before any of the others, as a priest; and it was the sight of the Christians suffering innocently that brought [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca), the Roman commander of Satalion, to give himself up of his own accord.

Of the five, he was beheaded. [Eugene](/saints/eugene-of-satala) and [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius) died under torture, [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) breathed his last on red-hot iron, and Eustratius died in a fiery furnace. Their relics were afterwards taken to Constantinople and buried in the church of their own name, the Holy Five, where they were seen alive. Nothing was added to this folder's ranks on the strength of the one word priest, which only this book gives him.
