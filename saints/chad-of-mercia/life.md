# Chad of Mercia

saint.gr makes Chad the brother of [Cedd](/saints/cedd-the-bishop), bishop of London, whose memory it keeps on the seventh of January, and writes his name in Latin letters with two Greek renderings beside it. He studied at the monastery of Lindisfarne under the guidance of St Aidan, and was called by his brother to help him found the monastery of Lastingham, near the hills of Yorkshire; and when Cedd was elected bishop of London, Chad took over the abbacy of that house himself.

The king Alchfrith then called him to take the episcopal seat of his kingdom, and the ordination was performed by one bishop only. The archbishop Theodore of Canterbury did not recognise the election and ordered the canonical bishop of the region installed there instead; but because he was impressed by the humility and the holiness of Chad's life, he installed him as bishop of the local church of Mercia.

Chad fell asleep in peace in the year 673, during a great epidemic, and was buried in the church of the Panagia of Lichfield, where many wonders were worked at his tomb. His relics, the page says, are kept today in the cathedral of Birmingham in Britain. It gives no year for his birth, no account of his work as bishop, and no hymn.
