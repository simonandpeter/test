# Menander of Hermoupolis

Menander was born at Hermoupolis in Egypt and was a soldier, and saint.gr says he was martyred after being dragged naked over sharp stones, in the years of the emperor Julian the Apostate, 361 to 363. That is the whole of the narrative: one sentence, with a birthplace, a trade, a manner of death and a reign, and no year, no judge, no relic and no hymn.

The page adds one thing about how he is kept. In the Lavriotic Codex 170, it says, the memory of St Menander is carried in common with that of St Sabinus, on 16 March. That is a second day in a manuscript rather than in a calendar, and it is recorded here and in the attestation's note; the row itself holds the day whose page was read.

The calendar line calls him only «Άγιος Μένανδρος», with no epithet at all, so the city is what tells him apart, and the city is what his name carries here. The three numbered notes the page prints under the name — the comic poet of 342 to 290 before Christ who took the first prize eight times, an Athenian general of the Peloponnesian War, and a friend of Alexander the Great — are the site's encyclopaedia entry on the word, and none of them is this soldier. Its couplet turns the stones into a robe: «Γυμνὸν συρέντα τὸν Μένανδρον ἐν πέτραις, Στολὴν ὁ Χριστὸς ἐνδύει σωτηρίας» — Menander, dragged naked over the stones, Christ clothes with a robe of salvation.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 31 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/31/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/152/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
