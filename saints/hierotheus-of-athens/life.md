# Hierotheus of Athens

Hierotheus was a Platonic philosopher and one of the nine members of the council of the Areopagus, the Greek synaxarion says. Taught the faith of Christ by the apostle Paul, he was ordained the first bishop of Athens, and his disciple was [Dionysius the Areopagite](/saints/dionysius-the-areopagite), who praises his teacher in his writings. After many years of pastoral work and writing he died in peace in deep old age. His venerable head is kept in the monastery named for him at Megara in Attica, and relics of his are kept also on Mount Athos, at the monastery of St Paul, and in the chapel of St Andrew at the archdiocese of Athens. By one tradition he was present at the Dormition of the Theotokos in Jerusalem.

The entry sets beside this the view of M. Galanos, in his synaxarion, that Hierotheus cannot have been the first to learn the faith from Paul, since Acts says plainly that Dionysius was the first to believe at his preaching, and a figure so great — a member of the Areopagus, the first bishop of Athens — would hardly have been left inside Acts’ general words that others also believed; more likely he believed after Dionysius and was taught by him once Paul had left Athens. Either way, the entry concludes, he was a man of great learning who became the first bishop of Athens and worked for his flock with faith and much zeal. Its heading gives his death as 52.

The Romanian calendar calls him a hieromartyr, one of the counsellors of the Areopagus, taught the Christian faith and made bishop by the apostle Paul, and keeps him on the same day; the Greek apolytikion calls him hieromartyr too, though the Greek entry has him die in peace. The Greek page prints two apolytikia and a kontakion, the Romanian a troparion and two kontakia. The first apolytikion is the Romanian troparion, and the text the Areopagite’s page carries under his own name; the Greek kontakion is the Romanian first kontakion, though the Romanian asks to be cleansed of offences where the Greek asks deliverance from misfortunes, so each carries its own English.
