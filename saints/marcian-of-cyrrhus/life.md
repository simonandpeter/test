# Marcian of Cyrrhus

Marcian was born and brought up in the city of Cyrrhus. He set the things of the world at nothing and went away into the desert, where he shut himself into a very narrow cell with severe fasting and prayer. His manner of life won him the reputation of a saint, and a multitude of the faithful took refuge in his spiritual protection and sought peace and rest of him. He fell asleep in peace.

saint.gr names the source of that account: his life was written by Theodoret of Cyrrhus in the work called the *Philotheos Historia*. The calendar gives him no year of birth and none of death, and prints no hymn for him. The day page also carries a note that some synaxaristae mention him here superfluously and sends the reader to 2 November. That page has now been read, and the paragraphs below are what it holds.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 2 November, which is the day saint.gr sent the reader to, and it gives him the year and the narrative the Greek page had not: «Прп. Маркиана Киринейского (388).» It serves two lives of him under one name page, and they agree on the shape of his life and differ in what they notice.

The first has him live in the fourth century, withdraw into the desert and spend many years alone in unceasing prayer and strict fasting; he built himself a small cell, shut himself into it, and never lit a candle when he kept the night rule by the Psalter, because God lit the cell with a divine light. After a time he took two disciples, who settled beside him while he stayed in his enclosure. The patriarch of Antioch and other bishops asked him to leave that severe solitude for the good of the Christians, and he would not; but without leaving the cell he taught those who came to him for instruction, and turned many from the heresies to the Orthodox faith. Before the end he charged his disciple to bury him secretly and far from the cell, so as to escape posthumous fame and to leave nothing for the neighbouring churches to quarrel over.

The second life sets the desert expressly near the city of Cyrrhus, has the fame of his life gather others to him until he founded a monastery for them, and keeps two wonders: a snake that crawled into the cell and died scorched at the sign of the cross, and a heavenly fire that lit his reading at night. It closes him about 388 where the first closes him in 388 flatly, and the folder takes the printed year with the hedge recorded.
