# Simon of Volom

Simon was born in 1586 into a peasant family. One of the two readings the source prints calls him Simon Mikhailovich and says he worked as a tailor in his youth before devotion moved him to take the monastic habit in a desert monastery of the Georgian icon of the Mother of God on the river Pinega, where the strictness of his life won him the brotherhood's love. The other calls him Simeon in the world, the son of the peasant Michael from near Volokolamsk, and says he was tonsured at twenty-four at the Makaryev house on the Pinega after a long pilgrimage among the Orthodox monasteries.

Avoiding human praise, in 1613 he withdrew into the trackless Volom forest, eighty versts from Ustyug on the river Kichmenga, and lived there five years alone, eating the vegetables he grew and sometimes begging bread in a village. When lovers of silence began to gather to him he built a church in honour of the Cross of the Lord, by charter of the tsar Michael Feodorovich and the blessing of the metropolitan Varlaam of Rostov, and in 1620 was set at the head of the house he had founded.

He was killed in his own monastery on 12 July 1641 by robbers who demanded of him the tsar's charter for the land, meaning to break up the house. His body was buried with honour on the left side of the church he had built. His veneration began in 1646 after what was witnessed at his relics, and the life was composed in the seventeenth century.

The source grades him three ways and they are not the same. The day's line says «Прп.», venerable; the name page and the first reading are headed «Священномученик», hieromartyr, of a man who was never a priest in anything either reading says; the second reading and the day's kontakion say «преподобномученик», venerable martyr, which is what this folder keeps.
