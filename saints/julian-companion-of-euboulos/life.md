# Julian, companion of Euboulos

saint.gr keeps the holy martyrs Julian and [Euboulos](/saints/euboulos-the-physician) the physician on one page, and begins by saying whose they were: they were disciples of the venerable [Arcadius](/saints/arcadius-teacher-of-julian), the Cypriot ascetic whom the same day keeps. Coming to him and leaning on his words, the page says, they took care for their virtue and for their growth towards God.

They were seized by the idolaters in the reign of the impious Julian the Apostate, and after being tortured excessively they were beheaded. When the venerable Arcadius learned what had happened he grieved, but he gave thanks to God, who had arranged it so.

That is the whole of the notice. It gives neither man a country or a city of his own — Cyprus is their teacher's and not stated as theirs — no year of birth, no name for the judge, no manner of the torture, no burial and no relic, and it prints no hymn. The distich the site sets over the pair puts the words in this one's mouth and plays on his companion's name: come, let us die by the sword, says Julian to Euboulos, bringing in good counsel.

The corpus keeps a good many men of this name — of Tarsus, of Emesa, of Antinoopolis, of Samosata, of the Euphrates, and the rest — and none of them stands on this day or is named on this page.
