# Synesius of Triglia

saint.gr numbers him with the monks [Benedict](/saints/benedict-of-konstamonitou), [Timothy](/saints/timothy-of-konstamonitou) and [Paul](/saints/paul-of-ioannina) of the holy monastery of Konstamonitou, among the monks and plain Christians who were seized by Rubut Pasha in the country round Thessalonica and on the Holy Mountain immediately after the beginning of the Revolution of 1821, taken into the city, tortured without mercy and put to death by martyrdom. Its source for the life and the martyrdom, which it names, is the monk Dositheos of Konstamonitou's «Νέον Υπόμνημα των νεοφανών Ιερομαρτύρων και Οσιομαρτύρων», written probably between 1830 and 1844, a few years after the events.

Synesius came from the East, the page says, and his own country was the village of Triglia in the province of Prousa in Asia Minor. When he came to the age for marrying he left his own people and, full of zeal for the monastic life, went to the Holy Mountain. He went first to the monastery of Iviron, where his brother Theophilos and his uncle Gerasimos were monks; on their recommendation he came at last to the monastery of Konstamonitou. There he found a certain monk Nathanael, son of the steward papa Dimitrios of his own home town, who took him in hand.

The page's account of the end is the common end of the four: taken with the other fathers to Thessalonica, tortured, and martyred at last for their faith, in the year 1824. Its head line brackets him «(; - 1824)», and it gives him no year of birth. His apolytikion and kontakion, both printed on his page, name him and Triglia.
