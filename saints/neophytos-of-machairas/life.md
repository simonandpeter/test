# Neophytos of Machairas

saint.gr's calendar for 13 December keeps four founders of the monastery of the Panagia of Machairas in Cyprus together, and Neophytos is the first of them. The monastery stands at the eastern end of the Troodos range near the peak of Kionia, at a height of 870 metres, built on a pine-covered mountainside that falls away to the Pedieos torrent. It is called royal because it was built with royal help, and stavropegial because it became self-governing in church order, which the laying of a cross in its foundations signifies.

By an oral tradition the page reports, in the time of the struggle over the icons, which it dates 716 to 843, an ascetic brought to Cyprus from Constantinople the icon of the Panagia the Hagiosoritissa and settled in a cave at the place where the monastery now stands. After his falling asleep the icon was forgotten and brambles covered the mouth of the cave, until in the twelfth century the Mother of God gave a knife — μαχαίρι — to the venerable ascetics Neophytos and [Ignatios](/saints/ignatios-of-machairas), so that they could cut the brambles away and find the icon.

Of Neophytos himself the page says only that, and that he fell asleep before the monastery was built; the apolytikion the page prints calls him the foundation of the monastery. After his death another aged monk, [Prokopios](/saints/prokopios-of-machairas), came to Ignatios, and those two raised the house with the help of the emperor Manuel Komnenos; [Neilos](/saints/neilos-of-machairas) was its first enthroned abbot. The page gives no year for Neophytos's birth and none for his death.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 13 Δεκεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/12/13/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3252/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
