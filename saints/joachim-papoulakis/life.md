# Joachim Papoulakis of Ithaca

Joachim Papoulakis was born in 1786 in the hamlet of Kalyvia on Ithaca, in Greece, of devout parents, Angelos and Agnes. His mother died when he was very young and his father married again; the stepmother was jealous of the child John — John was the saint's baptismal name — and it is well known, the life says, that she tormented him. That made him learn patience early, and hide from her for hours together, reading the Scriptures and praying in a little chapel of Saint Spyridon near their house.

Later, following his father's example, the young John became a seaman. On one of his voyages he visited Mount Athos, and there, kindled with zeal for holy things, he stayed at the monastery of Vatopedi and became a monk, receiving the name Joachim.

In 1827 he came back to Ithaca and served in the church there until the end of his life. He went to the Lord in peace on 2 March 1868. He was canonized in 1998, and works many miracles to this day, as he did while he was still on earth. doxologia prints no hymn for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and fills in the parts the Romanian page passes over. saint.gr gives his name in the world as John Patrikios and his parents as Angelos and Agni, of the hamlet of Kalyvia on Ithaca; it says he lost his mother young, that his father married again and that his stepmother ill-used and tormented him, and that in those hard years he trained himself in patience and humility, taking refuge in prayer at the little church of St Spyridon and in the reading of the sacred books. As a youth he worked as a sailor on his father's boat, and won the crew's respect and esteem for his virtues and his bearing; on one of those voyages he found refuge on the Holy Mountain, and at the great monastery of Vatopedi he was tonsured and given the name Joachim.

When the Greek revolution broke out his abbot chose him and sent him as a preacher into the Peloponnese, where he taught, guided, supported and encouraged the Greeks; and in the boat of the Kephallonian papa-Yannis Makris he carried old men, women and children out of the Peloponnese to the Ionian islands, saving them from Ibrahim's raids. About the year 1827 he reached his own island, then under English rule, and for forty-nine years served among the people there, guarding them from sin, from error and from heresy. There are reports, the page says, of his being seen at prayer above the ground and flooded with heavenly light; God gave him the gift of discernment, and he became the counsellor of the Ithacans, their tutor in Christ and their physician. His skull is kept at Vatopedi and the rest of his relics at the church of St Barbara on Ithaca, and his formal recognition as a saint came in March 1998.
