# Theodore

Theodore Rostislavich of Smolensk, called the Black, was born in the years of
the Mongol invasion of 1237–1239 and baptised for the great martyr Theodore
Stratelates. His father died in 1240; his elder brothers divided the
inheritance and left the child the small holding of Mozhaisk, where he grew
up on Scripture, the services and the art of war. Marriage in 1260 to Maria,
daughter of the holy prince Basil of Yaroslavl, made him prince of Yaroslavl,
but she died soon after, leaving a son, Michael, and Theodore spent the years
that followed on campaign — in 1277 with the allied Russian princes and the
Tatars in the Ossetian land, where he distinguished himself and won the
favour of Khan Mengu-Temir. He was kept three years at the Horde, and when at
last he came home Yaroslavl would not have him: this is Princess Xenia’s
city, they said, and Michael is our prince. He went back to the Horde. The
khan’s wife, who was fond of him, wished him to marry her daughter — a thing
the khan long refused, since it meant treating a Russian prince as an equal,
and meant too that the girl be baptised first. She was, as Anna, and the
Orthodox Church in America’s life makes much of what followed: a Russian
prince seated opposite the khan, a palace built for him, and Orthodoxy
gaining ground at Sarai, where a Russian diocese had stood since 1261. His
sons [David](/saints/david-son-of-prince-theodore) and
[Constantine](/saints/constantine-son-of-prince-theodore) were born there.

In 1290 word came that Michael had died, and the khan sent Theodore back to
Yaroslavl with gifts and a retinue. He ruled it well, built up the
Transfiguration monastery he loved, stood by Andrew, son of Alexander
Nevsky, and in 1296, with war between the princes breaking out, used his
standing at the Horde to help the bishops of Vladimir and Sarai make peace.
A campaign to take back Smolensk from his nephews in 1297 failed, and soon
after he fell ill. On 18 September 1299 he had himself carried to the
Transfiguration monastery to be tonsured, and stopped the rite to be carried
out into the courtyard, where the people of Yaroslavl had gathered, to ask
pardon of everyone he had wronged and to forgive everyone who had wronged
him. He died in the night, as the bell rang for Matins and the brethren
sang the Psalter, having received the Mysteries; his face, the life says,
shone like the sun. On 5 March 1463 his relics and his sons’ were uncovered
in a single grave — a man of great stature, the chronicler wrote, with his
sons laid beside him — and his life was written soon after by the hieromonk
Anthony of the Yaroslavl Saviour monastery.

The Greek synaxarion keeps the three on 19 September and says it has no
details of these Russian saints; the Russian calendar keeps them the same day
of its own reckoning.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 September old style with his sons as the wonderworkers of Yaroslavl, on 5 March for the finding of their relics, and on 23 May among the saints of Rostov, with troparia and kontakia for the three together and two troparia and a kontakion for Theodore alone. Its life dates his birth about 1237–1239, and adds that in 1239, when the martyr Mercurius delivered Smolensk from Batu, the boy had been carried away and hidden; that his mother-in-law, Princess Xenia, brought up his son Michael; and that in 1296, when war nearly broke out between the princes, he and Grand Prince Andrew stood on one side and Michael of Tver and [Daniel of Moscow](/saints/daniel-of-moscow) on the other, until at the congress of princes at Vladimir the bishops Simeon of Vladimir and Ismael of Sarai made peace. After him, it says, his son David ruled Yaroslavl, and Constantine seems to have died earlier; and in 1322–1327 the Fyodorovskoye Gospel was written and illuminated in his memory for Bishop Prochorus of Rostov, who had been abbot of the Saviour monastery at Yaroslavl and may have seen his tonsure and his confession before the people.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, gives him alone a line: righteous and merciful, he took the schema before his death and died in 1298. That year stands beside the Russian 1299 in his dates.
