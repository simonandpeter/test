# Methodia of Kimolos

Methodia was born Irene on the island of Kimolos on 10 November 1865, the second of the five daughters of pious parents, Jacob Sardis and Maria, who also had three sons. From childhood she was drawn to divine things and was always at church. When she came of age she married a seaman so as not to grieve her parents, and her zeal for the Church was not lessened. On one of his voyages her husband was shipwrecked off Asia Minor and never came back to Kimolos.

Irene then became a nun at the church of the Panagia Hodegetria on Kimolos, and Methodius, then archbishop of Syros, gave her the name Methodia. Her ascetic labours were great and a living example to all, and the fame of her virtue spread so that crowds of women came to her for spiritual refuge and a harbour from the storms of life; her word was dew and healing to troubled souls, and God gave her the gift of working wonders as well. She died in peace on Sunday, 5 October 1908, at the age of forty-three.
