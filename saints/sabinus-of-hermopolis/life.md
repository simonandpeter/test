# Sabinus of Hermopolis

Sabinus's country was Egypt and his city Hermopolis, where he was a man of renown and a nobleman. In the reign of Diocletian, when the persecution had thrown all Egypt into confusion and many of the faithful were being seized, tortured and killed, he left his house, his rank, his wealth and his friends, went secretly out of Hermopolis, and hid in a distant village with other Christians who were fleeing the same persecution.

Shut up in a small poor house, he laboured in fasting and prayer day and night. The servants of the idols searched a long while for Sabinus the nobleman of Hermopolis, meaning to take him for torture, and could not find him, and were vexed at it. But a certain poor man who used to come to him and take food and all he needed from his hands followed Judas the betrayer: he went to them and asked what they would give him to show where Sabinus was, and they gave him two gold pieces.

They followed him to the village, saw the house, surrounded it and knocked. Six Christians were inside with Sabinus, and thinking that a brother had come to them on some errand they opened the door. The men sprang in with great hatred, seized them and bound them all, and Sabinus apart from the rest with two heavy chains, and brought him to the judge of that place, whose name was Arianus.

Brought to the pagan judgement and pressed hard to sacrifice to the idols, he would not deny Christ. For that they tormented him cruelly: they tore him with iron claws and burned him with fire, and at the last drowned him in a river, and so he finished the struggle of martyrdom. The six Christians taken with him were tortured likewise and won the same glory from the Lord.
