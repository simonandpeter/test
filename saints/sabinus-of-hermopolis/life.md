# Sabinus of Hermopolis

Sabinus's country was Egypt and his city Hermopolis, where he was a man of renown and a nobleman. In the reign of Diocletian, when the persecution had thrown all Egypt into confusion and many of the faithful were being seized, tortured and killed, he left his house, his rank, his wealth and his friends, went secretly out of Hermopolis, and hid in a distant village with other Christians who were fleeing the same persecution.

Shut up in a small poor house, he laboured in fasting and prayer day and night. The servants of the idols searched a long while for Sabinus the nobleman of Hermopolis, meaning to take him for torture, and could not find him, and were vexed at it. But a certain poor man who used to come to him and take food and all he needed from his hands followed Judas the betrayer: he went to them and asked what they would give him to show where Sabinus was, and they gave him two gold pieces.

They followed him to the village, saw the house, surrounded it and knocked. Six Christians were inside with Sabinus, and thinking that a brother had come to them on some errand they opened the door. The men sprang in with great hatred, seized them and bound them all, and Sabinus apart from the rest with two heavy chains, and brought him to the judge of that place, whose name was Arianus.

Brought to the pagan judgement and pressed hard to sacrifice to the idols, he would not deny Christ. For that they tormented him cruelly: they tore him with iron claws and burned him with fire, and at the last drowned him in a river, and so he finished the struggle of martyrdom. The six Christians taken with him were tortured likewise and won the same glory from the Lord.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and tells the same martyrdom with other details. saint.gr has him born at Hermoupolis of Egypt of a rich and aristocratic family, marked out for his blameless life and his deep faith; and when the emperor Diocletian ordered a relentless persecution, the eparch there, Arrianos, sought out Sabinus among the first. The Christians of the region, who counted him their support and their consolation, persuaded him to guard his life for the good of the church, and he was persuaded and hid with other Christians in a house outside Hermoupolis.

The emperor's soldiers found him out, arrested him and brought him to the ruler of the city, who quickly understood that he would get nowhere either by flatteries or by threats; and for that reason he ordered a stone bound to the saint's neck and the saint thrown into the waters of the Nile. So Sabinus gave up his spirit and received the crown of martyrdom, the page says, in the year 287 — a year earlier than the persecution it began with, which is the page's own reckoning and is left as it is.

The same calendar keeps a saint on 13 March whose entry is one sentence and a
bracket: «Άγιος Αβίβος από την Ερμούπολη», Abibus of Hermoupolis, who was
from Hermoupolis of Egypt and received the crown of martyrdom after they had
bound a great stone to his body and thrown him into the river. Two lines of
verse beside it say the same: «Βληθεὶς Ἄβιβος εἰς ποταμὸν σὺν λίθῳ, / Ἐκπλεῖ
ποταμὸν συρφετώδη τοῦ βίου» — cast into a river with a stone, Abibus
sails out of the muddy river of this life.

Then the bracket: perhaps, the page says, he is the same person as the holy
Sabinus the Egyptian, kept on 16 March. The hedge runs one way only — the
16 March page says nothing back — and the author ruled on 3 October 2026 that
the two are one man, so this record is both days. Beyond the city, the stone
and the river the 13 March entry has nothing to add: no year, no persecutor, no
relic and no hymn, and the name is the only thing in it that differs.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and prices the betrayal. He was an Egyptian of the city of Hermopolis and the chief man of that city. During one of the persecutions of the Christians he withdrew into a mountain with many other Christians and shut himself in a hut, where he passed the time in fasting and prayer. But a beggar who carried food to him, and to whom Sabinus had done great kindnesses, informed against him, and sold his benefactor for money — for two gold pieces. Sabinus was seized by soldiers along with six others, bound and driven to judgement; and after great torments he was thrown into the river Nile, where he gave up his spirit to God in 287 — a year inside the reign this folder already records, which is therefore left as it stands.

The Russian church keeps him on two days of its own, 13 and 16 марта ст. ст., and his service stands on the second of them, which is also the julian day the Serbian book keeps him on. The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery tells his story in four sentences and gives it the detail the Romanian page leaves out: he was the governor of the Egyptian city of Hermopolis, and in the persecution under Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305, he hid himself with those of his mind in a distant village.

It was a beggar who gave the hiding place away, for two gold coins — an ungrateful one, the page says, whom the saint had been helping with money and feeding all along. He was seized with six other Christians, and after torments all of them were drowned in the Nile, in the year 287. The 16 марта page prints a kondak for him and the 13 марта page prints none.
