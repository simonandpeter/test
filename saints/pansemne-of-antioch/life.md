# Pansemne of Antioch

Pansemne lived at Antioch and for twelve years was a harlot, dragging many young men into the mire. [Theophanes](/saints/theophanes-of-antioch), a righteous man of that city who had shut himself in a narrow cell outside it, was wounded in his heart both by her misfortune and by that of the men who admired her; and being granted light from God he took off his hair garment, put on a costly robe, got ten gold coins from his father on the pretext of a second marriage, and came to her house to offer her an honourable marriage. She agreed, and was given the earnest; but on his next visit he set his condition — that they could live together only if she believed in Christ and became a Christian. At first it seemed to her hard to fulfil, and she asked for time to think.

Seven days later it happened that Pansemne heard somebody speaking about the judgement to come. She learned that the righteous would enjoy the everlasting life and the blessings prepared for them, while sinners would receive everlasting punishment and torment. Wounded by the reproach of her conscience, the ground of her heart was worked by the plough of those words; and by the grace of Christ she went forward into deep repentance, letting her heart, which before had received the seeds of sin, rejoice in the cultivation of the Gospel. Knowing her sinfulness, she decided to renounce the old polytheism and to embrace the teaching of the Gospel.

Overcoming her pleasure and taking the perfect road of godliness, it was not long before she decided to enter the ranks of the new light-bearers, the catechumens, and received holy baptism. At once she freed her train of servants and maids; all the money gained by sin, the costly plate, the riches got by a disgusting trade, were given to the Church of God for a just distribution. Taking her leave of this world, Pansemne now gave herself to gaining that quality her name suggests, becoming wholly humble; and then the holy woman went to the cell Theophanes had built for her.

Set alight by the holy love born of the worship of the Holy Trinity, she longed to contemplate the beauty of the bridal chamber, Christ, the fairest of men. Putting aside every thought of any worldly husband, her longing and her deep desire were turned towards union with God. In that ascetic shelter Pansemne, Christ's spiritual fighter, threw herself into the race for the virtues. She watched over her thoughts, guarding her heart and her mind; by asceticism, the discipline of body and soul, she sought to be born again, a spiritual birth. Her labours drew down divine grace and gained her the victory: an abundance of divine power was poured out upon her, showing her worthy of the grace of driving out demons by her strong prayers and of healing various passions and sicknesses.

The hermit Pansemne lived only forty months after her baptism, and was taken to her Lord on the very day on which the righteous and wonder-working Theophanes was called to the company of his fathers. The page gives no year and no troparion.
