# Nicholas (Okolovich)

Nicholas was born on 4 May 1863 in the village of Uzhlyatino in the
Vitebsk district, a psalmist’s son, marked from childhood by deep faith in
the Lord and love of God’s house, bred in him by devout parents. After the
Vitebsk church school he entered the Vitebsk seminary in 1877 and finished
it in the first class; from 1883 to 1887 he studied at the Moscow Academy,
finishing as a master, and, of a peaceful disposition and an inborn tact
with those around him, the future hieromartyr had already earned at school
the nickname “Nicholas the peacemaker” — gifts that became the ground of
his service in the missionary preaching. By decree of Marcellus (Popiel),
bishop of Polotsk and Vitebsk, he was appointed anti-schismatic
missionary of the Polotsk diocese on 4 December 1887; in 1888 he married
Alexandra Mikhailovna Detsikevich, daughter of the archpriest Michael
Detsikevich, administrator of the Kholm diocese in 1871–1875, and they had
two sons and two daughters; and soon Bishop Marcellus ordained him
deacon, and on 2 February 1888 priest. From 4 March 1889 he was catechist
of the Polotsk cadet corps, and at the same time a member of the Polotsk
diocesan guardianship, chairman of the council of the Polotsk church
brotherhood and chairman of the district branch of the school council,
and on 15 August 1897 the Holy Synod gave him a certificate and a Bible
for particular zeal in the ordering of the local parish schools. At the
first missionary congress, held at Vitebsk in June 1901, he was elected
chairman unanimously and confirmed by Tikhon (Nikanorov), the future
hieromartyr archbishop of Voronezh, then governing the Polotsk diocese. A
grave illness forced a change of climate, and in 1907 he moved for a time
to the Voronezh diocese, where from 13 August 1907 to 1 July 1913 he was
rector of the Voronezh seminary and chief editor of the Voronezh diocesan
gazette, organised an observatory and biological and electrical stations
at the seminary, chaired the diocesan missionary council and took part in
fourteen cultural and charitable institutions; in May 1910 he took part in
the translation of the relics of Euphrosyne of Polotsk to her monastery,
and there met Metropolitan Anthony (Khrapovitsky), whom he saw again at
Kharkov; in 1911 on his initiative pedagogical and missionary courses were
founded at Voronezh for the parish-school teachers and parish clergy. A
writer of no ordinary gift, later editor of the Polotsk diocesan gazette
too, he wrote more than a thousand pages; the Voronezh missionary Leo
Kuntsevich wrote that under his chairmanship the missionary council had
worked fruitfully and with life, and regretted the departure of a worker
of extraordinary responsiveness and warmth, who had warmed the council and
made it one family working for the cause without divisions or parties.
Back at Vitebsk, in December 1912 he was made catechist of the Dvinsk
modern school, in today’s Daugavpils, and ended his thirty years of
teaching in May 1919 at Rechitsa, where the school had been evacuated from
October 1917; after the German occupation ended he left Rechitsa and lived
at Vitebsk from May 1919. The world war and the civil strife that followed
broke the church life of the Vitebsk country and of all Belarus —
fratricidal war, persecution of clergy, wrecking of churches and parishes,
the Renovationist schism, the destruction of the old peasant order; the
Vitebsk seminary was closed by the new power, but by the blessing of
Archbishop Innocent (Yastrebov) and under his direction a pastoral school
began at the St Nicholas cathedral, where he lectured on the history of
religions, general and Russian church history and fundamental, moral and
dogmatic theology, and travelled on mission to the places where sectarians
lived. In 1922 he became rector of the St Nicholas cathedral, and the
campaign for the seizure of church valuables soon reached it and the
Dormition cathedral and hospital church attached to it; keeping in his
heart the Saviour’s word not to fear those who kill the body but cannot
kill the soul, he strove with his wonted wisdom to avoid needless
bloodshed, and even got the seized liturgical vessels returned to the
Dormition cathedral so that its services could resume. But, as Basil the
Great said, unjust judges respect neither grey hairs nor the labours of
piety, and punish without proof of the charges: many clergy were
imprisoned, and in 1922 he too was arrested, held some weeks, and given a
suspended sentence of a year. Deprived of the cathedral, he served at the
Novinka church in the Polotsk district and as rector of the Komsha church
in the Nevel district until the autumn of 1924; in 1924 and 1927 the
Renovationist metropolitan Joseph (Krechetovich) and archbishop Alexander
(Shcherbakov) wrote offering him the episcopate and the Polotsk diocese if
he joined them, and he answered each time with a firm refusal, keeping
unspotted his loyalty to the Mother Church, the pillar and ground of the
truth. From October 1924 until his arrest in 1931 he was rector of the
Elijah church of Vitebsk; when the battalion church of St Nicholas was
closed in 1929 he took its priest Ignatius Iosafatov into his clergy and
its flock under his care, and from then, feeling the times growing harder,
served the Liturgy daily and never left off the prayer for the peace of
the whole world, supporting people, holding them back from despair,
turning their hearts to Christ and their eyes to eternity. A zealous
worker for the revival of closed parishes and the return of the strayed
from the Renovationist schism, he was dean of the city churches of Vitebsk
from 1929 to 1931, and by his efforts about forty parishes came back
under the patriarchal Church in that time, thousands gathering at the
services in the reopened churches; in 1929, at the personal instruction of
Metropolitan Sergius and with Archbishop Nicholas (Pokrovsky)’s blessing,
he drew up a memorandum for the locum tenens on the church-historical
importance of Vitebsk as a seat for an autonomous church administration
for Belarus. On 24 April 1931, at sixty-eight, he was arrested again for
anti-Soviet and anti-collective-farm agitation, and died in custody in
1934; his life bore out the apostle’s confession that neither death nor
life, nor things present nor things to come, nor height nor depth, can
separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord. The Russian
calendar keeps him on 17 August (Julian) and in the synaxes of the saints
of Belarus and Voronezh.
