# Mal

Doxologia.ro’s calendar for 16 octombrie names him «Sfântul Cuvios Mal», and the life on his page is two sentences long. It says that our most venerable father Mal left his property and his kinsfolk and lived in desert and uninhabited places, rejoicing in fasting, in vigils and in every other labour; and that his face was always cheerful.

He worked great wonders in the name of Christ, it says, cleansing lepers, driving off demons and giving sight to the blind; and he did these while still alive. After he had gone to the Lord and left his body on the earth, it received grace from God so that myrrh flowed from it with a very sweet fragrance, for healing and for protection from every danger and from incurable sicknesses — for, says the Lord, those who glorify Me I will glorify.

The page gives no country, no century, no year and no name for the place of his labours, and it prints no troparion. Nothing further is added here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 16 October, and gives him a character. saint.gr says that Mal was sensible and content with little in his young years, and stayed poor in matter so as to grow rich in Christ; that he was not of great education but of much faith and warm zeal; and that because he could not teach in public he preached to each man separately. He had great spiritual discernment, the page says, so that he never became heavy or tiresome or wide of the mark in those private counsels, for divine grace had armed him with a great delicacy and love which always told him what it was useful to say and what should be left out.

Afterwards he took up the life of the desert; but those who learned where he was would come and seek him at his hermitage, and he himself came down from time to time into the cities, not to make speeches but to scatter benefits, for the Lord had endowed him with the power of healing various sicknesses by a wonder. Many indeed found their cure through his prayers and by the laying on of his hands alone; and when he departed to the Lord his relic welled up myrrh and became a spring of healing for various illnesses.
