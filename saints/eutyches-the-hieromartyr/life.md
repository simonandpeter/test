# Eutyches, disciple of John the Theologian

Eutyches was a disciple of John the Evangelist — the synaxarion opens with John’s test of a true spirit, that every spirit which confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh is of God, and says that Eutyches proved it by his life.

He preached the Gospel bravely, pulled down many idol temples, endured beatings and spent many years in chains. He was thrown into fire, then to hungry beasts; one of the beasts spoke with a human voice, and he came through the rest of the torments unharmed, so that all were astonished. Because of these wonders they let him go free to his own country, Sebaste, where he gave up his spirit to the Lord in peace. His memory is kept again on 30 May.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep him on 24 August (Julian). The Sretensky calendar makes him a disciple of the apostles John the Theologian and Paul, of the first and early second century, from Sebaste in Palestine — Sebastopolis, the Prologue says: not counted among the Seventy, he was named apostle for his labours with the elder apostles, who made him bishop, and having heard the preaching of Christ he was first John’s disciple and then, meeting Paul, preached with him in many lands, a missionary bishop with an angel for companion who fed him the bread of heaven in prison. He was tormented with hunger, his body raked with iron — blood flowing from it with a strangely fragrant myrrh — thrown into fire and to the beasts; once a lion loosed on him struck all with terror by praising the Creator in a human voice. He finished his contest in his own city, beheaded at the beginning of the second century, and the Russian calendar keeps him with a troparion and kontakion.
