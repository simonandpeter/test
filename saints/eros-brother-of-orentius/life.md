# Eros

[Orentius](/saints/orentius-the-martyr), [Pharnacius](/saints/pharnacius-brother-of-orentius), Eros, [Firmus](/saints/firmus-brother-of-orentius), [Firminus](/saints/firminus-brother-of-orentius), [Cyriacus](/saints/cyriacus-brother-of-orentius) and [Longinus](/saints/longinus-brother-of-orentius) were brothers, famous for their courage, and served as soldiers in Thrace in the years of the emperor Diocletian, whom saint.gr dates in this place to 301. In some battle with the Scythians, Orentius managed to kill their hard and brave leader, Mararon. For that feat he was honoured — and in the same breath invited to share in the sacrifices to the idols that were to be offered for the victory.

The Christian hero refused outright, and declared that he acknowledged and worshipped one true God, the God of the Christians. For the sake of his bravery they did not punish him at once; but he and his six brothers were sent on an unwelcome transfer to Armenia. There, a little while later, all seven were put to interrogation, and with one mouth they declared that to their last breath they would stay faithful to the author of their salvation, who would be their judge on the day of the universal resurrection and recompense.

After that declaration they were condemned to exile in distant and hard places, and all seven died one after another of the hardships and the ill usage, without a murmur from any of them. The page gives each brother a couplet. Orentius comes alive out of the depth of the sea, ends on land and runs towards heaven; Pharnacius rises from the clay of earth to the floor of the Lord's house; Eros, who loved the beauty of the heavens, has passed over to them; Firminus and Firmus are called thrones of the mind, on which the King of the Angels is seated; and Cyriacus and Longinus God sets equal in honour with the angels, as being their equals.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the seven of them together on the Julian 24 June, **a day before** the Romanian and Greek calendars keep them, and names Eros the third named in its heading. They were brothers by birth, it says, and Roman soldiers in the time of the emperor Maximian — where the Greek account above has them soldiering in Thrace in the years of Diocletian. When the Romans were at war with the Scythians beyond the Danube, Orentius went out to meet the Scythian goliath Marot in single combat and killed him; that page calls the champion Mararon and puts the fighting elsewhere.

For that victory the whole Roman army was offering sacrifice to the gods, but Orentius with his brothers declared that they were Christians and could not offer sacrifice to deaf and dumb idols. For all their soldierly merits, this book says, they were condemned to exile in the Caspian regions, and on the way all seven, one after another, ended their lives of hunger and torments and passed over into the kingdom of Christ; the Greek page sends them to Armenia instead and has them die one after another of the hardships.

Both exiles are written here and neither is chosen. The other six are [Orentius](/saints/orentius-the-martyr), [Pharnacius](/saints/pharnacius-brother-of-orentius), [Firmus, brother of Orentius](/saints/firmus-brother-of-orentius), [Firminus](/saints/firminus-brother-of-orentius), [Cyriacus, brother of Orentius](/saints/cyriacus-brother-of-orentius), [Longinus, brother of Orentius](/saints/longinus-brother-of-orentius). This book gives no year of any kind.
