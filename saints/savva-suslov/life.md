# Savva (Suslov)

**Twenty-seven years a monk of Оптина and a tailor for fourteen years after it was shut.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him fourteenth of the sixteen it closes with 1937, among the five it calls «прмчч.», and carries no life for him; the life here is the article azbyka.ru prints for the civil 5 декабря out of fond.ru, which it hangs behind all five pages of this company. He was born on 20 September 1873 in the village of Каменева in the Успенская volost of the Ливны district of the province of Орёл, son of a peasant, Андрей Суслов, and named Сергий in baptism; his education was the village school. In 1900 he entered Оптина Пустынь and was at first put to the obedience of cutter in the vestry store; on 19 December 1910 he was tonsured to monasticism and set to the candle box, then made assistant sacristan, and in 1916 moved back to the vestry store.

**After the closing he lived in Козельск by his needle.** He settled there when the godless shut Оптина, earned his living by sewing, kept to prayer, and went with the rest of the brethren who had stayed in the town to the town church. He was arrested with the other Optina monks on 30 October 1937 and shut in the prison at Сухиничи, and questioned the same day.

**His answer about power is the one the article prints at length.** Told that the investigation had it that he had long been a monk of the Optina monastery, that after its liquidation he had done nothing useful, held church services in people’s houses and taken food from the collective farmers for nothing, and that the truth was required, he said he had served twenty-seven years a monk in Оптина; that after the liquidation in 1923 he moved to a lodging in Козельск, went about the houses at the tailor’s trade, and had been deprived of the vote until recently; that «монашество в государственной политической жизни не участвовало, почему я ничего и не знаю, что делается»; that he lodged with Авенир Синицын and Евтихий Диденко; and that his property had been taken for unpaid state taxes, though he could not remember in what year. Told that he had taken part in monastic gatherings and spread counter-revolutionary agitation, he answered that working in the collective farmers’ houses he had said «всякая власть от Бога» — all power is from God — that any power was good to him which did him no harm, that he had everywhere said there is a God and one must believe in God, and that against the Soviet power he had not spoken: «а держусь крепко за Божественные убеждения и призывал всех веровать». Asked why he had sabotaged the payment of state taxes he said he had no means to pay them: «я жил, не имея хлеба», I lived without bread.

**He is the one of the five who names the elders he knew.** Of the elders of the monastery and the skete, he said, he had known well «Иосифа, Нектария, Феодосия и Варсонофия» — the four the corpus keeps, of those, as [Joseph of Optina](/saints/joseph-of-optina), [Nectarios of Optina](/saints/nectarios-of-optina) and [Barsanuphius of Optina](/saints/barsanuphius-of-optina) — «Все они уже умерли, они служили при мне в монастыре, в Оптине», they were all dead already, and they served in the monastery in his time.

**Five men of one house, one lodging and one case.** The article is headed for all of them — «Преподобномученики Евтихий (Диденко), Авенир (Синицын), Савва (Суслов), Марк (Махров) и мученик Борис Козлов» — and begins with what 1937 was: the last period of the destruction of the Russian Church by a government of militant godless men, which reached the monks of Оптина still at liberty, living after the closing of their house in the town of Козельск, the nearest town to the monastery they loved. Three of them — Евтихий, Авенир and Савва — lodged in one house, each in his own cell with his own icons; Марк lodged elsewhere in the town and Борис at the town hospital where he worked. All four monks were arrested on 30 October 1937 and shut in the prison at Сухиничи; Борис had been taken two days earlier, on 28 October.

**The evidence against them is their neighbour and their landlady, and it is candid.** On 4 November the standing witnesses were questioned. The neighbour said the landlady kept the monks «на квартире специально, потому что они молились Богу», that they paid twelve roubles between them for the lodging while he, a working man, paid eighteen alone, that the four of them «ничем полезным не занимались, ходили по домам, распространяли церковный дурман», went to the church in a group and lived in a group in one lodging each with his own cell fitted out with icons — and that in October, when a pre-election meeting was held in the school building, the monks were invited and all of them flatly refused to go, which told on the other citizens. The landlady said they lived in her house, each in his own cell, and «монашествовали»; that one of them had argued in her presence «что раньше в монастыре жилось лучше, чем теперь при советской власти»; and that another had said «теперь всех сажают в тюрьму, потому что будут выборы советов и уже арестованы попы».

**The troika sentenced all five on 21 November 1937, and they were shot a fortnight later.** The article closes with the one sentence that makes this day their own: it pleased the Lord that on the very day of the feast of the Entry of the Most Holy Theotokos, 4 December, they should spend the day in prayer, in prison but still on earth, and that they were shot «на следующий день, 5 декабря 1937 года» — on the next day, 5 December 1937 — and buried in a common unmarked grave. 5 December 1937 is the Julian 22 ноября, the day both calendars keep them on; the ten other names of the same line were shot on 4 December, which is the 21st.
