# Nectarios of Aegina

He was born Anastasius, and because his family was poor he went to
Constantinople at fourteen to look for work. He had no money for the
passage; the captain of the boat told him to take a walk and come back, and
the boy understood what that meant and walked sadly away. Then the captain
gave the order to start the engines and nothing happened, and after several
attempts he looked up and met the eyes of Anastasius standing on the dock.
He took pity and called him aboard, and the engines started at once and the
boat moved.

In Constantinople he found work with a tobacco merchant who paid him very
little. Wanting to give others something worth having, he copied short
maxims out of spiritual books onto the shop's paper bags and packages, so
that customers reading them out of curiosity might take some good from them.

He was made Metropolitan of Pentapolis, and died in 1920. This is the
Russian calendar’s entry for his principal feast, 9 November of the old
style; the same saint stands in this corpus a second time as [Nektarios of
Aegina](/saints/nektarios-of-aegina), the Greek and Romanian calendars’ 3
September for the translation of his relics, and it is there that his whole
life, from Selymbria in 1846 to Aegina in 1920, is told.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 9 November. saint.gr gives his name in the world as Anastasios and his birth as 1 October 1846 at Silivria in Eastern Thrace, the fifth of the six children of a poor and devout family, of Demosthenes and Maria Kephala; its own head line for him reads «Αγιος Νεκταριος Μητροπολιτης Πενταπολεως Αιγυπτου (1846 - 1920)». It also says where he is now: his skull and most of his relics are kept at the monastery of the Holy Trinity on Aegina, one ankle bone at the shrine of his name at Kamariza of Lavrio, and one vertebra at the shrine of his name at Chania in Crete.
