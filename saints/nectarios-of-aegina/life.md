# Nectarios of Aegina

He was born Anastasius, and because his family was poor he went to Constantinople at fourteen to look for work. He had no money for the passage; the captain of the boat told him to take a walk and come back, and the boy understood what that meant and walked sadly away. Then the captain gave the order to start the engines and nothing happened, and after several attempts he looked up and met the eyes of Anastasius standing on the dock. He took pity and called him aboard, and the engines started at once and the boat moved.

In Constantinople he found work with a tobacco merchant who paid him very little. Wanting to give others something worth having, he copied short maxims out of spiritual books onto the shop's paper bags and packages, so that customers reading them out of curiosity might take some good from them.

He was made Metropolitan of Pentapolis, and died in 1920.
