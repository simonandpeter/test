# Eusignius of Antioch

Eusignius was of Antioch by birth, and was a soldier by rank under the emperors of Rome Diocletian and Maximian, then under Constantius Chlorus, and then under Constantine the Great and his sons. He was a companion in conversation of the martyr Basiliscus and the writer of his passion, in the reign of Maximian; and when Basiliscus was beheaded, Eusignius and the other believers who were there were counted worthy to see a multitude of holy angels take the martyr's soul and carry it up to heaven, and to see our Lord Jesus Christ standing in heaven and receiving the soul of the sufferer.

In the reign of Constantine, being with him in the army, he saw together with the emperor the cross figured in stars, and took good courage against the enemy, armed with the power of the holy Cross. He served sixty years as a soldier and left the military order in the reign of Constantius, the son of Constantine, because he had grown old; and returning to his own country, Antioch, he lived according to God in prayer and fasting, always running to the churches of God.

He lived on into the reign of the lawbreaker Julian, hated of God, and when that pagan emperor came to Antioch, Eusignius was taken in this way. One day, going to church, he passed two idol-worshippers quarrelling over some matter, and they stopped him and said: We know you for an honoured man who has been long in the army and knows right judgement; hear our case and judge us justly. He weighed their case as he ought, and found one of them in the right and the other at fault; and the one at fault, in anger, went to the emperor and accused Eusignius of being a Christian.

The emperor had him seized at once and set before his judgement seat, and the saint, standing before the tyrant, rebuked him without fear for his lawbreaking — that having denied Christ he had worshipped demons and carried the honour due to God over to the idols, not following the great Constantine. Then he began to praise the faith and piety of Constantine, telling from the beginning how he had seen the cross in the sky, how by its power he had overcome his enemies, and how, casting off the madness of the idols, he had joined himself wholly to Christ and enlightened not himself only, with faith and baptism, but the whole world.

So, blessing the great Constantine with many praises, Eusignius reproached Julian, the man far from God, for his godlessness, and mocked him with words of reproof. Julian, unable to bear hearing them, ordered the head of the soldier of Christ cut off. So Eusignius ended for Christ as a martyr, being a hundred and ten years old, and made his dwelling in the life that does not grow old, where his years shall not fail for ever.
