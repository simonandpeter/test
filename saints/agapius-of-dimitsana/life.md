# Agapius of Dimitsana

Agapius the presbyter, Asimakis Leonardos in the world, was a distinguished man of letters and a church writer. He was born at Dimitsana in 1740, saint.gr says, and fell asleep at Argos in 1812. He learned his first letters in his own country — probably at the school of Philosophou, which stood near Dimitsana — and later at Tripoli, where he heard the lessons of the priest-teacher Parthenios. In 1759, at nineteen, he left for Constantinople, meaning to reach the Holy Mountain, where Eugenios Voulgaris was then teaching; but he ended at Smyrna instead, and studied at the Evangelical School of that Ionian city. There he was tonsured a monk and took the priestly name Agapius.

In 1764, with his countryman the learned hieromonk Gerasimos Gounas, he founded the school of Dimitsana, which ran in its first period until 1770. That year great persecutions and savage terror broke out in the Peloponnese in reprisal for the Greek population's part in the Orlov rising; bands of Albanians overran the Morea, plundering and laying the country waste, and Dimitsana was among the towns destroyed. Its school closed, and Agapius took refuge in Zakynthos while Gerasimos went to Smyrna.

From Zakynthos he crossed to Parga and taught there. In 1780, when relative quiet was restored in the Peloponnese, he returned to Dimitsana and took up his teaching again in the school as it was re-established; the next year he was called to direct the Evangelical School, though he did not stay long in that post either. Between 1783 and 1786 he visited the Holy Places, and from then he bore the by-name Hatzi-Agapios, and missionary work became the chief purpose of his life.

He visited, from time to time, Thessaly, Macedonia, Thrace, the Holy Mountain, Asia Minor, Palestine, Arabia, Egypt, Epirus, the Peloponnese and the islands. In 1812 he came back to the school of his birthplace, then withdrew to Argos, where he died. saint.gr prints no hymn for him.
