# Anna, renamed Euphemianos

saint.gr's calendar for 29 Οκτωβρίου keeps «Οσία Άννα η μετονομασθείσα Ευφημιανός» — Anna, who was renamed Euphemianos. She was born in Byzantium of devout parents, the page says, and her father, John by name, was a deacon at the church of the Theotokos at Blachernae. She was left an orphan early, and her grandmother married her to a devout deacon, by whom she had two children.

Her husband and both children died, and Anna divided what she had among the poor and withdrew into a monastery. Then the iconoclast Leo the Isaurian — about the year 716, the page says — broke up the monastery she belonged to. In that necessity she put on men's clothes and entered a men's monastery under the assumed name Euphemianos, and there she lived with great care and exactness.

After the death of Leo the Isaurian she put on women's clothes again and remained as a nun in Byzantium, where she gave herself to the service of the poor and the sick; and in such work, beloved of God, she gave up her spirit to the Lord. The epigram above the notice plays on the disguise: Christ, it says, makes the modest Anna, hidden in a man's dress, man enough against the demons.

The page prints no hymn, and gives her no year of her own — the only year on it is the one for Leo's dissolution of her monastery. It carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps her on this same day, prints the monastic name in brackets after her own — «Прп. Анны (Евфимиана) (826)» — and in two short lives gives everything else this folder knows about her. She was the daughter of a deacon of the Blachernae church at Constantinople. After her husband's death she put on a man's monastic dress and lived as a monk under the name Euphemianos in one of the Bithynian houses near Olympus, which is why the calendar names her Anna of Bithynia; and her son John was there with her. She was known for the gift of wonders while she was still alive, and she died at Constantinople in 826. The second of the two lives is titled for the two of them together, mother and son; the calendar's own line for the day names her alone, so she alone has a folder here, and that the son is commemorated with her on the page is recorded rather than acted on.

The calendar keeps her a second time on 13 июня ст. ст., which her own life page already named, and that day's line names her son beside her — «Прп. Анны (826) и сына ее Иоанна (IX).» — with a name link of his own. He is therefore kept in the corpus as [a folder of his own](/saints/john-of-bithynia), where the reader of 29 октября had written that no folder was made for him because that day's line gave only his mother. Her row stays on 29 октября, the schema keeping one feast to a church, and the June day stays recorded in that row's note; nothing on it was changed.
