# Dionysius the Philosopher

saint.gr's calendar for 10 Οκτωβρίου names «Άγιος Διονύσιος ο φιλόσοφος» and, instead of a life of its own, prints his entry from Perantonis's Λεξικό Νεομαρτύρων whole, in the lexicon's own older Greek. It calls him Dionysius the Skylosophos, metropolitan of Larissa — a most learned hierarch and a philosopher, whom the Turks mockingly called «σκυλόσοφος», the dog-philosopher. Seated at Trikke, and urged on by the Duke of Nevers, who had been recognised by many hierarchs as having hereditary rights to the throne of the Palaiologoi, he raised the Christian people against the Turks in 1600 and armed them, with no military preparation, on faith in freedom and religious zeal.

After that rising failed he fled to the West, pursued by the Turks. Setting out later from the monastery of Saint Demetrius of Dichouni, which lies between Paramythia, Karasovo and Radovitsi, he broke into Ioannina in 1611 and besieged the government house. When that rising too was put down he hid in a cave on the islet in the lake of Ioannina. Betrayed and seized by the Turks, he endured frightful tortures, his soul unenslaved and faithful to the Christian faith. They flayed him alive, stuffed his skin with straw and carried it round various cities; and when they brought it to Constantinople and showed it to the Sultan, he shuddered at the pitiable sight and rose from his throne.

The lexicon does not stop there, and the page prints the rest of it. His contemporaries criticised him so far that the Patriarchate declared him deposed from the throne of Larissa; and that, the lexicon says, probably accounts for the absence of any mention of this man, wronged by history, in the synaxaria. It adds that mention of him is lacking from the local hagiology of the metropolis of Ioannina and of Larissa, from the calendar of the Church of Greece, from the Great Euchologion and from the Hagiology of Sophronios Eustratiades — and that nevertheless this martyr is honoured as a saint in Epirus for his contest in Christ. All of that is recorded here as the page records it.

saint.gr gives the year of his death in its head line as «(; - 1611)» and prints no hymn for him. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.
