# Nicodemus (Shchapkov)

Nicodemus, in the world Nicholas Shchapkov, was born on 20 October 1852 in Vologda province, the son of Constantine Ivanovich Shchapkov, a townsman of Ustyug, and his wife Tatiana Lvovna. In his twenty-first year he left home for the remote Stefano-Ulyanovsky monastery of the Trinity in Vologda province, where he spent almost ten years in obedience, the shoemaker’s among them, before the monastic life: he became a novice of the monastery in 1883, took the habit on 7 August 1886 with the name Nicodemus, was ordained hierodeacon a year later and hieromonk on 25 May 1896, at forty-four. A year after that he was moved to the Koryazhma monastery of St Nicholas, won the respect of its brethren, and took an active part in its revival; in 1903 he was confirmed as its treasurer, confessor and dean.

After October 1917 a commandant sent by the new authorities lived in the Koryazhma monastery. On 23 September 1918 an illiterate villager told the Solvychegodsk Cheka that suspicious people were hiding in the monastery, and on 30 September the military commissar and members of the commission came there, demanded that its administration hand over every hiding place and the money, and recorded the refusal as resistance to the Soviet power. The commandant wrote all the answers on one sheet: [Paul](/saints/paul-moiseev) said no to every question and that nothing more was in the monastery; Nicodemus admitted nothing; [Seraphim](/saints/seraphim-kulakov) said he had been in charge of nothing; and [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-sobolev) said he took no part in the monastery’s affairs and lived apart. The commission counted Seraphim, who had no part in the monastery’s business, and the retired Theodosius among its administration, and ordered the four shot as ringleaders of a hostile band. No money was found, and without waiting for the sentence to be confirmed they were shot at two in the afternoon of 30 September 1918, on a deserted island in the Vychegda on the road from the monastery: Theodosius in his seventy-seventh year, Paul in his seventieth, Nicodemus at sixty-five and Seraphim at forty-five.

The Russian calendar keeps the four on 17 September old style.
