# Leontios the Myrrh-gusher

Leontios came to the monastery of Dionysiou in 1537 and struggled there beyond nature for sixty-eight years, never once leaving the house. His cell was a small cave beside the monastery's water cistern. Full of the grace of the Holy Spirit, he received the gifts of foresight and of clairvoyance: doxologia quotes his life as saying that, being full of these divine gifts, he saw clearly the thoughts and the deeds of those who came to him, and that they, learning from the saint the hidden things of their own hearts, went forward in the struggle, and many of them were saved.

He knew his end beforehand and fell asleep in peace after a short illness on 16 March 1605. After his burial, when his honourable relics were being moved, the brothers saw with their own eyes that whole body pouring out holy myrrh, and for that he was called the Myrrh-gusher.

His biographer, who wrote the life not long after his repose, is unknown; the life is kept in a manuscript of the monastery of Dionysiou, itself a reworking of the original. The page adds that his day of commemoration is 18 June, which is not the day of his repose.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 18 June, and saint.gr's account adds a good deal to the Romanian one and disagrees with it in one particular. The Life of the venerable Leontius the New, it says, survives in manuscript 677, folios 1 to 36, of the monastery of Saint Dionysius on the Holy Mountain, written towards the end of the eighteenth century and itself a translation of an earlier text from the beginning of the seventeenth. He was born in 1520 at Argos in the Peloponnese, according to Saint Nikodemos the Hagiorite and the tradition of the fathers of Dionysiou; of his parents and childhood nothing is known, though the page infers that they were devout, since at seventeen, in 1537, he was already living in stillness in a small monastery of the Argolid, having chosen the monastic life.

Having left parents, kinsmen and friends, he gave himself to fasting, to prayers, to the disciplines of the body, to the study of the divine Scriptures and to works pleasing to God. In 1537, when the Turks began their attack on Nauplion, Leontius prophesied that the castle would fall into the hands of the Hagarenes, which came about in 1540; and after Nauplion was taken he fled to the Holy Mountain, which he came to love greatly, and asked to be made a monk at the monastery of Saint Dionysius. They set him two conditions for receiving him: that he would never leave the monastery, and that he would take on any obedience laid upon him.

He settled in the coenobium of Dionysiou and for sixty whole years did not go out of the monastery, and so was counted worthy of the gift of insight and prophecy. After his death his holy relics welled up myrrh, as the priest Malaxos, the protopapas of Nauplion, also relates; and he reposed in 1605 at the age of eighty-five. On the years in the monastery the two sources differ: doxologia has him enter Dionysiou in 1537 and struggle there sixty-eight years, while saint.gr has him reach the Mountain only after the fall of Nauplion in 1540 and keeps sixty. The year of death, 1605, is the same in both, and saint.gr adds that a portion of his relic is at the monastery of Zerbitsa near Sparta.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 18 June, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep him on, and it is the third book to give the year 1605 for his repose. It calls him a Greek of the Peloponnese by origin, where the Greek page named Argos in it, and says he struggled in the monastery of Dionysiou for sixty years — which is saint.gr’s count and not doxologia’s sixty-eight, so two of this folder’s three books now read sixty. It gives his age at death as eighty-five years, which is a lifespan and goes nowhere near the dates.

And it tells one thing neither of the other books tells. In those sixty full years, it says, he went into the monastery once and came out of it once: in when he first entered Dionysiou, and out when they carried him out dead to bury him. He had the wonderful gift of clear sight and of foretelling, and after his death myrrh flowed from his relics.
