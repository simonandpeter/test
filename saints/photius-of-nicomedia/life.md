# Photius of Nicomedia

Photius was the nephew of [Anicetas](/saints/anicetas-of-nicomedia), a military dignitary of Nicomedia who denounced the emperor Diocletian for setting up instruments of execution in the city square to frighten the Christians, and who came unharmed through the beasts, the wheel, the fire and the cauldron of boiling tin. Photius greeted the sufferer, and turning to the emperor said: idolater, your gods are nothing — and the sword raised over him struck the executioner himself.

Both were imprisoned; three days later Diocletian offered them glory and riches if they worshipped his gods — perish with your honour and your riches, they answered — and had them tied by the feet to wild horses, which dragged them unharmed; a red-hot bath-house fell in without hurting them. At last he had a huge furnace lit, and many Christians, heartened by the two, walked into it of their own will crying, we are Christians, and died with prayer on their lips; the bodies of the two martyrs were untouched by the fire, even their hair, and many pagans believed. This was in 305. The Prologue tells it likewise for the Serbian calendar, and notes that the two are invoked in the prayers at the blessing of oil and of water.
