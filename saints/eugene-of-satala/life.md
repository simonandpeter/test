# Eugene of Satala

doxologia.ro prints one life for the five great martyrs of 13 decembrie, and this is it. Under Diocletian and Maximian the Roman power was full of the worship of the idols, and word came to the two emperors that Greater Armenia and Cappadocia were resisting their orders; so the persecutors they sent killed a great multitude of every age. [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca), a man of note in the city of Satala, first among his fellows in birth and in office — for he was of the rank of the commanders — was grieved in soul at what was being done to the Christians every day, and prayed and fasted for them; he wished also to enter the struggle of the saints and be counted worthy of their martyrdom, but, weighing the many torments and the cruelty of the tormentor, he was afraid.

He tested himself: he gave his belt to a trusted servant to carry to the priest, to see whether his confession would be received. In those days [Auxentius](/saints/auxentius-companion-of-eustratius) was arrested with other Christians, questioned before the tribunal, tortured and thrown into prison in chains. When Lysias sat in judgement in the middle of the city, [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) went into the prison first and asked all those bound there for Christ to pray for him, because he too would share their struggle that same day; and they knelt and prayed, and followed him out.

Standing before the judge with the company of soldiers, he confessed; and Lysias, astonished at so unlooked-for a boldness, had him hoisted in the air by ropes and torn. He answered that the torments were doing him good against the tormentor's will, and that he knew himself now to be a temple of God and of the Holy Spirit. When the sores vanished from his body before the eyes of the crowd and Eugene spoke out for him, the tyrant, saying he had no more time for questioning, had them both loaded with chains and shut up with the other Christians, to be kept for another hearing.

Lysias moved them by night, and after two days they came to Arabraca, the home town of [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) and Eugene. As the saints drew near, a citizen of the place, [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius), a simple and unlettered man, was sent out by his own wife — who asked him why he did not go the same road and choose the same portion — and, having kissed his sons and taken leave of her, and commended himself to a rich and honourable citizen named Mucaror, he ran after the saints, declared himself, and was bound with them and thrown into the common prison.

Then the tyrant gave sentence: [Auxentius](/saints/auxentius-companion-of-eustratius), who after many torments still stood by what he had chosen, was to die by the sword and to be denied burial. [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius), brought out next, asked [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) what he should answer, being a plain man and no scholar, and was told to say only, unchangeably, «I am a Christian, a servant of Christ» — which he said, and nothing else, to every question about his name, his trade, his country and his faith; so his ankles were bored through, he was hung head downwards and pricked all over with red-hot spits and burned, and gave up his spirit thanking God. Eugene, brought out naked after him, called the judge a vessel of Satan, and had his tongue cut out, both hands struck off with the sword and his shins broken with staves, and so died too.

[Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) was a soldier, and was found out when Lysias took in his hand the cross that hung at his breast and questioned him. At Nicopolis and then at Sebaste the governor Agricolaus took over the case, with a letter from Lysias, and [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) argued with him out of Plato and out of the Scriptures on the justice and the incomprehensibility of God and on why God the Word took our sufferings upon himself, until Agricolaus fell silent for a long while and could not answer.

Then the governor had an iron bed brought and heated red; and [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius), hearing it, leapt onto it of his own accord, signed himself with the Cross, stretched himself out on the fire and cried, «Lord Jesus Christ, into your hands I give up my spirit», and died, and [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) answered Amen.

[Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) was taken back to prison, where he wrote out what he had to say, kept the day fasting and the night in prayer, and was visited by the bishop of Sebaste; he asked the bishop to serve the holy Sacrifice at the third hour of the morning, and communicated. When Agricolaus saw in the morning that nothing would move him, he had a furnace heated.

[Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) prayed, made the sign of the Cross of Christ, and went into the furnace singing, «Lord Jesus Christ, into your hands I give up my spirit»; and so he gave up his spirit in peace, and the fire did not harm his body nor touch a hair of his head. He was made perfect on the thirteenth day of the month of December, and the bishop of Sebaste took his relics and those of [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) and did what was written in the martyr's own account.

Of the five, Eugene is the man of Satala who spoke out for Eustratius in the middle of the hearing and was chained with him for it, and who lost his tongue, his hands and his shins; doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mare Mucenic Evghenie» and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps the five on the same day, and of Eugene saint.gr says one sentence: the officer Eugene, after they had cut out his tongue and cut off his hands and broken his legs, breathed his last. The page calls him an officer, as it calls Eustratius a senior officer and Orestes a soldier, and it adds nothing else of his life; the apolytikion it prints names him last of the five. It reports that portions of his relics are kept at the monastery of the Dormition at Mikrokastro of Siatista and at Kykkos in Cyprus, and the greater part of them at the church of the Holy Trinity and the Five Martyrs at Polystylo of Kavala.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the five of them on the same menologion day under one heading, and it gives him a station and takes away a city. He was the comrade in the army of [Eustratius](/saints/eustratius-of-arabraca) — so a soldier, which no other calendar calls him and which has not been written into his ranks on one word of one book — and the Satalion this folder is named for is, in this book, the city Eustratius commanded, Eugene being given no city at all.

His one act in the entry is the confession. While Eustratius was being tortured he stepped before the judge and cried out: Lysias, I too am a Christian. He died under torture, as [Mardarius](/saints/mardarius) did; [Auxentius](/saints/auxentius-companion-of-eustratius) was beheaded, [Orestes](/saints/orestes-companion-of-eustratius) breathed his last on red-hot iron, and Eustratius died in a fiery furnace. Their relics were afterwards taken to Constantinople and buried in the church of their own name, the Holy Five, where they were seen alive.
