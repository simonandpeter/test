# Auxentius of Kartilio

The venerable Auxentius was born on Andros at the beginning of the eighteenth century. He served first as sacristan of the church of Christ the Saviour at Galata in Constantinople, and was then ordained deacon. As a deacon he settled in the seaside town of Katirli in Nicomedia and lived as an ascetic on Mount Katirlion on the Propontis. The holiness of his life and the gift of wonderworking that the Lord gave him brought many to him for his blessing and for healing.

Patriarch Cyril V of Constantinople had him as his spiritual father. When Cyril, by a synodical decision, laid down that Roman Catholics, Protestants and Armenians coming over to the Orthodox faith were to be rebaptised, Auxentius supported him in his preaching and became the leader of the party that held for the baptism of the heterodox. When Cyril was driven from the throne, those who prevailed pursued Auxentius too — in vain: the orator Kritias, sent to Katirlion, was driven out by the Christians of the town.

His synaxarion relates that he would throw into the sea the worn cloak he wore and use it for a boat, to cross the open water; so he reached Constantinople, having stilled the storm by making the sign of the honourable Cross over the sea. He fell asleep in peace, and his memory is kept with splendour at Stavros in Chalkidiki, where a portion of his relic was brought in 1922 by the refugees uprooted from Asia Minor, and at the church of the Forerunner at Katirli in Kalamaria.
