# Nicholas (Simo)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him on 5 апреля ст. ст. in four words, «Сщмч. Николая пресвитера (1931).», and carries no life for him; the name link behind the line, page 9382, gives the surname and the rank the line leaves out, «Николай (Симо), протоиерей, сщмч.», and lists beside this day only the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs on 26 января. azbyka.ru’s calendar for the same civil day has the life, signed игумен Дамаскин (Орловский) out of «Жития новомучеников и исповедников Российских ХХ века. Апрель», Тверь 2006, and it adds two further days of its own: the moveable synaxis of the saints of the St Petersburg metropolia and, on 1 декабря, the synaxis of the saints of the Estonian land. Both are synaxes and neither is written into the feast.

He was born on 6 December 1875 in the town of Аренсбург in the province of Estland, the son of a priest, «Адама Симо»; in 1888 his father was appointed to the Estonian parish newly opened at the «Андреевский собор» in Kronstadt. In 1887 he entered the Riga spiritual school, finished it in 1891 in the first class, and went on to the Riga seminary; in 1894 his father moved him to the seminary of St Petersburg. Finishing in 1897, he married «девицей Лидией», the daughter of a priest, «Павла Панова», and on 23 November 1897 was ordained priest to that same cathedral in Kronstadt, in his father’s place.

He was allowed to serve there only once a week, on Sundays, between the first liturgy and the second. The unfavourable circumstances in which the priest and his Estonian flock found themselves brought him to the thought that a separate church was needed for the Orthodox Estonians living in Kronstadt, of whom there were then about eight hundred. In 1902 the owners of the building of the former Anglican church resolved to sell it with its garden and its house, on condition that it be used as a church, with a reduction in the price for whoever met the condition; and a Kronstadt merchant and benefactor decided to buy it for the Estonian parish.

The clergy of the cathedral opposed the undertaking energetically, having no interest in a second church so near their own, and appealed for support to [John of Kronstadt](/saints/john-of-kronstadt) — **who did not support them**. On 4 July 1902 the building was sold to the merchant, and on 7 July he handed it over to the Estonian parish; and on 1 December 1902 John of Kronstadt consecrated it as an Orthodox church in honour of the Exaltation of the Cross. He served the liturgy after the consecration with nine priests and five deacons, in Russian and in Estonian. At the dinner the merchant gave for the consecration the dean of the Estonian parishes of the diocese, «священник Павел Кульбуш», said that they were celebrating the triumph of a good Russian heart.

Made rector of the new church, he gave himself wholly to his flock and began, after John of Kronstadt’s example, to serve every day — which the cathedral clergy did not like, and they complained of it to the elder himself. John of Kronstadt asked him once whether it was true that he served daily, and hearing that it was, wished him to go on being as zealous for the service of God. Besides the services he taught the law of God in the Kronstadt school at the customs house and in the Estonian parish school, of which he was also the head, and he led the Kronstadt branch of the «Александро-Невское Общество трезвости» and sat on the committee of the Kronstadt guardianship of popular sobriety.

His wife died in 1910 and left him three children between three and seven years old. He went the way of a zealot for Orthodoxy, and his service came in time to win the growing respect of the Kronstadt parishioners, and not of the Estonians only but of the Russians too. When one of the cathedral priests died in 1917 and the vacancy had to be filled, it was being practised then, after the manner of antiquity, to appoint priests by the election of the parishioners with the diocesan bishop’s confirmation after; three candidates were proposed, three hundred and eighty-five parishioners voted, and three hundred and seventeen of them voted for him. The dean wrote of him that he was known in Kronstadt as a good and modest pastor.

On 13 December 1917 the metropolitan of Petrograd appointed him a priest of the cathedral; in 1919 he was raised archpriest, and in 1923, the rector having died, made rector of it. He was arrested first in March 1921, on suspicion of taking part in the Kronstadt rising, and released a fortnight later, nothing criminal being findable in what he had done. When the renovationists tried to seize the church’s government he showed himself an active opponent of the movement: at a meeting of the cathedral’s board of twenty called to discuss it he declared that the renovationist current and the Living Church with it were heretical, and showed it from the new rules they had brought in against the canons, the second marriage of clergy among them.

When a representative of the renovationists came from Petrograd proposing a general meeting of the boards and the clergy of all the Kronstadt parishes, he and the cathedral clergy said that they would not go to a meeting arranged by a Living Church priest. Asked by the rector of another church how one should regard it, he answered that the cathedral’s priests would not follow the renovationists; and told that in that case the civil authorities might arrest them, he said: «И пусть арестовывают, у меня котомки уже готовы» — let them arrest us, my bundles are ready.

He was arrested on 13 October 1930. Sixty-four people were drawn into the case, admirers of John of Kronstadt and zealots of the monarchy, with their centre in the cathedral, which the authorities held to have become a centre of counter-revolutionary activity among the clergy. He gave the OGPU none of the information it wanted, and when the investigators went on insisting he declared at last that he refused to give any further testimony. While he was under investigation the OGPU began arresting those who disagreed with the declaration of the deputy locum tenens and saw in it a hypocrisy unworthy of a primate, and for convenience bundled them into an all-union counter-revolutionary organisation called «истинно-православные».

One such case was made in Leningrad, and on 14 February 1931 his case and those of some other priests and laymen of Kronstadt were joined to it. He did not change his position under the new investigation and refused to give the OGPU any information whatever. The indictment said that the leaders of the centre were extremely monarchical elements, in the past the closest associates of the elder of Kronstadt himself, who by open propaganda carried on a furious anti-soviet agitation with the aim of undermining and overthrowing the soviet power, and that at the head of the organisation stood the priest of the cathedral. On 13 April 1931 the collegium of the OGPU sentenced him to be shot; he was shot on 18 April 1931 and buried in an unmarked grave.

**18 April 1931 is the civil date of the Julian 5 April**, so this calendar keeps him on the day he died. azbyka prints a troparion for him in глас 4 and no kontakion, and that troparion is the Church’s own statement of the thing his whole life turns on: that he was a concelebrant of John of Kronstadt, and that in the godless times he did not deny his cross but was faithful to the Lord to the shedding of his blood. Two footnotes on the page identify two other men as saints — the dean of the Estonian parishes, afterwards bishop of Reval, and the dean who commended him, both hieromartyrs with days of their own — and neither is given a row here, a speech at a dinner and a letter of commendation being neither of them a companionship.
