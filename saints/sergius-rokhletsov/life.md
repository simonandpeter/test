# Sergius (Rokhletsov)

Sergius Rokhletsov was born in 1876 into the family of a priest, Dmitry Rokhletsov, who served in one of the cathedrals of Veliky Ustyug. After finishing the theological seminary he was ordained priest to the church of the great-martyr Paraskeva Pyatnitsa, and he served there the whole of his life. Before the revolution he taught the Law of God in the parish school of the village of Chizhkovo.

The Pyatnitsa church stood thirty kilometres out of Veliky Ustyug and not far from the monastic cell of Maxim Yugov, an ascetic well known in those parts. For his spiritual bent and his zeal in serving, the elder chose Father Sergius for his own confessor, made his confession to him until his death and charged him with his burial. Father Sergius, who honoured the elder in his turn, blessed his parishioners to go to him for counsel, and saw with his own eyes the people who had received healing there.

The church stood away from any dwelling, in the middle of a field, and its parish was a handful of thinly settled villages, so that sometimes a good number came to the service and sometimes nobody at all. However many came, Father Sergius served every day, helped by a woman who read the psalms and a woman who kept the altar. For his long and blameless service he was raised to the rank of archpriest and given the mitre.

He was arrested on 4 December 1937 and shut up in the prison at Veliky Ustyug. Questioned, he did not admit his guilt. Asked whether he confessed to having carried on active counter-revolutionary activity, he answered that he did not admit himself guilty of carrying on counter-revolutionary activity. On 10 December 1937 a troika of the NKVD sentenced him to ten years in a corrective labour camp.

Archpriest Sergius Rokhletsov died in the prison at Veliky Ustyug on 8 May 1938 and was buried in the city cemetery in a common grave. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for the veneration of the whole Church at the Jubilee Council of Bishops of the Russian Orthodox Church in August 2000. The calendar's line for the day names him together with a woman confessor, and the day page carries no hymn under either name.
