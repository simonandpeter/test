# Victor of Damascus

In the reign of Antoninus, emperor of Rome, there was a soldier of Italian birth named Victor, serving under the commander Sebastian, who believed in our Lord Jesus Christ and confessed His most holy name before everyone. When the persecution of the Christians began, the commander called him and said that letters had come from the emperor ordering that the Christians be brought to the sacrifices of their gods and that those who would not obey be tormented with heavy punishments; so Victor should sacrifice, and not fall under the torments and lose his life and his soul.

Victor answered that he would not bow to the command of a mortal emperor nor do his will, for he had the immortal King, his God and Saviour Jesus Christ, whose kingdom is without end, and those who do His will have eternal life, while those who do the will of a mortal emperor, whose kingdom is for a time, perish for ever. Told that he was a soldier of their emperor and should obey, he said that he was no longer a soldier of their earthly emperor but of the heavenly one; that although he had served a while under their emperor's authority he had never ceased serving his own King, and would not leave Him now nor sacrifice to their idols. Do what you will, he said: my body is in your hands, but over the soul only my God has power. Warned that he was putting himself in danger, he answered that this was the very thing he wished, to bear torments for his Lord.

With him suffered [Stephanida](/saints/stephanida), a woman of the people and the wife of one of the soldiers, who saw two crowns coming down from heaven, one to Victor's head and one to her own, and blessed him aloud for it. When the tyrant had made an end of her, he ordered Victor to be cut down with an axe.

Hearing the sentence, Victor gave thanks to God; and as they were about to cut off his head he prophesied the death of his tormentors, saying that after twelve days they too would die, and after twenty-four days their commander would be taken by his enemies. Having said this he prayed and bowed his head, and it was struck off with the axe; and after the stroke milk ran out mixed with blood. Many of the unbelieving who saw that wonder believed in Christ, and the more so when they saw the prophecy fulfilled, for those who had tormented him perished by a sudden death and the commander fell into his enemy's hands.

So Victor suffered together with Stephanida in the city of Damascus, on the eleventh day of the month of November; and now, the life says, they dwell both together in that city which needs neither sun nor moon to light it. The page gives no year; it places him by the reign of Antoninus and by nothing else.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 11 November, and sets him in a century and a country. saint.gr puts him in the martyric choir that watered the life-bearing tree of the Christian faith with its blood in the second century after Christ, when the emperor was Antoninus, which it dates to 160; and it says that the stage of his services for the Gospel was Italy, where Victor went about to various cities sowing the word of salvation — not Damascus, which the Romanian calendar's name for him gives. He was arrested for it and pressed to sacrifice to the idols, and because he did not bend they put out his eyes and hung him head downwards, and so he gave up his brave and holy soul. The page's distich says there was nothing fearful in Victor before the sword, since he had put all cowardice far away from his heart.

The Russian calendar keeps the two of them on one line, «Мч. Виктора и мц. Стефаниды (II).», and serves one life for both. In it Victor is a soldier in the reign of the emperor Marcus Aurelius the Philosopher, which the page dates 161–180. When the emperor began to persecute the Christians Victor refused to sacrifice to the gods — and the page says what that refusal was, that the obligatory sacrifice was the test of a soldier's loyalty to the gods, to the emperor and to his country.

He was given over to torture and came through all of it unharmed. By the strength of his prayer he overcame a sorcerer, who from then on gave up his spells and became a Christian; at his prayer soldiers who had been struck suddenly blind saw again. Seeing the wonders the Lord worked through him, the young and devout wife of one of his torturers openly glorified Christ, and for that she was put to a savage death: they tied her to two palm trees bent together, which straightened and tore her apart.

Victor was beheaded. The two suffered at Damascus in the second century, the page says, and there their honourable remains were buried. **The day page prints one kontakion for three of its martyrs together** — «Кондак мучеников Виктора, Стефаниды и Викентия», tone 4 — and it is therefore in all three folders. It names none of them in its body, which is why it can serve three; it is not a common of martyrs either, since it is printed under their three names and not under a class. The last clause as the page prints it reads «да омут непобедимую победу», where «омут» stands for «имут», and it is copied as printed: a reading that looks wrong is the source's and not the scrape's.
