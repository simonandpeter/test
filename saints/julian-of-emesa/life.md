# Julian of Emesa

doxologia.ro gives him five short paragraphs, and this is what they say. Julian came from the Phoenician city of Emesa and suffered in the year 312 under the emperor Maximian. He was a skilled physician, and he healed not only the diseases of the body but those of the soul, turning many people to faith in Christ the Saviour.

When the holy martyrs who had been imprisoned by the persecutors of the Christians were led out to be eaten by the wild beasts — the bishop of the city, [Silvanus](/saints/silvanus-of-emesa), the deacon [Luke](/saints/luke-of-emesa) and the reader [Mocius](/saints/mocius-of-emesa) — Julian encouraged them and urged them not to fear death for the Lord's sake.

He too was arrested, tortured for eleven months, and condemned to death. His head, his hands and his feet were pierced with long nails. He was left almost dead, but managed to reach a cave, where he gave up his soul into the hands of the Lord. A church was raised over the cave in which he died, and during repairs in the year 1970 his holy relics were found there.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and names the three men whose condemnation drew him in. In the years of the king Numerian, in 284, the idolaters seized Silvanus bishop of Emesa, Lucas the deacon and Mocius the reader, whom saint.gr keeps on 29 January, and the governor ordered them torn apart by wild beasts. Julian, who was not present at the sentence but heard of it, ran with all his strength to reach them, and learning at the court that they had already been taken to the amphitheatre he ran to them there and kissed them as a brother before the soldiers could stop him. That act was treated as a crime, and it was judged that those kisses must draw death after them. He was not frightened by his persecutors' verdict, and the executioners drove nails through his head, his hands and his feet, and then into his head again. The page's couplet sets his end beside the Cross: Christ was pierced with nails in the hands and the feet, and Julian adds the head as well.
