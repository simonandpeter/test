# Aristocleus of Tamassos

Aristocleus was a priest in the town of Tamassos on the island of Cyprus. During the persecution of the emperor Maximian Galerius, which doxologia's life dates 285 to 305, he hid himself in a cave in the mountains. After some time he learned how the Christians of the city of Salamis were being tormented, and he left the cave where he was hiding and went in among them to give them heart.

On the road he stopped at the church of the Holy Apostle Barnabas. From there he set out again in the company of two Christians, Demetrian the deacon and Athanasius the reader, who also wished to be among those suffering for our Lord Jesus Christ. All three received a martyr's death for the confession of faith in Jesus Christ.

That is the whole of what the Romanian calendar prints for him: the town, the island, the persecution and its years, the cave, the church of Barnabas, the two companions and the end. The calendar's line names him alone, so his companions are not given folders of their own here; and the page prints no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 23 June, and names two companions with him whom the Romanian calendar did not: [Demetrianus the Deacon](/saints/demetrianus-the-deacon) and [Athanasius the Reader](/saints/athanasius-the-reader). saint.gr says all three were Cypriots and lived during the persecution of the Church under Maximian and Diocletian, whom it brackets 245 to 310, and that in the year 302 Aristocleus, who came from Tamassos, at first did not want to put his own life in danger, because he wished to go on with the spiritual building up of his brothers in Christ; so he went up a mountain and hid in a cave. But from there he kept learning that in the cities many of the faithful were boldly pouring out their blood for the holy faith, and after a vision he saw he judged that it was not permitted to him, priest that he was, to be in safety while laymen, women and children defied the swords and the fire and watered the tree of the Gospel with their blood. So he made the sign of the cross and went down to Salamis of Cyprus, and in the church of the city he found the deacon and the reader at prayer. They prayed together, and after taking counsel all three went out and strengthened their persecuted brothers. As soon as the pagan governor learned of it he arrested them, and when he failed to overcome them with promises and with threats he beheaded them after many tortures.
