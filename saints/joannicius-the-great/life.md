# Joannicius the Great

Joannicius was born in the parts of Bithynia, in the village called Marikat, to Miritrichius and Anastasia, and as a boy he kept his parents' cattle. He was never given over to the learning of letters, yet the Romanian life says he understood what life is better than any of those who studied, because he sought the commandments of the Lord. He was meek, humble, patient and very obedient. Often he would leave the herd for a whole day and pray apart, signing it first with the cross; and it was never scattered, nor taken by beasts, nor stolen, until he came back in the evening to drive it home.

In those years Leo the unbelieving, son of Constantine Copronymus, sent men through his dominion to pick out strong young men for the army, and in Bithynia they took Joannicius and enrolled him. He was terrible to the enemy for his courage and loved by those who served with him for his meekness. But he was drawn into the heresy that fought the holy icons, so far that he would not hear of them. Returning from a campaign in the West, he passed by Mount Olympus, where a hermit who saw before him came out of his cell and said: if you are called a Christian, why do you overlook the icon of Christ? All the labours of your good works are in vain without the right faith.

Joannicius marvelled that a man who had never seen him called him by name and laid bare what he believed, and he fell to the ground asking pardon, for he had sinned in ignorance, and promised to give the honour due to the icon of Christ and to the images of all the saints. From then he punished himself with fasting, lying on the ground by the royal doors, keeping vigil the whole night and never eating to the full; six years he spent so. When the Bulgarians rose with all their strength and took Thrace, he fought before the emperor's own eyes with such courage that he was written in the book of the brave, and he cut down a Bulgarian whom the life likens to Goliath.

Then he saw Mount Olympus again, and the rest of his life belongs to it and to the monasteries about it. The life tells of the temptation two hermits had foretold for his old age. Epiphanius, out of envy, set fire to the thickly wooded mountain to destroy him with his cell, and God kept him unburnt as He kept the youths in Babylon. Joannicius was neither angered nor grieved, but went to him in humility to ask the cause of his anger and beg forgiveness; and Epiphanius struck him in the belly with a staff shod with sharpened iron, and he took no hurt from it, exactly as the two hermits had said.

Come to deep old age, his body spent with labours, he went to the monastery of Antidion, made himself a little cell and shut himself in it; and when he did walk out on the road he was not seen by those who wished to see him. In the fifth year of the reign of Michael, [Patriarch Methodius](/saints/methodius-of-constantinople) came with his clergy for a last prayer and blessing, and Joannicius, after teaching the right belief to those who had come with him, foretold that Methodius would follow him out of this life without delay. Three days after the patriarch left, Joannicius reposed, on the fourth day of November, ninety-four years old; and eight months after, on the fourteenth of June, Methodius died, and the prophecy was fulfilled.

The fathers living on Olympus saw, as he died, a pillar of fire rising to heaven with angels going before it and opening the doors of paradise. Many sick were healed at his relics afterwards, many were freed from unclean spirits, and the paralysed rose from their beds at the touch of his reliquary.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 4 November, and does not spare him. He was born in Bithynia in 740, saint.gr says; his father was called Myritrikis and his mother Anastaso, both devout, and they brought their son up by the commands of the Gospel. When Joannicius was conscripted the emperor was the harsh iconoclast Constantine V, who had distinguished himself in the wars against the Bulgars and stood high with his soldiers; and the frame of mind cultivated on the battlefields carried Joannicius onto religious ground with it, so that he became an iconoclast like the emperor. When he was discharged from the ranks, however, he was not slow to understand his error and the great faults it had led him into; he repented sincerely and confessed his lapse, and having been instructed accordingly he became a monk on Olympus, and died at ninety-four in the monastery of Antidion, teaching the world Orthodoxy.
