# John of Damascus

The city of Damascus in Syria brought forth John of believing parents of good family, whose right faith in Christ was dearer to them than gold tried in the fire. The time was a bitter one, for the Saracens had enslaved that country and taken the famous city, doing much evil to the Christians, killing some and selling others into slavery, so that they let no man glorify Christ openly. John's parents, covered by God's providence, were kept whole in health and in goods and kept their faith entire; for God had given them to find favour, as Joseph once found it before the Egyptians and Daniel before the Syrians, and the Agarenes did not hinder them from believing in Christ and magnifying his name openly.

They even made John's father judge of the city and governor over the offices of the people; and in that happy place he did much good to his brethren in the faith, buying back the enslaved, freeing the imprisoned from their bonds, delivering men from death and giving a hand to those in the worst troubles. So the saint's parents were in Damascus, among the people of the Agarenes, like a lamp in the darkness, a seed in Israel and a spark in the ashes, kept by God so that out of them should come the lit lamp of the Church of Christ. They bore John by nature and laboured to bear him also by grace, which in that time was very hard, for the Agarenes let no one be born of water and of the Spirit; yet his parents bore their son by baptism without hindrance, naming him John, the name of grace.

As the child grew, his father took care that he be raised well and taught not the ways of the Saracens, nor soldiering, nor hunting, nor any other human craft, but meekness, humility, the fear of God and the understanding of the divine Scriptures; and he prayed God earnestly to send him a wise and right-believing man to be his son's teacher. God heard him, and he found the treasure he desired: the blessed elder Cosmas, who took both boys — for John had a foster-brother, also called Cosmas — and taught them with all diligence. They were quick of mind, and in time the teacher said to the father that his wish was fulfilled, for the children had learned so well that they now surpassed him in wisdom; and though the father grieved to lose so honoured and wise a father, he did not dare to keep him by force, and let him go as he wished.

In that time Leo the Isaurian reigned over the Greeks, who rose against the Church of God like a beast, casting the holy icons out of the churches; and John laboured to run through the whole world, if not on his feet then by his God-inspired books, which, being spread through the Greek empire, strengthened the right-believing. Then the helpers of wickedness sought out and found among the faithful a letter written in John's own hand, and got it by cunning and gave it to the emperor, who had another letter written in imitation of it, as though John were offering to betray Damascus, and sent both to the lord of the Saracens in Damascus by a man of his own.

The lord of the city, reading them, called John and showed them to him; and being filled with anger, he ordered the innocent man's right hand to be cut off. John begged him to wait a little and give him some time to prove his innocence, but the hand was struck off, and he was carried home weak with pain and with the flowing of much blood. In the evening, thinking the barbarian's anger cooled, he sent to ask for his severed hand; and taking it, he went into his chamber of prayer and fell to the ground before the holy icon of the Most Pure Mother of God, who held her Child in her arms, and set the cut hand to its place and prayed with many tears. He fell asleep, and saw in a dream the icon of the Most Pure Mother of God looking upon him with bright and merciful eyes and saying: "Behold, your hand is whole; grieve no more."

He did not hide the wonder but proclaimed it with a loud voice, saying that his Lord, the almighty physician, through his Most Pure Mother had heard his earnest prayer and healed his hand with his own almighty hand; and his household and neighbours heard and wondered greatly, and word came quickly to the lord of the city, who cried out that he had judged unjustly and punished a righteous man without mercy, listening to a lying slander, and begged his forgiveness. Returning to his house, John at once gave away all his property, which was very great, to the poor, and freed all his slaves, and went to Jerusalem together with Cosmas.

He had the blessed Cosmas, who had grown up with him and learned with him from the same teacher, urging him to the labour of writing the divine books and composing the hymns of the Church, and helping him in it; and Cosmas was afterwards ordained bishop of the city of Maiuma by the patriarch of Jerusalem. That same patriarch then called John and ordained him priest; but not wishing to stay long in the world and be praised by laymen, he returned to the house of Saint Sabbas and hid himself in his cell like a bird in its nest, labouring at the reading and the writing of the divine books and caring for his salvation. He gathered up all the books he had written before and read them again, correcting with much attention whatever seemed to him to need correcting, in the words or in the composition, so that nothing in them should be without use; and he spent many years in such labours, useful to himself, to the Church of Christ and to the monastic struggle. Coming at last to perfect holiness and pleasing God well, he departed to Christ and to his Most Pure Mother.
