# Cuthburga

Cuthburga — Cuthburge — was the sister of King Ine of Wessex and was married
to Aldfrith, crowned king of the Northumbrians in 685; at her suit he let
her remain a virgin always and give herself to her heavenly Bridegroom in
the monastery of Barking in Essex. Afterwards she founded the house of
Wimborne in Dorset and governed it, giving herself wholly to fasting,
watching and holy prayer, humble before God and man, meek and tender to
others and always austere to herself, and she never ceased to exhort her
sisters to live up to the dignity of brides of the King of heaven, to keep
their hearts free from every affection for the things of this world, and to
sigh always after their heavenly home. Purified by a long and painful
illness and strengthened with the viaticum of the precious body of Christ,
she passed to everlasting bliss on 31 August in the beginning of the eighth
century. She is commemorated in the Sarum Breviary; Butler’s sources are
William of Malmesbury on King Ine, Leland, Harpsfield, Alford and Cressy.
The Greek synaxarion keeps her on 31 August among the saints of Britain with
no details of her life, and Butler on the same day.

*After Alban Butler’s Lives of the Saints (1866), volume VIII, August — [St. Cuthburge, Queen, Virgin, and Abbess](https://www.bartleby.com/lit-hub/lives-of-the-saints/volume-viii-august/st-cuthburge-queen-virgin-and-abbess), read 6 September 2026; the Greek synaxarion’s line (saint.gr, 31 August) was all this entry had until then.*
