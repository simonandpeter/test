# Anthousa of Rome

The Russian calendar names her among the Arian persecutions of the day it keeps her on — the same day carries the sixty-two priests and the three hundred laymen who suffered from the Arians in Africa — and it gives two readings of her under one heading. The shorter of the two says only this: she suffered for Christ at Rome in the fifth century, and because she would not accept Arian baptism she was burned.

The fuller one adds a husband, a baptiser and the name of the woman who pressed her. She was the wife of a Roman official and was baptised by [Ambrose of Milan](/saints/ambrose-of-milan); and when the wife of the city's governor, Sunilda, put to her the offer of Arian baptism, she refused it, and she was burned.

The two claims do not sit easily together, and the calendar does not notice it: Ambrose died in 397 and the pages put her in the fifth century. Both are written down here as the source has them, and nothing is chosen between them; the century the calendar's own line prints is what this folder records. Beyond that the pages give no year, no place of burial, no relic and no hymn — the day's four hymns stand under two other names.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 8 декабря ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261208.html) and [the lives](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/id2404.htm); read 13 October 2026.*
