# John the Long-Suffering

Nothing is recorded of John's birth or early life. He appears in the *Paterikon*
of the Kyiv Caves already a monk, and the whole of his story there is an account
of a single struggle carried on for thirty years against desire.

The measures he took were extreme even by the standards of the collection that
records them. He fasted for long periods, wore iron on his body, spent a winter
buried to the shoulders in a cave, and passed whole nights standing in prayer
before the relics of Anthony of the Caves. The text does not present this as
straightforwardly admirable so much as a report of what he judged necessary.

He is said to have died around 1160 and to have been buried in the near caves,
where he had spent most of his life. The tradition that keeps his memory names
him for the endurance rather than for the victory —
[Moses the Hungarian](/saints/moses-the-hungarian), whose relics lie in the same
caves, is remembered on similar terms.
