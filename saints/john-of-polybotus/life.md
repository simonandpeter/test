# John of Polybotus

John lived at the end of the seventh century and the beginning of the eighth, saint.gr says. From a child he stayed pure and unstained all his life; he was temperate, compassionate, generous in alms, and a knower of the holy Scriptures. So he became first a reader, then a subdeacon, then a deacon, and afterwards a presbyter; and in that office he was distinguished for the systematic preaching of the divine word and for the practicality of his sermons.

Later, by the common judgement of people and clergy, he was raised to be bishop of Polybotus — an ancient city of Asia Minor in Phrygia Salutaria, near Synnada, the page explains, whose ruins survive at today's Bolvadin near Afyonkarahisar. From that place John was a sleepless and tender shepherd.

At the time when the emperor Leo the Isaurian made war on the holy icons, bishop John was distinguished for his Orthodox convictions and his courage, in spite of the violence he received from the imperial officers. And once, when the Agarenes at Amorion of Phrygia had carried off a number of captives from his flock, John did not hesitate to go to their camp and ask for them. They refused; but heavy sicknesses began to fall on the camp, and then by his prayer John managed to stop the evil, and the Agarenes in return set his bishop's flock free.

The end of this worthy hierarch was quiet and peaceful, and his honoured relic became the cause of many wonders. The page gives him no year of birth and none of death, and no hymn; its couplet says that John, having left behind a bishopric of earth, delights now in the sight of the One who oversees all things.
