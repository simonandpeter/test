# Leucius of Vruntisiopolis

days.pravoslavie.ru’s calendar for 20 июня ст. ст. names him «Свт. Левкия исп., еп. Врунтисиопольского (V).» and gives him a long life of its own, read whole here. He was born at Alexandria of devout parents, Евдикий and Евфросиния, who called him Евтропий. His mother died when he was eleven; his father was tonsured in the monastery of «святой Ермий» and took the boy with him into the house, where he was brought up under the abbot Никита and the experienced elders. He proved very able and learned the Scriptures thoroughly, and grew up quiet, meek and obedient.

When he was eighteen the abbot died and the brotherhood chose him unanimously, though he had not yet been tonsured; thinking himself unworthy to govern anybody, he refused, and the monastery stood seven years without a superior while he laboured and came to a high degree of the spiritual life.

At twenty-five he went with his father to the feast of the Dormition at a church outside Alexandria, where a bishop of Heliopolis was keeping the festival with his clergy. In the night it was revealed to the father that his own end was near and that his son would be a bishop and would enlighten a city and a country in Italy; and in the same vision the son was told his new name, «Левкий», which the page glosses as «Прииде на него Дух Господень» — the Spirit of the Lord came upon him. At the feast the bishop heard a voice from heaven blessing Leucius for the work of a chief shepherd, and had his archdeacon ask which of those praying bore that name.

At the monks’ insistence the bishop ordained him and set him over the house. From that time he increased his labours and was given the grace of working wonders and of driving out demons: when a demon in the shape of a huge serpent had killed many in a village nearby, he came to the people’s help and freed them from it, and about three thousand pagans of that country were baptised at the sight. When the bishop of Alexandria died a martyr, Leucius was chosen in his place.

Seeing him turn many pagans to Christ, the prefect resolved to kill him; some of the Christians wanted to kill the prefect first to protect their shepherd, and Leucius forbade them to do the man any harm. He then told his flock that the Lord was commanding him to go into a pagan country and enlighten the city of Врунтисиополь and the land about it. He set a worthy bishop in his own place, took two deacons and five disciples, and hurried to a ship sailing for Italy; two presbyters joined him on the way, and on the road to the city he met a tribune with sixty-seven soldiers and turned them to Christ.

In the city he preached Jesus Christ. The ruler, Антиох, having questioned the converted tribune at length about the Christians’ teaching, wished to see him too, and said at their meeting: if thou wouldst have us believe in the God thou preachest, entreat Him to send rain on our land, which we have not seen for two years. The saint called the clergy and all the newly baptised together and prayed earnestly, and abundant rain fell and watered the parched ground; and seeing the wonder, Антиох and the whole city — twenty-seven thousand people, the page says — received holy baptism. A church of the Mother of God was built in memory of it, and another on the spot where the people were baptised, in the name of the Forerunner.

Soon the bishop fell ill, and it was revealed to him that the illness was mortal. He called his spiritual son Антиох and charged him to bury him on the place where the ship that had brought him from Alexandria had come to shore. Антиох did as he was charged and set a church there in the saint’s name, into which the relics were translated, and many wonders began to be worked at them. The page gives no year for any of it; the century in this folder is the day line’s «(V)» and nothing closer is recorded.
