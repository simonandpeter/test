# Erasmus of the Caves

He inherited a great fortune from his parents, the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović says, and spent all of it on the adorning of churches, above all on silvering and gilding icons. When the money was gone and he had nothing left he was despised by everybody; and the devil whispered to him that he had wasted his estate for nothing — that instead of adorning churches he should have given it away to the poor. He gave in to the temptation and believed it, and so came to despise himself, fell into despair and began to live loosely and lawlessly.

When his hour of death came near the brethren gathered round him and talked over his sins, because he himself was past knowing; and then he sat up in his bed and said that it was as they said, that he was sinful and unrepentant — but that [Anthony](/saints/anthony-of-the-caves) and [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) had just appeared to him, and after them the Most Holy Mother of God, and had told him that the Lord had given him more time for repentance. The Mother of God had said something else to him as well, to take his courage back: that the poor you have with you in every place, but my churches you have not.

He lived three days longer, repented and fell asleep in the Lord. The Prologue draws the lesson itself — that zeal for the church and the adorning of churches is a work pleasing to God — and dates his repose 1160.
