# Panteleimon the Healer

He was born at Nicomedia into the family of the illustrious pagan Eustorgius and named Pantoleon. His mother Euboula was a Christian and meant to raise him in the faith, but died while he was a small child; his father sent him to a pagan school, and afterwards he studied medicine at Nicomedia under the renowned physician Euphrosynus. He came to the notice of the emperor Maximian, who intended to make him physician to the court.

The priest Hermolaus, one of those who had survived the massacre of twenty thousand Christians in 303 and was living secretly in the city, saw the young man passing the house where they hid, called him in and spoke to him of Christ; after that Pantoleon came every day.

One day he found a dead child in the street with the great snake that had bitten it still beside the body. He prayed to Christ to raise the child and destroy the creature, resolving that if the prayer were answered he would be baptised. The child rose alive, and the snake died before his eyes.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 27 July, and its page fills in the names around him. saint.gr says Panteleimon — Panteleon was his earlier name — came from Nicomedia in Asia Minor and lived in the years of Maximian, whose reign it dates from 286 to 305. His father was Eustorgios, a pagan who became a Christian after his son's admonitions; his mother was [Euboule](/saints/euboula-mother-of-panteleimon), who came of a Christian family and whom the same calendar keeps on 30 March. He was trained in medicine by Euphrosynos, and was catechised in the Christian faith and baptised by the presbyter [Hermolaus](/saints/hermolaus-of-nicomedia), a priest of the church of Nicomedia, whom the same calendar keeps on the day before this one. Once, when a viper had bitten a young man and in effect killed him, Panteleimon called on Christ and raised him up.

The occasion of his martyrdom, the page says, was one more of his miracles. He had healed [a blind man](/saints/blind-man-healed-by-panteleimon), who reported the fact of his healing to the king and told him that Panteleon had healed him in the name of Christ, in whom he too now believed; the king, having heard him, at once ordered him beheaded, and Panteleon himself was brought before him and put to the torture to make him deny his faith. He was tortured hard and in various ways and did not give way, for the Lord appeared to him in the form of his spiritual father Hermolaus and gave him courage. At last his beheading was ordered, and then a voice was heard from heaven calling him not Panteleon but Panteleimon, the all-merciful. When the executioner stretched out his hand to cut off the saint's head with his sword, the sword bent and the iron melted like wax, and at such a wonder the soldiers standing by became Christians too. Then the saint gave himself up willingly to the martyrdom; and it is said that from his wound there ran not blood but milk, and that the olive tree to which they had bound him suddenly bore fruit. The Greek page names Florina and Tilos as the towns that keep him as their patron.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 27 July and gives him the longest of the day's five entries. He was of Nicomedia, of a Christian mother and a heathen father; his mother was called Euboula and his father Eustorgius. As a young man he learned the physician's sciences, and the priest [Hermolaus of Nicomedia](/saints/hermolaus-of-nicomedia) called him to himself, taught him the faith of Christ and baptised him. Panteleimon healed a certain blind man by a wonder, whom other physicians had treated in vain; he healed him in the name of Christ, and baptised him.

Out of envy the physicians accused Panteleimon as a Christian, and he came before the emperor Maximian for trial. And he stood before the earthly emperor in body, the entry says, but in mind he stood before the heavenly King. Before the emperor he declared himself freely a Christian, and before the emperor's own eyes healed a paralysed man of a long sickness; and that wonder drew many of the heathen to the faith of Christ.

The emperor put him to torments, but the Lord appeared to him several times and delivered him whole and unhurt. Then Hermolaus too suffered, with Hermippus and Hermocrates. Condemned to death, Panteleimon knelt down to pray; and at that the executioner struck him with the sword on the neck, and the sword broke as though it were of wax. Nor could the executioner put him to death until the saint had finished his prayer and himself said that they should behead him.

His relics became healing. Panteleimon was beheaded under an olive tree, which afterwards became all decked with fruit. Panteleimon means all-merciful; and the all-merciful God received his righteous soul and glorified him among his great saints. This wonderful martyr suffered honourably for Christ in his youth on the twenty-seventh of July in the year 304. Saint Panteleimon is called upon in the prayers at the blessing of water and at the blessing of oil, together with Saint Hermolaus and the other unmercenaries and wonderworkers. The most wonderful church dedicated to this saint, the entry ends, is on the Holy Mountain.
