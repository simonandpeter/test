# Patermuthius (17 December)

saint.gr's calendar for 17 December keeps three venerable-martyrs together, Patermuthius, Copris and Alexander, and Patermuthius is the first of them. The page says that they were Christians full of piety and zeal, that they were tortured and at last beheaded by order of Julian the Transgressor, whose years it gives as 361 to 363, and that this happened while Julian was on campaign against the Persians.

Of Patermuthius himself it says one thing, and it is the thing the company is remembered by: that [Copris](/saints/copris-17-december), who was a young man, lost his nerve a little at the start, and that Patermuthius strengthened him with his warm words. All three then underwent the martyr's death, the page says, chanting hymns to Christ; [Alexander](/saints/alexander-17-december) is the third of them. Its epigram has Copris cry out to him, father Patermuthius, I have been made sharer with you of your life and of your sword.

That is the whole of the entry. The page names no city and no province, no judge and no prison; it gives no year of birth for any of the three and dates the martyrdom only by the reign; it lists no relic and prints no hymn. The corpus keeps another martyr Patermuthius on 17 September, who suffered in an earlier persecution with Elias and the bishops Peleus and Nilus, and he is a different man.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 17 Δεκεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/12/17/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3283/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
