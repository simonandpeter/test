# Ignatius of Rostov

saint.gr says he lived in the thirteenth century and was elected bishop of the city of Rostov. He shepherded his flock in the love of God for twenty-six years and reposed in peace in 1288. The page gives him no year of birth, no monastery before the see, and no story of his election, and it names no place but the city itself.

On the day of his funeral service, it goes on, some of the faithful saw his honourable relic raise itself and bless them. God blessed him with the gift of working wonders, and many wonders, the page says, are worked at his tomb. That is the whole of what the Greek calendar keeps of him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and tells the wonder at his burial in full, where the Greek page gives it in a clause. As a hierarch he governed the flock of Christ for twenty-six years with great love and mercy. When he died and his body had been laid in the church, some saw how he rose out of the coffin and lifted up into the air above the church, and from that height blessed the people and the city, and then returned again into his own coffin.

And many other wonders, it says, happened at his grave. He reposed in the Lord on 28 May 1288 — the year the Greek page gives as well, and the same day of May that both calendars keep him on.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 28 мая ст. ст. and gives him no life: his line carries a link to his name and none to a reading, and the name page prints «Святитель Игнатий Ростовский, епископ» and nothing more. What the day does give him is two hymns under his own name, which say that he was enlightened by the teachings of right belief, received a flock in the hierarch’s office, became an heir of the apostles and was given from God the gift of wonders, and that his memory shines brightly in the world and shows all the divine radiance.

The same day page carries four more hymns, and none of them was copied into this folder. Two of them are addressed to three hierarchs of his see together and two to the hierarchs of that see as a body, one of those naming six men. They are not a mis-hang — he is named in three of the four — but they are not his proper either, and a hymn sung to three or to six, set down in one man’s folder, would tell the reader that the Church sings it to him alone. The calendar prints the same four on 15 мая, where another of the three stands, and they were refused there on the same ground.
