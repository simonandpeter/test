# Platonida (6 April)

Doxologia.ro's calendar for 6 aprilie names her and gives no life. The line is «Sfânta Cuvioasă Platonida», and her own page on the site carries no life tab and no troparion: an icon, the readings appointed for the day, and articles that treat her together with the other two the day keeps, Eutychius the patriarch and Gregory the Sinaite. There is no country, no century, no monastery and nothing of what she did.

The one thing the source asserts besides the day is that she was a monastic woman, «Cuvioasă» being the Romanian for the rank the Greek calendars give as ὁσία. She is not Platonida of Argeș, the Romanian nun whom the corpus keeps on 26 septembrie; that is a woman of the sixteenth century with a life of her own, and the two are kept apart here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and names the place the Romanian line has not got. saint.gr says Platonis was at first a deaconess, and afterwards withdrew into the desert of Nisibis, where she founded a monastery for women. The rule of her house was known for its strictness: the sisters ate once a day only, and when they were not at prayer they gave their time to monastic labours and to the various services. On the Friday, the day on which the Passion of Christ is honoured, they stopped all work and prayed in church the whole day long. She was a living example to all the sisters of strict monastic discipline, of meekness and of love towards her neighbour; she came to a deep old age and fell asleep in peace.

The page then sets a second account beside its own without choosing between them: «Κατα άλλους, η Οσία Πλατωνίς μαρτύρησε μαζί με δυο άλλους Χριστιανούς στον Ασκάλωνα της Παλαιστίνης» — according to others, the venerable Platonis was martyred together with two other Christians at Ascalon in Palestine. The same day page keeps two unnamed martyrs of Ascalon as an entry of their own, and says nothing to join them to her. Its heading gives the year 308, which its prose nowhere repeats, so no year is recorded here.

**The Russian church calendar keeps her on the Julian 6 April**, the same number the Romanian and the Greek keep, and its line is «Прп. Платониды Сирской (308).» — and it gives her two things neither of the other books gave. The first is a country: «Сирской», of Syria, where doxologia printed «Sfânta Cuvioasă Platonida» and saint.gr «Οσία Πλατωνίς» and neither added anything to the name. The name link, page 1854, repeats it, «Платонида Сирская, прп.» **The second is a year, and it is the first date this folder has ever carried**: the line closes «(308)» and the life ends «Достигнув глубокой старости, преподобная Платонида мирно скончалась в 308 году», having reached deep old age.

The life is four short paragraphs and it is the only narrative this folder has. She was first a deaconess — «первоначально была диакониссой» — and then withdrew into the desert of Nisibis, where she founded a women’s monastery. The rule of her house was distinguished by its strictness: the sisters ate once a day, and all the time left over from prayer they spent in the monastery’s labours and in various obediences, above all in handwork. On Fridays, the day of the remembrance of the saving sufferings on the Cross, all work stopped and the nuns were in the church from morning until evening, and between the services they listened to the reading of the Scriptures and to its explanation.

To all the sisters, the page ends, she was a living example of strict monastic struggle, of meekness and of love for her neighbours. **The deaconate is not written into `types`** — the day’s line and the name link both give «Прп.» and nothing else, and the standing ruling is that a rank read off the body of a life is not a rank the heading gave, the same answer that kept `virgin` off a folder of this wave in June. It is recorded here, where the page records it. Neither the Nisibis desert nor the monastery is given a coordinate on any page read, so no `locations` is written.
