# Magdalene (Zabelina)

**The only woman this day keeps, and the only one of its twenty who was not shot.** The Сретенский monastery's calendar gives her a line of her own, «Прмц. Магдалины (1931)», and carries no life for her; azbyka.ru prints one out of fond.ru, signed by Игумен Дамаскин (Орловский). She was born in 1863 at Orel, the daughter of a workman, Сергей Забелин, and was named Мария at her baptism.

In 1876, when she was thirteen, she settled at the village of Посошки in the Кромы district of the province of Orel. A merchant had set up an almshouse there that year, and it was afterwards turned into a community of John the Forerunner under a superior. She finished the school there and stayed on as a novice of the community, and when she took the monastic habit was named Магдалина. After the persecution began she moved to the Trinity-Odigitriya hermitage of Zosima in the province of Moscow, which was made an agricultural artel in 1920 but kept its monastic rule.

In 1929 the authorities closed the hermitage for good and its sisters scattered among the nearest villages. She settled with several novices in the village of Новинская in the Наро-Фоминск district; they earned their living by needlework, read the Psalter over the dead, and helped the priest at services for the living and the departed. In 1931 the authorities decided to arrest all the monastics driven out of the houses, and she was arrested on 22 May and shut in the prison at the Наро-Фоминск district executive committee.

She was questioned the day after. Asked what political and party convictions she held, she answered that she was indifferent to politics and to parties, though they did «мешать в жизни», get in the way of life. Asked the rest, she said: «Питалась своим трудом, занимаясь рукоделием, и помогала крестьянам. Ходить, никуда не ходила… агитации против колхоза не вела» — she fed herself by her own work at her needle and helped the peasants, went nowhere, and carried on no agitation against the collective farm. All the arrested sisters were charged with agitating against the farms, and with having urged the village women to go to the soviet and ask for the return of a cow taken from the local priest for unpaid taxes.

The investigation was short. On 29 May 1931 the troika sentenced her and the other sisters to five years' exile in Kazakhstan, with an order that they be sent there by prison convoy. She did not survive the long and bitter road: she died on 8 December 1931 and was buried in an unmarked grave.
