# Galaction of Vologda

Galaction was born Prince Gabriel, the son of Prince Ivan Ivanovich Belsky, who had fallen from favour; fearing the anger of Tsar Ivan the Terrible, his kin carried the seven-year-old secretly to Staritsa. Grown up and seeing the tsar’s disfavour towards his family, he withdrew to Vologda and lodged with a cobbler, from whom he learned to make shoes. His marriage was short: his wife soon died, and he brought up their little daughter. Resolved to give himself to God, he asked for a place on the river Sodima, dug a ditch round it, set his cell by the church of the Holy Trinity and took the monastic habit as Galaction. He kept to his trade and divided what he earned in three — one part for God, one for the poor and one to feed himself.

He later shut himself in his cell and chained himself to the wall; the faithful passed him food through a small window, and he rested little, kneeling and holding the chain, on dry bread and water. Rich and poor came to him alike for counsel. Once, in a long drought, Bishop Antony came in procession to the Trinity church and sent to ask the recluse to pray with them; he left his cell, prayed in the church, and the Lord sent abundant rain.

It was revealed to him that disaster was coming on Vologda, and in his chains he went to the council house and told them that their sins had called the Poles and Lithuanians upon them, and that they should fast, pray and build a church of the Sign of the Mother of God. When Nechai Proskurov mocked him, he foretold that the Trinity church Nechai had built would burn and his house be left empty, and passing the church of Demetrius of Priluki, crowded round with shops, he foretold its ruin too.

In September 1612 the Poles and Lithuanians broke into Vologda, killed many, plundered the churches and burned the city, and Nechai’s house and church and the church of Demetrius burned as he had said. Galaction was killed by the invaders on 24 September 1612 and buried in his cell, where healings followed. Under Bishop Varlaam (1627–1645) a church of the Sign was built over his relics and a monastery founded, and under Archbishop Marcellus (1645–1663) its cathedral of the Holy Spirit, after which the monastery was named.
