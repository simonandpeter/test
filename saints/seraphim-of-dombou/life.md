# Seraphim of Dombou

His name in the world was Sotiris, and saint.gr says he was born in the village of Zeli in Boeotia, of devout and virtuous parents who fed him on the streams of the Gospel, so that from childhood he had a leaning towards the study of the holy Scriptures and the ascetic life. Young, and against his parents' refusal for a time, he went off to the little monastery of the Prophet Elijah on Mount Karkara, where he built a church in the name of Christ the Saviour and lived his ascetic life there.

His fame spread quickly, and he began to have frequent visits from his parents, his friends and devout people who came to him for counsel and for help. For that reason he left the cave he loved and went to the monastery of the Holy Unmercenaries; and he soon left that house too, and went to the monastery of the Transfiguration of the Saviour on Mount Sagmation, between Boeotia and Euboea. There he shone quickly as a spiritual star of the first magnitude, and the abbot tonsured him a monk and gave him the name Seraphim. Later he was ordained deacon, and then presbyter.

A little after that, to escape the vanity that came with the fame of his virtues, he asked leave of the abbot and left the monastery of his repentance, and came to the western side of Helicon, to the place called Dombou, where he built a church of the Saviour and some cells and called a few monks to live with him. There he lived the ascetic life for ten years, teaching the other monks the saving lessons of the monastic life.

He foresaw his own falling asleep, received the immaculate Mysteries, and reposed at the age of seventy-five on 6 May 1602, on the day of Mid-Pentecost, at six in the afternoon. The page names him patron of Livadeia, and says that his head and part of his relics are at the monastery that carries his name in Boeotia, with a fragment at the monastery of Agathon in Phthiotis.
