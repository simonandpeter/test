# Eulampia, sister of Eulampius

Eulampia was the sister of [Eulampius](/saints/eulampius-of-nicomedia), and doxologia.ro prints one life for the two of them. He was a young man of a noble house in the country of Nicomedia who was taken and tried because he had laughed at the imperial decree for the killing of Christians posted over the city gates; he was beaten with sinews and laid on a red-hot iron bed, and rose from it whole.

When Eulampia heard that her brother was suffering for Christ she ran to the place, stood out in the middle of the crowd and cried to him: were we not borne by one mother, did we not feed at the same breast? The judge had her struck about the face, and she was beaten so long that the beauty of her face was changed and the blood ran from her nose and mouth, while her brother strengthened her with words.

A cauldron was heated for them, and at her brother's word she went into it beside him; the power of the fire went out at once, the cauldron cooled, and the two of them stood in it unhurt, singing and glorifying God. The life says two hundred of the people who saw that miracle believed.

The judge condemned them at last to the sword. Their hands were tied behind them and they were led far off to the place of execution, where Eulampius laid down his head and the soldiers struck it off; Eulampia gave up her soul into the hands of God before the blow fell, and the soldiers, seeing her already dead, did not behead her.

The Greek calendar keeps the two on the same day, 10 October, and tells how they were taken. saint.gr sets them in the years of the emperor Maximian, and says the persecution was harsh and merciless, so that Eulampius and his sister Eulampia hid in the mountain with other Christians, living there in prayer and the study of the holy Scriptures. One day Eulampius went into Nicomedia to buy food; the idolaters recognised him and arrested him on the spot. Asked by the king whether he believed in Christ, he confessed openly that he was a Christian, and they put him into an idol temple to make him sacrifice by force — where he shattered the idol of the god Ares by his prayer.

As they began to scourge him in the most inhuman way his sister Eulampia rushed forward, embraced him, and begged God to let her suffer with her brother. They then put the two of them into a cauldron of boiling water, and by a miracle they were refreshed in it and came out whole and unharmed. That made two hundred pagans believe in Christ, and they were beheaded together with Eulampius and Eulampia for the truth of the Lord; the same calendar keeps those two hundred as [the Two Hundred Martyrs of Nicomedia](/saints/two-hundred-martyrs-of-nicomedia) in an entry of their own on the same day.

The Russian calendar keeps her with her brother at the head of its own 10 October, «Мчч. Евлампия и Евлампии (303-311)», under the day's plain service icon, which stands immediately before the one line that names them both. Its life calls them brother and sister of one family, living at Nicomedia at the beginning of the fourth century; the years are the page's own difficulty, since its day line gives 303 to 311 and its life dates Maximian 284 to 305.

She comes into the account at the point where her brother is being taken back to the tortures, after the idol of Mars had fallen at his word. She appeared before the judges and declared that she too was a Christian — of her own accord, which is the whole of what the page says about how she was taken. [Eulampius](/saints/eulampius-of-nicomedia) gave her heart with the Lord's words, that she should not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul.

The two of them were then thrown into a heated furnace and the Lord kept them from the fire. He was beheaded; she, the life says, died of the torments themselves, which is the one place in the account where the two ends part.
