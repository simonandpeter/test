# Clement of Rome

In old Rome there was a man of great family named Faustus, who drew his descent from the emperors of Rome, and his wife was Mattidia, of imperial blood herself, a kinswoman of Augustus and Tiberius, whom Caesar had given him to wife. Both were unbelievers and busied themselves with the worship of idols. They had first twin sons, whom they called Faustus and Faustinian, and afterwards a third son, whom they named Clement.

Faustus had a shameless brother who, seeing how beautiful his brother's wife was, sought to draw her into adultery; and she, being full of wisdom, would not dishonour her house, kept him off in every way, and told nobody, not even her husband, fearing an evil report in the city. Seeing at last that she could not be rid of him unless she went out of his sight, she told her husband that she had dreamed of a grave old man who warned her that unless she and her twin sons left Rome for some ten years they would all die a sudden and ruinous death.

Faustus was frightened, and thought it better that his wife and sons should live abroad than die suddenly at home. So he fitted out a ship and sent her to the Greek land, to the city of Athens, with the twins, with servants and much wealth, charging her to put both boys to the Greek learning when she arrived; and they parted with unspeakable grief and many tears, Faustus staying at Rome with his youngest son, Clement.

Some days out a storm came up with great waves, and the ship was driven to an unknown country and broken at midnight, and all were drowned. Mattidia was carried by the waves onto a rock on an island off the city called Antandros in the parts of Asia, and mourned inconsolably for her drowned sons, and in her grief wanted to throw herself into the sea; but the people there, seeing her naked, weeping and sighing terribly, took pity on her and took her in. So she lived twenty-four years. Her sons had in fact been found alive after the wreck: pirates had seen them and carried them off.

Clement, hearing of Christ, was kindled with a longing to know the truth about him and his teaching more surely, and went to Judaea where the good news had spread, and became a disciple of the Apostle [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle). In Peter's company the family was gathered again one by one. Peter, going into Syria, sent Faustus and Faustinian ahead, kept Clement with him, and at a certain city went ashore while Clement stayed in the ship; and there he saw an old woman sitting, and knew in the spirit that she was a stranger, and asked her country. She wept and said there was no woman in the world so bitter as she.

As they came back to the ship Clement met them and wondered at the woman following Peter; and she, looking at him, began to know him by the likeness of his face to his father's. Then the mother and her sons rejoiced, seeing one another alive and telling how by the judgements of God they had been delivered from drowning. And going another way Peter found on the road a grave old man, grey-bearded but poorly dressed, who argued that all things happen by chance and not by God's providence, and who said he had been in great grief above twenty years and had prayed to all the gods in vain — and Peter knew from his story that this was Faustus, the husband of Mattidia and the father of Clement and his brothers.

He took him home, and when Faustus came in and saw Mattidia he was astounded, and looked at her in wonder, saying nothing, and then: *What wonder is this?* From then on they lived in the true faith, giving much alms to the poor, and continued in that good state a long time and into good old age, and at last divided all they had among those who needed help.

After the Apostle Peter's end, and after the bishops Linus and Cletus, Clement followed to the throne of Rome, and he steered the ship of Christ well through the storm and tossing of that time, when the persecutors troubled it. He turned to Christ a multitude of unbelievers, not of the common people only but out of the imperial palaces, many of the great and famous, among them one Sisinnius. The prefect could find no fault in him and wrote to the emperor Trajan that the people had risen against him for the gods' sake.

Trajan sent him into exile, and when the hegemon Aufidianus was sent into those parts and had killed many Christians, he found Clement unbent in the faith and standing fast in the Lord. So he ordered him put into a boat and taken into the middle of the sea, and there an anchor tied to his neck, and himself thrown into the deep. The faithful stood on the shore watching him drowned and lamenting him with great weeping; and two of his disciples, the more believing, Cornelius and Phoebus, called them to prayer, and the sea drew back, and they walked out and found a shrine and the body of the saint in it with the anchor beside him.

The faithful wanted to take the holy body away, but it was revealed to those disciples that it should be left where it was; and every year at his commemoration the sea withdrew and the people went out to venerate him. One year a child was left behind there, and his parents came the next year and found him alive and well beside the saint's coffin. But in the reign of Nicephorus, the Greek emperor who came after Irene the mother of Constantine, the sea did not withdraw at his commemoration as in all the years before, and so it remained for fifty years and more.

When the blessed George was bishop in Cherson he grieved that the sea did not withdraw and that the relics of such a saint lay covered with water as under a bushel. In his days two teachers and philosophers came from Constantinople to Cherson, [Methodius](/saints/methodius-of-moravia) and Constantine, who was afterwards called [Cyril](/saints/cyril-the-philosopher), on their way to preach to the Khazars; and hearing that the relics were in the sea they urged the bishop to seek out that spiritual treasure.

Bishop George went first to Constantinople to tell the emperor Michael the third, son of Theophilus, who reigned with his blessed mother Theodora, and the most holy patriarch Ignatius, who shepherded the Church after Saint Methodius; and the emperor and the patriarch sent honourable men with him and all the clergy of Holy Wisdom. Coming to Cherson they gathered with Methodius and Constantine and all the faithful people and went to the sea's edge with psalms and hymns — and the sea did not withdraw.

At sunset they sat down in a ship, and at midnight a light shone out of the sea, and first the head appeared and then the relics of the saint came up out of the water. The bishop took them into the ship and brought them into the city with honour and laid them in the church of the Holy Apostles; and as the Liturgy began many miracles were done — the blind saw, the lame and those with every kind of sickness were made whole, and demons were driven out of men.
