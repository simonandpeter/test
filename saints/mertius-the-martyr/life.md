# Mertius

Mertius was enrolled, in the years of the emperor Diocletian whom saint.gr dates 284 to 305, in the military company called that of the Moors, in Mauretania. There they pressed him to offer sacrifice to the idols, and he would not obey the commands of the idolaters. After his refusal they denounced him to his commander for worshipping Jesus, and he was brought before him.

The martyr stood before the governor with modesty and with a fearless courage. He bowed first, to show that he honoured the ruler, and then drew back and refused to comply; and showing the wounds he had taken fighting under the emperor's standard, he declared that he reverenced the emperor and was ready to give his blood for a soldier's duty, but that his soul and his conscience belonged to the worship of Christ.

Diocletian was enraged and began to put him to humiliations and torments. First he ordered the belt taken from him, the mark of his military rank, and so stripped him of his office; then they scourged him without pity. Because he bore it and did not utter the least cry, he astonished the tyrant and increased his anger, and the scourging went on a long while, until the martyr's whole body was one open wound. Then the beating was stopped and he was thrown into prison, and there in the year 298 he gave up his honourable soul.
