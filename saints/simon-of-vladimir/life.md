# Simon of Vladimir

The holy Simon, Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal, was the author of the book called the Paterikon of the Caves of Kyiv, saint.gr says, and he became a monk in the monastery of the Caves in the second half of the twelfth century. In 1206 he was made abbot of the monastery of the Nativity of the Mother of God at Vladimir, and in 1214, at the wish of prince George Vsevolodovich, who died in 1238, he became the first Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal.

In his bishop's lodging he kept up the same spiritual struggle as in the monastery. Once indeed, while he was at prayer, he was counted worthy to see in a vision the Most Holy Mother of God, accompanied by a multitude of radiant saints, while on her right and on her left stood the venerable Antony and the venerable [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves).

On the eve of his repose, in the year 1226, Simon received the great angelic habit. He was buried at first in the city of Vladimir, but afterwards, according to his own last wish, his honourable relic was carried to the Lavra of the Caves of Kyiv, where the page says it lies incorrupt. It gives no year and no place for his birth, no family, and no account of the writing of the Paterikon; it prints neither apolytikion nor kontakion for him.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 10 мая ст. ст. and gives him two lives, which agree in the main. He was the first bishop of the Vladimir and Suzdal land, born in the second half of the twelfth century, and laboured in the Kiev Caves monastery, where he took the tonsure. In 1206 he became abbot of the monastery of the Nativity of the Mother of God at Vladimir, and under the great prince of Vladimir, Георгий Всеволодович, he was made the first bishop of Vladimir-on-the-Klyazma and Suzdal — in 1215 by the one life and in 1214 by the other. He cared for the flock entrusted to him as a good shepherd, the first life says, and did not leave off a monk’s labours.

He consecrated the church of the Nativity monastery in 1218 and the cathedral at Suzdal in 1225. Burning with love for the ascetics of the Caves, he wrote the lives of several of them, which went into the Kiev Caves Paterikon, and he is the author of the Сказание about the holy wonderworking church of the Caves at Kiev, how it was built, adorned and consecrated. The great prince honoured him deeply and was ready to open a new episcopal see at Suzdal for his friend Поликарп, a monk of the Kiev Caves monastery who was being tempted by the spirit of ambition; but Симон, seeing Поликарп’s state of soul, talked the prince out of it and wrote to Поликарп himself a deeply instructive epistle against the sicknesses of his soul. Those epistles began the Paterikon and earned their writer the name of a teaching man.

He spent twelve years on the archpastoral throne, received the schema on the eve of his repose, and died in peace in 1226; the fuller of the two lives gives the day as 22 мая. The two lives differ about where he was laid: one says his honourable body was put, as he desired, in the Near or Antony’s caves of the Kiev Caves Lavra, and the other that it was first buried at Vladimir and carried some years later to the Lavra by his own bequest. Both have him resting in Antony’s caves at the end.
