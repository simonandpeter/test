# Baruch

Baruch was the son of Neriah, of a notable family of the kingdom of Judah, and the disciple and friend of the Prophet Jeremiah. In the year 605 before Christ Jeremiah sent him to Jerusalem with the letter announcing the punishments to come upon the Jews and the Babylonian captivity; the two were imprisoned until the city fell, and Nebuchadnezzar permitted them to remain in the Holy City, where Jeremiah wrote his Lamentations. After the revolt of Gedaliah, Jeremiah was carried into Egypt, where he died; Baruch was taken captive to Babylon, and there went on proclaiming to the Jews the fulfilment of the great promises - the release from captivity, the rebuilding of Jerusalem, and the gathering of all in the temple of the Lord.

The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 28 September too. Truthful and bold, Baruch — the name means blessed — lived in the seventh century before Christ, it says; he was the son of Neriah and the devoted follower and scribe of the prophet Jeremiah. While Jeremiah was in prison Baruch wrote down prophecies at his dictation, to be read to the people on a fast day; but king Jehoiakim, when he heard of it, threw the book into the fire rather than profit by the prophet’s warnings, and the prophecies were written again. Baruch was imprisoned too, because the Jews hated him for his truthful and bold tongue and accused him of setting Jeremiah against them; and when they fled in fear to Egypt, Baruch went there with Jeremiah. A rabbinic tradition, the synaxarion says, has him return to Babylon after Jeremiah’s death. In the book that bears his name he foretells plainly the incarnation of the Lord Jesus Christ. The Greek calendar keeps him with an apolytikion and a kontakion.
