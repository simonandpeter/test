# Iorest of Transylvania

He was the son of Transylvanian peasants, and was given the name Ilie at his baptism. Loving Christ from childhood, he became a monk in the brotherhood of Putna monastery and took the name Iorest. He went through that lavra's spiritual school and came out a practised monk, a good calligrapher and a painter of icons, zealous for the church's services and for the keeping of the Orthodox faith. For the purity of his heart the abbot had him ordained hieromonk, and the life says he was like a lit torch among the fathers.

Word of him reached Vasile Lupu, the prince of Moldavia. Ghenadie, metropolitan of Ardeal, died in the autumn of 1640, and Iorest of Putna was chosen father and first-hierarch of the Church of Transylvania. He received consecration as a bishop from the Metropolitan of Wallachia in 1641 and went up to the throne of the metropolia of Ardeal at Alba Iulia.

For the three years he shepherded that church he laboured as a confessor to defend the Orthodox faith against the foreign Calvinist teachings. He set zealous priests everywhere, consecrated churches and went about the villages comforting and teaching the faithful. In 1643 he was thrown into prison for that zeal, and suffered many dishonours, beatings and insults, enduring them, the life says, as a martyr endures and ready to give his life for the faith and for his flock.

After nine months he was let out of prison and made to pay a sum of money. Reaching Moldavia again, he was bishop at Huși between 1656 and 1657. Then he gave up his soul in peace; the page names no year for his death. The Romanian Orthodox Church canonised him in 1955 and keeps his memory on 24 April, and the troparion printed for the day is one hymn for him and for [Sava Brancovici](/saints/sava-brancovici-of-transylvania) together.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, under the name Elias the Confessor of Romania, and gives him the years the Romanian page withheld. He was born in 1600 in Transylvania, saint.gr says, of a poor and devout family, and from a young age loved the monastic life and became a monk at the monastery of Putna. Because of his God-loving manner of life, after the death of Gennadius, metropolitan of Transylvania — who reposed, the page notes, on 3 September 1640 — and with the consent of Vasile Lupu, voivode of Moldavia, he was elected bishop of Transylvania.

As bishop he defended the Orthodox faith with all his strength, and for that he was imprisoned and suffered terribly. After nine months shut up in the prison he was released and went to Moldavia; and in the year 1656 he was elected bishop at Huşi, where again he struggled hard in defence of the Church and of the Orthodox faith. He fell asleep in peace, the page says, in the year 1678.
