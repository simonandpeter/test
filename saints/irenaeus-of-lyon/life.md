# Irenaeus of Lyon

Irenaeus lived in the reign of Marcus Aurelius, around the year 160. He was probably born in Smyrna around 140 — he says in his Against Heresies (3, 3) that as a child he knew Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna, who had himself been a disciple of John the Theologian; whose pupil he was afterwards, and how he entered the clergy, is not known. The Romanian Lives add that Polycarp ordained him presbyter and sent him to Gaul.

He became bishop of Lugdunum — Lyon — after the martyrdom of his predecessor [Pothinus](/saints/pothinus-of-lyon), in the years when the Church there suffered both the persecution of the pagan emperors and the disturbance of the heresies that were then multiplying. He wrote many works, mostly on doctrine, against those heresies, by which he kept many Christians in the right faith and drew many out of error. At the last he was beheaded under Severus, in the year 202 according to the synaxarion, and received the martyr’s crown.
