# Irenaeus of Lyon

Irenaeus lived in the reign of Marcus Aurelius, around the year 160. He was probably born in Smyrna around 140 — he says in his Against Heresies (3, 3) that as a child he knew Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna, who had himself been a disciple of John the Theologian; whose pupil he was afterwards, and how he entered the clergy, is not known. The Romanian Lives add that Polycarp ordained him presbyter and sent him to Gaul.

He became bishop of Lugdunum — Lyon — after the martyrdom of his predecessor [Pothinus](/saints/pothinus-of-lyon), in the years when the Church there suffered both the persecution of the pagan emperors and the disturbance of the heresies that were then multiplying. He wrote many works, mostly on doctrine, against those heresies, by which he kept many Christians in the right faith and drew many out of error. At the last he was beheaded under Severus, in the year 202 according to the synaxarion, and received the martyr’s crown.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep him on 23 August (Julian). The Sretensky calendar has him born in 130 at Smyrna, where he had a brilliant education in poetry, philosophy, rhetoric and all the sciences thought necessary for a young man of the world, and where Polycarp, the disciple of John the Theologian, taught him the truths of Christian doctrine, baptised him, later ordained him presbyter and sent him to the Gallic city of Lugdunum to the aged bishop Pothinus; sent with the confessors’ letter to Pope Eleutherius (177–190), he was away when all the prominent Christians were imprisoned, and in 178, a year after Pothinus’s martyrdom, he was chosen bishop — in a short time, Gregory of Tours wrote of him, his preaching turned all Lugdunum into a Christian city. When the persecution quietened he set out the Orthodox teaching in his Exposure and Refutation of Knowledge Falsely So Called, the five books Against Heresies, against the gnostics of Valentinus who taught that God could not be incarnate because matter is imperfect and the bearer of evil; the Word of God, Jesus Christ our Lord, he taught, by his unspeakable goodness became what we are, to make us what he is. The Prologue says he suffered for Christ under Severus in 202 with many thousands of Christians, nineteen thousand, and in its verses calls him and Pothinus the two sons of Polycarp, children in the Spirit and not the flesh, two lambs given for the slaughter for the crucified Christ, two brave bishops of baptised Lyon.
