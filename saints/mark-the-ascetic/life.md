# Mark the Ascetic

This saint was a lover of labour in everything, and gave himself at the same time to the searching of the Holy Scriptures, reaching the highest perfection in the hermit's life and in virtue. The writings he left are the witness of it — doxologia points the reader to the first volume of the Romanian Philokalia — full of every kind of teaching and profit; and the working of wonders was entrusted to him by Christ the Saviour.

Of those wonders one must be told here. The saint was once in the yard, examining himself, when a wild hyena came to him bringing her cub, which was blind, and in a humble manner begged him to have mercy on her and heal the cub's blindness. The saint spat upon the sick eyes and prayed, and gave the cub its sight. A few days later the hyena brought him a large ram's skin in thanks; but he would not take it until she promised that she would attack the sheep of the poor no more.

If the saint was so merciful towards the nature of the beasts, the life asks, how merciful must he have been towards men, whom the sharing of one nature itself obliges to be full of compassion? So great was his purity that the priest of the monastery said on oath that he had never once given the Holy Mysteries to the monk Mark: whenever he came to communicate he was communicated by an angel, of whom only the hand was seen, from the elbow, holding the spoon.

He had withdrawn from all the cares and the noise of the world at the age of forty, and having passed sixty years in the hermit's life he went over to the Lord. He was small of stature, thin-bearded, and his head was lit from within by the grace of the Holy Spirit. doxologia gives no year for his birth or his death, and names no country.
