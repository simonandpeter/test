# Hilarion (Tsurikov)

Hilarion — Rodion Fyodorovich Tsurikov in the world — was born on 8 April
1856 in the village of Krivopolyany in the Ostrogozhsk district of
Voronezh province, to a peasant family deeply religious — both his sisters
became nuns — and he went to Athos, to the monastery of St Panteleimon,
and was tonsured there with the name Hilarion. In 1913 he was taken off
Athos in the affair of the Name-worshippers and tried, as he wrote himself
in a questionnaire, by the tribunal of a court-martial and sentenced to
death for the Name of God, freed for want of proof; coming to Moscow he
entered the brotherhood of the Moscow Protection monastery, and after 1924
moved to the farmstead of Solyony, where he lived with the Athonite monk
[John (Laba)](/saints/john-monk-martyr-1937), with whom he was soon
arrested on the charge that they went against the Soviet power and held
to the old, and sentenced to three years’ exile in the Narym country.
Freed from that first exile in October 1928 he joined Fr John again and
settled near the town of Frunze, in the hills, at a bee-farm; on 2
February 1929 the monks were arrested a second time on the same charge and
given three years’ exile at Kzyl-Orda. After the second exile he settled
at Mirzoyan, where his sisters the nuns Magdalene and Gabriela later
joined him, and Fr John too came from his exile after a time, and the two
hieromonks celebrated the services regularly. The Orthodox of Taraz, as
Mirzoyan is now called, remember and honour them to this day: in the
thirties, one recalls, the town was full of exiles without a roof, and my
parents John and Tatiana, deeply religious and wishing to please God,
took them into their house; my mother prayed that priests or monks might
come to the house even once, and one day there was a knock at the gate
and two monks stood there — Tatianushka, you called us, and we have come
to you. That was in 1936. They had an antimension and secretly served the
Liturgy in our house; my father built a cell in the yard for Fr Hilarion,
who spent the whole of Great Lent in it in seclusion on one prosphoron and
water a day, sleeping on the floor on a reindeer skin — my father wanted
to give him a bed with a mattress, but he said, I shall sleep soft and
sleep through my prayers. Many townspeople soon learned of the elders and
came for prayer and counsel, though they did not receive everyone; and
having served the Liturgy on Trinity Sunday, 20 June 1937, they said: that
is the last time we have served with you — soon we shall be arrested and
shot. Fr Hilarion, said my mother, we will hide you or take you somewhere.
No, he said, no need; we want to receive the martyrs’ crowns. On 23 June
1937 came the last arrest, on charges of taking part in a
counter-revolutionary organisation, defeatist agitation, and leading a
secret monastery; at the inquiry he admitted no guilt in any
counter-revolutionary organisation. The indictment of 23 August 1937 in
case no. 723 — that of the metropolitans Cyril (Smirnov) and Joseph
(Petrovykh), Archbishop Alexius (Orlov) and others, in which the monks
were included — added to the standard charges that Tsurikov Hilarion and
Laba Ivan were leaders of a secret monastery at Mirzoyan under Kobranov
and Cyril Smirnov, carried on agitation about the imminent fall of the
Soviet power and the restoration of the bourgeois order, and performed
secret tonsures into secret monasticism. The sentence was the highest
penalty, and on 4 September 1937 — 22 August of the old style, the day the
Russian calendar keeps him — he was shot. He was numbered among the saints
in 2000; the Russian calendar keeps him also among the venerable fathers
of the Russian monastery of St Panteleimon on Athos and in the synaxis of
the saints of Central Asia.
