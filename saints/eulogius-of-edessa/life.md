# Eulogius

Barses and Eulogius, bishops of Edessa, and Protogenes, bishop of Carrhae, confessors, suffered from the Arians in the second half of the fourth century. The emperor Valens (364–378), wanting to plant the Arian heresy, raised a cruel persecution of the Orthodox; at Edessa he drove Barses, a champion of Orthodoxy, from the episcopal throne and sent him into exile on the island of Aradus, where the Orthodox people received the exiled bishop with great honour; sent further, to Oxyrhynchus in Egypt, he met the same welcome, and was then banished to the very edge of the empire, the distant city of Phaeno, where, worn out by exile, he died in 378. At Edessa Valens unlawfully set on the see an Arian false bishop named Lupus — wolf — who proved a wolf in name and deed, ravaging the flock of Christ; the Orthodox of Edessa, clergy and laity, stopped going to their church, seized by the Arians, and gathered outside the city to worship in the open. Learning of it the emperor ordered the eparch Modestus to kill all the Orthodox who came to worship outside the city; the eparch pitied the people and warned them not to come, but the faithful did the opposite: burning to die as martyrs for Christ they came, every one of them, to the place where they usually gathered to pray. Modestus, carrying out the order, set off there with armed soldiers, and on the way saw a woman hurrying to the service with her child so that the two of them should not miss the martyr’s crown; struck, he turned back with his soldiers, and going to Valens persuaded him to revoke the decree that all the Orthodox be killed and to limit it to the clergy. The clergy, led by the senior presbyter Eulogius, were brought before the emperor, who tried to make them enter communion with the false bishop Lupus, and not one agreed; after that eighty clergy were sent in chains into exile in Thrace, and when the Orthodox met them on the way with great honour as confessors and supplied them with everything they needed, the emperor, hearing of it, ordered the martyrs split up in twos and sent to distant places. The presbyters Eulogius and Protogenes were sent to the Thebaid city of Antinoë, where by their preaching they turned many idolaters to Christ and baptised them; when Valens perished and Theodosius came to the throne the Orthodox confessors still alive were brought back from exile, and the two returned to Edessa — Eulogius was raised to the see of Edessa in place of Barses, who had died in exile, and Protogenes made bishop of Carrhae in Mesopotamia, and both governed their flocks until their deaths at the end of the fourth century. The Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps them as the holy confessors of Edessa, who suffered much in prisons and banishment for the Orthodox faith under Valens, who held the Arian heresy, and were freed under Theodosius.
