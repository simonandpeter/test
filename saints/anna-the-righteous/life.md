# Anna, mother of the Theotokos

The synaxis of the righteous parents of the Theotokos was fixed, by ancient church tradition, on the day after her Nativity, because by the birth of their holy daughter they became the agents of the world’s salvation; it was celebrated, the old note says, in the house of the Theotokos near the great church at the Chalkoprateia. [Joachim](/saints/joachim-the-righteous) was the son of Eliakim, of the tribe of Judah and the line of David, fallen from the throne and living as a private man in Judaea, mostly at Jerusalem, where he had a mansion with a royal garden; he married Anna, daughter of the priest Matthan of the tribe of Levi and of his wife Mary of the tribe of Judah — the royal and priestly tribes intermarried, kingship being counted equal to priesthood, and gave and took no daughters from the other tribes, held common.

Having passed their life pleasing God, as the notices of 25 July, 8 September and 9 December tell, Joachim died eight years after the Entry of his daughter into the Temple, at ninety-two, and Anna eleven months after him, at eighty-three; the Mother of God had been given them, by a miracle, when Joachim was eighty and Anna seventy. The Romanian calendar keeps the holy and righteous parents Joachim and Anna the same day, with a troparion and kontakion — rejoice now, holy Anna, loosed from the bonds of barrenness — and says their holy life is known from this, that they were given a daughter holier than all the saints and more honourable than the cherubim.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, gives the family otherwise: Joachim the son of Varpafir, of the tribe of Judah and a descendant of King David; Anna the daughter of the priest Matthan, of the tribe of Levi like Aaron. Matthan had three daughters, Mary, Sovia and Anna: Mary married in Bethlehem and bore Salome, Sovia married in Bethlehem too and bore [Elizabeth](/saints/elizabeth-mother-of-the-forerunner), the mother of the Forerunner, and Anna married Joachim in Nazareth. Fifty years they were married and childless, living quietly and with means, keeping a third of what came in for themselves, giving a third to the poor and a third to the Temple.

When in old age they went up to Jerusalem to offer sacrifice, the high priest Issachar told Joachim that a childless man was not worthy to have his gift received, and others with children pushed him to the back; the two went home in great grief and prayed that God would do for them what he had done for Abraham and Sarah. An angel announced a most blessed daughter, through whom all the nations of the earth would be blessed and salvation come to the world; Anna conceived, and in the ninth month bore Mary. Joachim lived eighty years, the Prologue says, and Anna seventy-nine.
