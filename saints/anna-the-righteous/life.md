# Anna, mother of the Theotokos

The synaxis of the righteous parents of the Theotokos was fixed, by ancient church tradition, on the day after her Nativity, because by the birth of their holy daughter they became the agents of the world’s salvation; it was celebrated, the old note says, in the house of the Theotokos near the great church at the Chalkoprateia. [Joachim](/saints/joachim-the-righteous) was the son of Eliakim, of the tribe of Judah and the line of David, fallen from the throne and living as a private man in Judaea, mostly at Jerusalem, where he had a mansion with a royal garden; he married Anna, daughter of the priest Matthan of the tribe of Levi and of his wife Mary of the tribe of Judah — the royal and priestly tribes intermarried, kingship being counted equal to priesthood, and gave and took no daughters from the other tribes, held common. Having passed their life pleasing God, as the notices of 25 July, 8 September and 9 December tell, Joachim died eight years after the Entry of his daughter into the Temple, at ninety-two, and Anna eleven months after him, at eighty-three; the Mother of God had been given them, by a miracle, when Joachim was eighty and Anna seventy. The Romanian calendar keeps the holy and righteous parents Joachim and Anna the same day, with a troparion and kontakion — rejoice now, holy Anna, loosed from the bonds of barrenness — and says their holy life is known from this, that they were given a daughter holier than all the saints and more honourable than the cherubim.
