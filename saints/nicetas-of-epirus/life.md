# Nicetas of Epirus

saint.gr says Nicetas came from Epirus and became a hieromonk on the Holy Mountain — first at the skete of St Anne and afterwards at the monastery of St Panteleimon, the Russian house. The longing for martyrdom took hold of him, and he went about the villages around Serres and Drama preaching Christ as the true God and Mohammed as a deceiver.

He was seized by the Turks and imprisoned at Serres. Then he was put to frightful tortures — the page names them: the smelling of fire through the nose, a crown of thorns on his head, reed splinters under his nails and burning of his secret parts. But Nicetas, with a wonderful steadiness, went on confessing his faith in Christ the whole time. In the end, on 19 February 1806, they hanged him, and so he received the crown of incorruption.

The page gives him no village in Epirus and no year of birth, names no relics, and prints no couplet and no hymn under his name; what it links beside him is the synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered after the taking of Constantinople, which is a company and not a person and is not kept here.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps a Nicetas on the Julian 4 April, which is not the day saint.gr keeps this one on, and the whole entry is four sentences; that it is this man is a reading of those four sentences and not of the name, which eighteen folders in this corpus share. He was a Slav from Albania, it says — the Greek page says Epirus, which is the same stretch of country in the older usage — and he was a monk of the Holy Mountain, which the Greek page says too. He went to Serres, where the Greek page also has him; and there he disputed with the mullahs about the faith. Not being able to overcome him by argument, the Turks put him to torments, in which the holy Nicetas died and gave up his soul to God.

The entry dates that 1808, where this folder's death is the 19 February 1806 saint.gr prints as the day of his hanging. **An attested year is not displaced on a second source's word**, so the date here stands as it was and the Prologue's year is recorded beside it; and the Serbian row carries the Prologue's own day, as the Greek row carries saint.gr's. What is new here is that he was a Slav, and that what he did at Serres was to argue the Muslim teachers down — which is what brought the tortures on him — where the Greek account has him preaching Christ through the villages around Serres and Drama.
