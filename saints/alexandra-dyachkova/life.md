# Alexandra (Dyachkova)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names her fifth of the six women it grades прмц. on the one line it gives to fourteen people of 1938 for the Julian 1 March, and prints no life for her. Alexandra Ivanovna Dyachkova was born in 1893 in the village of Chernogolovka in the Ivanovo volost of the Bogorodsk district of the province of Moscow, the daughter of the peasant John Dyachkov. She finished the village school and lived with her parents, helping them about the house, and in 1914 she entered as a novice the monastery of the Holy Trinity near the village of Akatovo in the Klin district, which was therefore often called the Akatovo house.

It had been opened in 1890, first as a community, and in 1898 it was given the status of a monastery with a coenobitic rule; it had two churches then, two guest houses and a hospice, and about seventy sisters lived in it. When the godless came to power and demanded that it be closed, the sisters turned it into an agricultural artel, which the abbess went on directing as before. But in 1927 the godless began closing every artel in which nuns were living: the working community at Akatovo was broken up and the abbess arrested.

She went home to Chernogolovka and settled in her parents’ house. But her love for the house where her monastic labours had begun was so great that she went back to Akatovo and settled in one of the villages near the monastery walls; and from 1930, when the watch kept on nuns living near closed houses grew sharper and the repressions against them harder, she returned to her parents’ house again. Their village church had by then lost its pastor, and she set about getting a priest appointed to them, which in the end she managed, and she took it on herself to settle him in his new place.

The arrests of clergy and monastics of closed houses were going on, and on 21 May 1931 she was taken with other monastics and held in the prison at Noginsk. One witness said she spread absurd rumours about the collective farm, saying the farmers went hungry, the cattle died, the milk was handed over and the children died of hunger, and that she was an active organiser of religious feasts. She was charged with agitating against collective farming, and in particular with having said at the time of the 1930 re-elections to the village soviets that no honest peasant would go into the farm, that only idlers gathered there and the communists might work with them.

Called for questioning, she said that she had indeed lived many years in the monastery and had left it only after it was closed and the abbess arrested; and as for the charge, «агитации против колхозов я не вела». On 29 May 1931 the troika of the ОГПУ sentenced her to five years in a corrective labour camp, and she was sent to the building of the White Sea canal. She was released in 1934 and settled in her native village; her mother died that same year, and after about a month and a half at home she went away to the Volokolamsk district, where there was less chance of being arrested as a woman who had served a camp term and settled near Moscow.

She lived with people she knew in one village and another, earning a little by day-labour, and in October 1937 she got work as watchman and cleaner at the church of the Nativity of the Mother of God in the village of Shestakovo in the Volokolamsk district and settled in the church hut, where the priest lived too. The priest was soon arrested, and though the church was not closed there was nobody to serve in it. She was arrested on 28 February 1938 and held in the prison at Volokolamsk.

The secretary of the village soviet, questioned as a witness, said that she was discontented with the Soviet power and the order of things in the USSR; that she went round the collective farmers’ houses in the evenings and told them the Soviet power had unlawfully arrested their own good and innocent priest; that there were no services in the churches, though when the farms were being organised the authorities had promised not to close the churches and that the priests would serve; and that since the farms were organised there had been no harvest and no bread, and the power had begun taking the bells off the churches, and still there was nothing — you came to the shop and it was empty.

Asked about a December 1937 conversation in the shop at Shestakovo, she said she had been in the shop but had made no counter-revolutionary or anti-Soviet statements, except that she had said their church had no priest yet, and that she had said even that in answer to the farmers’ questions. Told that the witnesses’ evidence convicted her, she said: she had carried on no counter-revolutionary or anti-Soviet activity, but she had called on the collective farmers to go to church and pray to God. On 4 March 1938 the troika sentenced her to be shot; she was moved to the Taganka prison in Moscow, and was shot on 14 March 1938 and buried in a common unmarked grave at Butovo.

Besides 14 марта her azbyka page keeps her on the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered at Butovo and the moveable synaxis of the saints of Moscow, and neither is a fixed day nor an offset from Pascha this corpus can hold.
