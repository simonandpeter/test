# Paisie of Sihăstria

Paisie of Sihăstria was born on 20 June 1897 in the village of Stroiești in the land of Botoșani, to the faithful Christians Ioan and Ecaterina Olaru, whom God had blessed with ten children. Being born near the feast of the Apostles Peter and Paul, he received the name Petru at his baptism. He went to the village primary school and was first in his class every year, receiving as his prize books of the Lives of the Saints, which made him love the monastic life.

In October 1921, at twenty-four, he entered the community of the skete of Cozancea near his native village. There he learned prayer, obedience, humility and the love of his neighbour, showing himself from the first eager to help both the brethren and all the pilgrims. Not long after he was granted the monastic habit he had longed for, being tonsured on 9 June 1922 with the name Paisie; and from his first years in the monastery he was known as a skilled guide of souls, sought out by many. Some of these became his disciples and grew greatly in the spiritual life, and among them was the brother Constantin Ilie, the future archimandrite [Cleopa](/saints/cleopa-of-sihastria).

In 1931, loving stillness greatly, he raised a little cell with a wooden chapel in the forest near the skete, where he often withdrew to struggle in fasting and prayer; and from that time he was known as the Hermit Father. In 1933 he took on a still harder rule of life, putting on the great schema. He was ordained hierodeacon on 14 October 1943 and hieromonk on 4 April 1947, and was then named abbot of the skete of Cozancea, which he led for nearly a decade.

In hard times, by his wisdom and by counsels breathed into him by God, he was known as a skilled spiritual guide. Between 1972 and 1985, loving solitude, he struggled in a more withdrawn place at the skete of Sihla, near the cave of Saint Theodora; but those who loved him sought him out there too, knowing that his blessing eased their souls and gave them strength to overcome the temptations of life. In the few moments when he was not beset by the faithful he found leisure to write pages full of the Spirit and many verses of repentance, and to work in the garden, making himself an example of diligence and humble service.

In the last years of his earthly life he was tried by much bodily suffering, which he took with great faith, showing his disciples a long and exemplary patience. In 1985 he returned to Sihăstria, where he went on caring for his spiritual children and giving them counsel to the last days of his life. In the autumn of 1990, on 18 October, at ninety-three years of age, he gave his soul into the hands of Christ, and is counted in the company of the venerable fathers.
