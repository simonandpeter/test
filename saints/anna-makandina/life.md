# Anna (Makandina)

Hers is the one name on the line of fourteen for which the Сретенский monastery’s calendar prints a life of its own, and the life page the day hangs behind all fourteen names is hers and nobody else’s. Anna Alexeyevna Makandina was born on 5 November 1892 in the village of Konstantinovo in the Alexandrov district of the province of Vladimir, the daughter of the peasant Alexy Makandin, and finished the village school. In 1914 she entered the Alexeyevsky monastery in Moscow, then standing in Verkhnyaya Krasnoselskaya street, as a novice, and all the time she lived there her obedience was in the kitchen.

In 1924 the monastery was closed by the godless power, and she settled with the nuns of it in a lodging, where they kept the monastic rule and order of life and earned their bread by sewing quilts. In 1930 the authorities resolved to arrest every man and woman of the closed monasteries, and she was taken on 28 December 1930. Asked whether she had belonged to political parties, with whom she lived and what she did, she answered that she had not belonged to them and did not; that she was deprived of the vote as a monastic; that her own sister lived with her and three more sisters of the monastery; and that they sewed quilts. «Занимаемую нами квартиру никто не посещал», she said — nobody visited their lodging, they kept up acquaintance with no one, and she had nothing to add.

The bill of indictment of 11 January said of all those charged that, being former monks of liquidated monasteries and metochia and living in crowds together, they had carried on active anti-Soviet work by organising illegal brotherhoods and sisterhoods, helping exiled sympathisers and agitating about religious persecutions by the Soviet power, and that their lodgings were a refuge for every kind of counter-revolutionary element. She was sentenced to three years’ exile in the Archangel region, and at the end of it, in 1934, she came home to Konstantinovo.

On 22 February 1938 she was arrested on a charge of spreading provocative rumours of the imminent fall of the Soviet power, and held first in the prison at Zagorsk and then in Moscow. False witnesses said she had told people that the Lord was punishing them so: the communists had made the collective farms, robbed the Orthodox, and now they worked day and night for nothing, and everything went to the communists, because people had renounced God and believed antichrist — better give up work, Orthodox, and go to church and pray to God.

The interrogator put four occasions to her and she answered each with what had really been said. In October 1937 she had been at day-labour, and remembered walking home from work with several others when the talk was that life in the collective farms had got better and the Soviet power had given the farmers a happy life; it was a private conversation and she had never spoken against the Soviet power. In December she had been chopping cabbage with the others and the talk was of war: she had said that the Japanese were coming against them, but that since the Soviet power had grown strong they would not let a war happen.

In November, by her own house in the evening, the talk with the collective farmers had been that life was better now than under the tsars, the taxes smaller and more of everything to be had; and nothing else was said, and she was at home most of the time. Asked whether she admitted making the leaders of the party and the government contemptible, she said: «Я к советской власти враждебно не настроена, я довольна советской властью... и виновной себя в антисоветской агитации не признаю.» On 8 March 1938 the troika sentenced her to be shot, and she was shot on 14 March 1938 and buried in a common unmarked grave.

She died at Butovo on the same day as her sister [Matrona (Makandina)](/saints/matrona-makandina), a novice of the same Moscow monastery, arrested on the same day in 1930 and exiled with her to the same region; it is azbyka.ru’s page for Matrona that says they died together, and the Sretensky life of Anna names the sister she lived with and does not name her. She was numbered with the saints at the jubilee council of bishops of August 2000 for veneration by the whole church. On her monastic grade the two hosts differ and no office is written: azbyka.ru heads her «послушница», a novice, which is what both lives call her throughout, while the calendar’s name link reads «инокиня».

Her azbyka page keeps her, besides 14 марта, on the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered at Butovo and the moveable synaxis of the saints of Moscow, and the Sretensky life page heads itself «Дни памяти: 26 января (Новомуч.) , 1 марта». None of those is a fixed day or a Pascha offset this corpus can hold, and the day this row records is the day whose page lists her.
