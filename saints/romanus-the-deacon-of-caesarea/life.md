# Romanus the Deacon of Caesarea

Doxologia's life gives him as a Palestinian by birth and a deacon of the church of Caesarea, who in the time of the persecution the pagans raised against the Christians was staying at Antioch, preaching the word of God and strengthening the faithful to stand unshaken in the confession of God. When the old eparch Asclepiades meant to pull the Christian church down to its foundations, Romanus told the faithful people to withstand him and not let him scatter the holy place.

He said to those of his own faith that the war before them was for the altars of God, since Asclepiades was the enemy of their patriarchate and wanted to take God away from them; and that they would do well to defend it. For this he was taken; and the account that follows is his trial and his torments, in which his tongue was cut out and he still spoke well.

He was thrown back into prison and stayed there a long while with his feet in the stocks. Afterwards it was told to the emperor Maximian that even with his tongue cut out he spoke well, and the emperor ordered him strangled; so the soldiers came in, put a rope about his neck and strangled him. So, the page says, the Martyr Romanus finished his contest of suffering for Christ. It gives no year. The calendar keeps him with [Plato](/saints/plato-of-ancyra) and prints one common of martyrs for the two of them.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 18 November, and tells the scene. saint.gr says Romanos lived at the beginning of the fourth century. Because Asklepiades, the eparch of Antioch, was shouting and blaspheming against Jesus Christ, Romanos's faithful heart took fire with holy indignation against him; and one day he waited for the moment when the eparch was about to enter the temple of the idols, and said to his face: the idols are not gods.

The idolatrous ruler, enraged, ordered Romanos's tongue cut out. But God by a miracle kept the power of speech in Romanos even without his tongue, so that when they shut him in prison he preached Christ to the jailers. The idolaters could not bear these wonders, and drowned the brave martyr, in 304. It was in the same exchange, the calendar says on another of its pages, that he told Asklepiades even the children know that the idols are not gods, and the governor asked a [child](/saints/holy-infant-18-november) which god it believed in.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him with the child on one line for the Julian 18 November, «Мчч. Романа диакона и отрока Варула (303).», and its life page makes him a deacon of the church of Caesarea in Palestine who moved to Antioch during one of the persecutions and there strengthened the Christians in the faith by his example and his fervent preaching. When Asklepiades, the governor of Antioch, purposed to destroy the Christian temple, he called the faithful to stand for their holy place, persuading them that if they kept it they would rejoice here on earth in the Church militant, and that if they perished in its defence they would rejoice in the triumphant Church in heaven; and seeing such resolution in the people the governor did not dare to carry out his intention.

Some while later, when a heathen festival had begun in the city and a multitude had come to Antioch from all the country round, he began to denounce the worship of idols and to call on everyone to follow Christ. He was seized and put to the torture, and during it he saw in the crowd [the Christian boy Varul](/saints/holy-infant-18-november) and said to the governor, pointing at him, that the young boy had more understanding than he who had reached old age, because the boy knew the true God while the governor worshipped idols. He himself was sentenced to be burnt, but a sudden downpour put the fire out; he began to glorify Christ and to revile the gods of the heathen; the governor ordered his tongue cut out, and deprived of his tongue he went on glorifying the Lord aloud. Then his tormentors sentenced him to be hanged.
