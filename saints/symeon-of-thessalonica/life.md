# Symeon

Symeon was born in Constantinople towards the end of the fourteenth century
and took the monastic habit there, living in a small cell, perhaps in the
monastery of the Xanthopouloi, Callistus and Ignatius, who were close to
him; he was a hesychast and a follower of the theology of Gregory Palamas,
was ordained hieromonk, and, the historians say, frequented the patriarchal
court. Between May 1416 and April 1417 he was consecrated archbishop of
Thessalonica. The new charge broke his health, but, as he said himself,
prayer to the great martyr Demetrius, the patron of his cathedral city,
restored him. In June 1422 he visited Constantinople and, on the way back,
the Holy Mountain. For thirteen years, until his death, he held the city to
the Orthodox Church under the temptation to put itself wholly under Rome for
protection against the Turks; through his efforts Thessalonica, besieged by
the sultan Murad II, did not surrender, and it was only after his death — in
September 1429, on the 15th — that the city was taken, in 1430. Besides his
pastoral work he left a rich body of writing, letters, commentaries,
encomia, dialogues and hymns, among them the book on the church building,
the treatise on the priesthood and the explanation of the Creed, and he was
numbered among the saints on 14 April 1981. The Greek and Romanian calendars
keep him on 15 September, the Romanian life counting him the last disciple
of Gregory Palamas and an important theologian of his time; the Russian
calendar keeps him the same day of the old style, 28 September.
