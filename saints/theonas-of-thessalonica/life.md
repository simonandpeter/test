# Theonas of Thessalonica

Theonas was born in the second half of the fifteenth century; by some accounts his birthplace was the island of Lesbos, by others Veria. He began the monastic life on Athos at the monastery of the Pantocrator, at the kathisma of Saint Onuphrius, and was ordained priest there. At that time the venerable Jacob was struggling at the skete of Iviron, and the fame of that father brought Theonas to him to become his disciple; and when Jacob went out into the world to preach the word of God, his disciple followed him.

After his teacher's martyric end, on 1 November 1519, Theonas became the guide of the remaining disciples, and for a short while in 1520 the superior of the monastery of the Honourable Forerunner at Dervekista in Aetolia. Then he returned with Jacob's disciples to the Holy Mountain, to the monastery of Simonos Petras; and after about three years, in 1522, he left the Mountain again and moved to the monastery of Saint Anastasia the Deliverer from Potions, near Thessalonica, which he restored.

As superior there he built a school and a hospital, and several chapels around the monastery — of the Holy Trinity, of the Holy Apostles, of Saints Cyricus and Julitta, of Saint Anthony, of Saint George, of Saint Demetrius and of Saint Elias — and founded a small skete to which he would withdraw for stillness. In that time about a hundred and fifty disciples gathered around him.

For the holiness of his life he was enthroned over Thessalonica around the year 1537, and for about seven years he shepherded the flock entrusted to him with holy wisdom and led many towards salvation. The calendar line for the day calls him archbishop and the life page calls him metropolitan; both words stand in the source and neither is corrected here.

He departed to the Lord on 4 April 1542. His relics are kept whole at the monastery of Saint Anastasia the Deliverer from Potions, where the page says they give off an indescribable fragrance and pour out miracles and healings on those who come to his reliquary in faith. The page adds that his memory is kept on 4 April and also on the fourth Sunday of Great Lent, which moves with Pascha and is not held here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr is franker than most about what is not known. Quoting Nicodemus the Hagiorite, who wrote his life, it says that «τίνα μὲν εἶχε πατρίδα ἐπὶ τὴ γῆ, ἢ τίνας γονεῖς, ἢ μὲ ποὶον τρόπον ἐγένετο ἀρχιερεὺς τῆς Θεσσαλονίκης… δὲν ἐμάθομεν» — what homeland he had on earth, or what parents, or how he came to be hierarch of Thessalonica, we have not learnt, from any written history or any tradition. One tradition makes him a man of Mytilene, for which many later researchers call him the Lesbian, either because he came from Lesbos or because he stayed there as a confessor at Plomari; the page guesses he may have been born in the second half of the fifteenth century, and says everything before his going to the Holy Mountain is unknown.

On Athos he practised asceticism at the monastery of Pantokrator as a presbyter, and then left it to be numbered in the company of James the New Martyr, whom the same calendar keeps on 1 November and who was living above the monastery of Iviron at the little house of the Forerunner. In 1518 James and six of his disciples, Theonas among them, left that skete for the inner parts of Athos and then, after a vision, left the Holy Mountain altogether, going by Thessalonica and the road to Thessaly, past the castle of Petra and Meteora, to the monastery of the Forerunner at Dervekista in Aetolia, where they stayed a year.

Theonas was the most faithful and the best of the disciples, and was sent to Akakios, bishop of Arta, for a letter of licence for the work among the Christians of that country; but Akakios came to envy James his standing with the people, took up the slanders of some false monks and denounced him to the Turks as a rebel.

James and two disciples were seized and held forty days at Trikala, where Theonas and Markianos visited them; James prophesied that the brotherhood would leave the monastery and gather at a house near Thessalonica, and by letter appointed Theonas his successor and abbot. On 1 November 1519 James and his two disciples were hanged after frightful torture. The next year Theonas and the company of Dervekista went, as the prophecy had said, to Simonopetra on Athos, recovering the three martyrs' relics on the way.

In 1522, for want of necessities and for the prophecy's sake, they left Simonopetra with the relics for the country round Thessalonica and settled at the monastery of St Anastasia the Deliverer from Poisons, then «μονύδριον μικρότατον, παλαιότατον καὶ σεσαθρωμένον» — a very small, very old and rotted little house — which they rebuilt from the foundations, with cells enough that a hundred and fifty brothers came together there in the common life.

He is attested as abbot of St Anastasia's down to 1535, so his raising to the throne of Thessalonica must have come after that year, since Ioasaph was metropolitan until then and a document of 1538 names Theonas in the office. He was not long on the throne: he is attested as metropolitan in May 1541 and spoken of as already reposed in April 1542, so the page puts his falling asleep about the middle of 1541. His relic was carried in a wonderful manner to the monastery of St Anastasia immediately after his death; in 1821 it was taken to Skopelos, from there to Esphigmenou on Athos, and back again to St Anastasia's, where it is kept to this day.
