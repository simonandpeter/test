# Agathonicus of Nicomedia

Agathonicus was one of the martyrs kept together on 22 August — Agathonicus, Zoticus, Theoprepius (Bogolep), Acindynus, Severian, Zeno and others — who died for Christ under Maximian. Agathonicus, of the noble family of the Hippasii, lived at Nicomedia; learned in the Scriptures, he turned many pagans to faith in Christ, among them the first member of the Senate, the princeps.

The count Eutolmius, sent to the Pontic country, crucified at Karpen the disciples of the Christian Zoticus who refused to sacrifice to the idols, and brought Zoticus to Nicomedia, where — the Prologue carries on — he seized and bound Agathonicus, Principus, Theoprepius, Acindynus, Severian, Zeno and many others, and led them bound towards Byzantium; on the way Zoticus, Theoprepius and Acindynus died of their wounds and exhaustion, Severian was killed near Chalcedon, and Agathonicus with the rest was taken to Thrace, to Selymbria, where after torture before the emperor himself they were beheaded.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 22 August with his company, and doxologia.ro prints one life under five of their names. It says Agathonicus, the martyr of Christ, was in Nicomedia in the days of the emperor Maximian, turning the Greeks from the idols and bringing them to Christ, and was seized by the count Eutolmius. That count, sent by the emperor into the country of Pontus to kill Christians, came by ship to a place called Carpin and there found [Zoticus](/saints/zoticus-of-nicomedia) with his disciples confessing Christ; he condemned them to death on the cross and took Zoticus back with him to Nicomedia. There he seized one Princeps, whom Agathonicus had taught the Christian faith, and bound him together with Agathonicus and the other Christians, among whom were [Theoprepius](/saints/theoprepius-of-nicomedia), [Acindynus](/saints/acindynus-of-nicomedia) and [Severian](/saints/severian-of-nicomedia), and led them into Thrace, where the emperor had come, to torture them. In the country called Potama the count killed Zoticus, Theoprepius and Acindynus, because they could no longer walk for the beatings they had been given; near Chalcedon he killed Severian, who confessed Christ boldly; and Agathonicus and the rest he took to Byzantium and, having tortured them there, on to Selymbria, to the place called Amus, where Maximian was, and there they were beheaded.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day, 22 August, and gives the march that gathered them. saint.gr says that when the count Eutolmios was returning from his circuit in Pontus, where he had gone to hunt Christians down, he stopped at Karpe, and there he found [Zoticus](/saints/zoticus-of-nicomedia) and put him to death together with his disciples. At Karpe he learned that the prince of the city had been made a Christian by a certain Agathonicus, who was from Nicomedia.

So he arrested the prince and Agathonicus, and having punished them along with other Christians he led the whole party off to the king, who was then in Thrace. But on the road, near a village called Potamos, he killed [Zeno](/saints/zeno-the-martyr-22-august), [Theoprepius](/saints/theoprepius-of-nicomedia), [Acindynus](/saints/acindynus-of-nicomedia) and [Severian](/saints/severian-of-nicomedia), because from the many wounds in their feet they could no longer walk.

When he reached the village of Ammous near Silyvria he beheaded Agathonicus, the prince and the other Christians by royal order, bound as they were. So all of them followed the example of the slain Lamb, Christ, and will worthily be numbered with those who are «γεγραμμένοι εν τω βιβλίω της ζωής του αρνίου», written in the book of the life of the Lamb. The page names neither the prince nor the disciples who died with Zoticus, and gives no year; it records the skull of Agathonicus at the church of the Forerunner at Kavala and a portion of his relic at the monastery of Mega Spilaio at Kalavryta.
