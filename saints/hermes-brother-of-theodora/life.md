# Hermes, brother of Theodora

saint.gr's whole notice for the two of them is two sentences: the holy martyrs Hermes and [Theodora](/saints/theodora-sister-of-hermes) came of a distinguished family of Rome, and they suffered a martyr's death in the year 132, under the emperor Hadrian, who reigned from 117 to 138. The day's own line adds one word about them — αυτάδελφοι, brother and sister of the same parents — and that is the whole of what the Greek calendar says.

There is no manner of death, no persecutor but the emperor, no place of burial, no relic, no hymn and not even the distich the site usually prints. What stands recorded is a name, a sister, a city, a rank and a year; the year falls well inside the reign the page names beside it, which is the only check there is on it.

The corpus keeps other men whose names fold onto his — the apostle Hermes of Dalmatia whom Paul names in the letter to the Romans, kept by this same Greek calendar in March, and Hermes of Bononia — and neither of them is a Roman of a distinguished house killed in 132 with his sister.
