# Achillas

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 17 ianuarie reads «Sfântul Cuvios Ahila», and his own page on the site carries no life, no icons and no articles about him — only the day, the rank *Cuvios*, which is the Romanian for venerable, and the readings appointed: the Apostle, Galatians 5:22–26 and 6:1–2, and the Gospel, Matthew 11:27–30. Where and when he lived the page does not say.

The rank is the whole of what the calendar claims about his manner of life, and the line does not say which Achillas is meant. This folder records what the Romanian calendar prints for the day and nothing beyond it; a fuller account would have to come from a source that has not been read here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and says who he was, where the Romanian one does not. saint.gr calls him an anchorite of the desert in the fifth century, who lived a holy life in Egypt and fell asleep in peace, and it prints two sayings gathered about him by Nicodemus the Hagiorite out of the Evergetinos. In the first, Abba Achillas found Abba Isaiah eating, with nothing in his bowl but salt and water, and Isaiah hid the bowl behind the basket he was weaving so as not to scandalise him; when Achillas learned that the man had been cutting palm-fronds in the heat and could not get dry bread down without wetting it, he called the fathers of Sketis to come and see Isaiah eating bread in Sketis, and told him that if he wished to eat bread he should go to Egypt. In the second, an elder found him spitting blood, and Achillas told him that the blood was a hard word a brother had spoken to grieve him, which he had struggled to reveal to nobody, praying God to lift the memory of it away; the word had turned to blood in his mouth, and now that he had spat it out he was at rest and the grief forgotten.
