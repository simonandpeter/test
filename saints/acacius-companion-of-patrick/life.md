# Acacius, companion of Patrick of Prusa

Patrick, bishop of Prusa in Bithynia, did not take up the work of the Gospel alone. saint.gr's account of 19 Μαΐου names three ready fellow-workers who went with him — Acacius, Menander and Polyaenus — and says that with them Patrick brought many idolaters to the Christian faith. That was denounced to the governor Julian the Consular, and the four of them were arrested at once.

The governor tried to argue Patrick out of the faith rather than torture him out of it, setting philosophical questions to prove that Christ is not God. Patrick had the rhetorical skill and the theological grounding for that argument, and overturned the governor's points one after another. Julian, seeing himself broken on the ground he had himself chosen, gave the order, and Patrick was beheaded together with his three fellow-workers.

Of Acacius himself saint.gr prints no separate life. What it gives him besides the shared narrative is a couplet of the synaxarion — «Kαλού μετέσχες Aκάκιε του τέλους, Aθλητικόν γαρ τούτο σοι διά ξίφους», you had a share in a good end, Acacius, for this was a contestant's end, by the sword — and the apolytikion that names him with Patrick and the others. The page records no country, no year and no burial place for him.
