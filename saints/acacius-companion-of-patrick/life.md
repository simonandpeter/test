# Acacius, companion of Patrick of Prusa

Patrick, bishop of Prusa in Bithynia, did not take up the work of the Gospel alone. saint.gr's account of 19 Μαΐου names three ready fellow-workers who went with him — Acacius, Menander and Polyaenus — and says that with them Patrick brought many idolaters to the Christian faith. That was denounced to the governor Julian the Consular, and the four of them were arrested at once.

The governor tried to argue Patrick out of the faith rather than torture him out of it, setting philosophical questions to prove that Christ is not God. Patrick had the rhetorical skill and the theological grounding for that argument, and overturned the governor's points one after another. Julian, seeing himself broken on the ground he had himself chosen, gave the order, and Patrick was beheaded together with his three fellow-workers.

Of Acacius himself saint.gr prints no separate life. What it gives him besides the shared narrative is a couplet of the synaxarion — «Kαλού μετέσχες Aκάκιε του τέλους, Aθλητικόν γαρ τούτο σοι διά ξίφους», you had a share in a good end, Acacius, for this was a contestant's end, by the sword — and the apolytikion that names him with Patrick and the others. The page records no country, no year and no burial place for him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 19 May, in an entry headed for [Patrick](/saints/patrick-of-prusa), bishop of Brusa, with his three presbyters Acacius, [Menander](/saints/menander-companion-of-patrick) and [Polyaenus](/saints/polyaenus-companion-of-patrick) — whose names stand in the heading and nowhere else in the entry, which speaks of them only as the other three of his presbyters and as these guileless followers of Christ. They suffered for the faith of Christ, it says, in the time of Julian the Apostate, at Brusa in Asia.

When the imperial deputy, whom this book calls Junius, had Patrick thrown into the boiling spring and saw him come out unhurt, he was enraged with shame and ordered that the bishop and his three presbyters have their heads struck off with an axe; and they read a prayer and laid their heads under the executioner’s axe. It is this book, and not the Romanian or the Greek, that calls the three of them presbyters outright. The heading names him first of the three.

The Russian calendar names him first of the three presbyters taken with their bishop at Prusa in Bithynia for preaching Christ openly and reproving the errors of the pagans. The four of them were brought before the governor of the city, Юлий, who was setting out for the hot springs to take a cure and had them led after him in iron chains. When he had bathed and sacrificed to his gods he called them up and offered them the same, with torture threatened for a refusal; the bishop answered for them that he was a Christian and worshipped the God who had made the warm springs too, and was thrown into the spring and came out unharmed. The governor then had the bishop and the three presbyters beheaded. The calendar gives him no birthplace, no age and no year of his own, and sets the company in the first century.
