# James (Brilliantov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him fifteenth of twenty-four, the last of the presbyters, and gives no life; the life here is the one azbyka.ru prints for the civil 2 декабря, out of fond.ru and the Moscow diocese’s lives. He was born on 7 March 1871 in the village of Nikulino in the Bronnitsy district of the province of Moscow, and finished the Moscow seminary in 1893. After his ordination he served until 1927 at the Vladychny convent in Serpukhov; when it was closed he was appointed to the Trinity church in the village of Luzhki, and in 1929 moved to the Theophany church of Kolomna, where he acted as dean for two months in 1931.

In 1930 the Kolomna OGPU tried to recruit him. He was called in in April and asked which of the merchants and priests he knew were engaged in counter-revolution; he refused to say anything and was let go. How often they talked to him is not known, but on 5 June he signed a paper of cooperation, understanding it in quite another sense than the OGPU did. On 12 December 1931 he was called in again and arrested, and told the investigator that he would not be a spy and a provocateur, that he had denounced nobody since April 1930, that if he knew of anything against the state he would as a citizen report it, and that he thought he would have to suffer under the Soviet power for his own truthfulness. He was let out on 3 January 1932, on the thought that prison would mend him.

On 29 March 1932 he was arrested and put in the Kolomna house of detention and questioned the same day. He confirmed what he had said in December and added that he had understood the undertaking he signed as a duty to report plots against the Soviet power, of which there had been none among the people he moved with; that there was and had been discontent with particular measures, which he had not thought it necessary to report, telling the discontented to be patient; and that the clergy were all frightened by the arrests and afraid to speak openly. On 1 April a priest of Kolomna, Michael Pokrovsky, who said himself that he hardly knew him, signed the testimony the investigation wanted and slandered him outright.

At the questioning of 8 April he was asked about his dealings with the clergy of Kolomna already arrested, and answered that he had heard of the split there before he came, that he had written to his brother-in-law the priest Mirolyubov urging that the Soviet power be commemorated at the service as the tsar’s had been, and that he had begun commemorating it himself in 1925, two or three years before the metropolitan Sergius required it. When the faithful complained that the Bolsheviks closed churches and made them work on feasts, he told them the fault was their own. Asked whether he would feel no moral degradation in helping the Soviet power fight its class enemies, he said: «Я как священник нахожу, что для меня несовместимо всеми мерами работать для советской власти».

The indictment of 19 April put his offence this way: that the priest Brilliantov, «являясь нашим секретным осведомителем», had both ignored the obligations he had taken on and carried anti-Soviet agitation among the clergy and the faithful. On 8 May 1932 the troika sentenced him to three years of exile in Kazakhstan. When the term was out he came back to the Moscow region, settled in the village of Gory in the Ozery district and was appointed to the church of Saint Sergius of Radonezh there.

He was arrested on 21 November 1937 and put in the Kolomna prison. Asked whether he had ever in his life done socially useful work he answered that he had not; asked whether he admitted counter-revolutionary agitation among the people he said he did not, «для меня каждая власть от Бога», and that the facts put to him had never been spoken by him. On 23 November 1937 the troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 2 December 1937 at the Butovo range near Moscow and buried in a common unmarked grave.
