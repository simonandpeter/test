# Theodosios the New of Argos

saint.gr says the venerable Theodosios the New, the healer, was born at Athens in 862 of pious Christian parents, and that from a young age his warm faith and his great love towards his fellow men were plain. When he decided to withdraw into the monastic life he shared out his property among those in need and went a little outside Athens; but so many came to see him and take his counsel that he could not keep to his contemplation, and for that reason, to be able to live as a monk, he took refuge at Argos.

There he built a church in the name of the Honourable Forerunner, where again many came for his counsel. Priests grew envious of him and denounced him to [Peter](/saints/peter-of-argos), archbishop of Argos, as a magician and a swindler. Theodosios then appeared in Peter's sleep — the archbishop being at that time in Constantinople on a visit to the patriarch — and made himself known to him, so that he should leave off his unjust judgement; and at the same hour the patriarch asked Peter whether he had a certain monk Theodosios in his province, and when Peter said yes and told the dream, the patriarch asked him to carry his blessing and his esteem to the venerable one.

Back at Argos, Peter decided to visit the place of his ascetic labour at the village of Panariti. Word reached Theodosios, who hurried to meet him a short way from his cell; and where the hierarch had sat down to rest, he saw the venerable one come up to him carrying his monastic cap in his hands, with live coals in it burning incense in place of a censer, and censing him — and by a strange thing neither the cap nor his hand was burnt. So the hierarch was assured of the holiness of the ascetic, and after a heartfelt embrace he ordained him deacon and priest.

The divine Theodosios reached deep old age and became famous for his virtue and his wonders through the whole region. He was granted to foresee his death three days before, and gathered his disciples to give them his last counsels and commands; and among them, as they mourned the parting, he went to the eternal blessedness murmuring, Lord, into Your hands I commit my spirit. His funeral was attended by the whole people, with Peter present and a multitude of priests and monks. His body was laid in the church of the Honourable Forerunner, and his tomb was shown to be an unfailing spring of healings down the centuries: the paralysed rose, the blind saw, the childless had children, and many sick found their cure.
