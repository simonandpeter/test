# Flavian the Recluse

saint.gr prints two Flavians on 16 February and this is the second of them. Its line for him is bare, «Όσιος Φλαβιανός», and the page behind it tells only how he lived: he went up to the summit of a mountain, where he built a small cell, and shut himself inside it and passed sixty whole years there in severe fasting and prayer, without coming into contact with any man. From some opening he would put out only his hands, to take in his food, which was soaked pulses once a week.

God endowed him with the gift of wonder-working, the page says, and it names four wonders: by his prayer he killed a great serpent, drove off from a field the locusts that would have destroyed it, cast the demon out of a young man, and healed a woman's breast of a cancer. So, having lived in holiness, he gave up his blessed soul to God. Its couplet says that having passed through the time of this vain life, Flavian lives for the age that is time without end.

The page gives him no country, no mountain by name, no century and no hymn. He is not [Flavian](/saints/flavian-of-constantinople) the patriarch of Constantinople, whom the same Greek day keeps and who died of his wounds in 449; nothing on either page joins them, and this one is an anchorite who died in peace.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 16 Φεβρουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/02/16/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3727/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
