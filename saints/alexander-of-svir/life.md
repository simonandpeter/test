# Alexander of Svir

Alexander was born on 15 June 1448, the day of the prophet Amos, and named
Amos at baptism, to Stephen and Vassa, peasants of Mandera on the river Oyat
by Lake Ladoga. Their older children were grown and gone; they had prayed for
another son, and heard a voice — rejoice, good couple, you will bear a son in
whose birth God will give comfort to his churches. The boy was obedient and
gentle, kept away from games and rough talk, wore poor clothes and fasted
until his mother worried for him. Monks of Valaam who came to the Oyat on
monastery business told him about the life of a skete and the life of a
hermit, and when he learned that a marriage had been arranged for him he
slipped away to Valaam at nineteen, an angel in the form of a traveller
showing him the road across to the island. Seven years a novice — working by
day, at prayer by night, sometimes praying bare-chested in the forest among
the mosquitoes until Matins — he was tonsured Alexander in 1474. His parents
found out where he had gone from some Karelians and, following him, were
themselves tonsured, as Sergius and Barbara.

After their deaths he lived alone on an island in a cleft of the rock, and in
1485 left Valaam altogether for a place in the forest by the lake that came
to be called Holy Lake, where he lived seven years in a hut on what the
forest gave, through hunger, frost, sickness and the demons’ temptations;
once, unable even to lift his head for a pain in his stomach, he lay and
chanted psalms until an angel laid a hand on the place and healed him. A
landowner out hunting deer, Andrew Zavalishin, found the cell in 1493,
having seen a pillar of fire over it, and drew the story from him under a
promise of silence he could not keep; Andrew went to Valaam himself and
became the monk Adrian, and disciples began to gather. In 1508, twenty-three
years after he had come, three men in shining white appeared to Alexander at
prayer, each with a staff, and told him to build a church and a monastery in
the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit — an appearance of the
Trinity, the life says, without another like it in the history of the
Russian church. He was ordained that year at Novgorod by Archbishop
Serapion, and made igumen against his will; as igumen he wore rags, slept on
the ground, kneaded the dough, chopped the wood, carried the water, ground
wheat by hand at night while the others slept, and tapped softly on the
door of any cell where he heard idle talk. Late in his life, while a stone
church of the Protection was being built, the Mother of God appeared to him
on its foundations with the child in her arms, and promised the monastery
would never want.

He foresaw his death a year ahead, asked to be bound by the feet and thrown
into the marsh under moss, was refused, and was buried instead by the
Transfiguration church. He died on 30 August 1533 at eighty-five; his
disciple and successor Herodion wrote his life in 1545, the whole church
kept him from 1547, and his relics were found incorrupt on 17 April 1641,
taken by the Bolsheviks in December 1918, and found again, still incorrupt,
in December 1997. The Romanian life adds that he gave himself wholly to God
and the brethren, his days in the monastery’s obediences and his nights in
vigil, prayer and psalmody, so that one might have thought him an angel in
the flesh; the Greek synaxarion keeps him on the same day and has no details.
