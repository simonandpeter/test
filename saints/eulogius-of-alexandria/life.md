# Eulogius of Alexandria

Doxologia's life of him is a single short paragraph. This saint lived in the reign of the emperor Heraclius, and was archbishop of Alexandria before John the Merciful. He worked many wonders, and the page tells one of them at length, as the thing worth remembering about him.

The most venerable pope Leo had written for the Council of Chalcedon the Epistle of Orthodoxy; and Eulogius, reading it, not only praised it and received it but preached it to everyone. God, wishing to comfort them both, sent an angel in the shape of Leo's archdeacon to thank Eulogius for having received that letter; and Eulogius spoke with the angel of God as with a man, taking him for the pope's deacon. After the angel had become invisible to him, he gave thanks the more to God for the wonder, and gave up his soul into his hands.

That is all the page prints. It gives no year of birth or death, names no place but Alexandria, and carries no hymn for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives the years the Romanian page had not: saint.gr has him a Syrian who served as presbyter at Antioch, living under Tiberius I the Thracian, Maurice and Phocas, and archbishop of Alexandria in the years 579 to 607, before [John the Merciful](/saints/john-the-merciful-of-alexandria). It tells one miracle of him. When Leo, bishop of Rome, wrote his famous letter of orthodoxy and sent it to the Fourth Ecumenical Council at Chalcedon, Eulogius read it and not only praised it but proclaimed it to everyone; and God, wanting to gladden both men, sent an angel to Eulogius in the form of Leo's archdeacon, thanking him for accepting the letter. Eulogius talked with the angel taking him for the pope's archdeacon, and only when he vanished understood what he had been; and having given thanks to God he gave up his holy soul into his hands in 607. The page also lists what survives of his writing: seven chapters on the two natures of Christ, fragments on the Trinity and the divine economy, and a homily of doubtful authenticity.
