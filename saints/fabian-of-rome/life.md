# Fabian of Rome

Fabian became bishop of Rome after the death of Saint Antherus. Bound by brotherly love to Saint Pontius, he shared out with him the whole of his property among the poor. In the days of the emperors Gordian and Philip he lived through a time of peace for the Church, and he baptised the emperor Philip and his son; and with their permission he pulled down the statue of Jupiter and raised a church on the place of the pagan temple.

The persecution began again under the emperor Decius, and in the year 250 Fabian, bishop of Rome, gave his life for Christ and received the crown of martyrdom. That paragraph is all that doxologia.ro prints of him: his page carries no Viață tab, no hymn and nothing further, only the epistle and gospel of the day.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 5 August, as the Romanian does, and gives him two sentences — one of which argues with the day it stands on. It names him «Άγιος Φάβιος ο Ιερομάρτυρας επίσκοπος Ρώμης», says that Fabius, or more correctly Fabianus, was martyred by the sword, and sets a couplet above it: Fabius lies dead, the sacrificer of Christ, brought to Christ as a new offering by the blade. Then it adds that in the lists of the popes, pope Fabianus is counted among the martyrs, martyred under Decius on the twentieth of January, citing the twenty-ninth chapter of the Ecclesiastical History of Eusebius. Both readings are the page's own; it does not say which day it prefers, and the year it prints beside the name, 236 to 350, is a slip for 250, the year under Decius that the Romanian source already gives.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him the second entry of its Julian 5 August and is the first of this folder’s three books to tell a life rather than print a line. He was a Roman by birth. At first he was a village priest, and afterwards, when at the election of a pope a white dove came down upon him, he was chosen pope. He was meek and without malice.

With great diligence he gathered the bodies of the holy martyrs, buried them honourably and raised churches over their graves; and in the same way he raised oratories and chapels in the caves where the martyrs had hidden in the time of the fierce persecution. He baptised the emperor Philip and the emperor’s son, and with the help of the baptised senator [Pontius](/saints/pontius-the-senator) he threw down many idols and many temples of the idols. It was this Fabian, the entry ends, who laid down that the chrism should be consecrated on Great Thursday.

But when the wicked Decius came to the throne a terrible persecution of the Christians began, and in it he suffered too, beheaded with the sword in 250. That is the year this folder already held from doxologia, and the Prologue gives it over again.
