# Joseph the Presbyter of Persia

Shapur, the ill-believing emperor of the Persians, raised a great persecution against the Christians of his land. In the very days of Christ's saving Passion he sent an order into every part of his dominion that all who confessed Christ in Persia should be killed; and a multitude of Christians were killed without sparing through the villages and the cities, for the servants of the idols and the magi grew the more savage against them and searched eagerly for those in hiding, dragging them out of houses and hidden places and killing them with cruel torments, while others of the faithful gave themselves up of their own accord and poured out their blood eagerly.

Many of those in the royal palaces were killed for Christ, and with them Azad the eunuch, whom the emperor loved greatly, a true servant of Jesus Christ and a confessor of his most holy name. Learning of the death of his beloved eunuch Azad, the emperor was very sorry for him, and stopped the killing of the people at large, but ordered that only the preachers and the teachers of the Christian faith should be sought out for death. The order being published everywhere, the chiefs of the magi and the sorcerers went eagerly through all Persia looking for the Christian bishops and priests and killing them with every kind of torment.

So the bishop Acepsimas was taken, and soon after him the presbyter Joseph, who was seventy years old, and likewise Aithalas the deacon; both of them, the life says, just and holy men, whose zeal for God was like the zeal of the prophet Elijah, whose look was honourable and whose word was like a two-edged sword cutting through unbelief. They were brought before the chief of the magi, who looked at them with pride and terrible eyes and said, "You lost race! Why do you go round the simple people and deceive them with your unjust teachings?" The presbyter answered that they did not deceive the people but turned them from deceit to the true God, who is Lord and Maker of the sun and of the fire and of all creation, visible and invisible, and that a teaching about the one just God is not unjust.

After long beating Joseph was bound with two iron chains and thrown into the prison beside Acepsimas, where they encouraged one another for the easing of their pains and comforted themselves with the hope of God's mercy. Then Aithalas the deacon was left bound before the chief of the magi, and asked whether he would worship the great sun and taste of the blood of the sacrifices or stay stiff and unbending like the others; and he answered that it was the work of his questioner's unclean grey hairs to drink blood and to have for a god what God had made, and that he would not honour the creature above the Creator. His hands were bound under his knees, a great beam put through the binding and six soldiers set to jump on him and trample him, so that his joints came apart, and then he was beaten with thorned staves until his flesh was broken.

Five days later the saints were brought out of prison to the place called paradise, where the temple of the fire-god stood that the pagan Persians honoured, and the chief of the magi asked whether they would stay in their madness or become one with him. They answered that they were now in the mind they had had from the beginning, and would remain in it to the end, and that neither threats nor torments nor gifts would change their good confession. So he found a new torture: they were bound with ropes under the arms and about the thighs and the shins, and a beam was put through the ropes and turned, so that their bones cracked and those standing far off heard it; and they were tortured so from the third hour to the sixth, and were then carried back like sacks and thrown into the prison.

The judge ordered the guards to let nobody in to them, and that whoever brought them food or drink or clothing should be killed at once. So the sufferers of Christ spent three years in that prison in great want, failing from hunger and thirst and the rotting of their wounds and bearing pain beyond measure, until even the guards were moved at the sight of it and often wept, but were afraid to bring them any relief for fear of their lord; only in secret one or another of them sometimes gave them a piece of bread or a little water.

After three years Shapur came there, and with him Ardasabor, the greatest lord over all the magi of Persia. Hearing of the martyrs in the prison he had them brought before him, and when they came out they were like reeds shaken by the wind, so weak had they grown; unable to walk, they were carried by others, and their look was a marvel, their faces like those of the dead who lie in graves, their eyes sunk into their heads and their bones covered with skin only. "Are you Christians?" he asked; and they answered that they were, and believed in the living God and worshipped him. He set before them the state their faith had brought them to and advised them to worship the most shining sun, or die a cruel death; and with one mouth they renounced his impiety and confessed the true God, who made not the sun only but all creation, and showed themselves ready to die for the Maker of all.

After many exhortations Ardasabor had the bishop [Acepsimas](/saints/acepsimas-of-naeson) beaten and beheaded; and after that, at the governor's order, the presbyter Joseph was beaten, while the soldiers shouted that he would live if he submitted to the emperor's wish. He shouted in answer: "One is God and there is none beside him, in whom we live and move and have our being. In him we believe and him we honour, and for the emperor's command we care nothing." Being beaten a long while he weakened and became like a dead man, and everybody supposing him dead they carried him out and threw him into the market place; then, seeing that he still breathed, they took him and threw him back into the prison.

Ardasabor, marvelling at the endurance of the deacon [Aithalas](/saints/aithalas-the-deacon), looked to a beloved friend of his named Adesh, from the city called Arbela, and told him to take these two Christians, Joseph and Aithalas, into his own city and have them stoned to death by Christians — for this was why he had not had them cut down with the sword, that the teachers of the Christians should receive their end at Christian hands. Adesh loaded them on animals like sheaves or lifeless wood, for they could neither walk nor sit, their limbs being ruined with countless torments, and took them to his city; and on the road, when it was time to rest or to spend the night, he took them off the animals like dead bodies and laid them on the ground, for they could move neither hands nor feet, and if anybody out of pity wished to give them food or a little water, it had to be put into their mouths by hand.

Arriving at Arbela he threw them into a filthy prison and let no Christian go to them or take any care of them, so that their wounds rotted badly and blood ran from them and there was nobody to bind them, or to turn them over, or to quench their thirst with a cup of cold water. But there was in that city a faithful and holy woman named Sinandulia, who secretly fed those who suffered for Christ in the prisons. Learning that Joseph and Aithalas were there, she came by night with her servants, gave the guards much gold, went in and found the saints alive, saying nothing, only breathing a little; and she begged the guards to let her take them a short while to her house, promising to bring them back before daybreak.

The guard allowed it, and she took them to her house, which was not far from the prison, laid them on a bed, washed their wounds and wiped the blood with a clean cloth, and anointed her own body with their blood; then she anointed their bodies with costly myrrh and bound up the wounds of their broken hands and feet, and wept over them. Their pain easing a little, Joseph came to himself and, seeing the faithful woman weeping and lamenting bitterly beside him, said that her kindness was acceptable to God and to those who suffered for him, but that to lament so bitterly for them was not fitting, being far from their faith and from the true Christian hope. She answered that she rejoiced that Christ had given them such courage as to bear the cruellest torments, and would rejoice still more to see them finish their suffering to the end; but weeping is human, and comes of pity. Before dawn they were carried back to the prison.

Six months on, their wounds were a little healed and they could stand and walk a little, only Aithalas's hands were like relics. Then Adesh was removed from his office, and in his place came a harsher judge named Zvrot; and as sacrifices were being offered in the fire-god's temple when he entered the city, the priests told him of the two Christian teachers in the prison who had been tortured by Ardasabor and sent there to be stoned by Christians, but had not been brought out because they could not walk, and had been left to heal in the hope of winning them over. He had them brought before him and pressed them long with coaxing and with threats to worship the fire and taste of the sacrifices; and getting nowhere, he had Joseph hung up naked, head downwards, and beaten with ox sinews, so that the wounds that had a little healed were opened again and the blood ran as from a spring.

One of the magi standing by came up and said gently that if he was ashamed to go into their god's temple before all the people and sacrifice with them, he might promise to do it in secret and be free of torments; and the saint cried out with a great voice, "Depart from me, all you that work iniquity, for the Lord has heard my prayer." He was beaten three hours, and then untied and carried back to the prison, and Aithalas was hung up in his place and beaten long without sparing, crying without ceasing, "I am a Christian!" After some time both were brought again before the judge, who told them to have mercy on themselves and taste of the blood of the sacrifices; and they answered with one voice that it is not fitting for a man to drink blood, but only for dogs that eat carrion.

The judge and his counsellors then gave the last sentence upon them, that they should be stoned to death by Christians. Joseph said that he wished to tell the judge something in secret, and the judge ran to him, believing he would consent to their impiety; the servants lifted him from the ground and the judge bent over him, and the saint, filled with grief, spat in his face and said, "You are shameless and inhuman; are you not ashamed to fall so cruelly upon human nature and pour out your anger on men who scarcely breathe?" Ashamed, the judge went back to his place and sent a crowd of servants through the houses of the Christians to bring them by force to the court, to stone Joseph.

They carried him a little way off from the court to a wider place, dug a pit and set him in it, for he could not stand; then they bound his hands behind his back and set the Christians in a ring about him, forcing stones into their hands and ordering them to throw and to kill him, which they did, though against their will. The blessed Sinandulia was brought there too and forced to throw stones at him, and she cried out that from the beginning of the world such a thing had not been heard, that a woman be forced to lift her hand against holy men — "you who, instead of fighting your enemies, have made war on us, and fill with blood and murder a country that is at peace." Then the pagans drove a spike into a long reed and gave it her, to prod the saint from a distance; and she said, "I would sooner drive this spear into my own heart than touch his holy and innocent body."

So they threw a multitude of stones about him until only his head could be seen; and one of the pagans lifted a great stone above him and struck him on the head with it and crushed it, and so the good soldier gave up his spirit into the hands of the Lord. Guards were set to watch the martyr's body like a treasure, that the faithful should not steal it. On the fourth day there was a great earthquake and thunder and lightning; then fire fell from heaven and burned the guards, and the stones were scattered like dust. When the earthquake and the thunder and the lightning had ceased the people came to the place where the stoned body lay, and found the stones scattered, and the body of the saint they did not find, for the Lord in his judgements had taken it away to a place unknown.
