# Nicander (Grivsky)

Nicander Grivsky was born on 26 June 1880 in the village of Bolchino in Pskov province, the son of the priest Simeon Grivsky. He finished the seminary in 1901 and served as a psalm-reader until 1905, when he married Olga Sergeyevna Belyaninova, the daughter of a Toropets merchant; they had five children. In 1912 he was ordained deacon and then priest for the wooden church of the Nativity at the Pochep pogost in the Toropets district, where his aged father was then serving, a parish of twenty-eight villages and about a thousand people, and he helped the poorest of them with money as well as with words. In 1916 dysentery took his wife, a son and a daughter, and left him with two sons of ten and five and a daughter of three.

On 29 September 1929 his son Peter, drunk, stole a blasting machine from the roadworks near Savino, and the OGPU, finding that the priest was much respected, arrested Father Nicander on 13 January 1930. A petition signed by dozens of peasants, asking that the tax in kind laid on him be reduced because they paid him for services in money, was read as proof that he rallied the people against the grain requisitions. He told the investigator that he had lived well until the autumn of 1929, when he was ordered to deliver 120 poods of grain and 350 of potatoes, could not, and had all his goods sold at auction; that after the service on St Nicholas’s day he had asked the people for whatever help they could give, and they had brought oil, potatoes and bread; and that he was guilty of nothing. On 4 March 1930 an OGPU troika sentenced him to ten years and his son Peter and the church elder Basil Chuprov to five, and all three were sent to Solovki. In 1932, as an invalid, he had the rest of his term changed to exile, which he served at Kargopol, and in 1936 he was refused early release.

He was arrested again on 27 July 1938 and held in the Kargopol prison. Three exiled priests, Nicholas Chernikov, Demetrius Milovidov and Nicholas Chumayevsky, testified that he pitied the prisoners, said the Soviet power persecuted believers and clergy, had made his lodging a house of prayer, and sent small sums to exiles in need through the nun Augusta; he called their testimony false. On 3 August 1939 a closed court at Kargopol sentenced him to five years in the camps. He reached the Obozersky camp point near Nyandoma in Arkhangelsk region on 17 September 1939, died there on 2 October 1939, and was buried in an unknown grave. The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 September old style; azbyka.ru keeps him also with the new martyrs of Solovki, the saints of Tver and the saints of the Arkhangelsk metropolis.
