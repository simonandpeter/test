# Ardalion the Mime

Ardalion was an actor — a mime, in the word the calendar keeps for him. saint.gr says he worked in the theatres in the years of Diocletian, and that he played comedies and dramas there. One day the idea came to him to imitate on the stage the resistance of the Christians before the tyrants. He had himself hung up high above the boards, and played at being torn apart because he would not offer sacrifice to the gods.

The performance was so astonishing, the page says, that the people began to applaud warmly both Ardalion's skill and the courage of heart of the Christians. Then Ardalion cried out and told the crowd to be silent, because he too was in truth a Christian. When the magistrate heard it he told him to change his mind; and because Ardalion held to his confession of Christ, they threw him into the fire, and so he received the crown of martyrdom.

That is the whole of saint.gr's account. The page heads him «Αγιος Αρδαλιων ο Μιμος (; - 298)» and sets him «στα χρόνια του Διοκλητιανού (298)», which is the year of his death and the only year it gives. It names no city, no judge and no relic, and it prints no hymn. The couplet above the entry turns on his trade: now Ardalion is a mime in truth, more than he was of old, for in imitating the martyrs he endures the fire.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and gives him five sentences, which is the fullest account of him in any source read for this folder. He was first an actor, a comedian, it says; and of all parts, for the amusement of the people, he liked best to play the martyr for the faith, mocking the Christians in every way he could. But when the persecution of the Christians arose, in the time of Maximian, his spirit was entirely changed. He cried out aloud before the people that he was a Christian and that he was not joking. For that Ardalion was tried, and suffered for Christ, and died tied to a white-hot iron grid — having played at last, the entry ends, the true and honourable part of a martyr.

The emperor it names is Maximian, where the Greek page sets the martyrdom in the years of Diocletian and gives the 298 this folder holds; the Prologue prints no year at all, so nothing here was added and nothing displaced.

**The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day, names the emperor the Prologue left half-named, and dates that reign.** Its line is «Мч. Ардалиона (IV).» and its life has him die for Christ under Maximian Galerius, whose years it gives as 305 to 311 — which agrees with the Prologue against the Greek page's Diocletian, and which is why this folder's death is now an interval holding both readings rather than the single 298.

It calls him a gifted mimic actor, «из лицедеев», out of the players, and it tells the scene on the stage differently from either of the other two. He was playing the part of a Christian in the circus, and the part as the author had written it was to refuse the sacrifice to the idols first and then agree to deny Christ. In the course of the action they hung him on the torture-tree and began to rake him with iron hooks, and he played the suffering so naturally that the audience was delighted and loudly praised his art.

Then he suddenly told them all to be quiet and announced that he really was a Christian and would not deny the Lord. The governor of the city tried to make out that the actor was still in his part and would deny Christ and sacrifice to the gods at the end of the show; Ardalion went on confessing his faith in Christ, and the governor ordered him thrown onto a red-hot pan. So, the life ends, he was counted worthy of the martyr's crown.
