# Thomas the Fool for Christ

saint.gr's whole account of him is one sentence: «Ο Όσιος Θωμάς, ο διά Χριστόν σαλός, έζησε κατά τον 6ο αιώνα μ.Χ. και κοιμήθηκε με ειρήνη» — the venerable Thomas, the fool for Christ's sake, lived in the sixth century and fell asleep in peace. The calendar's line for the day gives the same two facts and no third.

So the only thing the page tells about the folly is that he practised it. Where he did so, in what city and among what people, what he did and what was said of him, and whether anybody wrote the story down, it does not say; and it records no relic, prints no couplet and no hymn, and gives him no year within the century. This folder keeps what the calendar keeps and adds nothing to it.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him the one story the other calendars read for this folder do not, and the only dates it has. Whenever he was in the city of Antioch on monastery business, this Thomas **played the fool for Christ's sake**. A certain Anastasius would not give him the alms he asked for the monastery, but struck him in the face; and Thomas then prophesied: „од сада нити ћу ја што примити од Анастасија, нити ће ми Анастасије моћи што дати!“ — from now on I shall receive nothing from Anastasius, nor will Anastasius be able to give me anything.

After one day Anastasius died; and Thomas, on his way back to the monastery, breathed his last. So the holy man's prophecy came to pass. He reposed at **Daphne near Antioch**, in the time of the patriarch Domninus, whose years the entry prints itself as 546 to 560 — which is where this folder's first dates come from. The Anastasius of the story is a layman the entry names and nothing more; **he is identified with no folder in this corpus**, and no `locations` were taken from Daphne or Antioch.
