# Macarius Makres

saint.gr says Macarius Makres was born about 1383 at Thessalonica of devout and eminent parents — though others, the page adds, say he was a Jew who became a Christian. He was marked out for his learning, his virtue and his good sense, and he carried the byname Asprophrys. Young, he came to the Holy Mountain in 1401 and became a monk at the monastery of Vatopedi under the virtuous elder Armenopoulos for two years, and later under the ascetic David; and he was ordained deacon and priest.

With David he went to Constantinople at the invitation of the emperor Manuel II Palaiologos, who did not manage to keep them long with him as spiritual counsellors: they returned to the Holy Mountain, where David reposed in 1422. Later Macarius went back to Constantinople and became hegumen and restorer of the decayed monastery of the Pantokrator. He was sent by the emperor John VIII Palaiologos as his representative to Rome in 1429, and was marked out there, the page says, for his unbending stance and his genuine Orthodox mind. He was protosynkellos of the patriarch and spiritual father of the emperor. He fell asleep of the plague in January 1431.

He wrote the lives of Andrew archbishop of Crete, of Maximos the Kausokalyves and of David of Thessalonica, a discourse on the translation of the relics of Saint Euphemia and others; also a discourse against the procession of the Holy Spirit "and from the Son", where he presses the serious differences between the Orthodox and the Latins. He was a faithful follower of the strict hesychast life, and, according to his anonymous biographer, was counted worthy of the vision of the uncreated light. A service for him was composed by Ch. M. Bousias and is unpublished at Vatopedi; another is in a codex of Cairo published by Kerameus.
