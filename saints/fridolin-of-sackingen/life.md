# Fridolin of Säckingen

The Сретенский monastery's calendar gives him one line for the Julian 6 March, «Прп. Фридолина Зекингенского (V-VI).», and that is the whole of what the source of record has. The name link behind the line, page 14994, prints the heading «Фридолин Зекингенский, прп.» and «День памяти: 6 марта» and nothing else at all — no life, no hymn, no second day. He is the only one of the day's six people the corpus did not already hold, and the only one of them whose life had to be read somewhere else.

azbyka.ru prints two lives of him for the same civil day, a short one and a full one. By tradition he came of a noble Irish family and was born in the middle of the fifth century; the love of Christianity he took in as a child grew into a wish to preach among far-off barbarian peoples, and his family did not oppose it, so after a religious education at home he crossed to the continent. There he was present at the baptism of the Frankish king Хлодвиг — Clovis I — in the cathedral at Reims, an event the full life says marked him for the rest of his days; he grew close first to the king's wife, long a zealous Christian, and then to the king himself, and all his life afterwards turned to that household when he was in need.

Then Hilary of Poitiers appeared to him in a dream and asked him to go to Poitiers and find his relics, buried under the ruins of a church the Visigoths had destroyed. He went, and found both the grave and the church wrecked in the great migration of the peoples; he buried the relics he had found among the rubble and raised a new church in Hilary's honour, and for that the bishop of Poitiers made him abbot of the monastery belonging to the church. He did not stay. Hilary appeared a second time and told him that God had set him apart to be an apostle to the tribes of the upper Rhine, so he prayed at the relics and went — down the valley of the Moselle, setting up churches in Hilary's name everywhere he passed, through Strasbourg, Constance and Chur into the country of the Alemanni, and then up the Rhine. At the foot of the Alps a third dream showed him an island in the middle of the river where he was to found a monastery.

He found the island and settled on it, and it was Säckingen, east of Basel; the town of Bad Säckingen stands there now. The people who pastured their cattle on it took him for a thief and drove him off, and he went back to Clovis, who granted him the island. Even then the Alemanni were slow to trust him, and the full life says what turned them: he taught the pagans to plant fruit trees, and the upper Rhine came into orchards. The monastery he built there became the centre of the preaching of the gospel in the whole upper course of the river, he founded a Scottish monastery at Constance, and his preaching reached as far as Augsburg. The full life adds that he made Säckingen a double house, monks and nuns under one roof divided by a blind wall, which it says is a rare thing; and that he is painted with a staff and a book, for the pilgrim and the teacher together.

The longest episode in the full life is a lawsuit. Most of the land about the island belonged to two rich brothers, Урзо and Ландольф, as unlike each other as possible — the first well thought of, the second envious and treacherous. Урзо gave the monastery further land for new building, and the deed and all the papers were drawn up; but he died, and Ландольф seized the land by force. Fridolin, the life says, understood that only the judgment of God could set the wrong right, and went first to a human court all the same. The judge, bought by Ландольф, heard him out with contempt and said the case would be heard only if he brought the dead Урзо to testify. This happened, the life says, on the ground of the modern Swiss village of Rankweil. So Fridolin went up the hill, knelt on a stone and prayed through the night; towards morning a voice cried that he should go to the grave of Урзо and bring him to the court, and while it spoke the stone both grew and softened, so that his body sank into it as into butter, and when he rose it stopped growing and hardened again with the prints of his hands and feet in its surface. The stone is still kept and deeply honoured at Rankweil, in the chapel of St Fridolin in Vorarlberg. He went to the grave, and at his prayer the dead man rose and walked to the court on his arm; the sight settled the case on the spot, and the saint took Урзо back to his grave afterwards.

**The one page that gives him a life gives him two death years, and the calendar of record gives neither.** The short life opens «Преподобный Фридолин Зекингенский († 538)» and closes «Скончался в 538 году в Зекингене и был там погребён»; the full life says he departed to the Lord on 6 March 538. But the same site's own day index for the same civil day prints «прп. Фридолина Зекингенского (540)», and days.pravoslavie.ru prints no year at all, only the centuries, (V-VI). So the recorded death here is 538 or 540, both years from the one source that has any, and the centuries from the other.

**And the two azbyka lives disagree about where his relics are.** The short one says they now rest at Vienna, «в Вене, в соборе святого Стефана»; the full one says they rest peacefully in the cathedral church of the town of Säckingen. Nothing is recorded on the point and no `locations` is written at all, for that reason and because the places this folder could put him in — Ireland, Poitiers, the island, Rankweil — are four and the page settles none of them.

Why a saint of the upper Rhine stands on a Russian day at all is the last thing the page says, and it is recent: on 14 May 2018, at a session of the Holy Synod, the name of Fridolin of Säckingen was taken into the menologion of the Russian Orthodox Church. That is the youngest entry on this day by fourteen centuries, and it is the reason days.pravoslavie.ru carries a heading for him and no life behind it.
