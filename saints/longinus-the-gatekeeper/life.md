# Longinus the Gatekeeper

saint.gr's calendar for 16 October keeps «Όσιος Λογγίνος ο φύλακας της θύρας» — Longinus the keeper of the door — and then says that we have no details of the Russian venerable's life. That sentence is the whole of the page.

So what the calendar holds of him is the line and the one word Russian: a rank, Όσιος, the venerable; the charge he is remembered by, the keeping of a door; and the country. It names no monastery and no town, gives no year and no century, tells nothing of how he lived or how he died, and prints neither a verse nor a hymn for him. The same day's other Longinus, [Longinus the Centurion](/saints/longinus-the-centurion), is a separate entry with a page and a life of its own, and is a different man.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same menologion day and supplies what the Greek page said it did not have. Its entry is «Прп. Лонгина, вратаря Печерского (XIII-XIV)» — the venerable Longinus, gatekeeper of the Caves — and its life says that he kept his obedience in the Kyiv Caves monastery, and that his zeal in prayer and his humble industry were rewarded by the Lord: the Caves doorkeeper was counted worthy of the gift of foresight. Those who came to the Lavra with good intentions he encouraged; the evil he reproved without mercy and brought to repentance. He was buried in the Far Caves, and the page adds that he is remembered also on 28 August and on the second Sunday of Great Lent.

The Serbian calendar keeps him on the same day too, under a different epithet, and the identity is not a name match: the Ohrid Prologue calls him «Преп. Лонгин Трудољубиви», Longinus the Labour-loving. What the entry keeps is the same man — a Kyiv Caves monk of the fourteenth century who was the doorkeeper of the monastery — and it makes the gift particular in a way the Russian page does not. His heart was so pure and so full of grace, it says, that he always knew with what thoughts one man was coming into the monastery and with what thoughts another was going out of it. It ends where the Russian life ends: his wonderworking relics rest in the Theodosian caves, which are the Far Caves.

No calendar gives him a year, and the two that give a century do not quite agree: the Russian dates him to the thirteenth and fourteenth, the Prologue to the fourteenth alone. Both readings stand, so his death is recorded as the wider interval and nothing is chosen between them.
