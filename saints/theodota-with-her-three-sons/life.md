# Theodota, with her three sons

This saint was of the city of Cilicia, a woman of right belief, who laboured at the commandments of God and taught her children the knowledge of God; she lived in the time when the holy martyr Anastasia was living too. The ruler Leucadius pressed her to take him for a husband, and she would not at all.

For that she was sent to Nicetius, the ruler of Bithynia, who ordered that she be put, with her three children, into a furnace of fire; and there she received the crown of martyrdom. Their synaxis is kept in the place of their martyrdom, which is at Cambo.

That is the whole of what the Romanian calendar prints of her. It does not name her three sons, nor give their ages, nor set down any year; so the children are left here as the page leaves them, counted and unnamed, and they have no folders of their own.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 29 July. saint.gr says Theodota came from Nicaea in Bithynia — where the Romanian account says Cilicia — and that from a little girl she kept the commandments of God in her life with great consistency, something she tried to pass on to her children as well. Because of her Christian faith she was seized by the ruler Leucadius, who had asked her in marriage and had been refused, and he handed her over to Nicetius, the ruler of Bithynia; and he, hard and malicious as he was, threw Theodota and her three children into a white-hot furnace, where they gave up their souls to God and took the crown of eternal life.

The page names none of the three children either, and gives no year. It adds one thing the Romanian calendar does not: that the memory of Saint Theodota and her three children is kept again on 22 December, with similar biographical details.

The Охридски пролог keeps her on 22. децембар ст. ст., as the second of that day's two entries: „Света мученица Теодотија с три детета.“ Left a young widow with her three children, it says, Theodota gave herself wholly to the service of God and to the rearing of her children in a devout faith. [Anastasia](/saints/anastasia-pupil-of-chrysogonus), whose own entry heads that same day, lodged with her while she was in Macedonia, and the two went round the prisons together to the captives of Christ.

Brought out to trial, Theodota confessed Christ the Lord boldly, and was sent for that to Никитије, the proconsul of Bithynia. When a shameless heathen made to touch her body, he saw of a sudden an angel of God beside her, and was struck by the angel. Condemned to death and cast into a burning furnace together with her three children, she honourably ended her life and passed over into the kingdom of eternal glory. This book too names none of the three and gives no year at all.

The three accounts put her in three places. The Romanian calendar makes her of Cilicia, the Greek of Nicaea in Bithynia, and the Prologue sets her where Anastasia was, in Macedonia. All three agree on the proconsul of Bithynia and on the furnace, and nothing read here settles the city, so none of the three is claimed here as hers.

The same book keeps her again on 29. јул ст. ст., its third entry of that day, and there it says two things the December entry does not. It places her plainly: a young widow with three children, in Thessalonica, together with Saint Anastasia — and it prints the December day in a parenthesis beside Anastasia’s name, which is how the two entries are known to be one woman. And it names the persecution she died in, that of Diocletian, giving no year with it. She is not kept twice here: 22 December is her day in this calendar and 29 July is recorded as a second day on it.
