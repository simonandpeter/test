# Cosmas of Maiuma

The parents of Saint John Damascene, living in the city of Damascus, took into their house a poor child named Cozma, from Jerusalem, who had been left an orphan while still small by parents who were Christians. Being lovers of the poor they made him as their own son and brought him up together with John, their natural son. Then they ransomed from the Agarenes a monk who was also called Cozma, a man of great wisdom who had been taken captive in Italy, and gave both boys to him to be taught the divine Scriptures.

Cosmas grew in understanding and learned the craft of wisdom, and then left the vain world. Going to the monastery of Saint Sabbas he took the monastic yoke upon himself and laboured well under it, and shone in the Church of Christ, the page says, like a star in the vault of heaven, both by his life and by his divine understanding.

When the war against the holy icons broke out it was he who urged John Damascene to write to the faithful in defence of their veneration, and the two of them laboured much, in word and in writing, against those who fought the icons. He adorned the Church of God with very beautiful troparia and canons, and honoured in song the raising of Lazarus, the Lord’s entry into Jerusalem, the Mystical Supper and the voluntary Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, as well as other divine feasts.

Afterwards he was chosen by the patriarch of Jerusalem as bishop of Maiuma; and living well, he led his flock with godly zeal to the pasture of salvation, and coming to deep old age he rested in the Lord. The page gives no year for his birth or his death, and none is supplied here. The troparion it prints under his name is the common one for a hierarch, the same text that stands on other hierarchs’ pages with the name changed.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 14 October, and saint.gr tells how he was schooled. Cosmas the Melodist was brought up by the devout Sergius, who was the father of John of Damascus and finance minister to the Caliph of the Arabs; John and Cosmas both had a love and an aptitude for learning, whether of the sciences or of the holy knowledge of religion, and Sergius looked for a wise teacher for them. The chance came when the Arabs, in a raid on Sicily, took captive a monk named Cosmas from Calabria: Sergius freed him and offered him his hospitality in exchange for teaching his sons. That Cosmas was a man of the widest learning, the page says — theology, philology, mathematics, and able to teach philosophy and rhetoric, and he knew music and poetry too — and under his guidance the two advanced brilliantly.

They went on to Jerusalem, where they were received at the monastery of Saint Sabbas; and at the urging of the Patriarch of Jerusalem, Cosmas was ordained in 743 bishop of Maiouma, a coastal city in Palestine which in older times was called Anthedon. He carried out his duties with diligence and conscientiousness.
