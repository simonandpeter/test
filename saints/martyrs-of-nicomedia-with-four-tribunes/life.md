# The Martyrs of Nicomedia with four tribunes

doxologia.ro's calendar names them and gives no life. Its page for them prints the line «Sfinții 4 Mucenici tribuni, împreună cu cei 1003 mucenici din Nicomidia» over the date, 7 Februarie, and over the readings appointed for martyrs — the Apostle from Romans 8:28–39 and the Gospel from Luke 21:12–19 — and nothing besides: no emperor, no century, no account of their suffering, and no troparion.

The calendar names none of the four tribunes and none of the thousand and three, so they are kept here as one entry and not as a thousand and seven. What the corpus holds of them is the line, the rank the calendar gives them — mucenici, martyrs — the number, the city of Nicomedia, and the day.

The Greek calendar keeps them on the same day and gives them the account the Romanian line withheld. These martyrs, it says, belonged to the service of those four protectors and Roman officers who, on the emperor's order, seized the hieromartyr [Peter of Alexandria](/saints/peter-of-alexandria), archbishop of that city, and cut off his honourable head — a saint the Greek calendar honours on 24 November. When their masters became Christians after Peter's martyrdom and were martyred themselves, then these servants too repented and believed in Christ, together with the members of their households; so they came before the emperor Diocletian, who was at Nicomedia, and confessed Christ, and he gave the soldiers the order to put them to death by the sword. To their death they stayed fearless and glad, and so with their blood they won the unfading crowns of the life eternal. The Greek page, like the Romanian, names not one of the four and not one of the thousand and three, so the entry stands here as one and not as a thousand and seven. Its couplet does the counting instead: a multitude of mortals, three beyond a thousand, chose to die by the sword for Christ's sake.

The Russian calendar keeps them on 7 февраля ст. ст. and counts them the same way, «Мчч. 1003 Никомидийских», dating them to 303; it names none of the thousand and three and none of the four tribunes, and its line is the whole of its notice.

The Serbian church keeps them on the Julian 7 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue gives the entry itself one sentence — the thousand and three martyrs at Nicomedia, who suffered in the days of Diocletian — and then, in the verses it sets under the day, does the one thing neither the Greek nor the Romanian calendar does. It names the four. Four courtiers were beheaded by Diocletian's will: Eusebius, Bassa, Eutychius and the wonderful Basilides, cut down for the name of Christ, and Nicomedia, the eastern seat that shone like the morning star, was darkened by it.

Those four heads, the verses go on, were not the end of the horror nor even the first flowers of the mown grass: a thousand slaves, servants and attendants who had served the four martyrs faithfully made an uproar as though they were drunk on wine — no, not wine, it was the truth that made them drunk, and the blood and the victory of the Son of God — and cried, "We too are Christians, impious emperor; we too are Christians, do what thou wilt! And all of us wish to go where our glorious masters have gone." A thousand as one, and three more than a thousand. The verses call it a wonderful boldness and a marvellous faithfulness, and say it did not lessen the emperor's cruelty: a thousand souls left the earth and the gates of Paradise were opened wide to them.

The four names are the day's verses and not its entry, and no calendar's own entry enumerates them — the Greek and the Romanian both say outright that they name none of the four and none of the thousand and three — so they are recorded here and the company is left as one entry, not split into four folders and a thousand.
