# Laurence of Chernigov

Laurence of Chernigov — Luke Evseevich Proskura by his baptismal name — was born in 1868 at Karilsk, in the Chernigov region. His parents Evsevii and Hristina were simple people, hard-working and believing; of their seven children five were boys, and Luke was the youngest. He worked from childhood with the rest of the family and had a hard enough life: a fall at play left him lame in one leg for good, and when his father died and his mother took to her bed most of the work of the house fell to him, his brothers working outside.

He went to school in his own village, and because his mind was quick the teacher set him to look after the younger pupils. Already as a child he was drawn to church music and to the old chants; he came to play the violin well and sang in the church choir as the schoolboys did, guided the other singers at twelve, and by fourteen was directing the whole choir of the village church. His life was pure and his prayer humble, and God gave him many spiritual gifts — he would often see the devil, walking alone on the road or working in the house or the yard — so that the desire for the monastic life did not take long to appear.

When he could, Luke went to the monastery of Rahla, nineteen kilometres from his village, to watch the monks at prayer; and after his mother's death, giving up his share of the inheritance, he went there to be tonsured, and the abbot set him in the church choir until the right moment. His pure life and his enlightened mind were soon known through the whole region, and Bishop Anthony took him, to the grief of the monks of Rahla, and placed him in the Holy Trinity Monastery at Chernigov, saying to them: «This is such a Luke as the whole world will ask after.» Luke was sorry to leave the house he loved, but that night the Mother of God of Chernigov appeared and blessed him, and at dawn he took the abbot's blessing and went.

At the Holy Trinity Monastery he was tonsured and given the name Laurence; in 1895 he was made hieromonk, and after a time abbot. He was of middle height and never without his staff, with a mild and shining face, hair white as snow and blue eyes; the word of his goodness and his gift of foresight spread further and further, and people walked after him in crowds for a word of teaching or a counsel of salvation. All his life he said the prayer «Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner.» When the monasteries had to be closed by the authorities «for repairs» in 1930 he lived at the foot of a mountain with a devout widow, in a small room, in fasting and prayer; and in 1941, after the war began, he reopened the former men's monastery at Chernigov as a women's house of seventy-five nuns, and the Domnitskii monastery with thirty-five.

He foretold that he had a year and a half to live, that 1950 would show them, and that in 1952 they would see much evil; and so it fell out. In the autumn of 1949, very weak, he kept his last vigil and was carried to his cell on men's hands, and did not rise from his bed again; he received communion daily to the last day. After some months' illness he fell asleep in the Lord in 1950, on the day of the Theophany, at half past ten, after the reading of the Psalter and other prayers; and in 1952 the Domnitskii monastery was closed by the authorities, «for repairs».
