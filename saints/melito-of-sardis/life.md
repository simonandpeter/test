# Melito of Sardis

The Ohrid Prologue's entry for him is short and every line of it is about his work. He was a notable pastor of the Church in the second century, bishop of Sardis in Asia Minor, and a man of great learning; and his learning went into one labour above the others, for he worked to gather all the books of Holy Scripture together into a single codex. In his meekness and his piety he worked at another kind of gathering too, trying to bring peace into the church of Laodicea, which had been thrown into turmoil by the dispute over the keeping of Pascha.

He also defended Christianity before those outside it. About the year 170, the entry says, he travelled to Rome and laid before the emperor Marcus Aurelius a written defence — an apology — of the faith and of the Christian Church. That journey and that book are the one dated thing in his life here. This learned, pious and zealous man, it ends, reposed peacefully in the Lord in the year 177.

That is the whole of the entry, and the Ohrid Prologue is the only one of the four calendars read for him. It names no birthplace and no parents, says nothing of how he came to the see, names neither the codex nor the apology, gives no relic, and prints no hymn. The corpus holds four other men of this name — of Beirut, of Caesarea, a martyr of 30 June and one of the Forty of Sebaste — and not one of them is a bishop or a writer or a man of the second century, which is why this entry is a folder of its own.
