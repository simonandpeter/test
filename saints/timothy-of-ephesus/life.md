# Timothy of Ephesus

Timothy came out of Lycaonia and learned his letters at Lystra. His father was a Greek and an unbeliever, known, the life says, in his own bad religion as widely as his son later was for his goodness; his mother Eunice and his grandmother Lois were Jewesses by descent, and both of them holy and just women, whose unfeigned faith the apostle Paul remembers in the second letter to Timothy. Fed as a child less on bread than on the word of God by his mother, he turned away from the pagan and the Jewish error together.

The page tells how he came to Paul. Paul came to Lystra with Barnabas, and there healed with a word alone a man lame from his mother's womb, so that the crowds cried out that the gods had come down in the likeness of men; and when they learned that these were not gods but apostles of the living God, many turned from their error. Among them was Timothy's mother, who was then a widow. She took Paul into her house and kept him well, and gave him her son, a boy still young in years but apt and of good faith.

Afterwards Timothy went with his teacher through the ends of the earth, preaching at Ephesus, at Corinth, in Macedonia, in Italy and in Spain. Paul names him in the letters as brother, as servant of God and fellow-worker in the gospel of Christ. He fasted and laboured so hard that his teacher pitied him and told him to drink no more water but to take a little wine for his stomach's sake and his many infirmities; and his body, says the life, was never free of them, while his soul stayed sound.

He had a second teacher in [John](/saints/john-the-theologian). When Domitian, emperor of Rome from 81 to 96, sent John into exile on Patmos, Timothy took his place as bishop of Ephesus, and not long after that he suffered. There was a famous feast at Ephesus called the Catagogion, at which idolaters, men and women, put on strange faces, carried clubs and idols through the streets, shouted out of all order, fell like robbers on whoever they met, and killed many, thinking they served their gods by it.

Timothy went into that procession and preached the one true God, our Lord Jesus Christ, and showed them the deceit of their gods. Walking in the dark of idolatry they did not understand him, but rushed on him with one mind and with the clubs in their hands, beat him without mercy, dragged him on the ground and trampled him until he died of it. Christians came and found him still faintly breathing, carried him out of the city, and when he was dead buried his body with honour at the place called Pion, which is to say "the fat". Long after, by order of the emperor Constantius, the son of Constantine the Great, his relics were carried from Ephesus to Constantinople and laid in the church of the Holy Apostles beside Luke and Andrew the First-Called.
