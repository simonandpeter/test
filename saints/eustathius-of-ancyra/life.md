# Eustathius of Ancyra

saint.gr says Eustathius was born in the city of Ancyra and is one of the bright adornments of the Church. A bold herald of the Gospel and a tireless worker of its commandments, he was denounced to the eparch Cornelius. At the examination they put him through he persisted in his confession of Christ, and he was condemned to the most barbarous punishment of all.

They pierced his ankles; then, having tied him by the holes of his wounds with a rope, they dragged him to the river Sagaris and threw him into its waters. He was saved, however, by the divine grace, and came out. A few days later, when the eparch learned of it, he was put to shame and killed Eustathius with his own knife; and so Eustathius gave up his holy soul, crowned with the crown of martyrdom.

That is the whole of the Greek notice: it gives no year, no emperor, no relics and no hymn. Above it the page sets a distich: «Pυσθείς ποταμού παμμάκαρ παρ’ Aγγέλου, Tαις χερσί του σώσαντος εκπνείς Aγγέλου» — delivered from the river by an angel, all-blessed one, you breathe your last in the hands of the angel who saved you.

The Охридски пролог for the Julian 28 July is the whole of this folder's narrative, the Greek calendar having given it a bare line. This martyr of Christ was a soldier at Ancyra. Dragged to trial, he feared no torments at all, but freely praised the name of the Lord Christ. The voivode Корнилије gave orders, and they bored through the soles of his feet, drew a rope through them and dragged the man of God as far as a river, and threw him into the river.

Saved by the power of God and healed of his wounds, he came before the voivode perfectly well; and when the voivode saw him alive he was so terrified that he drew his sword and ran himself through. Eustathius lived on for some time, and ended in the Lord in 316 — the first year of any kind this folder has carried.
