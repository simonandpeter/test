# Paul of Russia

saint.gr's calendar for 11 Μαρτίου names him «Άγιος Παύλος τσάρος της Ρωσίας» and heads him «(1754 - 1801)». Paul Petrovich, emperor of Russia, was born in the year 1754, the son of the murdered tsar Peter III and of Catherine II, and stood always, the page says, under the disfavour of the emperor, who never acknowledged him as his son. In 1773 he married the princess of Hesse-Darmstadt, and after her death, in 1776, the princess Dorothea of Württemberg.

When on 19 November 1796 he succeeded his mother, he began his reign by annulling Peter the Great's ukase on the succession and restoring the hereditary principle by order of seniority from male to male. He attempted also the reform of the public finances, which the continual wars and the extravagance of the court had disordered. His orders and decisions had displeased his court, and the Russian nobility disapproved of his governing; the military governor of St Petersburg, count Pahlen, became the soul of the conspiracy.

One night officers of the guard came into the tsar's bedroom and, under threat of the sword, demanded that Paul sign an act of abdication. The emperor refused, and was strangled. That is the whole of the page: it tells nothing of any glorification, of any relic, of any hymn or of any veneration paid him, and unlike its own entry for Sophronius of Vratsa on this same day it names no synodal act. What is recorded here is that the Greek calendar prints him on 11 March, and what it prints.
