# Michael the Russian (1 April)

saint.gr's whole notice for him is one sentence: the holy Michael was a Russian fool for Christ and a new martyr. That is the entire entry — no year, no city, no monastery, no manner of death, no persecutor, no relic, no hymn and not even the distich the site usually prints. Three things stand: that he was Russian, that he took on the folly for Christ's sake, and that he died a martyr's death in one of the modern persecutions, which is what the word new marks.

The corpus keeps sixteen other saints of this name, and exactly one of them is a fool for Christ: Michael of Klops, whom this same Greek calendar keeps on 11 January and who was a monk and a wonderworker, not a martyr. Two entries on one calendar are two commemorations, so the man kept here on the first of April is another; but a day, a rank and a nationality are all that tell them apart, and that is little.

The Russian calendar, which would be likely to give him a surname, a place and a year, has not been read for him. Until it is, this folder says what the Greek calendar says and no more.
