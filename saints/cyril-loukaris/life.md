# Cyril Loukaris

saint.gr's calendar for 27 Ιουνίου keeps him as a hieromartyr and gives his whole career. Cyril Loukaris was born at Chandax in Crete on 13 November 1572, of parents conspicuous in the state and in the Church, and was given the name Constantine at baptism; his father Stephen was a priest and his teacher the hieromonk Meletios Vlastos. He went to Venice in 1584 for a wider education, where the bishop of Kythera, Maximos Margounios, took him under his protection and became his teacher; family money troubles brought him back to Crete in 1588, but a year later he returned to Italy and entered the university of Padua, where he studied philosophy and theology.

He came home to Crete in 1592 and was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Angarathos. He stayed there very little, because the next year his kinsman Meletios Pigas, patriarch of Alexandria, called him to Egypt, ordained him deacon and presbyter and made him his protosyncellus. From 1593 he was sent to Poland to strengthen an Orthodox flock harried by the Union, worked there three years and was nearly seized and put to death in the persecution the king Sigismund set going; afterwards he was sent again as great archimandrite and exarch, and from Poland went on to the Danubian lands in 1601. On Meletios's death he was elected patriarch of Alexandria at twenty-nine, called a synod at Cairo at once and condemned the Latins, pacified the Church of Cyprus in 1605, and worked to clear his throne of debt.

Elected overseer of the ecumenical throne in February 1612 he resigned, withdrew to the Holy Mountain and then to Wallachia, where he stayed four years teaching and fighting the Union and issued a tome condemning the teaching of the Latins; and he wrote two treatises in plain language, one against the primacy of the pope of Rome and one as a dialogue between a Lover of Truth and a Zealot on the methods of the Jesuits. On 4 November 1620 the synod of Constantinople elected him ecumenical patriarch. Two and a half years later he was removed, accused of preparing a rising of the Greek islands and exiled in irons to Rhodes; he refused to resign, came back to the City in September 1623 to a triumphant welcome, and was restored to the throne on 2 October.

From 1632 Cyril Kontaris of Veroia slandered him to the government as a conspirator; he was removed and brought back after seven days, exiled to Tenedos in 1634 and restored within the month, and removed again in 1635, when his enemies plotted, as the Austrian ambassador Schmidt's papers show, to blind or poison him or to have him carried to Malta by a hired pirate. That plan was undone and he found refuge at Rhodes with Bekir Pasha, and he returned to the throne in March 1637, an old man whom the Jesuits judged unbreakable. In June 1638 he was removed on the charge of preparing a Russian attack and a Greek rising; the sultan Murad ordered his death, and after five days in the fortress of Rumeli Hisar he was taken to the shore of Saint Stephen and strangled there on 27 June 1638. His body was buried in the sand, dug up and thrown into the sea, found again and buried secretly at the monastery of Saint Andrew in the gulf of Nicomedia; in 1641 the patriarch Parthenios had the relics brought to the Patriarchate, and in 1975 they were given to the monastery of Angarathos, where they are kept.
