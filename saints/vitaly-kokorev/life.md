# Vitaly (Kokorev)

Vitaly Kokorev was born on 10 August 1890 in the village of Dyakovo in Staritsa district of Tver province, the son of the peasant Ivan Kokorev. After the village school he went to the Valaam monastery, lived there half a year, and, confirmed in his resolve, came home and in 1912 entered the Nilus hermitage as a novice, where he was tonsured with the name Vitaly. He lived there until it was closed in 1928, when the monks were scattered and a children’s colony put in its place; he joined one of the communities the monks formed to keep their rule, called “God’s Work”, which was closed a year later. Arrested with its monks on a charge of not paying taxes, he was sentenced to five years in a labour camp and sent to Nikolayevsk-on-Amur.

He came home only in 1935, and on a priest’s advice took the post of watchman at the church of the village of Khvoshnya in Penovo district, serving also as sexton and lodging with peasants in the village of Pustoshka. In January 1937 villagers hostile to the Church told the NKVD that he visited farmers who had stayed out of the collective farm, even on the day of the census, and that believers gathered with him to read the Gospel and sing; questioned on Christmas Day, 7 January, he said he had organised no gatherings, and was released.

On 4 August 1937 he was arrested and taken to the prison at Ostashkov, where he denied every charge of anti-Soviet agitation, and the other witnesses, among them the church warden, did not bear out the investigator’s accusations. On 3 October the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 7 October 1937. He was glorified by the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 24 September old style on one line with [Andrew (Bystrov)](/saints/andrew-bystrov), [Paul (Berezin)](/saints/paul-berezin), [Basil (Vinogradov)](/saints/basil-vinogradov), [Sergius (Mikhailov)](/saints/sergius-mikhailov) and [Spyridon (Savelyev)](/saints/spyridon-savelyev), all shot on 7 October 1937 after sentences of 3 October.
