# Cornelius of Pskov

saint.gr says Cornelius was born in Russia in 1501 to a well-off and noble family, and was schooled beside an elder monk at the Mirozh monastery of Pskov, where his pious parents had sent him. When his studies were done he resolved on the monastic state, and carried the resolve out, by the grace of God, when he visited the Caves monastery of Pskov and was struck by the compunction of its services and the beauty of the country around it.

At twenty-eight he was chosen abbot, and in his days the monks grew from fifteen to two hundred. He set about renewing the house and raising churches inside it, and carried its charitable work out to the Ests and the Setos of the region: he spread Orthodoxy, and built churches, inns, orphanages and hostels for the sick and the poor. In a fearful plague around Pskov he imitated, the page says, [John the Merciful](/saints/john-the-merciful-of-alexandria), standing by the sick and tending them, communing them, and chanting the funeral service over those who died.

He wrote the names of the dead into a book he called his «πρυμναία βίβλο», his stern-book, after the symbolism of a ship's stern, and remembered them in his prayers. In the Livonian war he preached in the occupied towns, raised churches and gave generously to the Ests and Livonians whom the war had worn down; inside the monastery he nursed the wounded and the maimed with self-denial, buried the dead in the caves, and cut their names into the house's synodikon for their eternal memory. In 1570 he blessed the Russian troops besieging the town of Fellin, and that same day the besieged Germans gave the town up of their own will.

The Caves monastery became a beacon of Orthodoxy for the Russian people and a bulwark against Russia's outward enemies; but Cornelius fell victim to the country's inward troubles, and was beheaded by Tsar Ivan the Terrible in 1570, at sixty-nine, as the chronicle of the hierodeacon Pitirim informs us. The tsar recognised at once that he had fallen to slander, and having repented carried the body back to the monastery himself; the road he walked bearing the bloodied relics was afterwards called the road of blood. The body lay incorrupt in the Caves for a hundred and twenty years, was translated in 1690 by Marcellus, metropolitan of Pskov and Izborsk, to the cathedral of the Dormition of the Theotokos, and was translated again in 1872 and in 1892 into new reliquaries, where the page says the relics now are.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day and gives his years as 1501 to 1570. He was born at Pskov into a boyar family; his parents, Stephen and Maria, brought him up in piety and the fear of God, and while he was still a boy his mother saw in him a particular inclination to the life of the spirit, taught him to pray and gave him a love for pilgrims. To have him educated they put him into the Mirozhsky monastery at Pskov.

The man who had him killed, the calendar says, entered the venerable abbot's name in his own synodikon, and the memory of what he had done pursued him all his life. The body of Cornelius was laid in the wall of the «cave built by God», where it remained unharmed for a hundred and twenty years; in 1690 the metropolitan Marcellus of Pskov and Izborsk carried the relics, which had become famous for the healing of the sick, out of the cave into the cathedral church of the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God and laid them in a new coffin in the wall. On 17 December 1872 they were moved into a silvered copper shrine, and in 1892 into a new one; and the service to him is thought to have been composed for the uncovering of the relics in 1690.

The day page carries three hymns under his own name — a troparion and two kontakia — and beside them a troparion and a kontakion sung to three of the fathers of the Pskov Caves together, of whom he is one. Those two are not copied here, because they belong to the three and not to him alone.
