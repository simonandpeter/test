# Pherbutha, with her sister and her niece

saint.gr puts Pherbutha and her companions in the fourth century, in the days of the emperor Constantine, when Shapur II was king in Persia. When the Persian queen fell ill, some women laid the illness at the door of the Persian Pherbutha and of her sister, saying it came of their working magic. The two were arrested and examined, and they answered that they were altogether innocent of such a crime, since they held the Christian faith, which condemns all sorcery.

Their guilt was not proved. But the declaration that they were Christians turned the judge against them, and so, as supposed witches and as Christians, they were sentenced to death. Condemned with them was the daughter of Pherbutha's sister, her niece — a tender little girl, the page says, and very young. The monstrous sentence rather gladdened the holy women, because they were suffering for Christ.

The manner of their death was of the cruellest. They were sawn lengthways from the neck down to the feet, and their bodies, halved as they were, were nailed up on stakes. The page gives no year, no city and no judge's name; it names neither the sister nor the niece, so neither of them is kept here as a person of her own. It prints two couplets, one for Pherbutha and one for the other two, and no hymn.
