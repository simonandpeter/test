# Basil (Smirnov)

Basil Sergeevich Smirnov was born on 2 December 1889 in the village of
Kazeevka in the Narovchat district of Penza province, a priest’s son. In
1912 he finished the Penza seminary and was made psalmist of the cathedral
church of Chembar and teacher of the Law of God at the town’s second
boys’ parish school and its two-class girls’ school; in 1913 he was
ordained priest and sent to the Archangel church of the village of
Kulikovka in the Chembar district, and the same year moved to Vrazhskoye
in the same district, teaching the Law of God in the parish schools of
Kurilovka, Pyatina and Isaevka. In 1919 he was brought to court for
ringing the tocsin. He was living at Vrazhskoye when first arrested, on 22
November 1930, and for speaking against the persecution of the Church was
sentenced on 13 January 1931 to eight years in a concentration camp,
which he served in the White Sea–Baltic camp, where he learned the trade
of a medical assistant. Released early in 1935 he came to Penza and found
work at the Semashko hospital — as an orderly, then a registrar, then a
vaccinator — and on 15 October moved to the bacteriological institute as
a disinfector. He was a fierce opponent of every division in the Church,
holding that only united could the clergy and the faithful withstand the
Soviet power’s anti-religious policy; he went often to the Mitrophan
church and took part in its services, and enjoyed the great respect and
trust of the bishop of Penza, the hieromartyr
[Theodore (Smirnov)](/saints/theodore-of-penza). On 23 October 1936 — the
18th, the short life says — he was arrested with Bishop Theodore and other
priests for belonging to an illegal counter-revolutionary organisation of
churchmen led by the bishop, and held in the town prison; every effort of
the investigators to get a confession of counter-revolutionary activity
from him failed. The investigation ended on 4 April 1937, and on 10 April
the special collegium of the Kuibyshev regional court found the evidence
insufficient and sent the case back for further inquiry, and the court’s
presidium resolved to take it for trial in closed session at Kuibyshev
with both sides present; but after the secret NKVD order no. 00447 of 30
July 1937 on mass arrests and shootings the hearing was cancelled, and by
a resolution of the Kuibyshev NKVD troika of 7 August 1937 Bishop Theodore,
Basil Smirnov and the other priests were shot on 4 September 1937 — 22
August of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him — and
buried in an unknown common grave. The Council of Bishops of August 2000
numbered him among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia; the life was
compiled by the igumen Damascene (Orlovsky).
