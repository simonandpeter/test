# Germanus Maroules the Athonite

saint.gr's calendar for 11 Οκτωβρίου names Germanus Maroules the Athonite and gives him a long life out of his biographer. He was born at Thessalonica in 1252, the page says, of charitable, well-to-do and pious parents with many children, and was the third of their eight. In the world he was called George, and from childhood he was marked by an orderly and wonderful gravity and simplicity, by a well-timed silence of mouth with quietness of soul, and by a love of stillness, prayer, asceticism, fasting, vigil, compunction and the poor. He treated the worn-out labourers on his family's land with particular tenderness, and once told them to stop work and rest, for which his father reproved him while at heart rejoicing.

His parents thought of marrying him, but he wanted no bond with the vain world. Then the virtuous Athonite elder John, who lived in a cell belonging to Docheiariou, came down from the Holy Mountain to Thessalonica; and about 1270, a young man with his beard barely showing, Germanus followed him back to Athos. He was tonsured by that elder John and from George was named Germanus. He was marked out for a more than human asceticism and a ready obedience, and his handwork was calligraphy. The two would go constantly to the great lavra of Vatopedi for the services and for the sight and conversation of the brethren, and the Vatopedi fathers held them in great esteem.

After his beloved elder John was killed at Thessalonica by the Latin-minded, Germanus went to Karyes and then to the bounds of the Great Lavra. He put himself under the elder Job, who lived as an ascetic in the cave of the Mother of God near the Great Lavra; and when Job was exiled he put himself under the elders Myron, Malachias, Athanasius and Theodoretus, so that his whole life was passed in blessed obedience, as a child and a disciple. Towards the end of his life he took as his disciple the crippled Joannikios, whom he saved from certain death by his prayer, as he did his sick nephew John and his gravely ill kinsman James. At the age of eighty-four, after a short illness, he reposed in the Lord at prayer; only his first eighteen years were lived in the world.

[Sabbas the Vatopedine](/saints/sabbas-the-vatopedine), who was bound to him, told his later biographer that Germanus the divine was another Antony the Great among us, not in his way of life and the exercise of virtue only but in the purity of his heart and the grace and wisdom of the Spirit dwelling in it.

The author of that life, the page says, was his friend [Philotheus Kokkinos](/saints/philotheus-kokkinos), whose own feast it gives as 11 Οκτωβρίου, the same day. The page adds that his memory is honoured also on 10 July with the saints of Vatopedi; one attestation row per church is the schema, so that second day is recorded in the row's note and this folder renders on 11 October, which is the day page read here. saint.gr prints no hymn for him. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.
