# James of Pharatha, companion of Azas

saint.gr says that James the presbyter and [Azas](/saints/azas-the-deacon) the deacon lived under Shapur II, king of the Persians, whom it calls the fighter against Christians; that the presbyter James was from the village of Pharatha, and the deacon Azas from the village of Bithykora. In the year 332 they were denounced as Christians and examined before the chief magus, whose name the page gives in two forms, Achoschargan or Archogaichar.

Because they stood by their confession of Christ, they were first beaten hard, and then left naked through the night in a bitter and unbearable cold. In the morning, when they were barely breathing, but the life of faith kept all its vigour and its edge in their souls, they were asked: do you at last renounce Jesus? «Να πεθάνουμε θέλουμε γι' Αυτόν» — we wish to die for him, the martyrs answered. Then the ruler gave the order and they were beheaded.

The page's couplet for James says that he writes him down as one cut through, and as bearing the crown for the reward of that cutting. Nothing else is told: no age, no year of birth, no relic, and no hymn. The page names the two of them together and no one else with them.

**The Russian calendar keeps this company on the same menologion day and keeps it as three men where the Greek entry has two.** Its line is «Мчч. Иакова пресвитера, Азадана и Авдикия диаконов, Персидских (ок. 380).» — the martyrs James the presbyter and the deacons Azadan and Abdicius, Persians, about 380 — and each of the three names carries a name page of its own: 880 «Иаков Персидский, пресвитер, сщмч.», 80 «Азадан Персидский, диакон, сщмч.» and 10 «Авдикий Персидский, диакон, сщмч.» Each lists one day and only one, 10 апреля, and all three link to one life.

That life is four sentences and they are the whole of what this calendar records. The three died in Persia under king Shapur about the year 380, and they were taken together with the bishop [Acepsimas of Naeson](/saints/acepsimas-of-naeson), whose commemoration the page gives as 3 ноября. After a long wasting by hunger in prison, mustard with vinegar was put into the sufferers’ nostrils; they were stripped and hung the whole night through in the frost. In the morning, after fresh torments, they were shut in the prison again and beheaded there.

**That it is this company and not another was established on the event and not on the names, because the names do not match.** The Greek entry has «Αζάς» where this one has «Азадан», and a James with no epithet where the Greek gives the village of Pharatha; a scan by name finds nothing. What matches is everything else: the same menologion day, a presbyter named James and a deacon whose name begins Az-, Persia under a king Shapur, the two stripped and left the whole night through in a cold the Greek page calls bitter and unbearable, questioned again in the morning when they were barely breathing, and beheaded by the ruler’s order. One event told twice.

**And the Russian calendar has a third man in it.** saint.gr names the two of them together «and no one else with them», which is the reading this corpus recorded from that side; this line names a second deacon, Abdicius, gives him a name page of his own and a rank, and the life behind it speaks of all three throughout in the plural. So [Abdicius of Persia](/saints/abdicius-of-persia) is a folder made by this calendar and by no other, and he is the only arrival of this batch.

**The bishop they were taken with is named in that life without a link, and this corpus holds two Acepsimases**, so the `related` row is a reading. It rests on three things the page gives: it calls him a bishop, it sets the deaths in Persia, and it puts his day on 3 ноября. `acepsimas-of-naeson` is the bishop of Naeson in Persia, carries that see in `office`, and holds the Russian form «Акепсим Наессонский (Анисский), Арбельский, Персидский»; the other, `acepsimas-3-november`, holds «Акепсим Антиохийский, Сирийский» — of Antioch, Syrian — and carries no office at all.

There is nothing else on this calendar. The three name pages print nothing beyond the nominative, the office and the rank; the day page carries no hymn for them, the one kontakion it prints under «Молитвы» belonging to the company of Terentius above them on the same page; and the line gives no village, no see, no age and no manner of arrest beyond the bishop. The village names on this folder’s page are the Greek entry’s, and this one adds none.
