# John of Tobolsk

saint.gr's calendar for 10 Ιουνίου names him and gives almost no life. Its notice says that Saint John Maximovitch, metropolitan of Tobolsk in Siberia, was born in 1651 in the town of Nezhin and fell asleep in 1715, that he was numbered among the saints on 10 June 1916, and that we have no further details of his life. The day's own line is what supplies his see, and the head of the page brackets him «(1651 - 1715)».

The same day's synaxis of all the saints of Siberia lists him among its members, and there the page adds one word to the little it has said: «Ιωάννης, Μητροπολίτης του Τομπόλσκ, ο θαυματουργός (+ 1715)» — John, metropolitan of Tobolsk, the wonderworker. Nothing is said of what he did in that see, of how he came to it, or of the works for which he is called a wonderworker, and the page prints no hymn and no synaxarion verses for him.

He is not to be confused with the later hierarch of the same surname, John (Maximovitch) of Shanghai and San Francisco, whom this corpus keeps separately; the two are two men, and the Greek calendar keeps this one on the day of his canonisation.

The Russian calendar keeps him twice and supplies all of what the Greek page said it did not have. Its 16 October old style is «Обретение мощей свт. Иоанна, митр. Тобольского (1914)», the finding of his relics in 1914, and his other day is 10 June, the day of his repose and of the all-Russian feast established in 1916. He was born, this life says, at Pezhin in 1651 — where the Greek page says Nezhin, and neither spelling is corrected here — the eldest of the seven sons of Maxim Vasilievich and Euphrosyne, and his name in the world was John Maximovich.

Having finished the Kyiv-Mohyla Collegium he was kept on there as a teacher of Latin, and in the same year, 1680, took the monastic habit at the Kyiv Caves and went deep into the work of the inner life. The brotherhood by common agreement laid on the young monk the responsible obedience of preacher, and from then his exceptional talent for eloquence and his gifts of grace began to show. He gave particular weight to inward religious self-knowledge, and the chief question of his life declared itself at once: how is a man to bring his own will into agreement with the will of God?

The answer to it was the book published at the end of his long life of labour, the «Iliotropion», the Sunflower, or the conforming of the human will to the divine. He wrote it in Latin, as the graduates of the Kyiv school wrote, and first printed it at Chernigov in 1714; in the same year at Tobolsk he issued it in Slavonic-Russian so that Siberians too could understand it. Of all the many works of the holy fathers, the page says, it answers that great question of Christian soteriology most fully. A Latin-Greek-Russian lexicon is connected with his name as well.

In 1685 he was sent with an embassy to Moscow, and there Patriarch Joachim appointed him superior of the Bryansk Svensky monastery, which was then subject to the Kyiv Caves Lavra. In 1695, not long before his own death, [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-chernigov), archbishop of Chernigov, made him archimandrite of the Yeletsky monastery at Chernigov and marked him as his successor on the see. The page keeps the reason John revered Theodosius's memory: at the height of a grave illness Theodosius appeared to him and said, serve tomorrow and you will be well; the next day, to everybody's astonishment, he served the divine Liturgy in perfect health — and that wonder, the page says, was the beginning of Theodosius's own veneration as a favoured servant of God.

On 10 January 1697 Patriarch Adrian of Moscow and all Russia, with a council of bishops, consecrated him bishop of Chernigov in the Great Dormition cathedral of the Moscow Kremlin. Taking the diocese in hand he founded a Collegium at the Chernigov see on the model of the Kyiv academy, which was to adorn what he called the Chernigov Athens, a school of enlightened piety. Because of the high level of its theological teaching the school became widely known: it was in substance the first seminary in Russia, and seminaries in other dioceses of the Russian Church began to be opened on its pattern. He also opened a printing house, in which he and his successors published many works of spiritual and moral content.

His own writings were many, and the page lists them: a moral mirror, Chernigov 1703 and 1707; an alphabet set in rhymes, 1705; «Богородице Дево», 1707; a theatron, or moral spectacle, 1708; a commentary on the fiftieth psalm, Chernigov 1708; commentaries on the Our Father and the eight evangelical beatitudes, 1709; the royal way of the Cross, Chernigov 1709; a God-thinking for the benefit of the right-believing, 1710 and 1711; a synaxarion on the victory at Poltava, 1710; a wayfarer, left in manuscript; and spiritual thoughts, printed at Moscow in 1782. His connection with Mount Athos is known too: he took a particularly warm part in the fortunes of the Russian dwellers there, giving them substantial material help in hard years, and his hierarch's charter survives in the Russian Panteleimon monastery.

On 14 August 1711, having been raised to the rank of metropolitan, he came to the see of Tobolsk and all Siberia. There he cared tirelessly for the enlightenment of his diocese, improving the school opened by his predecessor, the famous missionary metropolitan Philotheus (Leshchinsky), and continuing the apostolic preaching among the pagans of Siberia, turning many thousands to Christ; in 1714 he sent a mission to Peking under the archimandrite Hilarion (Lezhaisky), and at Tobolsk he took up printing again with the press he had founded at Chernigov. Of his life in Siberia the chronicler says: he was quiet, modest, judicious, compassionate to the poor, merciful.

He often helped people secretly, sometimes going to the houses of the needy in the dress of a simple monk and bringing generous alms with the words, receive it in the name of Jesus Christ; and his house at Tobolsk was always open to all who needed help or a word of comfort. Even on the day of his repose, 10 June 1715, after the divine Liturgy, he set out a meal in his house for the clergy and the poor as his custom had been, and served at the table himself. Then, taking leave of everybody, he withdrew to his rooms, and at the ringing for vespers he died in prayer, on his knees. He was buried in the chapel of John Chrysostom in the Dormition and Sophia cathedral of Tobolsk.

He has long been honoured in Siberia, and in 1916, because of the many wonders and the long-standing local veneration, the Church established an all-Russian feast on the day of his passing to God, 10 June. He rests to this day in the Tobolsk cathedral of the Protection of the Mother of God, and his service was reissued at Novosibirsk in 1947 with the blessing of Patriarch Alexius by metropolitan Bartholomew (Gorodtsov). It is the finding of his relics, in 1914, that the Russian calendar keeps on 16 October, and that is the day this folder's Russian row carries.
