# Theodulus of Caesarea (17 February)

saint.gr names him and gives him one sentence, and the sentence carries three facts and no more: the holy martyr Theodulus was martyred under the emperor Maximinus, whose reign it gives as 307 to 313, in the year 308, at Caesarea in Palestine. There is no homeland here, no age, no office, no manner of death, no relics, no couplet and no hymn.

A [Theodulus](/saints/theodulus-companion-of-pamphilus) was crucified at Caesarea in Palestine on the Greek day before this one, among the twelve martyred with Pamphilus, and the two are easy to take for one man. saint.gr keeps them on two of its own days and separates them by the reign: the company of Pamphilus it puts under Diocletian, 284 to 305, while this Theodulus it puts under Maximinus and in 308. Two entries on one calendar are two commemorations, and here the source itself gives the ground of the distinction, so two folders stand — but the question is worth an author's eye, and the city they share is the reason.
