# Euthymius (Kochev)

Euthymius Petrovich Kochev was born in 1867 in the village of Vilgort in
the Ust-Sysolsk district of Vologda province, today Vylgort in the
Syktyvdin district of Komi, to a Komi peasant family; finishing the
three-year village school he knew Russian well and wrote correctly, and
made birch-bark baskets and worked as a joiner and a carter. Despite the
persecution of believers he took an active part in the church life of
Vilgort and made large gifts to the church of the Meeting of the Lord; in
1936 he was chosen churchwarden, and in June 1937, when the authorities
resolved to close the church, went from yard to yard collecting signatures
in its defence. On 13 September officers of the NKVD came to Vilgort, and
he refused to hand them the keys of the church and tried to gather the
people to prevent the inventory of the church’s property; he was held at
once — the arrest was written up on 14 September — and put in the
Syktyvkar prison. At interrogation he confirmed that as churchwarden he
had defended the church, and rejected the charge of active
counter-revolutionary activity; on 16 September the special troika of the
Komi NKVD sentenced him to the highest penalty, and on 18 September 1937 —
5 September of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him — he
was shot. He was buried in an unknown grave, and by a decision of the
Holy Synod of 6 October 2001 his name was entered in the synaxis of the
new martyrs and confessors of Russia; the life follows the Orthodox
Encyclopaedia.
