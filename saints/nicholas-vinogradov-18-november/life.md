# Nicholas (Vinogradov, 18 November)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for the Julian 18 November reads «Св. Николая исп., пресвитера (1948).», and the name link behind it gives «Николай (Виноградов), протоиерей, исповедник» — a title, a list of days and no text under it. He is the third man this one calendar keeps under that name and surname: a priest and confessor dead in 1931, kept on its 4 ноября; an archpriest and hieromartyr dead in 1937, kept on its 14 ноября; and this archpriest and confessor dead in 1948. Three commemorations, three years, three name pages. The life here is the one azbyka.ru prints for the civil 1 декабря out of the database of the St Tikhon’s university.

On 25 July 1908, half a year before his own death, [John of Kronstadt](/saints/john-of-kronstadt) wrote in his diary, in pencil: «Священник Николай Васильевич Виноградов, Ярославской губернии Романовского уезда, село Малахово» — the priest Nicholas Vasilyevich Vinogradov, of the Yaroslavl province, the Romanov district, the village of Malakhovo. The page says the reason for the entry can only be guessed at, and that it is known that in those days the elder was travelling round the Yaroslavl diocese. That diary entry is the whole of what links the two men, and it is why this folder relates him and he relates it.

Nicholas Vasilyevich Vinogradov was born in 1870 in that same village in which the all-Russian pastor found him a priest in 1908. In his native Malakhovo, in 1929, the priest Nicholas was arrested at fifty-nine. On 1 December the condemnation and the sentence followed: three years of a corrective labour camp. He held firmly to his faith, the page says, the man whom father John had marked out. After long years of confessorship the archpriest Nicholas Vinogradov fell asleep in God on 1 December 1948, on the day of his first condemnation — which is the civil day on which the Julian 18 November of that year fell, so that the day of his death and his feast are one day.

His grade is not written into this folder, because the two readings of it do not agree. The calendar’s line calls him «пресвитера» and its own name page «протоиерей»; azbyka has him a «священник» at the arrest of 1929 and a «протоиерей» at his death. The office that was wanted and refused is Archpriest. azbyka heads him with three days of memory: 7 февраля, which it marks «переходящая», the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs and confessors of the Russian Church; 5 июня, the synaxis of the saints of Rostov and Yaroslavl; and 1 декабря. A moveable feast cannot be held by this corpus, and the attestation’s day is the day the Sretensky day page listed him on.
