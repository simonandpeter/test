# Symeon the Pentaglot

Symeon the Pentaglot, the man of five tongues, was a Greek of Sicily, saint.gr says. He studied at Constantinople at the beginning of the eleventh century, and afterwards went to Palestine, where he was a monk in various monasteries; and then he came to the monastery of Sinai and lived his ascetic life there, which is why the calendar also calls him the Sinaite.

He was the one who carried relics of the Great Martyr [Catherine](/saints/catherine-of-alexandria) about the year 1026 to the cities of Trier and Rouen in Gaul, and at Rouen he founded a monastery dedicated to her. He fell asleep in peace in the year 1035.

That is the whole of the notice: no year of birth, no account of the journey, no relic of his own named and no hymn. The five languages are in the epithet and nowhere explained.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 1 Μαΐου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/05/01/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1448/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
