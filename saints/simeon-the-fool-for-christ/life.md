# Simeon the Fool for Christ

In the days of the right-believing emperor Justinian, when the peoples who love Christ were coming to the holy city of Jerusalem for the feast of the Exaltation of the honoured and life-giving Cross of the Lord, two young men set out from Syria by God's providence to venerate the honoured wood of the holy Cross. One was named [John](/saints/john-companion-of-simeon) and the other Simeon, and both were of good family and rich. John was twenty-four years old and had a young wife, and lived beside his aged father, his mother having died; Simeon was unmarried and had a widowed mother, an old woman of eighty.

The two had bound themselves together in the love of Christ, being of one country, and they stayed many days in Jerusalem, visiting the holy places together and venerating. On their way home they went down into the valley of Jericho and, travelling under the mountain, passed by the city; and seeing the monasteries around the holy Jordan, John said to Simeon: Do you know who lives in those dwellings? Who lives in them, said Simeon. The angels of God live there, John answered. Simeon wondered, and sighed, and asked whether they might see them; and John said that if they were willing to take up a life like theirs, they would truly have the sweetness of their faces and their words.

They were both on horseback; they dismounted and gave the horses to their servants, telling them to go slowly on ahead. So the servants went before with the horses while the two followed at a distance, talking of how they might save their souls. Going slowly they came to a crossroads, where one road led towards Syria, the way they were bound, and another went towards the Jordan, where those monasteries could be seen. And John said to Simeon, pointing with his finger at the road that led to the Jordan: This is the way that leads to life — and at the road towards Syria: This is the way that leads to death. Let us stand at this crossroads, brother, and pray God to guide us which to take.

The Romanian page prints one long life for the two of them, and the greater part of it is left here unsummarised; its end belongs to Simeon, who left the desert for the city of Emesa and hid his holiness under the appearance of madness. When the people learned after his death of the wonders God had worked through him, of his prophecies and of his much-tormented life, they knew that the madman had not been mad but wiser than all the wise of this age, and that the one who seemed a sinner was righteous and holy, hiding under the look of folly and of sin a life made wise by God and pleasing to Him. As Lot of old lived among the men of Sodom and was not defiled by their sins, so this new Lot, living in the middle of the world, took no hurt from the passions of the world.

Simeon ended his life on the twenty-first day of the month of July, and after him John, his companion in the desert, fell asleep with a blessed end in the wilderness of the Jordan. As they had begun together to serve the Lord on earth, so in heaven they stood together before the throne of God. The life of both of them Simeon's own holy and truthful mouth told to John the deacon of the church in Emesa, and that deacon told it faithfully and truly to the great father Leontius, bishop of Neapolis in Cyprus, who put it in writing.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 21 July, and pairs him with John as the Romanian does. saint.gr adds a year and tells the course of it more briefly. The venerable fathers Simeon and [John](/saints/john-companion-of-simeon), it says, came from Edessa of Syria and lived in the years of the emperor Justin the younger, about 518. They were great friends, and so decided to go together to Jerusalem to venerate the Holy Places. Having venerated them they went to the monastery of Saint Gerasimus and were made monks by the venerable Nikon; but before seven days were out in the monastery they left for the desert, where they stayed together forty years under a hard ascetic rule. Afterwards Simeon returned to Jerusalem and asked in prayer to remain unknown, and God so arranged matters that Simeon could pretend to be a simpleton. During his stay at Emesa in Syria he worked many miracles, and he rested in the Lord poor and ill-used. Later John came there too, learned of Simeon's death, and after a little while fell asleep himself.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the two of them in one entry on the Julian 21 July, and it is the parting advice that is the whole of it. Two young men left their house and their kin — Simeon an aged mother, [John](/saints/john-companion-of-simeon) a young wife — and received the monastic habit in the monastery of Saint Gerasimus from the abbot Nikon, and withdrew into the desert, where they lived many years in the hardest struggles. By many struggles they put their bodies to death, so that they were like two dry trees.

One day Simeon told John that by the command of God he must go out of the desert among the people and serve God there. And John gave him this advice: keep your heart from everything you will see in the world. Whatever your hand touches, let your heart not touch. When your mouth eats, let your heart take no pleasure in it. When your feet walk, let there be peace within you. And whatever you do outwardly, let your mind abide undisturbed. Pray to God for me, that God may not part us one from the other in the life to come.

Saint Simeon took his friend’s advice and kissed him, and then left the desert and went among the people, to teach them by playing the fool and turn them to the faith of Christ. He made himself out to be mad before men, but his heart was a temple of the Holy Spirit, and in that temple was unceasing prayer. He had abundant grace of God, so that he saw into all the secrets of men near and far, and healed people of evil spirits and of other sicknesses.

Dancing through the streets like a madman, he would come up to people and whisper their sins in their ear, calling them to repentance. He appeared even in dreams to sinners, rebuking them for their sins and calling them to repentance: so to a certain heathen actor named Balius, who publicly mocked the holy things of the Christians, Saint Simeon appeared in a dream and reproved him and threatened him, and the man repented and became a good Christian. And a certain youth went out of his mind through fornication; and Saint Simeon, seeing him raving, struck him with his hand on the cheek and said, do not commit adultery — and in that hour the unclean demon went out of the youth, and the youth became well.
