# Anthony of Dymsk

days.pravoslavie.ru’s calendar for 24 июня ст. ст. names him «Прп. Антония Дымского, Новгородского (1224).» and carries two readings of his life on one index page, both read here. He was born at Novgorod about the year 1157. He was tonsured at the Khutyn monastery, and the two readings differ over what followed: the first has him become abbot there after the death of its founder in 1192, the second has the founder, dying, appoint him in his own place. Neither reading is preferred here.

Avoiding the honour, he withdrew secretly from the house and settled on the shore of the Dymsky lake, fifteen versts from the town of Tikhvin, and gave himself to hard labours. For the further wearing down of his flesh, the first reading says, he wore an iron hat on his head continually. Brethren gathered about him little by little, and by common consent, having asked the blessing of the archbishop of Novgorod, they set up a church in the name of Antony the Great with a chapel of the Protection of the Mother of God.

The second reading says he left the world after hearing in church the words of Christ, that whoever would come after Him must deny himself and take up his cross and follow Him; and it adds that by tradition he made a journey to Constantinople and to the holy places. He lived in the monastery to the end of his life and died on 24 June 1224, and was buried in the church he had built.

His relics were found incorrupt in 1330 and from that time were glorified by many wonders; but in 1409, because of a Tatar invasion, the shrine with the relics was let down into the ground. His memory is kept also on 17 января, which the first reading says is probably the day the relics were found; that is a second day of the calendar’s own and not a second feast of this folder, which stands on the day the day page listed him.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 24 июня ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260624.html) and [the two lives](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/id211.htm); read 9 October 2026.*
