# Lupus

The synaxarion’s verse for him says it in two lines: Lupus was a slave, and by the sword came to Christ a free man and a friend. The Synaxaristes have no biographical notice of him; only the Paris Codex 1617 records that he confessed Christ boldly before a pagan ruler who tried by flattery to make him sacrifice to the idols and who, failing, tortured him horribly and finally beheaded him.

The Romanian Lives of the Saints tell a fuller story: a slave under the emperor Aurelian, but free in Christ; he smashed the lifeless gods of the pagans and threw others into deep water; the idolaters who came at him with drawn swords cut one another, and their arrows found one another rather than him; not yet baptised, he prayed, and water poured over him from heaven; he then gave himself up of his own will, refused the governor’s flattery, was beaten with rods and beheaded, and healings are given at his tomb.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep him the same day, 23 August (Julian), and tell a different story: Lupus lived at the end of the third and beginning of the fourth century and was the faithful servant of the great martyr Demetrius of Thessalonica; present at his master’s death he dipped his garment in his blood and took the ring from his hand, and with that garment, the ring and the great martyr’s name worked many wonders in Thessalonica, healing people of every pain and sickness. He smashed the pagan idols and was persecuted for it, but kept unharmed by the power of God — the soldiers who drew their weapons on him, the Prologue says, turned them on one another; since he was still unbaptised, though a Christian, he prayed that God would somehow contrive his baptism before death, and suddenly water poured on him out of a cloud, and so he was baptised from above. He gave himself into the torturers’ hands of his own will and at the order of Maximian Galerius was beheaded, after 306. His service, the Russian calendar notes, is moved to 22 August because of the leave-taking of the Dormition.
