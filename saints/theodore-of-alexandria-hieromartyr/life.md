# Theodore of Alexandria

Theodore was seized by the pagans of Alexandria because he preached Christ with particular boldness; they crowned him with a crown of thorns, struck him in the face and led him chained round the streets of the city, and after other tortures he was beheaded at the ruler’s order and so received the unfading crown of eternal glory — his head cut off by the sword, the verse plays, he shared in God’s gifts as his name says. Eustratiades notes in his Hagiologion that he is wrongly carried as bishop of Alexandria and was a simple God-fearing Christian; indeed in the list of the patriarchs the first Theodore is Theodore I the Scribe (608–610), kept on 3 December. The Greek calendar keeps him on 12 September.

doxologia.ro's Romanian life, printed for 3 decembrie, tells the same story and adds one thing to it: the people of Alexandria — the Greeks and the unbelievers above all — dealt harshly and cruelly with the saints and made martyrs of many, and when they learned that Theodore confessed Christ, served him and taught the Greeks to believe in him, they fell on him in anger and seized him. They tormented him bitterly, plaited a crown of thorns and crowned him with it, struck him about the eyes, laughed at him and mocked him; and at the last they threw him into the sea. Because he came out of the sea unharmed, they cut off his head at the ruler's order.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 3 December, with the Romanian calendar and not with the Greek and the Russian, and it is the one book of the three to give a year. After two years of patriarchate, it says, he was tortured and mocked by the heathen; they set a crown of thorns on his head, and at the last they beheaded him for the faith in the year 606. Its heading, like the Romanian line, calls him hieromartyr and archbishop of Alexandria — the title the Greek page records as an error — and its 606 falls two years short of the patriarchate of Theodore I the Scribe, by which that page measured the mistake. Neither reading is chosen here.
