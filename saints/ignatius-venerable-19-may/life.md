# Ignatius (19 May)

saint.gr gives the venerable Ignatius two sentences. He was called John in the world, and was born in Russia in 1477, the son of the prince Andrew Vasilyevich Blagovernogo and of Helen. At the age of thirty-two he left the world and was tonsured a monk with the name Ignatius. The page's own head line closes his life in 1522.

That is all the page holds. It names no monastery and no place where he lived as a monk, records no ascesis and no miracle, and prints neither a synaxarion couplet nor a hymn to him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 19 May and gives him the name, the place and the story the Greek page has not: Saint John, prince of Vologda, and a wonderworker. God-fearing and devout, it says, he was thrown into prison from his early years by his own uncle, the prince John Vasilyevich, together with his brother Dimitry, and there the two of them passed full thirty-two years. Before his death John was tonsured a monk and received the name Ignatius.

The thirty-two years are the same number the Greek page prints, read the other way round. saint.gr makes thirty-two his age when he left the world and was tonsured; this book makes it the length of the imprisonment and puts the tonsure at the end of his life. The years this life already records — born in 1477, dead in 1522 — are left as they stand, and nothing is reckoned from the thirty-two.
