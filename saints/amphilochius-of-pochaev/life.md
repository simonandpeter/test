# Amphilochius of Pochaev

Amphilochius and [Job](/saints/job-of-pochaev) are the two great saints of the Lavra of Pochaev, the largest monastery of monks in Ukraine; their whole relics stand in reliquaries in the church beneath the Cathedral of the Dormition. The whole life of Amphilochius was a self-offering to God and to his neighbour in the name of love, love being the chief fruit of the Christian's life and the aim of the monastic one; and after his death he showed himself a swift healer, comforting and visiting those in danger.

He was born on 27 November 1897 in the village of Mala Ilovytsia in the region of Sumshchyna, into the family of Varnava Holovatiuk, and was baptised Jacob in honour of the martyr James the Persian. He spent his childhood in that village and was brought up a Christian together with his nine brothers and sisters. His father followed several trades but was known above all for treating dislocations, and the little Jacob used to help him when bones had to be set straight. His zeal for prayer and for the church he had from his mother, a humble woman with the fear of God.

In 1912, at fifteen, he was enrolled in the tsar's army; he fought in the First World War and was then sent as an orderly to a medical unit in Siberia. In 1919 he returned to his native village and helped the sick who asked him for help. He might have married, but he listened to the voice of his heart, and in 1925 chose the way of salvation through monasticism. He went to the Lavra of Pochaev, where as a brother he carried out every obedience with joy. On 8 July 1932, with his bishop's blessing, he was tonsured with the name Joseph; a year later he was ordained hierodeacon, and in 1936 hieromonk. He had meanwhile finished the full course of the theological school of the Lavra; later, on receiving the Great Schema, his new name was Amphilochius.

In the years that followed he was assistant steward, assistant guardian of the Footprint of the Mother of God, keeper of the Lavra's orchard, helper at the candle desk and chanter at the kliros; and on 6 April 1957 he was appointed confessor of the pilgrims who came to the Lavra. Beside all these obediences he healed the sick, and was best known for the treatment of dislocations. Very many sick people were brought to him.

Having given his life to the service of God and of his neighbour, he kept a strong faith and a living love, and received from God the gift of knowing by the spirit and of working wonders. God showed him, in his own words, who was coming to him, what he was ill of, and how long he had left to live. He healed the sick, drove out demons, gave hearing to the deaf and sight to the blind, and comfort to those who suffered. Nearly all the people of Pochaev turned at some time of their lives — in childhood, in youth or in old age — to his prayers.

Spending the whole day with people and in his obediences, he prayed mostly at night. He said himself: the day is for obedience, the night for prayer. He loved humility, and fleeing the troubles of the world took every care to hide the good that he did.

In the autumn of 1962 the communists arrested him and shut him in a psychiatric hospital. He was released through Svetlana Alliluyeva, Stalin's daughter, whom he had healed of a mental illness. After that he settled with a relative in his native village, and there too people went on coming to him for healing. He would often say of himself: you think that I am a saint? I am a sinful man, and you have received your healings for your own faith. From 1965 he lived with a niece in the same village, and in her yard he raised a little chapel before which he prayed and blessed water, and set a long table beside it for the pilgrims, for he invited everyone who crossed his threshold to taste of his blessed table.

He lived seventy-seven years. After several attempts on his life he died of poisoned food on 1 January 1971. By faith, by love and by the mercy he showed to those in trouble he was a living example of how to live, and he left an unforgettable memory in people's hearts. His body was found whole at the exhumation thirty years later. On 23 April 2002 the Holy Synod met at the Lavra of the Caves and examined his life, his deeds and his miracles, and decided that he should be numbered among the saints and his earthly remains honoured as holy relics. The day of his commemoration is 29 April; the official proclamation took place on 12 May 2002.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 29 апреля, which is this civil day reckoned in the old style, and gives no life of him at all: its line is «Прп. Амфилохия Почаевского (1970).» and its page under his name carries the heading «Амфилохий (Головатюк) Почаевский, схиигумен, прп.», an icon section, the website of a convent, and one article about the Pochaev lavra which is not an account of him. What that heading adds is the surname in its own alphabet and the grade схиигумен, a schema-abbot — the great habit with the office of abbot — which is the one thing about his rank none of this folder's other sources had said.
