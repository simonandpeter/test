# Stephen of Rome

saint.gr says Stephen was a Roman of Greek parents, and that he came up to the throne of Rome in the year 253. The couplet the page prints over him has it that, beheaded, Stephen snatched a divine crown, being a pope before and now a great martyr as well. He catechised and baptised many of the idolaters who came to him, and some of them he went on to ordain presbyters, deacons and readers; and those men, the page says, were themselves martyred for Christ later on.

When Stephen was seized he was taken to the temple of Ares. There he prayed, and an earthquake followed which brought down part of the temple, so that the soldiers took fright, ran and left him. The saint then went to the tomb of [Lucia](/saints/lucia-of-rome-companion-of-rix), whom the same calendar keeps on 6 July, and there offered the bloodless sacrifice.

In that place the soldiers found him. They tortured him, and at the last they beheaded him, and so he received the crown of martyrdom. The day's listing adds «και οι συν αυτώ» — and those with him — and saint.gr heads a second entry for the holy martyrs who were martyred together with Stephen pope of Rome; but it names not one of them and gives them no story, so this folder is for Stephen alone. The page prints no hymn to him.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps him a day earlier than the Greek calendar does, on the Julian 2 August, and gives him five sentences where saint.gr gave a line. He was pope at Rome from 253 to 257, it says, and fought against the heretic Novatian. Having healed Lucilla, the daughter of the tribune Nemesius, he baptised them both. He suffered in the time of Valerian along with twelve of his own clergy.

And then the detail no other book of this folder has: he was beheaded while he was celebrating the liturgy. The years of the pontificate are the only ones the entry gives; it dates the suffering by a reign and not by a year, so the span of the papacy is what is recorded here and the year of the death is left where the sources leave it.
