# The Martyrs slain by the Bulgars

saint.gr tells the battle before it tells the martyrdom. In 811 the emperor of Byzantium, Nikephoros the Patrician, came out of Constantinople with all his forces, with his son Staurakios and his son-in-law Michael, to make war on the Bulgars. The fighting was hard and Nikephoros beat the Bulgars outright; but instead of seeing to the ordering of his troops after the victory he gave himself to feasting and drinking. The Bulgars re-formed, fell on the Byzantine forces in the night and broke them up, and the emperor himself was killed by Kroumos, the Bulgars' chief.

Those soldiers who were taken prisoner the Bulgars pressed to deny Christ. Because they refused to change their faith, some of them were put to death by beheading and others by hanging. The page names none of them and does not say how many there were, and the synaxarion's distich says only that murderous men killed in many ways men who were just and not many-wayed.
