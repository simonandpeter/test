# Aquila

Aquila, one of the seventy apostles, was a disciple of the Apostle Paul and was set bishop by him. He was a Jew by birth, from the parts of Pontus, and lived in Italy with his wife [Priscilla](/saints/priscilla) before they believed in Christ. When Claudius Caesar commanded that all the Jews should leave Rome, Aquila and his wife crossed over to Corinth.

Doxologia gives the reason for that expulsion at length. When the preaching of the Lord began in Rome and the Apostle Peter came there, some received the faith and more remained in unbelief, and there were many quarrels between them, some confessing that Christ is the true Messiah and others denying and blaspheming him. The unbelieving Jews rose often against the Christians and stirred the pagans up as well; those who had turned to Christ defended the believing Jews against them, and the strife was reported to Claudius. Because Christ was called by the Christians the Son of God and king of Israel, Caesar feared a rising and another emperor who might take his power, and ordered all the Jews, believing and unbelieving, out of Rome and out of all Italy. Other reasons are told too: the unbelieving Jews had drawn the empress Agrippina, the wife of Claudius, to their own Mosaic faith.

Driven out with the rest, Aquila went to Corinth and lived by the labour of his hands, for he was a tent-maker by trade. At that time the Apostle Paul, preaching Christ among the nations, came from Athens to Corinth, the seat of Achaia, and finding Aquila there with his wife Priscilla he lodged with them, and, being of the same trade, worked at tents with them; and teaching them to believe in Christ he baptised them. Paul stayed in Corinth a while, disputing with Jews and Greeks about Christ and teaching them the word of God for a year and more.

Paul remembered them in his letter to the Romans, in the last chapter: «Greet Aquila and Priscilla, my helpers in Jesus Christ, who for my soul laid down their own necks; to whom not only I give thanks, but all the churches of the nations.» After a time in Rome, Aquila went back with his wife into Asia, being appointed by his teacher to the preaching of the word of God; and at Ephesus he helped in his labours the Apostle Timothy, Paul's disciple, whom Paul had left there as bishop, as he says in the first epistle written to him from Laodicea: «I besought thee to abide at Ephesus, when I went into Macedonia.» When Paul was afterwards driven out of Jerusalem by the Jews and taken to Rome in chains, he wrote from there the second epistle to Timothy, and in it speaks of these beloved disciples: «Salute Priscilla and Aquila.»

Preaching Christ in Asia, in Achaia and in Heraclea, Aquila brought a great multitude of souls to salvation and turned many from the madness of idols to the knowledge of God, baptising them. He passed through other various countries as well, everywhere announcing the Kingdom of God and enlightening many with the holy faith; he broke many idols, raised many churches, appointed priests and suffered many trials. At the last he was killed by the unbelieving, and found rest in heaven with the other holy apostles. The page gives no year and no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, with [Priscilla](/saints/priscilla), on one page and under one hymn. saint.gr makes them a Jewish couple from Pontus who lived at Corinth and worked at the tentmaker's trade, virtuous and pious people; when the apostle Paul came to Corinth to teach the true faith they gave him warm hospitality, having been struck by his preaching, and were so touched by the apostle's burning and saving word that after being catechised and baptised by him they decided to follow him on his journeys as his helpers. Paul was so struck in turn by their virtues and their Christian work, the page says, that he remembers them in his letters, writing that not only he but all the churches of the nations are grateful for the services they gave and for their courage. Later the persecutors of Christianity arrested them and beheaded them, giving them the title of martyrs along with that of apostles. The page adds that Aquila is kept again on 14 July.
