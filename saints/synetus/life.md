# Synetus

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 12 decembrie reads «Sfântul Mucenic Sinet», and his own page on that site carries the name and the date and nothing else about him: no Viață tab, no troparion, no kontakion, no city, no century, no emperor, no account of what he was accused of or what he answered or how he died. One thing the line does assert, and it is all this entry rests on: that he was «Mucenic», the Romanian word for a man who died for the faith.

Nothing further is set down here because nothing further was read, and nothing is inferred from the name or from the company of the day. Another calendar may well carry his passion; none was consulted for this page, and the three other churches' rows say which.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 12 December, and gives the life the Romanian calendar did not. Synetus was martyred in the third century at Rome, saint.gr says, when the emperor was Aurelian. He was a reader of the church of Rome, made so by the holy Pope Xystus, whom the same calendar keeps on 10 August.

He was arrested and ordered to sacrifice to the idols; but he refused, and was cruelly tortured with scourges of oxen's sinews and then imprisoned. For all that he stayed by his Christian confession, and they laid red-hot irons on his body; and because they got nothing by it, in the end they beheaded him.

The page's own two dates will not stand together — Aurelian reigned from 270 to 275, and the Pope Xystus it names died in 258 — and it does not choose between them, so no year is written down here. The couplet it sets over his name plays on the name itself: senseless men kill Synetus, the sensible one, with the sword, the devout man who honoured the faith sensibly.
