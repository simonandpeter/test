# Paisius of Neamț

Doxologia's life gives him as born at Poltava in Ukraine on 21 December 1722, the eleventh of twelve children of a blessed priestly family: his father John was protopriest of Poltava, his mother was Irina, and at his baptism he was named Peter. His father died young, and in 1735 his mother sent him to learn at the theological Academy of Kyiv, founded by the Moldavian metropolitan Peter Movilă.

After four years he left the school and entered the monastic life at the monastery of Medvedeski, aged nineteen, where he was made a rasophore with the name Platon. After a short stay at the Pecherska monastery he came to Moldavia in 1745 and settled at the skete of Trăisteni in Râmnicu Sărat, and then at the monastery of Dălhăuți. In the summer of 1746 he went to Mount Athos and laboured there four years as a solitary.

In 1750 he was tonsured on the Mountain by his spiritual father [Basil of Poiana Mărului](/saints/basil-of-poiana-marului), receiving the name Paisius; then he was ordained hieromonk and founded a brotherhood at the skete of the Holy Prophet Elijah, where he laboured until the summer of 1763, gathering about him sixty-four monks, Romanian, Ukrainian and Russian. In that summer, for blessed reasons, he returned to Moldavia with all his disciples.

He settled at the monastery of Dragomirna and laboured there twelve years, until 1775, forming a great brotherhood of three hundred and fifty monks and translating, with those of his Romanian disciples who knew old Greek well, part of the philocalic writings of the Fathers — so becoming, the page says, the founder of the Romanian Philokalia. When northern Moldavia fell under Austrian occupation in 1775 he was obliged to move again, and in 1779 he united the two monasteries he then had under a single abbot.

In 1790 the metropolitan Ambrose, passing through Moldavia, made him archimandrite. In 1794, falling ill, he gave up his soul into the hands of the Lord on the evening of Thursday 15 November, at the age of seventy-two, and was mourned by the whole brotherhood of the two monasteries and their sketes — more than a thousand monks, Romanian, Russian, Ukrainian, Greek and Bulgarian. On Saturday 17 November he was buried in the burial vault of the great church at Neamț.

Honoured as a saint already in his lifetime, he was given 15 noiembrie by his disciples as his yearly commemoration. In the last decades of the eighteenth century and through the nineteenth, the page says, his Romanian disciples spread hesychasm through the Romanian lands and his Slav disciples through more than a hundred monasteries of Russia and Ukraine. The Russian Synod canonised him in 1988 and the Romanian Synod entered him in the calendar in 1992.
