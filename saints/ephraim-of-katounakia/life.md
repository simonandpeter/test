# Ephraim of Katounakia

He was born on the feast of Saint Nicholas, 6 December 1912, in the village of Ambelochori near Thebes, into a patriarchal household: his grave and devout grandfather, the priest Nikitas, parish priest of the village, the priest's wife, his father Ioannis, his mother Victoria, and their children — Epaminondas, Evangelos (the baptismal name of the future elder), Eleni and Haralambos — together with the priest's other children. Evangelos was born while his father was away at the Balkan wars.

He finished the gymnasium in 1930, a good pupil and a clean, honest boy, obedient to his parents and patient under hardship. Going to the nearby church of the Mother of God he attached himself early to monks and nuns passing through Thebes, and there he first came to know his future elders, the old Ephraim and the priest Nikephoros, who came from Thebes themselves and visited their birthplace. He learned to confess and to keep the rule his confessor gave him; his brother Haralambos remembered waking at night to the noise of Evangelos striking the floorboards with his prostrations, and being quieted with the words, «At midnight, Liami, the heavens are open.»

After the gymnasium he looked for a place in the world and met nothing but obstacles, until he was convinced that God wanted him to take the road to the Holy Mountain; and his mother saw in a dream an old monk who told her, «Where your son is going now, he will succeed.» On 14 September 1933 Evangelos Papanikitas kept the feast of the Cross in the desert of Katounakia, at the kellion of Saint Ephraim the Syrian. The old Ephraim was then past eighty, the hieromonk Nikephoros and Father Prokopios in middle age.

The elders were simple men and the young man, well read and used to his family's affection, found obedience hard at first; he sketched on the back of his cell door a grave and a few words — obedience, patience, prayer — and gave every hour and every corner of the house its own prayers, usually troparia of the saints. In 1934, on the fourth Sunday of Lent, he was tonsured rasophore with the name Longinos; two days later the old Ephraim, already ill with a double pneumonia, died. Nikephoros became elder of the kellion, and on the feast of Saint Ephraim, 28 January 1935, tonsured him to the great schema and gave him the name of the man who had fallen asleep a few months before.

Seeing his obedience and humility, Nikephoros had him ordained deacon in the summer of 1936 by Germanos, bishop of the Cyclades, and he was afterwards ordained priest. In those years he would go up with his elder to serve the liturgy at the kellion of the old Joseph, at the skete of Saint Basil above Katounakia, and so came to know Joseph the Hesychast. After one liturgy he asked to confess his thoughts to him; on the night they appointed he climbed up and told him three high spiritual states he had passed through, and Joseph said, well content, «You were seeking me and I you.» From that hour he called Joseph his elder, and used to say that he had never loved or feared any other man so much.

Four nights a week, about midnight, he hung a small oil lantern from his belt, took his staff in his right hand and his prayer rope in his left, and climbed the half hour to Saint Basil's to serve. He gave up the prayers he had said before and took to the Jesus Prayer, «Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy on me», performing all the services with it as the Teacher had taught him, and Nikephoros did not oppose it but sent him gladly. Joseph never showed him the road of his own soul, but said: «Go forward, and I will watch over you and take note of you. With tears you found grace; with tears go on.»

Joseph fell asleep on 15 August 1959 at the New Skete. Father Prokopios, second in the brotherhood, died in 1968 after a short illness, having had the elder's own father, Ioannis, as his inseparable companion from 1963. Elder Nikephoros fell asleep on 25 September 1973, and after so many years of obedience Ephraim was left alone; Joseph had foreseen the difficulties and advised him to take no disciples while Nikephoros lived.

In his last illness he could not speak or even make signs, and seemed not to hear what was asked of him; he could only groan when the pain was sharp. Yet he saw a little and heard very well, as could be told from the smile or the laugh with which he answered the stories he had always liked to tell — tales out of Greek mythology and popular tradition, used with much intelligence and kindness to mock himself or to prick somebody else. He did not like lying down; he preferred to sit on the bed with his feet on the floor and his back against the wall, bent far over, which was his own posture for prayer. He was sitting so when God took him quietly to Himself on 14/27 February 1998. He had given orders more than once that he should be buried among his near neighbours only, but the news went round quickly and many fathers came to give him the last kiss.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, under a very long page, and gives the names and the dates of his entry into the monastic life. saint.gr says he was born in 1912 at Ampelochori of Thebes, that his father was called Ioannis Papanikitas and his mother Victoria, and that his name in the world was Evangelos; he finished the gymnasium, but the grace of God was shutting the worldly doors of a settled career on him. At Thebes, where the family had moved, Evangelos came to know the elders who would be his, Ephraim and Nikephoros, and his life was already a monk's — the prayer of Jesus, prostrations, fasting, and obedience above all. His mother, the page says, was given word by St Ephraim the Syrian that her son's will to become a monk was God's will too, and that Evangelos would do honour to the monastic life.

On 14 September 1933 he left the world for the desert of the Holy Mountain, for Katounakia, to the hesychasterion of St Ephraim the Syrian, and made his prostration before the company of the elders Ephraim and Nikephoros. After his testing he was tonsured to the little schema with the name Longinos; in 1935 his elder Nikephoros gave him the great schema and the name Ephraim, and the next year he was ordained priest. The page then runs on at great length, with sayings, related texts and a service of its own, further than this reading followed; its heading dates him «(1912 - 1998)», and its couplet says that in obedience, in prayer and in hard living Ephraim excelled at Katounakia.
