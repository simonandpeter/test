# John (Vladimirsky)

**He is the one of this day's twenty for whom the Сретенский monastery's calendar carries a life of its own**, hung behind his name page and titled «Священномученик Иоанн (Владимирский)»; azbyka.ru prints the same life out of fond.ru with footnotes to the Tver FSB archive. He was born on 21 March 1877 at Боронкино in the province of Tver, the son of a priest, Михаил Владимирский. The two pages name his father's district differently — Луковниковского on the calendar, Старицкого on azbyka — and nothing read settles which. He was educated in a seminary and ordained priest in 1906 to the church of his own village, where his father was serving. In 1924 the authorities stripped him of the vote as a priest.

In 1930 the local authorities, meaning to close the village church, demanded taxes of him that he could not pay. He could not pay them and was arrested in 1931, and the district court sentenced him to five years' exile. He moved to the neighbouring region and served in a church there; when the term ended in 1936 he came back to his own village, where his wife Мария Федоровна and their son, then ten years old, had gone on living. Little more than a year passed after his return before the new and more savage persecution began.

He was arrested on 15 November 1937. As usual the duty witnesses were called — this time the chairman of the village soviet and the keeper of the reading-hut — and they agreed to sign papers the investigator had prepared, saying the priest had told people that life before the revolution was better than under the Soviet power, that there had been plenty of everything, and that now even the bread the farmers had was taken from them and they went hungry; they also testified that he had been against the closing of the church.

The next day the investigator questioned him, and the life prints his answer whole: he did not admit agitation against the party or the Soviet power, he carried on no counter-revolutionary propaganda, he had not spoken of a coming war or the fall of the Soviet power or the breaking up of the collective farms, he had not said that life had been better before, he had neither said nor heard that the farms were made for the ruin of the people, and he had never said that workers and farmers were powerless in the elections. The case was closed on 29 November and given to the troika of the NKVD, which sentenced him to death on 5 December. He was shot on 8 December 1937, and was numbered among the new martyrs by the jubilee council of bishops in August 2000.
