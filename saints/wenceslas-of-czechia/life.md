# Wenceslas of Czechia

The Greek synaxarion keeps Wenceslas, prince of the Czechs, on 28 September, and says that it has no details of the saint’s life. Its heading prints his death as 935. That is all it prints.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same 28 September, as «Блгв. кн. Вячеслава Чешского» — the right-believing prince Vyacheslav of Czechia, which is the Slavonic form of the name the West prints Wenceslas — and dates his death 935.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him fourth of the four it enumerates on this day, and it is the only one of the three calendars that keep him to tell anything of his life. It calls him «краљ Чешки», king of the Czechs, where the Greek entry prints prince and the Russian «кн.», and names him at once as the grandson of [Ludmila](/saints/ludmila-of-bohemia). As king, it says, he struggled in the faith after the manner of the great ascetics and strengthened the Orthodox faith among his people, and he watched strictly that no innocent man should suffer in the courts.

Out of zeal for the faith of Christ and love for his neighbours he bought pagan children who were being sold as slaves, baptised them at once and brought them up as Christians. He translated the Gospel of John into Czech, and he brought the relics of Saint Vitus and of Saint Ludmila, his grandmother, to Prague.

His brother Boleslav invited him as a guest and killed him in his own court; and immediately afterwards, the Prologue says, Boleslav began bringing Germans in as priests and introducing the service in Latin. Wenceslas suffered in 935 — the year the Greek and Russian calendars also print, and the only fact the three sources share — and his relics rest at Prague.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 28 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/3799/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026; the Russian church calendar's [28 September](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260928.html), read 4 October 2026; and the Serbian church's [Ohrid Prologue for the Julian 28 September](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-10-11&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
