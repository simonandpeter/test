# Neaniskos the Most Wise

saint.gr's calendar for 10 Ιουνίου names him «Άγιος Νεανίσκος ο σοφότατος Μάρτυρας» — Neaniskos the most wise martyr — and the notice behind that line is short. It says he lived in the days of Maximus, governor of Alexandria, and that it was the governor's own serving-woman who denounced to her master this most wise and comely contestant of Christ. The governor tortured him in various ways to make him deny Christ, and when he saw that he could not prevail he gave the order that he should be put to death.

As he was being led to the place of execution, among the crowd that followed him was the very servant who had betrayed him. Neaniskos made her a sign to come near, and when she had come he gave her his gold ring, as a token of his gratitude: because through her denunciation he was passing over to the One for whom he had so longed. When they reached the place of his condemnation he prayed, and then they beheaded him.

The page gives no year, no other name and no hymn, and above the notice it prints the two verses of the synaxarion for him: «Kακού αγαθόν αντιδούς στεφηφόρε, / Σοφώτατος δέδειξαι εκ των πραγμάτων» — that by returning good for evil the crown-bearer showed himself, by the very facts, the most wise of men.
