# Nazarius of Milan

Nazarius was born at Rome of a Jewish father and a Christian mother named Perpetua, who had received baptism from the Apostle Peter. When the boy grew up he thought long about which faith to hold, his father’s or his mother’s, and chose to follow his mother’s, whose prayer, the page says, helped him much towards that light. He was baptised by the bishop of the place; and coming to full age he served the Lord in earnest, not for his own salvation only but for others’, taking pains to bring unbelievers to Christ.

Taking from his parents the share of the property that fell to him, so as to do good to the poor with it, he left Rome and went to Milan, and spent it all well there in alms to the poor and in help to those who were suffering for Christ; for under Nero there was a great persecution, and many who confessed Christ were held in chains and tormented. At that time the martyrs [Gervasius](/saints/gervasius-of-milan) and [Protasius](/saints/protasius-of-milan) had been seized by the governor Anulinus and thrown into prison, and Nazarius went often to comfort them, and loved them so that he wished to suffer and die with them.

Word came to the governor that he was visiting the prisoners, bringing them what they needed and strengthening them in the Christian faith. Brought before him and found to be a Roman by birth and a Christian by faith, he was urged not to deny the ancestral gods; he not only refused but reviled them, and confessed that Jesus Christ is the one true God. They struck him on the mouth, and when he would not stop rebuking the governor’s impiety, all of them beat him and he was driven out of the city in dishonour — rejoicing, the page says, that he was counted worthy of blows for Christ.

The next night his mother appeared to him in a vision and bade him go to Gaul and labour there at the good news of Christ; and he went, and gave light to peoples sitting in darkness. In a city called Melia he took a boy of three named [Celsus](/saints/celsus-disciple-of-nazarius), the child of a believing woman of good family, baptised him and brought him up in the right faith; and the boy grew in years and understanding and came to be like his teacher, preaching Christ and enduring persecutions and torments.

Dionysius, the governor of that country, hearing that Nazarius had turned many cities to Christ, had him and the boy taken, beaten and thrown into prison, meaning to torment them the next day. But the governor’s wife, seeing the small and beautiful child led out to torture, pitied him and begged her husband to spare him and free him with his teacher, and won them both their liberty; and the saints grieved that they had not gained the martyr’s crown they longed for.

They went on to the city of Țimir and won a great many souls there, until the idolaters rose against them and sent them to Nero. Standing before the emperor they confessed Christ boldly: Nazarius was thrown down and trampled, Celsus was beaten with rods and forced to sacrifice, and at his word the idols were overturned on the ground. They were given to the beasts and the beasts did not harm them. Afterwards Nazarius and Celsus came back to Milan, found Gervasius and Protasius still alive in prison, preached again and were taken again by Anulinus, who marvelled that they had come alive out of Nero’s hands.

Anulinus wrote to Nero, and Nero, angry that Nazarius still lived, wrote back that he should be killed at once. Anulinus brought Nazarius and his disciple Celsus out of the prison and had their honourable heads cut off with the sword. One of the faithful who lived near the city took their relics in secret to his house, where a bedridden daughter of his rose up whole the moment they were brought in; and he and his household buried the bodies in the garden.

The relics lay hidden and unknown until Ambrose, bishop of Milan, found them by a divine disclosure and laid them in the church of the Holy Apostles. Paulinus the presbyter, who was present, writes that he saw blood in the grave as though it had run only lately, the head with its hair and beard untouched and the face so bright it seemed newly washed, and that the relics filled the place with a fragrance greater than all spices. The page names Nero as emperor and Anulinus as governor, and gives no year, and none is supplied here.

The Greek calendar keeps the four of them on the same day, 14 October, and saint.gr tells how they came together. The parents of Nazarius, Africanus and Perpetua, are said to have been disciples of the Apostle Peter, who drew them to the faith and baptised them; their son was orphaned young in a corrupt society and kept pure and blameless, and at twenty he divided what he had among the poor and set out preaching. At Mediolanum he met two devout men, Protasius and Gervasius, and the three of them together won many by the divine word, from the crowd and from the higher classes alike. Then Nazarius went into Gaul, where among those who believed was a young soul, Celsus. When he returned to Mediolanum he was arrested; Protasius and Gervasius had been taken before him; and the eparch Anoulinos, seeing that they would not change their faith, beheaded them all, together with the young Celsus. The page adds that the relics of Protasius, and the head and the rest of the relics of Gervasius, are at the Roman Catholic Ambrosian Basilica of Milan.

The Russian calendar keeps the four of them on 14 October too, and gives the only date any calendar gives them: the reign of Nero, 54 to 68. He was the son of a Christian mother, Perpetua, and a Jewish father, Africanus, born at Rome and baptised by the bishop Linus; from his youth he resolved to spend his life preaching Christ's teaching and helping Christians under persecution, and it was for that that he left Rome for Milan. Visiting the Christians in the prison there he came to know the twins Protasius and Gervasius, grew to love them, and eased their sufferings as far as he could; the pagans beat him for it and drove him out of the city.

He went to Gaul and preached there with success, turning many pagans; and at the city of Cimiez he baptised the son of a Christian woman, Celsus, and brought him up, and so got himself a faithful disciple and fellow worker. For preaching they were thrown to wild beasts, and the beasts would not touch them; an attempt to drown them in the sea failed because they walked on the water as on dry land, and the soldiers detailed to the execution were so struck that they became Christians themselves and let the martyrs go. Free, the two went back to Milan and visited Gervasius and Protasius in prison. It was reported to Nero, who ordered Nazarius and Celsus beheaded, and the brothers were executed soon after.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him with [Gervasius](/saints/gervasius-of-milan) and [Protasius](/saints/protasius-of-milan) on the Julian 14 October under one heading, and gives him a mixed house: he was born at Rome of a Jewish father and a Christian mother, and his mother Perpetua had been baptised by the apostle Peter himself. He took his mother’s faith and gave himself wholly to the keeping of all the Church’s ordinances.

Preaching the Gospel fearlessly he came to Mediolanum, and there he found Gervasius and Protasius already in prison and served them with great love. The local prince learnt of it, had him beaten and drove him out of the city; and his mother appeared to him in a vision and told him he must go into Gaul and preach the Gospel there, which he did. Some years later he came back to Mediolanum with a young man, [Celsus](/saints/celsus-disciple-of-nazarius), his disciple, whom he had baptised in Gaul. He found the two brothers still in the prison into which the prince — Anulinus, the book names him — soon threw him as well; and Christ’s martyrs rejoiced at this new meeting by God’s providence.

The emperor Nero gave the order that Nazarius should be killed, and the prince brought him and Celsus out of the prison and cut off their heads. The entry ends with what came afterwards: [Ambrose of Milan](/saints/ambrose-of-milan) translated his relics out of a garden beyond the city into the church of the Holy Apostles, and the relics of the two brothers were revealed to that same bishop in a wondrous vision.
