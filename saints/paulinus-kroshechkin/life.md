# Paulinus (Kroshechkin)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 21 октября ст. ст. at the head of a line of five, «Сщмчч. Павлина , архиеп. Могилевского, Аркадия , еп. Екатеринбургского, и с ними Анатолия и Никандра пресвитеров и мч. Киприана (1937)», and carries a life for him alone of the five. He was born Peter Kuzmich Kroshechkin on 19 December 1879 in the Penza province, into a peasant family; he lost his father early and was brought up by a devout mother, in a house that was a shelter for wanderers.

After going with his mother to the Sarov hermitage the desire for the monastic life took hold of him, and in 1895, at sixteen, he entered Sarov; from there he moved to the Nikolo-Babaevsky monastery. A pull towards learning then brought him to Moscow. He was received into the brotherhood of the Novospassky monastery, finished the seminary course in a single year, and in 1916 completed the course of the Moscow Theological Academy. He lived seventeen years at Novospassky, was tonsured there with the name Pavlin, and in 1920 and 1921 was its vicegerent.

On 2 May 1921 he was consecrated bishop of Rylsk, a vicar of the Kursk diocese, and travelled it without rest, holding Orthodoxy's ground against the Renovationists. From 1926 to 1933 he held first the Perm and then the Kaluga see. His life says he was simple to deal with and easy of access, that he loved to sing the church's hymns together with the people so that his flock would say the words of the prayers knowingly, and that he was without malice as a child is; nobody saw him angry, and if he saw that somebody was irritated with him he could not rest until he had asked that person's forgiveness. In his little garden he built a bridge over the path where the ants had made their trail, so as not to step on them by accident.

In the autumn of 1926, when the bishops were discussing whether a Patriarch could be elected in secret, he was the author of the idea and took practical charge of carrying it out, travelling to bishops all over Russia to collect signatures; by November there were seventy-two bishops' hands under the act electing the metropolitan Kirill (Smirnov). He was arrested suddenly in Moscow while on that errand. The documents did not reach the GPU, but a wave of arrests of the bishops who had signed followed, and he spent a year in a solitary cell — he afterwards called the prison his second Academy. He was released on 9 April 1927, in the wake of the metropolitan Sergius (Stragorodsky), who soon issued his Declaration.

In 1933 he was appointed to the Mogilev see, and on 11 October 1936, 24 October by the new style, he was arrested and sentenced to ten years' imprisonment. He was shot on 21 октября 1937, the 3 November of the new style, together with a group of clergy in the Kemerovo camp, and was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
