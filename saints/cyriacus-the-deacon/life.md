# Cyriacus of Rome

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps thirteen sufferers of Rome together on 7 июня under one line and gives them one reading. It has them suffering at Rome in the persecutions of Диоклетиан and Максимиан, who reigned from 284 to 305, and of their successors Галерий (305–311) and Максентий (305–312). Максимиан, who ruled the western half of the empire, stripped every Christian of his military rank and sent them to forced labour. A rich Christian named Фрасон began to send the prisoners food and clothing through four Christians of the city, and the pope of Rome thanked him for the charity and ordained two of those four deacons.

The two deacons were seized while helping the prisoners and sent to the works themselves, where they did not only their own task but the task of an aged prisoner beside them as well; and for that Максимиан handed one of them over to Лаодикий, the governor of the region, who shut him in prison. The warden of that prison called him up for questioning, saw his face shining with a heavenly light, believed in Christ and was baptised, and went with him to the pope to be chrismated; the pope served the liturgy and gave them both communion. On the same day, 7 июня, the deacon, the aged prisoner and the warden stood before Лаодикий.

The warden confessed that he was a Christian and was beheaded at once, and the other two were thrown back into prison. Brought to the pagan temple and ordered to sacrifice, the aged prisoner said, «Да обратит Господь в прах идолов языческих!» — may the Lord turn the idols of the heathen to dust — and in that moment the tripods on which the incense smoked before the idols melted away. Two soldiers who saw it confessed Christ. The deacon and the aged man were beheaded after long torments; the two soldiers were shut up, came out unnoticed to be baptised by the pope, returned to their cell, confessed again at the trial and died under torture. The presbyter Иоанн and Фрасон buried them.

The youngest daughter of Диоклетиан was tormented by a demon, and the emperor, hearing that one of the prisoners healed the sick and drove out demons, called the second deacon to her; he drove the demon out and baptised her, and the emperor freed him and two of his fellow-prisoners, and afterwards sent him to Persia to heal the daughter of the Persian king. On his return Галерий, the son-in-law of the abdicated Диоклетиан and angry with him for his daughter's conversion, had him led naked and in chains behind the imperial chariot for the mockery of the crowd.

The pope rebuked the emperor publicly for his cruelty to people who had done nothing, and was beaten with sticks and set to herd cattle. The deacon, the two men freed with him and one more prisoner died of their torments, and the emperor's daughter was executed with twenty-one other prisoners. The Roman clergy freed the pope secretly, and he dug up three of the dead and buried them in the estate of two Christian women outside Rome, turning the house of one of them into a church. Максентий, coming to the throne, had that church made a cattle yard and the pope sentenced to follow cattle for the rest of his life; worn out by hunger, cold and the stench he fell ill and died in 310, and the two women were driven out of Rome with dishonour and their estates plundered.

He is the Кириак of that reading, the second of the two deacons: the one who drove the demon out of Диоклетиан's youngest daughter and baptised her, was freed for it with two of his fellow-prisoners, was sent to Persia to heal the Persian king's daughter, was led naked and chained behind Галерий's chariot on his return, and died of his torments.

The twelve the same line names with him are [Marcellus of Rome](/saints/marcellus-pope-of-rome), [Sisinnius of Rome](/saints/sisinnius-the-deacon), [Smaragdus of Rome](/saints/smaragdus-of-rome), [Largius of Rome](/saints/largius-of-rome), [Apronianus of Rome](/saints/apronianus-of-rome), [Saturninus of Rome](/saints/saturninus-of-rome), [Papias of Rome](/saints/papias-the-soldier), [Maurus the African](/saints/maurus-the-soldier), [Crescentianus of Rome](/saints/crescentianus-of-rome), [Priscilla of Rome](/saints/priscilla-of-rome), [Lucina of Rome](/saints/lucina-of-rome), [Artemia of Rome](/saints/artemia-of-rome).
