# Daniel Katounakiotis

Daniel Katounakiotis was born at Smyrna in 1844 — 1846, the Romanian life says — the youngest son, Demetrius, of a large family of devout parents, and a top graduate of the Evangelical School of Smyrna. From nineteen he had the pious longing to give himself to monasticism, and on the advice of his confessor, a monk of the Holy Sepulchre, visited monasteries in the Peloponnese and the islands — Hydra, Tinos, Paros, Icaria — where he met famous holy elders; on Paros he came to know and was bound in spirit to Arsenios of Paros, who advised him to go to the Garden of the Mother of God, to the monastery of Saint Panteleimon, then flourishing as a coenobium. Obeying, he went there and was tonsured Daniel in 1866, taking on the service of the monastery’s secretary; in those years began the quarrels of the Russian monks, then a minority, who meant to carry out the Russian plans of pan-Slavism and take the monastery’s government, and after disputes the Greek monks were forced to leave, Daniel among them — and as he left he was summoned by the Ecumenical Patriarchate on account of certain slanders and unjustly punished with permanent removal from the monastery. His steps led him to Metropolitan Joachim of Thessalonica — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here; the Greek calendar keeps him with a full service, and its verse says the Christ he hymned on Athos in ascesis he now hymns with his lips in heaven.
