# Antony (Korzh)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him alone between the five presbyters and the six women on the one line it gives to fourteen people of 1938 for the Julian 1 March, and it is the only name on that line it grades прмч., a venerable-martyr. Antony Agafonovich Korzh was born in 1888 in the village of Rubanovka in the Melitopol district of the province of Taurida, the son of the peasant Agathon Korzh. He finished the parish school and then, like many young men out of devout peasant families, made the pilgrimage to Athos, where thousands of Russians were then living the ascetic life, and there he was tonsured a monk with the name Antony.

Coming back to Russia he was ordained hierodeacon and served in the Kiziltash monastery of the diocese of Taurida. When the persecution began the monastery was closed and he went to serve in the church of the Resurrection in the Livadia settlement; when that church too was closed he went to the Pleshcheyev church in the cemetery of the town of Yalta, and he served there until the persecution of 1937. By that time the authorities had closed the «ялтинский Александро-Невский собор», and the faithful and the clergy would not have it so and set about getting it opened again.

The authorities refused to consider the question, saying that the group of twenty registered earlier was taking no steps of its own. He went with a parishioner of that cathedral to the town executive committee to get leave to register a new twenty, and was refused. With the blessing of the rector of his own church he began gathering the active parishioners at his lodging to work out together how the cathedral might still be opened, and at one of these meetings early in 1937 they decided to go once more with a petition. They were refused again, and not arrested then, because the emergency laws were not yet passed; but in the second half of 1937 everyone who had worked actively for the cathedral was taken.

He was arrested on 9 December 1937. At the interrogations he flatly refused to incriminate himself or admit any guilt, and the investigator told him he was lying and that the material of the case convicted him. Four witnesses’ statements were read to him — gathered from false witnesses and from men who had broken under questioning — and he said he did not confirm them, because they were false. Asked whether he was on bad terms with any of the witnesses, he said he was on good terms with all of them; and the investigator concluded that since he was on good terms with them they could not have given false evidence. The investigation was closed on 24 December and on 9 February 1938 the troika sentenced him to be shot.

He was shot on 14 March 1938 and buried in a common unmarked grave. Two things this folder refuses and names. The office this corpus would have written for him is hierodeacon, which both hosts call him, and there is no such key in the five locale packs — only an abbot of Kiziltash and a hierodeacon of Zilantov — so none is written. And the grade: the day’s line grades him «прмч.» and azbyka.ru heads its page «Преподобномученик Антоний (Корж), иеродиакон», while the Сретенский calendar’s own name link behind his name reads «сщмч.», a hieromartyr. Two of the three readings are the venerable-martyr written here, and the third is set down rather than resolved.

azbyka.ru keeps him on more days than anybody else on this line: 14 марта, and besides it the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs and confessors of the Russian Church, and three fixed synaxes — of the venerable fathers of the Russian monastery of Saint Panteleimon on Athos on 20 мая, of the new martyrs and confessors of the metropolitanate of Crimea on 2 декабря, and of the saints of Crimea on 28 декабря. The moveable one is no day this corpus can hold, and the three fixed ones are that host’s commemorations and not the Сретенский calendar’s, which prints him on the 1 March line only.
