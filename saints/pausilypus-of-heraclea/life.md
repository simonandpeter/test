# Pausilypus

saint.gr's line for the day calls him «Άγιος Παυσίλυπος ο εν Ηρακλεία της Θράκης αθλήσας», Pausilypus who contended at Heraclea in Thrace, and the page behind it gives him a second form of the name, Παυσολύπιος. It sets him in the years of the emperor Hadrian, which it dates 117 to 138, and says he was born at Rome and came of a noble and wealthy family. Because he was encouraging and standing by the Christians who were walking to their martyrdom he was himself seized and brought before the emperor.

The emperor asked him whether he believed in Christ, and Pausilypus answered that he not only believed but was ready to shed his blood for him. For that he was handed over to Practus, the eparch of Europe, who gave orders that he be beaten with rods three hundred times without mercy. The martyr bore the torture and refused to sacrifice to the idols, and because he would not obey he was bound and led out to be beheaded.

On the road, the page says, he broke his bonds, got away from the soldiers, and so was delivered from a martyr's death by the sword. Having been saved he stood still in some place and prayed to God, and there he gave up his martyr's soul in love to the Lord, receiving an unfading crown and immortal life. The couplet over the entry plays on his name: Pausilypus, leaving the sojourning of earth, comes to a dwelling where grief is made to cease.
