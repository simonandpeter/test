# Anastasia, pupil of Chrysogonus

Anastasia, fairest among women, was born in the famous city of Rome, and surpassed everyone in the goodness of her family, in beauty of body and of soul, in good ways and in gentleness. Her father Pretextat was one of the great men of the city and a pagan, while her mother, Fausta, believed in Christ; and from childhood her mother put her, for her schooling, into the hands of a man renowned for wisdom and honourable in life, [Chrysogonus](/saints/chrysogonus), a faithful Christian perfectly versed in the things of God and afterwards a martyr too.

From him Anastasia learned not only the understanding of letters but to know him who is Alpha and Omega, and to know and love the true God, the Maker and Master of all. She laboured at the reading of Christian books day and night, learning the laws of the Lord and strengthening her heart in the love of God, and when she had finished her schooling with Chrysogonus she was renowned among all as wise and beautiful. Then her mother died, and her father gave her under great compulsion to a man named Pomplie, well born like herself but a pagan — the believer to the unbeliever, the life says, the lamb of Christ to a ravening wolf.

God kept her, and she lost neither her virginity nor the purity of her body, for she feigned a woman's sickness without ceasing; and when her husband pressed her she slipped out of his hands, her guardian angel helping her unseen. Often she changed her bright clothes and costly ornaments for poor ones, put them on in secret and went out of her house with nobody knowing but one maidservant, and went round all the prisons, giving much gold to the guards and visiting those who suffered for Christ, washing their hands and feet, cleaning the dust from their hair, wiping their blood and binding their wounds with clean cloths, and giving each of them food and drink before she went home.

Going out and coming back so often, she was not hidden from her husband, who grew angrier still because she would have nothing to do with the union of marriage; and when the maidservant who followed her told him everything, he beat her badly, shut her up in a house and set a guard so that her foot should not go outside. She grieved in spirit for the servants of Christ, whom she could no longer visit or serve, and her heart ached most for her teacher, who had then been two years in prison, enduring every kind of torture, and whom she could no longer see.

Then her father died, and her husband set about taking the whole inheritance that had come to her as his only heir, and thought to kill her for it and marry another. Letters went between her and Chrysogonus by an old woman. She wrote that her husband was spending on idolaters the wealth she had promised to God, that he had bound her under heavy guard as a witch and a stranger to holy things, and that she looked only to die; and she asked him to pray that her husband might live if he would ever become a Christian, and if not be taken from among the living to give place to others who honour God.

Chrysogonus wrote back to call her to patience: Christ who walks on the water would come to her quickly and calm with one word of his command the devilish winds raised against her; she should wait for a double gift, since the temporal inheritance would come back to her and the heavenly be made ready, and she should keep herself from every sin and seek her comfort from God himself. When she wrote again that the end of her body had come, he answered that darkness always goes before the light, that health returns after sickness and life is promised after death, and told her to bind herself with her whole mind to the Cross of Christ.

With that letter he foretold the quick ruin of Pomplie; for in those days the emperor sent him with letters to the king of the Persians, and the ship he sailed in was sunk by a storm that rose without warning, and he drowned. So Anastasia was delivered like a bird out of the fowler's snare, her virginity whole, and took back all the inheritance left her by her parents, and began again, hindered by nobody, to go round the prisons and serve the sufferers for Christ, not only with her wealth but with her understanding, comforting them with useful words and rousing them to courage and endurance and to facing death for Christ.

Brought at last before a governor who demanded her wealth, she answered out of the Gospel: Christ says sell what you have and give to the poor and you will have treasure in heaven; but you are rich, and who would be so mad as to give you the riches that belong to the poor, or so senseless as to give the food of the hungry to a man who lives in debauchery and good dinners? When I see you hungry and thirsty, naked and sick and thrown into prison, then I will do for you what we are commanded by Christ.

Angered, he shut her in a dark prison and afflicted her with hunger for thirty days, but she fed on hope towards Christ; and every night the martyr Teodotia appeared to her, filling her heart with joy and strengthening her. Asked once how she came to her after death, Teodotia said that this gift is given the souls of martyrs, that after their going from earthly things they may come to those who wish to speak with them and comfort them. Finding her after thirty days not weakened but healthy and bright of face, the governor was angry with her guards, sealed her into a darker prison with his own seal and starved her thirty days more; and she fed and drank on tears, praying without ceasing.

Brought out and found unchanged, she was condemned to be drowned at sea with others sentenced for various crimes, among them a believing man named Evtihian, who had been stripped of all he had for Christ. Out in the middle of the sea the soldiers went into a boat made ready for them, holed the ship in many places and made for the shore; but as it began to sink those on board saw the martyr Teodotia steering it to land, and so they sailed without danger. All the condemned, seeing that wonder and saved from drowning, fell at the feet of the two Christians, were taught faith in Christ by them and baptised — a hundred and twenty souls in all, whom the governor, when he heard of it, seized and destroyed with various torments. Anastasia he ordered stretched between four pillars, bound and burned with fire.

So her contest was finished, and she was loosed from the bonds of the body. A believing woman named Apolinaria asked the governor's wife for her holy body, took it unharmed by the fire and laid it in the garden of her house; and afterwards, when the persecution ceased, she raised a church over the grave. After many years those honoured relics were brought to Constantinople, for the defence and salvation of the city. doxologia.ro's line for the day names her «Sfânta Mare Muceniță Anastasia» and gives no year, and the troparion it prints for her is the one the Church sings to a woman martyr, with her name set in it.
