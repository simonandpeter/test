# Abo of Tbilisi

Abo was an Arab of Baghdad, a maker of perfumes and well read in the books of Islam. Nerses, the ruler of Kartli, had been summoned to the capital of the caliphate on the word of his enemies and held three years in prison; when he was released, Abo met him, was so struck by the man that he chose to follow him home and enter his service. In Georgia he won everyone's regard, learned the language, and then gave himself to the Old and New Testaments, going to the churches when he could, listening closely to the singing and the preaching, and questioning the priests about the Orthodox faith.

He kept his change of mind hidden, because the Muslims had risen against Nerses again and forced him out of Kartli. Nerses crossed the Darial pass with three hundred men, Abo among them, and went north to the Khazars, whose ruler received the Georgians with hospitality. There, where he was in no danger from the followers of Muhammad, Abo was baptised in the name of the Holy Trinity by a devout priest, and confessed his faith without fear. From there the company went on to Abkhazia, where the prince and the bishop called him to them often to speak of Christ; and from the memory of Saint Anthony until Pascha he kept three months of silence, fasting and prayer in strict seclusion.

In 786 the caliph confirmed the government of Kartli to Nerses's nephew Stephen, who asked that his uncle be allowed home. The prince of Abkhazia urged Abo to stay behind where he was safe, since men would try every way to bring him back to the faith he was born in. Abo answered that he would not hide the light Christ had given him, whatever came of it, and went. At Tbilisi he professed his faith openly and preached it without fear, and three years passed and no one laid hands on him.

At the beginning of December 789 he was seized outside the church and dragged before the judge, who gaoled him; he was let out through the prince Stephen, denounced again, and arrested a second time. On 27 December, the memory of the Protomartyr Stephen, the judge sent him back to prison. There he fasted, comforted the other prisoners, and at last stopped answering those who came to turn him. On the eve of Theophany he told the Christians in the cell that in three days he would depart, an angel having told him he would die for the Name.

On Sunday 8 January 790 the officers took him in chains through the streets, and he told the Christians who wept for him to rejoice instead. Before the judge he said only that he was a Christian and would remain one, and the judge ordered his head struck off. Outside he took off his own clothes, crossed himself, knelt and bent his neck; the executioner struck him three times with the flat of the sword to frighten him into denying Christ, and the fourth time with the edge. doxologia.ro keeps him on that day and calls him the protector of Tbilisi.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same eighth of January, and its notice is four sentences. saint.gr says Abo grew up in Baghdad as a Muslim; that at the age of seventeen or eighteen he found himself at Tiflis in Georgia, where he became a Christian; that he was martyred in the year 786 — a year earlier than the year the Romanian life gives; and that he is the patron saint of Tiflis. It prints no hymn for him.
