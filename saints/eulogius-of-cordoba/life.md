# Eulogius of Cordoba

Eulogius was born in the city of Cordoba in Spain in the eighth century, of a devout and noble family, and was reared from childhood on the waters of the Christian faith. The years were hard: the Arab occupation of the country in 711 had brought many evils on the Church there, which was divided into three provinces with twenty-nine bishops and was trying to bear up both under the persecutions of the Mohammedan conquerors and under trials from within, such as the heresies. Cordoba, with its great mosque of the thousand and one columns, took the first place in the Spanish martyrology of the ninth century.

Eulogius had studied the Fathers, the Tradition and the theology of the Church deeply, and his life, the page says, was wholly of a piece with what he read daily and with what the divine Scriptures taught; ascesis, prayer and fasting cleansed his soul. In the year 850 a persecution broke out against the Church of Spain, and the Moors, led on by an apostate bishop, arrested all the priests of Cordoba together with their bishop and shut them in prison. There Eulogius gave the brethren courage to bear the trial with patience, and his words upheld two of his women disciples, his spiritual children, who suffered martyrdom soon after they came out of the prison, in 851; he composed their synaxarion himself, to urge other persecuted Christians to imitate them, and wrote three books of the acts and the ends of the new martyrs of that persecution.

All this made him reckoned the most important churchman of his time, and in 858 the Church chose him for the episcopal office. Before he could take up his duties, before his enthronement, he was arrested again and taken to prison. The charge was that he had sheltered and hidden a young Christian woman, [Leocritia](/saints/leocritia-of-cordoba), whose own parents wanted her to take the religion of Mohammed; and he was accused not only of abduction but of corrupting her. In his defence he said that no shepherd has ever refused his support to any member of his flock, and, more importantly, that it is the duty of a priest of Christ to teach the faithful that if they must choose between God and their parents they are to choose God. Nor did he stop there: he proposed to the Muslim judge that they should debate, so that he might prove to him the deceit of the religion of Mohammed.

He was taken after that before the council of the conqueror king's court, and there went on with his Christian apologetic in the same manhood and boldness of speech; and the end of it was that he was condemned to death by beheading. On the road of his martyrdom, as his Lord had on the road to Golgotha, he received a blow from a eunuch of his executioners' escort; the saint at once turned the other cheek without protest, and the unbeliever struck a second time. Then, silent as always and praying for his persecutors and for his people, he bent his head under the executioner's knife. It was the year 859.
