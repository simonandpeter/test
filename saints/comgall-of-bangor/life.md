# Comgall of Bangor

The venerable Comgall — Comhghall, or in Greek Κομγάλλιος — came of Ireland, saint.gr says, and was born in Ulster, which the page calls a city, in the year 517. He was the son of a soldier. He studied in the school of Saint Finnian, and he founded the famous monastery of Bangor, which was the most populous and the most glorious in Ireland. It is said, the page goes on, that in his own days about three thousand monks lived in that house.

He applied the monastic rules of [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great), and he took Eastern monasticism as his pattern. Comgall fell asleep in peace in the year 602.

That is all the page holds of him. It gives no name to his father, no account of his tonsure, no year for the founding of Bangor, nothing of his rule or his disciples beyond their number, no relics, no burial place, and it prints neither apolytikion nor kontakion. The day's own line writes his name in Latin letters — «Άγιος Comgall» — and only the life page gives a Greek form for it.
