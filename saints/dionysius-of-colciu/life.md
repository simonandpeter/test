# Dionysius of Colciu

Dionysius was born Dumitru Ignat on 22 September 1909 at Vorniceni in the county of Botoşani, the youngest of eight children in a family of peasants. His father Ioan was a man of great faith, but died when Dumitru was little more than a year old. The hardship of the First World War and of the years after it left the boy four classes of primary school, in each of which he took the prize, and two years of trade school.

He was close to his brother Gheorghie, ten years older, and followed him into the monastic life — first in Romania from 1923, and three years later on Mount Athos. Gheorghie received the name Ghimnazie at the Skete of Măgura in Moldavia and was ordained hierodeacon there; Dumitru was to become a monk on the Holy Mountain. The two brothers entered Athos on 6 September 1926 with two companions from Măgura. Dumitru was then seventeen.

After a short pilgrimage the Ignat brothers tried to settle at the Romanian Skete of Prodromu and were not received, because of the troubles the skete was in at that time. With another Romanian monk, the schemamonk Sebastian, they borrowed a considerable sum and bought the cell of the Annunciation on the land of the monastery of Pantocrator. To pay the debt the two worked three years as hired hands at Iviron; and hard as the life was, it was at the Annunciation that they had their greatest spiritual joys.

Dumitru was tonsured in 1927 with the name Dionysius and ordained hierodeacon in 1931. After their elder Sebastian died the brothers went in 1933 to the cell of St Tikhon of Zadonsk at Kapsala, under the elder Ghedeon Chelaru; with him they took over in 1937 the cell of St George at Colciu, which they rebuilt from the foundations and which Dionysius never left again. He was made hieromonk in 1937 by Hierotheos, bishop of the Holy Mountain, and a spiritual father in 1945.

For decades he struggled in silence, following the example of his brother Ghimnazie, who had come to a great holiness before his death in 1965. When Ghedeon Chelaru died in 1979 Dionysius became head of the cell, with the schemamonk Ioan Şova beside him and other brethren added over the decades that followed. Having had the counsel of such fathers as Antipa Dinescu, Elisei Filibiu, Macarie Untan, Ioan Guţu and Dometie Trihenea, he became one of the most experienced spiritual fathers on the Holy Mountain — for Romanian, Greek and other monks alike, and for the thousands of pilgrims who came to Colciu for quiet and for counsel.

He passed to eternal life on 28 April / 11 May 2004, at ninety-five years of age, of which seventy-eight had been spent on the Holy Mountain.
