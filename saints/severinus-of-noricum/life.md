# Severinus of Noricum

saint.gr begins with the place: on the banks of the Danube, on the border of Pannonia and Noricum — today's Austria — lies the city of Asturis. About the year 454 an unknown monk who had come from Asia Minor settled at the gates of the church there. A few days later he began to go about the city warning that the barbarians were preparing a siege and that all the Christians should gather to beg God for their salvation. The Christians did not believe Severinus's words, and so he left the city and withdrew to the nearby city of Comagenis.

There he settled again at the doors of the church to go on with his prophetic preaching; and there an old man arrived from Asturis and announced that it had been taken. Then, as of old with the Ninevites, the Christians began to repent and to ask the mercy of God — and in three days a strong earthquake sowed panic among the invaders, who fled in terror and left the city free. Severinus continued his prophetic work in the city of Favianis, and after its deliverance withdrew to a deserted and quiet place to live according to Christ.

The holiness of his life drew many monks to him, Christians and pagans, and he taught them by his own life the Church's teaching of watchfulness, the renunciation of their own will, and the mystery of piety in the name of Christ. His love for the suffering, and above all for the poor and for those taken captive in the barbarian raids, was great. For thirty continuous years Severinus and his disciples worked in an evangelical way for the transformation of men and of their manners and customs in Christ, and so became, the page says, the enlighteners of Austria. When the Church called him to be ordained bishop he refused.

A few years later the barbarians besieged the monastery. The monks, trying to carry away whatever was precious in it, took his holy relic too, which they found incorrupt, and brought it to Naples. The page prints no hymn and no year of his death.
