# Roman

The Serbian calendar’s note for him is a troparion of the common kind for a monastic saint — a desert-dweller and an angel in the body, a wonderworker who by fasting, vigil and prayer received heavenly gifts and heals those who come to him in faith — and no life. The Diocese of Niš, on its page for his monastery, supplies what tradition holds.

On the right bank of the South Morava, near the village of Đunis, stands the monastery of St Roman with its church of the Annunciation, one of the oldest in Serbia: the first surviving written mention is in the chrysobull of the emperor Basil II of 1019, where it is called Sventeromon. It was raised on the place where the venerable Roman the Sinaite lived his ascetic life and reposed, having, by tradition, settled there after 888. Little is known of his life, and there are two suppositions: that he was one of the Sinaite monks who came to Serbia from Sinai and the Holy Mountain in the time of Prince Lazar and settled across the land; or that he was a disciple of Clement of Ohrid and came from Ohrid with Prochorus of Pčinja, John of Rila, [Joachim of Osogovo](/saints/joachim-of-osogovo) and Gabriel of Lesnovo to preach Christianity in Slavonic to the Slavs of these parts. His holy relics are kept to this day in the monastery church. Prince Lazar restored the monastery in 1377; after its destruction in the Austro-Turkish war of 1716–18, Đorđe Pila rebuilt it in 1795 in thanks for a healing at Roman’s relics.
