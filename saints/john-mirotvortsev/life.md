# John (Mirotvortsev)

**The line names three men, gives one year, and the host that prints it prints no life for any of them.** days.pravoslavie.ru heads him on name page 9620 and stops there; the life here is the one azbyka.ru prints, out of игумен Дамаскин (Орловский)’s «Жития новомучеников и исповедников Российских ХХ века. Март». He was born on 25 March 1881 in the village Елшанка of the Саратовский уезд, son of a peasant, and out of the Саратовская Духовная семинария was ordained priest to the church of the village Вязьмино, where he served nine years, in what the life calls a time of flowering in that diocese.

Moved to the church at Таволожка in the same уезд, he came to count for so much among his parishioners that they brought him first every question that troubled them. Where the authorities elsewhere were taking land away from priests to their parishioners’ indifference, here the attempt failed: the well-off peasants and the poor opposed it with one voice. In 1928 the state began again on the peasantry and the Church together, and that year he was given a month’s imprisonment «как злостный несдатчик излишков хлеба», as a malicious withholder of surplus grain.

At a general meeting of the peasants in May 1928 the local party man called for grain to be given up voluntarily, and the room answered with silence; then it was debated whether the priest should have land at all. Speaking to the peasants, he said he was being deprived of it on false certificates, «без ведома вас, старичков» — without the knowledge of you old men; and the meeting voted him his land. The next day, members of the village soviet testified, he came in with his wife and called them «не правители, а просто жулики и хулиганы» and their soviet «грачиное гнездо», a rookery. An act was drawn up and sent to the prosecutor.

In March 1929 almost everything he had was taken for the undelivered grain, and a month later the officials came for what was left. He was out of the village, but a great crowd of believers stood before his house and shouted «Грабители! Кровопийцы!» into their faces, and that time the authorities gave way. On 19 October a meeting demanded three hundred poods of grain from him. «Вы что же, опять на меня накладываете — что у меня, больше всех уродилось?!» he answered, and asked whose decision it was: «Да вы прямо скажите, что ты нам, батюшка, не нужен.»

He and three peasants were arrested on 9 November 1929 and shut in the prison at Аткарск. At the interrogation he remembered the quarrel of May 1928 well, and said he had been deprived of his allotment on the pretext of hiring labour to work it, and had gone to the general meeting for support; abusive words he denied, and he denied resisting the seizure of his property and organising anything on the back of it. The investigation closed on 15 December with the charge that the men had gathered in the priest’s house «под видом религиозности» to give an anti-Soviet activity its organised shape.

**The prosecutor wanted an open trial and the secret police did not, and said why.** The case was fully proved, the prosecutor held, and belonged in court; the authorised officer of the secret section wrote back that an open trial would give no positive result, «потому что проходящий по делу возглавляющий группировку священник Миротворцев И.Н. имеет значительный авторитет и религиозное влияние среди всех прослоек села, и особенно бедноты» — because the priest at the head of the group had great authority and religious influence among every layer of the village, and most of all among the poor.

On 24 and 25 January 1930 a troika of the secret police gave him five years, and he was sent to a camp on the building of the White Sea and Baltic canal. When the term was served he was forbidden to live in the Lower Volga country — that is, in the Saratov country where he had served more than a quarter of a century and where his whole flock remained. He went instead to the church of the village Приимково in the Гаврилов-Ямский district of the Yaroslavl country, and did not serve there long.

In the summer of 1937 a group of clergy and laity of that district was arrested, and some of those taken agreed to bear false witness. He was arrested on 6 August 1937 and shut in the Yaroslavl prison, and a week later the investigator put it to him that he belonged to a church insurrectionary group. «Участником церковно-повстанческой группировки я не был, и о существовании таковой мне ничего не известно», he answered; the gatherings in his flat he denied, and the counter-revolutionary work among the people he denied. On 28 September a troika of the NKVD gave him ten years in a corrective labour camp. **He died in Ухтпечлаг on 18 March 1938 and was buried in an unknown grave.**
