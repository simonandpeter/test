# Andrew of Oxyrhynchus

Andrew is one of four hermits whom the Greek calendar keeps together on the second of December. The four — [John](/saints/john-of-oxyrhynchus), [Heracleimon](/saints/heracleimon-of-oxyrhynchus) and [Theophilus](/saints/theophilus-of-oxyrhynchus) — came of the city of Oxyrhynchus in Lower Egypt, on the west bank of the Nile, saint.gr says, and they were the sons of Christian parents. They read the holy Scriptures, and out of that reading they wanted the life of the desert.

In the desert they came to know some very old and devout hermit and stayed a year beside him, labouring. When their elder died they stayed on in that place for sixty whole years with much asceticism and prayer. Every Saturday they would go out to the nearest settlements and preach the word of God to the people, and so they held souls up until they died peaceably.

That is the whole of the page's account, and it says nothing of any one of the four apart from the others: no year, no century, no emperor, no monastery, no relic, and no hymn. The couplet set over them counts them as a tetrad of men who came out of a place full of affliction into a place without grief. Because the page names all four, each of them has a folder here, and this is Andrew's.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the Julian 2 December with the other three, in one declined clause closing on a century: «Прпп. Иоанна, Ираклемона, Андрея и Феофила (IV).» It gives the four of them no life page, no notice and no hymn, and the fourth century it brackets is the only dating it offers. What it does add is a name page for each, and those pages put the city in the name and set the country beside it in brackets: Oxyrhynchite, the Egyptian. So this source confirms the city the Greek page gave the four and adds the country, and nothing else.
