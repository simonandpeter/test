# The Twenty-six Martyrs of Gothia

Twenty-six martyrs suffered in Gothia, and doxologia.ro's life names all but five of them. Two were priests, Vatus and Virca, and with Virca suffered two of his sons and three of his daughters, whose names the notice does not give. One, Aprilla, was a monk. Eleven were laymen: Avip, Agna, Riax, Igatrax, Iscoos, Sila, Sighita, Suiril, Seimvla, Terma and Filga. Seven were women: Ana, Alla, Varis, Moico, Mamica, Birgo and Animais.

These saints lived in the time of Jungurich, king of the Goths, and of Gratian, emperor of the Romans, and for the love of Christ they received the martyr's crown by fire, when Jungurich ordered the church to be set alight in which the twenty-six of them were gathered at prayer. There burned with them a man whose name has not been kept, who was bringing his offering to the altar.

That is the whole of what the Romanian calendar prints for them; it gives no year, and no troparion. The Romanian line calls them «din Goția/Dacia».

The Greek calendar keeps them on the same day and, where doxologia.ro counts them, saint.gr names them. It sets the martyrdom in the years of Iungourichos, king of the Goths, and of the emperor Gratian, between 375 and 383: while they were all in church and singing, the Gothic king had them seized and thrown alive into the fire. One more died with them, a Christian who was bringing his offering to the church, was seized on the way, confessed Christ, and was thrown into the flames, offering himself instead of any other offering. The relics were gathered by the wife of another chief of the Gothic nation, a Christian woman, together with presbyters and laymen; leaving the rule to her son, she wandered from place to place with her daughter as far as the land of the Romans, then went back to her own country, having bequeathed the holy relics to her daughter, who on her way to Cyzicus gave the city a portion of them and shortly afterwards came to the end of her life.

The page then sorts the twenty-six, with a variant spelling for several of the names: the two presbyters Bathouses (or Aathouses) and Ouirkas (or Ouerikas) with his two sons and three daughters; Arpylas the monk; the laymen Abepas, Agnas (or Angias), Ryax (or Ryias), Hegathrax, Eskoos (or Eskoes), Syllas, Sigetzas (or Sidetzas), Souerillas, Seimblas, Thermas (or Therthas) and Philgas; and the women Anna, Allas, Baris (or Barka), Moiko, Mamyka, Ouirko (or Oueko) and Animais. Its epigram is for the whole number at once: «Τόσην πυρὶ φλέγουσι πληθὺν Μαρτύρων, Ὄσας ἄγει Μὴν σήμερον τὰς ἡμέρας» — they burn in the fire as great a multitude of martyrs as the month has days up to today.

The Russian calendar's reading is fuller than the Romanian one and does not agree with it about the numbers. Its line enumerates twenty-three of the company and gives each a name page: the presbyters «Вафусий» and «Верк», the monk «Арпила», the laymen «Авив», «Агн», «Реас», «Игафракс», «Иской», «Сила», «Сигиц», «Сонирил», «Суимвл», «Ферм» and «Филл», and the women «Анна», «Алла», «Лариса», «Моико», «Мамика», «Уирко», «Анимаиса (Анимаида)», «Гаафа» the Gothic queen and «Дуклида» the Gothic princess. The shared life behind them adds two the line leaves out, «Констанс» and the king's son «Агафон», and puts the burning of the church at about the year 375 under king «Унгерих», with three hundred and eight people dead in the flames, of whom it says twenty-one names are known.

Two readings the same calendar prints disagree about which of the women was the queen, and neither was preferred here. The shared life makes «Алла» the widow of the Gothic king, who gathered the remains of the martyrs with her daughter «Дуклида» and carried them to Syria, went home again and was stoned to death there with her son «Агафон», the relics staying with the daughter, who brought part of them to Cyzicus for the consecration of a church in the reigns the page gives as Valentinian II (364–375) and «Феодосий Великий» (393–395), and died in peace. The other life, which the calendar hangs behind both «Аллы» and «Ларисы», makes «Гаафа» the wife of another Gothic king, who gathered the remains with her daughter «Дуклида» and withdrew with them to Cyzicus — which that page places in the Crimea — and who was stoned by the enraged pagans when she returned to Gothia, her daughter dying in peace at Cyzicus. The day line follows the second and calls «Гаафа» the queen.

Nothing in this folder was divided on the strength of that enumeration. One folder for each named member of a company the source lists is the corpus's standing ruling, but this folder was made from Romanian and Greek lines that named nobody, carries venerated rows for both of them, and already names twenty-one of the company in the paragraphs above; dividing it now would keep one company twice over, and that is the author's decision and not a reader's.
