# Lucia of Syracuse

When the honour of the martyr [Agatha](/saints/agatha-of-catania) was spreading through Sicily and the people of Syracuse were coming from far off to her tomb at Catania, Lucia, a virgin of note among the Syracusans, went to the feast with her mother Eutychia, who had had an issue of blood for four years and whom no physician could cure. When the Gospel of the woman healed by touching Christ's garment was read, Lucia said to her mother that if she believed what had been read she should believe this too — that Agatha, who suffered for Christ, is always in his presence — and touch her tomb with faith.

They stayed and prayed with tears before the tomb, and Lucia fell asleep and saw Agatha standing among the angels, who said: sister Lucia, virgin of the Lord, why do you ask of me what you can give at once yourself? Your faith has helped your mother, and she is well; and as the city of Catania is glorified through me, so shall Syracuse be glorified and adorned through you, because you have prepared in your virginity a dwelling acceptable to Christ.

So she talked with her mother day after day, and the goods were divided and given to the poor. Her betrothed, hearing that the estates, the costly ornaments and the pearls were being sold, asked her nurse what it meant, and the nurse told him his betrothed had found an inheritance worth a thousand gold pieces and more, and was selling these things to buy it for him; so he was quiet, and even urged the sale. When he learnt that everything had gone to the poor and to strangers and to the servants of God, he went to Paschasius, the governor of the city, and denounced her as a Christian and an enemy of the imperial laws.

Called before him and pressed to sacrifice, she answered that the living sacrifice and the pure faith before God is to visit the poor and the widows in their troubles; that for three years she had done nothing else but offer that sacrifice to the living God; and that now, having nothing left of her own to bring, she offered herself alive, for him to do with his own offering what pleased him. Paschasius ordered her taken to the brothel to be abused until she died — and the Holy Spirit gave her body such weight that they could not move her from the spot, nor with ropes tied to her hands and feet, nor with many men dragging: she stood like a mountain.

Then he had a great fire lit round her and burning pitch, brimstone and boiling oil poured over her, and she stood unmoved and unhurt in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, telling him she had asked her Lord that the fire should have no power over her, so that those who hope in Christ should laugh at him, and that she had asked for her suffering to be drawn out, to take away the fear of torments from believers and the voice of mockery from unbelievers. Some of his friends, unable to bear his rage and helplessness, ordered her struck through the throat with a sword.

Wounded as she was she did not weaken, but prayed and spoke to the people: she brought them the good news that peace had been given to the Church of God, that Diocletian had fallen from the empire and Maximian was now dead; and that as Catania has her sister Agatha for an intercessor, so they were to know that she had been sent by God to their city. While she spoke, Paschasius was led past her in chains — the Sicilians had sent secretly to Rome about his plundering — and was taken away to be condemned and beheaded. Her spirit did not leave her until the priests came and gave her the holy Mysteries; and when all had said Amen, she gave up her soul, and a church was built over the place where she was laid.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, and saint.gr fills out the story the Romanian line only sketched. It has her of Syracuse in Sicily, martyred in the year 304 when Paschasios was governor of Sicily and Diocletian emperor of Rome, a virgin betrothed to a pagan. Her mother Eutychia suffered from a long haemorrhage, and the two of them were driven to take refuge at Catania, in the church of the wonderworking [Agatha](/saints/agatha-of-catania), for Eutychia's healing. There Lucia saw Agatha in her sleep, and Agatha assured her that her mother would be healed and that she herself would meet a martyr's end.

When her mother's health was restored, Lucia shared out her property among the poor and waited in prayer for the end Agatha had spoken of. Her holding to the faith of her fathers, and her bearing altogether, were what moved her betrothed against her, and he did not hesitate to denounce her to the governor Paschasios as a Christian. He, after questioning her, ordered her shut in a brothel to be dishonoured; and yet a divine power held her unmoved on the spot where she stood, though the soldiers first dragged at her with force and then tied her to a yoke drawn by many pairs of oxen.

Angry at failing, the soldiers smeared her with pitch, resin and oil and set her alight to burn her alive; and it was a divine intervention, the page says, that put the fire out. Then they put out her eyes with a dagger, and a soldier sank his knife into her throat and they left her lying in her blood. She asked to receive the immaculate Mysteries, and so provisioned gave up her soul to the Lord, having first prophesied the swift end of idolatry and the victory and prevailing of Christianity.

In 886, the page adds, [Leo](/saints/leo-of-catania), bishop of Catania, raised a beautiful church in her honour at his own expense. Her body, incorrupt and fragrant, was carried to Constantinople and stayed there until 1204, when the crusaders took it, and in 1280 to Venice; since 1860 it is kept and venerated in the church of Saints Jeremiah and Lucy, and in 1955 her head was covered with a worked revetment of beaten silver in the shape of a head. In iconography she is shown holding a palm branch and a dish with eyes on it, to remind us of her martyrdom, and by old tradition she was honoured as the protectress of the eyes.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the same menologion day, agrees with the Greek page on the year and the governor — she suffered in 304, Paschasius judging — and where that page had little else it gives the whole story. She went with her mother to the grave of Saint [Agatha](/saints/agatha-of-catania) at Catania, and Agatha appeared to her there; and her mother, who had an issue of blood, was healed by a wonder in that same church.

She gave her whole estate away to the destitute, which embittered her betrothed, and it was he who accused her to the judge as a Christian. The wicked judge ordered her taken to a brothel to be defiled; but by the power of God she became immovable, as though nailed to the ground, and a multitude of men could not shift her from the spot. Then an enraged pagan drove a sword into her throat, and she gave up her soul to God and passed over into the kingdom that is everlasting.
