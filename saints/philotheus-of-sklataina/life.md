# Philotheus of Sklataina

saint.gr says he lived between 1475 and 1550, if one takes into account that he was abbot of the holy monastery of Saint Stephen at Meteora in the year 1545. He came from Sklataina, the village now called Rizoma; he was a contemporary of Bessarion, archbishop of Larissa and Trikke, whom the page sends the reader to find on 15 Σεπτεμβρίου, and he was the founder of the monastery of the Transfiguration of the Saviour at Dousiko.

Shortly before 1545, the page says, he renovated — or rather rebuilt — the old, small and elegant katholikon of the monastery, the church of Saint Stephen, with the help of the hieromonk Gerasimos; the little church he raised is a timber-roofed single-aisled basilica with an inner narthex. The two founders of the monastery of Saint Stephen, Antonios Katakouzinos and Philotheus the Simataniote, are depicted in wall-paintings on either side of the narthex door with haloes, in their monastic habit and with their austere ascetic features, and with the princes of the heavenly hosts Michael and Gabriel beside them as a guard of honour. The inscription on the right as one enters reads: Ο ΟΣΙΟΣ ΠΑΤΗΡ ΗΜΩΝ ΦΙΛΟΘΕΟΣ ΚΑΙ ΚΤΗΤΩΡ ΤΟΥ ΑΓΙΟΥ ΣΤΕΦΑΝΟΥ ΥΠΑΡΧΩΝ ΕΚ ΧΩΡΑΣ ΣΚΛΑΤΑΙΝΗΣ.

He also rebuilt «τας κέλλας και τας λοιπάς οικοδομάς» and generally improved the whole building complex of the monastery. That he spent so much on these works, and enriched the monastery's dependency in his own birthplace of Sklataina with his patrimonial lands, as Gerasimos did with his, means — the page reasons — that he was well off, and that he laid out his father's property on the renovation of the monastery and on vestments.

The page adds that a most elegant church in the saint's birthplace, dedicated to him, was raised with the metropolitan of Trikke and Stagoi, Alexios, and with the care of the priest of Rizoma, Stergios Lioutas, and the help of all the parishioners, and was consecrated by the metropolitan on 6 November 2004; and that a supplicatory canon to him was composed in 1997 by the hieromonk Athanasios of Simonopetra. It quotes one troparion of his service which asks him, as a lover of his own country, to keep his village from sickness and afflictions.
