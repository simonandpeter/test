# Bebaia

[Thathuel](/saints/thathuel-the-martyr) and Bebaia were brother and sister
and lived in the days of the emperor Hadrian — or, by others, Trajan — in
116. Thathuel was a priest of the demonic delusion and was taught
Christianity by a bishop; for this conversion the toparch Augarus had him
cruelly scourged, then his eyes put out, then hung by one hand, his belly
flayed and his sides burned with fire. At last both received the crown of
martyrdom, beheaded — cut down together, the verse says, Thathuel and
Bebaia found a sure life in place of the false one. Sophronius
Eustratiades in his Hagiologion adds that these martyrs were from Edessa
in Syria and were made Christians by the bishop Barsippus, or Barsimaeus.

The Russian calendar keeps Thathuel and his sister Bebaia — Thiphael and
Thibea or Vivea, as it spells them — on 5 September (Julian), between 98
and 138: they suffered for their bold and successful preaching of
Christianity among the pagans; after long and refined tortures Thathuel
was hung on a tree and sawn through, and his sister killed with a
spear-thrust to the neck.
