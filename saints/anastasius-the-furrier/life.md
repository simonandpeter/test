# Anastasius the Furrier

saint.gr says the glorious hieromartyr of Christ Anastasius was born about the end of the seventeenth or the beginning of the eighteenth century, and came from the village of Agios Vlasios — Souvliasi was its older name — in Thesprotia, near the town of Igoumenitsa. What is known of him, the page says, comes from the Life preserved in the Great Synaxarion of the Orthodox Church, which chiefly records his martyr's end; of his childhood and youth nothing is known. His devout upbringing and God's calling brought him to join the clergy and to serve God and his people as a presbyter.

The synaxarion's account begins while he was serving a church outside Constantinople. The martyrdom of Saint Constantius the Russian happened at that time, and Anastasius felt the longing for martyrdom kindle in him and begged God every day to count him worthy to give his blood too for the faith of Christ. There was living in the city a Cypriot hieromonk who, abandoned by God's grace for the sinful life he led, had at seventy denied Christ and embraced the faith of Mohammed, and who went with the sheikhs to the Yeni Cami and preached on its steps to the Muslims going in, taking money from them.

One day, going to the market, Anastasius passed the mosque and stopped to look at the apostate on the steps, grieving in his heart at the height of Christ's truth from which the man had fallen. Turks passing by told him: look, priest, this sheikh was a priest too, and he came to know our faith and became a Turk; come and be a Turk yourself and gain Paradise. The saint answered them hard words that were true: that they were blind and deceived, that what the man told them was all lies, and that they, Turks from their forefathers, had set a rotten old man to teach them their faith — one whom God had abandoned for his great wickedness and who had denied his own faith in his old age.

Having said that and more besides with great boldness, in Turkish, he fell silent. They seized him and brought him before the kadi and then the Vizier, and to them he said the same, so that they decided to exile him to Chios. When they were about to put him aboard the ship he contrived to be brought back before the Vizier, and asked him why he was being exiled for telling the truth, and said that they kept blind and deceived men to teach them because they would not hear it. He mocked their religion and preached the faith of the Christians so boldly that all who heard him were left speechless; and by order of the Mufti they beheaded him in front of the Yeni Cami. It was 8 July 1743.

After the cutting off of his honourable head, the page says, a heavenly light appeared by night over the relic of the hieromartyr, and was seen by the guards who were watching it and by those who passed that way, Christians and Turks, and all marvelled at the strange sight: the Agarenes were put to shame by the sign and the Christians glorified God, who gives glory back to his faithful servants. The relic was then given to the Christians for burial; but tradition did not preserve the place of that burial, nor any other word about him after his death.
