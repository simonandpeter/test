# Theodosius the Cenobiarch

Theodosius was born at Mogarion, a village in the parts of Cappadocia, of believing parents, Proaeresius and Eulogia, who raised him in good habits and in learning. Because he was sweet of speech and a reader such as no one else, he was appointed to read the holy books in church before the people; and reading them for the profit of those who listened, he drew the greatest profit himself. The word to Abraham, to go out from his land and his kindred, and the Gospel's counsel to leave father and mother and brothers for the sake of eternal life, set him burning to leave everything and follow Christ by the narrow and difficult road.

He took that road to Jerusalem in the reign of Marcian, near the end of that emperor's life, while the fourth council of the whole world was gathered at Chalcedon against Dioscorus and Eutyches. Passing through Antioch he turned aside to [Symeon the Stylite](/saints/symeon-the-stylite), and before ever setting eyes on him the stylite called out from the pillar, "Welcome, man of God, Theodosius", and foretold that he would be a shepherd of rational sheep and would snatch many from the wolf. At Jerusalem, under the patriarch Juvenal, he judged that he should first learn from the fathers how to fight the unseen enemies, and put himself under an elder named Longinus, whose cell stood beside the tower the old writers called David's.

Later he was moved, at the asking of a believing widow named Glyceria, to a church she had built to the Theotokos; and when the report of his goodness drew visitors he withdrew to a mountain cave, where his relics lie to this day and where, the old accounts say, the three magi rested on their way home. His prayer was unceasing and his vigil lasted the night, his tears ran always as from springs, and for thirty years he tasted no bread at all, living on dates, lentils, desert herbs and roots, and those very few; when even that failed he ate date stones soaked in water.

In the reign of Anastasius (491-518), who had taken the sceptre after Leo the Great (457-474) and Zeno (474-491), and who was infected with the heresy of Eutyches and of Severus and set aside the council of Chalcedon (451), thirty pounds of gold came to Theodosius as though for the food and clothing of the poor and the needs of the sick, and in truth to buy the will of a man all Palestine obeyed. He did not send the gold back, lest he seem to slight the emperor's faith, but gave it away in alms; and when the imperial letters asked him for a confession of faith framed to the emperor's mind, he gathered the citizens of the desert and wrote back that of the two things set before them, to live senselessly in hatefulness or to die with honour following the right dogmas of the saints, they honoured death the more, and that they received no new dogmas but kept what the fathers before them had laid down.

The emperor was shamed and quieted for a while, then rose against the right faith again, and orders went out everywhere, and to the holy city itself, setting the councils aside. Then the old man came down from his own house into the great church of Jerusalem, went up to the place where it is the priests' custom to read to the people, made a sign for silence and cried with a great voice that if anyone does not reckon the four holy ecumenical councils equal to the four Gospels, let him be anathema. Not one of his opponents dared to say a word. He was sent into exile for it; Anastasius was soon deprived of this passing life, and Theodosius returned to his own place with all who had suffered banishment for the faith, and Agapitus, bishop of old Rome, and Ephrem, bishop of Antioch, wrote to him in praise of his endurance.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and its own header dates him 423 to 529. saint.gr gives his birth at the village of Mogarissou in Cappadocia, of very faithful and virtuous parents, Proaeresius and Eulogia, and sets his life in the years of the emperor Leo the Great, whom it dates 457 to 474, and on into the reigns that came after. Over him it prints the line «Ἑνδεκάτῃ ὀλοὸν βίοτον λίπε Κοινοβιάρχης» — on the eleventh the Cenobiarch left this ruinous life.
