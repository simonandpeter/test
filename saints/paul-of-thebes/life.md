# Paul of Thebes

Almost everything told of Paul comes from a single text: the *Life of Paul the
First Hermit*, written by Jerome around 375. In it, Paul flees the Decian
persecution as a young man, hides in a cave in the Thebaid, and stays there for
some ninety years, fed by a raven that brings him half a loaf each day.

The narrative turns on a meeting near its end.
[Anthony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great), by then famous as the first of
the desert fathers, is told in a dream that another has preceded him, and goes
looking. He finds Paul near death; the raven brings a whole loaf that day. Paul
dies, and two lions dig his grave.

Jerome's purpose in writing was partly to argue that Anthony was not the first
hermit, and no earlier source mentions Paul at all. Historians have therefore
read the *Life* as anything from embroidered memory to deliberate invention.
Both traditions that keep his feast keep it regardless, which is the situation
this project records rather than resolves.
