# Arethas the Recluse

saint.gr says the venerable Arethas lived at the end of the fourteenth and the beginning of the fifteenth century, came of the city of Tver in Russia, and became a monk in the Lavra of Kyiv. He was elected bishop, but a little later withdrew from it, to follow instead the hesychast road of the enclosed monastic life — the ἔγκλειστος of his epithet, the one shut in.

He fell asleep in peace in the year 1409, and the translation of his holy relics took place in 1483. The page gives no year for his birth, no name for the see he was elected to and laid down, no cell, no elder, no disciple, no resting place for the relics it says were translated, no distich and no hymn.

The same Greek day keeps [Arsenius](/saints/arsenius-bishop-of-tver), bishop of Tver, whose particulars run alongside his almost exactly — Tver, the Lavra of Kyiv, an election to the episcopate, and the same year of repose. They are kept apart here because the calendar prints two different names, and the corpus's rule is that two entries on one calendar are two commemorations; a third entry of that day, a bare Arsenius of Russia, was not drafted at all for want of anything to tell it from the bishop.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 2 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/02/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3892/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
