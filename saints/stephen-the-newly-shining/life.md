# Stephen the Newly Shining

saint.gr's calendar for 9 December keeps the venerable Stephen the newly shining, he who lies in the church of Saint Antipas. He was born at Constantinople, the page says, and brought up in piety by his parents Zacharias and Theophano; and he was a fervent contender for Orthodoxy during the persecutions under the emperor Theophilos the iconoclast.

In the years of the empress Theodora the patriarch Methodios ordained Stephen presbyter and enrolled him in the clergy of the archbishopric of Constantinople. When his father died he inherited a great fortune, and spent it on works of mercy. He lived at the church of Saint Antipas, into which crowds of people of every class used to come to hear the divine word from him. He died, the page ends, when he had completed the seventy-third year of his age.

The page's head gives his years as 838 to 912, which agrees with that seventy-third year and with the reigns it names; its own first sentence calls him a man of the eighth century, which does not. The couplet set over his name turns on it: you led the crown out of the earth and out of life, and you, Word, crowned him with the crown of glory.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, and its one year makes a dossier that was already in dispute harder rather than easier. He reposed in the Lord, it says, in 829, in the seventy-third year of his earthly life, having struggled fifty-five years for the sake of the kingdom of Christ. The Greek page gives the same age of seventy-three and the years 838 to 912, while its own body puts him in the eighth century; so this book agrees with that body against that head. And then it contradicts itself in the same four lines, by making his father a priest of the Great Church in the days of the patriarch Methodios, whose patriarchate fell between 843 and 847. No interval has been built out of these years here: the only one that would hold 829 and 912 together would have him die before the birth the folder records, and the birth cannot be moved without subtracting an age from a year.

What the book adds is a childhood. He was born and brought up at Constantinople in the house of his parents, Zacharias and Theophania; his father was a priest of the Great Church, and while his mother carried him she fed on nothing but bread and water. When the child was born a cross of light shone on his breast, and for that, and for his pure and God-pleasing life, he was called the Newly Shining.

In his eighteenth year he shut himself up at the church of Saint Peter the Apostle and gave himself to the labour of fasting and prayer; and Saint Peter appeared to him once and said, peace to you, child, you have begun well, may the Lord strengthen you. Afterwards he lived many years at the church of the holy martyr Antipas, and that saint also appeared and strengthened him with the words, know that I will not leave you. He laid greater and greater labour on himself, and took food twice a week, and that only unsalted herbs.
