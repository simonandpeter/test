# Jacob the Anchorite

saint.gr's calendar for 26 Νοεμβρίου names «Όσιος Ιάκωβος ο Αναχωρητής» and names its source for him: Theodoret of Cyrus, who knew the venerable man personally, tells of him in the Philotheos Historia, number 21. He came from the city of Cyrus, and practised his asceticism at the beginning inside a very narrow cell.

Afterwards he went up the mountain near the city of Cyrus, and there practised his asceticism without building himself even a hut. He reached such great heights of spirituality that God once counted him worthy to raise the child of a family from the dead. So, having lived strictly in the ascetic life, he died in peace.

The couplet set over the line turns on his epithet: Jacob, having withdrawn from the world, now inhabits the homeland that is above the world. The page gives him no year, no century, no monastery, no relics and no hymn.

The Russian calendar names him on the same day and adds three things to the account above: a year, a pair of ascetic habits, and a gift. Its line closes «(457)», which is the only year any calendar read gives him. Its life has him struggling on a mountain not far from the city of Cyrus in Syria under a heavy sickness, wearing chains all the while, taking food only in the evening and praying without ceasing. By those labours, it says, he came to a high spiritual perfection and received from the Lord authority over the demons, the gift of healings, and even the raising of the dead — which is the same wonder the Greek page reports of him once, over the child of a household — and in advanced age he departed to the Lord in peace.
