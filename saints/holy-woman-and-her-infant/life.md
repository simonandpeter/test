# The Holy Woman and her Holy Infant

saint.gr's calendar for 24 Οκτωβρίου names a holy woman and her holy infant and gives neither of them a name. They were martyred together with [Arethas](/saints/aretas-of-nagran), the page says, and the whole of its own notice is this: the woman was thrown into the fire, and the holy infant, seeing it, fell into the fire itself, for all the coaxing of its torturers. The couplet above says that the infant followed its mother into the fire with stammering cries while she was quietly being turned to ash.

For the rest the page quotes Nicodemus the Hagiorite, who says he cannot be silent about this child, and that the story was written in Greek by Symeon the Metaphrast and is found translated in the New Paradise. A devout and virtuous woman had a boy of about five years. When Arethas had been beheaded she went up to the body, took of the martyr's blood and anointed herself and her child with it; then, warmed by divine love, she cursed and reviled the Jewish tyrant, and the soldiers who heard her seized her and took her to the king, who at once gave sentence that she be burned.

The child, left behind, grieved and sighed like a foal taken from its mother, and called nothing but her name; and seeing the king on his throne it ran and fell at his feet and begged for her in its unformed, stammering voice. The king took it on his knees and asked what it loved best of all the things in the world; it said, I love my mother, and I came to beg you to order her untied, so that she may take me with her to martyrdom — for she taught me many times, urging me to it. And what is this martyrdom? asked the king. To die for Christ, the child answered, and to live again with him. And who is this Christ? Come, let us go to the church and I will show him to you.

Then, seeing the soldiers dragging his mother to the fire, the child wept and asked to be let go after her. The king said, leave your mother and come with me, and I will give you fine fruit; and the child answered that it had thought him a Christian, which was why it had come to beg for its mother, but now that it understood he was a Jew it would never live with him nor accept anything at all from him — only let it go to its mother. They thought to send it to the queen, that her coaxing might keep it in the palace; but it gave no answer at all and looked only towards its mother. When it saw her thrown into the fire its heart ached, and sitting on the king's knees it bent down and bit his thigh hard; the king in pain threw it off and ordered a lord to take it and make it deny Christ, but it slipped away from the man who was pulling it, ran to the furnace and leapt gladly into the middle of it, and embracing its mother sweetly it inherited the crown of martyrdom with her.
