# Matrona (Grosheva)

She was born in 1882 at Varyukovka in the Yegoryevsk district of the Ryazan province, to the peasants Naum and Platonida Groshev, who had four daughters and a son; her elder sister [Maria (Grosheva)](/saints/maria-grosheva) had been born there in 1876. The calendar keeps the two of them on one day and the one life it has is one life for both. Three of the daughters, Maria, Matrona and Pelagia, went in their youth to an elder whom many honoured for his ascetic life and his foresight and asked him how they should live. To Maria and Matrona he said: «В монастырь, в монастырь...» — to the monastery, to the monastery; and to Pelagia, to an ungodly family, marry.

In 1909 the two sisters entered the Alexander and Maria monastery, ten versts from Yegoryevsk, as novices; Matrona was set to the monastery farm and Maria to the sewing. After the monastery was closed they went home, lived a while in their father's house, and then moved into the watchman's cottage beside the two churches of the Kazan icon and the great-martyr Paraskeve at Tugoles in the Shatura district, one of stone and one of wood, both set on a high hill in a sea of pine. They lived there twenty years, baking the bread for the church, serving in the sanctuary, cleaning it, and quilting blankets for what else they needed. The village loved them for their gentle and open ways, and they taught their nieces to pray and one of them to read the Psalter.

The chairman of the village soviet hated the church bitterly and would come out onto the road whenever a service began to try to frighten away the peasants going to it; when the services had stopped he pulled the wooden bell-tower down to its foundations and put a lemonade-bottling shop inside the church itself. After the church was shut the sisters walked the fifteen kilometres to the Kazan church at Petrovskoye, and before every service they urged the peasant women to come with them. In November 1937 the two priests and the psalmist of Tugoles were arrested and from then on the sisters were the only women left in the neighbourhood who could read the Psalter over a dead kinsman and teach a person to pray. On 15 February 1938 the chairman of the village soviet wrote, as a false witness, that the nuns carried church books into the farmers' houses and read them about the birth of Jesus Christ, about God's making of the world, about paradise and about the Last Judgement.

The sisters were arrested on 26 February 1938 and shut in the prison at Yegoryevsk. Asked what she did, living at the church of Tugoles, Matrona answered that for twenty years she and her sister had served at the services and lived on what the church gave them. Asked whether she held services in her house and went to the farmers' houses to hold them, and whether she spoke against the collective farms and the Soviet power, she said that there were no services in her house, but that she had told the farmers and whoever came about Christ; that she had not gone to their houses to perform rites and had voiced no discontent with the power; and that she had said prayer was what feast days were for and the work of the farm could wait, which was true, and in which she saw no agitation. On 11 March 1938 an NKVD troika sentenced the two of them to be shot and they were taken to the Taganka prison in Moscow. They were shot on 20 March 1938 and buried in an unmarked common grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow, which civil day is the Julian 7 March the calendar keeps them on.
