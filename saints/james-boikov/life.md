# James (Boikov)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him on 6 апреля ст. ст. in four words, «Cщмч. Иакова пресвитера (1943).» — and the C that opens the line is a Latin C and not a Cyrillic one, a slip of the page’s own markup that splits the entry in two for anything reading it mechanically. The name link behind it, page 9581, gives the surname and the office the line withholds, «Иаков (Бойков), иерей, сщмч.», and the same day carries his elder brother, [John (Boikov)](/saints/john-boikov), under the same surname and a different year.

He was born in 1896 in Bezhetsk, the son of a priest of his own name. From childhood his favourite occupation was the reading of spiritual books and his favourite game was «игра в храм», playing at church: in the attic of the house he fitted himself out a small library and set up a «church» — hung icons and little bells, and in the morning, when the time for the service came, rang them. Everybody about him supposed the boy would give his life to the service of God, most likely as a monk, and they were much surprised when he married.

He finished the spiritual school, entered the Tver seminary, and on finishing it in 1915 went to the Moscow Theological Academy. The academy was closed after the overthrow of 1917; he had no relations at Sergiev Posad and the hunger and the ruin forced him to go to his sister at Bezhetsk. The gymnasium there had been renamed a technical school but the teaching staff was the same, and they took him as a master. He worked two years and was dismissed, because he was a priest’s son and did not hide his views of the life around him. Shortly before taking orders he married a girl who had finished the diocesan school and was working as a teacher in a soviet one, and in becoming a priest’s wife she lost what little material security she had.

In 1923 he was ordained priest to the church of the great martyr Catherine in the village Закрупье, where he served until 1930, when he moved to Bezhetsk. There were no vacant places for a priest in the town, so he went to the village Кирилловское in the Максатихинский district and served two years; then a place fell vacant at Княжево, near Bezhetsk, and he served there until 1938. He had the unbroken persecution of the twenties and thirties to live through. Where the authorities did not arrest a priest they laid arbitrary and deliberately impossible taxes on him and his family to force him out of the parish, and in those years, the page says, priests had it harder than their peasant parishioners.

His wife said to him more than once: «Яков, бросай ты служить в церкви, уходи, ведь мы только и делаем, что налоги платим, хуже нищих живем.» — give up serving, we do nothing but pay taxes and live worse than beggars. «Я сана с себя никогда не сниму, — никогда не стану предателем Церкви.» I will never take off my orders; I will never become a traitor to the Church.

In 1937 and 1938 all the priests of the region were arrested, with single exceptions; in Bezhetsk the arrest passed over one priest only, who was in his eightieth year, the NKVD men having decided, the page supposes, that he would die soon of himself. On 5 February 1938 a secret informer under the nickname Килограмм laid a report against him, quoting him on the oppression of believers, on the distance between the constitution and what was done, on the elections to the Supreme Soviet in which the communists had elected themselves, and in words about Stalin that the page prints. Such «information» was most often the informer’s own invention, it says, but in the time of the terror nobody checked it, and that was what made it useful. Three days after the report the warrant for the arrest was written out.

In the few years before the arrest he prayed long and earnestly, most often at night: he would light the lamps before the icons in the holy corner, open the book on the lectern and begin to pray in the words of the saints. At one in the morning of the night between 8 and 9 February 1938 there was a knock at the door — the NKVD with witnesses, the neighbours who lived in the same house. A search had to be made; the officer opened the lid of the lectern, poked about in the church books lying in it, shut the lid, and that was the end of the search. He took leave of his wife and daughter and left the house for ever under escort. His wife went several times to the prison in Bezhetsk to ask for a visit, and was refused.

He was interrogated on 9 February and again on 10 February, and the page prints both in question and answer. He told them when he had been deprived of the vote — in 1923, the moment he became a priest; that he had never been tried, except that in 1935 the archbishop had given instruction that births and deaths be registered locally, which he had done, and had been called to account for the unlawful registration of acts of civil status, the case being dropped; and that the records of births and deaths had been kept about a year, in the church, and only for church purposes, for the commemoration of those buried, until the Bezhetsk NKVD took the notebook away.

To every question about counter-revolutionary agitation he answered that he had carried none on, anywhere, ever, and did not acknowledge himself guilty; and when the investigator said the second day that the investigation was not satisfied with the answers of the first, he confirmed them. «Вторично отрицаю.» I deny it a second time. After he had rejected every charge the «duty witnesses» were called, informers among them, and gave the evidence the page quotes: the churches closed and broken up, the collective farmers starved, the new constitution an empty paper, and a famine coming that the Bible foretold. The whole investigation lasted the two days, and on 10 February the indictment was drawn.

On 13 February 1938 a troika of the NKVD sentenced him to ten years in a corrective labour camp and he was sent to the Ekaterinburg region. His complaint to the head of the first special department the page prints at length: that he had been arrested and did not know for what and felt no guilt; that not having committed so grave a crime he could give the investigator no evidence about the charge; that he had signed the record of his own answers having read it, and in it had denied his guilt, the charge not touching him.

He added that he was tormented by the one thought that the ground of the arrest was that he had served as a priest until the day of it, and that he was an unnoticeable, harmless man; that neither persons nor facts had been shown him which would confirm any criminal activity, and there could be none; and that a punishment is a means of correcting a man who knows what he is being punished for.

The last letter his family received is the one the page ends with, written in the war when he had had nothing from them for more than a year: that he sent it at a venture and at a risk, quite unsure it would reach their hands in such a time; that by God’s mercy he was alive and well and in the same place, with no particular change in his condition; that he had not written because letters were not taken, and when they began to be taken he had sent a postcard in November 1941 and had no answer.

Then the question itself: that a man of his own town had just had a letter from his son and was beyond words glad of it, so he was venturing a question — were they alive? Let them send the shortest note, and say whether his mother was living, how they stood for money and work, and whether Вера was alive and where she was studying. Nothing further, of course, was needed. He would wait for the glad news that his own town was not yet «abroad» and that they were all alive and well, and after that, what God gave; and he asked them to be as far-sighted and circumspect as they could be at such a time. He signs it «Горячо любящий вас иерей Яков Бойков».

The conditions in the camp grew heavier through the war: the prisoners were forbidden to write, they were hardly fed, their strength was no longer enough to fill the norm, and such prisoners were stopped being fed altogether. **He died in the camp of hunger on 19 April 1943, which is the civil date of the Julian 6 April**, so this calendar keeps him on the day he died. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
