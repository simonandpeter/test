# Protion, companion of Demes

saint.gr says the holy martyrs [Demes](/saints/demes-companion-of-protion), whom it also spells Demis, and Protion contended together with other Christians in the years of the reign of Maximian, which it dates 285 to 305. They came before the governor of their country of their own accord, and confessed their faith in Christ in front of him; whereupon he gave orders and they were tortured in various ways. They were stripped and bound with chains, and thrown to the ground and beaten without pity, so that their inward parts could be seen.

The governor then shut them in prison and left them there without food and water for thirty days. But an angel of the Lord healed their wounds and gave them food from heaven, the page says, according to the word: man ate the bread of angels. When the governor called them up again to examine them and to see whether they had changed their minds, he found them whole and in health; and as soon as the crowds of the impious saw the wonder of the saints' preservation, they fell at their feet and cried out «Χριστιανοί εσμέν», we are Christians. At that the governor gave orders that they be beheaded.

The page's couplet gives Protion the one thing that tells him from his companion: he was first to have his head taken off, and after him Demes held out his own head to the executioner. Nothing else is told of either of them — no city, no year, no relic and no hymn — and the other Christians who contended with them are neither named nor numbered.
