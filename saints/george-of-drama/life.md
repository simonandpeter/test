# George of Drama

George was born in 1901 at Argyropolis in the country of Pontus — Gümüşhane in present-day Turkey — the son of devout Christians, Sava and Sofia Karslidis, and at baptism he was given the name Athanasius. Orphaned of both parents while small, he was brought up with an elder brother and a sister by his father's mother, who planted in them the love of God and of the Church's services and took them on pilgrimage to the famous monastery of Panaghia Soumela in Pontus, then a flourishing monastic hearth. When she died she left him as a blessing an icon of the Mother of God that he never parted with, and he went away with his grandfather, first to Erzurum and then to Tbilisi.

Full of divine longing, the young Athanasius entered the Georgian monastery of New Athos. There he learned Georgian, and laboured above all at obedience, humility, patience, vigil and prayer. In 1917 the Bolsheviks came to the monastery, hanged all the clergy, and tied the rest of the brotherhood to one another beside a common grave and shot those who would not deny the faith; by God's providence the novice and a few others escaped alive. On 20 July 1919 he was tonsured with the name Simeon, and soon after was imprisoned and mercilessly tormented by the atheists, losing his teeth and suffering grave injury to his legs. Released in 1923, he moved to Sukhumi in Abkhazia, and on 8 September 1925 a Georgian hierarch ordained him priest and changed his name to George.

Young as he was, he received from God the gift of discernment and of seeing before him, for which Georgian, Russian and Greek believers alike honoured him. In 1929 he went to Greece and settled at the village of Sipsa, now Taxiarhes, near Drama, where, though he suffered in his legs, he did a rich spiritual work among the faithful, drawing them to the Church and guiding them chiefly through confession, on which he laid particular weight. In 1936 he travelled as a pilgrim to the Holy Land, longing to see the places where Christ worked the salvation of the world. Returning to Sipsa, he resolved to build a monastery of the Ascension of the Lord, and in 1939 raised a modest church and a few dwellings.

Most of what is remembered of him comes from his spiritual children of these years. He asked his disciples to confess and to fast before communion, and countless witnesses say that by the gift given him he reminded those who confessed of the sins they had forgotten or were ashamed to speak. He would not commune those with grave sins unrepented, those who did not keep the penance their confessor gave, those dressed indecently, and above all those at enmity with anyone or wronging their neighbour.

He was a great ascetic. He did not talk much, and was very serious and modest. He slept a few hours on a mat spread over boards, and though his clothes were old and patched they were always clean. He never ate meat, and fish very rarely. Though he suffered from anaemia and other bodily weaknesses he received the faithful every day, and every night prayed in the church from midnight until morning, often serving the Liturgy before dawn; several disciples witnessed that his face shone as he served. On Sundays he would sometimes send someone round the houses to call people to leave their work and come to the Liturgy, and he taught them to make the sign of the cross properly and to keep silence in church.

He worked many healings and sought to hide them out of humility. By the gift of foresight he prophesied the beginning of the Second World War and the civil war in Greece, and through both, with prayers and tears, he sheltered the monastery and all the faithful round about, who came to no harm.

In 1959 he foresaw his end and told his close disciples of it more than once. At dawn on 4 November, having prayed before the icon of the Mother of God with the words *Open unto us the door of thy compassion*, he gave up his soul into the hands of Christ. Three days later his body, untouched by corruption, was buried beside his own foundation by Philip, Metropolitan of Drama. Many healings and wonderful appearances followed his passing, and his fragrant relics were found on 9 February 2006 by Paul, Metropolitan of Drama.

The Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople canonised him on 18 March 2008, appointing 4 November, the day of his passing, as his memorial. In 2011 Metropolitan Paul of Drama gave the Romanian Patriarchate a fragment of his relics and a painted icon of him, which is kept in the historic chapel of the Patriarchal Residence in Bucharest; and on 28 October 2015 the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church approved his entry in the church calendar on 4 November.
