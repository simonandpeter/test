# Alina of Dilbeek

Alina, whom doxologia also calls Alena, was born in the seventh century in Belgium, at Dilbeek. Her parents were pagans, Levoldus and Hildegarde, castellans of Dilbeek in Brabant, in the time of the king the page calls Clovis the Second, who reigned between 635 and 637. A hermit spoke to her of the Christian religion, and she was baptised a Christian without her parents' knowledge, and went often to the services without telling them.

One night her father ordered the servants to follow her. After watching her for a good while they saw her going into a chapel at Vorst, and her father, understanding that she was a Christian, ordered his daughter arrested. When the soldiers he sent came to take her she resisted; in the struggle her arm was cut off, and at the last she gave her soul into the hands of the Lord. The wounded arm was taken up by an angel and laid before the altar of the chapel where she used to pray, and her parents, seeing that miracle, were converted to Christianity.

The priest of Forest and some of his parishioners, shocked at finding the arm on the altar and understanding whose it was, tried to learn how it had come there, and so found Alina's body near the Senne. They buried her near the parish church, in the wood dedicated to Saint Denis, and a chapel was built over her grave; many who came to it received healing. On icons, the page says, she is shown with a severed arm, or healing a blind man, or with an angel helping her.
