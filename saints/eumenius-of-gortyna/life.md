# Eumenius

From a young age Eumenius subjected himself to many hardships and exercises, and temperance was what marked him most, for he kept always in mind the apostle’s counsel that everyone who strives is temperate in all things (1 Corinthians 9:25) — even in food and drink, to reach his spiritual goal; and following the God-inspired apostle’s words he succeeded. He was counted worthy to be ordained and became bishop of Gortyna in Crete, and from his new place his virtue shone still more, and God gave him the grace and power to work wonders — once, tradition says, he burned up with lighted candles a dragon that rushed at him.

Then he went to Rome, where by his divine teaching and wonders he strengthened the faithful; but longing for more hardship and ascesis he went to the Thebaid of Upper Egypt, near the great ascetics, and there gave his spirit to God, and his relics were carried and buried with honour at the seat of his bishopric in Crete. The gentle eye of Gortyna, the verse says, looked on the all-gentle eye of the Lord; the Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 18 September, the Romanian with a troparion and kontakion, and the Greek keeps his successor [Basil](/saints/basil-bishop-of-gortyna) two days before.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 18 September old style, in the seventh century, with the kontakion the Greek and Romanian calendars sing. Its life says he gave all he had to the poor, was chosen bishop of Gortyna in Crete, comforted his flock and cared for orphans and the poor, and once brought down plentiful rain in a drought by his prayer. It adds what the Greek synaxarion does not: that he defended the Orthodox faith against the Monothelite heresy of his day, was exiled to the Thebaid for it and died there, and that his body was carried back to Gortyna and buried.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, says he freed himself of two heavy burdens, wealth by giving it to the poor and the body by great fasting; that he was chosen bishop of Gortyna, a father to orphans and a healer of the sick, killed a venomous serpent by prayer, drove out demons and healed many, at home, in Rome and in the Thebaid; and that in the Thebaid he prayed rain out of God in a drought, and ended his life there, in the seventh century.
