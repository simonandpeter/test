# Eumenius

From a young age Eumenius subjected himself to many hardships and exercises, and temperance was what marked him most, for he kept always in mind the apostle’s counsel that everyone who strives is temperate in all things (1 Corinthians 9:25) — even in food and drink, to reach his spiritual goal; and following the God-inspired apostle’s words he succeeded. He was counted worthy to be ordained and became bishop of Gortyna in Crete, and from his new place his virtue shone still more, and God gave him the grace and power to work wonders — once, tradition says, he burned up with lighted candles a dragon that rushed at him. Then he went to Rome, where by his divine teaching and wonders he strengthened the faithful; but longing for more hardship and ascesis he went to the Thebaid of Upper Egypt, near the great ascetics, and there gave his spirit to God, and his relics were carried and buried with honour at the seat of his bishopric in Crete. The gentle eye of Gortyna, the verse says, looked on the all-gentle eye of the Lord; the Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 18 September, the Romanian with a troparion and kontakion, and the Greek keeps his successor [Basil](/saints/basil-bishop-of-gortyna) two days before.
