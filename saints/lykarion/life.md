# Lykarion

The Russian calendar's life for 6 февраля ст. ст. tells one story of three. The virgin martyrs [Martha](/saints/martha-sister-of-lykarion) and [Mary](/saints/mary-sister-of-lykarion) were sisters by blood, lived in Asia — Asia Minor, the life glosses — and burned with the desire to suffer for the Lord Jesus Christ. A pagan commander was passing their house one day, and the sisters came out to him and declared aloud that they were Christians.

He paid them no attention at first, but they went on insistently crying their confession after him; and then both of them were seized together with their brother, the boy Lykarion. All three were crucified, and their mother stood by at the execution and strengthened them in their suffering for Christ. The sisters were run through with spears, and the boy Lykarion was beheaded by the sword. The calendar's line calls him «прмч. Ликариона отрока», and gives no year for any of the three.

The Serbian church keeps the three of them on the Julian 6 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue gives them one sentence between them: holy martyrs Martha and Mary and their brother Lykarion, all three crucified on the cross for Christ, and then pierced and killed with the spear. For the two sisters that is what the Russian life says. For him it is not: there the sisters are run through with spears and the boy Lykarion is beheaded by the sword. Neither reading is preferred here. The Prologue names no place, no commander and no year, it does not call him a boy, and the verses it sets under the day add their old mother, who taught the three of them to love Christ because he had suffered for them.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 6 февраля ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260206.html) and [the life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life404.htm); read 3 October 2026; and the Ohrid Prologue at pravoslavno.rs for 19.02.2026, the Julian 6 February — [the Serbian day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-02-19&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
