# Procopius the Palestinian

saint.gr's calendar for 22 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Προκόπιος ο Παλαιστίνιος». He was a famous ascetic, the page says, and a learned theologian and philosopher besides. He settled at Scythopolis in Palestine and carried out the duties of a reader and a preacher. But he was denounced for drawing crowds of idolaters into the Christian religion, was arrested, and was taken in bonds to Caesarea in Palestine.

There they tried to persuade him that the truth lay in the polytheistic religion. He overturned his opponents' arguments, and among other things quoted them the line of Homer: «Οὐκ ἀγαθὸν πολυκοιρανίη, εἷς κοίρανος ἔστω» — the rule of many is no good thing; let there be one ruler. Insisting so on his true faith, he was condemned to death by the sword.

The couplet set over the notice has him rushing to the cutting as a man rushes to a wrestling bout, Procopius, the nursling of Palestine. The page gives him no year, no emperor, no relics and no hymn, and its own name-gloss, which names the historian Procopius under Justinian as a famous bearer of the name, says nothing about him.
