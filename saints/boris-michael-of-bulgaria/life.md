# Boris-Michael of Bulgaria

Boris, renamed Michael, was king of Bulgaria, and his missionary work had been prophesied by his uncle, saint.gr says. As soon as he came to the throne, succeeding in all probability Presiam, he showed that he knew what he wanted and by what means; but the first years of his reign were marked by unsuccessful campaigns against the Croats and the Serbs, and it was only later, by political action and by war, that he gained some ground to the north-west, taking Ohrid, and to the south-east.

In one of the many battles between the Bulgarians and the Greeks he took prisoner an eminent counsellor, Theodore Kouphara, who had become a monk; and he was the first man to plant the seed of the Gospel in the soul of the Bulgarian tsar. In one of the Greek campaigns the tsar's younger sister was taken captive by Leo the Armenian and brought up in the Orthodox faith at the court of the Byzantine emperor. When Theophilos died, Boris thought to take his chance to avenge earlier defeats, and, under the impression of the Byzantine splendour, set his ambition on taking Byzantium itself. In 853 he thought he could profit from the fact that Theodora governed the throne, and declared war abruptly; and they say she answered him: if you attack my country, I shall meet you hoping for victory, but if I am beaten it will be no great glory for you to have beaten a woman.

The bold answer pleased the Bulgarian monarch and he agreed to negotiate. Famine and plague were besetting the country, and Bulgaria was in terrible difficulties, and Boris saw the salvation of a land lying in the dark of idolatry in the Orthodox faith. So he agreed a treaty of peace; Theodore Kouphara was exchanged for the Bulgarian princess, and she catechised her brother, showing him the wretchedness of the idols and the height of the teaching of the God-man. And so, towards the end of 864 or the beginning of 865, he was baptised by the bishop Joseph and renamed Michael.

Catechists were sent out at once everywhere to enlighten and baptise the people, and at Michael's request the Patriarch [Photius](/saints/photius-of-constantinople) sent many more. Michael asked for archbishops and bishops and for the founding of a patriarchate as well, ambitious that his throne should be clothed in the same political, military and ecclesiastical brilliance as the emperor's at Byzantium. Photius avoided it, and Michael turned to Rome; and Pope Nicholas avoided it too, fearing the new church would be assimilated to the Orthodox and holding that a multiplication of autocephalous churches went against the Vatican's imperial notion of unity. He sent only two bishops, Formosus of Porto and Paul of Populonia, and answered a hundred and six doctrinal and other ecclesiastical questions, to which Photius, when he was asked, also replied.

In 888 Boris-Michael gave up the throne and entered a monastery, leaving his kingdom to his sons Vladimir and Symeon. But Vladimir, who had succeeded him, renounced Christianity and was working with the boyars to root it out; so Boris-Michael came back, seized his son and punished him, and having given the throne to his younger son Symeon returned to the monastery. He fell asleep in peace in the year 907.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same menologion day as the Greek, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells him from the Slavonic side. He was born and brought up a pagan, it says, and was baptised under the influence of his uncle and of his own sister, taking the name Michael at the font. The Patriarch [Photius](/saints/photius-of-constantinople) sent him priests, who baptised the whole Bulgarian people by degrees. Many of the magnates in Bulgaria set themselves against the new faith, but the new faith won, and the cross shone out on the many churches the devout tsar Michael raised.

The faith among the Bulgarians, and among the Serbs too, was established above all by the Five of Ohrid, the disciples of the two holy brothers, who preached the teaching of Christ to the people in their own Slavonic tongue. Michael took the monastic habit in his old age and withdrew to a monastery. But when his son Vladimir began to spoil his father's work and to put down Christianity, Michael put on a commander's clothes again, girded a sword, took Vladimir off the throne, and set up his younger son Symeon in his place; then he put on the monastic robe once more, withdrew into silence, and in asceticism and prayer ended his earthly life in peace.

He died, the book says, „у благој вери, у правом исповедању Господа нашега Исуса Христа, велик, чесан и благоверан“ — in the good faith, in the right confession of our Lord Jesus Christ, great, honourable and right-believing — and passed over into the heavenly life **on the second of May in the year 906**, where the Greek page gives 907 and no day. Neither year was chosen: his death now reads 906 or 907. This book calls him tsar where the Greek calendar's reading made him a prince, and that disagreement about his rank was recorded and not settled.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day and gives him two lives, the second almost word for word the first. They begin by saying that his apostolic labour had been foretold him by his uncle, «святой Боян» — the Bulgarian prince the Greek calendar keeps on 28 March. The first years of his reign went badly: the Bulgarians had often to fight their neighbours, famine and plague visited the country, and by 860 Bulgaria was in a wretched state. He saw the saving of a land still in paganism in enlightening it with the faith of Christ.

Two captives began the work. In one of the wars with the Greeks a notable courtier, «Феодор Куфара», who had already been tonsured a monk, was taken prisoner, and he was the first man to sow the seed of the Gospel in the Bulgarian king's soul; and in one of the campaigns the Greeks took his young sister captive and brought her up at the Byzantine court in the Orthodox faith. When the emperor «Феофил» died he thought to use the chance to avenge his earlier defeats, but the emperor's widow «Феодора» sent a messenger to say that she was ready to defend the empire herself and would put her adversary to shame. He chose a peaceful alliance instead, and in token of the reconciliation the courtier and the Bulgarian princess were exchanged; and his sister inclined him further to the Christian faith.

Somewhat later «святитель Мефодий», who with his brother «Кирилл» enlightened the Slavs with the light of the faith of Christ, was sent to Bulgaria, and it was he who baptised the king, his household and many of the boyars. The pagan Bulgarians, learning of it, wanted to kill him, but the newly baptised king broke their conspiracy, and the Bulgarian people, left without their rebellious leaders, accepted baptism of their own will. A peace founded on one faith was concluded between Byzantium and Bulgaria and was not broken to the end of his reign, and the Greek patriarch [Photius of Constantinople](/saints/photius-of-constantinople) took a large part in the spiritual settling of the Bulgarian people.

In 867 preachers were sent to Bulgaria from the pope of Rome, and from then for three years there was strife in Bulgaria between the Greek and Roman Churches; the council held at Constantinople in 869 made an end of the quarrel, and on 3 March 870 Bulgaria joined the Eastern Church finally. He adorned the country with churches and furthered the spread of piety, and a patriarchal see was afterwards established in Bulgaria.

In his later years he withdrew to a monastery, leaving the throne to his sons «Владимир» and «Симеон». There he learnt that Владимир, who reigned after him, had set out on the way of apostasy from Christianity; grieving over it he put on the royal robes again, punished the disobedient son, blinded him and shut him in prison, committed the government to the younger son Симеон and went back to the monastery. Only once did he leave it, to beat off an invasion of the Hungarians. Both lives close: «Преставился святой благоверный царь Борис, в святом крещении Михаил, 2 мая 907 года».
