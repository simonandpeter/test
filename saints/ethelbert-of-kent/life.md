# Ethelbert of Kent

Ethelbert was an Anglo-Saxon king of Kent with his seat at Canterbury — so the body of saint.gr's page, where the day's own line calls him more broadly king of England. His wife Bertha was a Christian who came of France. In the days of his reign the Christian missionaries arrived from Rome under the monk Augustine; the king received them and gave them every help in carrying out the work of spreading the divine word, and in the end he was baptised a Christian himself.

He founded the church of the Apostle Andrew at Rochester, the church of the Apostle Paul in London, and the famous monastery of the Apostles Peter and Paul at Canterbury. He also did the most, the page says, towards the christianisation of the East Saxons, whose king was Sæberht. He fell asleep in peace in the year 616.

The page gives no year for his birth, no relic and no hymn; the churches it names are his foundations and not associations with the apostles they are named for.
