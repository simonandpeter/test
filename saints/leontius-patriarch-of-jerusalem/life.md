# Leontius of Jerusalem

Leontius came from Tiberioupolis in Phrygia, born of rich and devout parents — saint.gr says in the second half of the twelfth century, which is its own reckoning and sits oddly beside the death it gives him. When his father died he left his birthplace, studied under a reverent priest, and entered the monastery of Ptelidion, where he became a monk. Later he went to Constantinople and attached himself to the metropolitan of Tiberias, under whose obedience he put himself; following his elder back to his diocese, he sailed with him to Patmos to venerate at the monastery of Saint John the Theologian.

He set out from there for Cyprus, but God's counsel brought him back to Patmos again. There, under the spiritual direction of the abbot Theoktistos, a man experienced in the things of the spirit, he became a pattern of brotherly love and humility, and when Theoktistos died the monks chose him as his successor by unanimous vote. For the material needs of the house he used to visit Crete as well, having as his base there the small monastery of the Apostle and Evangelist John, inside the ancient city of Aptera above the Turkish fort of Isdezin, then a dependency of the Patmos house.

On the monastery's business he went to Constantinople, where he drew the respect and regard of the emperor Manuel Komnenos, who offered to make him a bishop in Russia or in Cyprus; he refused out of humility. But when the throne of Jerusalem was offered him he could not refuse, and he became Patriarch of Jerusalem in 1170, at a time when the Church of Sion was under the trial of Latin rule. For the rebuke he administered to the new emperor Andronikos Komnenos he was exiled, and he died in deep old age on 14 May 1190. His memory is kept at Patmos and at Jerusalem on that day, and a sung service of his was published at Jerusalem in 1912.
