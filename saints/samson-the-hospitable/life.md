# Samson the Hospitable

Samson, whose fame went everywhere, was of the city of Rome, born of honourable and wealthy parents of great family, of imperial blood. He learned the outward learning well and took up the physician's art besides, not out of need and not to enrich himself, for he had wealth enough, but partly so as not to spend his time in vanity and partly so that by his craft he could serve God and the poor. He healed those held by incurable diseases, for beside the craft there had been added to him the Lord's gift, which gives healings.

He had also the understanding of Scripture, and spent much time in the holy books, and was woken by faith and hope to the love of Christ. When his parents died and left him much property, he did not delay: he began at once to exchange the wealth that perishes quickly for the wealth that does not, giving alms with both hands to every need of the poor and the hungry, and making of their perishable stomachs an imperishable treasury. Then, wanting a humbler life still, he freed the whole crowd of his slaves, keeping one only for his own service, and was content with one garment and a rope about his waist.

Wishing rather to enrich his soul, he left Rome and went out into the desert, as Elias did of old; but God, willing that His servant should be of use to many, brought him from the desert to the new Rome, that is to Constantinople. There he found himself a house and lived in it, resting strangers and the poor and serving them in every way, taking in the sick and healing them. He did not only cure their diseases by his craft: he saw to their food and their bedding with such zeal that the mercy seemed natural to him, as it is the nature of the sun to give light and of fire to burn.

In that time the emperor fell into a sickness no physician could touch. The learned men of the Greek and Roman dominions were searched out and none could help him; and the emperor drove them all from his sight and turned to God with tears. Sleeping a little, he saw in a vision a multitude of physicians standing in order in shining garments, and a young man bearing light who showed him each of them, who he was and in what rank and in what years he had lived, and pointed out one humble of face, grey-haired, in a priestly garment, and said: This one, and no other, can heal thee of the pain that brings death.

The emperor woke glad, called all the physicians together to look for the man, and could not find him; then had the likeness cried through the city with a promise of gifts. One of the imperial servants, who knew the emperor and was Samson's friend, remembered him at the description, and Samson was brought with honour. Knowing him at once for the man of the dream, the emperor rose, ran to him, embraced him and kissed not his mouth only but his head and his hands, and asked healing of him.

Samson was grieved at such humility in an emperor, and told him not to abase himself so, lest he lead him into pride and be the cause of his condemnation: in what did he surpass other men, who being poor and sinful had need himself of Christ's merciful visitation for his own sins? But the emperor's great faith and burning hope, he said, would bend Christ the King to mercy. Then he touched with his hand the place where the emperor suffered, as though laying on some medicine, wishing to hide the gift of healing given him from heaven; and at that touch alone the sickness lifted and he was quickly whole.

The emperor offered him much gold and silver, and Samson answered that he had had gold and silver and much else and had left it all for Christ, to gain the everlasting good things of heaven; but that if he wished to show his kindness, he should build beside his dwelling, for God's sake and for his own salvation, a house where he might take in the sick and the strangers he was used to serving. The emperor took the request for a gift, had a hostel and an infirmary built as the saint asked, and endowed it richly for the strangers lodged there and the sick who were healed there.

Samson lived many years in that service of hospitality and medicine, and coming to a great age grew weak and was a little ill. When his soul parted from his body his face was bright, and he was in no way grieved at death as other souls are that are held by cares and by many sins, for he knew who was calling him, and from what labours, and to what rest. His body was buried with honour in the church of the holy martyr Mocius, from whose kindred he came by his natural birth.

He was glorified with great wonders after death as in life. A fire that began at the church of Holy Wisdom burned so that no man could quench it, and the finest palaces and buildings of the city were consumed; but when the flame reached Samson's hostels it surrounded them and could not touch them, and they were seen like the bush that was not burned. Many people saw Samson himself above the building, walking quickly round the enclosure and rebuking the flame in anger; and the fire drew back as though ashamed of his face, and then thunder was heard and a great rain came down and quenched it all.

He healed every sickness after his death as before it. A man of note named Theodoritus, a spatharius, hurt his leg at the joint running down his stairs and lay three days unable to eat or drink or sleep for the pain, and dumb besides, praying in his mind to God and to Samson; and a voice told him that his feast was that day and that he should pray at Samson's tomb. Feeling strength, he rose from his bed, undid the physicians' bandages and plasters and found the hurt perfectly healed, put on his festal clothes and went to the church, and all who saw him whole glorified God.

It was the custom of many, when anyone fell ill, to have him carried to Samson's infirmary, and there, according to his faith, he received health. A servant named George, of a priest called Ephedimus, suffered badly from dropsy; sent to the infirmary and finding no relief, he went home, and his master in anger at his unbelief sent him back to ask for the myrrh that flowed from the wonderworking tomb and anoint himself with it. He stayed the night and came back whole, saying he had seen Samson in a dream take him by the belly and tell him to go, for he was well now. A woman of the church's servant, named Irene, suffering the same sickness, saw in a dream Samson coming to her with the unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian, and at their touch was made whole. The life names no emperor by name in what it tells of the healing, and prints no year, so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 27 June, and names in the telling the two men the Romanian life leaves nameless. saint.gr says that after he had shared out the great fortune he inherited he went to Constantinople and visited all its monasteries, finding in them a refuge in which to be quiet and study the Scriptures; that his fame drew the favour of the emperor Justinian himself; and that his theological learning and his other virtues moved the patriarch of Constantinople, Menas, who ordained him presbyter. When Justinian was struck by a grave illness and asked his help, Samson prayed earnestly and saved the emperor's life; and the emperor, wishing to thank him, built the hospital that soon grew into a great house of charity, where the destitute and the weak took refuge to be healed and to find comfort and support. Having finished a work so great and so pleasing to God, the page says, he fell asleep in peace in deep old age. Portions of his relic are at the Great Lavra of the Holy Mountain and at the monastery of Kremasti in Elis.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 27 June, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep him on, and gives him the fullest entry of its day. He was born of rich and notable parents in old Rome, it says, learned all the worldly knowledge of his time there and gave himself particularly to the science of medicine; and he was a merciful and unmercenary physician, giving the sick medicines for the body and for the soul and counselling everyone to keep the rules of the Christian faith. Afterwards he moved to Constantinople, where he lived in one small house out of which he scattered on every side, as the sun scatters rays of light, alms, comfort, counsel, hope, medicine and help of every kind to the helpless.

The patriarch heard of his high virtues and ordained him priest. In those days the emperor fell ill of a sickness all the physicians declared incurable; he prayed to God with great earnestness, and God revealed to him in a dream that Samson would heal him. And so it was: the emperor sent for him, and no sooner had the old man laid his hand on the sore place than he recovered. When great treasure was offered him for it Samson gave thanks and would take nothing, saying that he had had gold and silver and goods of his own and had left them all for the sake of Christ, to gain the eternal goods of heaven.

When the emperor pressed to do something for him he asked instead that a house be built for the poor; and in that house he served the poor as a parent serves his own children, mercy towards the poor and the weak being in him like a thing of nature. At last this holy man, filled with power and heavenly goodness, reposed peacefully on the twenty seventh of June in the year 530, and was buried in the church of the holy martyr [Mocius](/saints/mocius-of-amphipolis), whom this book calls his kinsman. After his repose he appeared more than once to those who called on him for help. The year is the only one any of the three books gives him, and the day it gives is the day his commemoration stands on.
