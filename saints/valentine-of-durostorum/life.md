# Valentine of Durostorum

Valentine and [Passicrates](/saints/passicrates-of-durostorum) were Christian soldiers serving under the governor of their place, a man the life calls Avsolan; doxologia's one account covers them both, and its opening sentence sets them at Rodostol, a city of Moesia, while the calendar line for the day names them of Durostorum. Seeing the people caught in the deception of the idols, and seeing Christians who feared the torments run away and hide themselves, the two confessed openly and with boldness that they were Christians, glorified the one true God and cursed the lifeless idols.

The idolaters seized them and brought them before the judge, who pressed them to offer incense. There was an idol of Apollo standing there; Passicrates ran up to it, spat in its face and said that such was the honour the god deserved. For that he was loaded with heavy iron chains and thrown into prison, and he wore the chains as if they were the gold ornaments of an emperor. Valentine was brought in with him, and both were set again before the governor.

Passicrates' brother Papian came to the place. He was a Christian, but fear of the torments had made him sacrifice to the idols, and he begged his brother in tears to do as he had done and pretend for a while. Passicrates drove him off and called him unworthy of his family; then he ran to the shrine, put his hand into the fire, and told the governor that this mortal body burns, as he could see, while the soul, being immortal, takes no notice of visible torments. Valentine, questioned in turn, said the same, and the governor condemned them both to the sword.

As the executioners led them out of the city, Passicrates' mother walked behind them, urging her son not to be afraid, for she feared he would lose heart, being young; and so their heads were struck off. Passicrates was twenty-two years old and Valentine thirty. The mother took up their bodies with joy and gladness and buried them with honour. The page gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them on the same day and gives them ages and a year. Passicrates and [Passicrates](/saints/passicrates-of-durostorum) came from Durostorum in Lower Moesia — a region of the Balkan peninsula, the page explains — and were Christian soldiers at the time when the prefect was Aulozanes. Because they could not bear the worship of the idols by the heathen, they made a very sharp remonstrance to those who believed in them and confessed Christ true God; and they were arrested and brought before the governor, who urged them to offer sacrifice to Apollo.

Passicrates refused, and they bound him with chains and threw him into prison. There his own brother came to him and advised him to offer at least a little incense, so as to save himself; but Passicrates drove him away, and accepted with joy the governor's sentence that he be beheaded. Valention, when he was asked whether he agreed with what Passicrates believed, answered that he too held to his Christian faith and would never sacrifice to lifeless idols. Then both were beheaded — Passicrates at twenty-two years of age and Valention at thirty — in the year 297.

The Serbian church keeps the pair on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović names them both in its heading — „Св. муч. Пасикрат и Валентин.“ — and tells the scene at the trial. When the judge was urging [Passicrates](/saints/passicrates-of-durostorum) to offer sacrifice to the idols, and urging the martyr's brother Papian to the same — who had fallen away from Christ out of fear of the torments — then Passicrates put his hand into the fire and cried out: „тело је смртно и огњем сагорева, но душа је бесмртна и не осећа видљиве муке!“ — the body is mortal and burns in fire, but the soul is immortal and does not feel visible torments. **His mother supported and encouraged him greatly**, to hold out to the end. He was beheaded with [Valentine](/saints/valentine-of-durostorum), and both passed over into the kingdom of Christ.

**What the entry says of Valentine is that he was beheaded with Passicrates and passed over with him, and nothing else at all**: no trade, no age, no city, no word at the trial. The scene it tells belongs to the other man; this folder gains from it only the company and the manner of death.

**The year is where this book and saint.gr part, and neither folder's dates were touched.** The entry closes „око 228. год.“; saint.gr gives 297 and gives it with both their ages — «σε ηλικία, ο μεν Πασικράτης 22 ετών, ο δε Βαλεντίων 30, το έτος 297 μ.Χ.» — which is where the birth years 275 and 267 these two folders carry come from. A death in 228 cannot stand beside a birth in 275, so **widening the interval to hold both readings would make each folder say its man died before he was born**; and narrowing or displacing a sourced reading is not a reader's act either. So the dates stand as they were, the disagreement is recorded here and in the batch's commit message, and the choice between them is the author's.

The same book keeps him a second time, on the Julian 25 May, and says of him there exactly what it says on the 24 April: that Pasicrates and Valention were beheaded together. What the second entry adds is the company and the setting — Roman soldiers who suffered for Christ at Durostorum, which it places in Macedonia where the other two calendars place it in Moesia, and with them [Julius](/saints/julius-the-veteran) the veteran, [Nicander](/saints/nicander-of-durostorum) and [Marcian](/saints/marcian-of-durostorum).

The 25 May entry spells his name Валентион where the 24 April entry spells it Валентин, and puts the company’s suffering at about the year 302 where the earlier entry puts the beheading at about 228. This folder keeps the 297 the Romanian and Greek calendars agree on, and the two guesses of the one book are recorded rather than averaged.

The Russian calendar sets him beside his companion in one short life and gives him an age. The two came of the Moesian city of Durostorum and were soldiers under the governor Avsolan; he was thirty years old and the other twenty-two. When the persecution began both confessed the faith of Christ openly. Of his companion the page tells the spitting on the idol of Apollo, the weeping brother who urged a sacrifice for appearance’s sake, the hand laid on the altar in the fire and the mother who walked to the execution exhorting her son; of him it records one thing only — «Мученик Валентин также заявил о своей готовности пострадать за Христа», that he too declared his readiness to suffer for Christ. Both were beheaded with the sword.

The calendar prints two years for that death and they are sixty apart: the day’s own line heads the pair «(228)» and the life ends «(+ 288)». This folder’s interval comes from the Greek calendar and agrees with neither. All three readings are left standing and nothing in `dates` was altered.
