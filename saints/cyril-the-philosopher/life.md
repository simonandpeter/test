# Cyril the Philosopher

Constantine, who was called Cyril only at the end of his life when he took the holy schema, was born at Thessalonica to Leo and Maria, the younger brother of [Methodius](/saints/methodius-of-moravia). From the cradle he showed something wonderful in himself: given to a nurse, he would take no milk but his mother's. At seven he dreamt that a voivode had gathered all the maidens of the city and bade him choose one to be his helper all the days of his life; he chose the one most beautifully adorned, whose name was Sophia. His parents understood that the maiden was the Wisdom of God.

He loved [Gregory the Theologian](/saints/gregory-the-theologian) above all other writers, learning many of his words and interpretations by heart; under a cross he had drawn on a wall he wrote a prayer that Gregory would be to him father, teacher and enlightener. The logothete Dromi, who knew his parents, had him brought to Constantinople to study beside the young emperor Michael. There he quickly mastered grammar, the verse of Homer, rhetoric, philosophy, arithmetic, astronomy and music, and Latin and Syriac besides; and he was called the Philosopher, because he had gone further in wisdom than the rest.

Fleeing the world, he went in secret to a monastery at the mouth of the Bosphorus and took the monastic habit; they found him only after six months, brought him back by force, and made him take the priesthood in the days of the patriarch Methodius, when the war on the icons had ceased. He was set as librarian at the cathedral church of Holy Wisdom and teacher of philosophy. When the Saracens of Syria sent Constantinople a taunt against the Holy Trinity, the emperor sent him to answer it at Samarra by the Euphrates. He argued the Trinity from the disc, the ray and the warmth of the one sun, and from the three men who stood by Abraham at the oak of Mamre; he was given poison in secret, and the Lord kept His servant unharmed.

With his brother he went next to the Khazars, disputing daily before the Kagan with Jews and with Saracens. To the Khazar who boasted that his people carried all wisdom swallowed within them and needed no books, he answered with the man who claims to own gold and clothing and walks about naked. The Kagan, his nobles and a part of the people were baptised. At Cherson on the way the two brothers recovered the relics of Clement of Rome; and going home through a waterless place he drew sweet, cold water out of a bitter salt lake for the two hundred Greek captives they had asked for in place of the Kagan's gifts.

When Moravia asked the emperor for a teacher, Constantine fasted forty days, found the Slavonic alphabet of thirty-eight letters, and began the translation with the Gospel of John: in the beginning was the Word. For four years and more he and Methodius taught in the Slavonic lands, translated the books needed for the services, and answered the men who held that the Liturgy might be served only in Hebrew, Greek and Latin. At Rome, where Pope Adrian approved and confirmed their work, he fell ill and was shown his end in a vision, and spent that whole day singing, I was glad when they said unto me, we will go into the house of the Lord. The next day he took the holy schema and was named Cyril. After fifty days on his sickbed he entrusted his see to his elder brother and gave up his spirit, and was buried with honour in the church of St Clement, where the portion of Clement's relics that he had carried was also laid.
