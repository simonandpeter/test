# Laurence (Knyazev)

He was born in 1877 at Kashira, of the clerical estate, and was called Eugene Ivanovich Knyazev in the world. He finished the seminary of Tula and went on to the academy of St Petersburg, and when he had finished there in 1902 he was tonsured a monk at Valaam. **The page's account of his monastic name contradicts itself twice over and is given here as it stands**: it says he was tonsured with the name Alexis, calls him father Alexander in the sentence about 1912, and from his consecration onward calls him Laurence, which is the name the calendar keeps him under.

In 1912 he was made rector of the seminary of Lithuania and superior of the Holy Trinity monastery at Vilna. There he came to know the archbishop who became patriarch, and who in 1917 presented him for consecration; in the February of that year the archimandrite Laurence was consecrated bishop of Balakhna, vicar of the diocese of Nizhny Novgorod. He was a diligent practiser of the Jesus Prayer, and a disciple and friend of the elders of Optina; shortly before his martyrdom one of those elders bowed to the ground three times before him.

At Nizhny Novgorod he blessed the founding of a Transfiguration brotherhood for the reviving of church and public life, and took part in the sittings of a religious and philosophical society formed at the same time. He lived in the Pechersky monastery, served often and preached. He was left alone to govern the whole diocese of Nizhny Novgorod, because the ruling archbishop, Joachim (Levitsky), had received the martyr's crown — hanged in the Crimea by brigands.

When the arrests of the clergy of the diocese began he visited them in prison, and at the end of August 1918 the Cheka arrested him too. In the cell he prayed without ceasing and paid no attention to the mockery of the other prisoners, and before long they, seeing the strength of his spirit, began to try to imitate him. A hierarch who was sent the bishop's worn-out prayer rope from the prison, with his request that it be replaced, said of it: «Трудовые чётки» — a working man's beads. He was allowed to serve in the prison church on the feasts, and that was his consolation and his support.

The authorities refused to let him out even when the faithful raised a bail of sixteen thousand roubles and petitioned for their archpastor. After he had put his signature in the summer of 1918 to the believers' protest against the confiscation of the church valuables, he was moved to the prison of the Cheka. In October they offered him his freedom in exchange for giving up his orders. It goes without saying, the page says, that such a renunciation was unthinkable; and hearing his answer they announced the final sentence, which was shooting.

With the Holy Gifts he had with him he communicated himself and [Alexis](/saints/alexis-porfiryev) the priest, to whom the same sentence had been announced. On 24 October, which is 6 November in the new style, 1918, they were led out into a garden where the grave had already been dug. The bishop stood facing his executioners and prayed with his hands raised. The Russian soldiers refused to shoot him, and he was shot by Latvians. The bodies of the bishop and the priest were taken to the Silent Island and thrown into the Volga. The third man the calendar's line keeps with them, [Alexis](/saints/alexis-neidgardt) the layman, is not named in this life at all.

He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for veneration throughout the Church at the jubilee council of bishops in August 2000.
