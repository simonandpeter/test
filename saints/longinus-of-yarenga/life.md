# Longinus of Yarenga

Longinus and his brother monk John lived in the sixteenth century, and the Russian calendar tells them in one life. They were monks of the Saviour and Transfiguration monastery of Solovki at the time when Philip, later metropolitan of Moscow, was its abbot; under Philip's direction they kept a strict fast and prayer, did his will in everything, and grew by degrees in the spiritual life. The calendar's 16 October names Longinus alone, and nothing is added here about John beyond what the shared life says of the two of them together.

In 1561, the life says, the two were sent on monastery business to the land of Tver. On the way back a storm caught them on the White Sea, the boat was broken, and both were drowned. Some while afterwards their bodies were found incorrupt a hundred and twenty versts from the monastery, at the mouth of the river Sosnovka, and were laid in the chapel of Saint Nicholas the Wonderworker in the village of Yarenga, which is the place that gives him his name.

Wonders soon confirmed their holiness, the page says, and the sick who came to their honoured relics received healing; in time a monastery grew up where the two monks were buried. In 1625 a monk of the Yarenga house named Elijah Telov reported the wonders at their tomb to Patriarch Philaret; the testimonies of healing were carefully examined and confirmed. On 2 July 1638 the relics were moved into a newly built church of the monastery, and in honour of that event the commemoration of John and Longinus of Yarenga was set on 3 July.

The calendar gives two different dates for his death and does not reconcile them. The day line for 16 October prints «(1544-1545)»; the life it links says the journey was made in 1561 and the drowning happened on the return. So the death stands here as an interval from 1544 to 1561, and neither reading is preferred. His own name page keeps him on four days — the Novgorod synaxis on 8 June, the translation on 3 July, the Solovki synaxis on 9 August, and 16 October — and only this one was read.

The Greek, Romanian and Serbian calendars were read for the same menologion day and none of the three keeps him. He is not the Longinus of Koryazhemka whom this same calendar keeps on 10 February, a hermit who died in 1540; the two are different men, and this is the Solovki monk who drowned in the White Sea. The page closes with its own three books, named below.
