# Constantine (Golubev)

Constantine Alexeyevich Golubev was born in 1852 in the village of Baranovka in the Volsk district of Saratov province, the son of a psalm-reader. His father died when he was nine; he went through the Saratov seminary in the first class, and at twenty-four joined the Saratov brotherhood of the Holy Cross and went as its missionary to Baranovka, a village strongly under the Old Believers’ influence, where the first thing he did was found a parish school. With the blessing of Bishop Tikhon of Saratov he became in 1876 teacher of Russian and Slavonic at the Volsk theological school and the brotherhood’s missionary there, and three years later the diocese’s missionary against schism and the sects. His work caught the notice of Metropolitan Sergius (Lyapidevsky) of Moscow, who on 4 March 1895 gave him the archpriest’s place at Bogorodsk, and Bishop Tikhon (Nikanorov) of Mozhaisk ordained him priest on 12 March. At Bogorodsk he won the goodwill of Old Believers as well as the Orthodox, served on the prisons committee, chaired the local branch of the Cyril and Methodius brotherhood, sat on the board of the girls’ progymnasium, opened a parish school for girls at the Epiphany cathedral in 1900, and joined the temperance committee in 1901. He and his wife had seven children, brought up strictly in the faith, and his family remembered him as spiritually close to the hieromartyr [John Vostorgov](/saints/john-vostorgov).

After the revolution of 1917 he was soon arrested at Bogorodsk, held a few days in the town prison, and condemned to death without trial; told of the sentence, he sent his pectoral cross and service book out of the prison. A report by V. P. Shein, a member of the Council of 1917–1918, says the executioners only wounded him, threw him alive into a pit and began to fill it in; he lifted his head and begged them to finish him, and his daughter on her knees begged them not to bury her father alive, but he was buried alive. Witnesses remembered that a woman who fearlessly defended him and a soldier who refused to carry out the sentence were shot with him and thrown into the same ditch. The place, at the edge of a pine wood beyond the town stadium, was kept by the faithful for decades, however often the mound was levelled, and at the end of 1995 his incorrupt relics were uncovered there with the remains of the two killed with him. He was glorified in the Moscow diocese in 1996 and for the whole Russian Church at the Jubilee Council of August 2000.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 September old style, on 26 January with the new martyrs, and on 7 November for the finding of his relics.
