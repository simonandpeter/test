# Dionysius of Suzdal

saint.gr's calendar for 26 Ιουνίου names him and gives him no life on this day. Its line reads «Άγιος Διονύσιος Αρχιεπίσκοπος Σουζδαλίας», the page behind that line is headed «Αγιος Διονυσιος Αρχιεπισκοπος Σουζδαλιας (; - 1385)», and the whole of its text is a pointer to another day: «Για τον Άγιο Διονύσιο βλέπε στις 15 Οκτωβρίου» — for Saint Dionysius see at 15 October.

So the Greek calendar keeps him twice over, on 26 June and on 15 October, and holds whatever life it has on the later of the two days. What is recorded here is the day page that was read, which is this one; the pointer is set down in the attestation's note and the October page has not been opened. The year of death, 1385, is the only other thing the page prints, with the year of birth left as a question mark, and it glosses the name itself as meaning one of divine origin. There is no hymn, no see beyond its name, and no narrative of any kind.

The second of saint.gr's two days for him, 15 Οκτωβρίου, has since been read, and it is the one that holds the life. The line there is «Άγιος Διονύσιος Αρχιεπίσκοπος Σουζδαλίας», the page behind it is headed «Αγιος Διονυσιος Αρχιεπισκοπος Σουζδαλιας (; - 1385)» as the June one is, and its whole text is a single sentence: that Saint Dionysius was a monk in the monastery of the Caves of Nizhny Novgorod and afterwards archbishop of Suzdal, until his death on 15 October 1385. That is three things the June page does not have — the monastery, the order of his life, and the day of his death — and the day of his death is the second of the two days the calendar keeps him on. The attestation row still records the June page, because that is the day this folder was written from and one row is what the schema holds; the October page is cited below beside it.

The Russian calendar prints a life where the Greek page printed none, and it keeps him on 26 июня ст. ст. for a reason it states itself: he was called David in the world, and 26 June is the day of the venerable «Давид Солунский», his namesake, whose line stands beside his on the same page. Both of the readings the calendar gives put his death on 15 October 1385, which is the year this folder already recorded.

He was born in the Kiev country at the beginning of the fourteenth century and took the habit young in the Kiev Caves monastery. With his abbot's blessing he went north, dug himself a cave on the Volga below Nizhny Novgorod and lived there alone; when seekers of silence gathered to him, about 1335, he founded a monastery of the Ascension of the Lord. His contemporaries, the calendar says, saw in him «мужа кроткого, рассудительного, знающего Священное Писание, учительного, славного постничеством и исполненного любви ко всем» — a man meek and judicious, knowing the Scriptures, apt to teach, famous for his fasting and full of love towards all.

Two of his disciples are kept by this corpus: [Euthymius of Suzdal](/saints/euthymius-of-suzdal) and [Macarius of Zheltovodsk](/saints/macarius-of-zheltovodsk), both of whom the calendar names as brought up under his guidance. In 1352 he sent twelve of his brethren into «верхние грады и страны, идеже Бог кого благословит», the upper towns and countries wherever God should bless a man, to teach the people and found new houses; and in 1371 he gave the habit to the widow of prince Andrew Konstantinovich, after whose example many noblewomen, widows and maidens took it too. The famous Laurentian Chronicle was compiled in 1337 with his blessing and perhaps under his editing.

He was ordained bishop of Suzdal in 1374 and afterwards raised to archbishop, and he fought the heresy of the strigolniki in Pskov and Novgorod. In 1379 he alone of all the bishops assembled at Moscow by the prince's order spoke against the election of the prince's candidate to the metropolitan see, and he went to Constantinople that same year to protest it; the patriarch called him «воистину Божиим и духовным человеком», truly a man of God and of the spirit, and he sent back from the city two copies of the Hodegetria icon for the cathedrals of Suzdal and Nizhny Novgorod. He received the title of archbishop from the patriarch in 1382, went a second time in 1383, and in 1384 was made metropolitan of Russia; but on his way home he was seized at Kiev by the order of its prince and died in prison there on 15 October 1385, and was buried in the caves of the Kiev monastery. A synodikon of 1552 calls him a venerable wonderworker.
