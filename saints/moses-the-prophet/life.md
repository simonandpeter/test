# Moses the Prophet and God-seer

Moses, the great leader and lawgiver of the people of Israel, was born in Egypt — the Greek synaxarion reckons 1569 BC, the Prologue about 1550 — to a Hebrew father, Amram of the tribe of Levi, and his mother Jochebed. When Pharaoh ordered the Hebrew infants killed his mother put him in a basket and left it on the bank of the Nile in God’s providence; Pharaoh’s daughter found the child, adopted him and named him Moses, saved from the waters, and he was raised as the princess’s own son. The Prologue divides his hundred and twenty years into three forties: forty in Egypt at Pharaoh’s court, forty as a shepherd in the contemplation of God and the world, and forty leading the people through the desert to the Promised Land, which he saw and did not enter, having once sinned against God (Numbers 20:12). A wonderworker, he was a type of Christ, in the words of Basil the Great; he appeared from the other world on Tabor at the Lord’s Transfiguration, and, John Climacus witnesses, to the monks of Sinai. The Romanian life tells how as a child on Pharaoh’s knee he threw the royal crown with its little idol to the ground and trod on it; the Sretensky calendar says only that his life is told in the Bible, from Exodus to the end of Deuteronomy, and the Russian calendar keeps him with a troparion and a kontakion.
