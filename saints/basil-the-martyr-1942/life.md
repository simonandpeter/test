# Basil, Martyr (1942)

Basil Afanasyevich Aleksandrin was born on 8 February 1872 in the village of Vertelim in the Insar district of Penza province, now in Mordovia — a man thorough in everything, the life says: before the revolution he farmed ten hectares of his own and rented seventy or eighty more, kept horses, cows and forty sheep, with two permanent hands and fifteen seasonal workers, and had a wife and three children. His fellow villagers respected him, and in 1918 the parish meeting elected him psalmist of the village church, which the Penza diocesan administration confirmed that August; the priest’s report of 1929 calls his conduct excellent, his reading and singing superb, his life outside the services that of an Orthodox Christian — which may be why that year the village soviet not only stripped him of his property and his vote but voted to expel him from the village. He left of his own accord, came back in 1932 with his rights restored, and the faithful gathered round him again.

In 1934 the authorities decided to turn the church into a smithy for the tractor station; at the end of July the women of the parish declared in the soviet that they would not give it up, and Basil stood firmly with them. The church was left to the faithful, but the psalmist, as chief offender, was arrested and held for a time. Freed, he went back to his duties, and was asked to help with the services at Shagayevo too; such firmness could not go unpunished, and on 17 October 1937 he was arrested again for counter-revolutionary activity and anti-Soviet agitation — sixteen religious books and a copper cross were confiscated at the search. He held himself bravely under interrogation, hid nothing of his beliefs and blackened no one, and in December 1937 was sentenced to ten years in the camps; from February 1938 he served them at the Unzha camp at Sukhobezvodnoye in the Gorky region, where he died on 26 August 1942, unable to bear the inhuman conditions. He was glorified at the Council of 2000. The Sretensky calendar prints no life for him, and this one follows azbyka.ru.
