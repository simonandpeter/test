# Philemon the Flute-Player

doxologia.ro prints one life for the martyrs of 14 decembrie, and its second half belongs to Philemon and his companions in Egypt. Long after the martyrs of Bithynia, under the emperor Diocletian, the order went out again through all the provinces that everyone should share in the worship of the heathen and that whoever would not should be put to death. [Arianus](/saints/arianus-of-the-thebaid), governor in the parts of the Thebaid, set about carrying it out; at the city of the Antinoites he had already tortured and killed two leading Christians, Asclas and Leonidas.

He then had every Christian in the place arrested, set the instruments of torture before them, and offered two roads: sacrifice to the idols and go free and whole, or be punished with torments and with death. Thirty-seven men came out into the middle of the assembly rather than obey. One of them, [Apollonius](/saints/apollonius-the-reader), a reader of the Church, faltered when he saw the many and various torments, and looked for a way to save his soul out of the devil's hands and his body out of the tormentors'.

Standing near him was a man of the Greek faith named Philemon, a comedian by trade, who used to entertain the governor at his festivities. Apollonius called him over and promised him four gold pieces if he would sacrifice to the idols in his place, covered in his cloak so as not to be known. Philemon agreed at once, put on Apollonius's cloak and hid his face; and the life says that God, who orders salvation for all by wonders, meant to draw Philemon to himself through Apollonius, and Apollonius through Philemon.

As Philemon came near the altar in the other man's cloak, the divine light shone in his heart and opened the eyes of his understanding to know the truth; he signed himself with the Cross and stood before the governor as a Christian. Told to sacrifice, he cried out with a loud voice that he would not, being a Christian and a servant of Christ the living God. Asked whether he had not just seen with what torments Asclas and Leonidas were punished and by what death they died, he said that was the very thing that had made him dare, for they had left him the pattern of manly endurance; and he named as well the wonder of the governor's own boat, held fast in the middle of the river because he would not call Christ God.

The governor then sent for Philemon the comedian, to pipe and, with the voice of the flute speaking against him, to hunt this Christian round and coax him towards the idols — not knowing, the life says, that this was Philemon himself who was speaking, and that the only difference was that he had sounded before with trumpets and now spoke with the divine Spirit. Philemon was looked for everywhere and not found, until the governor called his brother Theon, who knew that his brother was wearing Apollonius's cloak but not what his purpose was, and said: Philemon is standing in front of you.

The governor had his face uncovered, saw Philemon, and began to laugh, taking it for a joke against the Christians to amuse the bystanders; and he told him to throw off the cloak and come to the sacrifice. But Philemon confessed in earnest that he was a Christian and made fun of the gods of the heathen, and answered that he did not pray for the health of the emperors but for his own salvation to his Master, Christ the King; that he was not mocking the Christians but showing the true change of his heart; and that he was ready to die not one death but a thousand for that confession.

The people, who loved him for his piping, begged the governor not to destroy the common comfort of the city; and the governor offered him the great festival just beginning, at which it fell to him to sing the praises of the gods with trumpets and flutes. Philemon answered that their festival put him in mind of the festival kept above, and that the sound of the trumpets woke in him the longing to hear the songs of the angels. Reminded that he was not even a complete Christian, having never been baptised, he cried out that he owed the governor thanks for putting him in mind of holy Baptism.

He went out into the middle of the assembly and asked whether any Christian priest there would come and give him baptism; and when he saw that all were held by fear and none dared show himself a priest, he grieved, and cried to God with hot tears not to leave him without baptism but to show him, in whatever way he knew, a priest and water. As he prayed a cloud of water came down, surrounded him and baptised him while all looked on and wondered, and then went up again. He prayed too for his flutes and trumpets and pipes — which were Apollonius's, taken with the change of clothes — that they might be burnt to ashes, so that no unbeliever could ever say, these are Philemon's trumpets; and fire came down from heaven and burnt them all in front of Apollonius.

Theon told the governor in detail what Apollonius had done, and so Apollonius's own contest was opened. Both men had their ankles bored through and were dragged by ropes through the whole city. Philemon was hung on an olive tree and shot at by everyone, and as he prayed the arrows would not come near him: some struck the tree, some fell to the ground, and some, by a wonder, stayed in the air. When the governor came close to look, an arrow came down out of the air with such force that it struck him in the right eye and blinded him on the spot.

Blinded, he knew the true God, and unbound the martyr and begged him to heal the eye. Philemon answered that he would not heal him now, in case the kindness were taken for sorcery, but that his own end was near: afterwards the governor should come to his grave, take earth from it, lay it on the eye and call on the name of Christ, and he would be healed at once. Then, by the governor's order, both martyrs were beheaded, and their honoured bodies were laid beside the martyrs Asclas and Leonidas.

The governor did as he had been told and was healed in the eye and in the soul, and cried out before them all that he was a Christian; and that is [Arianus](/saints/arianus-of-the-thebaid), who was baptised with all his house, released in peace the thirty-six Christians still held for Christ, and came with two bishops and a great crowd to bury the martyrs with clean linen and costly spices. doxologia.ro's line for the day names Philemon «Sfântul Mucenic Filimon» and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 14 December too, and saint.gr tells the story with the money in it. These saints, it says, lived in the years of the emperor Diocletian, which it glosses as 290. [Arrianus](/saints/arianus-of-the-thebaid), the governor of Thebes in Egypt, arrested thirty-seven Christians; and one of them, [Apollonius](/saints/apollonius-the-reader), lost his nerve before the tortures and gave four florins and his own clothes to Philemon, who played the reed-pipe, so that Philemon should sacrifice to the idols in his place.

Philemon put the clothes on, and at the same moment was bound in mind to the Christian faith as well; and when he was ordered to sacrifice to the idols he confessed Christ instead. The governor then commanded that Philemon be fetched to play on his pipe, so that the supposed Apollonius might be drawn out — and Philemon showed himself, and the governor was left astonished. He told him, though, that he was no Christian, since he had not been baptised; so Philemon prayed fervently, and at once it rained hard, and he was given to know that this rain was his baptism.
