# George the Chozebite

George was born of a devout family on the island of Cyprus. When his parents died, wanting the desert life and escaping a marriage his uncle was arranging for him, he fled to the holy places to find his elder brother Heraclid, who had already lived some years as a hermit in the lavra of Calamon by the Jordan. His brother judged him too young for the desert and took him instead to the monastery of the Mother of God at Chozeba.

Tonsured there, he was put under a harsh and unjust elder, and obeyed him with meekness and patience. One day, late in bringing water from the stream, he was struck across the face before the whole brotherhood; the elder's hand withered on the spot and stiffened, and was not healed until the disciple prayed for him before the graves of the fathers of the monastery. To escape the honour this brought him George fled to Calamon and spent long years there with his brother, obeying him as a father, eating mouldering scraps kept from one week to the next. He made a dead tree bear fruit, and tamed a lion with a word.

After his brother and the abbot of the community had died he returned to Chozeba, where the abbot gave him a cell of his own. He shut himself in all week and came out on Sunday only, to speak a word of profit to the brothers and to hear the confession of their thoughts. He rebuked those who boasted of their years in the monastery and despised the laity and sinners: no one can come near God, he said, who is not at peace with his neighbour. He taught his disciples to be freed from the passions by the fear of God, and to outdo one another in humility.

Before the pagan Persians came in 614 he foretold the fall of Jericho and the siege of Jerusalem, so the abbot and the brothers had time to flee. He would not leave the place God had set him in, but at his disciples' pleading he sheltered at Calamon. Most of the monks were found by the invaders and killed or led away captive; George was honoured by them and let go. He returned to Chozeba and stayed enclosed to the end of his days, served by his disciple Antony. At midnight, when the disciple came, he kissed him and said, "Go out, my soul! Go to the Lord."
