# Ias of Persia

Ias, already an old woman, was taken captive by the Persians with nine thousand Christians, who were tortured in various ways; with them she stood before the chief magi of the king of Persia and was punished with various torments, and at last beheaded. Tradition says that when she was beheaded the earth that received her blood swelled and rose supernaturally, the executioners who had tortured her were paralysed and blinded, and the air was filled with a wonderful fragrance — the martyr Ias breathed the scent of myrrh, the verse says, of red-dyed steaming blood.

Her memory is repeated on 4 August, and the Greek synaxarion keeps an Ia on 10 September of whom it says that she may be the same woman, though the verses differ.

This record is the two: the author ruled on 3 October 2026 that they are one, and the synaxarion's own hedge is left standing, because the source has not settled it. The 10 September entry adds that she came from the fortress called Bizala, and that after the magi failed to persuade her she was imprisoned and brought out fifteen months later to be tortured and beheaded — cut from the earth of the valleys by the sword, its verse says, and brought to the Saviour like a sweet-smelling violet.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, says that she was accused by the priests of the idols and suffered for the Lord in Persia in the time of Shapur II, in 363; by tradition the sun was darkened at her death and the whole air was filled with a wonderful fragrance.

**The Russian calendar’s 9 апреля ст. ст. names her inside another company’s life**, and this folder gains its first `related` rows from it. The Сретенский calendar’s line for that day is a company of two hundred and seventy-three — «Мчч. Дисана еп., Мариава пресвитера, @7[Авдиеса] и прочих 270-ти (362-364).» — and the life behind it closes on her: «В их числе была и мученица Ия, которая воспоминается 11 сентября», among them was the martyr Ia, who is commemorated on 11 September. That is the day this folder is kept on, and «(362-364)» is the same bracket her own Сретенский line prints, so the two lines agree about when as well as about whom.

The three men that line names are [Desan of Persia](/saints/desan-of-persia), [Mariab of Persia](/saints/mariab-of-persia) and [Audiesius, with the martyrs who suffered with him](/saints/audiesius-of-persia); the first two were made folders by the batch that wrote this row and the third was already here from the Greek side. Nothing in this folder’s own `dates`, `names` or attestation rows is changed by the reading: her Russian row stands on her own day, 11 сентября ст. ст., and one church keeps one row.
