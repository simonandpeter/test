# Ias of Persia

Ias, already an old woman, was taken captive by the Persians with nine thousand Christians, who were tortured in various ways; with them she stood before the chief magi of the king of Persia and was punished with various torments, and at last beheaded. Tradition says that when she was beheaded the earth that received her blood swelled and rose supernaturally, the executioners who had tortured her were paralysed and blinded, and the air was filled with a wonderful fragrance — the martyr Ias breathed the scent of myrrh, the verse says, of red-dyed steaming blood. Her memory is repeated on 4 August, and the Greek synaxarion thinks the [Ia](/saints/ia-of-persia) of 10 September may be the same woman.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, says that she was accused by the priests of the idols and suffered for the Lord in Persia in the time of Shapur II, in 363; by tradition the sun was darkened at her death and the whole air was filled with a wonderful fragrance.
