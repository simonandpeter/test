# Seraphim

Nikolai Zvezdinsky was born in Moscow on 7 April 1883, in the sixth week of Lent, the son of the priest John Zvezdinsky — a convert from the priestless Old Believers who had walked from Soligalich to Petersburg to petition for admission to the Church, been received, named Zvezdinsky and made a reader, then priest, and at length rector of the Trinity church in Moscow and dean of its Edinoverie parishes. His mother died when he was three, and his father was father and mother to him, waking the small boy for the night vigils and teaching him to love the services, the singing and the reading. He went to the church school of the Edinoverie parish, then in 1895 to the Zaikonospassky school and in 1899 to the Moscow seminary, praying daily before a full-length icon of St Nicholas for the gift of preaching — which he received. In 1902, in his second seminary year, he fell ill with a lymphangitis then all but incurable and was healed by prayer before an image of the not yet glorified elder Seraphim of Sarov, brought by the igumen of the Sarov hermitage; the little icon became a family treasure and went with him nearly all his life, and a year later he saw the elder’s glorification. He finished the seminary in 1905 among the best, entered the Moscow Academy, and became known for his sermons — people came from the town and the Lavra to hear the student Zvezdinsky preach.

He was tonsured in 1908, and in 1920 consecrated bishop of Dmitrov, vicar of the Moscow diocese. For his zeal as a pastor he was arrested and exiled more than once; in 1932 he was sentenced to three years’ exile in Kazakhstan and then moved to the Omsk region, and on 26 August 1937 he was shot at Ishim for counter-revolutionary activity. He was glorified among the new martyrs at the Jubilee Council of 2000. The Russian calendar keeps him on 13 August (Julian) with the presbyters [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-presbyter-martyr-1937-13-august) and [James](/saints/james-presbyter-martyr-1937) and the deacon [Alexius](/saints/alexius-deacon-martyr-1937).
