# Cleopatra

doxologia.ro's own page for Cleopatra carries no life; everything the site tells of her it tells in the life of [Varus](/saints/varus-the-martyr), who is kept on the same day. She was a widow of Palestine whose husband had been a captain of soldiers and had died in Egypt, and she was a Christian. She watched from a distance while Varus was tortured, and when his body was thrown out of the city she rose in the night with her servants, carried it home, and dug a grave for it in her own chamber beside her bed.

Every day she lit candles over the grave and burned incense there. When the persecution slackened she wished to return to her own country, and obtained the governor's leave by asking for the bones of her husband; she took the martyr's relics instead and laid them in the village of Edra near Tabor, beside her own people. Christians came with their sick, healings followed, and she resolved to build a church in his name.

Her son John had been granted a commission in the emperor's army, and at the dedication she laid his belt and soldier's dress upon the relics to be blessed. That same day the boy fell ill and died. She cried out at the tomb until she slept, and saw Varus holding her son, the two of them shining like the sun in white robes with golden belts and crowns; the martyr told her that he had asked God to enrol the child in the heavenly Emperor's army instead, and the boy begged not to be sent back.

She gave away what she had, lived beside the church of Saint Varus in fasting and prayer, and was shown the martyr and her son again on every Sunday that she prayed. After seven years of that life she died, and was buried in the church near her son.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 19 October, and tells the same story. saint.gr says Cleopatra died in peace, and that she had tended Saint [Varus](/saints/varus-the-martyr) while they were torturing him; that after his death she took his relics, buried them in Palestine and built a magnificent church to him. She had one only son, an officer at the king's court, who fell gravely ill and died. Then Cleopatra ran to the church of Varus and begged him either to raise her son or to take her too along with him. Worn out, she slept, and saw in her sleep Varus together with her son, both in shining clothes, speaking words of comfort; and waking she was filled with joy, understanding that her son was in Paradise. She buried his body near the martyr's tomb, shared out her property among the poor, stayed by the church doing many good works, and after seven years fell asleep in peace.
