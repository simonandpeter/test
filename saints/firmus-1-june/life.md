# Firmus (1 June)

Doxologia's life for Firmus is four lines, and this is all of it. He was in the days of Maximian, it says, and for his faith in Christ he was taken and brought before Magon the governor; and not bending to offer sacrifice to the idols he was stripped and beaten with sinews, then hung up and tormented in many ways, and at the last they cut off his head, and so he received the crown of his contest.

The page gives him no country, no office and no year: it names the emperor and the governor and nothing else, so nothing else is recorded here. He is entered under the day, apart from the martyrs of Justin's company whom the same calendar prints for 1 June, because the life doxologia gives him is a separate one and names none of them.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives what the Romanian line lacked. Firmus lived in the years of the emperor Maximian, saint.gr says. In the year 299 he was arrested because he confessed Christ, and was taken before the Magos — perhaps, the page allows, the governor's own name; and because he would not be persuaded to sacrifice to the idols they first stripped him and scourged him with whips made of oxen's sinews, then hung him up and tore his flesh and cut his back with a razor. After that they put his limbs out of joint and slashed his belly, his feet and his hands with knives. But because Firmus stood firm in his Christian faith, they beheaded him. The distich over him says that Firmus, having endured many torments, found many recompenses too in heaven.
