# Stefan the First-Crowned

Stefan was the first crowned king of Serbia, anointed at his own royal foundation of Žiča by St Sava - his brother in the flesh and his father in the spirit - and together, the life says, the two of them raised Serbian Orthodoxy to great heights of glory. He was a true and zealous Christian, a wise ruler and a lover of peace. Of his four sons, three ruled Serbia one after another, and the fourth, Predislav, at the urging of his uncle Sava took monastic vows and became in time Sava II, third archbishop of the Serbs and a saint himself.

Before his death, at his own desire, Stefan was tonsured a monk by his brother with the name Simon, and he departed to the Lord on the twenty-fourth day of September in the year 1224. His wonder-working relics rest at the monastery of Studenica.

The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 24 September as Stephen, king of the Serbs, and says only that his father was the Venerable Symeon, founder of the monastery of Hilandar on Mount Athos, and that he fell asleep in 1224; his body lies incorrupt in the monastery of Studenica.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 24 September old style on one line with his son [Vladislav](/saints/vladislav-of-serbia), and says he was the first ruler of Serbia crowned with a royal crown, the son of Saint Stefan Nemanja, and that he died in 1224, having taken the monastic habit with the name Simon, and was buried at Studenica.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps him on 24 September old style: he was crowned king in his own foundation of Žiča by his brother and spiritual father [Saint Sava](/saints/sava-of-serbia), was a pious Christian and a wise and peaceable ruler, and with Sava raised Orthodoxy to great triumph among his people. At his wish Sava tonsured him before his death with the name Simon; he died on 24 September 1224, and his wonder-working relics rest at Studenica.
