# Seraphim (Ostroumov)

The Сретенский monastery's calendar heads with him the longest clause of its 25 ноября ст. ст., «Сщмчч. Серафима , архиеп. Смоленского, Григория , Иоанна , Василия , Космы , Иоанна , Симеона , Илариона , Ярослава , Александра , Иоанна , Виктора , Андрея , Варлаама пресвитеров и мч. Павла (1937)», and carries no life for him; azbyka.ru prints one out of grad-petrov.ru. In the world he was Михаил Митрофанович Остроумов, born in 1880 at Moscow to a psalm-reader's family. He finished the Moscow theological academy and stayed on as a professorial stipendiary, and in 1904 was tonsured with the name Serafim.

The young monk went to Optina, where he worked in the kitchen and then served as canonarch, and after that monastic trial returned to teaching at Moscow. In 1905 he visited the Chełm country, came to love its religious and national life, and left the academy without hesitation to serve there — at first as a plain monk of its poorest house, the Jabłeczna monastery of Saint Onuphrius, which was in such decay that there was talk of closing it and had debts it could not pay.

In 1906 he was made its deputy superior, and in a few years turned it into one of the centres of the religious and cultural revival of that country: he drew in men with higher theological and secular degrees, founded a school for psalm-readers, opened teaching courses and several other schools, one of them agricultural, and built a skete with a fine church. Eight years went to that work. He was made archimandrite in 1908, and was besides dean of all the monasteries of the diocese, its missionary, chairman of its learned council, editor of its papers, and from 1914 rector of its seminary.

A year before the revolution he was consecrated bishop of Bielsk, vicar of Chełm, and in 1917 became bishop of Orel. He was arrested during the Council of 1917–1918 and held at Orel, forbidden to write letters, his diocesan assembly broken up and his house searched. In 1922, in the seizure of church valuables, he was arrested again and sentenced to seven years in strict isolation; let out after nearly two, he was raised archbishop, fought the renovationists, was arrested again in 1926, and was moved to the see of Smolensk, never allowed back to Orel.

No wave of the persecution passed him by. In 1936 he was arrested in a group case as one of the leaders of a counter-revolutionary group of churchmen, charged with using the pulpit for counter-revolutionary sermons and with telling the people that the Soviet power would soon perish. He was sentenced to five years and sent in March 1937 to the Karlag at Karaganda; in November he was arrested there among a group of thirty-one on «вновь вскрывшихся обстоятельствах», newly disclosed circumstances, and taken under escort back to Smolensk. The troika of the NKVD administration for the Smolensk region passed sentence of death, and he was shot in the forest outside Smolensk on 8 December 1937.
