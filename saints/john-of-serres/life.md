# John of Serres

John was the son of a well-to-do family of Serres, born and brought up in that city in the last half of the fifteenth century. saint.gr says his infant crying was mixed with the wailing of the people of Serres for David, king of Trebizond, and his three children, who were hanged at the end of the royal road just outside the city by Mehmed II's order. In the prime of his youth his bearing — he would appear in public as the equal of the conquerors — and the brightness of his looks drew the envy of the Turks, who denounced him to the authorities.

The charge was that he had publicly reviled the Prophet's faith and refused to follow it after promising to. He was arrested and brought to court, and his judges began with offers of office, reckoning on the gain if others followed his example; he answered every offer by confessing Christ aloud. When the indirect methods failed they took him out of prison and dragged him naked over the ground «ώσπερ θήρες ανήμεροι», like untamed beasts, tore out his hair and beat him without pause.

He held out and went on praising the Lord, and there appeared at his right a rider of great bearing whose face shone like the sun — Saint Theodore, the page says, counselling him not to lose heart. They put him back in prison, and when it was plain that nothing would move him they wrote to the Porte for instructions. The judge of Serres then ordered his death if he would not deny Christ.

So they brought him out to a public place, set him on a heap of brushwood and dry sticks, bound his hands and feet, and set the pile alight. He stayed calm throughout, asking his tormentors whether they had really given him to the fire, and singing to God in a quiet voice, until one of the executioners drove a stake into his mouth to silence him and so ended his earthly life. He suffered about 1480.
