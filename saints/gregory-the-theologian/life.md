# Gregory the Theologian

Gregory was born in 329 at Arianzos, not far from Cappadocian Nazianzus, into a Christian family of eminent lineage. His father, also Gregory, was bishop of Nazianzus; his mother Nonna had prayed for a son and vowed to dedicate him to the Lord, and named him when the prayer was answered.

When the child could read she put the Scriptures into his hands. He was schooled at home by his uncle Amphilochius, an experienced teacher of rhetoric, then in the schools of Nazianzus, of Caesarea in Cappadocia and of Alexandria, and at last set out for Athens to finish his education. On the voyage from Alexandria a storm raged for many days, and Gregory — still only a catechumen — lay twenty days in the ship's stern in terror of dying unbaptised, vowing himself to God; he was saved when he called on the name of the Lord.

He is the Gregory Nazianzus of the patristic books, and died in 389.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same twenty-fifth of January, and saint.gr's page adds his household and the end of his course. He was born in 329 at Arianzos, a small town of Cappadocia, to Gregory, bishop of Nazianzus, and to [Nonna](/saints/nonna-mother-of-gregory-the-theologian); his brother was Caesarius and his sister [Gorgonia](/saints/gorgonia). He was schooled at Nazianzus and at Caesarea, where he came to know [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great), and afterwards under teachers of rhetoric in Palestine and Alexandria and at the schools of Athens, thirteen years in all. At Constantinople he turned a room of the house that lodged him into a church and named it Saint Anastasia, for the resurrection of the Orthodox faith, and there he spoke the five theological orations that won him his title. He left 408 poems of some 18,000 lines. His honoured head is kept at the monastery of Vatopedi on the Holy Mountain, and his body in the church of his name at Nea Karvali.

The Russian calendar keeps him again on 30 января ст. ст., in the synaxis its own heading names outright: «Собор Вселенских учителей и святителей Василия Великого, Григория Богослова и Иоанна Златоустого», the Synaxis of the Ecumenical Teachers and Hierarchs. The heading enumerates the three and nobody else, and the day prints a troparion and a kontakion over them together, headed «святителей» and calling them of one mind with the apostles and teachers of the whole world. The row for his own feast stands; this day is a note on it.
