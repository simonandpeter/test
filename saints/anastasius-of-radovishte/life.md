# Anastasius of Radovishte

Anastasius was born in 1774 at Radovishte in the diocese of Strumica, a
Bulgarian, and was at Thessalonica at twenty with the master he was
apprenticed to. The master wanted to sell some Turkish clothes without paying
the duty on them, and sent the boy into the city dressed as a Turk. The
collectors stopped him and asked for his receipt; he said he was a Turk;
they told him to recite the Muslim salutation, and he fell silent. Brought
before the commander, he was invited to become a Muslim and refused, and was
passed on to the chief tax-collector, who flattered and then threatened him.
Anastasius admitted the customs offence and would not deny his faith. The
official asked the mufti what to do and was told, you have the sword in one
hand and the law in the other, use which you wish — meaning that by law the
duty was owed, and by the mufti’s reading the boy would not be counted a
Muslim armed with a sword. So the youth was sent to the local mullah with
five Turks to swear he had blasphemed Muhammad. He answered honestly that
he had not, though he would allow he had shown disrespect to Muslim
customs. He was tortured and condemned to be hanged, urged all the way to
renounce his faith, and, bleeding and exhausted, fell by the roadside and
died there on 29 August 1794.

The Serbian calendar keeps him on 29 August (Julian), and the Ohrid
Prologue for the day tells the same story — the trade at Thessalonica, the
pressure to turn Turk, the refusal, the torture.
