# Demetrius (Benevolensky)

Demetrius Mikhailovich Benevolensky was born on 10 October 1883 in the town of Vyshny Volochok in the province of Tver, the son of a priest. He studied at the Tver seminary and then at the one in Saint Petersburg, finishing in 1909, and took a teaching post at the Nikolo-Stolpensky monastery. In 1911 he was offered the priesthood and the parish of Ostrovno, whose priest had died and left the flock without a pastor; he married that year, and on 21 November 1911 was ordained to the church of Saint Demetrius there. In 1919 he was moved to the Trinity church of Panoshino in the Udomlya district.

He was arrested on 16 January 1929 together with the warden of his church, charged with having served a solemn service «с целью ... возбуждения суеверия в массах населения для извлечения таким путём материальных выгод». The parish loved him: the village assembly signed a petition for the release of both men and offered sureties if sureties were wanted. The court convicted them anyway, fining the priest a hundred roubles and giving the warden six months. His sons were expelled from school as the children of a disenfranchised man and had to be sent to relatives in Saratov to be educated at all.

He was denounced again in 1930 by a collective-farm official and by the communist in whose house he rented a room, who wrote that the priest's house was the centre for all the believers of the district. Asked whether he meant to stay a priest or accept the new order and give up his orders, he answered: «Я убеждён в правоте своей веры и священнического сана никогда не сниму». Three years of exile in the Northern region followed. He came home in May 1933, and [Thaddeus](/saints/thaddeus-of-tver), archbishop of Tver, placed him at Sinevo-Dubrovo in the Sonkovo district.

He was arrested again in November 1937, charged with counter-revolutionary activity and with spreading rumours of war. The investigation lasted one day and he admitted nothing. The troika ruled on 25 November 1937 that he be shot, and he was shot and buried in a common grave whose place the authorities hid and which is unknown to this day. He was canonised as a locally venerated saint of the Tver diocese in September 1999 and for the whole church in August 2000.

The day's line names twenty-seven new martyrs of 1937 together — twenty-three presbyters, a deacon, a hieromonk, a lay martyr and a woman — and the calendar carries a life page for ten of them, of whom he is one. The men of that line were tried separately, in different districts and different prisons, and no one of these lives names another of them, so no relation is recorded between them here.
