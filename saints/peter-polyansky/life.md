# Peter (Polyansky)

Pyotr Fyodorovich Polyansky was born in 1862 in the devout family of a priest of the village of Storozhevoye in the Voronezh diocese. He finished the Voronezh seminary in the first class in 1885, the Moscow Theological Academy in 1892, and was kept on there as assistant inspector. After a series of posts at the church school at Zhirovitsy he was moved to Petersburg onto the staff of the Synodal Education Committee and became a member of it — a high synodal official who, his life says, was marked by taking nothing for himself and by strictness. He travelled on inspection over very nearly the whole of Russia looking at the state of the church schools, and found time in the middle of it to defend a master's dissertation in 1897 on the first epistle of St Paul to Timothy.

He sat in the Local Council of 1917–18. After the revolution, until 1920, he managed a Moscow factory called Bogatyr. Then, with the persecution of the Church begun, Patriarch [Tikhon](/saints/tikhon-of-moscow) asked him to be tonsured, ordained, and made his assistant in the government of the Church. He told his brother what he thought of the offer: «Я не могу отказаться. Если я откажусь, то буду предателем Церкви, но, когда соглашусь, я знаю, я подпишу сам себе смертный приговор» — I cannot refuse. If I refuse I shall be a traitor to the Church; but when I agree, I know I shall be signing my own death sentence.

He was consecrated bishop of Podolsk in 1920 and exiled to Veliky Ustyug immediately; he came back to Moscow when Tikhon was released from arrest and became the Patriarch's closest helper. Archbishop in 1923, metropolitan of Krutitsy in 1924, a member of the Provisional Patriarchal Synod, and in the last months of Tikhon's life his faithful assistant in everything. At the beginning of 1925 the Patriarch named him a candidate for locum tenens of the patriarchal throne, after which he held that office in law for the remaining twelve years of his life and spent all of them in prison.

On the compromise with the communists he was explicit, and his demand of Metropolitan Sergius is the sentence he is remembered by: «если Вы не в силах защищать Церковь, уйдите в сторону и уступите место более сильному» — if you have not the strength to defend the Church, step aside and give place to a stronger man. He held that the Russian bishops should themselves impose a penalty on Sergius for his anti-canonical acts. [Agathangel (Preobrazhensky)](/saints/agathangel-preobrazhensky), the other locum tenens of those years, is kept on 3 October, two days before this synaxis.

The imprisonment is recorded in detail and the detail is the point. Scurvy first. Then, in 1931, after a visit from Tuchkov offering to make him an informer of the GPU, he was partly paralysed. In 1933 the asthmatic old man was deprived of his walks in the common prison yard and given instead a separate well of a yard whose air was thick with prison fumes; he lost consciousness on the first such walk. Moved under a harsher regime to the special-purpose prison at Verkhneuralsk, he was put in solitary again and given the number 114 in place of a name — strict isolation.

There is testimony that Sergius, expecting the lawful locum tenens to be released, wrote to the Soviet government that if Metropolitan Peter came out the whole church policy of concessions would reverse. The authorities answered as required: Peter waited out his release day, 23 July 1936, in the Verkhneuralsk prison and was given a fresh three-year term instead. He was seventy-four. The authorities then decided to declare him dead and told Sergius so, and in December the title of locum tenens was assigned to Sergius while the locum tenens was still alive.

In July 1937 an operational order was drawn up on Stalin's instruction to shoot, within four months, every confessor held in the prisons and camps. Under it the Verkhneuralsk administration drew up his charge: that he showed himself an irreconcilable enemy of the Soviet state, blamed its officials for the persecution of the Church, and slanderously accused the NKVD of partiality towards him — his imprisonment being, supposedly, the result of his refusing the NKVD's demand that he renounce the office of locum tenens. He was shot at four in the afternoon of 27 September 1937, the 10th of October by the new calendar, at Magnitogorsk.
