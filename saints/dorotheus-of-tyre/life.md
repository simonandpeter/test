# Dorotheus of Tyre

Dorotheus was bishop of the city of Tyre in the reign of Diocletian, the pagan emperor of Rome; and because of the dreadful persecution then raised against the Christians he left his see and hid himself in places unknown. Afterwards, when the great Constantine reigned and the Church of Christ had relief, Dorotheus returned again to Tyre and to his throne, and shepherded the flock of rational sheep well, bringing many from the error of idol-worship to God.

He lived on until the days of Julian the Apostate, who, on taking the empire, at first did not fight the Church of Christ openly but in secret, ordering the nobles of his own mind who ruled the provinces to torment and to kill the Christians. Seeing the great torment done to the faithful, Dorotheus again left the throne of the Church of Tyre and fled the hands of the tormentors; for the Lord commanded that we should not give ourselves to dangers that come openly from persecutors, but flee them, saying: when they persecute you in this city, flee into another.

So he went out from Tyre into Thrace; but there too he did not escape the servants of the idols, God calling him to the martyr's crown. He was seized by Julian's nobles in the city of Diopolis, where, suffering many beatings and enduring torments of many kinds, he gave up his blessed soul into the hands of Christ God, being very old: he had a hundred and seven years from his birth. The page gives no year.

He left behind him writings of many kinds useful to the Church, in Greek and in Latin, for he was well skilled in both these tongues, a man perfect in spiritual and in outward wisdom. He wrote the lives of the prophets and of the apostles and other useful discourses and commentaries; and now, himself written in the book of life, he dwells with those saints whose lives he wrote.
