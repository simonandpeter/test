# Elijah (Cheredeev)

He was born on 20 July 1878 in the town of Kashin in the Tver province, a son of the priest John Cheredeev, finished the Tver theological seminary in 1901 and was ordained priest in the same year, and served in the church of the village of Vysokovo. When the persecution began again at the end of the twenties the local authorities turned him out of his house, took everything he had, and told him under threat of arrest to leave the district. He went to relatives at Kashin, and from there the archbishop of Tver appointed him to the church of Stelkovo in the Kalyazin district.

In 1931 he went round the houses of his parishioners with molebens and the offices, as the custom was, and was arrested for it, sentenced to six months’ forced labour, and sent to serve the sentence on the collective farm of the same village. In 1934 the archbishop moved him to the church of Konstantinovo in the same district. His wife had died by then, and his unmarried sister helped him bring up his only daughter, who was twelve.

In the summer of 1937 it became known that the authorities meant to close that church on the ground that the faithful had stopped repairing it, and to use its bricks to build a theatre at Kalyazin. After the vigil of the Dormition he called the most active of his parishioners into the church watchhouse and told them so, and asked them to resist the giving over of their church to be profaned: it had stood two hundred years, he said, and they would do better to collect money and adorn it. He and the churchwarden went round the parish the next day, collected enough, and in a fortnight the roof was painted and the walls whitewashed, so that the church looked newly built.

He was arrested on 21 December 1937. A medical examination at Kalyazin found him gravely ill with myocarditis and unfit for physical labour, but able to walk as far as the prison at Kashin, and he was sent there the same day and soon questioned. He answered that he had never held conversations against the Soviet power and did not admit his guilt, and in spite of the investigator’s threats he refused to speak against himself or anyone else. A troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot on 27 December, and he was shot on 29 December 1937. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
