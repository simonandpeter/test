# Pionius of Smyrna

The Apostle bids us remember those who have gone before us and have spoken to us the word of God, so that, calling to mind their faith, their life and their good end, we may be moved to follow them. So it is fitting to keep the memory of the martyr Pionius, who while he was in the world turned many from the deceit of the devil to God; he was an apostolic man in those times, and being crowned with martyrdom he left us the pattern of his good deeds.

In the sixth month — February, counting from September — on the twenty-third day, when the memory of the hieromartyr Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna, is kept, there were seized at Smyrna, where a great multitude of Jews lived, Pionius the presbyter, and Sabina, a woman zealous for the right faith, and Asclepiades, Macedonius and Linus, a priest of the catholic church; for there was then a great persecution under Decius.

Pionius had been forewarned that they would be taken on Polycarp's feast. So he fasted with Sabina and Asclepiades, took three plaited chains and put them on himself and on them, and sat at home waiting for those who would come. On the day of the memorial, after prayer, when they had tasted a little bread and water, Polemon, the officer of the idol sacrifices, came with soldiers; and when he asked whether they knew the imperial orders to sacrifice, Pionius answered that they knew the commandments of their God, who tells them to worship him alone.

They walked to the assembly without being forced, and the people ran after them as after a new wonder, to see men carrying their bonds willingly. The life sets out at length what was said before the magistrates and before the crowd of Greeks and Jews. Garlands were set on the martyrs' heads by force and they were pressed to taste of the sacrifices; but they tore the garlands off, trod them underfoot and spat on the offerings, and were taken back to prison with insults and blows.

When the proconsul Quintilianus came to Smyrna he set Pionius alone before him for questioning, and finding him unmoved had him hung up and his body torn with iron claws. Asked why he hurried towards death, he said that he was hurrying not to death but to everlasting life. Then the sentence was read out in the Roman manner: that Pionius, who had confessed himself a Christian, should be crucified and burned alive.

At the place of execution he stripped himself, looked at his body and was filled with great joy at its purity, and gave thanks to God that he had been kept unstained to the end. The wood, made in the shape of a cross, was lying on the ground; he laid himself down and stretched himself upon it and gave himself to the soldiers to be nailed. When they offered to draw the nails out and have physicians heal him if he would obey the emperor, he said, after a silence, that he wished to sleep, so as to rise the better at the common resurrection.

They raised the wood, fixed the lower end in the ground, heaped wood about it and set it alight. The great flame went round him and he did not burn; he shut his eyes and prayed in secret, so that all who saw him thought he was already dead. When the blaze sank a little he finished the prayer he had made in the secret of his heart and opened his eyes, and the people marvelled greatly to see him still alive in so great a fire. Then with a glad face he said “Amen”, and added, “Lord, receive my spirit”, and fell asleep.

When the fire had gone out his body was found whole, and not a hair of his head was burned, and his face was bright. This was in the city of Smyrna under Decius, in the time of the proconsul Quintilianus, on the eleventh of the seventh month by the reckoning of Asia — that is, of March — on a Saturday, at the tenth hour of the day; and the life says that so far these are the words of Symeon Metaphrastes. Of the others taken and held in chains with him it is not written with what torments they finished their course. Pionius himself had written the life and passion of Polycarp. doxologia prints no hymn for him.
