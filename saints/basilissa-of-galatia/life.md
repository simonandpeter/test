# Basilissa of Galatia

saint.gr's calendar for 22 Μαρτίου names two women together, [Callinice](/saints/callinice-of-galatia) and Basilissa, and gives each of them a heading of her own. They came from Galatia, the page says. Because Basilissa was rich she used to give money to Callinice to take to the prisons and hand out to the Christians shut up there for their food, so that they should pray for her; she wanted to make them ready for martyrdom and to stand by them, so that they should not lose courage in their afflictions.

One day Callinice was arrested and asked whose the money was, and because she did not know how to tell a lie she confessed; for that they bound her and handed her over to the ruler. Basilissa was arrested as well and brought before him in court, and both of them confessed Christ with boldness and were put to various tortures, being pressed to deny their faith and sacrifice to the idols. Since they would not be persuaded, their holy heads were cut off with a sword, and so they conquered the devil and enjoyed the kingdom of the heavens.

Their martyrdom, the page says, took place in the year 252, under the emperor Gallus (251–253). The couplet over the two turns on both names at once: they cut down Callinice with Basilissa, the fair-victorious ones and queens of heaven — the play on her own name being βασίλισσα, a queen. The page names no city in Galatia, no ruler by name and no relics, and prints no hymn.
