# Dosoftei of Moldavia

Dosoftei was born in 1624 at Suceava, into the Barilă family. His parents, Leontie and Misira, were right-believing Christians and Romanians by descent, with kin in Transylvania and in the country about Lwów. At baptism the child was given the name Dimitrie, because he had been born near 26 October, the day the Church keeps the martyr of Thessalonica. He learnt his letters from the most renowned teachers in Moldavia and at the school of the Orthodox Brotherhood at the monastery of the Dormition in Lwów, and came to know Greek, Latin, church Slavonic, Polish and Ukrainian, so as to carry over the treasure of Orthodoxy into Romanian.

At the school of the monastery of Probota he learnt the other lore too — prayer, obedience, humility and the ascetic labours — and there in 1649 he received the angelic habit with the name Dosoftei. He became a spiritual father and the head of the community at Probota, developed its school greatly and raised up many enlightened disciples. In a Romanian note of 14 September 1649 he signs himself «Ieromonah Dosoftei de la Pobrata, feciorul lui Leontar Barilovici», and beside it, in Greek, «Dositheos, ieromonah, fiul lui Leontar Barila și al Misirei».

For his standing as a scholar and for the virtues that lit his face and his deeds he was called to the hierarchy: bishop of Huși in 1658, of Roman a year later, and in 1671 metropolitan of Moldavia. The chronicler Ion Neculce describes him: «Acest Dosoftei mitropolit nu era om prost (simplu) de felul lui. … Prea învățat, multe limbi știa: elinește, slovenește, și altă adâncă carte și-nvățătură. Deplin călugăr și cucernic, și blând ca un miel. În țara noastră, pe ceasta vreme nu este om ca acela».

His pastoral work was the translating and printing of holy books in Romanian, in the quiet of his cell at home or in exile abroad. His first printed books were the Psalter in verse and the Akathist of the Mother of God, at Uniev in 1673; understanding the poetry of the psalms, he put them into Romanian verse to bring the treasure of the divine word closer to his people, and he revised the translation of the Old Testament made by the spătar Nicolae Milescu. In the autumn of 1673 he went into exile in Poland, because of the changes of prince at home and of his own convictions against the Ottomans, and was replaced at Iași in 1674, and restored the year after.

After the printing house at Iași was rebuilt he printed more service and teaching books in Romanian: the Divine Liturgy in 1679 and again in 1683, the Psalter Made Plain in 1680 with parallel Romanian and Slavonic, the Book of Prayers Made Plain in 1681. Between 1682 and 1686 he translated from Greek and Slavonic sources and printed in Romanian the Lives and Conduct of the Saints in four volumes, left unfinished by his forced departure for Poland; in it he writes of Romanian monastic saints he had known himself and of others whom the people venerated.

In 1686 the Polish king Jan Sobieski entered Moldavia in his campaign against the Ottomans to draw the voivode Constantin Cantemir to his side; forced to withdraw, the Poles carried off a great deal, and Dosoftei was taken hostage with the treasury of the metropolitanate and the relics of [John the New](/saints/john-the-new-of-suceava). He lived his last years far from his country, keeping up his scholarly work and his correspondence with the Orthodox hierarchs of Moscow and Kiev, and, to quiet the theological disputes over the eucharistic epiclesis, translating from Greek into Slavo-Russian writings of the Fathers.

Pressed as he was, he refused to go over to the union and remained Orthodox until he passed into eternity on 13 December 1693. From the testimony of the monks who went with him into exile it is known that he foreknew the time of his calling. He was buried at the church of the Nativity of the Lord at Żółkiew, today in Ukraine.
