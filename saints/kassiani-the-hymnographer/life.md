# Kassiani the Hymnographer

Kassiani — Kassia, Ikasia or Eikasia — the hymnographer was born between 805 and 810 at Constantinople and lived in the reign of Theophilus (829–842). Grown up, she joined beauty of body to quickness of mind; three Byzantine chroniclers, Symeon the Metaphrast, George the Monk and Leo the Grammarian, say she took part in the bride-show for the emperor Theophilus arranged by his stepmother Euphrosyne, at which the emperor chose the wife he liked by giving her a golden apple. Dazzled by her beauty the young emperor came up to her and said: through a woman flowed the base things — meaning the sin and misfortunes that came from Eve; and Kassia, ready of tongue, answered: but through a woman too spring the better things — meaning the hope of salvation through the incarnation of Christ from the Virgin. Theophilus’s pride was wounded; he passed her over and chose Theodora. The next thing known of her is that in 843 she founded a convent to the west of the city near the walls and became its first abbess — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here. The Romanian Lives call her the best-known woman hymnographer and the only one whose hymns have stayed in the service books to this day, a nun of the ninth century whom most calendars do not mention; the Greek and Romanian calendars keep her on 7 September, the Romanian with a troparion that praises her for wisely disdaining the love of the earthly emperor for love of the Emperor of all.
