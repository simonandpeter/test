# Bebaia of Edessa

Bebaia was the sister of [Sarbelus](/saints/sarbelus-of-edessa), and the Russian calendar's life for 15 October tells the two of them in one short notice. It says that the holy martyrs Sarbelus and Bebaia were brother and sister by blood, and that they suffered in the second century, under the emperor Trajan, for confessing Christianity. Her brother had been a priest of the idols at Edessa; turned to Christ by one bishop, he received baptism together with her. The pagans tore at the two of them for a long time, and then cut off their heads.

The page says nothing of her apart from her brother — not where she lived, not what she did, not a word she spoke — and nothing is added here to fill it. The day line dates the pair «ок. 98-117», about 98 to 117, which is the span of Trajan's reign, and the life gives no year of its own, so her death stands as that interval rather than as a date. The line carries no Typikon sign and the calendar prints no troparion for her.

Her name is unsettled on the calendar's own page. Its name index heads her «Вевея (Февея, Вивея) Едесская, мц.» — Bebaia, with Phebeia and Bibeia printed beside it as the same name — and the day line itself gives only «Вевеи». The Greek, Romanian and Serbian calendars were read for the same menologion day and none of the three keeps her.

One thing is left open. The corpus already holds a «Bebaia» and a «Sarbel» out of the Russian 5 September and the Greek 4 September, and the two pairs are kept apart here because the Russian calendar keeps them apart itself. Its name index gives this martyr page 2433 and the September one page 2733, «Фивея (Вивея), мц.», and each of those pages names one day and no other. The passions differ as well: the September sister is killed by a spear thrust to the neck and her brother Thathuel is hung on a tree and sawn through, while these two are beheaded, and the calendar prints a separate life for each pair. The names have long been run together in the tradition. Nothing read here joins them, and nothing read here separates them further than the source does.
