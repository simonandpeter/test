# Peter (Yurkov)

Peter was born on 24 June 1880 in the village of Vyshegorod in the Vereya
district of Moscow province, the son of the peasant Alexis Yurkov, finished
the village school, married and farmed. Mobilised in 1915 as the front
worsened, he served until 1917 as a senior non-commissioned officer, then
settled in Moscow as a factory book-keeper; but the chief thing in his life
was singing in the church choir, where several from his factory sang too. In
1926 he was ordained deacon for a church at Vereya and soon after priest,
and moved to the Dormition church at Simbukhovo in the Vereya district,
leaving his older children at Vyshegorod as the persecution grew and living
at Simbukhovo with his wife. He and his warden [Simeon
Turkin](/saints/simeon-turkin), chosen in 1933, were arrested together on 8
September 1937 and put in the Mozhaisk prison. The duty witnesses testified
that priest and warden sought supporters everywhere, that the warden started
religious talk with the collective farmers in the village tea-room,
threatened those who did not go to church, and argued that under the new
constitution the church had large rights and no one could touch it now, that
among his building gang he called the Soviet power unstable and short-lived,
and that the priest, using a feast day, had called the collective farmers
back to the faith — the time will come, and it will be too late; you have
forgotten the church, forgotten God, but it will be remembered to you. Told
that the investigation had material that he and the warden had
systematically agitated among the collective farmers, Father Peter said he
had never engaged in counter-revolution and had purely business relations
with the warden; that he did not remember any talk in April about hard life
under the Soviet power and worse to come; that he did not confirm agitation
against the Soviet power — there may have been talk against it, but I do not
remember it — and, asked twice whether he admitted guilt, that he did not,
but said again that there might have been such talk and he did not remember
it. On 22 September the troika sentenced priest and warden to death, and on
23 September 1937 both were shot and buried in an unknown common grave at
Butovo. The life is by the igumen Damascene (Orlovsky); the Church keeps him
on 10 September of the old style, 23 September, and in the synaxes of the
new martyrs of Butovo and of Moscow.
