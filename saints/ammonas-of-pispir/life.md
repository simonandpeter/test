# Ammonas of Pispir

Ammonas lived in the days of [Anthony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great), in the fourth century — Anthony, saint.gr says, held him in great love and esteem — and he was Anthony's successor in the spiritual guidance of the monks of the monastic settlement of Pispir, which lay on the left bank of the Nile.

Seldom, the page says, has a man become so far master of himself as to stand above insults, blessing and doing good to those who insulted him. He took particular spiritual trouble over the enlightenment and the return to virtue of wretched women who had taken the road of sin; and while the world spoke ill of them, he counselled, entreated and prayed, so that many of them came to repentance and lived in reverence and chastity.

Ammonas fell asleep in peace before the year 403. His writings, the page says, show an ascetic well practised in the hidden ascents toward God. Asked once what the narrow and afflicted way is, he answered: «Στενὴ καὶ τεθλιμμένη ὁδὸς εἶναι νὰ πολεμᾶ κανεὶς τοὺς λογισμούς του καὶ νὰ κόβει γιὰ χάρη τοῦ Θεοῦ τὸ θέλημά του» — the narrow and afflicted way is that a man make war on his own thoughts and cut off his own will for God's sake.

saint.gr adds that according to some sources this is the Ammonas mentioned in the Lausiac History, who was a priest, and who once saw an angel at the right of the sanctuary writing in a book the names of the brethren who came to the Divine Liturgy, and erasing the names of those who did not, so that they died three days afterwards. The page prints no troparion and no kontakion, and credits the Apostoliki Diakonia of the Church of Greece.
