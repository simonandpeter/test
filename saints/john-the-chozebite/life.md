# John the Chozebite

John, the Greek synaxarion says, at first fell into the error of the Monophysites: when, under the empress Pulcheria, the Fourth Ecumenical Council at Chalcedon condemned the heretical teaching of Eutyches, he would not accept its dogmatic judgement. Yet he was not moved by pride, for his rich and devout parents at Thebes in Egypt had brought him up in Christian learning. He went to Jerusalem to venerate the Lord’s tomb and receive communion there, and before the day came Orthodox clergy tried to free him of his error, but still he wavered; on the eve of the day he was to commune he heard a voice in a dream saying that those who separate themselves from the truth of the Orthodox Church are unworthy to commune, and he was convinced for good.

Returning, he became a monk at the place called Choziba, which the entry puts in Egypt, and grew so in the virtues of Orthodoxy that God gave him to heal the possessed by his prayer. He was made bishop of Caesarea but went back to his hermitage, and died there, having helped countless souls with his Orthodox teaching. The Greek calendar keeps him on 3 October; the entry gives no year.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him second of the three it enumerates on this day, and its four lines disagree with the Greek entry by about a century. It makes him «Мисирац», an Egyptian, which agrees with the Greek's Thebes in Egypt; but it has him struggle in the Chozebite monastery «у време цара Јустинијана», in the days of the emperor Justinian, where the Greek sets him under the empress Pulcheria and the Fourth Council at Chalcedon. Both readings are recorded and neither is preferred. It says nothing of the Monophysite error or of the voice in the dream that cured it.

What it tells instead is two things the Greek does not. Whenever he served the Liturgy he saw a heavenly light in the altar. And an elder named Ananias struggled near him, and the humility of the two of them was a wonder: when a man brought his mad son to Ananias to be healed by prayer, Ananias sent him on to John as the greater of the two, and John, who could not disobey the elder, cried out — „У име Исуса Христа заповеда ти Ананија а не ја да изиђеш из овог младића!“, in the name of Jesus Christ it is Ananias and not I who commands you to come out of this youth — and the youth was well at once. The Prologue calls them both saints but commemorates only John on this day, and Ananias gets no folder here.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 3 Οκτωβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2586/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026; and the Serbian church's [Ohrid Prologue for the Julian 3 October](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-10-16&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
