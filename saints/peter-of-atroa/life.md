# Peter of Atroa

Peter “in Atroa” — by others “in Agrea” — lived in the years of the emperor
Nicephorus I (802–811) and Stauracius. He came from Asia and from a young
age had a calling to the monastic life; he went to Atroa in Bithynia, where
he passed his life in the strictest ascesis and died in peace — broken by
the loosing of the flesh, the verse says, Peter passed to God, the
unbreakable rock. The Greek calendar keeps him on 13 September, and an
[Andronicus of Atroa](/saints/andronicus-of-atroa) the day before. The
Russian calendar’s life has him sanctified to God before his birth,
labouring at Atroa near the Asian Olympus in extreme abstinence and
unceasing prayer, working many wonders in his lifetime, and dying in peace
in the days of Patriarch Tarasius of Constantinople (784–806), a generation
earlier than the Greek reckoning; it keeps him on 13 September of the old
style, 26 September.
