# Zenobius of Aegae

In the parts of Cilicia was a city called Aegae, and there were born the martyrs Zenobius and his sister [Zenobia](/saints/zenobia-sister-of-zenobius), of right-believing parents who raised them in good teaching and the fear of God. Their parents went to the Lord while they were still young and left them much property; and the two, young in years but old and perfect in good works in their understanding, saw the vanity of this world and took counsel together to leave everything and follow Christ.

Zenobia handed her share of the inheritance to her brother to be divided among the poor and lived willingly in poverty and stillness, keeping her virginity undefiled for the heavenly Bridegroom. Zenobius took both portions, his own and his sister's, gave them to those who needed them, and in a little while had given it all away, so that he too became as one of the poor. But God, who cares for the destitute and does not forsake those who hope in him, enriched Zenobius with his heavenly gifts in return for the wealth he had divided.

He received the gift of healing sufferings: the hands that had shown mercy to the poor the Lord had mercy on with the power of working wonders and with the healing of every infirmity and wound, so that when he touched a sick man with his hand the man was healed at once. So he did a double almsgiving in his life, the life says, one when he gave his property to the poor and another when he gave health to the sick. He drove a multitude of unclean spirits out of men, comforted the grieving and helped those in danger, and for these good works and wonders he was chosen bishop of that city, and governed the Church of God well.

A woman of Antioch came to him with an incurable wound on her breast, having spent much on physicians and found no help, but rather a worse pain, so that she was near death. He pitied her, touched the wound with his hand and made the sign of the Cross, and the pain went and the wound was healed, and she went back rejoicing to her affairs. Another woman, an unbeliever and the wife of a magistrate, suffered from the same thing and was healed by him too; and from that time she and her husband believed in Christ and baptised their children, and gained health of soul as well as of body.

Then the great persecution of the Christians came on from the unbelieving emperor Diocletian, and the governor Lysias came into the parts of Cilicia to torture all who confessed the name of Christ. First three young Christians, Claudius, Asterius and Neon, were taken, tortured in many ways and at last nailed to crosses outside the city. Hearing of Zenobius, the bishop of the Christians, Lysias sent soldiers to seize him, and set before him two things, life if he sacrificed to the gods and death if he would not.

Zenobius answered that a temporal life without Christ is not life but plain death, and that death for Christ is not death but life without dying; so he would rather die for Christ a temporal death and live with him for ever than deny him for a temporal life and be tormented for ever. The governor had him stripped, hung on a beam and beaten without mercy, saying that they would see whether his Christ came to help him.

Zenobia, hearing that her brother was suffering for Christ, ran to the place and, seeing him hung and wounded, took fire with zeal and came forward to the torturer: she was a Christian and confessed the same one God and Lord Jesus Christ as her brother, and asked to be tortured as he was, wishing to drink the same cup and be crowned with the same crown. Lysias marvelled at her courage and warned her of the shame of being stripped and the pain that would follow; she answered that the nakedness of the soul brings greater shame than the body's and that everlasting torments are more painful than temporal ones, and that she had put on Christ and was crucified with him, and he would not turn her from her Lord.

He had her stripped and beaten like her brother. Then they made a bed red-hot in the fire, stretched them both on it and set fire underneath, telling them to let Christ come and help them; and the saints answered that Christ was with them though he could not see him, bedewing them with the dew of his grace, so that they took no thought of the torments. After that they were thrown into a boiling cauldron and stayed unharmed, standing as in warm water and singing the psalm of David: Thou hast saved us from them that afflict us, and hast put to shame them that hate us.

At the last the torturer ordered them taken outside the city and beheaded. They went to their death with unspeakable joy, and at the place they prayed: thanks be to God, who had counted them worthy to fight the good fight, to finish the course and to keep the faith; let him make them sharers of his glory and number them with those who have pleased his holy name. When the prayer was finished a voice was heard at once out of heaven, promising them crowns and calling them into everlasting rest, and they were beheaded and passed from earth to heaven. Their bodies lay unburied until the priest Hermogenes took them away secretly by night and laid them in a tomb.
