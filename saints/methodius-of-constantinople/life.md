# Methodius of Constantinople

Methodius was of Sicily by birth and took the monastic life from his youth. Under Leo the Armenian, who warred on the icons, while the most holy Nikephoros held the throne of Constantinople, Methodius had the rank of apocrisiarius and was sent by Nikephoros to the pope of Rome on the business of the Church. He was kept there long, for Leo drove Nikephoros from the throne and raised in his place a man of his own mind, the heretic Theodotus the Melissene, surnamed Kassiteras.

When Leo and the false patriarch Theodotus had both perished, Methodius came back from the old Rome to the new, and as a priest contended without ceasing against the heresy that fought the icons, which by then had spread very far, for after Leo the Armenian the empire fell to Michael surnamed Balbus or Traulos, of the same heresy, and the patriarchal throne was held by the faithless. Doxologia cites the Greek Zonaras for that emperor, who at first released the fathers Leo had exiled and then threw off the pretence: he drove out Euthymius, archbishop of Sardis, for honouring the icons, and shut Methodius in a prison the page calls Crit.

Methodius stayed in that tomb of a prison until the death of Theophilus, and the page says that when the grave took the dead emperor in, it gave this living dead man back to the world, as the whale gave up Jonah. Michael the Third, Theophilus's son, then took the empire, but his mother Theodora governed it, the boy being too young; and she set herself to destroy the iconoclast heresy and to give back to the Church the honour of the holy icons. She ordered every confessor of the right faith released from chains and prisons and brought to a council at Constantinople, and Methodius came out of his burial.

The false patriarch John, called Annes, whom Theophilus had raised, was driven from the throne, and Methodius was chosen in his place and enthroned with great joy by the whole Orthodox people. The life notes that the choice had been foretold by Joannicius the Great. With the empress Theodora he gathered a council of fathers at Constantinople and confirmed the dogmas of the seventh Ecumenical Council, which had met at Nicaea under Constantine and Irene against the enemies of the icons; and thereafter he multiplied daily the honour paid to the icons, and worked to restore the honour due to the relics of the saints as well.

He governed the Church in quiet after all those troubles. Joannicius, when the patriarch visited him, foretold that he too would pass without delay out of this life; Joannicius died on the fourth day of November, and in the eighth month after him the most holy patriarch Methodius reposed on the fourteenth day of June, having shepherded the Church four years and three months. There was great lamentation for him in all the Church of Constantinople, and he was buried with the honour due to so great a bishop.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 14 June, and saint.gr brackets him «(; - 846)». Its account says he came of a great and devout family, in which he was brought up according to the commandments of the Orthodox Christian spirit; that he was born at Syracuse in Sicily and came to Constantinople under Leo V, to take up some civil post worthy of his high education and ability. At that time the question of the veneration of the holy icons held the Reigning City. The emperor Theophilos, valuing Methodius's learning, tried by various attentions to bring him over to his side; but Methodius rebuked him harshly and straightly for his foolish attitude to the holy icons. Theophilos in his rage shut him up on the island of Antigonos, inside a tomb, together with two robbers; later, however, because Methodius explained to him some difficult passage concerning the occult sciences, he released him under surveillance. After the death of Theophilos on 12 February 842, and in the days of the empress Theodora, Methodius became patriarch of Constantinople. The page's hymns keep to the same matter: they call him the foundation of the Orthodox, say that he broke the devices of the heretics firmly and set up again the icon of Christ to be honoured as a divine hierophant, and that by the dogmas of true religion he lit up the Church and drove off the mist of the heresies, preaching to all that Christ is to be depicted.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 14 June, the same menologion day the folder’s other two rows carry, and makes him a native of the city of Syracuse in Sicily. When his secular schooling was done he was tonsured and began to struggle in a monastery, and the patriarch then in the city took him into his own service — a man this paragraph does not name, because the corpus keeps some twenty of that name and not one of them is he. Under the iconoclast emperors he became known everywhere as a decided defender of the veneration of the icons, and for that the wicked emperor shut him up on an island, in a prison, with two common robbers, where he spent seven full years in the damp, without light and without enough food, as in a grave.

He was freed under the pious empress and her son, and chosen patriarch, as [Joannicius the Great](/saints/joannicius-the-great) had prophesied of him beforehand. On the first Sunday of the Great Fast he brought the icons solemnly into the church, and he wrote a canon in honour of the icon — which this book records as an authorship and not as a text, and no hymn is taken from it. Then the heretics, who could get at him no other way, hired a woman to declare that the patriarch had unclean bodily relations with her, and the whole of Constantinople was in an uproar over the slander.

Knowing no other way to prove his innocence, the patriarch went past shame: before the court he had himself demanded he stripped naked and showed them his dry body, wasted with fasting. The court was plainly convinced that he had been slandered. The people heard of it and were glad, and the heretics were ashamed; and the woman herself owned that she had been put up to it and paid to bring the slander against God’s holy one. So those who meant to shame Methodius increased his glory without meaning to. This great confessor of the faith went in peace, this book says, in the same year the Greek page already gave, and passed over into the Kingdom of God.

days.pravoslavie.ru’s reading of him (life1301) adds what the Greek account of this folder does not. Born in Sicily to a wealthy family, he went while still young to a monastery on Chios and renewed it at his own cost; under Leo the Armenian he held the office of apokrisiarios to the patriarch Никифор and was sent by him to Rome, where he was detained while the emperor put Феодот Мелиссинский on the throne in his patriarch’s place. Returning as a presbyter he fought the iconoclasts without rest, and was shut up in prison at Акрит.

Under Феофил he was beaten about the face until his jaw was broken, leaving a scar he carried afterwards, and was exiled to the island of Антигон and shut in a deep cave with two robbers, where he spent seven years in the dark until the emperor died. The confessors Феодор and Феофан Начертанные, themselves on their way into exile, sent him a greeting in verse and he answered them in verse. Raised to the throne under the empress Феодора, he presided over the council of 842 that restored the icons, composed the «Чин православия» that is still sung on the first Sunday of the Fast, rebuilt the churches the iconoclasts had wrecked, gathered up the relics they had destroyed, and brought his own patriarch’s relics back to Constantinople from the place of his exile. Slandered in his chastity by the heretics and cleared of it, he governed quietly to his death in 846.
