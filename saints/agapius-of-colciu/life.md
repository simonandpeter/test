# Agapius of Colciu

Agapius was, doxologia says, the most notable of the flowers of the Athonite skete of Colciu, near the monastery of Vatopedi, over the centuries; he was canonized and is kept on the first of March. What is known of him comes from the synaxarion in the Service of the Athonite Saints, written by [Nicodemus the Hagiorite](/saints/nicodemus-the-hagiorite), and from the oral tradition of the Holy Mountain.

He lived with his elder in the cell of the Holy Trinity in the second half of the eighteenth century. In a raid of Turkish pirates he was carried off and held a slave for twelve years; and in all that time he served the Muslim masters who had bought him so faithfully that they showed him more indulgence than any other of their slaves.

Freed wonderfully by the Mother of God, he came back secretly to his elder on the Holy Mountain. The elder rebuked him sharply, being displeased at the way his disciple had left his masters, and sent him back; and Agapius, a true son of obedience, returned into slavery without resisting.

When he arrived and told his master everything that had happened, the Turk and his household were astonished at his virtue and asked to learn more about the God of the Christians. Later Agapius came to the Holy Mountain together with his master and the man's two sons; there the three received the enlightenment of baptism, became monks, and ended their lives in holiness at the skete.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and tells the same story, with the details of the Holy Mountain's own record about it. saint.gr's line couples him with four venerable ones who were with him, and names none of them; its page reports from Gerasimos Smyrnakis that the place itself took its name from Agapius's elder, the hesychast Koletios, and that he was taken by pirates when he went down to the shore for a piece of work. [Nicodemus the Hagiorite](/saints/nicodemus-the-hagiorite), it says, has the pirates Saracens whom Agapius turned and baptised, while others have them Agarenes who sold him as a slave to a hard master; that master kept him in chains for twelve whole years at Magnesia and used him meanwhile in hard and heavy work, and Agapius worked willingly and conscientiously all the same and kept asking the Mother of God to free him. After his elder sent him back, the master was astonished at his sincerity, obedience, devotion and conscientiousness, asked to be told more of the Orthodox faith, and was catechised together with his two sons; all three asked for baptism and followed Agapius to the skete of Colciu, where they were tonsured monks and lived a holy life until their blessed repose. The page says the time of their ascesis is not known, adds that the service for him was composed by St Gerasimos Mikragiannanitis and by Charalampis Bousias, and prints no hymn.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the same story in five sentences, with the monastery named rather than the skete: he was the novice of a spiritual father at Vatopedi, taken by sea-robbers and sold as a slave in Magnesia. After twelve years he was wonderfully freed and brought back to Vatopedi by the help of the Most Holy Mother of God.

He baptised his former masters and became their spiritual father, the Prologue says, and spent the rest of his life in asceticism at Vatopedi, ending peacefully in the Lord. It gives him no year.
