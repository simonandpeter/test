# Agapius of Colciu

Agapius was, doxologia says, the most notable of the flowers of the Athonite skete of Colciu, near the monastery of Vatopedi, over the centuries; he was canonized and is kept on the first of March. What is known of him comes from the synaxarion in the Service of the Athonite Saints, written by [Nicodemus the Hagiorite](/saints/nicodemus-the-hagiorite), and from the oral tradition of the Holy Mountain.

He lived with his elder in the cell of the Holy Trinity in the second half of the eighteenth century. In a raid of Turkish pirates he was carried off and held a slave for twelve years; and in all that time he served the Muslim masters who had bought him so faithfully that they showed him more indulgence than any other of their slaves.

Freed wonderfully by the Mother of God, he came back secretly to his elder on the Holy Mountain. The elder rebuked him sharply, being displeased at the way his disciple had left his masters, and sent him back; and Agapius, a true son of obedience, returned into slavery without resisting.

When he arrived and told his master everything that had happened, the Turk and his household were astonished at his virtue and asked to learn more about the God of the Christians. Later Agapius came to the Holy Mountain together with his master and the man's two sons; there the three received the enlightenment of baptism, became monks, and ended their lives in holiness at the skete.
