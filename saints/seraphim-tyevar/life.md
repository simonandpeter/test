# Seraphim (Tyevar)

**An army librarian who became a patrologist’s closest pupil, was tonsured in secret, and died in a camp at thirty-two.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar gives him a sentence to itself — «Прмч. Серафима (1931).» — and carries no life for him; the life here is what azbyka.ru prints for the civil 6 декабря out of fond.ru, from the hegumen Дамаскин (Орловский)’s volume for the Moscow diocese. Антоний Максимович Тьевар — his forebears were French, the life says — was born on 30 July 1899 in Moscow. His father died when he was six and his brother one, and their mother Наталия Дмитриевна brought them up. He finished a modern school, went in 1917 as a statistician to the Дедовская manufactory, and in 1918–1919 studied at Moscow University.

**The Red Army made him a librarian and the Church made him a scholar.** In 1919 he entered courses in out-of-school political education and the same year, as a man liable for service, was sent as librarian to the front of the Southern Army, but was kept in Moscow and served in the political directorate of the Revolutionary Military Council as librarian-instructor; in 1920 he helped organise a congress of the Red Army’s library workers and afterwards became assistant head of the library. Demobilised in 1922, he worked until 1924 as bibliographer of the Главполитпросвет. In 1920 he met Иван Васильевич Попов, professor of the Moscow Theological Academy, and became the closest pupil of that outstanding patrologist; the study of the fathers called him to an ascetic life of his own, the more so as his teacher was an ascetic himself.

**The work he was arrested for was a list of bishops and a file on the name-glorifiers.** In 1924, the schisms being many, a full list of the episcopate was wanted — Orthodox and schismatic both, and all those by then in exile or in the camps and so unable to take a vacant see. The patriarch blessed his teacher to make such a list and his teacher took him as helper; he copied out and brought in the list of hierarchs that was then going about among believers in manuscript. The chief work given him was the gathering of information and theological judgments about the имябожники, because his teacher had sat on the Council’s subsection on the Athonite movement over the honouring of the Name of God, a question the Council of 1917–18 had left unresolved and which some surviving members of the subsection went on working at; the patriarch had been told of an Eighth Ecumenical Council supposedly being prepared for the spring of 1925, and it was thought this question might be brought to it.

**Arrested with his teacher on 10 December 1924, he admitted the list and nothing else.** He was shut in the OGPU prison on the Лубянка and questioned on 19 December. He said he had met the professor some three years before, in the reading room of the Румянцевский museum or at the University, and had heard his lectures on philosophy and his lectures on patrology and church history in a Moscow church in 1922–23; that the professor had set him work on the church history of the ancient and middle ages and, he did not remember, nothing on recent church affairs. Pressed on the lists he said he had got them in August 1924 in some church he could not name, handwritten, from a girl he did not know with whom he had fallen into talk about an ordination; that he had copied them out in his own hand because the original was torn and dirty, destroyed the original, and given his copy to the professor at his request. Of the Council he said they had spoken of whether it might open and what might be discussed — the marriages of the clergy, autocephaly, the schism in the Church — and that the work on the name-glorifiers was possibly connected with it. **The one other thing he wrote to the investigator that day** was a request: «Прошу разрешить мне передачу, так как я, будучи вегетарианцем, не могу есть тюремной пищи. Если возможно, верните мне книжечку "Новый Завет", отобранную у меня в комендатуре в день ареста».

**Three years in Solovki, in the same barrack as his teacher.** The investigation closed on 4 May 1925 finding him guilty «в сношениях с представителями иностранных государств, с целью вызова со стороны последних интервенции», of dealings with representatives of foreign states to provoke their intervention, by giving them false information about the persecution of the Church and the episcopate; on 19 June 1925 the Special Conference gave him three years in the Solovki camp. He arrived with the professor and they were housed in one barrack. An archpriest who was there wrote of him afterwards as the professor’s «душеприказчик», his executor — the name given half in jest to the young candidates for a chair whom the old professors made ready to succeed them, handing on all they knew — and said the two lived together, had their beds side by side, ate and walked together, and that the pupil worked on the teaching about Christ of Athanasius the Great and wrote and read in his free minutes. His obedience in the camp was work in the office.

**Tonsured in Holy Week 1928, arrested again in 1931, dead within the year.** His term ended at the close of December 1927; in January 1928 he went to Moscow and settled with his mother. He chose literary work by contract for a living. In the Great Fast of 1928 he went to Дивеево and Сарова and spent Holy Week at Арзамас, and there the bishop of Серпухов tonsured him to the mantia with the name Серафим and afterwards ordained him hieromonk; his mother was tonsured with the name Пантелеимона, and in their own Moscow flat he began to serve the services in his cell.

**Arrested again on the night of 14–15 April 1931, he told the investigator everything and denied nothing.** He said plainly that he had received secret monasticism and from whose hand, that he had entered a theological academy in 1920 in his religious convictions and not finished it because it was closed, that he attended it alongside his army service and his service did not know, that of churchmen he knew nobody, and that his attitude to the Soviet power was negative on the exiling of the clergy and the closing and destruction of churches; and that being an anti-militarist he would not defend the Soviet Union with arms if it were attacked, but would agree to serve in the medical service. Thirty-nine people were arrested in that case. On 30 April 1931 the Special Conference gave him three years in a camp and he was sent to the Вишерские camps in the Perm country, where he died on 6 December 1931. All loved the meek and zealous ascetic; a cross was set over his grave, and for a long time it was still adorned with love by the confessors who were in the camp.
