# Onesiphorus of the Caves

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 9 ноября ст. ст. prints him as «Прп. Онисифора Печерского (1148).» — the venerable Onesiphorus of the Caves, with a year and nothing else. The name link behind the line gives the calendar's own nominative with the by-name and two grade words, «Онисифор Печерский, иеромонах, исповедник», and it is the only entry on this day's line of eleven and more that has a life page of its own behind it. azbyka.ru prints him «Прп. Онисифора Печерского, в Ближних пещерах, иеромонаха (1148)», naming the cave where his relics lie.

The life days.pravoslavie.ru serves is three sentences: he struggled in the Kiev Caves monastery, he was a presbyter, he was granted the gift of seeing into men, he died in 1148 and he was buried in the Near Caves beside [Spyridon of the Cave](/saints/spyridon-of-the-cave). azbyka prints that much and then the source behind it, which is why this folder can say more than three sentences without inventing any of it.

That source is a letter: «Послание смиренного Симона, епископа Владимирского и Суздальского, к Поликарпу, черноризцу Печерскому», the epistle of Simon, bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal, to Polycarp a monk of the Caves. Simon says he had the thing from the blessed elders of the Caves, and that they had it from men who saw it: «Слышал я правдивую вещь, – писал св. Симон, – от блаженных старцев Печерских; они же говорят от самовидцев этого чуда».

What the letter says is this. In the time of the abbacy of Pimen — «Во время игуменства Пимена (1132–1141)» — there was in the holy Caves monastery a man perfect in every virtue, Onesiphorus by name and a presbyter in rank, «пресвитер саном». He was granted by God the gift of discernment, so that he saw in the heart of every man the sins he had done. Many struggles of his were told, Simon writes, and he will tell one.

A monk of the house died suddenly, and a heavy stench began to come from his body; at the prayer of Onesiphorus the dead man's body ceased to stink. That is the whole of the one wonder the letter keeps, and it is the whole of what any page read here tells of him. He died in 1148, and his relics lie in the Antony caves, which are the Near ones.

**His name stands in the Church's own canon to the fathers of the Caves**, in the third ode, among those who saw what was to come: «Онисифоре блаженне, предвидевый вся и предвещая ясно, со Иеремием и Матфием, яко пророцы предвидящие, беды належащия скоро мя избавите молитвами вашими». The two names beside his there are two other clairvoyant fathers of the same house, whom the Russian calendar keeps on 5 октября.

**Why he is kept on this day is a guess azbyka prints as a guess**, and it is recorded here as one: his memory, besides the one he shares with all the venerable of the Antony caves, falls on «9/22 ноября (вероятно, в память его тезоименитства со св. мучеником Онисифором IV века)» — probably for his name-day with [Onesiphorus (9 November)](/saints/onesiphorus-martyr-9-november), the martyr of the fourth century whom this same day's first line keeps. The word is «вероятно», and no page read says it plainly.

**Two grade words sit on his name page and only one of them is written into this folder.** «Иеромонах» is the grade both hosts give him and it is what azbyka's own heading says, and it is no quarrel with the «пресвитер саном» of the letter or the «был пресвитером» of the shorter life, since a hieromonk is a presbyter in monastic orders. «Исповедник» is the other, and it is refused: the corpus's `confessor` is the man who confessed the faith under persecution, and nothing on any page read puts this twelfth-century monk under any persecution at all. Whether the word is used here in its other sense, of the father who hears confessions — which would sit oddly well on a man who saw the sins in men's hearts — is a reading nothing on these pages settles, so both readings are set down and no type is written for it.
