# Demetrianus the Deacon

saint.gr says all three saints were Cypriots and lived during the persecution of the Church under Maximian and Diocletian, whom the page brackets 245 to 310. In the year 302 Aristocleus, who came from Tamassos, at first did not want to put his own life in danger, because he wished to go on with the spiritual building up of his brothers in Christ; so he went up a mountain and hid himself in a cave.

But from there he kept learning that in the cities many of the faithful were boldly pouring out their blood for the holy faith of Christ. After a vision he saw, Aristocleus judged that it was not permitted to him, priest that he was, to be in safety while laymen, women and children defied the swords and the fire and watered the tree of the Gospel with their blood. So he made the sign of the cross and went down to Salamis of Cyprus.

He went to the church of the city, where he found the deacon Demetrianus and the reader Athanasius at prayer — the narrative calls the deacon Demetrios, where the page's own heading and the day's line both have Demetrianus. They prayed together, and after taking counsel all three went out and strengthened their persecuted brothers. As soon as the pagan governor learned of it he arrested them, and when he failed to overcome them with promises and with threats he beheaded them after many tortures. The page gives each of the three the year 302 and no year of birth, and prints no hymn under their line.

The day's line names all three of them — [Aristocleus of Tamassos](/saints/aristocleus-of-tamassos), Demetrianus the Deacon and [Athanasius the Reader](/saints/athanasius-the-reader) — so the entry is one folder for each, and the corpus already kept the first of them from the Romanian calendar.
