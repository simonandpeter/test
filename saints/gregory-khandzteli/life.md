# Gregory Khandzteli

Gregory was born in 759 at Kartala, the Romanian life says, of noble and pious parents, and was brought up at the palace by the wife of the ruler Nerses, his aunt. As a boy he would not taste wine or meat and was called “the hermit”; he learned to read and write in many languages, knew the Psalms by heart and studied the Fathers translated into Georgian. Against his own wish, for his youth, he was ordained priest. Soon after, fighting a fierce war of the flesh, he left secretly for the monastic life with three friends — his cousin Sabbas, later bishop of Ishan, Theodore, later founder of Nedzvi, and Christopher, later founder of Kuiriketi — and they stayed two years at the small monastery of the Forerunner at Opiza under its abbot George.

Longing for the hermit life, he came to Khandzteli in the region of Klarzeta, where an old hermit, Hvedie, had seen a cloud of light in the shape of a church and heard that the servant of God Gregory would build a monastery there. The four built cells, a refectory and a wooden church, brothers gathered, and they lived very strictly — a bed, a stool and a vessel in each cell, dried cabbage, bread and water, no fire in any cold, nights in prayer.

King Asot of the Bagratids gave the monastery arable land; when the king in old age took a young woman into his palace, Gregory rebuked him, and while the king was away took the woman, at her own tearful plea, to the convent of Mere under the abbess Febronia, where the king came after her and went away ashamed and repentant. Gregory travelled to Constantinople with Sabbas and a disciple to gather a rule for the services, and on the way home learned that Asot had been killed. Țkir, the unworthy bishop of Adsi, whom Gregory had rebuked, twice sought his death — an archer who fell at the saint’s feet and was forgiven, then a band hired to destroy the monastery, which turned back when Țkir himself died on the road.

He founded many monasteries across the land and set his best disciples over them. At 102 years, his sight and his strength undimmed, he called the brothers, blessed the monastery with the sign of the cross and died in peace on 6 October 861, the Romanian life says, after the Georgian Paterikon. The Greek calendar says only that he was the teacher of Ephraim the Great, whom it keeps on 17 April, and that it has no further details of his life.
