# Roman Medved

Roman Ivanovich Medved, archpriest and confessor, was born in Kholm province on 1 October 1874. With a theological education he gave himself to pastoral service, trying in everything to follow the example of his spiritual father, John of Kronstadt, who in 1900 married him to A. N. Nevzorova, who also became the Kronstadt lamp’s spiritual daughter; the same year he was ordained priest, and served first at the church of the Exaltation of the Cross in Chernigov province, in 1902–1903. From 1907 he served selflessly as rector of the Admiralty cathedral of St Vladimir at Sevastopol, and as protopresbyter of the Black Sea fleet had great authority among the sailors, with fifty ships’ priests under him; to dispel the revolutionary “romance” in the fleet he published a pamphlet on the temptations of revolution and the foundations of Orthodoxy, by his preaching ended the unrest of 1912 on the battleship St John Chrysostom, and there confessed the sailors condemned to be shot. On the eve of Christmas 1918, though, a sailor whom he had caught stealing church money, who had become chairman — the Sretensky calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. He died a confessor in 1937; the Russian calendar keeps him on 26 August (Julian) with the hieromartyr Victor and the martyrs Demetrius and Peter, and the finding of his relics on 21 July.
