# Thecla

When Paul and Barnabas preached in Iconium and lodged with Onesiphorus - the man Paul blesses by name in his letter to Timothy - a young woman named Thecla heard Paul speaking of purity and virginity, and resolved in her heart from that hour to leave her betrothed and all the sweetness of this world and serve Christ in virginity to the end of her life. The Romanian synaxarion's long life follows her from that window in Iconium through the trials the resolve cost her: she was condemned and delivered to the fire and to the beasts, and the fire did not touch her and the beasts would not, and she followed Paul and taught, which is why the Church calls her great martyr and equal of the apostles - the first woman to suffer for Christ, though her sufferings did not kill her.

She settled at last near Seleucia, healing the sick and turning many to Christ, until the physicians of the place, their trade ruined, sent shameless young men against the virgin in her old age. She who had not feared the beasts fled from them, and at her prayer the rock of the mountain opened by God's command and received her; the stone was both her protection and her tomb. She was ninety years old.

The Greek synaxarion keeps her on 24 September as equal to the apostles. It makes her the daughter of a pagan family of Iconium and gives her mother’s name, Theocleia, and has her turned to the faith by the preaching of the apostle Paul himself, which he gave in the neighbouring house of [Onesiphorus](/saints/onesiphorus-of-colophon). She was already betrothed to a pagan named Thamyris; when he and her mother saw that she had become a Christian they tried to make her take back her decision, and when that failed they denounced Paul to the governor Castillius, who imprisoned him and then expelled him. Thecla left her betrothed and followed the apostle to Antioch of Pisidia; many times she underwent torments, and her faith saved her. From Antioch she went to Myra in Lycia to meet Paul, and from there to Seleucia, where she took refuge on the mountain of Kalamon and fell asleep in peace at ninety. The Greek calendar keeps her with two apolytikia and a kontakion.
