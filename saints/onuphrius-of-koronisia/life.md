# Onuphrius of Koronisia

saint.gr says the information about him is very little, and that what there is has been preserved by the inhabitants of Koronisia themselves. «Θεοσεβέστατος και ταπεινός» — most god-fearing and humble — he lived as a monk in a monastery of the Nativity of the Theotokos at Koronisia. His humility was such, the page says, that the other monks, unable to understand his holiness, unhappily made a mock of him; and it was the appearance of two wonders that changed at once the picture they had of him.

By the first, he was found out by his fellow monks when he came back to the monastery from a little island called Vouvala, where they used to gather timber, and where the other monks had left him behind for a joke: after praying he threw his rason into the sea and travelled upon it to the island, using it for a boat. By the second, after his prayer he was given a beard that reached the ground, although he had been beardless.

The time of his repose is not exactly known. Over his grave a church was built in his honour, and the page adds — in the first person, as its source has it — that his memory is kept on 12 June together with his namesake [Onuphrius of Egypt](/saints/onuphrius-the-great). The page goes on to describe the little vaulted chapel of Saint Onuphrius itself, and cites as its source extracts from the book «ΑΓΙΟΙ ΤΗΣ ΗΠΕΙΡΟΥ» of Konstantinos Ir. Despotis, Parakatatheke, Thessalonica 2009, second edition.

It prints an apolytikion, a kontakion and a megalynarion for him, all of which name him and Koronisia, and the apolytikion tells the wonder of the tunic again.
