# Demetrius the Myrrh-streamer

Demetrius was born in the city of Thessalonica of well-born and right-believing parents. His father was voivode of the city and believed secretly in Christ, not daring to confess the name openly because of the persecution of the pagan emperors; he kept in his palace a hidden chamber of prayer with two icons adorned with gold and precious stones, one of the incarnate Saviour and one of His Most Holy Mother, before which he burned a lamp and incense and prayed with his wife. They were childless and grieved for it, and after long prayer a son was given them, at whose birth the whole of Thessalonica rejoiced with the voivode, who made a great feast for the city and above all for the poor.

When the boy was old enough to understand, his parents took him into their house of prayer, showed him the icons and taught him the faith, and he believed with his whole soul. The Romanian life follows him from there to his father's office, to his open preaching of Christ in the city, and to the prison into which he was thrown for it. There a scorpion was set against him, and he signed himself with the cross and trod on it, saying the words about treading on serpents and scorpions; and an angel appeared to him in a great light with a beautiful crown out of Paradise, saying: "Peace to thee, sufferer for Christ, Demetrius; take courage and be strong, and overcome thine enemies."

The emperor was meanwhile holding the public games, the five contests the Greeks called the pentathlon, from a high seat built for him. His champion was one Lyaeus, a Vandal, tall and strong and terrible to look at, who fought the bravest men and killed them by throwing them from a height onto spears, and killed Christians above all. A young Christian named Nestor, who knew Demetrius, was stirred to anger at it, ran to the prison and asked the saint's blessing and prayers that he might overcome the pitiless man. Demetrius signed him with the cross and said: "Go, and thou shalt overcome Lyaeus, confessing Christ."

Nestor went out and called Lyaeus to fight him. The emperor, seeing a handsome youth of about twenty, called him over and offered him riches if he would not go to his death; Nestor answered that he was not poor and did not wish to lose his life, but to fight Lyaeus and beat him. Then he cried "God of Demetrius, help me!", closed with him, threw him down on the sharpened spears and killed him. The emperor grieved for Lyaeus more than if he had lost his empire, and asked by what sorcery it had been done; Nestor said it was by the power of Christ, the true God, and was taken outside the golden gate and beheaded.

Learning that Demetrius had been the cause of Lyaeus's death, the emperor ordered that he be killed with spears, so that the one who caused it should die the death Lyaeus died. At daybreak on the twenty-sixth day of October the soldiers came into the prison and found him standing at prayer, and ran him through. The first spear went into his right side, the place where Christ was pierced on the cross, for when he saw the soldiers he lifted his right hand himself; and so, in the image of the Lord's passion, he gave up his soul. His body lay on the ground without honour until one of the faithful took it secretly by night and buried it.

His servant [Lupus](/saints/lupus-the-martyr) was present at his death and took his master's cloak, soaked in the blood, and dipped his ring in it too; and many wonders were worked with the cloak and the ring, healing every sickness and driving out evil spirits, until the emperor had him seized and beheaded, so that the good and faithful servant followed his master.

When the persecution ceased a small church stood over the grave, where healings were given. A great and believing nobleman of Illyricum named Leontius, carried there in an incurable sickness, was healed at once and resolved to build a great church in thanksgiving; and when the little church was pulled down and the foundation of the new one begun, the relics of Demetrius were found whole and incorrupt, streaming fragrant myrrh that filled the whole city, and a multitude of the sick were healed by the anointing with it. Leontius finished his church and laid the relics in it in a casket bound with gold.

The life closes with a vision. Christians coming from Thessalonica met a soldier on the road from Larissa, and the soldier greeted the bishop who was walking there: "Rejoice, Achillius, hierarch of God!" — and the bishop answered, "Rejoice thou also, soldier of Christ, Demetrius." Each told the other that God had commanded him to leave the city he guarded, for its sins, and that it would be given into the hands of the Hagarenes. Then both bowed their heads to the ground and wept, and kissed one another and took leave, and vanished; and within the month Thessalonica and Larissa were taken by the Turks.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 26 October, and saint.gr fills in the soldier's career and the name he is known by. He was the offspring of an aristocratic family at Thessalonica, it says, and rose quickly through the grades of the Roman army, so that at twenty-two he held the rank of chiliarch; serving as an officer under the tetrarch and later emperor Galerius Maximian, while Diocletian was emperor, he became a Christian and was imprisoned at Thessalonica in 303 for ignoring Diocletian's edict on the renunciation of Christianity — having shortly before founded a circle of young men for the study of the Scriptures. In the prison with him was a young Christian, [Nestor](/saints/nestor-of-thessalonica), who was to meet the dreaded gladiator Lyaios in single combat; before the fight he visited Demetrius and asked his help, and Demetrius gave him his blessing, with the result that Nestor beat Lyaios and so provoked the emperor's anger, upon which both of them, Nestor and Demetrius, were ordered to be put to death. The writers of his encomia — Eustathius of Thessalonica, Gregory Palamas and Demetrius Chrysoloras — record that his body was buried at the place of his martyrdom, and that the grave turned into a deep well which welled up myrrh, whence his surname of the Myrrh-streamer. In Byzantine icons and in modern iconography, the page adds, he is often shown as a horseman on a red horse, as against the white horse of Saint George, trampling the unbelieving Lyaios; and he is honoured today as the patron saint of Thessalonica, together with thirteen other towns the page names.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day and tells the story at length, with particulars the Greek and Romanian pages do not carry. His father was a Roman proconsul at Thessalonica and both his parents were secret Christians, so that he was baptised and taught the faith in the house church inside the proconsul's own residence. When his father died and he had come of age, the emperor Galerius Maximian, who took the throne in 305, questioned him, found him learned and able in war and government, and set him in his father's place over the province.

The charge laid on him was to hold the city against the barbarians and to wipe out Christianity, and the emperor's will was put in one sentence: «Предавай смерти каждого, кто призывает имя Распятого» — put to death everyone who calls on the name of the Crucified. He went back to Thessalonica and confessed Christ before them all instead, teaching the city openly and pulling down its heathen customs; the compiler of the life calls him for that zeal a second Apostle Paul, because it was Paul who had founded the first community of believers there.

What follows is the day's own telling of the end. Warned that the emperor was coming through the city, he had his servant [Lupus](/saints/lupus-the-martyr) give away his estate to the poor — «Раздели богатство земное между ними — будем искать себе богатства небесного» — and gave himself to fasting and prayer. He was summoned, confessed, and was shut in prison, where the page says an angel came down to comfort him. The emperor meanwhile amused himself with the games, where his favourite strong man, a German named Lyaeus, threw the Christians he beat down from the platform onto the soldiers' spears.

[Nestor](/saints/nestor-of-thessalonica), a young man of the city, came to him in the prison and asked his blessing to fight the man; he threw Lyaeus down onto the spears, and was executed for it. The guard was sent to the prison the same night, and at dawn on 26 October 306 they ran Demetrius through with their spears. Lupus caught the blood on a cloth, drew the imperial ring from his finger and dipped that in it too, and began healing the sick with them until he was killed in his turn.

A church went up over the grave in the reign of the emperor Constantine, and a hundred years later, while a greater one was being built in its place, the incorrupt relics were found. From the seventh century the fragrant myrrh began to flow at his tomb, which is what gave him the name; the page quotes a fourteenth-century description of it — the myrrh «по свойству своему не вода, но гуще ее и не похоже ни на одно из известных нам веществ» — by nature not water but thicker, and like nothing else known.

The last part of the page is about the Slavs, and it is the reason this calendar is worth reading on him. Attackers were turned back from the walls more than once by the sight of a terrible shining youth walking round them; the Russian chronicle's first pages have the Greeks saying of Oleg's defeat at Constantinople in 907 that it was not Oleg but Demetrius sent against them from God; a monastery under his name was founded at Kyiv in the early 1070s, and a cathedral at Vladimir between 1194 and 1197, whose icon of him was painted on a board brought from Thessalonica in 1197. The page even reports an opinion that his parents were of Slavic descent, and says that the Greeks for their part reckoned him a Slavic saint.
