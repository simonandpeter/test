# Constantine of Kappoua

In the dark years of the Turkish rule, saint.gr begins, thousands of Christians were martyred, and one of them is Constantine, the new martyr of the Thessalian Kappoua. Before his baptism he was a Muslim, the son of a Turkish official, and his name was Saïm. At twenty he was instructed in the Christian doctrines by a learned monk of the Kappoua monastery of Saint Nicholas, who, when he judged that the young man's faith in Christ had taken root, baptised him there and renamed him Constantine.

His father's answer was immediate and violent. He meant to put the three monks of the monastery to death and failed, because they escaped to the Meteora and asked the monks there for shelter; so he burned the monastery instead. Enraged at his son's firmness, he ordered the soldiers of his guard to throw him into a dungeon and not to bring him out unless he returned to the faith of his fathers.

They did as they were told, and morning and evening they asked him whether he repented of his apostasy. Constantine answered them with sweetness and steadiness: «Το Χριστό μου δεν θα τον αρνηθώ ποτέ ο, τι και να μου κάνετε· δεν φοβάμαι τίποτα· είναι δίπλα μου και με ενισχύει» — my Christ I will never deny, whatever you do to me; I fear nothing; he is beside me and he strengthens me. Harsher tortures followed, and then the threat of the gallows, and he answered that nothing could separate him from the Christ he loved so much that he did not mind giving his life for that love.

They led him out of the town to the place where the village of Kappa now stands, threw a rope over a thick branch of the great plane tree there, and hanged him; but the rope broke three times, and at the order of his father, who was standing by, the executioners beheaded him instead. Christians buried his body near the place of his martyrdom, at the spot called «Τρία Δέντρα», the Three Trees. He was martyred on 18 August 1610.
