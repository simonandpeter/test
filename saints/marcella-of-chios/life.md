# Marcella of Chios

Marcella was born in the north-west of the island of Chios in the fifteenth century, of a Christian mother and a pagan father. She lost her mother while she was small, but inherited from her the love of God. Her father, a worshipper of idols, took fire with a carnal desire for his own daughter; and the girl, understanding what he was about, fled into the mountains nearby.

He came after her in a rage and set the bushes alight to drive her out of her hiding place, and wounded her with a spear as she ran. Marcella prayed the Lord to split a rock open that she might go into it and her body be left undefiled. The Lord heard her, and the stone closed about her as far as the breast.

Seeing the wonder, her father cut off the virgin's breasts, and then her head, and threw it into the sea. A holy light took hold of the martyr's head, and the rock became a place of pilgrimage for the faithful of the whole world. In her own country, on the beach of the bay called Volissos, there is a church dedicated to her, where the page says people find healing of soul and body; and a particle of her holy relics is in Iași county too, at the monastery of the Holy Rock, the Tărâța skete, at Bârnova.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 22 July, and saint.gr tells the martyrdom as doxologia tells it, with a few things added. She was born and grew up at Volissos, the historic large village of north-western Chios. On when she was born, lived and suffered, the page says plainly that there is confusion and vagueness among her biographers; according to her own biographer, the venerable Nikephoros of Chios, she lived and flourished about the year 1500. Her father was an idolater and her Christian mother died young. When Marcella saw what her father had become she left the family house and sought refuge in the mountains of the district; a great bush hid her, until a shepherd saw her and pointed the bush out, and the father set fire to it. She found a way out and ran over the stones and rocks, and he, seeing how hard it was to reach her, shot an arrow at her, so that her pure blood watered the rocks. When her bodily strength failed she prayed, with her eyes turned to the heavenly Bridegroom, that the rock would split and hide her; and it did, and received her body as far as the breast. Her father, coming up and seeing it, cut off her breasts with a knife and threw them onto the mountain, then beheaded his daughter and threw the head into the sea, from which, the tradition says, an unusual brightness began to shine.

The split rock is still a place of pilgrimage and a spring of healings, and those who pray there with faith see the red colouring of the rocks and the water steaming. The page says that witnesses of her wonders have included bright spiritual figures of the Church — [Macarius Notaras](/saints/macarius-notaras), bishop of Corinth, [Nektarios](/saints/nektarios-of-aegina), bishop of Pentapolis, and Nikephoros of Chios, her biographer and the composer of her service — who often came to the place of her martyrdom to pray. She is the patron of Chios; a portion of her relics is at the Monastery of Daou Pentelis; a great festival is kept at her church on the sandy shore of the bay named after her at Volissos, and churches and chapels stand in her name at Botanikos in Athens, at Kato Kifissia, and on Spetses, Andros, Milos, Santorini and Samos.
