# Reginus in Cyprus

saint.gr says plainly at the outset that Reginus and [Orestes](/saints/orestes-in-cyprus) are unknown to the synaxarists, and that what it has of them comes from their own Service. The two are kept together on 20 August under the epithet the page gives them, «οι εν Κύπρω», the ones in Cyprus, and the page tells their story as one story throughout.

They came, the page says, from Chalcedon — more likely Carthage — where they were seized by the governor and imprisoned because they were Christians. When they were set free they took refuge in Cyprus, and there, near Limassol, they were martyred for their Christian faith. Their memory was kept in Cyprus, where they suffered that death at the hands of the idolaters.

saint.gr gives no year, no account of the trial and no manner of death, and it says where its information comes from: their Service, published at Limassol in 1902, with a reference to Delehaye's *Les Saints de Chypre*, and a pamphlet of the presbyter Charalambos Neophytou printed at Limassol in 1995. It records a portion of the relic of each of the two at the church of the Panagia at Phasoula in Cyprus, and a church of the pair at Apliki in Pitsilia.

The service it prints calls them the pair of martyrs and the glory of the Trinity, and its megalynarion remembers them as the two contenders «οἱ ἐκ Χαλκηδόνα ἀριστεύσαντες ἱερῶς» — who did valiantly and in holiness, out of Chalcedon.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 20 Αυγούστου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/08/20/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2305/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026.*
