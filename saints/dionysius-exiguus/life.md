# Dionysius Exiguus

Dionysius Exiguus — Dionysius the Small, or the Humble — was a monk from Scythia Minor, today’s Dobrogea, who lived in the fifth and sixth centuries. He spent the greater part of his life at Rome, where he was abbot of a monastery, and is remembered as a renowned translator of theological works from Greek into Latin, and as the inventor of the Christian era, the count of years from the birth of Christ. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 1 September with a cross; the Russian calendar keeps him the same day, marked as a saint of the Romanian church, dead before 556.
