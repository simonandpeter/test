# Mitrophan

Mitrophan, bishop of Voronezh — Michael in the world — was born on
8 November 1623. In the memorial book that was his the list of names begins
with men in priestly orders, which gives ground to think he was born into a
family of hereditary priests, and his spiritual testament says he was born
of devout parents and raised by them in the blameless piety of the Eastern
church. Until forty he lived in the world, married, with a son John, a
parish priest at the village of Sidorovskoye on the Molokhta near Shuya.
Widowed, he was tonsured Mitrophan at the Zolotnikovo hermitage in 1663 —
its memorial book begins his family “the line of the black priest
Mitrophan of Sidorovskoye” — and after three years was chosen igumen of the
Kosmin monastery at Yakhroma, which he ruled ten years, building its church
of the Saviour Not Made by Hands. Patriarch Joachim, hearing of his piety,
made him archimandrite in 1675 of the famous Makariev monastery on the
Unzha, where he built the Annunciation church with its refectory and bell
tower; and when the Moscow council of 1681–1682, against the Old Believer
schism and for the better instruction of the people, resolved to open new
sees — Voronezh, Tambov, Kholmogory and Veliky Ustyug — he was summoned to
the capital and consecrated bishop of Voronezh by the patriarch and sixteen
bishops on 2 April 1682.

His episcopate began in the hard years of the troubles and the schism. His
first act at Voronezh was a circular to his clergy calling them to amend
their lives: honourable priests of God most high, leaders of Christ’s
flock, you must have bright eyes of the mind, lit by understanding, to lead
others on the right road; Christ, handing his flock to his apostle, said
three times, feed, as if to say there are three ways of feeding — the word
of teaching, prayer with the help of the holy Mysteries, and the example of
a life; give the example, teach your people, pray for them and strengthen
them with the Mysteries, enlighten the unbelieving by baptism and bring
sinners to repentance, and see that the sick do not leave this life without
communion and holy oil. He built a new stone cathedral of the Annunciation
in place of the old wooden one, consecrated in 1692; in his twenty years the
churches of the diocese grew from 182 to 239 and two monasteries were
founded; he rooted disorder out of the existing ones and held them to the
rule. He comforted rich and poor, was the protector of widows and orphans
and the defender of the wronged, his house a hostel for strangers and a
hospital for the sick, and he prayed for the dead as for the living, above
all for the soldiers fallen for the fatherland, whose names he wrote in his
memorial book. His friendship with Pitirim of Tambov was close — they
wrote, and met for spiritual talk, and on 15 September 1688, walking
together with a priest Basil in the places where Pitirim prayed alone,
chose the site of the Tregulyaev monastery of the Forerunner. A patriot of
high feeling, he backed Peter’s reforms with his authority, his mercy and
his prayers, understanding their necessity, urged the people to help build
the fleet for Azov at Voronezh when many thought a fleet useless, and gave
all he had to the treasury for it — and would not go to the tsar’s palace
because pagan statues stood there, and held firm under threat of disgrace
until Peter had them removed, and respected him the more.

He died in 1703 in great old age, having taken the schema as Macarius, and
was buried on 4 December, Peter himself carrying the coffin from the
cathedral to the vault: I have no such holy elder left, he said; eternal
memory to him. His testament survives — by God’s judgements I have reached
old age and my natural strength fails, so I have judged it right to write
this last writing; when my sinful soul is loosed from the bond of the flesh
I commit it to the mercy of the all-wise God who made it, and my sinful
bones to the mother of all, awaiting from there the resurrection of the
dead — with its rule for every man from the wise: use labour, keep measure,
and you will be rich; drink with restraint, eat little, and you will be
healthy; do good, flee evil, and you will be saved. His memory was
established in 1832. The Russian calendar keeps on 4 September (Julian) the
second finding of his relics in 1964 and their translation in 1989, with a
troparion and kontakion.
