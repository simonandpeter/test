# Stephen

Stephen — Stepan Ivanovich Kuskov in the world — was born on 10 December
1870 in the town of Vyshny Volochyok in Tver province, the son of the owner
of a turner’s workshop. After school he entered a monastery, where he bore
the obedience of a singer, and in 1898 took the tonsure. The civil strife
that followed the revolution of 1917 found him at Petropavlovsk, cut off
from the Tver diocese by the front, where he found a place as psalmist in a
church. The town was taken now by the Reds, now by the Whites; some priests
left with the retreating White units, others died as martyrs at the
Bolsheviks’ hands, priests grew scarce, and the monk Stephen was ordained
hieromonk. When the Whites left Petropavlovsk he went first to the Kuban,
then home to the Tver diocese, which sent him to the church of Nikolskoye
in the Beloomut district. The pressure on believers and clergy grew year by
year: after Easter 1934 he went round the peasants’ houses with molebens as
he always had, and the village soviet, which had not objected before,
threatened to arrest him if he did it again; in 1936 the state began a new
persecution aimed squarely at the Orthodox church, stripped many bishops and
clergy of their registration and so forbade them to serve on pain of
immediate arrest, and he lost his place in the church. He was among the
first arrested once the state had decided on the extermination of the
Orthodox priesthood. The day after his arrest, 7 August 1937, the
investigator questioned the woman whose room he rented, who said she had
heard him complain that the authorities mocked the people, making them work
day and night, worse than under serfdom; and the chairman of the village
soviet, who said the priest went round the houses on the feasts though
forbidden, that what he said there was unknown, but that it could be assumed
he did anti-Soviet agitation, since political campaigns in the village were
so hard to carry through. Then the priest: told he had stirred up the
faithful against Soviet power, he said he had done no counter-revolutionary
work; told he had gone from yard to yard under cover of rites, agitating
about a coming war and the fall of the regime, he said he had indeed gone
round the faithful’s yards to perform the rites, for which the chairman had
stopped and forbidden him, but had done no agitation against Soviet power
and admitted no guilt in it; told he knew of a counter-revolutionary
organisation run by former landowners, he said he knew of none, and knew no
landowners. The same day the investigator wrote his indictment: that the
priest “conducts insurrectionary agitation, systematically agitating among
the collective farmers about a coming war and the ruin of Soviet power”. On
13 September the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on
17 September 1937 — 4 September of the old style, the day the Russian
calendar keeps him, with the bishops Gregory and Sergius and their company.
The Jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000 numbered him among the new
martyrs and confessors of Russia.
