# Emmelia

saint.gr says Emmelia came of a devout family of Caesarea in Cappadocia, and that her father was shown forth as a martyr in the last persecutions. Her life, the page says, was the good root from which the sweetest fruits sprang: her children, who became eminent members of society and most of them saints of the Church — [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great), [Gregory of Nyssa](/saints/gregory-of-nyssa), [Peter of Sebaste](/saints/peter-of-sebaste), the nun [Macrina](/saints/macrina-sister-of-basil-the-great) and the monk [Naucratius](/saints/naucratius-brother-of-basil). From a holy root came sanctified shoots: from holy parents, blessed and holy children.

She was tried, as the chosen usually are, with many sorrows. Her parents died before she was even married; her husband died just as their son Peter was born; her son Naucratius died untimely; and from a certain point onward she had to bring up her children alone, in the nurture and admonition of the Lord. She met all of it, the page says, with exemplary faith, courage and patience. She taught her children chiefly by her own example, and gave them, along with her own milk, the unadulterated milk of the faith, and taught them the mystery of the Church.

She ended her life as a nun, with her daughter Macrina as her abbess. That is the whole of the prose; the page gives no year for her birth or her death, and names her father not at all. The one hymn it prints names her husband, [Basil](/saints/basil-the-elder), whom the prose leaves unnamed, and speaks of the ascents she made in the wilderness together with her own children.
