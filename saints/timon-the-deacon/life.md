# Timon the Deacon

In the days of the holy apostles, after the Lord's Ascension and the receiving of the Holy Spirit, while they had not yet scattered through the whole world to preach Christ and the churches of the faithful in Jerusalem had grown many, seven men full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom were chosen for the service of the diaconate: Stephen, Philip, [Prochorus](/saints/prochorus-the-deacon), [Nicanor](/saints/nicanor-the-deacon), Timon, [Parmenas](/saints/parmenas-the-deacon) and Nicolas of Antioch. The apostles prayed, laid their hands on them and made them deacons.

Of these, the memory of the first martyr and archdeacon Stephen is kept on 27 December and that of Philip on 11 October; the rest — except Nicolas, who is not written in the number of the saints — the holy Church has been accustomed to keep on this day, although they ended by suffering at different times, preaching Christ apostolically in various places.

After the service of the diaconate, Timon was set by the holy apostles as bishop of the city of Bostra in Arabia, and preaching the name of Christ he suffered much from the Jews and from the Greeks. Thrown into a heated furnace, he came out of the fire unharmed; and the Roman martyrology says of him that after he was unhurt by the fire he was crucified, and so ended.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 28 July, and keeps the four deacons together. saint.gr says Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon and Parmenas — apostles of the Seventy — were among the seven chosen deacons of the first Christian Church of Jerusalem, in Acts 6:5, and that their work was to serve and to see to the feeding of the poor members of the Church, especially the orphans and the widows, while they served in the spreading of the divine word as well. Of Timon it says that he suffered a martyr's death at Bostra in Arabia, where he had been sent to serve the Gospel. Above the notice the page sets a distich for each man, and his own runs: «Tιμῶ σέ, Tίμων, ὡς Ἀποστόλων ἕνα, Ὡς Mαρτύρων τε τῶν κεκαυμένων ἕνα» — I honour you, Timon, as one of the Apostles, and as one of the martyrs who were burned. The page gives no year, no emperor and no judge for any of the four.
