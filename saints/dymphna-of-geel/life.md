# Dymphna of Geel

saint.gr says that Dymphna — Dympna or Dimpna, and Δάφνη in the page's own Greek — was the daughter of the local king of some region of Ireland in the seventh century. Her father was a pagan and her mother a Christian, and so the girl came to love Christianity and was baptised secretly, without her father's knowing. Her life was a deeply spiritual one, given to Christ, and she made a promise of chastity.

After her mother's death the king mourned terribly, but in time wished to marry again, and set his men to find him a beautiful and good wife. They searched, and told him there was no woman in his kingdom more beautiful or more prudent than his own daughter; and the king then resolved to marry her.

Faced with that dreadful trial, Dymphna fled with the help of her spiritual father, Saint [Gerebernus](/saints/gerebernus-of-geel) — Gerebran, as the page also spells him. But the king pursued them, found them in a small village called Cheel, or Geel, near the present-day Antwerp, and killed them both. It was 15 May, somewhere between 620 and 640, and she was fifteen years old.

Soon wonders began to be recorded at her tomb, the page says, chiefly healings of the mentally ill, and many began to come to Geel and venerate there, so that a tradition of charity, of hospitality and of care for people with mental illness grew up in that community and continues to this day. Her biography was drawn up in the thirteenth century, and many of her miracles are also recorded. She is a saint of the West's Orthodox past, the page adds, and although she is honoured especially by the Western Church she is an Orthodox saint.
