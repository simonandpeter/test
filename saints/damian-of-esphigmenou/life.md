# Damian of Esphigmenou

Damian lived in the thirteenth century and holds an important place, doxologia's life says, among the saints of the monastery of Esphigmenou on the Holy Mountain. Wanting to be a monk while he was still young, he went to Athos, to the Garden of the Mother of God, and took up a life of hard spiritual labour on Mount Samaria, which lies between the monasteries of Esphigmenou and Hilandar, near the cave where [Anthony of the Kyiv Caves](/saints/anthony-of-the-caves) had struggled before he founded the Lavra of the Caves at Kyiv.

It is told of him that he went once to [Cosmas of Zographou](/saints/cosmas-of-zographou) and stayed so long that the sun had set. Cosmas asked him to spend the night there, but Damian would not, for it was his habit to pass the night in his own cell. Halfway back a storm came on so heavily that he could see nothing and stood in danger; and lifting the eyes of his mind to the Mother of God, he prayed to be delivered out of it. While he was still praying he found himself caught up and set down at the door of his own cell.

He fell asleep in peace on 23 February in the year 1280, and for forty days after his passing a fragrance beyond telling was felt through the whole valley around the monastery. The page prints no hymn for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and is careful about what it does not know: when and where Damian was born, saint.gr says, we do not know, and his life survives in a later manuscript of the monastery of Esphigmenou; the rest of the page is given «σύμφωνα με προφορική παράδοση», by oral tradition. By that tradition he left the things of the world while still young and became a monk at Esphigmenou on the Holy Mountain, where he was a type and a pattern of a monk to the monks there, and with the abbot's leave he withdrew for greater ascesis to the mountain opposite the community, the one they called Samareia. Once he went to a monk friend of his and did not find him in his cell, and sat waiting until he came in the evening; and when they had talked and he set off home the hour was late and a torrential rain had begun outside. But because his elder had told him never to sleep away from his hut, he made a perfect obedience and set out for his cell in that weather; and at some point he lost his way and could not take a step forward for the downpour, and lifted his voice at once to God — «Κύριε σώσε με, χάνομαι», Lord save me, I am lost — and the wonder happened, and he found himself, without understanding how, in front of his own cell. So, in a manner pleasing to God, he died peacefully in 1280; and for forty days after his repose a marvellous fragrance of myrrh came out of his grave, which the fathers at Esphigmenou could smell a mile off, and they glorified God.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him five sentences: a monk of the monastery of Esphigmenou on the Holy Mountain, a contemporary and companion of [Cosmas of Zographou](/saints/cosmas-of-zographou), who struggled on the mountain of Samaria between Esphigmenou and Hilandar. He fell asleep peacefully in 1280, and for forty days afterwards a wonderful and fragrant scent came out of his grave.
