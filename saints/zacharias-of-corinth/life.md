# Zacharias of Corinth

Zacharias was bishop of Corinth, and he was denounced to the Turks — falsely, saint.gr says — as working with the Venetians during Morosini's campaign in Greece for the liberation of the Greeks. He was arrested, and after being tortured harshly he was brought before the judge, who urged him to deny Christ and become a Muslim. Zacharias heard the proposal with disgust, and for that he was tortured horribly.

He was condemned to be burned alive in the fire, turned on a spit. The Christians of Corinth managed, by offering a large sum of money to the Turkish governor, to have that dreadful manner of execution changed; and so the new hieromartyr Zacharias was beheaded at Corinth on 30 March 1684, the Sunday of the Veneration of the Cross. The page gives no year for his birth, and heads him with a question mark where it would stand.

It says nothing of his monastic house, his election or his years in the see, and prints no hymn, though it names a service of his own; it sets him among the Synaxis of the New Martyrs who suffered after the fall of Constantinople. Its couplet gives him both crowns at once: «Δέχου στεφάνους ἐκ Θεοῦ Ζαχαρία, Ὡς Ἱεράρχης καὶ ἀθλητὴς Κυρίου» — receive crowns from God, Zacharias, as a hierarch and as a contender of the Lord.
