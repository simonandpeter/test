# Thecla of Aza

Thecla, [Mariamne](/saints/mariamne-of-aza), [Martha](/saints/martha-of-aza), [Mary](/saints/mary-of-aza) and [Ennatha](/saints/ennatha-of-aza) were five canonical virgins — nuns — who lived in the years of the Persian king Shapur, about 330, with a priest named Paul near a village called Aza. Paul was rich and loved money, and he served and sang with them and hoarded for himself whatever the virgins gave him. When it became known how rich he was, the Persians demanded, on pain of death, that he and the virgins with him either deny their faith or hand over his treasure. He chose his wealth and took up the religion of the Persians, and the five, who would not follow him and deny Christ, he slaughtered with his own hands; but he did not enjoy his riches, for the next night the Persians strangled him to take them.

The Greek synaxarion tells this on 9 June, and on 26 September, where their memory is repeated, says only that their money-loving spiritual father killed them.

The Romanian calendar keeps this company on 9 iunie, where doxologia.ro prints the five names on five separate lines — Mariamni, Tecla, Maria, Enata and Marta — each with a page that gives the name and the day and no life at all. It is the set of five names together, and not any one of them, that identifies the company there.

The Russian calendar keeps the company on the Julian 9 June and names three of the five in its line, «Мцц. Феклы , Марфы и Марии Персидских (346).» Behind that line it prints a single sentence, which is the whole of its account: the holy martyrs Thecla, Martha and Mary were cut down by the sword for Christ on 6 June of the year 346, under the king Shapur II, in Persia. Two things there differ from what this folder holds, and both are left standing side by side rather than reconciled. The year is 346 where the Romanian and Greek pages gave about 330; and the day of the death is 6 June, three days before the day the same calendar keeps the feast on. Its name page links an article that names all five virgins and the man who pressed them to worship the sun, and that article was not read.
