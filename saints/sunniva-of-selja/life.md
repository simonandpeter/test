# Sunniva of Selja

Sunniva was the first Christian to settle in Norway, doxologia says. She fled from Ireland to escape marriage to a pagan king, and set out with a number of her people in three boats, putting all her hope in the Lord; they left Ireland without oars, and God brought them to the island of Selja off the western coast of Norway, where they settled in a cave.

After a time the local ruler, Håkon Jarl, accused them of stealing his sheep and set out to take them and kill them. Sunniva prayed the Lord to deliver them out of the pagan ruler's hand, and while she prayed a mass of stones fell and blocked the mouth of the cave; and so Sunniva and those with her came to their end.

Her relics were discovered when the Christian king Olaf dug in the cave in the year 996, and in that same year he declared the island of Selja the first holy place in Norway, so that Sunniva was canonised as the first saint of that country. Her feast is kept on 8 July, the day her relics were found. The page gives no year for her death and does not name her companions.
