# Arcadius (Dobronravov)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him on 7 апреля ст. ст. in four words, «Сщмч. Аркадия пресвитера (1933).», and carries no life for him; the name link behind the line, page 14452, gives the surname and the rank the line withholds, «Аркадий (Добронравов), протоиерей, сщмч.», and lists beside this day only the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs on 26 января. azbyka.ru’s calendar for the same civil day has the life, taken there from the database of the St Tikhon’s University, and adds a day of its own, the moveable synaxis of the saints of the Chuvash metropolia — a synaxis, and not written into the feast.

He was born in 1868 in the village Устье in the Ядринский uyezd of the province of Kazan — now, the page says, the Аликовский district of Chuvashia — the son of a deacon, «Павла Добронравова». In 1889 he finished the Kazan seminary and was ordained priest to the village Пичкасы in the Спасский uyezd of the same province; in 1893 he had the blessing of the Holy Synod with a certificate for zealous labours in the building of a new church, and in 1894 the thigh-shield. In 1896, at his own petition, he was moved to the church of the Kazan Mother of God in the town of Цивильск.

The page then prints the whole of his record of awards, which is the shape a parish priest’s thirty-five years take in such a document: the skufia in 1898, and in the same year the thanks of the Kazan diocesan school council entered on his form for a diligent attitude to the church-school business; a silver medal on the Alexander ribbon, a silver medal for work with the Red Cross during the war with Japan, a silver medal on the double Alexander ribbon; the blessing of the Synod with a certificate for excellent service in 1905; another Red Cross medal in 1907 and a silver medal on the double Vladimir and Alexander ribbons in 1909; the kamilavka in 1909; the order of Saint Anna of the third degree in 1912, and the rank of archpriest.

In 1925 he was dismissed from his place for a time by a renovationist bishop; and a bishop of Alatyr wished to elect him to the diocesan council and asked the authorities’ leave for it. Having served thirty-five years without a break in that one church, he was arrested on 30 April 1931. The witnesses the page quotes are a catalogue of what he said from the ambo: that he had shown counter-revolutionary sabotage straight after the revolution and said openly in church that the soviet power was antichrist’s; that on 19 December 1929 he preached on *Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness’ sake* and said that under the soviet power many had suffered innocently in prisons and exile for the truth and for the Gospel.

And that on 6 January 1931, at the funeral of a dead abbess, he said that the civil power had had greater pillars than she in its hands, and that great men of the Church, governing monasteries, had been forced under its pressure to give them up. The same witness, a priest of the town’s cathedral, reported a conversation of 26 April about the closing of a church, in which he had said: **«пока я жив - церковь не закроют»** — while I am alive they will not close the church, because there are people who will stand up for me and for it; and that on the fourth day of Pascha he had walked to a neighbouring village where the peasants had voted unanimously to close their church, and after he had been there they began to go back on their own resolution.

The search took away the notes of the sermons he had read at the services of that last Great Lent, and the page prints two of them. In one he wrote that unbelief was growing, and that the enmity against the Church of Christ which began in the first days of its existence was not ceasing but strengthening: that Christ mocked was being led to Golgotha again, that the wild cries of *crucify him* were heard again, meaning that Christianity must be destroyed and wiped from the face of the earth; that men gathered again and, like the scribes, taught the people to ask Pilate to release the robber and crucify Christ — taught the people to renounce Christ and the Christian faith and the Church; and that everything was being used to root out faith, mockery of the Church’s pious institutions, of Pascha, of going to church, of fasting, of communion, of the crowning of marriage.

The other was on the Sunday Gospel of the woman who anointed the Saviour’s feet with myrrh and wiped them with her hair, and it asks his people whether they repent of their sins as she repented: whether they fall down in humility and the knowledge of their sinfulness at the feet of Jesus Christ, seeking mercy; whether they weep over their sins as she wept. With grief, he says, we must own that we shall hardly notice anything of the kind among us: we all live in sins, but we have no sense of our sinfulness, our conscience sleeps, we feel no need of repentance; we fast unwillingly and confess insincerely, and therefore we shall not hear the encouraging voice of the Saviour, *go in peace, thy faith hath saved thee*.

He was interrogated on 1 May 1931 and did not acknowledge himself guilty of anti-soviet agitation. On 8 May the prison doctor certified that the citizen Dobronravov, sixty-four years old, suffered from insufficient action of the heart from chronic myocarditis, with breathlessness on exertion, and could not therefore walk long distances. The same day the investigation was declared closed, the charge being systematic anti-soviet agitation in the form of sermons, using the faithful in church for it; and asked what he wished to add he said that he had carried on no anti-soviet agitation and had wanted only to say that the teaching of God should not be let fall among the faithful.

On 10 June 1931 a special troika of the OGPU of the Nizhny Novgorod Territory sentenced him to five years in a corrective labour camp, and he served it in the Темниковский camp in Mordovia. **Not bearing the heavy conditions of the imprisonment, he died on 20 April 1933**, the camp doctor giving the cause as decompensated myocarditis and psychosis; 20 April 1933 is the civil date of the Julian 7 April, so this calendar keeps him on the day he died. He was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia by the Holy Synod on 27 December 2007 — later than the Jubilee Council that numbered most of the men and women of these April days.
