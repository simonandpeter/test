# John, companion of Simeon

In the days of the right-believing emperor Justinian, when the peoples who love Christ were coming to the holy city of Jerusalem for the feast of the Exaltation of the honoured and life-giving Cross of the Lord, two young men set out from Syria by God's providence to venerate the honoured wood of the holy Cross. One was named John and the other [Simeon](/saints/simeon-the-fool-for-christ), and both were of good family and rich. John was twenty-four years old and had a young wife, and lived beside his aged father, his mother having died; Simeon was unmarried and had a widowed mother, an old woman of eighty.

The two had bound themselves together in the love of Christ, being of one country, and they stayed many days in Jerusalem, visiting the holy places together and venerating. On their way home they went down into the valley of Jericho and, travelling under the mountain, passed by the city; and seeing the monasteries around the holy Jordan, John said to Simeon: Do you know who lives in those dwellings? Who lives in them, said Simeon. The angels of God live there, John answered. Simeon wondered, and sighed, and asked whether they might see them; and John said that if they were willing to take up a life like theirs, they would truly have the sweetness of their faces and their words.

They were both on horseback; they dismounted and gave the horses to their servants, telling them to go slowly on ahead. So the servants went before with the horses while the two followed at a distance, talking of how they might save their souls. Going slowly they came to a crossroads, where one road led towards Syria, the way they were bound, and another went towards the Jordan, where those monasteries could be seen. And John said to Simeon, pointing with his finger at the road that led to the Jordan: This is the way that leads to life — and at the road towards Syria: This is the way that leads to death. Let us stand at this crossroads, brother, and pray God to guide us which to take.

The Romanian page prints one long life for the two of them, and the greater part of it is left here unsummarised; its end belongs to Simeon, who left the desert for the city of Emesa and hid his holiness under the appearance of madness. When the people learned after his death of the wonders God had worked through him, of his prophecies and of his much-tormented life, they knew that the madman had not been mad but wiser than all the wise of this age, and that the one who seemed a sinner was righteous and holy, hiding under the look of folly and of sin a life made wise by God and pleasing to Him. As Lot of old lived among the men of Sodom and was not defiled by their sins, so this new Lot, living in the middle of the world, took no hurt from the passions of the world.

Simeon ended his life on the twenty-first day of the month of July, and after him John, his companion in the desert, fell asleep with a blessed end in the wilderness of the Jordan. As they had begun together to serve the Lord on earth, so in heaven they stood together before the throne of God. The life of both of them Simeon's own holy and truthful mouth told to John the deacon of the church in Emesa, and that deacon told it faithfully and truly to the great father Leontius, bishop of Neapolis in Cyprus, who put it in writing.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 21 July, named in one line with Simeon as the Romanian names him. saint.gr adds the year. The venerable fathers [Simeon](/saints/simeon-the-fool-for-christ) and John, it says, came from Edessa of Syria and lived in the years of the emperor Justin the younger, about 518. They were great friends, and decided to go together to Jerusalem to venerate the Holy Places; having done so they went to the monastery of Saint Gerasimus and were made monks there by the venerable Nikon. Before seven days were out in the monastery they left for the desert, and there they stayed together forty years under a hard ascetic rule. Then Simeon went back to Jerusalem, prayed to remain unknown, and at Emesa in Syria passed for a simpleton and worked many miracles, dying poor and ill-used. John came to Emesa later, learned there of Simeon's death, and after a little while fell asleep himself. The synaxarion's verse for John says that he chose the desert, and by it made the passions of the flesh a desert too.
