# John the Presbyter (4 September, the first)

John Vasilevsky was born on 18 March 1869 in the Krasny Kholm settlement of
the Vesyegonsk district of Tver province, the son of a psalmist, Nicholas
Vasilevsky; after the Tver seminary he was ordained and served at the
cathedral of Vesyegonsk. When the godless came to power he was arrested in
1922, charged with resisting the seizure of church valuables and sentenced
to a year; his appeal succeeded, he went back to the cathedral, served there
until February 1924, then at Selikhovo until October 1926, and was then
appointed to the Resurrection church at Selizharovo, where three priests
served and he was the most active of them — which is why he was arrested on
15 July 1927 and put in the Ostashkov prison. The investigator asked what he
had preached about the godless upbringing of children, which bishop he
commemorated, and whether he had gone round the parish with an icon on St
Michael’s day; he answered that he had spoken of parents’ duty to bring up
their children as Christians and had said nothing against the pioneers or
the komsomol by name, that he commemorated Metropolitan Seraphim
(Alexandrov) as the other two priests did, and that he had gone round the
parish and stopped carrying the icon when the psalmist told him it was
forbidden. The witnesses said little: a deacon who had heard him ask the
village children at Easter whether they had crosses, and tell them to ask
their parents for one if not; priests who remembered a sermon of December
1926 — do not send your children where the crosses are taken off them; a
communist official who reported that the new priest went from house to house
hanging little crosses on ribbons round the necks of children who had none;
a peasant who said the priest gave the crosses free to anyone who wanted
one, and spoke no politics; and the psalmist, who had watched him tell a
householder with icons on one wall and portraits of Lenin on the other to
take down that filth before Trinity. Asked once more by the OGPU what he
had preached, he answered: on “obey your leaders, who watch over your
souls”, holding up Nicholas the wonderworker as the enemy of Arius, and
telling the faithful to keep their children in the faith, since a cold
attitude to the church would lead them to the pioneers and the komsomol. It
was a year before the mass persecution began, and the investigation closed
the case as unproven and let him out.

In the 1930s he served at the Annunciation church in Bezhetsk, whose
community was the nuns of the convent that had lately stood there, and there
1937 found him, the year few of the clergy lived through. He was arrested in
the night of 14 to 15 August, questioned the same day and put in the
Bezhetsk prison, and held himself with dignity and calm, refusing the
investigator’s reading of events: the gatherings of the clergy were the
bishop’s name-days and patronal feasts, he had witnessed no
counter-revolutionary talk at them, he had been under investigation in 1934
only in connection with another priest’s case, and he did not recall
calling anyone an NKVD spy. Asked at the end whether he admitted his guilt,
he answered that he did not admit belonging to any counter-revolutionary
group of clergy or to any anti-Soviet agitation, and that his statements
were correct and he had nothing to add. The indictment described a
“counter-revolutionary grouping of churchmen” in Bezhetsk resisting the
closing of churches and the removal of bells, sabotaging collectivisation
and slandering the Stalin constitution, with Vasilevsky an active member;
on 13 September the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot
on 17 September 1937 — 4 September of the old style, the day the Russian
calendar keeps him, with the bishops Gregory and Sergius and their company.
The Jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000 numbered him among the new
martyrs and confessors of Russia.
