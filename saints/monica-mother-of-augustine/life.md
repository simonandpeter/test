# Monica, mother of Augustine

doxologia.ro's calendar keeps her on 4 mai and gives no life. The line names her «Sfânta Cuvioasă Monica, mama Fericitului Augustin» — Monica the venerable, mother of the blessed Augustine — and that clause, naming her by her son, is all the calendar says of who she was. Her page on that site opens no Viață tab: it has icons, homilies and video, the epistle and the gospel appointed for the day, and a single sentence of its own.

That sentence is about her husband: «Cu trecerea timpului, soțul ei a fost câștigat pe deplin de îngăduința și dăruirea ei, încât la sfârșitul vieții el s-a lăsat de păgânism și a devenit creștin» — with time her husband was entirely won over by her forbearance and her giving of herself, so that at the end of his life he left paganism and became a Christian. Nothing more is printed here, and nothing more is set down: not a country, not a year, not the story of her son's conversion, which this page leaves to his own.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, and saint.gr's notice gives the years, the town and the marriage. Monica, the mother of [Augustine](/saints/augustine-of-hippo), was born in 332 at Tagaste in north Africa of Christian parents, devout and God-loving. She married the pagan governor Patricius, a man of an entirely different character from hers: he felt put upon by her unceasing prayer, found her charitable disposition excessive, and could not understand her wanting to visit the suffering and the sick. She met all of it with prayer, and struggled to bring up her two sons and her daughter in the instruction and admonition of the Lord although her husband would not agree to their baptism.

She did not set herself against his violent temper or his moral lapses, knowing that what is impossible for men is possible for God, and so she was very discreet and very patient; and her prayer was heard, and God enlightened Patricius's heart, and after sixteen years he was baptised a Christian, and fell asleep in peace in 371. A few years later, on the night of Pascha, 25 April 387, Augustine was baptised by [Ambrose](/saints/ambrose-of-milan), bishop of Milan. Of her praying for a son he saw as morally dead Augustine himself wrote, as the page quotes him: and Thou, Lord, didst hear her supplication and didst not despise her tears, with which she watered the ground everywhere she prayed; her pains to bring me to birth again by the Spirit were harder than those she suffered to bear me in the flesh.

Feeling that she had finished her struggle for the faith of Christ, she went back to Africa, and fell asleep in peace after a short illness in the city of Ostia. She was buried at Ostia, and a few years later her holy relics were carried to Rome, on 9 April. The page gives 332 and 388 in its head and does not repeat the second year in the story; nor does it explain how a woman returning to Africa died in an Italian port.
