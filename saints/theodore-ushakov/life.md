# Theodore Ushakov

Theodore Ushakov was born on 24 February 1743 and was an admiral of the Russian navy. The Greek synaxarion says he played an important part in the history of modern Corfu and of Greece at large, as the liberator of the Ionian islands from the French and the founder of the first Greek state, the Septinsular Republic (1800–1807), 347 years after the fall of Constantinople in 1453.

Setting out from the Black Sea as commander in the Mediterranean with the ships of the fleet Prince Potemkin had built, he crossed the Aegean and freed Kythera, Zakynthos and Kefalonia, Lefkada on 1 January 1799, and on 21 February 1799 Corfu, amid the islanders’ joy. When the Septinsular Republic was founded in 1800 the people were reconciled with his help and guidance, and he saw to the return of the Orthodox bishop of Corfu, whom the Latins and the French had driven out; the patrols of the Russian fleet in the Mediterranean kept would-be conquerors from the West at a distance, and the Turco-Albanians of Ali Pasha too.

In the last years of his life he lived as a monk at the monastery of Sanaksar, giving away his possessions to needy sailors of Sevastopol, and he died on 14 October 1817. Small pieces of his relics are kept at the monastery of St Paraskevi at Sgourades. The Greek calendar keeps him on 2 October.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 2 October and agrees with saint.gr on 1817. It calls him «прав. воина Феодора Ушакова», Theodore Ushakov the righteous soldier, and its index of names adds the monastery, «Феодор (Ушаков) Санаксарский». That index lists two further days, «23 июля» and «23 мая (Ростов.)», the second an abbreviation it does not expand, and both are recorded in the Russian row rather than guessed at.
