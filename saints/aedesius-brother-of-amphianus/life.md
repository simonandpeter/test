# Aedesius, brother of Amphianus

Aedesius and [Amphianus](/saints/amphianus-brother-of-aedesius) were brothers in the flesh, sons of one father, born in the heathenism of the Greeks; their home was the city of Patara in the country of Lycia. Their parents sent them from there to Beirut for the learning of the outward wisdom, and there they passed the days of their youth with such honour, gentleness and good sense that everyone wondered at their blameless life: they put off the ways young men usually have and were like honoured elders, having wisdom instead of grey hairs and a clean life instead of old age.

The light of the knowledge of the truth rose in their hearts, and they began to see the error of the idol-worshippers and to know the straight road of the Christian faith. Coming home from Beirut, they found their father in office at Patara and still unwilling to leave the Greek heathenism, and their kindred with him; and rather than share in it, they went away secretly, leaving parents, house, wealth and the ease of life for Christ. They came to Caesarea in Palestine and found there the Christian presbyter Pamphilus, who himself became a martyr afterwards, and gave themselves to him for teaching; they were enlightened with holy baptism and lived with their teacher, reading and learning in the law of the Lord day and night.

The persecution under Maximian, the second emperor of the name, who had taken the East from Maximian Galerius his uncle, took Amphianus first: he laid hold of the governor's sacrificing hand in the temple at Caesarea, was tortured and drowned in the sea, and the waves put his body back on the shore. After that the other Christians were seized, Aedesius among them. Some were tortured in many ways at once; others were sentenced and sent into Palestine, under instruments of brass — the account is no plainer than that — and Aedesius was one of them.

After some time they brought him to Alexandria, the city of Egypt. There he saw Hierocles the governor sitting in judgement and raging beyond all measure against the Christians, handing over virgins consecrated to God and Christian women of great good sense to shameless men to be mocked; and Aedesius was filled with zeal for Christ. In front of everyone he rushed at that official, struck him across the face, threw him to the ground and beat him with his hand, and with words reproached the heathen judge for his unjust judgements.

Those standing by seized him at once and tortured him as they had tortured his brother, and so he gained his end: after the cruel torments they drowned him in the sea, and he took the crown of victory from Christ together with Amphianus. The page gives no year for either of them, and prints no troparion.
