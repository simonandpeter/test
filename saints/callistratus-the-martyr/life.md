# Callistratus

A brave soldier of Christ under Diocletian and Maximian, Callistratus went the fair road of martyrdom, the life says, not alone but with many others. Betrayed as a Christian in his cohort, he was tortured and thrown into the sea in a sack; the sea gave him back alive, and forty-nine soldiers who saw it fell at his feet - "behold, we know that your God is great and true, who delivered you from the deep" - and asked him to lead them out of the deception of idols to his Christ. "My Lord Jesus Christ drives away none who come to Him," he answered.

Imprisoned together, they were baptized in the prison, and Callistratus raised them all in prayer: Lord, who wills that all men be saved, who drew this flock of Yours out of the lions' mouths, grant that we finish in the holy faith. When night fell, soldiers entered the prison on the tormentor's orders and cut them all to pieces with their swords - Callistratus and the forty-nine with him. A hundred and thirty soldiers who had come to believe gathered their relics and buried them with honour, and afterwards built a church over them - fulfilling the word Callistratus had spoken to his persecutor: in the midst of this city I will build a church. The Romanian calendar keeps him and his company on this day.

The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 27 September with his forty-nine and says he was martyred at Rome under Diocletian (284–304). He came from Carthage, and his parents and forebears were most devout Christians. When he grew up he enlisted in the Roman army as a recruit without giving up his devout habits, among them his evening prayer, and some pagan soldiers denounced him to the pagan general Persentinus. Persentinus had him tortured and then tied into a sack and thrown into the sea; by a wonder the sack split and two dolphins saved him. Forty-nine soldiers who saw it believed in Christ, and Persentinus in his rage had them all beheaded. The Greek calendar keeps him with an apolytikion and a kontakion.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same 27 September and narrows the year to 304, inside the reign the Romanian life gives. Its own account of him is the fuller one: he was of Carthage, and his grandfather Neoscorus had served under Tiberius in Palestine under the procurator Pontius Pilate and had been a witness of the Crucifixion, the death and the Resurrection. A soldier beside him heard him calling on the name of Jesus in the night and reported it. Sewn into a leather sack and thrown into the sea, he came ashore unhurt — the sack struck a rock and tore, and dolphins held him up — and at that forty-nine soldiers believed.

Thrown into prison with them, he went on preaching; brought out again, the whole company confessed, and were bound hand and foot and thrown into a pool, where the bonds fell off them and they stood in the water rejoicing in a baptism that was also their martyrdom. A bright crown was seen over their heads and a voice heard: «Мужайся, Каллистрат, со стадом твоим, и иди успокоиться в вечных селениях». The ground shook, an idol nearby fell and broke, and a hundred and thirty-five more soldiers believed. The commander, afraid of a mutiny, did not try those, and had Callistratus and his company cut to pieces with swords by night; the hundred and thirty-five buried them, and a church was later built where they suffered, as he had foretold. The one companion the calendar's day line names is [Gimnasius of Byzantium](/saints/gimnasius-of-byzantium), whom this long life never mentions.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him first of the four it enumerates on this day, and tells the forebear's story once more with one difference worth keeping. Where the Russian calendar makes Neoscorus his grandfather and has him serve in Palestine under Tiberius, the Prologue says only that he was «један од Калистратових предака», one of Callistratus's forebears, by name Neocorus, who served as a soldier in Jerusalem under Pontius Pilate at the time of the Lord's passion. Seeing the many wonders at Christ's death he believed, was taught the faith and baptised by the apostles, and brought it home to his own, the Prologue says, like a precious pearl.

So Callistratus in his turn was born, baptised and brought up a Christian, his father and his grandfather having been Christians before him, and in his regiment there was no other Christian but himself. The Prologue's account of the contest is the short one: beaten much and thrown into the sea, saved by the power of God and coming out of it unhurt, and forty-nine soldiers who saw his endurance and his wonders believing in Christ. He taught and strengthened them in the prison; the wicked tormentor sent soldiers in by night to kill them all; and they suffered for the truth in 304, the year the Russian calendar also prints. A church was afterwards raised over their relics.
