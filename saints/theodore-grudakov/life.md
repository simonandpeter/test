# Theodore (Grudakov)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him alone on 14 ноября ст. ст., in a line of one — «Сщмч. Феодора пресвитера (1940).» — and carries no life behind it; the name link, name page 13852, supplies the surname and the grade. azbyka.ru prints the whole of his life, signed by archpriest Maxim Maximov and taken from the third supplementary volume of the lives of the new martyrs of the Moscow diocese.

He was born on 13 February 1889 in the village of Pesye in the Podolsk district of the province of Moscow, the son of a peasant. He finished the Pererva church school in 1905, went on to the Moscow seminary, and in 1911 was ordained priest to the Tikhvin church at Bogorodskoye-Vatutinki in his own district. When a rising against the Bolsheviks broke out in that district in 1919 he was called up for some months into the Red Army's rear militia, where he did whatever labour was set him, and from June 1919 he went back to his church.

He was arrested on 15 August 1930 and held in the Butyrka prison in Moscow. A priest who had kept a letter of his from December 1929 gave it in evidence, and it is the best of what survives of him: «Пусть воинствующее безбожие объявило поход против Церкви, но декретом нам разрешено организовывать общины, отнимут храм, будем совершать богослужение по домам… не унывай, будем верить, что сверх сил Бог испытаний не пошлет, а умирать когда-либо придется». Understanding that any word about anybody could harm that person, he answered the investigator sparely and denied everything, including the letter.

On the one charge he would not deny he was plain. The decree forbidding the Law of God to be taught in schools had dismayed him, he said; he could not reconcile himself to it and thought it a violence done to conscience, and he had proposed at a school meeting that the believers write to the Council of the Russian Church asking it to petition for the teaching to be allowed. He agreed with the measures of the Soviet power, he added, except for the one that made religion the enemy of the people, and against that he preached. The investigator wrote his own words into the indictment as the proof of the case.

The OGPU troika gave him five years on 5 September 1930. He served them on the White Sea canal and then at the building of the Sorbsky port, and was released in April 1934. He served first in the Ryazan diocese, and in April 1937 was given the church of the holy unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian at Staraya Kashira. In 1937 the chairman of the local collective farm sent the district NKVD a written demand that he be arrested at once, because on the evening of 3 December the farm was to meet to be talked to about the coming elections to the Supreme Soviet and the priest had served vespers instead and called all the voters into his church, «огораживая себя праздником Введения».

He was arrested on 4 December 1937 and questioned the same day; the witnesses called said that he urged people to come and pray and that his standing was very high although he had served in the village only a short while. He denied the agitation twice and the investigation closed that day. The NKVD troika gave him ten years on 7 December 1937. He reached the Samarlag camp with a prisoners' convoy on 27 December 1937, and died there of forced labour and hunger on 27 November 1940, and was buried in an unmarked common grave.
