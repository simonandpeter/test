# Elijah (Bazhanov)

Elijah Vasilievich Bazhanov was born on 24 July 1877 to Basil Alekseevich
Bazhanov, psalmist of the Protection church of the village of Selishchi in
the Kasimov district of Ryazan province, and his wife Tatiana Ismailovna,
and finished the Ryazan seminary. In 1900 he went as teacher to the parish
school of Kochemary, but in 1901 Bishop Polyeuctus of Ryazan made him
deacon of the Kazan church of the village of Malakhovo and ordained him
there, and he taught in the village school; at his own petition Bishop
Arcadius moved him on 13 November 1904 to the deacon’s post at the
Protection church of Nikitkino in the Yegoryevsk district, built in 1842
by the Moscow merchants Simeon and Chrysanthus Kurov, where he served
nearly ten years; on 23 June 1914 he was moved to the priest’s post at the
church of St Nicholas at Ksenofontovo and on 19 July ordained priest by
Ambrose (Smirnov), bishop of Mikhailov, vicar of Ryazan — and all that
time, his service record says, he was head and catechist of the Kochemary
parish school. Before the revolution much was said and written of the St
Vladimir parish school at Karamyshevo in the Kasimov district, the pride
not only of the parish but of the whole diocese, where children were
cared for, taught, given free books and interesting meetings, and where in
1892 a free canteen was opened with money from an unknown benefactor in
Moscow, sent anonymously to the doctor A. O. Afinogenov, who with the
priest and the head of the school set it up for the children — a good
beginning that moved many to good works. Many years later Elijah came to
serve in that village, and there for long and zealous service to the
Church he was made archpriest; in time the fine school was gone, and the
faith began to fail. The dean of the Zaraysk district wrote in 1919 that
the people’s piety, their devotion to the Church, to prayer and to their
Christian duties, was under heavy trial in the revolutionary time; a fall
of faith and morals was noticeable, above all among the young — the
government’s hostility to the Church as a cultural force, the expulsion
of the teachers of the Law of God, of the icons and of prayer from the
schools, the planting in every village of so-called cultural-educational
youth circles, the spread of blasphemous books and pamphlets, all worked
to demoralise the young generation, to eat out of it the last remnants of
its fathers’ faith, and to unbridle and corrupt it. The clergy were kept
from teaching in the schools they themselves had once opened, but many
priests went on meeting the young and preaching, and so did Elijah; the
local authorities more than once caught him talking with the lads,
preaching the word of God to them and speaking of the usefulness of
learning. He was arrested on 20 August 1937, and admitted his guilt in
part — that he had “carried on religious propaganda” not only among the
young but among the rest of the population; he was indignant that in the
village processions with icons were forbidden, and baptising and
marrying too. Once they had wanted to arrest him and the women had not
allowed it, and his parishioners had stood up for him again when he was
turned out of his own house. The case went to the troika of the Moscow
regional NKVD, which on 15 September 1937 sentenced him to be shot, and
the sentence was carried out on 16 September 1937 — 3 September of the old
style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him; he was buried near the
settlement of Butovo, in the fenced ground of the former NKVD range, and
was rehabilitated by a decree of the presidium of the Supreme Soviet of 16
January 1989. He was the elder brother of the hieromartyr archpriest
Constantine Bazhanov, canonised by the Council of Bishops of 13–16 August
2000 — Elijah was shot on 16 September 1937 and Constantine on 23
December — and the Holy Synod glorified him as a hieromartyr on 12 March
2002. The Russian calendar also keeps him in the synaxes of the saints of
Ryazan and Moscow and of the new martyrs of Butovo.
