# James of Borovichi

saint.gr says there is not enough hagiological material about him, and that is the whole of its account: «Δεν υπάρχουν επαρκείς αγιολογικές πληροφορίες για τον Άγιο Ιάκωβο τον Δίκαιο, που καταγόταν από το Μποροβίτσι της περιοχής του Νόβγκοροντ, όπου και εργάσθηκε και έζησε κατά Θεόν.» He came from Borovichi in the region of Novgorod, and there he worked and lived according to God; the page gives him no parents, no trade, no year of birth and no year of death.

What it does date is what happened after him. His holy relics were found on the Tuesday of Bright Week in the year 1540, and the page adds that the translation of those relics is kept on 23 October. Neither of those is a year of his life, so none is recorded for him here, and the page prints no hymn to him.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 23 октября ст. ст. as well as on the day the Greek one gives him, and it has the story the Greek page said it did not possess. Of his origin, his life and his death, it says, there is no reliable information: by the local tradition he was a plain and pious boatman who played the fool for Christ and was killed by lightning about 1540. Its second reading has him take up that hard labour in his boyhood and says nothing more of his life at all, because the Lord glorified him after his death.

What the pages do tell at length is what happened on the river. In 1540, on the third day of Pascha, a great ice floe came up the river against the current to the village of Borovichi in the Novgorod country, and on it stood an oak coffin without a lid with the body of a young man in it. The peasants, afraid of being held answerable, pushed the floe out to midstream with poles, and it came back to the bank; this happened three times. That night the young man they had seen on the floe appeared in a dream to the village elders and said: «Я такой же христианин, как и вы. Не гоните меня. Мое имя Иаков, я получил имя в честь святого Иакова, брата Господня» — I am a Christian as you are; do not drive me away; my name is James, and I was given it in honour of the apostle whose day this is.

His relics were put first in a chapel and then, in 1544, carried into the church of the Holy Spirit, and his feast was appointed in the same year; the polyeleos celebration dates from 1572. The painters' manual describes him as it would a boy: «Подобием млад, наг, препоясан платом» — young, naked, girded with a cloth. In 1657 the patriarch took part of the relics to Valday, to the Iversky monastery.
