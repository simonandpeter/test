# Peter of Galatia

Peter lived in the fourth and fifth centuries in the region of Galatia in Asia Minor. He stayed with his parents only the first seven years of his life and spent all the rest of his years as an ascetic. From childhood he was marked out by a burning piety and by a settled determination to gain spiritual treasure, and when he was seven he left for Galatia.

From there he went to Jerusalem, to see how the ascetics and the fathers of that region lived and to learn the monastic life from them, and from Jerusalem to Antioch. In that city he found a tomb with a hut built over it on the surface of the ground and shut himself inside. In front of the tomb there was a small space closed off with a grating, and there he received the devout Christians who came to him. For his food he took, every second day, a piece of bread and a little water.

God granted him the grace of working wonders, so that even his clothes had a miraculous power at the touch of them, and many came to him to gather counsel and comfort for the wounds of their grieving souls. For his great virtue he was given the gift of mastering the demons: he freed a possessed man named Daniel, instructed him in the ascetic life, and then received him as his disciple.

Later he healed the mother of his biographer, Theodoret of Cyrus — a devout woman suffering from an incurable disease of one eye, who had heard of Peter's gift and hoped he would cure her. He laid his hand on the sore eye, made the sign of the cross, and she was healed. Bishop Theodoret, who wrote his life, tells of many other great wonders; among them, that Peter struck blind the general of the city, who meant to violate a nun, and so kept him from his mad purpose. After ninety-nine years spent in prayer and piety he gave up his soul to God, in the year 429.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same first of February, and saint.gr's page adds his places and his wonders. He came from Galatia in Asia Minor, from a district near Ancyra, and stayed with his parents only the first seven years of his life, passing all the rest of it as an ascetic. He practised asceticism first in Galatia, then went to Jerusalem to learn the ways of the ascetics and fathers there, and from there to Antioch, where he found a tomb with a small house above ground over it and shut himself inside; in front of the tomb was a little railed space in which he received devout Christians, and every second day he took a piece of bread and a little water. God had given him the gift of working wonders, and he worked many: he freed by his prayer a man possessed, named Daniel, from the demon that tormented him, and kept him afterwards as a fellow-lodger; he healed the mother of Theodoret, bishop of Cyrus, of an incurable disease of the eyes; and he blinded the general of the city, who meant to force a nun, and so prevented him. Having lived ninety-nine years in a way pleasing to God, he gave up his spirit to the Lord in peace in 429.
