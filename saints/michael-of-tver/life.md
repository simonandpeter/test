# Michael of Tver

Michael was born at Tver in 1272, after his father was already dead. The Grand Prince Yaroslav Yaroslavich, own brother of [Alexander Nevsky](/saints/alexander-nevsky), had fallen ill on a journey to the Horde, had been tonsured under the name Athanasius, and had died, leaving his second wife Xenia with a child not yet born. She brought the boy up in a burning love of God, and he was taught, the calendar says, under the archbishop of Novgorod — «вероятно, Климента», probably Clement, and the page will not say more than that. He took the principality of Tver after his elder brother Svyatoslav, and in 1285 he built a stone church of the Transfiguration of the Saviour where a wooden «церковь Космы и Дамиана» had stood.

When the Grand Prince Andrew Alexandrovich died — in 1304 by one of the two lives the calendar hangs behind his name, in 1305 by the other — Michael had the elder right and received in the Horde the patent for the grand-princely throne. Yuri Danilovich of Moscow would not submit to him and sought the power for himself. Being often at the Golden Horde before the new khan Uzbek, who had taken the Mahometan faith and was cruel and a fanatic, Yuri made himself agreeable, married the khan’s sister Konchaka, and was made grand prince; and then he began a fresh war against Tver, with Tatar detachments under Kavgady in his army.

On 22 December 1317 the men of Tver under Michael routed Yuri utterly. Many prisoners were taken, Kavgady among them, whom Michael let go, and the Moscow prince’s wife Konchaka, who then died unexpectedly at Tver. Yuri slandered Michael before the khan, charging him with poisoning her. The khan was angry, threatened to lay waste Michael’s patrimony, and summoned him to answer for himself. Not wishing to spill the blood of Russian soldiers in an unequal struggle with the khan, Michael went humbly to the Horde, understanding that it threatened him with death.

He took leave of his family and of the men of Tver and asked a blessing for the struggle of martyrdom from his spiritual father, «игумена Иоанна»: Father, he said, I have taken much care for the peace of the Christians, but for my sins I could not put an end to the strife among us; now bless me, if it must come to the shedding of my blood for them, only that they may rest a little, and that the Lord may forgive me my sins. The other of the two lives parts him from his mother on the bank of the river Nerl, and has him confess his sins to his confessor there.

In the Horde an unjust trial found him guilty of disobedience to the khan and sentenced him to death. A heavy wooden block was put on his neck and he was given over to a guard. In his confinement he read the Psalter constantly, as he was used to, and thanked God for the sufferings sent him; because his hands too were locked in the block, a boy sat in front of him and turned the pages. He wandered long with the Horde as a prisoner, enduring beatings and mockery. He was offered escape and refused: all my life I have never fled from enemies, and if I alone am saved and my people are left in trouble, what glory is that to me? Priests visited him, «игумены Александр и Марк», and he confessed and communicated every week.

The first of the two lives has him open the Psalter at hazard just before the murderers came and read: *My heart is sore pained within me, and the terrors of death are fallen upon me.* At the prompting of Yuri and of Kavgady, who was avenging his defeat, the killers broke into the camp where the prisoner was held; they threw him on the ground, tormented him and trampled him with their heels, until one of them stabbed him with a knife. That page names the man Romanets, says he cut out the heart, and dates the act «22 ноября 1319 г.», where the other page closes simply «+ 1318» and the day line prints «(1318)». The corpus records the disagreement and chooses neither year.

The naked body lay for the mob to insult while it plundered the prince’s goods, and was then covered and laid on a board tied to a cart. Two guards were set over it in the night and ran away in terror; by morning the body was not on the board. That night many people, Tatars as well as Orthodox, saw two bright clouds light the place where it had lain, and though wild beasts ranged the steppe not one of them touched it. In the morning everyone was saying: prince Michael is a saint, killed innocently. Yuri sent the body to Madzhary, where many were eager to carry it into a church and the boyars would not let them, setting it in a cattle shed instead.

From the Horde the body was taken to Moscow and buried in the church of the Saviour on the Bor in the Kremlin. Only a year afterwards, in 1319, did Tver learn what had become of its prince. At the wish of his widow the princess [Anna of Kashin](/saints/anna-of-kashin), whom the Russian calendar keeps on 2 октября, and at the asking of the men of Tver, the relics were brought home and laid on 6 September 1320 in the Transfiguration church he had built himself. The local feast began soon after that translation; the Council of 1549 glorified him for the whole church. His incorrupt relics were found on 24 November 1632 by one of the two lives and in 1655 by the other.

In 1606 the Poles and Lithuanians besieging Tver saw many times a wonderful horseman ride out of the city on a white horse with a sword in his hand and put them to flight; shown an icon of the saint, they swore on oath to «Тверскому архиепископу Феоктисту» that the rider who had appeared was Michael. The chronicler, the first of the two lives ends, calls him as great a lover of his country as the great martyr of Thessalonica was; and beyond his qualities as a ruler it names his family ones, since he was brought up in the rules of piety by his virtuous mother, who ended her own days a nun.
