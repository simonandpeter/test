# Irene (Frolova)

Irene, the daughter of the peasant Theodore Frolov, was born in 1899 in the village of Levino in the Medyn district of Kaluga province, and worked the land with her parents until she was twenty-five. In 1924 she entered the Spaso-Borodino convent in the Mozhaisk district of Moscow province as a novice, and when it was dissolved she stayed on to work in the agricultural commune the sisters formed on its grounds. When the authorities broke up the commune in 1928 the nuns gave her the obedience of staying in the monastery to keep watch over it, and she took it.

Turned out of her room, she lived in the attic with a single icon, and turned out of the attic, in an unheated shed through the winter; each time she was driven away she came back, and the nuns, scattered to the nearby churches as psalm-readers and watchmen, brought her what little food they could. She would not join the new commune of local peasants, and refused to sign for any work she was paid for, holding that to put her name to the papers of a state that persecuted the Church was to share in what it did.

She was arrested on 19 May 1931 with fourteen nuns and the priest of the Borodino monastery, charged with resisting the Soviet measures in the countryside, and held in the Mozhaisk prison. Questioned on 24 May and accused of anti-Soviet activity and of spreading rumours of war, she refused to give any statement or to sign anything. On 10 June 1931 the OGPU troika sentenced her to three years of labour camp. In the prison she fell ill with tuberculosis and was moved to its hospital, where she died on 30 September 1931.

The Russian calendar keeps her on 17 September old style.
