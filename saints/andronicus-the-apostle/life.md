# Andronicus, companion of Junia

Andronicus, together with Junia his helper, was renowned in the company of the seventy apostles, because he believed in Christ before the apostle Paul. Both were kinsmen of Paul, as he himself bears witness in the epistle to the Romans, where he writes: Salute Andronicus and Junia, my kinsmen and my fellow prisoners, who are of note among the apostles, who also were in Christ before me.

Andronicus was bishop in Panania, as doxologia's life spells the place. He was a preacher and teacher, the page says, not of one city or one country only but of the whole world, for he ran everywhere without slackening and tore up the deception of the devil from its foundation, preaching Christ. Having the wonderful Junia as his fellow worker, and being dead with her to the world but living for Christ alone, he drew many to the knowledge of God.

The two pulled down idol temples, built churches, drove unclean spirits out of men and healed the sufferings of the sick. They paid the common debt of nature, the life says, as good payers do, and going from here they received from the Lord the crown of apostleship and of martyrdom, having suffered much from the unbelievers. Their holy relics, together with those of many martyrs, were found in the places at Eugenia and confirmed by a revelation, of which the calendar writes on 22 February.
