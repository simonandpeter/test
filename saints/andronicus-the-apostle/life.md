# Andronicus, companion of Junia

Andronicus, together with Junia his helper, was renowned in the company of the seventy apostles, because he believed in Christ before the apostle Paul. Both were kinsmen of Paul, as he himself bears witness in the epistle to the Romans, where he writes: Salute Andronicus and Junia, my kinsmen and my fellow prisoners, who are of note among the apostles, who also were in Christ before me.

Andronicus was bishop in Panania, as doxologia's life spells the place. He was a preacher and teacher, the page says, not of one city or one country only but of the whole world, for he ran everywhere without slackening and tore up the deception of the devil from its foundation, preaching Christ. Having the wonderful Junia as his fellow worker, and being dead with her to the world but living for Christ alone, he drew many to the knowledge of God.

The two pulled down idol temples, built churches, drove unclean spirits out of men and healed the sufferings of the sick. They paid the common debt of nature, the life says, as good payers do, and going from here they received from the Lord the crown of apostleship and of martyrdom, having suffered much from the unbelievers. Their holy relics, together with those of many martyrs, were found in the places at Eugenia and confirmed by a revelation, of which the calendar writes on 22 February.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day, on one page with [Junia](/saints/junia-the-apostle), and grounds the pair where the Romanian line does: saint.gr counts them both among the Seventy Apostles of the Lord, whose common feast it puts on 4 January, and says they worked together with the Apostle Paul, who writes of them to the Romans — «Greet Andronicus and Junia, my kinsmen and my fellow prisoners, who are of note among the apostles, who also were in Christ before me». The page glosses the verse as it goes: his own countrymen, persecuted and imprisoned along with him, distinguished among those who carry out the mission of preaching, and men who came to Christ earlier than he did himself. For more about Andronicus, it adds, the reader should go to 30 July.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 17 May and settles the place doxologia spells Panania: it says he was appointed bishop of Pannonia, and then that he preached the Gospel through the whole of Pannonia, naming the province twice. It also reads that sentence more narrowly than the Romanian page does, which made him a teacher of the whole world: here he did not dwell in one place but went about that one province, and with [Junia](/saints/junia-the-apostle) brought many to Christ and pulled down many temples of the idols.

Both of them, the Prologue says, had the gracious power of wonderworking, by which they drove demons out of men and healed every infirmity and every sickness; and both suffered for Christ, and so received a double crown, of apostleship and of martyrdom. It calls him a kinsman of the Apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), citing Romans 16:7 as the Romanian and Greek pages do, and sends the reader to 22 February for the finding of their relics in the region of Eugenios.

The Russian calendar keeps the two of them together on this day and says plainly what it knows and from where. They were kinsmen of the apostle Paul, and it quotes his own greeting to them in the letter to the Romans. He was ordained bishop of Pannonia, but the preaching of the two of them spread into other countries far beyond that diocese; by their labours the Church was established, pagans turned to the knowledge of God, many idol-temples ceased to exist and Christian churches rose in their place. That they suffered as martyrs for the name of Christ the calendar takes not from a narrative but from the service sung to them, and it says so.

It adds the finding of the relics. In the fifth century, under the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, they were found in a suburb of Constantinople together with the relics of other martyrs, those «иже во Евгениевых», kept on 22 февраля; and it was by a revelation to a devout cleric named Николай Каллиград that his relics were known to be among those seventeen. A great church was afterwards built on the place. One of the calendar's other readings for this day, a joint notice of five of the Seventy, repeats the Romans verse and says only that he was bishop in Pannonia.
