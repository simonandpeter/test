# Zosimas the Phoenician

Zosimas's own page on doxologia.ro carries no life: it gives his name, his day and the readings, and one article, written by Nicolae Pintilie for the site, from which what follows is taken. The tenth of June, that article says, remembers the venerable Zosimas, a monk who lived at the end of the fourth century. He was born in Phoenicia, not far from the city of Tyre, entered the monastic life young, and lived in the monasteries near Caesarea in Palestine, being known for a holy man while he was still alive.

The synaxarion's information about him is poor enough, the article says, but the historian Palladius and John Moschus add two things. The first is that he foresaw and foretold the fall of Antioch and its loss for ever as a Christian city. The second is the taming of a lion in the desert of Palestine, which the article quotes at length.

"The venerable Zosimas, going at one time to Caesarea, had with him a donkey that carried his things. A lion came out on the road, seized the donkey and went off with it into the desert; and Saint Zosimas went after him. When the lion had eaten the donkey and had his fill, the elder came up to him and said, smiling: 'So then, friend, the road is now harder for me, because I have grown weak with age and cannot carry on my back the load I used to put on the donkey. So carry the burden thyself, if thou wouldst be rid of me — although this thing is against thy nature. Afterwards thou shalt return to thy first way, wild and beastly.'

"Then the lion forgot his natural anger, began to fawn upon him and grew gentle as a lamb; and Zosimas put on the lion the load the donkey had carried, and he carried it as far as the gates of Caesarea, where the saint took up his burden and let the beast go back into the desert. By this was shown the boundless power of the Lord, who makes even the wild beasts obedient to his servants."

The corpus keeps another Zosimas, of Cilicia, who was tortured by the governor Domitian and beside whom a lion spoke with a human voice; that is a different story on a different day, and nothing here makes the two one man. This page prints no troparion.
