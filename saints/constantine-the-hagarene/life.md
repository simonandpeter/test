# Constantine of Mytilene

saint.gr says he was born of Muslim parents in the village of Psilometopo on Mytilene. With his mother and his brothers he came to Magnesia and later to Smyrna, where he helped his brothers in their fruit shop, carrying to the houses of the well-born what they had bought at the stall. Going often to the cathedral church of Smyrna, he heard and learned the Greek tongue and the Christian religion, and so left for the Holy Mountain; but nobody there would receive him.

Then Patriarch Gregory V, who was in exile at the Great Lavra, put him to the test and baptised him a Christian at the Kavsokalyvia under the name Constantine. At the skete of the Honourable Forerunner he venerated the relics of the newly appeared martyrs and was seized by a longing to imitate what they had done. After a time of fasting and prayer under a spiritual father he decided to go to Magnesia to have his sister baptised a Christian; but on the advice of the fathers he sailed from the Holy Mountain and landed instead at Kydonies.

At Kydonies a Turk recognised him and he was taken before the agha. There he confessed Christ, and when he had made his origin plain he was imprisoned and cruelly tortured. Questioned again, he made the sign of the Cross in front of them all, proving the immovability of his faith; and he was imprisoned and tortured again, in a dreadful manner. When the governor saw that every attempt of his was wasted, he sent him to Constantinople, where he was put to hard tortures once more and at the last hanged, on 2 June 1819.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 2 June, the menologion day the Greek calendar keeps him on, and its whole account is four lines; but it gives the beginning of the story, which the Greek page does not. He was born a Muslim on the island of Mytilene, it says, and he was healed of a grave illness by the holy water of a church; and it was that, with the other wonders of the faith of Christ which he saw, that brought him to be baptised on the Holy Mountain in the skete of Kapsokalyvia. The Greek page has him learn the tongue and the religion at the cathedral of Smyrna, and be refused by everyone on the Mountain until the patriarch tested him; of a healing it says nothing at all.

Then he fell into the hands of the Turks, who after dreadful torments of forty days hanged him at Constantinople on 2 June 1819. The forty days are new — the Greek page has three rounds of prison and torture, at Kydonies and then in the capital, and counts no days — and on the day and the year the two books agree to the letter, so the date this folder records is left exactly as it stood.
