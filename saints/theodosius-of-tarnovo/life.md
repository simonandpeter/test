# Theodosius of Tarnovo

Theodosius left his own country while still young and went to the monastery of Saint Nicholas at Vidin, where he learnt the whole Psalter by heart and gave himself to the practice of the virtues. After the falling asleep of his elder Job he moved to the monastery of the Hodegetria at Tarnovo, which stood at the place called Holy Mountain, and he would often go off into the forests of Sliven to enjoy the grace of stillness. From there he went on to the monastery of the Mother of God of Epikernous.

When he heard that the venerable [Gregory of Sinai](/saints/gregory-of-sinai) had come from the Holy Mountain with his disciples to Paroria, saint.gr says that the news drew him as the magnet draws iron to itself. After Gregory's death he came to Athos with his friend the venerable Romylos and spent a good while studying the life and the writings of the fathers. Then he went off to Thessalonica, Veria, Constantinople, Paroria and Sliven.

He settled at last on the mountain of Kelifarevo near Tarnovo, where with the help of his friend the emperor John Alexander he founded a monastery, and a good number of monks gathered in it. The house became a lighthouse of Orthodoxy and a centre against heresy whose light reached Serbia, Hungary and Wallachia. Again and again he had to leave his stillness to defend the Orthodox teaching against the followers of Barlaam and Akindynos, against the Bogomils, the Adamites and the Jews, and he took the lead at the Bulgarian council of 1359 against the heretics.

For three years he withdrew into a cave and gave himself to great labour, and in the twenty months of heavy illness that followed he kept to his bed but never left off prayer and reading. Before the end he wished to visit in Constantinople his old fellow-student, the ecumenical patriarch Callistus I, who received him kindly and gave him a cell in his own monastery of Saint Mamas to be quiet in. There his illness worsened and his end came. He called the disciples who were with him, charged them strictly to be exact in the dogmas of the Church and persistent in the vision of God and in the prayer of the mind, recited the creed, received the holy mysteries, pointed out the angels who stood by, smiled, and gave up his spirit.

The cell filled with fragrance. He fell asleep on 27 November 1362 or 1363, saint.gr notes, the same date on which his teacher Gregory of Sinai had died, and he was buried with great ceremony, the patriarch and the synod following. Among the many disciples who carried on his work the page names [Cyprian of Kyiv](/saints/cyprian-of-kyiv) and [Euthymius of Tarnovo](/saints/euthymius-of-tarnovo), and Romylos, the most ascetic of them, who followed him on his journeys and succeeded him worthily in his monastery. His life, it adds, was written by the most holy patriarch of Constantinople Callistus himself.
