# Pimen

Pimen — Peter Zakharyevich Belolikov in the world — bishop of Semirechye and
Verny, was born on 5 November 1879 in the village of Vasilyevskoye in the
Cherepovets district of Novgorod province, into the large and devout family
of the priest Zachary and his wife Maria, daughter of the priest John
Ornatsky; the well-known Petersburg priests Philosoph and John Ornatsky were
his cousins, and through Father John he stood in family connection with John
of Kronstadt, in whose circle, with its vivid church life, the future
hieromartyr was formed — Metropolitan Arsenius of Novgorod called him a
pupil of the Kronstadt pastor. After the Novgorod seminary in 1900 he went
to the Kyiv academy and finished among the best in 1904, with a thesis on
the councils’ use of the church writers; while still a student, on 7 August
1903, he was tonsured Pimen for the much-suffering Pimen of the Kyiv Caves,
and his spiritual father, Metropolitan Flavian of Kyiv, blessed him for
mission. Ordained priest on 3 June 1904, he was sent to the Urmia mission in
north-west Persia — meeting first, in Petersburg, its former head Kirill
(Smirnov), whom John of Kronstadt especially loved. Nine years of his short
life went to that mission: a missionary by vocation, he quickly mastered Old
and Modern Syriac and the Turkic dialects, preached among the Syrian
Nestorians who had come over to Orthodoxy, defended them before the Persian
authorities, taught at the mission school, found time for scholarship and
for translating early Syriac Christian texts, and edited the mission’s
journal. In 1911 he was made rector of the Ardon seminary in the Vladikavkaz
diocese and archimandrite, and worked a year on the enlightenment of the
Ossetians, but asked to go back to Urmia and was allowed to, corresponding
in those years with Nicholas of Japan; two more years as deputy head of the
mission were the flowering of his gift, until the outbreak of the war ended
the reunion of the Syrians. From 1914 he was rector of the Perm seminary
and the right hand of the future hieromartyr Andronik of Perm, who hoped to
have him as his vicar and gave him his own panagia at his consecration.
Guard your precious possession, he warned Perm in one address, the Orthodox
faith and its holy memories — otherwise you will raise in the people not a
meek and patient soul but the soul of a beast, which will bring untold
misery on itself and on you; he also led the city’s temperance movement. He
was consecrated bishop in Petrograd on 6 August 1916 for the frontier see of
Salmas in Persia; all Orthodox Perm came to see him off — he was a true
pastor, the chronicle said, without possessions, helping to right and left —
and when he boarded the train the crowd on the platform bared their heads
and sang. His third time in Urmia, as bishop, brought only sorrows — the
mission impoverished, unable to help the starving, the intrigues of the
other missions — and after a year he asked to be recalled, and was
appointed vicar of Turkestan at Verny.

There he stood almost alone against the lawlessness of the new power,
supported the White movement and called people to its ranks, preached openly
for deliverance from the adversary, and in August 1918, when the news of the
tsar’s shooting reached him, condemned the crime from the pulpit of the
bishop’s church. On the evening of 16 September 1918 — 3 September of the old
style — as he was holding his children’s religious circle at home, Red Army
men of Mamontov’s punitive detachment, recalled from the Semirechye front
for the purpose, burst into his rooms, insulted him, and demanded he come
with them; after a moment’s thought he obeyed, was put on a cart and driven
to the Baum grove outside the town. Even the punitive detachment hesitated
long to shoot him; it was a known bandit serving in the town militia who
killed him at point-blank range, and fell from his horse and broke his leg
on the spot. The bishop had told the clergy to ring the bells and raise the
people if he was long away, and by going with the soldiers he had chosen to
make his death the signal; the clergy decided not to ring, one reason more
cowardly than another, and he listened in vain for the tocsin. There was a
meeting in the town demanding his body, and a protest broken up with
weapons; the next day children gathering nuts in the grove found the bishop
dead in a clearing, and the faithful buried him secretly in the dead of
night in the old family vault of the governor-general beside the cathedral.
Where his body had lain a neat rectangle of red moss the size of a man could
be seen until recently, found nowhere else in the whole old grove; a granite
obelisk stands there now, and a baptistery church in his name was founded
beside the cathedral in 1999. He was canonised locally for the Alma-Ata
diocese on 12 October 1997 and for the whole church by the Council of
Bishops of August 2000. The Russian calendar keeps him on 3 September
(Julian) with the presbyters Sergius, Basil, Philip and Vladimir and the
monk-martyr Meletius, killed the same year.
