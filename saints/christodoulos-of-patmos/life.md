# Christodoulos of Patmos

Christodoulos was born in 1020 in the region of Nicaea in Bithynia, the child of believing parents, Theodore and Anna, and was given the name John at his baptism. Doxologia's life for the day says that the seeds of piety and of the love of holy things were received into his heart from his earliest years, and were to bear their harvest later; and that he took the monastic habit while still young, receiving with it the name Christodoulos, «robul lui Hristos» — the servant of Christ.

He lived the monastic life first in several places, and afterwards obtained the consent and the material help of the emperor Alexios I Komnenos, who reigned from 1081 to 1118. With it he built on the island of Patmos a church and a monastery in honour of John the Evangelist, near the cave where the Apostle stayed and wrote the Apocalypse. The walls of that monastery stand today, the page says, as they were raised in the eleventh century.

When the Muslim Arabs attacked Patmos he had to take refuge with his disciples in Euboea, and there he died, on a sixteenth of March. Later his disciples found his relics whole and brought them back to the monastery he had founded on Patmos, where they are kept to this day and are a spring of many healings and wonders for those who ask his intercession with faith.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and follows the road that brought him to Patmos. saint.gr has him born in a small town near Nicaea in Bithynia about 1020, John in the world, wanting from very young to leave the world for the monastic life; he began at a monastery on the Olympus of Bithynia, where he was tonsured, went from there to the Holy Places and lived for a short while in a desert spot there, and came back to Asia Minor when the raids of the Saracens drove the monks out.

He settled then on mount Latros in Mysia, where he excelled in all the virtues and the monks elected him their first superior with the rank of archimandrite — which is where his by-name Latrenos comes from — until the raids of the Muslims forced him to flee from Latros too. Looking for a place of ascesis he came to Strobilos, a coastal region of Asia Minor, and stayed there a little while, and a new raid drove him to Leros and then to Kos. The page heads him 1020 to 1093 and records that his relic lies incorrupt in the monastery of St John the Theologian on Patmos.
