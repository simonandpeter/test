# Paul of Latros

doxologia.ro prints a long life of him, and this is read out of its opening and its close. Near the city of Pergamum in Asia, it says, is a district called Elea, and there this godly plant sprang up. His father Antiochus was a captain in the army of Constantinople and was killed in a war near Chios against the Agarenes of Crete; his mother Eudocia took her children into Phrygia and settled in a village called Marocatul, the village of Joannicius the Great, to whom they were related. She put her two sons, Paul and the elder Basil, into the monastery of Saint Stephen to learn letters.

After a while she married Basil off against his will; and Basil, once the wedding was over, put away everything of the body and went to the lavra on Mount Olympus, where he cut off his worldly cares with his hair and became a good monk. Troubled by relations and acquaintances he withdrew to the quieter parts of the mountain, and there set his mind on bringing his brother to him — for three times God told him in a dream to bring Paul too, so that they might struggle together. He sent a monk three times to the village, who found Paul enlightened and already longing to see his brother, their mother being dead and the boy left an orphan, and brought him.

After a few days the two went to Mount Latros, where there was a monastery called Caria whose abbot, Peter, shone with virtues like a bright star. Basil knew his life and his holiness and handed Paul over to him to be guided. When Paul first saw Peter he fell on his face as though struck by lightning and lay there stunned; and when the abbot raised him and asked what had happened to him, he answered that he was a sinner and begged forgiveness. Peter said to him: if only I had as few sins as you have. Peter, who told the story himself, had seen a pillar of fire when he first looked at the young man, and knowing that he would outrun him in virtue, took him gladly as his own disciple. Basil went back to Olympus, became abbot of the monastery of Saint Elias, and fell asleep in the Lord there.

Paul set himself to follow his elder in everything. The old man had two hair cassocks and wore each for some days at a time; Paul would put on the one the elder laid aside and wear it unwashed as it was, and though Peter told him to his face that he had never asked this of him, he did not stop him, and rather rejoiced. There were many monks in that monastery of Caria, and Peter let none of them live in idleness but wanted them all at the services diligently. Seeing Paul asleep in church he reproached him, and Paul asked pardon humbly and had it; and when he found him nodding on another night he gave him a slap on the cheek, after which, the page says, he never again slept during a service, the slap having been his healing.

He chose punishments of his own to wear the body down and put it under the spirit, and the page is careful about them: this will seem to the slack beyond belief, it says, but those who saw it bore witness, and we believed them, knowing them to be lovers of truth. Gabriel assured its author that he had seen with his own eyes how Paul tied two great stones to a rope, hung them on his back and went round the monastery with them all night until the wooden bell was struck, to beat sleep and himself, and to humble the body and overcome the passions like an unbeaten soldier. The more diligent monks kept those stones for many years after he fell asleep and showed them to the pilgrims who came to venerate his relics. He would not lie on bedding or a mat or on the ground, but dozed leaning against a tree or a stone when weariness overcame him.

On the evening he fell asleep, men standing by heard the voices of the demons in the air, as though somebody were beating them, while his holy soul went up. A monk named Photinus, living in Byzantium, saw the angels carrying him up, and put incense on the coals and called out: remember me, venerable father, there where you are going, before God. The bishop of Monemvasia, who was in Photinus's cell, asked what he had seen, and he answered: the holy angels are carrying to heaven the soul of the venerable Paul, the one from Latros. Another monk named Cosmas, abbot of the monastery of the Armamentaria outside the city, saw the same and told all his monks.

They buried his body in the narthex of the church, and at the burial a monk named Ephrem, who had an evil spirit that nobody had known of, was badly troubled by it, and it came out of him, driven by the grace given to the relics. When the abbot who followed him wanted to build a church in the saint's name in a fitting place and move the relics there, two great stones were in the way which the masons could not break; a monk saw the venerable one in a dream holding a reed and tracing the honoured Cross on those stones, told the abbot, and the workmen then cut them as easily as wood, and most of the church was built out of them and finished on the day the saint had fallen asleep.

At the translation a crowd of the faithful gathered, and when the grave was opened a little a fragrance came out that filled the church and the ground round about; and when it was uncovered altogether the holy body was found full of sweet myrrh like costly nard, and the scent stayed on the hands of those who touched it for many days and was not lessened by washing. A monk of that lavra who had lain three months unable to move for the pain in his knee saw the saint in a dream beckoning him to get up, and was well at once; and a woman with a great sore in her forehead that had eaten the flesh and the bone of her skull, and was near death, anointed herself with oil from his lamp and was healed. doxologia.ro gives no year for his birth or his death, and this folder gives none.
