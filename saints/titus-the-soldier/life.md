# Titus the Soldier

saint.gr says that before he became a monk Titus was a soldier; that a serious wound once left him gravely ill, so that he was forced to give up the army and to go and live in the Caves of the Lavra of Kiev; and that there, having passed the rest of his life in prayers and prostrations, he fell asleep in peace about the eleventh century.

That is the whole of the entry, and the page adds that his memory is kept again on 27 January. It gives him no birthplace, no year, no relics, no couplet and no hymn. The calendar prints a second Titus of the same Lavra on this same day, a presbyter who died in 1190, and keeps the two apart by their epithets, the soldier and the presbyter, which is how they are kept apart here.

The 27 January page is his too, and prints no life at all. It heads the day as his, says the feast is a fixed one kept on 27 January every year, and then, in place of a life, sends the reader back: for Titus the soldier, see 27 February. This corpus held that stub as a second folder until the author ruled on 3 October 2026 that the two are one man, which is what the two pages between them already said; both days are rows on this record now.
