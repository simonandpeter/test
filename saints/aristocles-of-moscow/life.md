# Aristocles

Aristocles — Alexis Alexeyevich Amvrosiev in the world — was born in 1846 at
Orenburg in a devout peasant family and lost his father early. At ten, after
a grave illness, his legs failed him, and his mother Matrona prayed with
tears to St Nicholas for his healing, vowing to give her son to God and to
enter a convent herself when he came of monastic age; on St Nicholas’s day,
6 December, the boy was healed, and when he was seventeen Matrona went to a
convent. In 1876, straight after his wife’s death, Alexis went to Athos and
entered the Russian monastery of St Panteleimon, was tonsured on 11 March
1880 with the name Aristocles after the hieromartyr Aristocles of Salamis in
Cyprus, ordained hierodeacon on 2 December 1884 and hieromonk on 12
December, and tonsured into the schema without change of name on 12 February
1886. In 1887 he brought to Moscow an olive branch from the place of
Panteleimon’s beheading, and from 1891 to 1894 was superior of the
monastery’s metochion there, then a small town house on Bolshaya Polyanka
given by the benefactress Akilina Smirnova in 1879, and rector of the chapel
of St Panteleimon; with the gifts of healing and foresight he received
hundreds a day in need of help, gave the faithful’s offerings to the
suffering, and paid for the schooling of poor children, and by his efforts
the metochion began in 1888 to publish the Soul-Profiting Companion, which
told of the Russian monks of the Holy Mountain, printed the lives of the
Athonite ascetics and the elders’ letters to their spiritual children, and
by which the Russian houses on Athos filled with new novices and were
rebuilt after their fires. In 1894 a false denunciation drove him back to
Athos. In 1909 the council of confessors of St Panteleimon’s made him
superior of the Moscow metochion again, and from 29 November 1909 he was in
Moscow, where thousands came to him once more; between 1909 and 1918 two
three-storey houses were built at the metochion, one for a library and one
for works of charity, with a house church on its top floor to the icon of
the Mother of God he loved and honoured most, the Quick to Hear, which
Patriarch Tikhon consecrated after his death, on 30 September 1918. He died
in the Lord on 24 August 1918, 6 September by the new style, and was buried
by three bishops of Moscow — Arsenius, Tryphon (Turkestanov) and Joasaph,
superior of the Theophany monastery and then acting as metropolitan — in a
marble vault at the metochion; when the metochion was searched, its monks
arrested and its property seized after the revolution, his spiritual
children moved him in 1923 to the Danilov cemetery. In 2001 the Synodal
commission found no obstacle to his glorification as a locally venerated
saint of Moscow; his relics were found at the Danilov cemetery in the summer
of 2004; on 6 September 2004 Patriarch Alexy II glorified him at the liturgy
in the Dormition cathedral of the Kremlin as the venerable Aristocles
(Amvrosiev), elder of Moscow, and on 13 November 2004 his relics were
carried in procession from the Danilov monastery to the Athonite metochion,
where they rest in the church of the great martyr Nicetas. He is kept on 24
August of the old style, 6 September, and in the synaxes of the Moscow
saints and of the fathers of the Russian monastery of St Panteleimon on
Athos.
