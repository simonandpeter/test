# Ioanichie of Muscel

On the valley of the Dâmbovița, between Târgoviște and Câmpulung, stands a high hill called the Hill of the Citadel, or Negru Vodă's Hill. The first monastic settlement known on it dates from the beginning of the fourteenth century, when the ruler of Wallachia founded the skete still called Negru Vodă (Cetățuia). On the steep south-eastern face of the hill, and along the valley of the Cetățuia brook, was one of the oldest Romanian hesychast places, between the thirteenth and the eighteenth centuries; many hermits of holy life struggled there whose names are no longer known, and for hundreds of years that valley has been called the Valley of the Cells, for the number of solitaries who lived in it.

One of the most renowned of them, in the first decades of the seventeenth century, was the schemamonk Ioanichie. He is believed to have been tonsured in the neighbouring skete of Negru Vodă, where he struggled at the end of the sixteenth century. Then, longing for the blessed life of the desert and burning with the love of Christ, he shut himself of his own will in a cave dug in the wall of the mountain, and struggled there, unknown to men, for more than thirty years. Only his disciple brought him bread and water once a week, letting it down to the mouth of the cave on a rope, because the place was so sheer; and the abbot of the skete brought him the Holy Mysteries from time to time.

How he struggled there, how many temptations he bore and what spiritual measure he reached, God alone knows. But after so hard a labour, having come to the measure of holiness and knowing his end beforehand, he dug his own grave in the back of the cave; and lying down in it, he gave his soul into the hands of the Lord.

With the passing of the years his name was forgotten and his cave abandoned, the mountain being so steep. In the first decades of the twentieth century the abbot of the skete had himself let down into the cave on a rope and found the whole bones of this great hermit laid out reverently at the back of it, yellow, sweet-smelling and covered with a spider's web; and over the grave these words were cut in the stone: Ioanichie Schimonah, 1638.
