# Martyrius of Zelenets

Martyrius was born in the town of Veliki Luki in Russia in the sixteenth century, and received the name Mina at his baptism. His parents, Cosmas and Stefanida, died when he was only ten years old, and he was brought up by his spiritual father, a priest of the church of the Annunciation in his native town, who then resolved to become a monk at the monastery of the Holy Trinity.

The saint visited his elder regularly, and before long entered the monastic life in that same monastery himself, receiving the name Martyrius at his tonsure. He ate only once a day; after the services in church he said his prayers in his cell, and then worked through the night grinding corn.

After seven years of asceticism and prayer he took with him, with his spiritual father's blessing, the icon of the Holy Trinity and the Tikhvin icon of the Most Holy Theotokos, and withdrew to the Green Isle, where he lived in complete asceticism and founded a monastery in honour of the Mother of God. Life in that wilderness was harsh and painful, but neither the cold nor the want nor the wild beasts nor the cunning of the enemy could shake his desire to serve the Lord.

One night he saw in a dream the figure of the Virgin Mary floating upon the water, and the Archangel Gabriel called him to go and find the holy icon; going down to the lake he saw the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos floating there, took it up with reverence, and carried it to his place of asceticism. In 1570 he was ordained presbyter by the Archbishop of Novgorod, and in 1582 he was made abbot. He fell asleep in the Lord in 1603, crying out to God for the last time: “Lord, give peace to all Orthodox Christians.”

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and fills in the years the Romanian page left out. saint.gr gives his name in the world as Menas and his birthplace as Veliki Luki in Russia, of pious and God-loving parents, Kosmas and Stephanida, who died ten years after he was born; a spiritual and good levite of the church of the Annunciation in his own town took over the raising of the child, and a little later became a monk at the monastery of the Holy Trinity under the name Bogolepios. The boy visited his elder regularly, and in time was tonsured and given the name Martyrios. After seven years of ascesis and prayer he took with him, at his spiritual father's blessing, the icon of the Holy Trinity and the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos of Tikhvin, and came to the island of Verde near the district of Zelensk, where he lived as an ascetic and built a small church in honour of the Lord and the Theotokos. One night after his prayer he saw in a dream the icon of the Mother of God floating on the water and the Archangel Gabriel calling him to go and find it; he went down to the lake, saw it floating there, and carried it with reverence and awe to his place of ascesis. In 1570 he was ordained presbyter by the Archbishop of Novgorod, and in 1582 appointed abbot; and giving up his holy soul in 1603 he sent up his last petition on earth: «Κύριε, χάρισε την ειρήνη σε όλους τους Ορθόδοξους Χριστιανούς» — Lord, grant peace to all Orthodox Christians.

The Russian calendar gives him two lives behind one index, and between them they name what no other calendar read here does. In the world he was Мина, from the town of Velikiye Luki; his parents, Косма and Стефанида, died before he was ten, and he was brought up by his spiritual father, the priest of the town's Annunciation church. That man, widowed, took the habit as Боголеп in the Trinity–Sergius monastery of Velikiye Luki, and the boy, visiting him often, was tonsured there himself with the name Martyrius.

Teacher and disciple laboured seven years in one cell, each vying with the other in work and prayer, and the monk Martyrius bore the obediences of cellarer, treasurer and sacristan. It was then, the page says, that the Mother of God first showed her particular care for him: dozing at midday in the bell tower he saw the image of the Hodegetria on a pillar of fire, and venerated it with awe, hot from the pillar, and waking still felt that heat on his forehead.

Wanting a solitary struggle he settled on the island called Зеленый — the green one, which is where his by-name comes from — and founded a monastery there, the wilderness having been shown him, his troparion says, by a cross-shaped light. He was famed for the gift of wonderworking: the longer life has him heal the son of the former Kasimov tsar Симеон Бекбулатович, praying before his own icons of the Life-giving Trinity and of the Tikhvin Mother of God and laying the image on the sick man's breast, and two churches were built on the grateful man's gifts. In 1595 the tsar Feodor Ivanovich gave the monastery a charter of confirmation.

In deep old age, preparing for death, he dug his own grave, set in it a coffin made with his own hands, and wept there much. Feeling his departure near he called the brethren and begged them to hold an unshaken hope in the Life-giving Trinity and to lay their whole trust on the Mother of God as he had always done; and having communicated, he blessed them and with the words «Мир всем православным» fell asleep in spiritual gladness on 1 March 1603. **That is the day the Romanian and Greek rows on this folder keep, and it is not the day the Russian calendar listed him on.** A former monk of his own monastery, the metropolitan Корнилий of Kazan and Novgorod, composed his service and wrote his life out of the saint's own notes and testament.
