# Athanasius, once a magician

saint.gr's whole account of him is one sentence: «Ο Άγιος Μάρτυς Αθανάσιος τελειώθηκε διά ξίφους» — the holy martyr Athanasius was perfected by the sword. Everything else about him is in two words of the calendar's line, «ο από μάγων», from among the magicians, and in the couplet above the entry, which calls him φαρμακός, a sorcerer, and says that in the cutting off of his head he found a strange medicine for a sick soul.

The page stands among the entries of the day of [George the Trophy-bearer](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) and links to him, but it does not say, as the pages of Anatolius and Protoleon say of them, that this Athanasius confessed at George's martyrdom; and nothing has been supplied here for that. What he practised, where, before whom he was tried and in what year he died the page does not say, and it names no relic and prints no hymn.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the great martyr alone — „Св. вел. муч. Ђорђе.“ — and names the rest in its body. [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) was born in Cappadocia, the son of rich and devout parents; his father suffered for Christ and his mother moved to Palestine. Grown, he went into the army, reached the rank of tribune in his twentieth year, and in that rank was in the service of the emperor Diocletian. When the emperor began his terrible persecution of the Christians, George stepped before him and confessed boldly that he was a Christian too.

The emperor threw him into prison, ordered his feet set in the stocks and a heavy stone laid on his chest; then had him bound to a wheel with boards of great nails set under it and turned on it until his whole body was one wound; then buried him in a ditch with only his head above the earth and left him there three days and three nights; and then had a magician give him a deadly poison. Through all of it George prayed without ceasing, and God healed him on the instant and saved him from death, to the great wonder of the people. And when by his prayer he raised a dead man, many received the faith of Christ.

Among them, the entry says, were the emperor's wife [Alexandra](/saints/alexandra-the-empress), the chief priest of the idols Athanasius, the farmer [Glycerius](/saints/glycerius-the-farmer), and [Valerius](/saints/valerius), [Donatus](/saints/donatus-23-april) and [Therinus](/saints/therinus). At the last the emperor condemned George and his own wife to be beheaded with the sword; the blessed Alexandra gave up her spirit at the place of execution before the sword came to her, and George was beheaded in 303. The wonders at his grave are without number, the entry says, and so are his appearances, in dreams and waking, to those who have remembered him and asked his help from then to this day.

What the entry adds to this folder is his office in so many words: he was „главни жрец“, the chief priest of the idols, where this corpus's English name has him a magician — and the Prologue's magician is somebody else in the same entry, the man the emperor used to bring George the poison. **Neither reading was altered on the strength of the other**, and no type was added: жрец is what he was before his conversion, not a rank in the Church. The entry gives this folder no year of its own. It dates only the great martyr's beheading, „св. Ђорђе би посечен 303. год.“, and the empress's death just before it; of the others it says only that they received the faith, and nothing at all about how or when they died. **No date was added here**, and the folder stands as it was.
