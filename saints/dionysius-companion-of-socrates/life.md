# Dionysius, companion of Socrates

Dionysius and [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) were two soldiers who came to believe in Christ, and saint.gr names them in one clause of the passion of [Theodore of Perga](/saints/theodore-of-perga). When Theodore had been stretched on a red-hot gridiron and dragged behind wild horses, his torturers «τον έριξαν μέσα στο καμίνι της φωτιάς, μαζί με δύο στρατιώτες που είχαν πιστέψει στον Χριστό, τον Σωκράτη και τον Διονύσιο» — they threw him into the furnace of fire together with two soldiers who had believed in Christ, Socrates and Dionysius.

The page gives them no more than that. It sets the whole company in the years of the emperor Antoninus and of a governor called Theodotus, and tells at length what became of Theodore: imprisoned after the furnace, crucified at the last and shot with arrows, and dying after three days upon the cross.

It tells too of his mother [Philippa](/saints/philippa-mother-of-theodore), beheaded for confessing Christ after her son's death, and of [Dioscorus](/saints/dioscorus-19-april), a priest of the idols burned for the faith that Theodore's wonders had won him. The couplet the page sets over the two soldiers is the only other thing it says of them: the spear that once pierced the dead body of the Most High now pierces two living martyrs.

Neither of them is given a city, a year or an end of his own, and the page prints no hymn for the company.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the young martyr alone — „Св. муч. Теодор.“ — and then opens its body with „И други с њим.“, *and others with him*, before naming the rest. [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-perga) suffered for the faith of Christ at Perga in Pamphylia in the time of the emperor Antoninus. He was young and fair of face; and when the governor of that region chose him with other youths to be sent for service at the imperial court, he refused and declared that he was a Christian. For that he was tortured with various torments and then thrown on a fire — but water welled up out of the earth and quenched it.

The governor put that down to some magic of Theodore's, and the martyr answered him: this is not the work of my power but of Christ my God; and if you wish to know the power of your gods, lay another fire and throw in one of your own soldiers, and you will know their power and the omnipotence of my God. The governor meant to do it; but the soldiers begged him in fear to throw in the priest [Dioscorus](/saints/dioscorus-19-april) instead of them, and Dioscorus begged him to throw in the idol of Zeus and the rest of the idols, for if they are gods they will easily save themselves — which he said because his heart was already turned to Christ, having seen the wonder of the fire.

Learning this, the governor condemned Dioscorus to death and burned him; and he gave Theodore to death too, with the two soldiers [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) and Dionysius, and with [Philippa](/saints/philippa-mother-of-theodore), Theodore's mother. Theodore they stretched on a cross, on which he gave up his spirit only on the third day; Socrates and Dionysius were run through with a spear, and Philippa was beheaded with the sword. All of them, the entry ends, were crowned with the crowns of glory in the kingdom of Christ.

What the entry adds to this folder is that Dionysius was the second of the two soldiers put to death with Theodore, and how he died: run through with a spear, where Theodore was crucified and Philippa beheaded. The `soldier` in its types is the rank this entry gives him too. The entry gives no year for this company, only „у време цара Антонина“, in the time of the emperor Antoninus. More than one Roman emperor bore that name, so no interval was read out of the reign and no date was added here; this folder stays undated, as it was.

The Russian calendar says what brought the two soldiers over. [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-perga) had been tied to wild horses and dragged, and the horses fell and were broken at the city wall while he was unhurt; and Dionysius with [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) saw a fiery chariot come down out of heaven to him and the martyr lifted into it as he was dragged. They cried out «Велик Бог христианский!» and were seized at once.

On the next day they were thrown with him into a white-hot furnace, which a heavenly dew cooled so that all three lived; the soldiers sent to count the bones came back amazed, with word that three young men stood unhurt. At the end the commander had Theodore crucified and the rest of the company beheaded. The calendar places the whole of it in the reign of Антонин Пий (138–161) at Perga in Pamphylia, and that reign is the only dating it gives him.
