# Agapius of Galatista

The hieromartyr Agapius was born about 1710 in the small town of Galatista in Chalkidike, which was then the seat of a bishopric, and there he had his general schooling. saint.gr notes that Galatista, like other parts of Chalkidike, then had schools well enough furnished and a staff of teachers to teach in them, and that the celebrated kindred iconographers called the Galatsanoi came from the same town in the same years, and that much of their work survives at the monastery of Vatopedi.

Young, he went on pilgrimage to the Holy Places at Jerusalem, stayed there, was tonsured a monk and became a member of the Brotherhood of the Holy Sepulchre; the patriarch of Jerusalem Parthenios ordained him priest of the Holy Sepulchre. He was then sent as steward of the Sepulchre's dependency in Thessalonica, the church of the Nea Panagia near the White Tower, which stands there still, and while he was there he taught in the city school.

In 1743 Parthenios sent the archimandrite Agapius to Moscow to gather alms towards the great debt the Holy Sepulchre owed its creditors. Troubles of many kinds kept him in Russia until 1747, and he came back with a sum sufficient to discharge it. In Thessalonica he took up his teaching again, then taught at the Athonias Academy beside Vatopedi, and on the recommendation of the learned Eugenios Voulgaris he was appointed its principal by the Ecumenical Patriarchate.

On 18 August 1752, coming from Galatista towards Thessalonica, he was killed near Therme, outside the city, by Janissaries — bands who in those years were plundering and shedding the blood of the Orthodox people in that country. saint.gr says that what happened at his arrest and his death is unknown, and so is the place of his burial and the fate of his relics. His memory, the page adds, is unknown to the synaxarists: he is honoured on 18 August, the first observance was held in his birthplace in 1977, and his service was composed by the hymnographer Charalambes Mpousias.
