# Alexander (Shchukin)

Alexander, archbishop of Semipalatinsk, was Alexander Ivanovich Shchukin in the world, and was born on 13 May 1891 in the city of Riga, in the large family of a priest. He grew up quiet, modest and obedient to his parents' will, dreamed from childhood of being a priest and tried to imitate the ancient ascetics; he liked to shut himself in his father's room and pray. In 1915 he finished the Moscow Theological Academy with the degree of candidate of theology and was appointed a teacher at the Nizhny Novgorod seminary.

In 1917 he took the monastic mantle in the Lavra of [Sergius of Radonezh](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) and was soon ordained hieromonk. From 1918 to 1923 he served in the church of the village of Lyskovo in the Nizhny Novgorod diocese, together with his own father. In 1923 came his consecration as bishop of Lyskovo, vicar of the Nizhny Novgorod diocese. He lived in the Makaryev Zheltovodsky monastery, organised the teaching of the Law of God to children, and preached often, reproving godlessness and those who were wrecking the churches.

In the autumn of 1928 he was arrested and shut in the prison of Nizhny Novgorod. They offered him his freedom if he would stop preaching, and he refused. He was beaten and threatened, and to everything he answered: my body is in your power, and you may do with it what you like, but my soul I will not give you. On the charge that by delivering sermons with an anti-Soviet slant he had instilled his counter-revolutionary convictions in the population, he was sentenced to three years in the camps, which he served at Solovki, working first as a watchman and then as a bookkeeper.

After his release at the end of 1931 he was appointed first to the Volkhov vicariate in the Oryol diocese, and from the summer of 1932 was bishop of Oryol. He preached much, and drew the people into the church by it, and the clouds began to gather over him again. His sister wrote to him: retire, come to me at Lyskovo, sit it out. However much I may love you, the archbishop answered, I did not take up the staff in order to lay it down.

In September 1936 he was appointed to the see of Semipalatinsk, and a year later he was arrested there. He signed none of the accusations and bore himself bravely at the interrogations. On 15 (28) October he was sentenced to be shot on a charge of espionage and counter-revolutionary agitation, and two days later, on 17 (30 new style) October 1937, he was shot. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops of the Russian Orthodox Church in August 2000.

The Greek, Romanian and Serbian calendars were read for the same menologion day and none of the three keeps him. The Russian keeps him on two days, 26 January among the new martyrs and confessors and 17 October, which is the day of the shooting in the calendar's own reckoning; only the October day was read here.
