# Silas

Silas was chosen by the greater apostles for the service of preaching the word of God, and bore many labours for the gospel of Christ, first with the Apostle Paul and afterwards alone. When the assembly of the apostles at Jerusalem sent word to the church at Antioch that those coming to the faith from the nations were not to be circumcised, Silas was sent with the other men of repute, as Luke writes in the Acts: the apostles and the elders with the whole assembly chose men from among themselves and sent them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas — Judas called Barsabas, and Silas, a man of repute among the brethren.

At Antioch they gathered the people and gave them the letter, and those who read it rejoiced at the comfort of it; and Judas and Silas, being prophets, comforted the brethren with much speech and strengthened them. Judas returned to Jerusalem and Silas chose to stay at Antioch. When Paul afterwards wished to go to other countries to preach, he chose Silas, and the two went out commended by the brethren to the grace of God, passing through Syria and Cilicia and strengthening the churches.

At Derbe and Lystra they took Timothy with them and went into Macedonia. At Philippi they drove a spirit of divination out of a girl whose soothsaying brought her masters much gain; and the masters, seeing that their hope of profit was gone, seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the market place before the magistrates, accusing them and saying that these men were troubling their city. A great crowd gathered against them, and the magistrates tore their clothes from them and ordered them beaten with rods; and after many blows they were put in prison with their feet fastened in the stocks.

At midnight Paul and Silas were singing and praying to God when suddenly a great earthquake shook the very foundations of the prison, the doors opened and the bonds were loosened. The keeper of the prison woke, saw the doors standing open, drew his knife and would have killed himself, supposing that the prisoners had escaped; but Paul called out to him and stopped him. Trembling, the keeper fell at the feet of Paul and Silas, brought them out and took them to his own house, washed their wounds, and was baptised with all his household.

From there they went on to Amphipolis, to Apollonia and to Thessalonica, and many of the Greeks and many well-born women joined themselves to them. There the unyielding Jews took certain wicked and quarrelsome men out of envy and stirred up the people against the apostles of Christ, wanting to kill them, and the brethren sent Paul and Silas away by night to Beroea; but Jews from Thessalonica came there too and roused the people against the preachers of the word of God. Paul went on by sea, while Silas and Timothy remained; and not many days later they went and found him at Corinth. Silas was then made bishop of Corinth, and having laboured enough in the preaching of the word of God and worked signs and wonders, he departed to the Lord.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 30 July, and in the same company. saint.gr says that Silas, Silouanos, Epaenetus, Crescens and Andronicus were five of the seventy disciples of the Lord, and that all of them served the Gospel of Christ «ἐν κόπῳ καὶ μόχθω, ἐν ἀγρυπνίαις πολλάκις, ἐν λιμῷ καὶ δίψει, ἐν νηστείαις πολλάκις, ἐν ψύχει καὶ γυμνότητι» — in toil and hardship, in watchings often, in hunger and thirst on the long journeys, in fastings, in cold and nakedness — which it takes from the second letter to the Corinthians.

Of Silas it says that he was imprisoned together with Paul at Philippi in Macedonia, citing the Acts, and that after many labours, and after following Paul on many of his journeys, he became bishop of Corinth — the see this folder already carries. Its second apolytikion adds the places: that he was Paul's companion on the road and fellow traveller, and that he enlightened Neapolis and Philippi with the light of the divine knowledge.

The Охридски пролог for the Julian 30 July keeps five of the Seventy together and says of Silas that he was sent from Jerusalem to Antioch with Павле and Варнава to settle the dispute among the faithful about circumcision — namely, that the heathen need not be circumcised when they come over to Christianity (Дела Ап. 15, 22). After that, it says, Silas travelled with Павле through Asia and Macedonia, and was appointed bishop in Corinth, where he reposed in peace.
