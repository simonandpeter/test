# Ananias of Crete

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 22 April reads «Sfântul Cuvios Anania din Creta» — the venerable Ananias of Crete — and his own page carries no Viață tab, no troparion and no notice of any kind: icons, and the readings appointed for a venerable father, and nothing else.

Those readings are the Epistle to the Galatians on the fruit of the Spirit — love, joy, peace, long-suffering, kindness — and the Gospel of Matthew in which the Lord calls to himself all who labour and are heavy laden. They are the common of a monastic saint and say nothing of this man in particular.

So what stands here is what the calendar keeps and no more: a venerable man named Ananias, of Crete, commemorated on 22 April. His years, his monastery, his teachers and the manner of his life are not given, and nothing has been supplied for them. He is not the apostle Ananias of the Seventy who baptized Paul at Damascus, whom the same calendar keeps on 1 October and this corpus already holds.

The Greek calendar keeps a man of this name, this rank and this island on this same day, and its page is a full life where doxologia's is blank. Hadji-Ananias, Antonios Barberakis in the world, was born in 1837 at Malles of Ierapetra, of simple, poor but God-fearing parents, John and Athena, who raised their child in the teaching and admonition of the Lord. He never learned letters, but from childhood had a bent for the sacred writings and wished to be clothed in the angelic habit. He avoided every bodily pleasure: as an infant, the page says, he would not suckle on Wednesdays and Fridays and refused his mother's breast stubbornly; he never ate meat, fish or cheese, took oil only at weekends and on the great feasts, and at Pascha broke his fast with shellfish, cuttlefish and squid; he went always barefoot, wearing coarse hair cassocks next to the skin, with an animal's hide — usually a sheep's — for a bed and a rough stone for a pillow.

At only fourteen he left his father's house and took refuge at the monastery of the Forerunner of Kapsa in Sitia, where he was tonsured a monk and became the disciple and fellow monk of Joseph Gerontogiannis, the founder of that house, who appointed him his successor. After Joseph's death in 1870 he was elected abbot, but certain slanders later drove him to take refuge in Jerusalem; and because he stayed in the Holy Land, where he had gone to venerate the holy places, he bears the title Hadji, which in Arabic means pilgrim. Longing for home brought him back to Malles in 1877, where he went to the Exakousti and set about rebuilding the monastery — one of those active monks who at the end of the nineteenth century restored forgotten monasteries and founded new ones.

There was a story behind the place. As a small boy, herding his family's animals nearby, Antonios had sheltered from the rain in a little natural cave and fallen asleep, and had seen the Mother of God in a dream, who told him that she was there and that he should search and find her icon. He woke frightened and fled; the next day, driven into the cave again by the rain, he dreamed the same dream, and when he woke he searched the back of the cave and found an icon of the Mother of God, which he carried home that evening. His father was afraid to keep it in the house, thinking the place unworthy and unfitting for her, and told the boy to take it back where he had found it; and from then on Antonios went there daily and lit a lamp before it, and later, with his father, made the cave into a little chapel.

As the monk Ananias he rebuilt that cave church and the old church above it, which he turned into the katholikon of the new monastery; the work was finished five years later, as an inscription surviving at the base of the bell tower records, «ΤΗ 21η ΑΥΓΟΥΣΤΟΥ 1882 / ΙΕΡΑ ΜΟΝΗ ΕΞΑΚΟΥΣΤΗΣ / ΜΝΗΣΘΗΤΙ ΚΥΡΙΕ ΤΟΥ ΔΟΥΛΟΥ ΣΟΥ ΑΝΑΝΙΟΥ ΜΟΝΑΧΟΥ / ΚΑΙ ΤΗΣ ΣΥΝΟΔΕΙΑΣ ΑΥΤΟΥ». Pilgrims came daily to the new monastery and candidates for the monastic life settled in it; by 1881 it counted eight monks and two lay residents. He remained abbot until 1895, working with his rich administrative gifts to get the house resources enough to survive, and even after a canonical abbot was elected in 1893 nothing was done there without his opinion, for everyone acknowledged him as its founder; from 7 April 1898 to 3 February 1899 the bishop of Ierapetra and Sitia, Ambrose, put the charge of it back into his hands.

The monastery was judged dissoluble under the Cretan State's statute law 276/1900 and its ten monks were transferred to the monastery of Phaneromeni; but in 1903 it was reconstituted and Ananias came back with the whole brotherhood to the place of their repentance. A few years later the abbot Ananias himself fell asleep in holiness on the night of Pascha, 22 April 1907, at the very hour of the Resurrection service. In the minds of those who knew him at close quarters he is a saint, and his fame is alive to this day among the people of Ierapetra and Viannos; besides the gift of healing the sick he was endowed by God with foresight, by which he helped many of the faithful to feel their own sinfulness and repent; and his relics, after the exhumation, give out an ineffable fragrance and work wonders for those who call on his help with faith.
