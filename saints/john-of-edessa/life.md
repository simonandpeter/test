# John of Edessa

John was of Edessa by birth, a well-believing Christian, and held in honour in the soldierly rank. doxologia.ro's calendar for 31 ianuarie names him, as it names his companion, a physician without silver and a wonderworker, and prints under his name the same life it prints under [Cyrus](/saints/cyrus-of-alexandria)'s.

When Diocletian raised the persecution against the Christians, John left the army and the glory of this world, his country, his house, his family and his wealth, counting all of it refuse, and went to Jerusalem. Staying there a while he heard of Cyrus and of the wonders he did, for the report of him had gone everywhere, and he wanted greatly to see him and to live with that man who pleased God.

He went first to Alexandria to look for him, and not finding him there asked carefully where he was; hearing that he was in Arabia, he went there in haste, found him, and attached himself to him with his whole soul, becoming an eyewitness of his wonders and a follower of his way of life.

When Athanasia and her three young daughters were held for the faith at the city of Canopus, John followed Cyrus there to strengthen them. Both were denounced — one in a monk's habit, one in a soldier's — seized by the governor Syrianus, tortured at length, and beheaded on the thirty-first day of January in the same place where the mother and daughters had been beheaded before them. Christians buried the two of them in one grave in the church of the Evangelist Mark, and the women in another.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them on the same thirty-first of January, and not by themselves: saint.gr's line for the day sets beside them the widow [Athanasia](/saints/athanasia-the-widow) and her three daughters, [Theodote](/saints/theodote-daughter-of-athanasia), [Theoctiste](/saints/theoctiste-daughter-of-athanasia) and [Eudoxia](/saints/eudoxia-daughter-of-athanasia), who were arrested in the same persecution. It was for those five women that the two unmercenaries came out of hiding: fearing that they might lose heart under the tortures, they hurried to them to give them courage, and were taken themselves and brought before the governor, where they declared their faith with boldness and were beheaded with the sword in the year 292. The page adds that it was Sophronius, patriarch of Jerusalem, who wrote down their wonders, because they had healed his eyes, and that their synaxis was kept in the martyrion raised in their honour in the quarter of Phorakion.
