# Nicander of Myra

Nicander was enlightened in the faith and set to the sacred work by the [Apostle Titus](/saints/titus-the-apostle). Turning many Greeks from their error and bringing them to Christ, he was seized by the pagans and brought for questioning before Livanius the count, who pressed him to deny Christ; and when he saw that he could not, he ordered him tied to swift horses, and the horses driven, so that the servant of Christ should be dragged.

He was dragged a long while, and the ground was reddened with his blood, for his body was torn as it struck the stones and the wood; and he was ready to die, had the Lord not strengthened him in such torments. Barely alive, he was thrown into prison and tormented there with hunger and thirst, and the Lord fed him with heavenly bread and healed his wounds. Brought out to be questioned again after a time and refusing to obey, he was hung on a tree, raked with iron claws and burnt with torches.

After that he was thrown into a furnace of fire and stayed unharmed, for an angel of the Lord came to him and cooled the furnace and kept him from the fire. Hearing this, the governor ordered iron nails driven into his head, his heart and his belly, and had a pit dug for him; and while he still breathed they dragged him to it and covered him with earth. So he was made an end of by a hard and violent death, and now lives with the Lord.

doxologia.ro prints this same narrative, word for word, under the name of [Hermaeus](/saints/hermaeus-companion-of-nicander), whom the Apostle Titus also enlightened and who was brought before the same count; the Romanian calendar keeps the two together on 4 November, and the troparion the site prints is printed for both of them at once. The page gives no year, and says nothing of his see beyond the title the calendar's line gives him, Bishop of Myra.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them on the same day, 4 November, and gives them a teacher. Nicander and Hermaeus were disciples of the apostle [Titus](/saints/titus-the-apostle), saint.gr says — whom the same calendar keeps on 25 August, and who was so dear a fellow worker of the apostle Paul. They preached the Gospel with abundant zeal and devotion, and by their preaching many idolaters came to believe the one true faith of Jesus Christ. For that they were denounced to the governor of the city, Libanios; they were brought before him by force and proclaimed the truth of the Gospel; and Libanios in his rage ordered them tortured — specifically, that their flesh should be torn — but by the help of divine grace the saints were healed. That wonder, instead of bringing the governor to his senses, angered him further, and he ordered them tortured with fire and then buried while they were still alive. In that martyr's way they gave up their spirits to the Lord.

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 4 ноября ст. ст. keeps the pair as «Сщмчч. Никандра , еп. Мирского, и Ермея пресвитера (I).», and the life behind the line makes both of them disciples of the apostle Titus, the follower and fellow-worker of Paul, who ordained them himself. Preaching without rest, they turned many pagans to Christ, and for that they were seized and brought to be questioned by the governor Livanius; neither persuasion nor threats would make them deny Christ.

Livanius then had them tortured. They were tied to horses and dragged over stones, their bodies were torn with iron hooks and they were thrown into a burning furnace, and the life says the Lord helped them bear what a man cannot bear of himself. At the last their torturers pierced their heads and hearts with nails, threw them into a pit and covered them with earth.

The «(I)» the line and the life both print is the first century, and it is the first date this folder has held. The day page carries one troparion for a Nicander, headed «Тропарь священномученика Никандра»: it is the common of a martyr with this name set into it, and as a common printed under his name it is his and is copied whole. Nothing is printed under the presbyter's name, and his folder takes no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the two under one heading on the Julian 4 November and gives this folder its first account of the passion. Both of them, it says, were ordained by the apostle [Titus](/saints/titus-the-apostle) — which is the first century the Russian calendar prints for them and the only chronology the entry has. They were marked by great zeal in the faith and by a great love of labour in winning the heathen for Christ the Lord, and for that they were accused to a judge named Libanius, who put them to bitter examinations.

They were beaten with stones and dragged over stones; they were kept in prison and starved; and they endured many other torments which no mortal man could have endured without particular help from God. The Lord appeared to them in various ways, and when the two were thrown into a fiery furnace he sent his angel, who cooled the fire for them. At the last the heartless torturers buried them alive in graves and covered them over with earth. The book closes on the two of them rather than on a hymn: men kill in vain, when the Lord brings to life; and in vain do they dishonour him whom the Lord glorifies.
