# Barses of Damascus

saint.gr's calendar for 28 Φεβρουαρίου names him and gives one sentence. Its line reads «Όσιος Βάρσος Επίσκοπος Δαμασκού», and the page standing under it says only that the venerable Barses was bishop of Damascus and fell asleep in peace. There is no more of him than the see and the manner of his death.

Its couplet sets one festival against another: having left the festival of this flowing life, Barses is in the company of the festival of the angels — «Πανήγυριν ῥέοντος ἐκλιπὼν βίου, Σύνεστι Βάρσος Ἀγγέλων πανηγύρει». No year, no birthplace, no relics and no hymn are given for him; the see is what tells him apart from the Barses whom this corpus keeps at Edessa.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps him on the same day of the menologion and gives the dossier the Greek page has none of. He was a Palestinian, who received holy baptism at eighteen and the monastic tonsure soon after, and for his ascetic life he was ordained bishop of the city of Damascus. But loving the solitary life, he left the bishopric and went secretly to Alexandria, naming himself Barsanuphius.

From there he withdrew into the Nitrian desert, came to a monastery, and asked its abbot to receive him into the house that he might serve the elders. That obedience he kept faithfully by day and passed the night in prayer. After some time saint Theodore the Nitriote, who knew him for a bishop, saw him, and he hid himself again and went into Egypt, where he laboured to the end of his life in the fifth century.

So two calendars keep one bishop of Damascus on one day under two names, and this folder now holds both. The Russian calendar prints the name he was born with and the name he hid under; the Greek prints only the one of the same stem, and tells nothing beyond the see.
