# Alexander, soldier of Tiberianus

Alexander lived in the years of the emperor Galerius Maximian, who reigned from 286 to 305, and he was a soldier in the company of the count Tiberianus. He was marked out, saint.gr says, by his fine bearing and by the faultlessness of his moral life. When Tiberianus was one day sacrificing to the idols, Alexander refused to take part in the sacrifices and declared with courage that he was a Christian, and for that he was put to hard torture.

His mother Poimenia, as soon as she learnt of it, ran and asked leave to see her son; and when she saw him she urged him to show himself a true Christian and to die with the bravery that befits the soldiers of Christ. The son did not disappoint his mother's hope. They opened his sides and then burnt them with lighted torches, and he did not give way under so hard a torment, being strengthened by divine grace. At the last they beheaded him, and so he was enrolled in the glorious choir of the martyrs of Christ.

saint.gr's own entry for 13 May, «Άγιος Αλέξανδρος ο Μάρτυρας επίσκοπος Τιβεριανών», says that this is the man behind it: the synaxaria record that name with no notice attached, and what the Lavriotic Codex 1170 describes is this soldier of the count Tiberianus's company, whom later writers of saints' lives turned into a bishop, giving him as a see the name of the regiment he served in. The page refers the reader to 14 May, and this folder stands on the 14 May page alone.
