# Basilissa, wife of Maxentius

saint.gr's calendar for 25 Νοεμβρίου names «Αγία Βασίλισσα» and its whole notice is one sentence: she was the wife of the king Maxentius, she believed in Christ through [Catherine](/saints/catherine-of-alexandria), whom the same calendar keeps on the same day, and she was martyred by beheading. Catherine's own page on that calendar names Maxentius as the emperor, son of Maximian, under whom the persecution was being carried on in which Catherine was arrested.

The couplet set over her line plays on her name, which means queen: cut down, the wife of a mortal king, she found the incorruptible bridal chamber of the King. Beside her the same day keeps [the hundred and fifty rhetors](/saints/hundred-and-fifty-rhetors), who the page says believed through Catherine too. It gives Basilissa no country, no city, no year and no hymn, and prints no relics for her.

**The Russian calendar keeps her on 24 ноября ст. ст., prints both of her names in one heading, and gives her the story saint.gr had in a sentence.** The name link behind its line heads her «Августа (Василисса) Римская, императрица» — the calendar's own pairing of the two names, and the reason this folder and that line are read as one woman. Its life page for the day is a joint one, of the great-martyr Catherine, the martyr Augusta and the martyr Porphyrius with his two hundred soldiers.

In it she is the empress at the court of the emperor whose persecution [Catherine](/saints/catherine-of-alexandria) had walked into, and she had heard much of her. She talked the commander [Porphyrius](/saints/porphyrius-the-stratelates) into escorting her with a detachment of soldiers, and came to the prison. The empress was struck by the martyr's strength of spirit, and by her face, which shone with the grace of God; Catherine opened the Christian teaching to those who had come, and they believed and turned to Christ.

The next day Catherine was brought back to the tribunal and threatened with the wheels, and an angel broke the instruments to pieces. Seeing that, the empress and the courtier confessed their faith in Christ before them all, together with the two hundred soldiers, and were beheaded. **The two calendars do not agree about which emperor she was married to**: saint.gr calls her the wife of Maxentius, and this page sets the whole passion under Максимин, whose reign it dates 305 to 313. Neither reading is preferred here; the display name is the one the Greek page gave this folder, and the Russian page's emperor stands in its attestation note.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 25 Νοεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/11/25/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1058/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 24 ноября ст. ст. — [that day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261124.html) and [the joint life behind her name](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life3218.htm), read 7 October 2026.*
