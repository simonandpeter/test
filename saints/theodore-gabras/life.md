# Theodore Gabras

Theodore Gabras, according to his service, was born in the tenth century at Arta, or Atra, a small town of Chaldia — one of the three themes of Pontus, with its capital at Trebizond — to devout and illustrious parents. He became military commander and governor of Trebizond, a defender of the Orthodox faith and a protector of the poor and the weak. Taken prisoner in a clash with the Seljuk Turks, he would not deny Christ and his faith, and was martyred after great and dreadful tortures on 2 October 1098 at Theodosiopolis, Erzurum. One of the kathismata of his service says the tyrant made his skull into a cup and brought it to his feasts.

His synaxarion says a church was built at Trebizond to honour his memory, the gift of his nephew Constantine Gabras, who like his uncle was general of the theme of Chaldia, from 1118 to 1143, and carried the martyr’s skull there in procession; it stood east of the city in the quarter of St Basil, near the church of St Anne. The saint.gr entry traces what became of it: a monastery of St Theodore Gabras named in a chrysobull that Alexios III Megas Komnenos of Trebizond issued in 1364, which the synaxarion does not mention; a small two-apsed church of the last years of the Grand Komnenoi, with his apse on the left and St Demetrius’s on the right, whose women’s gallery, with its painted Last Judgement, I. Miliopoulos still saw in ruins by 1930 and of which nothing now remains; and a chapel with old wall paintings dedicated to him near Kounaka in upper Matzouka.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 2 October as a great martyr, with an apolytikion and a kontakion.
