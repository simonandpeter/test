# Kyra of Beroea

saint.gr's calendar for 28 Φεβρουαρίου names two women ascetics in one line — «Οσίες Μαράνα και Κύρα» — and says that their life was written by Theodoret of Cyrrhus in his Philotheos Historia. They came, the page says, from Beroea in Syria, which it calls by its present name of Aleppo, and lived at the beginning of the fifth century; their birth was distinguished and noble and their education matched it, and their devotion was turned to the spiritual life and the life of stillness.

So they left the world and built themselves a wall of stones outside the city and gave themselves there to the spiritual struggle. The door of their enclosure they closed with clay, so that nobody should come in, and left only a small window, to speak with those outside and to take their food through. They trained themselves in silence, and wore irons on their hands, their feet, their neck and their waist, to mortify the body and overcome the temptations. Their devout longing took them to the Holy Places and to the church of St Thekla in Isauria, from which they came back to their hermitage strengthened in spirit and went on with their life in humility and good works; and so, having lived, they fell asleep in peace and gave their souls to Christ the Bridegroom. The couplet over the pair says that Marana and Kyra are counted worthy, by the withering of the flesh, to have the mastery of heaven. Because the line names each of them, each is kept here: Kyra with [Marana](/saints/marana).

The Russian calendar's reading of the pair is longer than the Greek one and differs from it in the country, and its life page for the two is headed in her name. It puts them at Beria in Asia Minor in the fourth century, noble and wealthy by birth, leaving the house on coming of age and fencing off a small corner of ground outside the city, the entrance blocked with stones and clay and only a small opening left for food to be passed in; they lived there under the open sky, in heavy iron chains, eating once in forty days for three years together.

It is she whom the chains told on. The bishop of Cyrus, who alone was let in to them out of respect for his rank, urged the two not to burden themselves with the iron, which was so heavy that she, the weaker in body, was always bent under it and could not straighten. They laboured so for forty years, breaking their solitude twice only — once for Jerusalem and the Lord's tomb and once for the tomb of the first martyr in Isauria — and taking no food on either road until they had bowed before the holy places. The calendar closes with «около 450 года», the year this folder already holds.
