# Julian, companion of Caesarius

Julian was a priest at Rome in the reign of Claudius, and doxologia.ro prints for him the same life it prints for [Caesarius](/saints/caesarius-companion-of-julian), in which he appears at the moment the deacon's prayer brings down the temple of Apollo. Leontius, who had watched it fall, believed in Christ and was baptised in front of the crowd, and it was Julian who came and gave him the holy Mysteries, after which he gave up his soul into the hands of God, as he had asked.

Loxorius the commander arrested Julian the priest together with Caesarius the deacon and ordered the two of them sewn into sacks and thrown into the sea. They answered that they would indeed be cast into the sea, but that he would be bitten by an evil serpent and lose his soul by a bad death, and so it fell out two days afterwards.

The sea gave up their bodies, and a priest named Eusebius and a man named Felix, sent by a vision, received them and buried them near the city. The life ends with the son of the baptised Leontius beheading those two in turn, and with Quartus, a priest of Capua, taking up their relics at the bidding of an angel and laying them in a reverent place.

The Greek calendar keeps the two on the same day, 7 October, and gives the whole story the Romanian line left out. saint.gr sets them in the days of the Caesar Claudius II, about 268. Caesarius was from Africa and came to the city of Tarracina in Italy; seeing the people sacrificing to idols he rebuked them sharply, was arrested and brought before the proconsul, who imprisoned him, and was then led under guard to the temple of Apollo, which fell down at his prayer. Seeing that, [Leontius](/saints/leontius-the-hypatikos) the hypatikos threw himself at his feet and asked for baptism.

Julian the presbyter baptised him, and Leontius had no sooner communicated of the immaculate mysteries than he breathed his last. The tyrant Loxorios then ordered Caesarius and Julian put into sacks and thrown into the sea, and so they gave up their souls to God. Shortly before the end Julian foretold to Loxorios that within two days he would not be able to walk that shore; two days later he was bitten by a venomous snake as he walked where the saints had been killed. Two Christians, Eusebius the presbyter and Felix, found the bodies and buried them with fitting honour, and the tyrant had them beheaded for it.

The Russian calendar keeps the two on its own 7 October, «Мчч. Иулиана пресвитера и Кесария диакона (I)», and dates them to the first century where the Greek page puts them about 268, under Claudius II; the disagreement is recorded on this folder's Russian row and is not resolved. Its index of names calls him «Иулиан Терракинский, пресвитер, сщмч.», Julian of Terracina.

The short life behind the line says that [Caesarius](/saints/caesarius-companion-of-julian) was shut in prison at the Italian town of Terracina for insulting the pagan gods, and that when he was led there bound towards the temple of Apollo the shrine fell down at his approach, killing the priests and a crowd of people. It was then that the idol-servers seized Julian, a Christian priest. By the emperor's order both martyrs were thrown into the sea; their bodies floated up and Christians buried them, and the relics of Caesarius are at Rome.
