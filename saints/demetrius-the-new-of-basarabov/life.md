# Demetrius the New of Basarabov

The Romanian life doxologia.ro prints says that this venerable father, Demetrius the New, lived in the days of the right-believing Romanian-Bulgarian emperors, and came from a village called Basarabov on the bank of the river Lom. He was at first a herdsman there; then, seeing that everything of this world passes away, he left the village and settled in a cave near it, and afterwards became a monk in the monastery that stood inside the cave. The life will not try to count his labours, his fasting, his prayer and his vigils, and says only that by them he was granted the gift of working wonders.

He knew beforehand the hour his soul would leave his body, and lying down between two stones he gave it up into the hands of God. Long afterwards the Lom rose and carried away the timber and the stones about it, and the two stones fell into the water with the saint's relics and lay there a great while. Then, the life says, God willed to disclose him: an angel appeared in a dream to a girl who was tormented by an unclean spirit, showed her the place, and promised to heal her if her parents drew him out of the water.

Priests and a crowd went to the place she named, where a light had often been seen, and found the relics in the water, full of silt and gravel, and drew them out whole and shining like gold, and carried them into Basarabov. When the news reached the prince in Bucharest he sent boyars and priests for them, but near a village called Ruși the relics stopped at a well and would go no further; young untrained oxen were yoked to the cart to show the saint's will, and they turned straight back to Basarabov and stood still in the middle of the village. The prince then sent money and built a church there in his name.

The life records a few of the wonders that followed. Two sisters of Cernavodă, Aspra and Ecaterina, who had built a church of the Dormition, secretly took a small particle of the relics, and their horses could not stir from the place until they had put it back and wept for forgiveness. A monk called Lavrentie, kissing the relics in the suite of Nichifor, metropolitan of Tarnovo, tried to bite off a fragment and was left speechless with his mouth open until he drew away and asked pardon. Ioanichie, bishop of Preslav, was carried into the church on his bedding in a grave illness and rose up whole after three hours.

In the war between Russia and the Porte, between 1769 and 1774, General Pyotr Saltykov crossed the Danube and passed through Basarabov, and took the relics meaning to send them to Russia. A Christian named Hagi Dimitrie, who was with him, begged him not to carry them off but to give them to this country for what it had suffered in the war; the general gave way, and gave them to Wallachia. The people received them and laid them with great honour in the great church of the metropolia of Ungro-Wallachia, in the days of Metropolitan Grigorie, and the life says that the war between Russians and Turks ceased and the dreadful plague with it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 27 October, and saint.gr tells his life from the Bulgarian side of the Danube. He was born in the village of Basarabovo, it says, twenty kilometres from the river and on what is now Bulgarian soil, and he lived in the thirteenth century under the reign of Peter and John Asen; his parents were pious but poor, so from childhood he pastured cows in his village, and the quiet of the place, its solitude and his longing for God helped him forward day by day, until he received the gift of cleansing tears, of pure prayer and of humble thoughts. How far he had gone in virtue and in love of God and of the whole creation the page shows by one incident: he once trod without meaning to on a bird hidden in the grass, and grieved so much that he punished the guilty foot, going unshod on that one foot for three years, so that it suffered from stones and thorns in summer and from snow and frost in winter. Afterwards he left his village for a monastery near the river Lom, where he was tonsured and continued his hard asceticism with vigils, fasts and prayers; and not content even with that, he went off for perfect stillness to a stone cave, where he struggled until he gave up his soul, hiding himself when he felt his end near between two great stone slabs as in a coffin. His body stayed in the cave unknown and incorrupt for about three hundred years; then the Lom came down in flood and swept the stones away with the relic, which lay covered in mud and water for about a hundred years more, until he appeared in a dream to a girl with epilepsy and promised to heal her if her parents took him out of the river bed. They searched with priests and other Christians and found him, and the girl was healed on the spot, and others with her. The relic was carried to his native village and later, in 1774, after the Russo-Turkish war and at the asking of a devout Romanian named Demetrius Hatzi, it came to Bucharest instead of Russia, and was laid with great honour in the metropolitan church of Saints Constantine and Helen, where it remains.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 26 октября ст. ст., a day earlier in the menologion than the Romanian one, and its life for him is four sentences. He struggled in the wilderness in Bulgaria, near the town of Rushchuk on the Danube, and his relics were carried to Bucharest on 8 July 1779 — a translation the same calendar keeps as his other day. The Romanian and Greek pages put that translation in 1774, after the Russo-Turkish war, and the five years between the two readings are left standing as they are printed.

**The two calendars disagree about when he lived, and neither reading is chosen here.** This page says «Скончался в 1685 г.» and prints the same year after his name on the day line; the Greek page the folder was first read from puts him in the thirteenth century, in the reign of Peter and John Asan. That is a gap of nearly five hundred years in two calendars that keep the same man on the same menologion days, and no third source was read to break it, so his death is recorded as the whole interval with both readings in the note.
