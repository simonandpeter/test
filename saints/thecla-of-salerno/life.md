# Thecla of Salerno

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps three women together on 6 июня and gives them one reading. It has them living in a small monastery near Rome; and in the persecution of Диоклетиан, who reigned from 284 to 305, the virgins changed into men's clothing, cut off their hair and withdrew into the Italian province of Campania. Settling in a remote place they went on in fasting and prayer, and having received from God the gift of healing they treated the people round about and turned many pagans to Christ.

The governor of the region, Леонтий, heard of them and had them brought to the city of Salerno. He threatened the first of the three with outrage and with death if she would not sacrifice to the idols; she refused and reproached the folly of those who bow to lifeless images. He then had her thrown to hungry lions, and the beasts lay down meekly at her feet; in his fury he ordered the lions killed and the virgins shut in prison.

In the morning, the reading says, they hung her up and raked her sides with iron tools and poured hot pitch into the wounds, and she only prayed aloud: a light appeared above her and a voice was heard, «Не бойся, Я с тобой», fear not, I am with thee. When they meant to crush her with a great stone an angel pushed it aside and it crushed the executioners instead. The judge ordered the soldiers to behead the three, and the soldiers did not dare raise a hand against them; so the three said to the soldiers, «Если не исполните приказания, то не будете иметь чести с нами» — if you do not carry out the order, you will have no share of honour with us — and they were beheaded, in 293.

She is the Фекла of that reading. Name page 2702 heads her «Фекла Салернская, дева, прмц.» The reading tells nothing of her apart from the other two: it names the three together at the beginning, gives the whole of the trial to [Archelaa of Salerno](/saints/archelaa-of-salerno), and names the three again when they speak to the soldiers and are beheaded. Her other companion is [Susanna of Salerno](/saints/susanna-of-salerno).
