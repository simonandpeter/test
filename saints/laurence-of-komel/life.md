# Laurence of Komel

saint.gr says that Laurence of Komel was himself also a disciple of the venerable Cornelius of Komel, and that in 1538, on Cornelius's own recommendation, he was elected abbot unanimously by the brothers of the monastery; and that he went on according to the spiritual counsels and directions of his teacher.

For all his monastic occupations, the page says, Laurence did not give up the work that was dear to him, which was the copying of church books. He fell asleep in peace in 1548, and the page's head line gives the same year with his year of birth left open.

He shares his day and his page with [Cassian of Komel](/saints/cassian-of-komel), the disciple of the same teacher in the same monastery, who died eleven years before him. Nothing else is printed for Laurence — no birthplace, no parents, no year of tonsure, no wonder, no relics and no hymn — and nothing else is set down here.

The Russian calendar's notice of him is short and has three things in it the Greek page does not. He was a disciple of Корнилий Комельский, and after labouring in work, fasting and prayer he was made abbot of the monastery at his teacher's direction. When the Tatars came, he withdrew with the brethren into the forests on the old man's advice, and brought them back to the place when the danger had passed. After his teacher's death he governed the house ten years and looked to its good order; and among the burdens of being its head he did not give up what the page calls his favourite occupation, the copying of books. He died on 16 May 1548, which is the day of this feast.
