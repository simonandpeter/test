# Patroclus of Troyes

Patroclus lived in the third century under the emperor Aurelian (270–275). He came from Tricassina — Troyes, in France — and led a devout Christian life: he loved prayer and the reading of Scripture, fasted, and gave to the poor, and the Lord gave him the gift of wonderworking; the Prologue adds that he had inherited a great estate and gave alms from it every day, eating once a day after sunset.

Aurelian summoned him and ordered him to worship the idols, promising honours and rewards. The saint refused and told the emperor that he was himself a beggar: you have many earthly treasures, he said, but not the treasure of heaven, because you do not believe in Christ and will not receive the blessedness of paradise — therefore you are poor. Aurelian condemned him to the sword. The soldiers led him to the bank of the Seine to behead him in the marsh and leave his body there; he prayed not to be left in the mud, and by God’s power became invisible to them and was carried to the other bank, where after a long search they found him and beheaded him on dry ground. Two beggars to whom he had often given alms came by, recognised their benefactor’s body and buried it with honour.
