# Theodoula of Anazarbus

In the reign of Diocletian and Maximian, emperors of Rome, there was in the city of Anazarbus a governor named Pelagius, who told his servants to go through the whole of his province, seek out the Christians and bring them to judgement, so that by the imperial command they might worship his gods. Going to do it, they seized a Christian woman named Theodoula; and she, not fearing the torments so much as she feared to be dishonoured by unbelievers, offered them much gold to let her go. They would not take it, but brought her to the governor and told him she had tried to buy herself back, and he praised them for it and honoured them with gifts.

Sitting in judgement, he asked her name, and she answered: "I am a Christian." He said that before the torments began she should tell him her name, and she answered: "I told you once, and I did not lie: I am called Christian, and this is my honourable and everlasting name; by men I am called Theodoula. I was born of believing parents and very well brought up in the Christian law." He said she answered him without sense, and she said: "It is you who are without sense, who have left God and worship stones."

He coaxed her as a brother might, promising her his wealth and great honour; she told him to enjoy his wealth himself with his father Satan, and to be his fellow heir in the everlasting torment. He said that women are headstrong and rail at their masters, but that he was slow to anger and taught her gently to submit and sacrifice, so as not to lose her soul. "I do not lose my soul," she said, "but save it, staying in the confession of God; and the body will go into the earth. So it is not without sense that I tell you to torture my body as you please, for God, seeing my patience, will send me His help."

In the idol temple she saw the image of Adrian, and praying to the true God she breathed upon it: it fell at once, struck as if by thunder, and broke in three. She went out and said to the governor, "Go in and give your god a hand, for he has fallen and is broken"; and he went in and saw it and lamented over it bitterly. Word of the breaking of Adrian came to the emperors, who sent a man to Anazarbus to learn whether it was true and to throw the governor to the beasts. Hearing it, Pelagius fell weeping before Theodoula and begged her to make his god whole, promising to become a Christian if he saw it standing in its place again. She prayed to almighty God and commanded the idol to rise whole and set itself where it had been, and it did; and the emperors' man came, saw it whole, and went back doing the governor no harm.

Then a letter came from the emperors ordering that Theodoula be tormented in various ways and put to death. Pelagius not only did not keep his promise but forgot her kindness and set her to the torments again, ordering her feet to be burned with red-hot spits. An idolater named Comentarisius came to him and asked to be given power over her, saying he would make her worship the gods or lose his own head. He drove five long nails into her — one through the left ear and out at the right, another through the right ear and out at the left, the third into her forehead and the last two into her breast — and she lifted her eyes to heaven and prayed for patience, and at once the nails fell out and she was left whole.

Comentarisius took her into his house and coaxed her with flattering words, that he might have honour from the governor and she great glory from the emperors. She told him rather to become a Christian himself, and to seek not the honour of a short time but the everlasting honour in the Kingdom of Christ, who will judge the living and the dead and repay every man according to his works; and with these words and more she brought him to the knowledge of the truth. He said: "I beg you, lady Theodoula, do not remember the evils I did you, but pray your God for me, that I too may be a Christian." The next day he stood before the governor and confessed Christ, and the governor had his head struck off at once and his body thrown into the sea; his suffering was on the twenty-fourth day of January.

Theodoula was cast into a burning furnace and stayed in it unharmed. After that she was given into the keeping of one Helladius, an assistant of the governor, to be turned to the worship of idols; and he too, like Comentarisius, was brought to Christ by her God-inspired words, confessed before the governor, and was beheaded. Then the tyrant heated a pan, stretched her out upon it and poured over her boiling pitch, wax and butter; but the pan burst with the fire and threw out great sparks and a blazing flame, which burned the crowd standing by and reached the governor himself and gave him a bitter death, while Theodoula was left unhurt.

Seeing the wonder, the crowd believed in Christ, and among them were two honoured citizens, Macarius and Evagrius. Afterwards the unbelievers heated the furnace again and threw into it Theodoula, and with her Macarius and Evagrius and the multitude of those who had believed in Christ; and there, all of them praying together, they gave up their end and passed to the life that has no end.
