# Mark the Cretan

The new martyr Mark was born and bred on the island of Crete. His father, named Andreas, came from Zakynthos and his mother from Crete. While he was still small, after his mother's death, he followed his father to Smyrna, and there, because of his father's violent conduct, he was made a Muslim by the Turks. Four years Mark remained in Islam, and he was renamed Mustafa.

Then, having repented and come back to himself, he rejected the error of the Hagarenes with loathing and fled to Zakynthos and from there to Crete. He took refuge in the then flourishing monastery of the Lady of the Headland, Toplou, in Sitia, for recollection and ascetic practice. Later he went to Constantinople, where he made his confession and studied under the wise and widely learned teacher Meletios Syrigos.

He returned to Smyrna, where he preached Christianity in public. For that he was arrested, and when he steadfastly refused to betray his faith he was tortured hard and at last beheaded, at Smyrna, in 1643. His honoured relic was bought back by the Christians and buried in the church of Saint Photini, and God, in recompense for his sacrifice, made him a spring of healings for those who came to him in faith. His martyrdom was told by the Jesuit Isaac I.
