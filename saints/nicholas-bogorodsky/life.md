# Nicholas (Bogorodsky)

Nicholas was born in 1877, the son of a priest who served the Dormition church of the Okatovo churchyard in the Kimry district of the province of Tver. He finished the seminary and served at that church as psalmist from 1892 until 1906, when he was ordained deacon to the church of the Annunciation at the Blagoveshchenye churchyard in the Rzhev district. He served that one church through the whole persecution; his confession and his last arrest both belong to it. By the end his two daughters and his son had scattered and he was left with his wife, helped in the house by the woman who had been the children's nurse and had tied her life to the family, as often happened then.

Until 1929 he had a little husbandry — some land by the church, a horse, two cows. In 1929 he was told to pay a tax so large that he could not pay it in time, and the authorities took his property, his personal things included, and sentenced him for the late payment to a year in a labour camp and a fine of three hundred roubles. He served the term and returned to his church, and served there until the persecution of the late thirties.

He was arrested on 6 November 1937 and held in the Rzhev prison. The investigator questioned him the next day and asked a single question — that the investigation had information about his anti-Soviet activity, and would he tell about it in detail. «Антисоветской деятельностью я не занимался и виновным себя в этом не признаю», the deacon answered, and as there was no proof of any crime the interrogation ended there. On 19 November the chairman of the village soviet and the reading-room man were questioned and could say nothing of any criminal activity; what they did say was that he was the moving spirit of the parish council, its organiser one might say, and that he was always calling on the peasants not to leave the faith and to come to church. Six days later the troika sentenced him, and he was shot on 27 November 1937. He was canonised for the whole church in August 2000.

The day's line names twenty-seven new martyrs of 1937 together — twenty-three presbyters, a deacon, a hieromonk, a lay martyr and a woman — and the calendar carries a life page for ten of them, of whom he is one. The men of that line were tried separately, in different districts and different prisons, and no one of these lives names another of them, so no relation is recorded between them here.
