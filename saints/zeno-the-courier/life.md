# Zeno the Courier

The venerable Zeno came from Caesarea in Cappadocia and was the son of rich and noble parents. Under the emperor Valens he served as the keeper of the emperor's letters, which is why saint.gr calls him the courier. Valens supported the Arian heretics, and it was he who tried to put pressure on [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great) through the prefect Modestus, and was forced to give way before the holy hierarch's firmness. Living in such an atmosphere, Zeno kept his Orthodox mind whole.

When Valens died, Zeno threw off the soldier's belt at once. He found one of the large tombs with which the mountain of Antioch was well supplied, went inside it, and set about cleaning his soul with the strictest ascesis. His bedding was a heap of grass on stones; he was dressed in a worn cassock; his food was a little bread which a friend brought him every second day, and the water he fetched himself from a long way off.

So he received much grace from God. It is said that when the Isaurians overran that country and were killing many ascetics, Zeno blinded them by his prayer, so that they could not see the door of his cell. Having lived in that holiness, Zeno gave up his righteous soul to God in deep old age. The distich over him is the page's whole judgement on the life: «Ζήνων τὰ τερπνὰ τῆς Ἐδὲμ ζητῶν μόνα, Εἰς τέρψιν εἶχε τὴν λύσιν τοῦ σαρκίου» — Zeno, seeking the delights of Eden alone, had for his delight the loosing of the flesh.
