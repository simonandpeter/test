# Cornutus

Cornutus was born and bred in the city of Iconium, of which he afterwards became bishop. He was once in a village called Soursalos teaching the word of the faith to the unbelievers, and the persecutors of Christianity there seized him and led him to the governor Perinius, who tortured him harshly and at last beheaded him, and so he gloriously received the crown of martyrdom — condemning present glory, the verse says, Cornutus trades by the sword for the glory to come. The Greek calendar keeps the same day a Cornutus who lived as an ascetic in Acheras, one of the three hundred saints who came from Palestine to Cyprus, much honoured in the villages of Paphos, whom it does not identify with him.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, makes him a native of Nicomedia, of the village of Salata, and already very old when the persecution of Decius and Valerian began. A persecutor of Christians named Perennius came to Nicomedia to hunt them out; the Christians left the town and hid, but their aged bishop would not go, and presented himself to Perennius and declared himself a Christian. The persecutor had him bound all over and dragged through the town until much of his blood had flowed out, and under the sword he gave up his holy soul to God.

The Russian calendar keeps him a second time on 17 August, under the name
Coronatus, with the martyrs [Thyrsus](/saints/thyrsus-of-caesarea),
[Leucius](/saints/leucius-of-caesarea) and their company, who suffered at
Caesarea in Bithynia and at Apollonia under Decius; and the Sretensky life of
that company says of him that Coronatus may be the same man as Cornutus, whose
memory is kept on 12 September. This corpus held the two as two folders. The
author ruled on 3 October 2026 that they are one man, and this record is both
days.

What the ruling does not settle is the name, and the Greek calendar's own
account of it runs a different way. On 17 August saint.gr keeps the same three
— «Άγιοι Θύρσος, Λεύκιος, Κορωνάτος και η συνοδεία τους» — and
hesitates: perhaps, it says, they are confused with those of 14 December, and
the name Callinicus of the third martyr became Coronatus. The corpus keeps that
[Callinicus](/saints/callinicus-of-caesarea) in the same company. Neither page
knows of the other's guess, and both stand here unreconciled.

Of the three the Greek page records one thing besides: their synaxis is held
near the Helenianai in Constantinople, «τελεῖται αὐτῶν ἡ σύναξις πλησίον τῶν
Ἐλενιανῶν». Its distich is for the three together: «Οἱ τρεῖς ἀθληταὶ νῦν
παρίστανται πόθῳ, / Τριάδι θείᾳ, ἧς ἐνήθλησαν χάριν» — the three contenders
now stand with longing before the divine Trinity, for whose sake they
contended. A separate «Άγιος Κορωνάτος» stands on the Greek 13 August,
martyred by the sword and nothing more, and nothing joins him to this record.
