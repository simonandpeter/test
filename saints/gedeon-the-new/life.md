# Gedeon of Tyrnavos

saint.gr's calendar for 30 December keeps Gedeon the New, a venerable martyr, and dates his death to that day in 1818. He was born in the village of Kapourna of Demetrias, in the prefecture of Magnesia, and in the world he was called Nicholas; his devout parents were named Augerinos and Kyratza, and he had three other brothers and four sisters.

At twelve years old he came with his family to the village of Yermi and from there to Velestino, where he worked beside his uncle. There a Turk seized him and made him a Muslim under the name Ibrahim. Two months later Nicholas managed to escape and came back to his family, and his father got him away to the village of Keramidi, from where he went with some builders to Crete. There he confessed to a priest and found asylum in the priest's country chapel.

After the priest's death Nicholas left for the Holy Mountain. There he confessed again, received the undefiled Mysteries, and at the monastery of Karakallou was tonsured a monk with the name Gedeon; and the fathers of the house gave him the office of ecclesiarch. On 6 June 1797, with the blessing of the fathers, he was appointed with the former abbot Gabriel to the metochion of the Transfiguration in the region of Rethymno in Crete, and after six years there he returned to the monastery of his repentance.

But with the longing for martyrdom he came to Velestino, to the place where he had denied his faith, and in the market there he confessed Christ with boldness. Hunted by the Turks, he came to Agia and was arrested. They paraded him through the streets of Tyrnavos, then cut off his feet and his hands, and then threw him into the latrines; and there, in dreadful pain, he gave up his spirit on 30 December 1818. His honoured head was treasured in the holy table of the metropolitan church of Tyrnavos, the Panagia Phaneromeni, and the town holds him its patron.

The Охридски пролог for the Julian 30 December tells the same passion and differs from the Greek account in one plain particular. It says he was a Greek by descent, of very poor parents, and that he was made a Turk by force while still young; that he repented and fled to the Holy Mountain, where he took the monastic habit in the monastery of Karakallou; and that, longing for martyrdom, he took the blessing of his spiritual father and went back to the very place where he had denied the faith, and there confessed Christ openly before the Turks and reviled Mohammed as a false prophet.

The Turks shaved his head, set him backwards on a donkey and led him through the town — and he rejoiced in that mockery for the sake of Christ. Then they cut off with an axe every finger of his hands and every toe of his feet, as was once done to the holy Јаков Персијанац, whom this same book keeps on 27 November; and at the last they threw him into a latrine, where he gave up his soul to God.

The one particular in which the two books differ is the place. This entry puts his death in 1818 «у месту Турнову у Тракији», at a place Turnovo in Thrace, and says his wonderworking relics rest in the church of the Holy Apostles in the village of Turnovo with one part of them at Karakallou; the Greek calendar puts it at Tyrnavos in Thessaly and keeps his head in the metropolitan church there. Both readings stand as their books give them, and this folder records no place of its own.
