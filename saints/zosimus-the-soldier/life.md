# Zosimus the Soldier

saint.gr's calendar for 19 June names Zosimus and heads his page «(; - 98)». He belonged to the military ranks, the page says, and came from Apollonias of Sozopolis, when the king of Rome was Trajan, whose years it gives as 89 to 117. He received the Christian teaching, was baptised, and became one of the most fervent of Christians.

But in the year 98 Domitian, the eparch at Antioch of Pisidia and a hard enemy of the Church, ordered his death, having seen that no threat availed to drag him back to the worship of the idols. First they beat him and tore his sides, which they then burned with red-hot iron; and when these too brought no result, they beheaded him, and so he received the immortal crown of martyrdom.

The page prints an apolytikion and a kontakion that name him, a megalynarion that calls him a shining star risen out of Apollonias, and a kathisma; and its note on the name, that Zosimus comes from the verb to live and means the one full of life, is followed by a line saying that the name belonged to a Byzantine writer of the fifth century — a remark about the name and not about this saint, which is not taken into this life.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 19 June, the same menologion day the Greek calendar keeps him on, and it is four sentences long. He was a Roman soldier in the time of the emperor Trajan, it says, who confessed his faith in Christ the Lord bravely and suffered fierce torments for it. In the middle of the torments he heard a voice from heaven: Be a man, Zosimus, and be baptised. I am with thee. Angels of God appeared to him in the prison. And after many torments he was cut down with an axe, in the year 116.

Two things in that are a second reading rather than more of the first. The Greek page has him receive the Christian teaching and be baptised before ever he was arrested, and this book has the voice tell him to be baptised in the middle of his suffering; both are written here and neither is chosen. And the Greek page gives the year as 98 where this one gives 116 — both inside Trajan’s reign, eighteen years apart — so the folder’s date now reads 98 or 116, widened to hold both books rather than deciding between them.

The Russian calendar's own account of him adds the tortures, which the Greek page passes over. It has the governor order a bed of brass heated red-hot and the martyr laid on it; he made the sign of the cross, lay down, and was unhurt. Leaving the city, the governor had him shod in iron sandals with sharp nails driven through their soles and made to walk behind the horses, and the Lord gave him strength to keep up with them lightly. He was then shut in prison and starved of food and drink, and an angel of the Lord strengthened him with bread and water; and after long torments he was beheaded.
