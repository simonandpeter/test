# Nilus of Stolobnoe

saint.gr's calendar for 6 December names the venerable Nilus, the New and wonderworker, «ὁ ἐν τῇ νήσῳ Στολοβνόε ἐν τῇ λίμνῃ Σεληγέρα» — he who is on the island of Stolobnoe in the lake Seliger — and then says in one sentence that it has no details of the Russian venerable's life. That is the whole of the page: no century, no parentage, no manner of death, no relic, no hymn, and no synaxaristic couplet.

What the entry says is in the line itself, and it is set down here as the line has it: a Russian ascetic whom the Greek calendar places on the island of Stolobnoe in lake Seliger, calls the New, and calls a wonderworker. Nothing further is claimed of him here.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 7 December, a day later than the Greek calendar, and where that calendar gave only the island, the lake and his two by-names, this book gives the story and a year. He was a tiller of the soil, it says, a native of Novgorod. Having withdrawn into the wilderness he fed himself on herbs and husks, and at a voice from above he moved to the island of Stolobnoe.

Robbers came once into his cell to plunder it, and were struck blind on the instant. He dug himself a grave close by the cell, and wept over it every day. He reposed and passed over into the kingdom of Christ in the year 1554 — the first year any source has given this folder — and his wonderworking relics, the book ends, rest in the very place where he had kept his fast.
