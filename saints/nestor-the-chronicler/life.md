# Nestor the Chronicler

saint.gr's calendar for 27 Οκτωβρίου names Nestor the Chronicler, «ὃ ἐν τῷ Σπηλαίῳ», and heads him «(1050 - 1114)». Its whole notice is one sentence: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Ρώσου Οσίου παρά μόνο ότι γεννήθηκε στο Κίεβο το 1050 μ.Χ. και κοιμήθηκε περί το 1114 μ.Χ.» — we have no details of the life of the Russian venerable one, except that he was born at Kyiv in 1050 and fell asleep about 1114.

Beside that the page records one thing more, that his relic lies incorrupt in the Lavra of the Caves at Kyiv. There is no couplet, no hymn, no teacher and no companions; the chronicle his epithet is taken from is not named, and nothing here supplies a title the page does not print.

The Russian calendar is where this folder stops being a line. saint.gr had one sentence and a pair of years; days.pravoslavie.ru keeps him on 27 октября ст. ст. with two lives and three hymns, and the whole of what follows is from them.

He was born at Kyiv in the 1050s and came as a young man — seventeen, one of the two tellings says — to the monastery of the Caves, where he was a novice under [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves). The tonsure he received from Theodosius's successor as abbot, Stephen, and under the same abbot he was ordained hierodeacon. In purity of life, in prayer and in obedience, the shorter telling says, the young ascetic soon outran even the known elders of the house; what the pages offer as the measure of his spiritual stature is that he was among the fathers who drove a demon out of a recluse of the monastery who had been led astray into Jewish speculation, and who was afterwards a hierarch of Novgorod.

He valued true learning joined to humility and repentance, and the longer telling quotes him on it at length: great is the profit of book-learning, for books chasten us and teach us the way to repentance, and from the words of books we find wisdom and self-command. They are rivers watering the world, and out of them wisdom comes; in books is depth beyond counting; by them we are comforted in sorrow, and they are the bridle of continence. Whoever reads books, he ends, converses with God or with holy men.

In the monastery his obedience was the chronicle. In the 1080s he wrote the Reading on the life and the destruction of the blessed passion-bearers, the two murdered princes whose relics had been carried to Vyshgorod in 1072, and in the same decade the life of his own elder; and in 1091, on the eve of the monastery's patronal feast, the abbot John charged him with digging that elder's relics out of the earth to be carried into the church.

The chief labour of his life was the Повесть временных лет, finished by 1112 or 1113, and the pages give its opening as his statement of purpose: these are the tales of bygone years, whence the Russian land came to be, who first began to reign at Kyiv, and whence the Russian land arose. The range of what he worked from is set out — earlier Russian chronicle compilations and tales, monastery records, the Byzantine chronicles of John Malalas and George Hamartolos, various historical collections, and the spoken accounts of an aged boyar, of traders, soldiers and travellers — all of it read from one strictly churchly point of view, so that the history of Rus' comes out as part of the history of the world and of the salvation of the human race.

What the chronicle preserved is listed, and the list is the reason the pages call him the first Russian church historian: the earliest mention of the Russian people in church sources, under the year 866; the making of the Slavonic letters; the baptism at Constantinople of the first Christian princess of Kyiv; the first Orthodox church in the city, under 945; the confession of the Varangian martyrs, under 983; the testing of the faiths in 986 and the baptism of Rus' in 988; and the names of the first metropolitans and of the founders and ascetics of his own monastery.

His own times were hard for the land and the Church — princes at war with one another, and the Polovtsian horsemen burning churches and driving people off into slavery — and he was an eyewitness of the sack of his monastery in 1096. He died about 1114 in deep old age, leaving the chroniclers of the Caves the continuation of his work, and he is buried in the Near caves. The day page carries two troparia and a kontakion for him, the kontakion resting on the one act of his life it thought worth singing: that he was the first to see his elder's honoured relics with his own eyes.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 27 October and spends most of its five sentences on his humility. He entered the Kyiv Caves monastery in his seventeenth year, while [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) was still abbot. He wrote the first history of the Russian people, and wove into it the history of Russian asceticism — which is this book’s description of what the chronicle is, and is on neither of the other two pages read for this folder.

He was marked by an unusual humility and meekness. In his own celebrated literary work, the entry says, he often calls himself unworthy, coarse, an ignoramus, and filled with every sin. But God, who knows the hearts of men, glorified this wonderful servant of his; and when Nestor fell asleep — on 27 October 1114, which is the year this folder already displays, though it holds it as an interval and this book states it flat — his relics worked a multitude of wonders.
