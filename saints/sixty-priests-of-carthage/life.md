# The Sixty Priests of Carthage

saint.gr's calendar for 7 December keeps sixty priests whom it counts and does not name. They were martyred at Carthage, the page says, in the years of the emperor Zeno, which it dates 474 to 476. Having arrested them, their persecutors put them to a martyr's death after cutting out their tongues. No name, no origin and no relic is printed for any of them.

The couplet set over the entry turns on exactly that: a sixfold decade without a tongue speaks again, it says, making the truth plain to everybody in a strange way. The same day's other Carthaginian entries add the Arian ruler Honorichos, whom this page does not name.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps them on the Julian 8 December, a day after the Greek calendar, under a single heading for the whole company — the holy martyrs in Africa — and names them by their number and their wound: sixty had their tongues cut out. The same sentence accounts for the other two groups that calendar keeps as separate entries, two priests and three hundred laymen. It does not call these sixty priests, where the Greek page does; the word priests in this folder's own name is that page's and is left as it stands.

It also dates the persecution by a different ruler: they suffered from the Arian heretics in the reign of the Vandal king Hunneric, or Genseric, whose years it gives as 477 to 484, where saint.gr dates them by the emperor Zeno and brackets his reign 474 to 476. All of them suffered, it ends, but by their death they conquered the lie: they established Orthodoxy and left it to us pure and bright, and the Lord crowned them with the crown of glory in his immortal kingdom.

The Russian calendar keeps the whole company in one line and names the persecution in its life page. Under the emperor Zeno, it says, the ruler of the Vandal kingdom in North Africa, Hunneric, with the help of the Arian bishops, raised a cruel persecution against the Orthodox. When the faithful had gathered in one of the churches and were secretly celebrating the divine liturgy, the troops of the barbarians burst in. Some of those praying fled; three hundred, the firmest in the true faith, gave themselves up voluntarily to torture and were beheaded.

Then it counts the priests, and the count is what makes three folders out of one line: «Из 62 иереев двух сожгли, остальным отрезали языки» — of the sixty-two priests, two were burned and the rest had their tongues cut out. But by the wonder-working power of God, the page says, those whose tongues had been cut went on preaching and refuting the Arian false teaching. The line closes with the year 477, which falls inside the reign this folder's dates already span and is not allowed to narrow them.
