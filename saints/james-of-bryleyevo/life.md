# James of Bryleyevo

saint.gr's whole account of him is one sentence: James of Bryleyevo lived in Russia in the fifteenth century; he became a monk of the monastery of Zheleznyi Borok, which was in the province of Kostroma, was a disciple of [Saint James of Zheleznyi Borok](/saints/james-of-zheleznyi-borok), and later lived the ascetic life in the Bryleyevo hermitage, where he founded a monastery in honour of the Entry of the Most Holy Mother of God. He fell asleep in holiness and in peace and was buried in the church of the Entry of the Mother of God.

The page is not steady about the names of the places. It writes his master's house as «μονή Ζέλεζνιυ-Μπορόκ» in one clause and calls the man «Αγίου Ιακώβου του Ζελενί-Μπορ» in the next; and where the day's line has Βρυλέεφ, the body of the page has «έρημο Βρυέεφ». Nothing is decided here between those readings; they are the page's own and are set down as it has them.

That is all. The page gives no year of birth, none of death, and no name in the world; it says nothing of what he did in the monastery he founded, lists no relic, and prints neither couplet nor hymn. What tells him from the other James of this day is the house and the century, and the page's own statement that he was that other man's disciple.

**The Russian calendar keeps him on the same menologion day as his teacher, in the same line, and gives him three sentences where the Greek page gave one.** It makes him the disciple of [James of Zheleznyi Borok](/saints/james-of-zheleznyi-borok) and a *трудник* of his monastery, and it stops to gloss the word itself: it carries two senses, a labourer and a novice, and the page does not choose between them. The day's line calls him «сподвижника его», his fellow-struggler.

Afterwards he founded the Bryleyevo hermitage, in honour of the Entrance of the Most Holy Theotokos into the Temple, five versts from the Forerunner house at Zhelezny Borok in the direction of the town of Buy — which is the first time any calendar this corpus reads has said what his own foundation was dedicated to, or where it stood. He reposed in the fifteenth century and was buried in the church of the Entrance.

The folder held a floruit in the fifteenth century, from saint.gr's «έζησε στη Ρωσία κατά τον 15ο αιώνα», he lived in Russia in the fifteenth century. This page says «скончался в XV веке», he reposed in the fifteenth century, which is a statement about the death and not about the life, so a death century is recorded beside the floruit rather than in place of it. Neither page gives a year.
