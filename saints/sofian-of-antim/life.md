# Sofian of Antim

Sofian of Antim was a member of the Burning Bush movement, a political
prisoner from 1958 to 1964, a church painter and “the confessor of
Bucharest”. He was born on 7 October 1912 at Cuconeștii Vechi in Bălți
county, entered the monastery at the Rughi skete in Soroca in the autumn of
1926, followed the school of church singers at Dobrușa-Soroca from 1928 to
1932, was tonsured on 25 December 1937 and ordained hierodeacon in the
cathedral of Bălți on 6 August 1939; in 1940 he finished the monastic
seminary at Cernica, and gifted for painting studied at the Academy of Fine
Arts in Bucharest (1940–1945) and at the Faculty of Theology (1942–1946).
Ordained hieromonk in 1945 for the Antim monastery in Bucharest, he was
among the best-known members of the cultural and spiritual meetings of the
Burning Bush at Antim, which gathered the heights of the Orthodox clergy and
the Christian lay intelligentsia between 1945 and 1950; from 1950 to 1958 he
was abbot at Antim and then at Plumbuita, painted several churches and
taught for some years at the monastic seminary at Neamț. In 1958 he was
condemned to hard labour for his activity in the Burning Bush and freed in
1964 under the general decree of pardon; he stayed at Antim to the end of
his earthly life, serving with great gentleness and devotion as confessor,
with hundreds of disciples, whence he was called the confessor of Bucharest.
He went to the Lord on 14 September 2002 and was buried at Căldărușani. The
Holy Synod of the Romanian Church canonised him in 2024, with his feast on
16 September, together with fifteen other confessors of the last century;
the general proclamation of the sixteen was made on 4 February at the
patriarchal cathedral in Bucharest, on the hundredth anniversary of the
Romanian patriarchate, and he is the first Orthodox saint canonised who
lived into the twenty-first century. The troparion calls him a worker of
prayer and a skilled iconographer of the church, who painted the Saviour’s
image in souls by the grace of the Spirit.
