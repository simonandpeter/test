# Codratus of Nicomedia

saint.gr says he lived in the years of the emperors Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251, and Valerian, who reigned from 251 to 259, at Nicomedia. He was arrested as a Christian and brought before the proconsul Perinios, and when he was examined he confessed with courage that he was a Christian. They stretched him on the ground, flogged him with whips made of ox sinew, and threw him into prison covered in blood.

From Nicomedia, by the proconsul's order, he was carried to Nicaea, where many believed in Christ through him and were put to death, some by fire and some by knives; and there they hung him up, flayed his flesh, flogged him and sent him on to Apamea, where he suffered many and various torments. From there the proconsul led him bound towards Caesarea, trying many times on the road to persuade him to sacrifice to the gods, while the martyr stayed unbending in his faith.

Then, near Hermoupolis, the proconsul ordered him set on a red-hot grill, and afterwards had him beheaded. His synaxis, the page says, used to be kept near Xerokirkou. On the same day it names [Rufinus](/saints/rufinus-7-may) and [Satorninus](/saints/satorninus-7-may), who were killed by the sword at Caesarea of Cappadocia at the time when this same Perinios was bringing Codratus there.
