# Ita of Ireland

Ita was from Ireland. She was born in the fifth century, in the region of Waterford, and her parents were noble people and devoted Christians. She loved Christ from a small child and took the way of monastic life. She founded the monastery of Killeedy and gathered about her many devout virgins, and lived as an ascetic, in fasting, in study and in prayer.

She also set up a school, where children were taught about the Orthodox faith, and she breathed into their hearts the love of Christ. She rested in peace in the year 570, in her own monastery, and her day of commemoration is 15 January. That is the whole of what doxologia.ro prints of her: the page names no bishop who blessed her work, no miracle, no disciple and no companion, and nothing further is added here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, and its header dates her 475 to 570. saint.gr says she came from Ireland; that from a young age she loved Christ and followed the way of the monastic polity; that she founded the monastery of Killeedy and gathered many devout virgins about her. She passed a most ascetic life, grounded on fasting, study and prayer, and she taught concerning the mystery of the Holy Trinity, reaching very high measures of perfection and of contemplation — so that she is recognised, the page says, as the spiritual mother of many Irish saints. She founded a school in which she taught the little children the Orthodox faith and planted in their hearts the love of Christ. She fell asleep, venerably, in peace in the year 570, in her own monastery.
