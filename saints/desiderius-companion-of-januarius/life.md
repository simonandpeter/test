# Desiderius, companion of Januarius

Desiderius is named by saint.gr among the fellow workers of [Januarius](/saints/januarius-of-benevento), the bishop, and is the reader of the company, «ο αναγνώστης Δησιδέριος» — the day's line spells him Δισιδέριος and the body of the page Δησιδέριος. Januarius was bishop at Naples in Italy, the page says, in the years of Diocletian, when the prefect of that region was Timothy; and with a very good band of Christian fellow workers he fought the holy struggle of virtue and brought many idolaters to Christ.

When the great persecution against the Christians came, they were all arrested and endured fearful tortures. Januarius they threw into the fire to be burned, but by divine grace he remained unharmed; so they led him at once to another place, where they cut his sinews, and thus he received the crown of martyrdom. The page tells of no separate trial and no separate death for the six who were with him, and gives the year of all seven as 305.

The couplets it sets over them divide them as the day's line does. Of the deacons: with Proculus, Sossus also and Faustus, who bowed before the scabbard, the sword killed. Of the reader: Desiderius, giving his neck to the sword, underwent the cutting and stood before the Lord. Of the last two: hear the voice of Acutius, saying, Eutychius, be cut down with me. And of the bishop: the month of April saw Januarius, a noble man, cut through.

The others of the company, as the calendar's line names them, are [Proculus](/saints/proculus-companion-of-januarius), [Sossus](/saints/sossus-companion-of-januarius), [Faustus](/saints/faustus-companion-of-januarius), [Acutius](/saints/acutius-companion-of-januarius) and [Eutychius](/saints/eutychius-companion-of-januarius). The page gives none of the six a country, a parentage or a relic of his own — the relics it records are the bishop's, in the Roman Catholic cathedral of Naples that bears his name — and the hymns it prints belong to the seven together and are kept on his folder.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the bishop alone — „Свешт. муч. Јануарије.“ — and then opens its body with „И други с њим.“, *and others with him*, before naming all seven. [Januarius](/saints/januarius-of-benevento) was bishop in Campania in Italy, it says, and in the persecution of Maximian he was brought before the court and racked with various torments, which he bore without malice and with patience. When they cast him into the fire, the fire was cooled by an invisible dew, and the martyr stood unharmed in the midst of it singing praise to God; then they scraped his body with iron combs until the bones showed white, and still he bore it.

His deacon [Faustus](/saints/faustus-companion-of-januarius) and his reader Desiderius watched their spiritual father's torments and wept for him, and for that they too were bound, brought with the bishop to the city of Puteoli and thrown into prison. In the same prison, for Christ's sake, were the deacons of Puteoli, [Proculus](/saints/proculus-companion-of-januarius) and [Sossus](/saints/sossus-companion-of-januarius), and two plain Christian men, [Eutychius](/saints/eutychius-companion-of-januarius) and [Acutius](/saints/acutius-companion-of-januarius). The next day all seven were thrown before the beasts, and the beasts did not so much as touch them; then all of them were cut down with the sword.

What the entry adds to this folder is Desiderius's place in the story, and his rank. He was the bishop's чтец, his reader — the second of the two who wept at their spiritual father's torments and were bound for weeping, and one of the seven who went to the beasts and then to the sword at Puteoli. **No type was added for it**: this corpus's vocabulary has no reader, and saint.gr's «ο Αναγνώστης» had already gone untyped here for the same reason. The entry gives him no birthplace, no age and no year of his own.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 21 Απριλίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/04/21/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/321/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026; and the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 21. април ст. ст. — [the Serbian day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-05-04&prolog=1), read 4 October 2026.*
