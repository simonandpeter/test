# Willibrord

Willibrord was born in 658 in Northumbria of Britain, saint.gr says, of Anglo-Saxon parents. His devout father Wilgils, later a hermit, sent him to be taught at the famous monastery of Ripon, whose founder and abbot was Saint Wilfrid, afterwards bishop of York, whose memory the page puts on 12 October; and he studied twelve years more in Ireland, famous for its brilliant monastic schools, under the virtuous and learned elder Egbert.

In 688 he was ordained presbyter, and the next year, at his teacher's urging, he left with twelve monks to preach the Gospel in Frisia — today's north-western Holland, the page calls it, the melancholy country of the marshes. There he was warmly received by Pepin of Herstal, mayor of the palace of the kingdom of Austrasia, who had shortly before defeated the powerful native ruler Radbod, a fanatical supporter of the old religion; and Willibrord began his mission in the district Pepin had taken from his adversary. His preaching succeeded so well that in 695 Pope Sergius I, on Pepin's warm recommendation, called him to Rome, ordained him bishop and renamed him Clement.

Returning, he settled at Utrecht and went on labouring for the christianising of Frankish Frisia, extending his work into neighbouring countries — Thuringia, Luxembourg and Denmark. Denmark he visited barely a year after his consecration, but the dreaded Viking Ongendus frustrated every attempt at founding a local church there, so the saint contented himself with taking back to Utrecht thirty young Danes who had believed in Christ, to train them as future missionaries to their own country. After Pepin's death in 714 he found undivided support from his successor Charles Martel, whom he had himself baptised.

In the years that followed he visited every part of his province again and again, built churches, founded monasteries and freed the people from their old barbarous customs. When news of what he had done reached England many of his countrymen hurried to strengthen the work: Adalbert came to preach in northern Holland, Werenfrid at Elst, Plechelm, Otger and Wiro in Gelderland, the brothers Ewald in Westphalia, where they were martyred, and Wulfram, afterwards bishop of Sens, in Frisia proper, where he baptised the son of the ruler Radbod and multitudes of other Frisians.

Willibrord fell asleep on 7 November 739 and was buried in the monastery of Echternach in Luxembourg. His contribution to the christianising of Europe was great, the page says: he is justly recognised as one of the founders of the British mission and as the apostle of Holland. It prints no hymn for him.
