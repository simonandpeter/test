# Luke of Sicily

Luke was born in the city of Tauromenium in the province of Sicily. While he was still young, about eighteen, his mind was already wise, and he went continually to the house of God and made himself an obedient follower of the divine words. When his parents took counsel to marry him to a wife, he rose in the night and went off to an untrodden place and made his dwelling among the wild beasts; and passing forty days there without food he was counted worthy of a divine and angelic vision and revelation.

Then he went to a monastery and put on the angelic habit. There he gave himself to a hard life — he tasted bread and water once in three or four days and never rested at all — and stayed eighteen months. Leaving it, he went with another monk to Mount Etna, and they lived on the herbs they found there. He slept little, had only one garment, and went barefoot.

He set himself a rule and kept to it strictly: not to leave his cell until he had read the whole Psalter through. After that he would keep the office of the third hour, and for the rest of the day take up work until the sixth hour came; and after the sixth hour he saw to a little food and to the rest of his rule. Living so, he was counted worthy of a great gift from God, and of the unlocking of hard sayings, so that whoever heard him said in wonder: how does this man know letters, having never been taught?

Afterwards, by a revelation, he went to a certain place and gathered twelve monks and cared for their salvation. For the same cause he went also to Byzantium, and visited the houses of the monks and took counsel with the fathers, and went on as far as Corinth. There, in a village, having lived no more than seven months, he fell asleep in peace. That is the whole of the life doxologia.ro prints: it gives no year, and the page carries no Tropar tab, so the folder has no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 6 November, and takes him further than the Romanian page did. saint.gr has him of Tauromenium in Sicily, marked from a small child by a living piety: out of his own sweat he gave to the poor, and many a time he kept watch beside the beds of the wretched who, having no family, passed their illness in grief and loneliness. When his parents wished to marry him he would not have it, and the page gives his answer — he did not despise marriage, he said, which the Lord and the Church had honoured so greatly, but was it really necessary that everyone marry? There are so many services to one's neighbour that an unmarried man can do more easily. And is it obligatory to have children? Is it not holy and beautiful to give bread and bring some ray of comfort to the hearts of destitute orphans? Later he became a monk and practised ascesis somewhere on Etna; then he travelled to Byzantium, where so many treasures of the Church were; and at last Corinth drew him, where he taught and built up the people with his devout discourses and his fatherly counsels, and where the peaceful death of the righteous found him.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps him on the same day as the other two calendars and calls him by his city rather than his island: «Прп. Луки Тавроменийского (800-820).», Luke of Tauromenium. Its life page is five sentences and is the first account of him this folder has had. He was born at the Sicilian town of Tauromenium; in his youth he left his parents and his betrothed and went out into the desert, where he spent many years in fasting and prayer, and he practised his asceticism on Mount Etna.

At the end of his life, the page says, a revelation came to him and he founded a monastery; and in order to learn the rules and the manner of life of other houses he visited many different cities. He died on one of those journeys, at Corinth, at the beginning of the ninth century. The day page prints no hymn under his name.
