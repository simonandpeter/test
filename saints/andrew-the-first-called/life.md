# Andrew the First-Called

Andrew was the first called. He was at the beginning a disciple of [John the Baptist](/saints/john-the-baptist), and he was the first to go after the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world. He was the brother of the apostle [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle), whom he also brought to Christ.

After he had received the Holy Spirit he preached Christ in Bithynia and in all the countries about the Black Sea, in Cherson and in Russia. Coming there, by God's ordering, he set up a cross on the hills of Kiev and foretold beforehand the faith of that people from his apostolic throne in Byzantium. Then, having preached in Great Scythia, in Sinope and in Maeotis, he returned to Thrace and came down into Greece and the Peloponnese, where he was crucified by the proconsul Aegeates in the city of Patras in Achaia.

Doxologia prints this as one paragraph of its account of the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles, which the Romanian calendar keeps on 30 June. The page gives no year, so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar names him again on 30 Ιουνίου, where saint.gr keeps the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles. The page lists all twelve whom the Lord first chose, Judas Iscariot excepted and the twelfth place filled by the apostle elected in the upper room on the eve of Pentecost, and it says plainly that it sketches each man's life at his own feast and not here. The first of the three apolytikia it prints names him in its roll of the twelve. What the synaxis is for, the page says, is the reminder of the unity the twelve had among themselves and of the manner of life that did so much for the world's rebirth in Christ; and the duty it draws from that is that Christians should move in their tracks with the same warm zeal for spreading the Gospel.

The Russian calendar keeps him on his own day, 30 ноября ст. ст., prints him a year the other two did not, and adds a reception. Its line is «Апостола Андрея Первозванного (62).», and both of the lives it carries close on that year, one of them saying his martyr's end followed about sixty-two years after the Nativity of Christ. One of them opens in Galilee, the fertile and well-favoured north of the Holy Land whose people, it says, lived easily beside the many Greeks among them, spoke Greek and even bore Greek names — of which his own is one, and it means courageous.

Both close at the cross. He prayed without ceasing on it, and asked not to be taken down when the soldiers were sent to do it; their hands would not obey them. Having given God praise he said «Lord Jesus Christ, receive my spirit», a heavenly light shone round the cross, and in that brightness he went to the eternal Kingdom; and when the light was gone the governor's wife took the body down and buried it with honour.

What follows is the Russian part of his story, and the calendar states it as a claim of succession: the Russian Church, having received the faith from Byzantium whose bishops trace their succession from this apostle, reckons itself his heir too, which is why his memory was kept so solemnly in Russia before the revolution. The emperor Peter the First founded the first and highest order of the empire in his honour, and from that reign the Russian fleet took his flag for its ensign — a blue saltire on white, the shape of the cross he died on. The day page prints him a troparion in the fourth tone and a kontakion in the second, both now stored here in Church Slavonic.

The Охридски пролог has him the son of Jonas and the brother of [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle), from Bethsaida, and a fisherman by trade. He was first a disciple of John the Baptist; but when John pointed with his finger at the Lord Jesus and said «гле, јагње Божје!» — behold, the Lamb of God, which the entry cites as John 1:36 — then he left his first teacher and followed Jesus. And after that he brought his own brother to the Lord.

After the descent of the Holy Spirit the lot that fell to this first-called apostle of Christ was to preach the Gospel in Byzantium and in Thrace, then in the lands of the Danube, then in Russia and about the Black Sea, and at the last in Epirus, in Greece and in the Peloponnese, where he also suffered. In Byzantium he set the first bishop in the person of [Stachys](/saints/stachys-of-byzantium); at Kiev he planted a cross on a height and foretold a bright Christian future for the Russian people; and through Thrace, Epirus, Greece and the Peloponnese he brought multitudes of people over to the faith and appointed bishops and priests for them.

In the city of Patras he worked many wonders in the name of Christ and won many for the Lord, among them the brother and the wife of the imperial deputy Aegeates. Enraged at that, Aegeates put him to the torture and then crucified him. As long as he was alive on the cross the apostle of Christ went on giving useful teaching to the Christians who had gathered round it; the people wanted to take him down from it, and he himself resisted.

At the last the apostle began to pray to God, and as he prayed an unusual light shone all over him. That shining lasted half an hour, and when it vanished the apostle gave up his holy soul to God. So the first-called apostle ended his earthly course — the first of the twelve great apostles to know the Lord and follow him. He suffered for his Lord in the year 62, the book says flatly, where this folder holds about 62 from the Russian calendar, which uses the word itself on one of its two life pages; a flat year does not strike out another book’s about, and nothing here has been moved. His relics were carried to Constantinople; later his head was taken to Rome and one hand to Moscow.
