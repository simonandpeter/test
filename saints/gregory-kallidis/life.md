# Gregory Kallidis

Gregory Kallidis was born in 1844 at Cumbao in Eastern Thrace. He served as Metropolitan of Heraclea and Rhaedestus, of Ioannina and of Thessalonica, and it was at Thessalonica that he fell asleep, on 25 July 1925. That is the whole of what the Romanian calendar sets down of his life: the year and place of his birth, the three sees he held, and the day of his death.

At his exhumation on 20 October 1979, when his relics were moved from the cemetery of the Annunciation in Thessalonica into the holy church of Saint Demetrius, it was found that they gave off a sweet fragrance; and from then until today, the page says, he has worked many wonders for those who have prayed to him with faith.

He was recognised as a saint of the Orthodox Church of Greece by the corresponding act of canonisation of the Patriarchate and of the Holy Synod, dated 22 May 2003. He is kept on 25 July, and on 20 October, the day of the translation of his honourable relics.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 25 July, and its page fills in the dates the Romanian life left out. saint.gr says Gregory Kallidis was born on 24 January 1844 to devout parents, John and Euphrosyne, at Koumvao in the province of Heraclea in Eastern Thrace; he took the first degree of the priesthood on 26 February 1862 from Meletius Theophilides, metropolitan of Selymbria and afterwards of Serres, studied at the schools of Serres and then at the Rizarios School and the Theological School of the University of Athens, and was made archdeacon at Athens. He was named bishop of Nazianzus on 24 March 1875, and as auxiliary of the metropolitan of Heraclea he kept the town of Rhaidestos from the raid of forty-five thousand Circassians during the Russo-Turkish war. He was elected metropolitan of Trebizond on 12 May 1879 and enthroned at Thessalonica on 20 March 1885.

After the catastrophe of 1922, the page says, he led his uprooted flock in safety into Greece with the refugee's staff like another Moses, and spent his last years at Thessalonica without taking a new metropolis. On 12 April 1925, Palm Sunday, the city honoured his fifty years as a bishop in the church of Saint Gregory Palamas, with the Ecumenical Patriarch Constantine present. The page then says he gave up his last breath at midnight on Thursday 23 July 1925 and was buried at the Evangelistria cemetery at public expense — and in the same notice calls 25 July the day of his repose. His relics were uncovered on 20 October 1979 and found to be fragrant, and by the patriarchal and synodal act of 22 May 2003 his memory was fixed on 25 July, the day of his repose, with 20 October, the anniversary of the uncovering, as a second feast; the act was brought by the Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew himself and proclaimed at the church of Saint Demetrius on 29 May 2003.
