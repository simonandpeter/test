# Pamphamer

The holy martyrs [Solochon](/saints/solochon), Pamphamer and [Pamphylon](/saints/pamphylon) came from Egypt and contended in the years of the emperor Maximian, who reigned from 286 to 305. They were serving in the army, and while they were at Chalcedon, the emperor having ordered that the Christian soldiers be sought out, the tribune Campanus commanded them all to sacrifice to the idols. All complied except these three, who refused and confessed their faith in the Trinity; they were arrested at once and put to frightful tortures.

Pamphamer and Pamphylon found a martyr's death under those tortures. Solochon endured them, rebuked the tribune for his inhumanity, was pierced from the ear to the brain, and died afterwards in a widow's house. saint.gr prints a couplet for the two who died first: take courage though you are struck, you two martyrs, for God will make of your wounds a soothing balm and crowns. Nothing further is said of Pamphamer — no age, no home town within Egypt, no relics and no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 17 May inside the entry it heads for [Solochon](/saints/solochon), and names him twice. When the imperial order came that all the soldiers should sacrifice to the idols, and Solochon declared himself a Christian, his two comrades Pamphamer and [Pamphylon](/saints/pamphylon) did the same; and the commander had them beaten and tortured with frightful tortures, under which the holy Pamphamer and Pamphylon expired. The Prologue calls the officer a commander where the Greek page calls him a tribune, and it names no place. It gives the year 298 for Solochon, who outlived the two of them and whose end came later, in a widow's house, and it gives no year for these two, so none is set down here.

The Russian calendar names him second of the three and gives him one sentence of his own. He served with the other two in the imperial regiment of the tribune Кампан, which was sent to Chalcedon in the persecution of Maximian and Diocletian, and where the whole regiment was required to sacrifice in an idol-temple. Three soldiers refused, declaring that they worshipped only the true God, the Lord Jesus Christ; and when the tribune had them cruelly tortured, he and the third of them died in the torture itself, while their leader bore it and lived to be tried further. The calendar gives him no birthplace, no age and no year, dating the three only by the reign.
