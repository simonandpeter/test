# The Three Hundred Martyrs of Africa

saint.gr's calendar for 7 December keeps three hundred martyrs who suffered in Africa in the fifth century after Christ, in the years of the emperor Zeno. It names none of them. What it does name is the cause: two Monophysite bishops in Africa, Kyrillos and Valinardis, who persuaded the ruler Honorichos, himself a Monophysite, to set a savage and murderous persecution going against the Christians.

These martyrs, the page says, were denounced and held to the Orthodox confession, and so all of them died by beheading. Among them were old men, young men and householders, but all of them underwent the martyrdom most bravely, true heroes of the religion of the Cross. The couplet set over the entry asks the Trinity to receive, cut down for its glory, a double and triple company of sixty.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps them on the Julian 8 December, a day after the Greek calendar, under a single heading for the whole company — the holy martyrs in Africa — and names them by their number and their station: three hundred laymen beheaded. That word settles something the Greek page left open, since the other two groups of the same persecution were priests and these three hundred were not. The same sentence accounts for those two groups, two priests and sixty with their tongues cut out.

It dates the persecution by a ruler saint.gr does not name here: they suffered from the Arian heretics in the reign of the Vandal king Hunneric, or Genseric, whose years it gives as 477 to 484 — inside the reign of Zeno by which this folder was already dated, so nothing was moved. All of them suffered, it ends, but by their death they conquered the lie: they established Orthodoxy and left it to us pure and bright, and the Lord crowned them with the crown of glory in his immortal kingdom.

The Russian calendar keeps the whole company in one line and tells how the three hundred came to be in it. Under the emperor Zeno, whose reign its life page brackets 474 to 491 — the same bounds this folder already held out of the Greek — the ruler of the Vandal kingdom in North Africa, Hunneric, with the help of the Arian bishops raised a cruel persecution against the Orthodox. The faithful gathered in one of the churches and were secretly celebrating the divine liturgy when the troops of the barbarians burst in.

Some of those praying fled. Three hundred, the page says, the firmest in the true faith, gave themselves up voluntarily to torture and were beheaded; and these are the three hundred this folder keeps. The line closes with the year 477, which falls inside that reign and is not allowed to narrow it. Of the sixty-two priests of the same line, the page adds, two were burned and the rest had their tongues cut out, and by the wonder-working power of God those went on preaching.
