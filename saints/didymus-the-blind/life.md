# Didymus the Blind

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps him on the Julian 18 October, under one heading with [Julian of the Euphrates](/saints/julian-of-the-euphrates), and calls him in the heading itself what he is remembered as: Didymus the Blind. He was at Alexandria, and the entry sets him beside Julian because the two of them, far apart, were given the same thing to see.

At the very hour in which Julian the Apostate perished, the hermit by the Euphrates perceived it in the spirit and announced it to his disciples. His contemporary, the blind Didymus at Alexandria, likewise foresaw in the spirit the emperor’s destruction: he was at prayer in the night when a voice came to him from heaven saying, today the emperor Julian is no more — carry word of it to the patriarch [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria).

[Anthony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great) held this wonderful blind man in great esteem, the entry says, for the spirit of foresight that was in him; and whenever he came in from the desert to Alexandria he would call on him, and the two of them would pray to God together. Both Julian and Didymus, these wonderful servants of God, fell asleep after the year 362 — which is a floor and not a year, and is recorded on this folder as one.

*After the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 18. октобар ст. ст. — [the day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-10-31&prolog=1); read 6 October 2026.*
