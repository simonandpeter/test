# Christodoulos (4 December)

The Greek calendar keeps two martyrs of one name together on the fourth of December, and saint.gr's prose for them is a single sentence: the holy Christodoulos and Christodoule were martyred by the sword. Christodoulos is one of the two, and [Christodoule](/saints/christodoule) the other; the day's own line calls her the Virgin, and the page gives each of them a heading of their own in the «Άγιοι που εορτάζουν» block and no life apart.

Everything else the page says of them it says in the hymns printed beneath that sentence. The apolytikion calls them a pair who contended together, the flowers of the faith, given wings by the divine love, who contended with one mind and brought down the serpent. The kontakion calls them brave martyrs and brother and sister according to the flesh, and says they shamed the contrivances of the tyrants by the power of him who was crucified. The kathisma adds that they bore the clouds of torments patiently, were bound together in God by a brotherly affection, and received after their end an unending grace to heal sicknesses.

No year, no country, no emperor, no judge and no burial; no relic, and no couplet beyond the one set over the entry, which plays on the shared name — Christodoulos, bearing the same name as you, virgin Christodoule, contends beside you by the sword. Because the page names both of them, each has a folder here, and the other's is linked above.
