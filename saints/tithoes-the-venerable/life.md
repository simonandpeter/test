# Tithoes

Tithoes died in peace, the synaxarion says, and then lets Nikodemos the Hagiorite speak. Abba Matoes said of him that he was so blameless that no man could open his mouth to accuse him of anything — like pure, unalloyed gold weighed in the scale. He was a supreme lover of stillness: sitting once at Klysma with others, and thinking himself in his solitude, he said to his disciple, let the water out to the palms, child; we are at Klysma, abba, said the disciple; then what am I doing at Klysma, he said — take me back to the mountain. His is the saying that pilgrimage is for a man to hold his own tongue.

From that stillness he came to such perfection that, as the Paradise of the Fathers records, when he prayed, unless he brought his hands down quickly his mind was caught up into divine rapture; so when he happened to pray with other brothers he made haste to lower his hands, lest his mind be carried off, his prayer lengthen and his virtue be known.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 26 August — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/900/saint.aspx); read 23 August 2026.*
