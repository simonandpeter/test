# Anthony the New of Veria

Anthony was born into a devout and well-off family of Veria in Macedonia; the life says his birth date is not known. Burning for the ascetic life, he renounced from his youth the empty pleasures and comforts of the world and became a monk at the monastery of Pieria, which was then at its height. He soon became an example to the brethren in every monastic virtue, and, wanting the life of the desert, received his abbot's blessing to withdraw to a hidden cave near the river Aliakmon.

He lived there fifty years on the herbs that grew about the place, known to nobody but a priest who came from time to time to give him the Holy Mysteries. Like his illustrious namesake [Anthony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great), he bore for years the repeated assaults of the demons, who showed themselves to him in the most terrible shapes, or made him think the river was swelling to flood the cave, so that he would break off his unceasing prayer and leave his cell. He kept to this way until he was ninety, and gave up his spirit to God in the cave.

Some while after, hunters passing nearby had their attention caught by the barking of their dogs; they looked up to the cave hidden behind the leaves and saw a hand beckoning to them. They cleared the entrance and found the hermit's body incorrupt. The bishop of Veria came in haste with a great crowd, but the people of Pieria and of Veria fell into a dispute over who should keep the relics; to part them, the bishop had the body laid in a cart drawn by two oxen, as the Ark of the Covenant once was, and let it go where God's providence would take it. Without hesitating, the cart turned towards Veria and stopped in front of Anthony's family house, where a great church was afterwards raised in his honour.
