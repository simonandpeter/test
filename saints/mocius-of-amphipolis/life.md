# Mocius of Amphipolis

In the reign of Diocletian, when Laodicius and Maximus were proconsuls in Europe and the persecution of the Christians stood everywhere, there was a priest of the holy Church of God named Mocius in the city of Amphipolis in Macedonia. While the unbelievers were keeping the foul feast of Dionysus, he stood in the middle of the people and cried out to them: how long would they wander in the darkness of the madness for idols, and not see their own ruin, and not turn and know the Master God, made known through His only-begotten Son?

He said this not once but daily. The proconsul Laodicius, come from Apollonia to sacrifice to Dionysus, was told that this Christian teacher had turned many away from the feast, and had him seized. Brought to judgement, Mocius answered that from the Holy Scripture he had learned that the idols of the pagans are demons and that hope set on them is empty; and when he was told to sacrifice and save the life he was throwing away, he said: «O, antipate, moartea cea pentru Hristos îmi este mie mare câștig!» — O proconsul, death for Christ is great gain to me.

He was hung up naked and torn with iron claws from head to foot, and prayed aloud while they tore him. The torturers wearied, and the proconsul drove them off as idle and useless; the saint was loosed and stood before him stronger in body and bolder than before. A great furnace was made ready with pitch and tow and brushwood, its flame going up some sixty cubits and darkening the air with smoke, and still he would not sacrifice, answering that a thing made by men's hands, unmoving and without voice, is no god but an idol. The life then tells how the idol of Dionysus was broken and Laodicius burned; the saint said that not he but Almighty God had done it.

Sent on to the proconsul Maximus, he was bound between two torture-wheels to be broken, and said as they turned: how sweet is the love of God. He prayed, and the wheels shattered, and he was loosed whole; the tormentor, shamed, threw him into prison. After three days he was brought out to the spectacle and two lions roaring with hunger were let loose on him; they came near, bowed before him, and took his feet and licked them as though kissing them, and the people cried out that a righteous man whom God loves and the beasts honour should be set free.

Maximus sent him to the governor Philippus at Perinthus in Thrace, the city now called Heraclea, who kept him eight days in prison and then had him taken to Byzantium to be beheaded there. At the place of execution he lifted his eyes to heaven, blessed God who founded the ages and brings the struggles of the saints to their end, and asked that his soul be received in peace; and a voice came from heaven bidding him rejoice, for he had plainly overcome the tormentor's power, and come and dwell with the fathers in the heavenly kingdom.

He was beheaded on the eleventh day of the month of May. The emperor Constantine afterwards kept his feast, having built a church in his name and laid his honoured relics in it; and on the day of his memory he made the renewal of the city that he called by his own name, Constantinople.
