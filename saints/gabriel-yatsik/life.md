# Gabriel (Yatsik)

Gabriel was born on 20 November 1880 at Borzna in Chernigov province, the
son of the trader Peter Yatsik, who kept a small tannery. He finished the
teachers’ seminary at Kiev in 1901 and in 1906 entered the Trinity-Sergius
Lavra as a novice, serving in its printing house as assistant to the
manager. Mobilised in May 1917 as a private of the 56th regiment in the
Kremlin barracks, he was tonsured at the Lavra in 1918 with the name
Gabriel; in 1919 the Saratov Cheka arrested him on suspicion of being a
professional soldier and a White, and let him go when it had satisfied
itself that he was not. In 1920 he settled in the Donskoy monastery in
Moscow, where Patriarch Tikhon often served in those years and was held for
months under strict house arrest, and was ordained hierodeacon that year,
hieromonk in 1924 and archimandrite in 1929; when the monastery was closed
soon after, he was made rector of the church of Peter and Paul at
Petrovskoye-Lobanovo in the Krasnogorsk district. He was arrested on 7
September 1937 and put in the Taganka prison; the search had found among his
church books Farrar’s Early Days of Christianity and Nilus’s Great in Small,
and asked the next day what he used them for he said he had taken both from
the Lavra’s press in 1912, that Farrar was still his desk book and gave him
matter for sermons, that he kept Nilus for his collection, and that on the
persecution of the faith he had said only, persecution of the faith of
Christ was, is and will be; do not be surprised at it. The priest of the
neighbouring village testified that he outwardly imitated the lives of the
saints he read, that he was hostile to the Soviet power, and that he saw in
the spread of unbelief and the closing of monasteries and churches the sign
of the near coming of the kingdom of antichrist; the priest who served with
him called him a rabid enemy of the Soviet power. Asked whether he had
passed himself off at Khimki as a saint and healer, he said he had not, but
had read prayers when asked; on 20 September, asked for the last time
whether he admitted anti-Soviet agitation, he said no. The troika sentenced
him on 22 September 1937, and he was shot on 23 September 1937 at Butovo and
buried in an unknown common grave. The life is by the priest Maxim Maksimov;
the Church keeps him on 10 September of the old style, 23 September, and in
the synaxes of the new martyrs of Butovo, of Moscow and of Radonezh.
