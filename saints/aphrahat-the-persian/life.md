# Aphrahat the Persian

Aphrahat, called also Aphraates and surnamed the Persian Sage, is known, the Romanian page says, as the first Syriac church father. It puts his birth on the border of Syria with present-day Iraq, between 260 and 275, and says he was not only the first Orthodox Syrian writer but the first Christian to write a spiritual work under the rule of the Sassanid Persians, and that he carried out a real Christian mission without being either a bishop or the abbot of any monastery.

His work, the page says, consists of twenty-three homilies set in alphabetical order, together with one homily entitled *On the cluster of grapes*. What is known of his life comes from the second homily, where he says: «I praise God, who drew me out of the error of the idols»; from which the page concludes that before he became a Christian he was a pagan.

Becoming a Christian, he embraced the Gospel, gave himself to the service of Christ and led an ascetic life. He died a Christian martyr, put to the sword, about the year 345, when Shapur was king of Persia. The page gives no more than that, and nothing further is added here.
