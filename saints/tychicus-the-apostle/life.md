# Tychicus

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 8 decembrie reads «Sfântul Apostol Tihic», and his own page on that site carries the name, the date and nothing else about him: no life, no troparion, no kontakion, no country, no century, no word of where he preached or how he died. What the line does assert is that he was an apostle, which is what «Apostol» says, and that the Romanian church keeps his memory on this day.

What the page prints beside the name are the readings appointed for the commemoration: the epistle 1 Corinthians 4:9-16, in which Paul says that God has set the apostles forth last of all, as men condemned to death, made a spectacle to the world and to angels and to men; and the Gospel Luke 10:16-21, the sending and the return of the seventy — «Iar cei şaptezeci s-au întors cu...». Those are the readings the Romanian calendar gives an apostle, and they are the whole of what stands under his name.

Nothing further is set down here because nothing further was read. Other calendars carry more of him; none was consulted for this page, and the three other churches' rows say so.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 8 December with five other apostles of the Seventy in one entry, and names all six: [Sosthenes](/saints/sosthenes-the-apostle), [Cephas](/saints/cephas-the-apostle), [Apollos](/saints/apollos-the-apostle), Tychicus, [Caesar](/saints/caesar-the-apostle) and [Epaphroditus](/saints/epaphroditus-the-apostle). The apostle Paul calls Tychicus a beloved brother and a deacon in the Lord, saint.gr says, and their acquaintance must have been made at the end of Paul's third apostolic journey.

He preached the Gospel to the Christians of Ephesus and, by one tradition, in Cyprus also, where he was the first bishop of Neapolis-Lemesos; and when he had carried his mission through he returned again to Paul's side. After the martyrdom of that divine apostle, by another tradition, Tychicus held the see of Chalcedon, a city of Bithynia in Asia Minor. The page marks both sees as traditions and neither is recorded here as a fact. In the village of Mesana of the Kelokedara district in Paphos, it adds, there is a half-ruined church dedicated to Saint Tychicus, with two icons of him in it, one very old and one somewhat later, painted in 1830. His couplet warns against the pun his name invites: do not think this Tychicus a chance man, for he was one of the apostles.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, in a company of seven where the Greek calendar printed six, and it is the only one of the calendars to place him: he was the successor of [Sosthenes](/saints/sosthenes-the-apostle) in the same place, which it names Caesarea. It does not call him a bishop in so many words, and nothing has been added to this folder's ranks on the strength of the succession alone.

Of all seven together it says that they preached the Gospel of Christ with a burning love, that they suffered torments for his holy name, and that they passed over into the kingdom of everlasting joy. It records that all of them are celebrated on 4 January with the rest of the lesser apostles, and gives separate days to three of the others; for him it gives no second day and no year.
