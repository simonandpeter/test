# Cornelius of Paleostrov

Cornelius of Paleostrov was born in the city of Pskov in Russia and lived, saint.gr says, in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries. He practised his ascesis on the island of Pali in Lake Onega, where he founded a monastic community and raised a church in honour of the Nativity of the Mother of God. For a greater ascesis he went on to live inside a cave near the monastery, where he prayed without ceasing, and there he fell asleep in peace in 1420.

That is the whole of what the page holds about him: no parentage, no teacher, no relics and no hymn. saint.gr prints a second Cornelius on the same day, of Komel and of the next century, on a page of his own.

The Russian calendar gives him a birthplace the other books do not. He was born at Pskov, and at the end of the fourteenth century he was the founder of monastic life on the island of Палий in lake Onega. Remote as the island was, brethren soon gathered to him, and for them he built a church in honour of the Nativity of the Mother of God and a refectory church in honour of the prophet Илия. His last years he spent in a cave half a verst from the monastery, in continual prayer, and he weighted the struggle further by wearing heavy irons. He died about 1420, and his relics were carried into the monastery church by his disciple Авраамий Палеостровский, who is kept on 21 August, who was famous in his own right for the ascetic life, and who was afterwards buried beside his teacher in the same house. The calendar's own line and name link call him the abbot of that monastery.
