# Florus of Illyricum

Florus and his brother [Laurus](/saints/laurus-of-illyricum) were brothers in the flesh and in the spirit. They lived in the second century in Byzantium and then moved to Illyria; by trade they were stonemasons, taught the craft — and a God-pleasing life — by the Christians Proclus and Maximus. Lycaon, the governor of Illyria, sent them to a neighbouring district to work on a pagan temple that was being built; they laboured on the site, gave their earnings to the poor, kept a strict fast and prayed without ceasing.

One day the son of the local priest Mamertinus came carelessly up to the works and a splinter of stone struck and badly injured his eye; when the priest shouted at the brothers and would have beaten them, they told him that if he believed in the God they believed in his son would be healed — and they prayed with tears, signed the boy’s eye with the cross, and it was whole as before. Mamertinus and his son were baptised, and soon after suffered for Christ in the fire.

When the brothers had finished the temple they set a cross on it, called the Christians together and consecrated it in the name of the Lord Jesus with an all-night vigil; the governor of Illyria, learning of it, burned many of those Christians and had Florus and Laurus thrown alive into a well and the well filled with earth. Their relics were later revealed and taken to Constantinople. The Prologue tells it the same way for the Serbian calendar, with a long poem on the two brothers who cut stone and straightened souls with the cross.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 18 August with his brother. Doxologia.ro's life says Florus and [Laurus](/saints/laurus-of-illyricum) were brothers in the flesh and in the spirit, stonecutters by trade, taught both their craft and the Christian faith by two holy teachers named Proclus and Maximus, who were killed for Christ before them. A ruler of another country asked the governor of Illyricum for skilled masons to build a fine stone temple to the Greek gods, and the two, being more skilled than the rest, were sent. When all was known, the magistrate seized them and those with them, among whom was Memertinus with his son; he condemned the others to the fire, and sent Florus and Laurus, cruelly beaten, to Lykion, governor of Illyricum, who found them unmoved in the Christian faith and had them thrown into a deep well and covered over with earth. Many years later their relics were found, pouring out healings of every sickness, and were carried with honour to Constantinople.

The Greek calendar keeps the two brothers on the same day, 18 August, and adds the detail of their teachers and their craft. saint.gr says they were twins, inseparably joined by the warm faith and love they had towards Christ, that they came of Byzantium, and that they had been taught both Christianity and the stonemason's art by Proclus — or Patroclus, according to Saint Nikodemos — and Maximus, who themselves suffered a martyr's death for Christ. After their teachers died the two withdrew into Illyria and chose the city of Ulpiana to live in, working their craft there and using it at the same time to spread the Gospel. A priest of the idols there named Merentios had a son, Athanasius, blind in one eye, for whom medicine had found no cure; the brothers called on the name of Christ and healed it, and father and son both believed.

When the eparch Lykon learned of it he seized the two, tortured them horribly and threw them into a well, where they gave up their spirits — and so, the page says, the two craftsmen entered the eternal city «ἧς τεχνίτης καὶ δημιουργὸς ὁ Θεός», whose craftsman and builder is God. Faithful Christians gathered their relics and carried them to the Queen of Cities. The Greek calendar also keeps on this day the [Poor Martyrs](/saints/poor-martyrs), whom its page says were given the money for a temple by the two brothers and burned by Licinius for pulling its idols down.
