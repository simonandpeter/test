# Florus of Illyricum

Florus and his brother [Laurus](/saints/laurus-of-illyricum) were brothers in the flesh and in the spirit. They lived in the second century in Byzantium and then moved to Illyria; by trade they were stonemasons, taught the craft — and a God-pleasing life — by the Christians Proclus and Maximus. Lycaon, the governor of Illyria, sent them to a neighbouring district to work on a pagan temple that was being built; they laboured on the site, gave their earnings to the poor, kept a strict fast and prayed without ceasing. One day the son of the local priest Mamertinus came carelessly up to the works and a splinter of stone struck and badly injured his eye; when the priest shouted at the brothers and would have beaten them, they told him that if he believed in the God they believed in his son would be healed — and they prayed with tears, signed the boy’s eye with the cross, and it was whole as before. Mamertinus and his son were baptised, and soon after suffered for Christ in the fire. When the brothers had finished the temple they set a cross on it, called the Christians together and consecrated it in the name of the Lord Jesus with an all-night vigil; the governor of Illyria, learning of it, burned many of those Christians and had Florus and Laurus thrown alive into a well and the well filled with earth. Their relics were later revealed and taken to Constantinople. The Prologue tells it the same way for the Serbian calendar, with a long poem on the two brothers who cut stone and straightened souls with the cross.
