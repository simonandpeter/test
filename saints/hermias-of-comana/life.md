# Hermias of Comana

There was a great persecution against the Christians in the reign of Antoninus, emperor of Rome, doxologia's life says, and he sent out a governor named Sebastian to torment them. Travelling from Cilicia to Cappadocia, Sebastian found at Comana an old soldier called Hermias, white-haired and a Christian by law, believing in the one true God and Maker of all. He showed him the emperor's letters, which ordered all Christians to sacrifice to the gods of the Romans, and told him to sacrifice and be the emperor's friend and have much honour.

Hermias answered that he was a soldier of Christ, the heavenly and deathless King whose kingdom will have no end, and would not submit to a mortal and pagan emperor whose reign is of a little while. He had served Christ in secret before, he said, and served Him openly now. You have authority over my body, and that by my God's leave; over my soul no one has power but God alone. Told to sacrifice and enjoy the gladness of this life, he asked what gladness it would be to turn from his Maker and worship demons: not gladness but grief, not sweetness but bitterness, not life but everlasting death.

Sebastian said he spared him for his grey hairs and his understanding, and Hermias answered that he needed no mercy from him, for he sought the mercy of God. The governor then had his jaws broken with stones, his teeth smashed and the skin flayed from his face, and the martyr thanked God that the grace of his Lord Jesus Christ remained with him. Asked why he never drew his soldier's pay as the others did, he said that because they robbed the poor unjustly he would not take their wages or eat of them, having spiritual food from the Holy Spirit.

He was thrown into a furnace heated fiercely, and after three days it was opened and he was found alive and whole, singing and glorifying God, the fire not having touched him. The governor then called a sorcerer and ordered him to kill Hermias with poison. He prayed, drank it, and suffered nothing. The sorcerer prepared a stronger poison and said that if this too did him no harm he would leave his arts and believe in the crucified God whom he served; and Hermias drank the whole of it and was unhurt.

The sorcerer cried out that Hermias had won, that his own lost soul was brought out of hell, and that, grown old in sins and wickedness, he was made new in turning to the living God. The governor in his anger had him beheaded at once, so that he was baptised in his own blood and went to God as a new Christian and a martyr. Hermias he ordered tortured further, the veins drawn out of his whole body. I feel no pain in my body from these torments, the martyr told him; as the physician's little knife cuts the veins and lets out the unhealthy blood and makes the body well, so I, in the tearing out of my veins, am the healthier in the faith of Christ.

He was thrown into a cauldron of scalding oil, and the cauldron cooled at once and the boiling oil became like cold dew. Strong vinegar mixed with gall was poured down his throat, and he said the bitterness was to him like a honeycomb from his God, for whom he bore it. When the governor ordered his eyes put out, he said: if you have need of my bodily eyes, which see the vanity of this world, take them for yourself; I have the eyes of the heart, with which I see the true light clearly. So they put out his eyes.

Then they hung him head downwards and kept him so for three days, and much blood ran from his nostrils. After the three days some went to see him, thinking him dead; finding him alive and glorifying God they were terrified, and for their unbelief a darkness fell on their eyes and they were blinded. They cried out to him for mercy, and he told them to come near, laid his hands on them and said: in the name of my Lord Jesus Christ, I tell you, see. They saw at once, and went back and told the governor what had happened to them.

Burning with anger, he ordered the skin flayed from the martyr's whole body. Hermias mocked him and reviled the unclean gods, and the tyrant, roaring like a lion, cut off his honoured head with a knife, and so the saint ended. Christians came secretly, took his body and his head, and laid them in Cappadocia at a place called Coman, where many miracles were worked at his honoured relics. The life names the emperor Antoninus and prints no year, so none is recorded here.
