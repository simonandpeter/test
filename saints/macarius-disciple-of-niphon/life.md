# Macarius the New, disciple of Patriarch Niphon, Monk-martyr (1527)

Where Macarius came from and who his parents were is not known. He was a disciple of Niphon, patriarch of Constantinople, who died in 1508 and is kept on 11 August, and imitated his life; reaching the height and perfection of divine love, his heart burned daily with longing to be counted worthy to end his life by a martyr’s death. He revealed this God-inspired longing to his holy teacher, who was then again at the monastery of Vatopedi, and Niphon, knowing that the desire was indeed by God’s will, admonished him fittingly, prayed over him, blessed him, kissed him and sent him off on the road of martyrdom: go, child, on the road of martyrdom, for according to your eagerness you will be counted worthy to receive the crown of the contest and to rejoice for ever with the martyrs and the righteous. Macarius had accompanied his elder, with his fellow-disciple Joasaph, kept on 26 October, on his long and fruitful missionary journeys in Macedonia and Wallachia, and in 1505 they came and settled at Vatopedi for two years — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here. The Romanian life says he stayed steadfast in his Orthodoxy and for it was condemned to death and beheaded, on 14 September 1527; the Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on the feast of the Exaltation.
