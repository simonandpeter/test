# The Fourteen Thousand Infants of Bethlehem

doxologia.ro's line for 29 decembrie names a company and not its members — «Sfinții 14.000 de prunci uciși din porunca lui Irod» — and the life it links names no infant among them, so this folder is the company. Herod, seeing that he had been mocked by the magi, was greatly angered; and the life asks against whom. Against the magi, who had not come back to tell him about the child; and against Christ, for fear that he should take his kingdom, since Herod supposed that Christ meant to reign with an earthly reign, not knowing that his kingdom is not of this world.

So, being able neither to repay the magi, because they had gone, nor to kill Christ, because he could not find him, the wretched man poured out his anger on the innocent infants — like a cruel beast that, when it is wounded by someone and does not mark the one who wounded it, tears whatever comes before its eyes as though that were the one. Sending armed soldiers as though to war, he killed all the children who were in Bethlehem and in all its borders, from two years old and under, according to the time he had ascertained from the magi.

The infants were killed in many ways: some were cut down with the sword, some killed against stone and wall, some thrown to the ground and trampled underfoot, some strangled with the hands, some torn and split; some were run through with spears and some cut in two. The mothers wept bitterly and cried up to heaven, tearing their hair, their clothes and their bodies, so that what the prophet Jeremiah said was fulfilled: a voice was heard in Rama, weeping and lamentation and much crying, Rachel weeping for her children, and she could not be comforted, because they were no more.

Rama, the life explains, was a small city called so because it stood in a high place within the borders of Benjamin; and Rachel was reckoned by some to be Bethlehem, because Rachel, the wife of the patriarch Jacob and the mother of Benjamin, was buried there. So after Rachel's tomb Bethlehem had got the name of Rachel, and when infants were being killed in Rachel, that is in Bethlehem, it was Rachel who wept.

The middle of the page — some six thousand characters of it — was not read; what it prints at the end is Herod's other killings. Besides the infants he cut down seventy most wise men, who made up the council called the Sanhedrin, leaving only one of them alive, and him he ordered blinded. There were then in Jerusalem two most wise teachers, renowned and loved by all, Iuda called the son of Avrifa and Matei Garguloti, and these, with two other friends of theirs, he burned alive.

Before that he had risen cruelly against his own house, for he killed his brother Feror, his sister Salomi and her husband, who was of the tribe of David, and his own wife Mariamna, who was likewise of the tribe of David, and Antipatru her son, and two sons, Aristobol and Alexandru, and put many other innocent people to death. At the last, when the wretch was giving up his soul, he ordered his most trusted soldiers to kill, at the moment of his own death, all the other honoured men of the Jews — of whom not a few were in chains — so that the Jews should not rejoice at his death but rather weep, seeing so many of their own dead; and so it was done.

So the evil man perished evilly and with a great noise, and destroyed many along with him, and he with those who were of his mind took up his dwelling in hell; but of such infants as these, the life ends, is the kingdom of heaven. The day's line gives no year, and the page the site keeps for the company's troparion is an audio recording of the hymn in Greek and prints no text.

The Greek calendar keeps them on the same day, 29 December, and tells the killing in its own words. When the Magi did not go back to Herod to tell him where Christ was, saint.gr says, that cunning king contrived another plan to destroy the divine Infant. He had heard that according to the Scriptures the place of Christ's birth would be Bethlehem; but because he did not know which child Jesus was, or whether he was inside Bethlehem or in its surrounding country, and because he reckoned the child would be under two years old, he gave the order that all the children of Bethlehem and of the country round it, up to the age of two, should be slaughtered.

The slaughter was made suddenly, the page says, so that the families should not be able to get away with their infants; and the wretched mothers saw their children killed in their own arms. The Christian Church very rightly declared these slaughtered children saints, because they died at an innocent age and were in a manner the first martyrs of Christianity: they may not have been baptised in water, but they were baptised in the blessed blood of their own martyrdom.

The Охридски пролог keeps them on 29. децембар ст. ст. and heads the day with them: „Светих четрнаест хиљада младенаца витлејемских.“ Because the wise men of the east did not go back from Bethlehem to Jerusalem to tell Herod of the newborn King, but went home another way by the command of an angel, Herod raged like a fierce beast, and gave orders that all the children of two years old and under be killed in Bethlehem and in the whole country round about it.

«Та страшна заповест царева би извршена дословце» — that dreadful order of the emperor’s was carried out to the letter. His soldiers cut some of the children down with the sword, broke some against a stone, crushed some underfoot and strangled some with their hands. And the weeping and wailing of the mothers rose up to heaven: „Глас у Рами чу се, плач и ридање и нарицање много, Рахила оплакује децу своју, и неће да се утјеши, јер их нема“ — a voice was heard in Ramah, weeping and lamentation and much wailing, Rachel weeping for her children and refusing to be comforted, because they are not; as had been prophesied, the book says, citing Jeremiah 31:15 and Matthew 2:18.

«Овај злочин над многом невином децом изврши се годину дана по рођењу Христовом 29. децембра» — this crime against so many innocent children was done a year after the birth of Christ, on 29 December. That is a distance from the Nativity rather than a year, and it is recorded here and written into no field; the day is the one this folder already keeps.

The second half of the entry is not about the children but about what became of the man who killed them, and the people it names are left here in the book’s own letters, because the heading names the children alone and none of these suffered with them. Herod went on searching for the divine Child; he demanded of Захарија his son Јован, meaning to kill him, supposing Јован to be the new King, and when Захарија would not give him up Захарија himself was killed in the temple at Herod’s order. Свети Симеон Богопримац would have been killed too, had he not been taken to God soon after the Meeting. Having killed the children of Bethlehem, Herod turned on the Jewish elders who had expounded to him where the Messiah was to be born, and so killed Иркан the high priest and seventy elders of the Sanhedrin.

After them he killed his own brother and sister and wife and three of his sons. At the last the punishment of God overtook him: he began to shake all over, his legs swelled, the lower part of his body festered and worms came out of the festering, his nose closed up, and an unbearable stench spread from him. At the very point of death he remembered them, and that they might not rejoice at his death he gave orders that they all be killed. So this dreadful ruler cast out his inhuman soul and handed it over to the devil as his everlasting property.
