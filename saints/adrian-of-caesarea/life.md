# Adrian of Caesarea

Adrian and [Eubulus](/saints/eubulus-of-caesarea) came, saint.gr says, from the country of Vanea — that is, the part of Armenia around Lake Van. They wanted to see for themselves the martyrs and confessors of Christ at Caesarea, and set out for that city. There they made it known that they were Christians, and were taken straight before the governor Firmilian, in front of whom they confessed Christ with great courage.

For that they were beaten without mercy on the back and the ribs. Led to the governor a second time, they confessed Christ again; and he, enraged, threw them to the beasts for food. Adrian was set to fight a lion, and when he came away unhurt he was beheaded. Eubulus went through exactly the same, and his end too was the sword. So the two of them together received the unfading crowns of eternal glory.

The page gives no year and no century for them, and prints no hymn; over Adrian it sets a couplet that the executioner's hand, which rejoices in killing, cuts down an Adrian who rejoices to be cut with the sword.

The Serbian church keeps the two of them on the Julian 3 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue answers three things the Greek page left open and contradicts it in a fourth. The Caesarea is Caesarea in Cappadocia, where the Russian calendar's name link says Palestine; the year is 309, where saint.gr gives none and the Russian line has about 308 to 309; and the two came from their own place of Vanea not to look at the martyrs but to visit the Christians shut up in the prison, to comfort them and give them heart. For that they were taken themselves and condemned to death — and here Adrian is cut down with the sword and Eubulus thrown to the beasts, where saint.gr has both of them come away from the beasts unhurt and both end by the sword. Both readings are left standing as they were read.
