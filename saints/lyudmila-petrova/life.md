# Lyudmila (Petrova)

Lyudmila Vladimirovna Petrova was born on 26 February 1879 at Rostov, the
daughter of a prison warder, and worked all her life as a teacher of
needlework, retiring in 1924. On 15 November 1930 she was arrested as a
member of an anti-Soviet church-monarchist group who kept up personal links
with its ideologues, Metropolitan Joseph (Petrovykh) and Archbishop Eugene,
and ran the illegal collection of money for the group and for the exiles; on
30 April 1931 the OGPU college gave her three years’ exile in the Northern
region, she was sent to Kotlas in Archangel region, and she came home to
Rostov when the term was done. In 1936 she was arrested again for
corresponding with the exiled Metropolitan Joseph and sending him help, and
the Special Board of the NKVD gave her three years’ exile in Kazakhstan,
where she was held in a labour camp. At Merke in Kazakhstan she was arrested
once more in the group case of Archbishop Alexis (Orlov) and others, charged
above all with belonging to a counter-revolutionary group of churchmen in
the South Kazakhstan region; she admitted nothing and said she had engaged
in no anti-Soviet activity. On 28 August 1937 the troika of the South
Kazakhstan NKVD sentenced her to death, and on 27 September 1937 she was
shot in the Lisya Balka, the Fox Gully, near Chimkent, the same day and
place as the nun [Maria Rykova](/saints/maria-rykova). She is kept on 15
September of the old style, 28 September, and in the synaxis of the
Rostov-Yaroslavl saints.
