# John of Prislop

Prislop has played from its beginnings to this day a notable part in strengthening the spiritual life of the Hațeg country, the Strei valley and the “land of the foresters” near it, and to the monastery’s past is bound the memory of a saint, honoured as such by the Orthodox of those parts to this day. It is said that a young man named John, from the village of Silvașul de Sus, within whose bounds the monastery stands, left his parents’ house and entered the community of the monks of Prislop, leading a chosen life of prayer woven with work and good deeds; after some years, wishing a quieter life wholly withdrawn from the world, he found a place some five hundred metres from the monastery on the precipitous bank of the river Slivuț, where he dug himself with great labour a cell in the rock, known to this day as the cell or house of the saint, and there lived the rest of his days in unceasing prayer and fasting, like the great spiritual strivers of the first Christian centuries. But it was God’s will that his life should end too soon: popular tradition says that as he was making a window in his cell two hunters on the other side of the ravine shot him, not knowing who he was — so ended the life of the hermit, the saint John of Prislop, as Father Mircea Păcurariu tells it. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 13 September with a troparion and kontakion — in the cave, the kontakion says, you made friends with the cold of the stone.
