# Polycarp of Bryansk

saint.gr gives the venerable Polycarp of Bryansk one sentence, and it is mostly a statement of what the site does not know: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Ρώσου Αγίου παρά μόνο ότι κοιμήθηκε το 1620 ή 1621 μ.Χ.» — we have no details of the Russian saint's life except that he fell asleep in 1620 or 1621. The day's line adds the place his name is kept with, «του Μπρυάνσκ», of Bryansk.

Nothing else stands on the page. It gives no year of birth, no monastery, no office, no foundation, no relic and no hymn, and it does not choose between the two years it offers for his repose. The same day page keeps Polycarp of Smyrna, and the two are not the same man.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this day and says who it supposes him to have been. In the world, it is thought, he was prince Peter Ivanovich Boryatinsky, a descendant of [Michael of Chernigov](/saints/michael-of-chernigov); the supposition rests on the part the Boryatinskys took in the affairs of the Saviour-Transfiguration monastery at Bryansk, and prince Peter's name is met with often in the acts of the sixteenth century. He was sent with others to the river Sestra to make war on the king of Sweden; in 1576 he was appointed governor of Tula; in 1580, set as governor at Kholm, he was taken prisoner by the Lithuanians together with the commander of the siege; and after his return from captivity he fell into disgrace.

In 1591 he was appointed governor of Tyumen, and some years later he left the world, withdrew to Bryansk and was tonsured with the name Polycarp. At his own cost he built the monastery in honour of the Lord's Transfiguration and led a strict ascetic life in it, and he was its first superior; there he died, in 1620 or 1621, and was buried. The calendar sets two kontakia for him on the day and no troparion.
