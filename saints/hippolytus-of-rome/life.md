# Hippolytus of Rome

Hippolytus was the chief and keeper of the prisons in Rome under Decius and Valerian. Converted to Christ by the martyr [Lawrence](/saints/lawrence-of-rome), he buried his body — and this was reported to the emperor, who had him arrested and asked him laughing whether he too had turned sorcerer, to steal Lawrence’s body. He confessed himself a Christian. They beat him cruelly with rods, and the only answer was the words, repeated: I am a Christian.

The emperor had him dressed in his military uniform and said, remember your rank and be our friend, sacrifice to the gods with us as before; I am a soldier of Christ my Saviour, he answered, and I wish to die for him. Then all his property was taken, his nurse, the martyr [Concordia](/saints/concordia-of-rome), was beaten to death with leaden rods, all his household beheaded before his eyes, and he himself tied to wild horses, which dragged him over the stones until he died. This was on 13 August 258, the third day after the archdeacon Lawrence, as Lawrence had foretold to him.

By night the presbyter Justin buried the martyrs secretly on the place of execution; Concordia’s body had been thrown into a sewer, and after a time two Christians, [Irenaeus](/saints/irenaeus-of-rome) and [Abundius](/saints/abundius-of-rome), learned from a soldier where, and buried it beside Hippolytus — for which, on 26 August, they were drowned as she had been; the Christians took the bodies by night and buried them by the relics of Lawrence.

The Prologue tells it for the Serbian calendar with more of the conversion: Hippolytus, a pagan, saw Lawrence give the blind Lucillus his sight in the prison, believed, was baptised with his whole house — nineteen, with the old nurse Concordia — and had a vision of innocent souls in great joy; stripped before the emperor he said, you have not stripped me but begun to clothe me; and Concordia answered for the household, we would rather die honourably with our master in the faith of Christ than live dishonourably with you.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 10 august, with Lawrence, and its short page adds the burial. Hippolytus was a Roman soldier, the guard of the prison where the Christians were held; seeing the many healings Lawrence worked there he was baptised with his whole household. It was he who, with a priest named Justin, took Lawrence's body and buried it in a cave on the land of a Christian woman named Chiriachi outside Rome. Unshaken, he confessed himself a Christian, stood up to the emperor Valerian, and was beheaded on 13 August 258, three days after Lawrence; and Justin buried him in secret outside the city, beside the Salarian way.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 August, in the one line it gives the three Romans, and it tells his death in a single sentence that follows straight on the archdeacon's. saint.gr says that a certain pious Christian, Hippolytus, took up Lawrence's body after the burning; and that when the governor learned of it he gave the order, and wild horses dragged Hippolytus through thorns, and so he too finished his life in martyrdom.

The page gives him his own distich, which turns on the name: «Τὸν Ἱππόλυτον ἱπποδέσμιον βλέπω, / Ἐναντίαν πάσχοντα τῇ κλήσει πάθος» — I see Hippolytus bound to horses, suffering a suffering contrary to his name, for the name means the one who looses horses. It dates the three to 253 rather than the 258 printed above, gives no year of birth, and prints no hymn that names him.
