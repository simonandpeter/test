# Hippolytus of Rome

Hippolytus was the chief and keeper of the prisons in Rome under Decius and Valerian. Converted to Christ by the martyr [Lawrence](/saints/lawrence-of-rome), he buried his body — and this was reported to the emperor, who had him arrested and asked him laughing whether he too had turned sorcerer, to steal Lawrence’s body. He confessed himself a Christian. They beat him cruelly with rods, and the only answer was the words, repeated: I am a Christian. The emperor had him dressed in his military uniform and said, remember your rank and be our friend, sacrifice to the gods with us as before; I am a soldier of Christ my Saviour, he answered, and I wish to die for him. Then all his property was taken, his nurse, the martyr [Concordia](/saints/concordia-of-rome), was beaten to death with leaden rods, all his household beheaded before his eyes, and he himself tied to wild horses, which dragged him over the stones until he died. This was on 13 August 258, the third day after the archdeacon Lawrence, as Lawrence had foretold to him.

By night the presbyter Justin buried the martyrs secretly on the place of execution; Concordia’s body had been thrown into a sewer, and after a time two Christians, [Irenaeus](/saints/irenaeus-of-rome) and [Abundius](/saints/abundius-of-rome), learned from a soldier where, and buried it beside Hippolytus — for which, on 26 August, they were drowned as she had been; the Christians took the bodies by night and buried them by the relics of Lawrence. The Prologue tells it for the Serbian calendar with more of the conversion: Hippolytus, a pagan, saw Lawrence give the blind Lucillus his sight in the prison, believed, was baptised with his whole house — nineteen, with the old nurse Concordia — and had a vision of innocent souls in great joy; stripped before the emperor he said, you have not stripped me but begun to clothe me; and Concordia answered for the household, we would rather die honourably with our master in the faith of Christ than live dishonourably with you.
