# Vincent of Spain

Vincent's country was Spain. From his youth he gave himself to the service of God and laboured at the divine books, learning the law of the Lord day and night. He had a wise teacher full of good works, the blessed Valerius, bishop of Augustopolis, who, seeing his disciple's good understanding and chosen manners, ordained him deacon and made him a preacher of the word of God. For the bishop, though very skilled in the divine Scriptures, had an impediment in his speech and was not clear of utterance, and so charged his deacon, who was worthy, wise and well-spoken, with teaching the people in church. And Vincent, having his bishop's command and blessing, taught diligently not only in church but in every place he happened to be, and guided human souls on the way of salvation.

In that time the pagan emperor Diocletian sent into Spain a judge named Datian, a Greek by race, evil by nature, a cruel persecutor and tormentor of Christians, sent to kill without mercy all who called on the name of Christ. Coming into a city called Valentia he shed much Christian blood, seizing the sheep of Christ like a wolf. Hearing of Valerius the bishop and Vincent the deacon, who were at Augustopolis, he sent his soldiers to bring them to him bound for judgement. They were both taken and bound with heavy irons, and tormented on the road with hunger and thirst; and many times, unable to run fast beside the horses they were tied to, they fell and were dragged along the road like logs.

After his death the judge had the martyr's body thrown out for the birds and the beasts to eat, and set guards at a distance so that the Christians should not steal it. But God, who keeps His saints, set an unaccustomed guard over the martyr's body: He commanded a raven to watch it, and when the crowds of carrion birds came and fell upon the body the raven drove them all away and let not one of them eat, although a raven by its nature loves to eat dead bodies — held back by the power of God, it did not touch the body itself and let no other bird touch it. The most wonderful thing of all was that it drove away a wolf that tried to carry the body off.

The guard reported it to Datian, and the pagan wondered greatly, but would not acknowledge the power of God: he ordered the holy body thrown into the sea. The soldiers put it in a ship, carried it far out and threw it into the deep, and were returning to the shore; but when they were near the edge they saw the martyr's body lying again on the beach, and fled in terror. The Christians took it and buried it with honour. The Romanian life gives no year; it places him by the reign of Diocletian and by nothing else.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 11 November, and gives the two cities of his life. saint.gr has Vincent born at Huesca in Spain and given to letters from a young age; a soul burning with the divine flame, he attached himself from his teens to Valerius, the bishop of Zaragoza. Valerius, valuing his purity, his good sense and his devout zeal, ordained him deacon at Augustopolis — Zaragoza — and because the bishop, for all his learning, had no fluency of speech, he often used Vincent as a teacher of the divine word; which is why Valerius used to say pleasantly that Vincent was his voice in the Church. With time, the page says, Vincent increased in the power of his speech, in the heat of his zeal and in his tireless effort to draw others. He is one of the three martyrs this day whom a single apolytikion honours, with Menas, Victor and Stephanida.
