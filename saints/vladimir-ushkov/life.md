# Vladimir (Ushkov)

The Russian calendar prints three words for him and no life: «Мч. Владимира (1942)» — the martyr Vladimir, 1942 — and its index of names adds the surname and what he was, «Владимир (Ушков), псаломщик». He was born in 1885 at Konobeyevo in the Bronnitsy district of the Moscow province, the son of the peasant Yegor Ushkov. He finished the village school, married when he came of age, and had four children; and because the family was large he carted goods besides working the land. Called up in 1914 to the First World War, he was badly wounded, lost an arm, and was discharged. After the revolution, looking for a way to live, he set up a workshop making buttons, and in the twenties he was elected warden of the Trinity church in his own village.

In 1930 the authorities listed and took away everything he owned, and then demanded that he pay his taxes. Having nothing to pay them with, he was arrested on 2 February 1930 and sentenced to five years in a corrective labour camp and five years of exile. He appealed, and in April a decision was taken to release him, on the ground that if he had traded at all he had done it «не систематически, а лишь потому, чтобы иметь средства к существованию, так как обременен многочисленным семейством» — not systematically but only to have the means to live, being burdened with a numerous family — and that Ushkov himself, an invalid of the imperialist war, could not hold regular work. He was let out of the Kolomna prison on 25 April 1930, went back to the village and took up his duties as warden again, and from the second half of the thirties he served in the church as psalmist.

He was arrested on 19 January 1938, and after short questionings in the district prison was shut in the Taganka prison in Moscow. The investigator took evidence from the chairman of the local collective farm, who testified that Ushkov belonged to the farm but did not come to work, that he said working in the collective farm was a sin, that he carried on religious agitation, and that he said it was forbidden to work in the farm's fields on church feasts and a great sin to do it; and the chairman pointed to his serving in the church as itself proof of his criminality.

On 23 January the investigator questioned him. Asked how long he had been psalmist of the Konobeyevo church, he said about a year, and a member of the church council before that. Asked how often he visited the church's priest, he said that he met him mostly in church, but had been at his house too, and stayed the night there when the priest was away from the village. Asked whether he had met any outsiders at the priest's house, he said he had met nobody. Told that he had carried on counter-revolutionary activity at Konobeyevo through 1937 and asked whether he admitted it, he said that he did not. The investigator read out the witnesses' statements and asked whether he granted that they were just, and he rejected every one of them, saying that everything written in them was false.

The investigation closed on 9 July 1938, and on 19 September the Special Conference of the NKVD sentenced him to five years in a corrective labour camp. He died in custody in Oneglag on 21 March 1942 and was buried in an unmarked grave. That civil day is the Julian 8 March the calendar keeps him on, so the day of his death is his feast.
