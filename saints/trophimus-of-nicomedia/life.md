# Trophimus of Nicomedia

When the persecution of the Christians at Nicomedia was at its height, in the days of the emperor Maximian, Trophimus and [Eucarpion](/saints/eucarpion-of-nicomedia) were soldiers there. They were strong of body and bold, and because they had not yet come to know the Christian teaching they persecuted the Christians themselves and became their bitterest enemies, arresting them, shutting them in prison, and having every power over them, tormenting whom they chose.

Going out one day to take some Christians, they saw a great fire come down upon them out of heaven like a cloud, and heard a voice out of the fire: why did they hasten to bring ruin on His servants? They should not deceive themselves, for no one would be able to break those who had believed in Him; better that they join them, and gain the kingdom of heaven. At that the two, who had been full of cruelty and arrogance, fell to the ground and could no longer look or listen, and from where they lay said only that great indeed was the God who had appeared to them that day, and that they would be blessed if they became His servants.

The cloud of fire divided in two, and the voice said, Rise and repent, and your sins shall be forgiven you. Rising, they saw standing in the middle of the cloud one clothed in white and altogether beautiful, with a great multitude about him, and they said with one voice that their sins were many and past counting, for they had rushed like drunkards against the true God and His servants — and asked to be received. Then the cloud closed again and went up into heaven; and they wept much, turned back from their road, embraced as brothers all those they had imprisoned, bowed to them, and told them to go home.

The ruler of the place heard of it, was filled with anger and had them brought before him, and asked why they had changed. When they had told the whole vision in every detail, he had them hung on wood, their sides torn with iron hooks and the wounds rubbed with hair-cloth. They bore it bravely, praying and giving thanks with joy, at which he grew angrier still and ordered a furnace kindled in the middle of the city and the saints thrown into it; and entering it they received there the crown of martyrdom.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them on the same day and on one page, and tells the turn of their lives more briefly than doxologia does. saint.gr says they were soldiers at Nicomedia who lived in the years of Maximian, about the year 298; that they were idolaters at first and persecuted the Christians harshly, taking their part in the imprisonments and the torturing; and that Christ worked his wonder upon these persecutors too, his grace opening their eyes and bringing them to faith in him. Now fervent Christians and heralds of their Redeemer, they proclaimed his name freely and set about multiplying the ranks of his followers. When they were denounced at Nicomedia they stood firm in their confession and were condemned to death: first their flesh was torn with iron implements, and then they were put to death by being thrown into a burning furnace. The page prints a couplet on the name — Eucarpion and Trophimus, the prizewinners, found in the furnace unending food and fruit — and no hymn.

The Russian calendar's reading gives the two of them a conversion the other calendars leave out. They were soldiers at Nicomedia under Diocletian, and the page says plainly that they went about the emperor's decrees with great cruelty. Hunting Christians one day, they saw a great cloud of fire come down out of the sky, thickening as it drew near, and a Voice came out of it: why did they press so hard upon the servants of God, when no power of theirs could bend those who believed, and they would do better to join them and gain the Kingdom.

They fell on their faces, not daring to lift their eyes, and said to one another that the God who had appeared was truly great. Told to rise and repent, they saw within the cloud a Shining Man seated and a great multitude standing before him, and they cried out that there was no other God but him. The cloud closed and went up, and the two soldiers opened the prison and let every Christian in it go free. For that they were hung up and torn with iron hooks, and when the fire was lit they walked into it themselves.
