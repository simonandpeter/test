# The Two Priests of Carthage

saint.gr's calendar for 7 December keeps two priests whom it counts and does not name. They were martyred at Carthage, the page says, in the years of the emperor Zeno, which it dates 474 to 476, and when the ruler there was Honorichos the Arian. Having arrested them, their persecutors put them to a martyr's death by sawing them in two.

The couplet set over the entry says that the two priests chose rather to be sawn through, and eagerly, than to abandon their own worship. That is the whole of the page: no names, no origin, no age, no relic and no hymn. The other entries of the same day call the persecutors at Carthage Monophysites rather than Arians, and that difference is the source's own and is left as it stands.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps them on the Julian 8 December, a day after the Greek calendar, under a single heading for the whole company — the holy martyrs in Africa — and in one sentence it accounts for all three of the groups that calendar keeps as three entries: two priests burned, sixty with their tongues cut out, three hundred laymen beheaded. On the manner of these two it does not agree with the Greek page: this book says they were burned, where that one has them sawn in two. Neither reading is chosen here.

It also dates the persecution by a different ruler. They suffered, it says, from the Arian heretics in the reign of the Vandal king Hunneric, or Genseric, whose years it gives as 477 to 484, where saint.gr dates them by the emperor Zeno and brackets his reign 474 to 476. All of them suffered, the book ends, but by their death they conquered the lie: they established Orthodoxy and left it to us pure and bright, and the Lord crowned them with the crown of glory in his immortal kingdom.
