# Aristobulus of Britain

doxologia.ro's calendar names Aristobulus among the apostles it keeps on 31 October, and the life it prints for him is a single sentence: he was bishop in Britain, preaching the Gospel of Christ, and after many labours and ascetic struggles gave up his blessed soul into the hands of the Lord. The sentence is written in the plural at its end, because the same formula serves several of the apostles named on this day.

That is the whole of what the page says. It gives no year, no account of how he came to Britain, no manner of death and no place of burial, and names nobody beside him. doxologia.ro prints no separate troparion for him. The hymn on his page is the one it gives to all six apostles it names on this day — Apelles, Stachys, Ampliatus, Urban, Aristobulus and Narcissus together — a short common of apostles in the third tone, asking them to entreat the merciful God for the forgiveness of our offences.

The Greek calendar keeps the six of them together on the same day, 31 October, and saint.gr gives most of them a see. All six belonged to the seventy Apostles of the Lord, it says, and all were «Χριστοῦ εὐωδία τῷ Θεῷ ἐν τοῖς σωζομένοις», from 2 Corinthians 2.15. Of this one it says only that he too was a bishop, and that he died in peace, preaching Christ to the end of his life; it names no see for him at all, and adds «βλέπε σχετικά και την 15η Μαρτίου», sending the reader to 15 March for him as well. The epigram over his name says that Aristobulus, having hunted souls with the word, comes to Christ asking the wages of the hunt.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him alone on the Julian 16 March and tells a life neither of the other calendars gave. He was one of the Seventy apostles, the brother of the apostle [Barnabas](/saints/barnabas-the-apostle), and born in Cyprus. He followed the apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), who mentions him in the letter to the Romans; and when that great apostle was appointing many bishops for the various parts of the world, he appointed Aristobulus bishop of Britain.

In Britain, the Prologue says, the peoples were wild, unbelieving and evil-minded, and among them Aristobulus bore sufferings, hardships and spite beyond describing. They beat him without mercy, dragged him through the streets, jeered at him and mocked him. But in the end this holy man came through to success by the power of the grace of God: he enlightened the peoples, baptised them in the name of Christ the Lord, built churches, consecrated priests and deacons, and at the last died there in peace and went to the kingdom of the Lord whom he had served faithfully. The entry gives no year, so none is recorded here.
