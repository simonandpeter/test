# Nicodemus of Elbasan

saint.gr says Nicodemus came from Elbasan in Epirus and was reared by pious parents. When he grew up he married a Christian woman and had children by her; but he was worked upon by the Turks and became a Mohammedan, and then had his children forcibly circumcised too — all but one, whom the Christians smuggled away to the Holy Mountain. When he went to the Holy Mountain to find that child and make a Muslim of him, he repented instead, came back to Christianity, and stayed there, leaving the things of the world behind.

He was then made a monk with the name Nicodemus at the skete of Saint Anna, in the Kalyva of the Ascension, which is near the Kyriakon. But the longing for martyrdom took hold of him, and he prepared himself for it with ascesis, with fasting and with much prayer. After that he went to [Acacius the Kausokalyvite](/saints/acacius-the-kausokalyvite) to take counsel of him, and Acacius strengthened him.

He went then to the Turks and confessed Christ with courage. He was handed over to the enraged crowd, which tortured him without mercy for three days; and then he was led to the place of his martyrdom, where, after a prayer he made, he was beheaded on 11 July 1722. Above the notice the page sets a distich: «Ὁ Νικόδημος ῥάβδον ἐν χερσὶν φέρων, Πλάνην πατάσσει Ἀγαρηνῶν ἀνόμων» — Nicodemus, carrying a staff in his hands, strikes down the error of the lawless sons of Hagar.

The Christians buried his martyred body in the church of the Theotokos, where, the page says, it is preserved to this day, works wonders and gives off a fragrance; his relics are at the monastery of the Mother of God at Elbasan, and part of them is kept at the skete of Saint Anna on the Holy Mountain, where there is also a manuscript service for him. A newer service was composed by the hieromonk John Danielides and by others.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same 11 July, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović ranks him Преп. муч., a venerable-martyr, and tells the fall as well as the martyrdom. He was a native of Elbasan, it says; he was married and had children; and, deceived by the Turks, he took Islam and brought his children over into it by force — all but one son, who fled to the Holy Mountain and was tonsured there.

Nicodemus went to the Holy Mountain to fetch that son back, and the Mountain made such an impression on him that he repented instead, returned to the faith of Christ and was tonsured himself. For three years he wept over his apostasy. Then he resolved to go back to Albania, to expiate his sin in the very place where he had committed it: he returned, declared before the Turks that he was a Christian, and was beheaded on 11 July 1722. The Prologue adds that his wonderworking relics lie whole and healing to this day.
