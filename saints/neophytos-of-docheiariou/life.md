# Neophytos of Docheiariou

Neophytos was the nephew of [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-the-cellarer), the founder of the monastery of Docheiariou, and the son of the grand duke John. In the world he was called Nicholas; he was born at Constantinople, was the first writer of the imperial chronicles, and was honoured for his wisdom and his virtue. He came from Constantinople to Athos, became the disciple of his uncle, was made a monk, and was appointed abbot by him.

He loved the monastery as a mother: he renewed it, enriched it, enlarged it and made it splendid, giving away for it the whole of his inherited wealth, so that, the notice says, he showed himself truly its second founder. He was also appointed Protos of the Holy Mountain. The divine Archangels, hearing his prayer, helped him to enlarge the monastery and to paint the church, which he dedicated to them for that very wonderful intervention of theirs. Before his death he gave up the abbacy in order to enjoy the grace of stillness.

His memory is kept on 9 November together with Euthymius, and, as the notice on [Barnabas](/saints/barnabas-of-docheiariou) has it, the monastery's own codex names all three together — Euthymius its first founder, then Neophytos and Barnabas. The notice, which doxologia.ro takes from Moses the Athonite's *Saints of the Holy Mountain*, gives no year in its prose, and his page carries no Tropar tab, so the folder has no hymn.
