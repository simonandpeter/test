# Constantia of Paphos

Constantia was from Paphos in Cyprus. Jerome, in his book on ecclesiastical writers, says she was a disciple of Hilarion the Great, who lived the ascetic life at the village of Episkopi near Paphos.

After Hilarion’s death she stayed at his tomb, striving by ascetic labours to imitate him, praying without ceasing and working for the perfection of her soul; by her faith and his intercessions she was granted to work miracles. The chronicler Kyprianos mentions her, and the Cypriot scholar Stephen Lusignan, in the sixteenth century, calls her the protectress of Paphos — a very noble lady of the city, he writes, who died of unbearable grief when she heard of her master’s death and learned that a disciple had stolen his body; a praiseworthy fidelity, that loved him living and went on honouring him dead. She has a full service in the Cypriot Menaia, and her apolytikion names her Protectress of Paphos.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 25 August — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/4337/saint.aspx); read 23 August 2026.*
