# Therapon of Lythrodontas

saint.gr's calendar for 30 Οκτωβρίου names «Όσιος Θεράπων ο παρά τον Λυθροδόνταν ασκήσας», and gives him a long notice. He came, the page says, with the company of the «Three Hundred» refugees out of Palestine, during the persecutions worked in the seventh century by the Muslim Arabs. He had lived in the desert of Palestine and been taught there the monastic life, asceticism, prayer, humility, vigil and self-restraint; and when he came to Christian Cyprus and looked for a place fit for ascetic labour, he found one, with water beside it, near the village of Lythrodontas in the district of Nicosia.

In that desert place, after many spiritual struggles, he was granted by God the gift of wonderworking. The faithful of the country round ran to him to be healed of various sicknesses, and also to take from him the right Christian teaching for the good of their souls. When he departed to the Lord his body was buried there, in the place of his asceticism, by the faithful or by his disciples.

The finding of his bones came centuries later, and the page tells it as the older villagers told it. Before the present village was built there was only a small settlement where the cemetery now stands, and the inhabitants used to watch at night a light that appeared where his church stands today. They went up and saw nothing; they saw the mysterious light a second and a third time, and at last it stood over a bramble. They decided to cut the bramble out by the root, and when they did they found the grave of the venerable Therapon and his bones in it, and they built a church in his name.

That church stood until 1863, when it was pulled down so that the large church which is there now could be built. In it is kept a small piece of the saint's forehead, in a silver-gilt case, which is carried in procession on his feast. A troparion of the eighth ode of his service makes the same point as the story: Moses, lit before by a pillar, showed you a ray, a pillar all of light from earth to heaven, where even now your enduring and much-contending body lies.

After the finding, the page says, the relic worked many wonders and chiefly healed many of marsh fever, and it works wonders to this day for those who come with faith to his church; for that he took the name of wonderworker, and there is a holy spring of his beside the church. From the doxastikon of the lauds of his service the page gathers what he taught: love God and you will find eternal grace, prefer nothing to his love, so that when he comes in his glory you may find rest with all the saints. And on a scroll in a more recent icon of his is written: restraint of tongue and of belly is the greatest philosophy.
