# Basilla (24 December)

saint.gr's calendar for 24 December keeps Basilla, and takes care to say how little is certain of her. What the synaxaristes note, it says, is only this: that she suffered martyrdom together with the holy [Eugenia](/saints/eugenia), whom the same calendar keeps on this day, and that she was put to death by beheading. Everything else on the page is given as somebody's opinion, and the opinions do not agree with one another.

S. Eustratiades, it says, thinks this saint is Eugenia's mother, because her memory is recorded together with that of Eugenia's father Philip and of Eugenia's servants Protas and Hyacinth, who all were martyred at Rome under Commodus, whose reign the page dates 180 to 192. Galanos, in his Lives of the Saints, has it otherwise: that Eugenia drew Basilla to Christianity at Rome, and that Basilla's own betrothed, Pompeius, being an idolater, denounced both women to the authorities — so that Basilla was beheaded, while Eugenia was first thrown into the river Tiber, was saved out of it, and was beheaded afterwards.

The two readings cannot both stand, and nothing here settles them. No year is drafted for her: the years of Commodus belong to the grouping the first opinion argues from, and the same calendar's page for Eugenia puts her in the second half of the third century. What the page states of Basilla without hedging is that she died with Eugenia and by the sword, and that is what is kept. No hymn is printed, and the feast is marked fixed. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence by which saint.gr marks an entry it will not vouch for.

The Охридски пролог keeps her on 24. децембар ст. ст., inside the entry it heads „Преподобна мученица Евгенија и други с њом.“, and what it says of her is one sentence and its sequel. At Rome, it says, [Eugenia](/saints/eugenia) turned the heathen to the right faith fearlessly and zealously, girls above all; «Тако преведе у веру и неку красну девицу Василију» — so she brought to the faith a beautiful virgin named Basilla. «Ускоро Василија би посечена за Христа, како јој Евгенија и предсказа» — soon Basilla was beheaded for Christ, as Eugenia had foretold to her.

The book gives her no year, no parentage and no place but Rome, and nothing of the questioning or the sentence. It is the heading’s own «и други с њом», and others with her, that holds her on this day; the year 262 the entry prints belongs to Eugenia’s own beheading and is not written here.
