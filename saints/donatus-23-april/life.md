# Donatus (23 April)

saint.gr's whole account of the two of them is one sentence: «Οι Άγιοι Δονάτος και Θερινός μαρτύρησαν δια ξίφους» — Donatus and [Therinus](/saints/therinus) were martyred by the sword. The calendar's line for the day names them in that order and adds only the rank word, «οι Μάρτυρες»; the page's own heading takes Therinus first and Donatus second.

The couplet above the entry is the only other thing the page gives, and it plays on Therinus's name, which means of the summer: reaped together with Donatus by the sword, Therinus, you both become strange sheaves of God. There is no year, no country, no city, no judge and no relic, and the page prints no hymn.

Both names stand among the entries of the day of the great martyr George, but — unlike the pages of Anatolius, Protoleon and Glycerius — this page says nothing to tie the two of them to his passion, and nothing of the kind has been supplied for them here.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the great martyr alone — „Св. вел. муч. Ђорђе.“ — and names the rest in its body. [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) was born in Cappadocia, the son of rich and devout parents; his father suffered for Christ and his mother moved to Palestine. Grown, he went into the army, reached the rank of tribune in his twentieth year, and in that rank was in the service of the emperor Diocletian. When the emperor began his terrible persecution of the Christians, George stepped before him and confessed boldly that he was a Christian too.

The emperor threw him into prison, ordered his feet set in the stocks and a heavy stone laid on his chest; then had him bound to a wheel with boards of great nails set under it and turned on it until his whole body was one wound; then buried him in a ditch with only his head above the earth and left him there three days and three nights; and then had a magician give him a deadly poison. Through all of it George prayed without ceasing, and God healed him on the instant and saved him from death, to the great wonder of the people. And when by his prayer he raised a dead man, many received the faith of Christ.

Among them, the entry says, were the emperor's wife [Alexandra](/saints/alexandra-the-empress), the chief priest of the idols [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-once-a-magician), the farmer [Glycerius](/saints/glycerius-the-farmer), and [Valerius](/saints/valerius), Donatus and [Therinus](/saints/therinus). At the last the emperor condemned George and his own wife to be beheaded with the sword; the blessed Alexandra gave up her spirit at the place of execution before the sword came to her, and George was beheaded in 303. The wonders at his grave are without number, the entry says, and so are his appearances, in dreams and waking, to those who have remembered him and asked his help from then to this day.

What the entry adds to this folder is the company it names him in — he is the fifth of the six converts, listed between Valerius and Therinus — and nothing else: no trade, no city, no rank. The entry gives this folder no year of its own. It dates only the great martyr's beheading, „св. Ђорђе би посечен 303. год.“, and the empress's death just before it; of the others it says only that they received the faith, and nothing at all about how or when they died. **No date was added here**, and the folder stands as it was.
