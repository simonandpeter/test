# Narcissus of Athens

doxologia.ro's calendar names Narcissus among the apostles it keeps on 31 October, and the life it prints for him is a single sentence: he was bishop in Athens, preaching the Gospel of Christ, and after many labours and ascetic struggles gave up his blessed soul into the hands of the Lord. The sentence ends in the plural, because the same formula stands under several of the apostles the day names.

Nothing else is printed. The page gives no year, no account of his preaching at Athens, no manner of death and no burial place, and names nobody beside him. doxologia.ro prints no separate troparion for him. The hymn on his page is the one it gives to all six apostles it names on this day — Apelles, Stachys, Ampliatus, Urban, Aristobulus and Narcissus together — a short common of apostles in the third tone, asking them to entreat the merciful God for the forgiveness of our offences.

The Greek calendar keeps the six of them together on the same day, 31 October, and saint.gr gives each of them his see. All six belonged to the seventy Apostles of the Lord, it says, and all were «Χριστοῦ εὐωδία τῷ Θεῷ ἐν τοῖς σωζομένοις», from 2 Corinthians 2.15. Of this one: Narcissus was ordained bishop of Athens; but the truth of the Gospel, which he taught with zeal, roused the idolaters, with the result that they tortured him and he gave up his soul as a martyr. The page gives him no year and no birthplace, and sets over him and three of his companions the epigram of the four-stringed lyre of the Apostles, which falls silent when its pegs are taken away.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps the same six apostles of the Seventy on the Julian 31 October, and of him its one life for the six says one thing only, which the other two calendars had already said: he was a hierarch in the city of Athens. It gives him no ordainer, as it gives three of the six, no manner of death, as it gives two, and no cross-reference to another day, as it gives one. What the Russian adds to this folder is the century — the line closes the six with «(I)», and no calendar had dated him at all — and the day's troparion and kontakion of the apostles, the second of which names him among the six.
