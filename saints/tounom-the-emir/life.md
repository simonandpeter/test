# Tounom the Emir

saint.gr sets him at the Holy Fire of Jerusalem. At the Pascha of 1579, it says, the Armenians managed to bribe the Turkish governor into issuing an order forbidding the Greek Orthodox patriarch Sophronius IV access to the church of the Resurrection for the ceremony of the Holy Light. The guards shut the Holy Door, and the patriarch with his clergy and his faithful stayed outside praying and waiting on the outcome.

The Lord's answer was immediate, the page says. For all the desperate efforts of the Armenian patriarch, the Holy Light did not shine in the sacred Kouvouklion or anywhere else inside the church; it came out through the column, which to this day is seen split and blackened, and to everyone's astonishment the candles in the Orthodox patriarch's hands took fire. He gave the Holy Light to the Orthodox who were in the courtyard, and the Armenian went away ashamed.

The emir Tounom, who was at that moment the guard at the Holy Door, witnessed the wonder, and it was the cause of his believing and becoming a Christian. The Turks killed him, the page says, so that the thing should not become known; according to another version, when he understood the wonder he cried out to the crowd, «Αυτή είναι η αληθινή πίστη», this is the true faith, and the Turks burnt him alive for changing his religion. His bones are kept at the monastery of the Megali Panagia.

When the sultan learnt of the wonder he issued a firman recognising the exclusive right of the Greek Orthodox patriarch to receive the Holy Light. The page's couplet is of light: «Φῶς ἅγιον, Τούνομ, φωτίσαν σὸν σκότος, Πρὸς Φῶς εἴλκυσέ σε, Χριστόν, ἐν τῷ πόλῳ» — the holy Light, Tounom, lighting your darkness, drew you to the Light, to Christ, in heaven. Of the year it is honest: the writings that mention the event do not fix it, and 1579 is the page's own reckoning from the tenures of four patriarchs and the reign of sultan Murad III.
