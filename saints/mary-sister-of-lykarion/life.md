# Mary, sister of Lykarion

Mary and [Martha](/saints/martha-sister-of-lykarion) were sisters by the same mother, and doxologia's life says that, burning with right faith and with love towards Christ, they were like the Martha and Mary of the Gospel; their brother was the child martyr Lykarion. They lived in the parts of Asia, in the times when the Church of Christ was persecuted by pagan emperors and magistrates who worshipped idols, and the two of them kept their virginity, serving Christ and wishing to die for him.

When the pagan governor passed their house, Martha opened the door and cried out with her sister after him that they were Christians; he took no notice, and when they called again he answered that he spared them for their youth and did not wish to kill them. Martha told him that a martyr's death is not death but life, and he grew angry and had her seized. Then Mary came out and said to the governor: «What my sister has spoken, I also confess.» After the sisters their brother Lykarion, a child, came out and cried: «As Martha and Mary have said, so say I also — I am a Christian.»

The governor ordered the three of them to be hung upon crosses. Their mother stood by and said: «Be saved, my dear children, and receive the crown from Christ.» Mary, hanging on her cross, answered her: «Be thou saved also, our mother, with the fruit of thy womb, for thou hast brought us to Christ.» A soldier ran all three through with a sword as they hung, and they gave up their spirits into the hands of the Lord. The page gives no year and no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and gives the trial the Romanian line withheld. [Martha](/saints/martha-sister-of-lykarion) and Mary were sisters, saint.gr says, and were found guilty by the idolaters because they had given their lives to spreading the Christian faith among the world of women. At their trial the eparch made a show of pitying them and adjured them to spare their own youth; and they answered him that everyone will lose his life, but woe to the man who loses his soul — for, as John has it, he that believes on the Son has life everlasting, but he that does not obey the Son shall not see life, and the wrath of God abides upon him. Then the eparch ordered that they should die the death of the cross; and when the idolaters who stood by marvelled at the courage of two so young, he ordered the executioners to behead them instead. Its couplet on the two sisters turns on the one womb: those whom a single belly brought forth into the light, Martha and Mary, a single sword deprives of the light. Of Lykarion, whose name the Greek line prints beside theirs and this corpus keeps for their epithet, the page says that in some synaxaristes he is commemorated on this day with the two virgins, that he is reported to have been a child who lived as a monk with them, and that he was crucified with them and afterwards beheaded — and then it doubts him: «Πιθανόν ο Οσιομάρτυρας Λυκαρίων να είναι ίδιος με αυτόν της 7ης Ιουνίου», perhaps this venerable martyr Lykarion is the same as the one of 7 June. Because the page hedges his day and his identity both, nothing is recorded of him here beyond what it says of him.

The Russian calendar keeps the same three on 6 февраля ст. ст. and tells their end in a few lines. Mary and Martha were sisters by blood, lived in Asia and burned with the desire to suffer for the Lord Jesus Christ. A pagan commander was passing their house one day; the sisters came out to him and declared aloud that they were Christians. He paid them no attention at first, but they went on crying their confession after him, and then both were seized together with their brother, the boy [Lykarion](/saints/lykarion). All three were crucified, and their mother stood by at the execution and strengthened them in their suffering for Christ. The sisters were run through with spears, and the boy Lykarion was beheaded.

The Serbian church keeps the three of them on the Julian 6 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue gives them one sentence between them: holy martyrs Martha and Mary and their brother Lykarion, all three crucified on the cross for Christ, and then pierced and killed with the spear. For the two sisters that is what the Russian life says. For their brother it is not — there he is beheaded by the sword — and the difference is left standing on his own page. The Prologue names no place, no commander and no year, and the verses it sets under the day add their old mother, who taught the three of them to love Christ because he had suffered for them.
