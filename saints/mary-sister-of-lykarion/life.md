# Mary, sister of Lykarion

Mary and [Martha](/saints/martha-sister-of-lykarion) were sisters by the same mother, and doxologia's life says that, burning with right faith and with love towards Christ, they were like the Martha and Mary of the Gospel; their brother was the child martyr Lykarion. They lived in the parts of Asia, in the times when the Church of Christ was persecuted by pagan emperors and magistrates who worshipped idols, and the two of them kept their virginity, serving Christ and wishing to die for him.

When the pagan governor passed their house, Martha opened the door and cried out with her sister after him that they were Christians; he took no notice, and when they called again he answered that he spared them for their youth and did not wish to kill them. Martha told him that a martyr's death is not death but life, and he grew angry and had her seized. Then Mary came out and said to the governor: «What my sister has spoken, I also confess.» After the sisters their brother Lykarion, a child, came out and cried: «As Martha and Mary have said, so say I also — I am a Christian.»

The governor ordered the three of them to be hung upon crosses. Their mother stood by and said: «Be saved, my dear children, and receive the crown from Christ.» Mary, hanging on her cross, answered her: «Be thou saved also, our mother, with the fruit of thy womb, for thou hast brought us to Christ.» A soldier ran all three through with a sword as they hung, and they gave up their spirits into the hands of the Lord. The page gives no year and no hymn.
