# Acutius, companion of Januarius

Acutius is named by saint.gr among the fellow workers of [Januarius](/saints/januarius-of-benevento), the bishop, and is named without an office, «ο Ακούτιος και ο Ευτύχιος». Januarius was bishop at Naples in Italy, the page says, in the years of Diocletian, when the prefect of that region was Timothy; and with a very good band of Christian fellow workers he fought the holy struggle of virtue and brought many idolaters to Christ.

When the great persecution against the Christians came, they were all arrested and endured fearful tortures. Januarius they threw into the fire to be burned, but by divine grace he remained unharmed; so they led him at once to another place, where they cut his sinews, and thus he received the crown of martyrdom. The page tells of no separate trial and no separate death for the six who were with him, and gives the year of all seven as 305.

The couplets it sets over them divide them as the day's line does. Of the deacons: with Proculus, Sossus also and Faustus, who bowed before the scabbard, the sword killed. Of the reader: Desiderius, giving his neck to the sword, underwent the cutting and stood before the Lord. Of the last two: hear the voice of Acutius, saying, Eutychius, be cut down with me. And of the bishop: the month of April saw Januarius, a noble man, cut through.

The others of the company, as the calendar's line names them, are [Proculus](/saints/proculus-companion-of-januarius), [Sossus](/saints/sossus-companion-of-januarius), [Faustus](/saints/faustus-companion-of-januarius), [Desiderius](/saints/desiderius-companion-of-januarius) and [Eutychius](/saints/eutychius-companion-of-januarius). The page gives none of the six a country, a parentage or a relic of his own — the relics it records are the bishop's, in the Roman Catholic cathedral of Naples that bears his name — and the hymns it prints belong to the seven together and are kept on his folder.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 21 Απριλίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/04/21/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/321/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
