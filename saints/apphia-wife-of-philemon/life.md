# Apphia, wife of Philemon

Apphia was the wife of [Philemon](/saints/philemon-the-apostle) of Colossae, on the witness of Saint John Chrysostom which doxologia's life cites. Her husband's house in Colossae was the church of the city, where all the faithful gathered and the divine services were celebrated, and it was Apphia who set that church in order, serving God day and night in fasting and in prayers and giving rest to the saints who laboured at the good news of Christ.

She fed the poor, the destitute and the stranger, so that her house was not only a church but a guest-house, an infirmary and a shelter for all who had nowhere to lay their heads. Once, when the god-hated feast of the unclean Artemis was being kept at Colossae, [Archippus](/saints/archippus-the-apostle) with those who were there and with Philemon gathered all the faithful in the house Apphia kept, and they offered their prayers and celebrated the holy service.

The idolaters, who hated the faithful and knew where the Christians were gathered, fell on them without warning and drove out the flock, beating some and killing others. Archippus, Philemon and Apphia they seized and brought to Artocles, the governor of Ephesus, and at his order all three were tortured: stretched on the ground and dragged and beaten with staves, then buried each in the earth up to the hips and stoned. Under the stones Apphia and Philemon died, and the apostolic three went to heaven together.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 22 November, and says a little more of her household. saint.gr makes her the wife of [Philemon](/saints/philemon-the-apostle) and a Christian of Colossae with him, with a developed sense of charity, the two of them using their wealth readily for the relief of the poor and the sick; they came to Christianity through the Apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) when he came to their city, about the year 54. Of [Archippus](/saints/archippus-the-apostle) the page says he was a kinsman and perhaps a son of Philemon and Apphia. By the tradition all four were martyred for the spreading of the Gospel, and Apphia's own couplet has her beaten where she lay stretched on the ground, the eye of her heart turned to heaven.
