# Sven of Arboga

Sven lived at the end of the first millennium, when the Vikings were still sunk in paganism. Born at Arboga in the kingdom of Sweden, he left for the Christian lands of Europe and came home a stranger no one recognised - a baptized Christian and a monk. The life the Romanian synaxarion keeps shows a peaceful, gentle, humble young man preaching that a man's most precious thing is his soul; the warriors who ruled the settlement thought him insignificant, even mad, while the poor, the oppressed and the war-captives gathered to him, and the chapel he raised from tree trunks grew too small for the crowds. Some warriors and even Viking chiefs began to listen, and catechumens prepared for baptism.

The keepers of the idols did not wait. They stirred the people against him as once against the Archdeacon Stephen, and a mob of enraged Vikings dragged him from the chapel to the place of blood-sacrifice at Arboga and laid him on the altar stone. He rose to his knees, stretched his hands to heaven and said, "Father, do not count this sin against them," his face shining like an angel's, and they stoned him. His blood ran down the altar stone into the earth, and a healing spring rose there; the few Christians of Arboga hid his body like a treasure, and later built a little church on the place of the sacrifice out of the stones of the pagan altar.
