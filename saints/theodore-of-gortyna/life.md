# Theodore of Gortyna

saint.gr says Theodore, bishop of Gortyna, took part in the Fifth Ecumenical Council of Constantinople in 553, alone out of Crete and alone among the metropolitans of Eastern Illyricum. At that council, the page says, the condemnation of the Three Chapters was made. He had also taken part in the Resident Council of Constantinople in 536, where he was accompanied by an archimandrite of the Monastery of the Cretans named Polychronius, who co-signed the acts of that council.

Theodore's name was found with the title of archbishop in an incised inscription on an epistyle at Gortyna, and lately also in a mosaic of the great early Christian basilica in the village called Mitropolis, south of Gortyna, which came to light by chance in 1978 and where the excavations began in 1991. That great basilica was, in the estimate of the honorary ephor of Byzantine antiquities Emmanuel Borboudakis, the metropolitan church of Saint Titus, and all the archaeological evidence there agrees with it.

The page gives no year of his birth and none of his death, no account of his repose, no relics and no hymn. It places him on this day beside the hundred and sixty-five fathers of the Fifth Ecumenical Council, whom the same calendar keeps here, and ranks him with the synaxis of all the saints who shone in Gortyna and Arcadia of Crete.
