# John Vostorgov

John Vostorgov was born on 20 January 1864 in the stanitsa of
Novoalexandrovskaya in Stavropol province, a priest’s son whose father died
early. After the Stavropol seminary in 1887 he taught for two years and was
ordained in 1889; his first parish, the Kuban village of Kirpilskoye, was
mostly Old Believers, and at his own expense he set up a parish school,
preached at every chance and opened a temperance society, so that within a
year more than a hundred of the village’s Old Believers were reunited with
the church. From September 1890 he taught religion at the Stavropol boys’
gymnasium, then was moved to the Tiflis diocese as missionary of the
Georgian Exarchate, learned the Syro-Chaldean language of the Nestorians and
went to Persia to organise their reunion with Orthodoxy — three bishops were
reunited. He came home in the disorders of 1905, saw that the struggle
would be to the death, and chose the road of struggle and confession:
with like-minded patriots he founded the Union of the Russian People,
travelled endlessly on its business and published its papers, Tserkovnost,
Russkaya Zemlya and the journal Vernost, and the Sretensky life credits
that rallying of the people with the defeat of the 1905 revolution — and
blames the government’s manifesto of 17 October, and its later breaking of
the patriotic organisations, for the unpreparedness of 1917. He went on
working with great authority: in 1910 to Harbin in China, to found the
Brotherhood of the Resurrection to tend the graves of the Russian dead in
Manchuria; in 1911 he arranged the purchase of land at Bari in Italy for a
hostel for Russian pilgrims to the relics of St Nicholas; in 1913, back in
Moscow, he was among the founders of the Women’s Theological Institute, and
the Synod made him synodal missionary-preacher, in which office he was
present at the examination of the incorrupt relics of Sophronius of
Irkutsk. In May 1917 he was rector of the Intercession cathedral on Red
Square — St Basil’s.

When the news of the murder of Metropolitan Vladimir, the first martyr,
reached the Local Council, he spoke words that brought Patriarch Tikhon to
thank him: our people have sinned, and sin demands expiation and
repentance, and for that a sacrifice is always required, and the best are
chosen for it, not the worst — that is the secret of the old metropolitan’s
martyrdom; a death like his life, without pose or phrase, cannot pass
without trace. He called the Orthodox to gather at once round their pastors
in “companies of the flock”, through parish meetings and brotherhoods, to
fight for the faith and the church, and told the pastors to make ready for
a confessor’s struggle — there is a field, the faith and the church, where
we pastors must be ready for torment and suffering and must burn with the
desire of confession and martyrdom. On 20 May (2 June) 1918 he was arrested
in Moscow with Bishop [Ephrem](/saints/ephrem-of-selenginsk) of Selenginsk,
who was staying in his flat, and held in the Butyrka. A prisoner set to dig
the graves told the rest: on 23 August 1918 the guards announced an
“important shooting” of priests and ministers — Bishop Ephrem, Father John,
the Catholic priest Lutostański and his brother, the former ministers of
the interior Maklakov and Khvostov, the president of the State Council
Shcheglovitov and the senator Beletsky. They were stood along the grave
facing it; at Father John’s request the executioners let them pray and take
leave of one another, and all knelt and prayed, then came under the
blessing of the bishop and the priest and embraced. Father John went first
to the grave, having said a few words inviting the rest to offer the last
expiating sacrifice in faith in God’s mercy and the speedy rebirth of the
motherland. I am ready, he told the guard. The executioner came up close
behind him, twisted his left arm behind his back, put a revolver to the
back of his head and fired, pushing him into the grave as he did. Covering
the bodies, the executioners spoke of their deep surprise at Father John
and at Maklakov, whose composure had shaken them. The Jubilee Council of
Bishops of August 2000 numbered him among the new martyrs and confessors of
Russia; the Russian calendar keeps him on 23 August (Julian).
