# Modestus, tutor of Vitus

The Russian calendar names him second of three martyrs it keeps together on this day, and what it tells of him is told inside the boy's story. He was the tutor of [Vitus the Roman](/saints/vitus-the-roman), the son of a Sicilian nobleman called Гилас, and he was a Christian; so was the boy's nurse, [Crescentia, nurse of Vitus](/saints/crescentia-nurse-of-vitus). When the father's love for his son had turned to hatred and he had resolved to kill him for his faith, the two of them took the boy secretly out of the house to save his life.

At the river they found a boat, and the page says an angel went aboard with them and brought them to Lucania in Italy, where they lived hidden from their pursuers. Brought at last before Диоклетиан with the boy, he was shut in prison with him, and there Christ appeared to the two of them and the chains fell from their hands. They were hung on stakes together and raked with iron claws; and when the earthquake came that broke off the torture, an angel took them down and carried them back to Lucania, where all three gave up their souls about the year 303.

The calendar's own index for this day heads him «Модест Римский, Пестун» — the Roman, the foster-father — and that second word is the whole of what distinguishes him from the other men of his name. It gives him no birth, no city of his own and nothing before the flight.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 16 мая ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260516.html) and [the life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life1311.htm); read 8 October 2026.*
