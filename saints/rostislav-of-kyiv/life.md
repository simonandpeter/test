# Rostislav of Kyiv and Smolensk

saint.gr's calendar for 14 Μαρτίου names him «Άγιος Ροστισλάβος ο Πρίγκιπας Κιέβου και Σμολένσκ», and heads him «(; - 1176)». The page behind that line holds one sentence about him: that the holy Rostislav Mstislavovich, prince of Kyiv and of Smolensk, lived in the twelfth century in Russia and fell asleep in peace in the year 1176. The patronymic is the only thing it adds to the line.

There is nothing else on the page: no parents beyond that patronymic, no account of his rule, no church or monastery of his, no relic and no hymn, and not even a note on the name, which this calendar usually gives. What is recorded here is the commemoration, the two principalities, the century, and the year of his peaceful repose.

The Russian church keeps him on this day because it is the day he died, and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery gives the whole of a reign the Greek page passes over in a sentence. He was a son of a great prince of Kyiv and ruled Smolensk for more than forty years after his father gave him the city in 1125, and under him the Smolensk principality grew, was built up with towns and villages, was adorned with churches and monasteries, and came to weigh in the affairs of all the Rus; the page names the towns he founded in that land and credits him with the raising of Smolensk, its principality and its diocese.

It also records the years in which the see of Kyiv stood widowed and he was left without a metropolitan's counsel, and that two Greek metropolitans in turn held the Russian Church less than a year each. His only consolation, the page says, was the company of the elders of the Caves monastery and of the monastery his father had founded at Kyiv. Coming back from a campaign in the spring of 1167 he fell ill, and when they reached Smolensk his family begged him to stay; he ordered them to carry him on to Kyiv, saying that if he died on the road he was to be laid in his father's monastery, and that if God healed him he would take the habit in the Caves.

He died on the road on 14 марта and was buried where he had asked. The page gives the year as 1167 and adds that other sources give 1168, against the 1176 this folder held from the Greek; the interval now holds all three. The Russian day page prints a troparion for him, the only one of his four calendars that prints a hymn at all.
