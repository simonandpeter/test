# Marcellus of Sicily

Doxologia's life for this day is one paragraph and holds three men together. Marcellus, bishop of Sicily, Philagrius, bishop of Cyprus, and [Pancratius](/saints/pancratius-of-tauromenium), bishop of Tauromenium, were disciples of the holy Apostle Peter. While Christ was still walking the earth in the body and working miracles, Marcellus heard of it and went from Antioch to Jerusalem together with Pancratius, in order to see him.

Pancratius became known to Saint Peter from that time, and after the Ascension of Christ he followed Peter; made bishop of Tauromenium by him and a servant of Christ, he was killed in secret by the pagans. Marcellus, ordained in the same way bishop of Sicily, turned many of the unbelieving to the Lord, and so departed this life. Philagrius likewise, becoming hierarch of Cyprus and teaching there too in the name of Christ and suffering many torments for the true faith, passed over to the Lord.

That is the whole of what the page prints about him. It gives no year, names no place beyond Antioch, Jerusalem and Sicily, and the page carries no hymn for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and adds to the page a third bishop, [Philagrius](/saints/philagrius-of-cyprus) of Cyprus, whom nothing but the day joins to him. saint.gr puts all three in the first century, and it tells of Marcellus what the Romanian page did not: that he had been a follower of Simon the Magician before he came to the true faith through the teaching and the miracles of the apostle Peter, whom he then followed with his son Pancratius; that he was afterwards made bishop at Syracuse in Sicily, where he brought many idolaters to the true religion; and that the three of them fell asleep having borne many trials in their pastoral duties, and a life of daily martyrdom.
