# Theodore (Bogoyavlensky, 6 July)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery closes its list for 6 июля ст. ст. with «Прмч. Феодора (1943).», and behind that line stands a reading of some length. Oleg Pavlovich Bogoyavlensky was born on 26 December 1905 at Tehran, where his father was Russian consul in Persia; the father was killed by Persians in January 1911, and his mother brought three small children back to Saint Petersburg, where they lived on a government pension and she gave piano lessons. Famine in Petrograd and then in Saratov took even that, and the family came to Moscow in 1922.

There he finished a preparatory course and entered the medical faculty of Moscow University in 1923, joined a literary and philosophical circle, and threw himself into work with the city's abandoned children. The family's parish was the church of the Georgian icon of the Mother of God, where he and his sister sang in the choir and his younger brother read; his mother's saying to her children was that whatever happened, they were never to forget that all of it is God's will. He left the university in his fourth year when she fell ill, and she died in 1927.

After army service he began going to the Vysokopetrovsky monastery, and on 4 November 1930 he was tonsured with the name Theodore — «в честь преподобного Феодора Студита», in honour of the venerable abbot of the Studion — and ordained deacon; he lived then in a small room in the bell tower, and in 1933 the painter Pavel Korin drew him for the study he called *A Young Monk*. He was arrested on 28 March that year, one of twenty-four taken in the same case, and was later ordained priest. When the war came in 1941 he left Zavidovo for his sister's house at Vostryakovo and was arrested there on 8 July.

The questioning ran nearly two years, and the record of it in his reading is a long exchange in which he refused to name anyone: by February 1943 everyone taken with him had been released, and the NKVD opened a second case against him for desertion instead. On 26 June 1943 he was sentenced to five years' exile in the Krasnoyarsk region. He did not go. Long imprisonment and torture had broken his health, and he died in the prison at Balashov in the Saratov region on 19 July 1943 and was buried in an unmarked grave. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia by the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
