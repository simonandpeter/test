# Nicholas (Pavlinov)

Nicholas was born on 20 November 1881 in the village of Bologoye in the
Velikiye Luki district of Pskov province, the son of the psalmist Alexis
Pavlinov, went to school at Velikiye Luki and to the church school, and in
1899 was made psalmist of the church at Bologoye, moving to Velikiye Luki in
1907; on 24 May 1911 he was ordained deacon for the Trinity church, and in
1921 moved to the church of John the Forerunner at Tver. In March 1928
Metropolitan Seraphim (Alexandrov) of Tver ordained him priest and made him
rector of the Theophany church at Nikitskoye near Tver, a parish the
Renovationists had taken, sending him because he was known to be immovable
in Orthodoxy; the parish came back to the Patriarchal church and he served
it six months, until the warden and the psalmist, who favoured the
Renovationists, preferred to have the authorities close the church, which
became a school. On 15 September 1928 he asked to be retired and began to
wander, coming several times to Moscow and staying at churches with
acquaintances, living on alms. Early in March 1930 he came to Moscow again,
and on the morning of 8 March stood at the doors of the Mostorg store at the
Rogozhskaya gate and begged; within the hour a policeman came to the crowd
that had gathered, saw a priest saying something he could not hear, and
asked what he was doing — begging alms; I am walking from one town to
another — and took him to the 43rd station, where he was himself the deputy
chief. Questioned the same day he confirmed that he lived by alms; a note
went to the secret department of the OGPU that a priest had been held for
begging with a crowd round him, and on 10 March he told the OGPU
interrogator that since 1928 he had been a wandering priest, going about the
districts of Moscow and its surroundings collecting alms, that he had been
arrested at the shop by the Rogozhskaya gate, and that he had nothing to
add. The charge was that, vagrant and begging, he had agitated about the
persecution of the clergy, and on 23 March 1930 the Special Board gave him
three years’ exile in the Northern region; he went from the Butyrki with the
next transport north. When the term was over he came to Tver, and Archbishop
Thaddeus (Uspensky) made him rector at Berezino in the Turginovo district,
where he served until 1937. He was arrested on 10 August 1937 and put in the
Tver prison; the chairman and secretary of the village soviet testified that
he supported the fascist regime in Germany, frightened the people with the
life to come to make them fight the Soviet power, and said, brothers,
peasants, fight the antichrist communists, who are leading you to terrible
torments in the next world; remember that without the holy Church there is
no real life; the Church is the holy place where you may find relief for
your souls; pray to God and He will help you. Questioned the same day and
asked whether he admitted counter-revolutionary activity, he said, only that
among the collective farmers I agitated that they should not work on church
feasts — you are lying, said the interrogator, the investigation demands
truthful testimony — and that at the start of 1937, when the elections to
the soviets were being discussed at Berezino, he had spoken for electing
religious people to strengthen the Church and so destroy the godless Soviet
power, and that he had nothing more to say. On 10 September the troika
sentenced him to death; he was shot on 23 September 1937 and buried in an
unknown common grave on the edge of Tver. The life is by the igumen
Damascene (Orlovsky); the Church keeps him on 10 September of the old style,
23 September, and in the synaxes of the Pskov and the Tver saints.
