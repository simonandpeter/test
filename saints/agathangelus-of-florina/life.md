# Agathangelus of Florina

saint.gr calls him a brave young Greek from Florina in Macedonia, and says he practised the trade of a shoemaker at Monastiri. Protesting against the forced conversions to Islam that the Muslims made a custom of during the three days of Bairam, kept after Ramadan, he went to Constantinople and obtained a firman forbidding those practices. The Turks of his own region, beside themselves, seized him, and having tortured him savagely they beheaded him on 17 February of the year 1727.

Beside that account the page prints the inscription engraved on the new martyr's reliquary, which tells the same story in the words of those who kept his head. This holy head, it says, is the new martyr Agathangelus's, who was martyred in 1727 for this cause: that all the Hagarenes had the evil custom, in the time of their Bairam, of turning Turk whatever Christians they found for three days; and he, not bearing it, set off for the Imperial City, and getting a firman — God working with him — they were undone; and they, not bearing that, killed him. The inscription then names the priests in whose days his head was adorned, in the time of the lord Gregory of Pelagonia, and dates itself 25 February 1827.

The page prints an apolytikion for him, which it attributes to the hieromonk Athanasius of Simonopetra, and two megalynaria, and it names as his the metropolis of Florina, Prespes and Eordaia. The megalynaria are not copied here, this corpus keeping no kind for them; the apolytikion is.
