# Therapon of Sardis

Therapon was hierarch of the holy Church of Sardis. By his teaching he turned many pagans from the error of idol-worship to Christ and gave them light through holy baptism. He was then seized by a ruler whom doxologia's life names Julian, who put him in irons, shut him in prison and wore him down for a long time with hunger and thirst, and afterwards brought him out and tormented him with many torments.

He was led bound to Synaon, a city of Phrygia, and to Ankyra, a city of Galatia, and was tormented in many ways everywhere. At a river called Astalin he was turned naked face down to the ground and bound to four stakes driven into the earth, and beaten so long that the skin came away from his body and his bones, and the earth was watered with his blood. The stakes he had been bound to put out green shoots and leaves and grew into great trees, and by their leaves every human infirmity and sickness was healed.

After this he was brought into the province of Thrace, to a city of Lydia near the river Hermus that waters Lydia, in the bishopric of Satalia under the metropolis of Sardis. There, after many kinds of torment, he was slaughtered for Christ, and so received the incorruptible crown. The life gives no year, and none is recorded here; the troparion doxologia prints for him is the common of hieromartyrs.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day, tells the same strange wonder in two sentences, and is the first book read for him to give a year. He turned many Greeks to the faith of Christ, it says, and for that was cruelly tormented by the heathen with hunger, with prison and with beating. They laid him naked on the ground and bound him to four dry stakes, and beat him without mercy until the flesh was torn from his bones.

Yet the martyr stayed alive; and those four dry stakes put out green shoots and grew into tall trees, from which many sick people received healing. At the last, in the days of Valerian, Saint Therapon was slaughtered like a lamb, and passed in soul into the Kingdom, to look upon the glory of God in eternity. He suffered honourably, the Prologue says, in the year 259 — the only year any calendar read for him gives, and it is stated rather than reckoned from the reign that stands beside it.
