# Zeno of Cappadocia

The Russian calendar keeps him on this day as «Прп. Зинона, ученика свт. Василия Великого (V)», and the life behind that line names him by that discipleship: he was a disciple of [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great). He was born in a wealthy family in the city of Pontus, and he served at the court of the emperor Valens, who reigned from 364 to 378, among the soldiers by whom the imperial letters were sent out.

After Valens died he left the world and settled in a cave near the city of Antioch, and for forty years he struggled there in complete solitude, leading a hard life, purifying his soul and occupied with the contemplation of God. Every Sunday he walked to church and received the holy mysteries of Christ. In his cell, the life says, he had neither bed nor hearth nor lamp; he dressed in a worn rag and ate nothing but bread and water, and the water cost him a hard walk into the hills to a spring.

He loved the holy books with a particular love, and borrowed them to read from those who came to him for spiritual counsel. Out of deep humility, and full of the gifts of grace, he reckoned himself spiritually poor. He died at the beginning of the fifth century. The calendar prints no year for him beyond that and no hymn under his name.

*After the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 30 января ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260130.html) and [the life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life341.htm); read 3 October 2026.*
