# Savvas the New of Kalymnos

Savvas the New was born in 1862 at Herakleitsa in the eastern part of Thrace, to poor parents, Constantine and Smaragda, simple people but believing, and was given the name Basil at his baptism. From a small boy he was used to working for his family, but his soul longed for stillness; and at the age of twelve he came to the Holy Mountain, to the skete of Saint Anne, to the cell of the Dormition of the Mother of God, where he was a beginner in the monastic life for twelve years and learned painting and Byzantine music.

He spent a time at Jerusalem, at the monastery of Choziba, and came back to the Holy Mountain for three years, from 1894 to 1897; then he returned to Jerusalem and stayed until 1916, and lived for a short while in that time at the monastery of Saint Sabbas the Sanctified. Afterwards he was two years on the island of Patmos, then on the Holy Mountain again, and then on the island of Aegina, near [Saint Nectarios](/saints/nectarios-of-aegina). After Nectarios died, Savvas shut himself in his cell for forty days, and there, with fasting and prayer, painted the first icon of Saint Nectarios and set it in the church to be venerated.

From 1926 or 1928 — the page allows both — he moved to the island of Kalymnos, to the monastery of All Saints, and lived the last twenty years of his life there in asceticism and in perfect watchfulness until his blessed end in 1948. His word was full of mercy and of comfort, and he became a loving father both to the monks and to the people of Kalymnos.

On 7 April 1957 his holy relics were found whole and giving off a sweet fragrance, and he is held to be the protector of the island. The Ecumenical Patriarchate entered him officially among the saints of the calendar on 19 February 1992. His memory is kept on 7 April and on the fifth Sunday of Great Lent; the second of those moves with Pascha and is not held here, and his own page on the site prints no troparion.
