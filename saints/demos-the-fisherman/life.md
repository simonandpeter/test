# Demos the Fisherman

saint.gr calls him «Άγιος Δήμος (ή Δημήτριος) ο αλιεύς», Demos, or Demetrius, the fisherman, and glosses the name from δῆμος: the man of the people. This new-martyr came from the village of Ozoun Kioupru — Long Bridge — in the province of Adrianople. He followed the trade of a fisherman and worked at a fishery in Smyrna, and there he fell out with the Turkish owner of the fishery, who slandered him with the claim that Demos had sworn to become a Turk.

He was brought before the judge with false witnesses, and because he held firmly to his faith he was thrown into prison and dreadfully tortured with cudgels and other means. Three times he was brought out of the prison, and each time, the page says, Demos went on confessing Christ. Then the judge condemned him to death by beheading, which happened on 10 April 1763, on a Thursday, at Smyrna.

The saint's relic was buried with honours in the church of Saint George at Smyrna, and his grave became a place of pilgrimage for the faithful, giving them many healings. The page's couplet says that Demos, a new contestant, is numbered with the hosts of contestants, not having feared the executioners. Nothing else is told of him: no age, no year of birth, and no hymn.
