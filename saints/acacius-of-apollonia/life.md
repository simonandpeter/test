# Acacius of Apollonia

saint.gr says Acacius came from Apollonia and was martyred in the years of the king Licinius. He was a fervent supporter of the Gospel and a formidable herald of it, and because of that work of his he was denounced and tortured. But by the divine intervention he came out of the torments unwounded: he suffered nothing when they threw him into a cauldron of boiling water, and he was saved from the ferocity of a starved lion. Seeing these wonders, the eparch Terentius set him free.

Afterwards the saint went over to Miletus, where he carried on his preaching of the Gospel and of the one true faith of the Lord. There he was arrested and taken into the temple of the idols, and with the threat of torture the idolaters tried to make him sacrifice to them. But Saint Acacius, by the power of his prayer and by nothing else, was able to shatter the statues of the idols; and for that act of his he was beheaded.

His holy relic was received by the presbyter Leontius, who anointed it with myrrh and laid it in the city of Synada. Above the notice the page sets a distich: «Τράχηλον Ἀκάκιος ἐκτμηθεὶς ξίφει, Ψυχῆς τὸ λευκὸν μηνύων, βλύζει γάλα» — his neck cut through by the sword, Acacius gushes milk, declaring the whiteness of his soul. The page gives no year of its own and prints no hymn.
