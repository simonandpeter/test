# John V

John V was patriarch of Constantinople from 669 to 674, and [George I](/saints/george-i-patriarch-of-constantinople) from 678 to 683, both under the emperor Constantine Pogonatus (668–685). That is the whole of the Sretensky calendar’s life for the two hierarchs kept together on 18 August (Julian).

The Greek calendar keeps the two patriarchs together on 18 August and gives both of them their years in the see. saint.gr says that the patriarch John V succeeded Thomas II in the year 668 and was patriarch until 674; that before this, as a presbyter of the Church of Constantinople, he had served as protekdikos and chartophylax of the Patriarchate and as skeuophylax of Hagia Sophia; that he was marked out for his Orthodox mind and shepherded the church peacefully under Constantine Pogonatos; and that he was a lover of mankind and no lover of money, living with great simplicity, and always saying that nothing honours a cleric, and a hierarch most of all, so much as the simplicity of his life and the spending of his income on the hungry and the naked.

Of George I it says that he was patriarch from 678 to 683, that before this he had been synkellos of the Patriarchate and skeuophylax of Hagia Sophia, that the Sixth Ecumenical Council, which anathematised the Monothelite heretics, was assembled at Constantinople in his time, and that of his character it was written «Νομεὺς ἀγαθὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ νόμων φύλαξ» — a good shepherd, a keeper of the laws of God. The page's own distich for George has him dying with the words «Ὡς κελεύεις Χριστέ μου … ἔρχομαι κληθεὶς ἄνω», as you command, my Christ, I come, being called above; the one for John says the faces of the Church were downcast, not bearing the loss of him. saint.gr prints no hymn for either.

*After the life printed by the Sretensky calendar (days.pravoslavie.ru) — [the entry](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life4469.htm); read 23 August 2026; and saint.gr's calendar for 18 Αυγούστου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/08/18/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2279/saint.aspx), read 1 October 2026.*
