# Nicholas of Thessalonica

The name of Nicholas, archbishop of Thessalonica, is written in the Constantinopolitan Synaxarion on the twenty-ninth of November, and in the Menaia and the later printed synaxarists as well, but with no biographical notice attached to it: «Ὁ ὅσιος πατὴρ ἡμῶν Νικόλαος ἀρχιεπίσκοπος Θεσσαλονίκης ἐν εἰρήνῃ τελειοῦται» — our venerable father Nicholas, archbishop of Thessalonica, is perfected in peace. That one line, saint.gr says, is the whole of the record: that he held the see, and that he died peaceably.

Modern episcopal lists of Thessalonica place his hierarchy in the second half of the second century, and specifically in the year 160 — a dating proposed by Anthimos Alexoudis, metropolitan of Amaseia, without his having had any sufficient evidence for it. The page says so in those words, and no date is recorded in this folder on that account.

It is equally firm against the other guess. The identification of this Nicholas with the archbishop Nicholas the Confessor, whose name stands in the Synodikon of Orthodoxy, is attempted timidly by M. Gedeon; it is wholly unfounded, the page says, and consequently cannot be accepted. His couplet is the only other thing there, and says only that his care was for his own people, to persuade them and lead them where they ought to go.
