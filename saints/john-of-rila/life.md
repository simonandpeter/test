# John of Rila

John of Rila, the great ascetic and lamp of the Orthodox Church, was born at Skrino near Sofia, in the days of Tsar Boris, of poor but honest parents. After their death he became a monk and withdrew to a wild mountain, where he began his struggle in a cave, suffering many assaults from demons and from men, from robbers and from his own kin. Then he moved to the Rila mountain and settled in a hollow tree, living on grass and on the beans that by God’s providence began to grow nearby; for many years he saw no human face, until — again by God’s providence — shepherds searching for lost sheep found him. So the saint became known, and people began to come to him for help in sickness and trouble; the Bulgarian tsar Peter himself visited him and asked his counsel, many zealots of the spiritual life settled near him, and a church and monastery soon rose there. He reposed in peace on 18 August 946, in his seventieth year, and appeared to his disciples after his death. His relics were taken first to Sofia, then to Hungary, then to Tarnovo — the Sretensky calendar keeps the day for that translation, in 1238 — and at last to the monastery of Rila, where they lie today; through the centuries, the Prologue says, the monastery of Rila was a lighthouse, a place of wonderworking power and spiritual comfort for the Christian people of Bulgaria, above all in the hard years under the Turks.
