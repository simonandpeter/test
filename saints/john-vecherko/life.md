# John (Vecherko)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 4 апреля ст. ст. in five words, «Сщмч. Иоанна пресвитера (1933).», and the name link behind the line, page 9602, gives his surname and his grade: «Иоанн (Вечерко), иерей, сщмч.» The life it serves is not his own but the collective page for the twenty-three hieromartyrs of Minsk, where he has one paragraph of three; azbyka.ru has a life for him alone, and the two agree except in two places.

He was born in 1890 in the village Косаричи of the Бобруйский uyezd of Minsk province, the son of a psalm-reader. He finished the Minsk theological seminary — in 1913, azbyka says — and was ordained priest to the Protection church in the village Кривоносы in the same uyezd, where he served the whole of his life. In 1920 part of the province was occupied by Polish troops, whose officers were quartered in the priest’s house and were unfriendly to him because he defended his peasant parishioners; in the end they arrested him and held him some time in the Slutsk prison. That episode is in azbyka only.

His first arrest by the Soviet authorities was for the late payment of the tax laid on the church, and he spent nine months in prison and went back to serving when he came out. **The two books date that arrest differently** — 1926 in the Сретенский page, 1928 in azbyka — and both years are set down here and neither is chosen. Then the authorities, meaning to close the church, began to demand larger and larger taxes of the priest; he had nothing of his own left and went from house to house among the peasants asking for help, some giving and others lending.

Seeing that he went on defending the church, the OGPU decided to arrest him and the members of the parish council, and on 18 March 1933 they were taken, charged with using the collection of money for the taxes as a pretext for organised anti-Soviet work meant to wreck every measure of the Soviet power, the spring sowing campaign among them, and to break up the collective farms. Both books print his answer: «Виновным себя в антисоветской агитации, направленной на срыв хозяйственно-политических кампаний, я не признаю. Считаю, что предъявленное мне обвинение является препятствием для выполнения обрядов религиозного культа и имеет целью закрытие церкви.» azbyka adds one clause more, that he had not gone beyond the bounds of religious rites and considered himself an apolitical man.

He was sent to the Slutsk prison, and on 17 April 1933 the troika sentenced him to be shot. He was shot and buried in an unmarked grave. 17 April 1933 is the civil date of the Julian 4 April, so the calendar keeps him on the day of the sentence and of his death; the line closes «(1933)».

**The two books spell his surname differently** — «Вечерко» on the name link and in the Minsk page, «Вечорко» in azbyka’s title and throughout its life — and the form stored here is the calendar’s own. The Minsk page ends by saying that the twenty-three were glorified as locally venerated saints of the Minsk diocese by the Synod of the Belarusian Exarchate in 1999 and entered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of August 2000. azbyka lists the moveable synaxis of the New Martyrs on 7 February and the moveable synaxis of the Belarusian saints on 11 July beside this day; both are synaxes and neither is written into the feast. Neither book prints a hymn under his name.
