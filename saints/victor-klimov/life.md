Виктор Степанович Климов was born on 17 April 1889 in the village of Semiyarskoye in the Semipalatinsk country, now Semiyarka in East Kazakhstan. He came of a settled and cultivated family; his father kept a post station, and his brother became a priest at the Trinity cathedral in Tomsk and also died a martyr's death. On finishing the Tomsk theological seminary in 1909 he taught letters in the boys' gymnasium at Semipalatinsk. He married in 1911, was ordained deacon in 1913, and on 8 December of that year was ordained priest by the bishop of Semipalatinsk.

From 1914 to 1923 he served in the church of the Resurrection at Semipalatinsk, and was also of the clergy of the [Alexander Nevsky](/saints/alexander-nevsky) church and bore the obedience of dean. In 1922 he helped to organise and to hold assemblies of the clergy and laity of the Semipalatinsk vicariate whose purpose was to stand against the Renovationists. On 4 December he was detained with the others who had taken part, but was soon let go on an undertaking not to leave; on 5 March of the next year he was fined «за участие в тайных собраниях».

From 1924 he was of the clergy of the Znamensky cathedral of Semipalatinsk, and when that was closed in 1931 he returned to the church of the Resurrection. He was arrested on 17 November 1937, charged with «активной контрреволюционной деятельностью путем агитации среди населения», with spreading «клеветнических слухов о мероприятиях советского правительства», and with espionage. He did not admit the guilt laid on him.

He was shot with the three other priests of the city on the strength of the troika's decision of 19 November 1937. 9 ноября ст. ст. is the civil 22 November, so the calendar keeps him on the very day he was shot. He was entered in the synaxis of the new martyrs and confessors of Russia by a determination of the Holy Synod of 27 December 2000.
