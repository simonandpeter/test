# Isaac the Syrian

Isaac the Syrian had no fixed day in the Greek calendar, the Greek synaxarion says: his name used to be remembered on 28 January together with the other great Syrian father, the venerable Ephraim. Some years ago, at the initiative of the elder Paisios of the Holy Mountain, who revered him greatly, a service was composed for him and 28 September was chosen for his feast, and the first church in his honour was built on the Holy Mountain, in the cell of a monk of Paisios’s brotherhood.

The entry goes on in the words of an edition of his Ascetical Homilies. Isaac was a Syrian by race, born by some accounts at Nineveh in Mesopotamia and by others in a small town not far from Edessa; who his parents were is unknown. In the prime of life he renounced the world and went with his brother to a cenobitic monastery in the region called after the holy martyr Matthew, where he took the monastic habit and was trained in ascetic struggle; then, longing for the deeper contemplation of the Spirit, he left it for a solitary cell in the desert. His brother, who had become abbot, wrote to him again and again begging him to come back, and he would not; but a divine revelation called him to be bishop of the church of Nineveh, and he bowed his neck and was made bishop of that great city.

It lasted only a little while. Soon after his ordination a creditor and a debtor came before him, both Christians: the debtor owned the debt but asked a few days’ grace, and the creditor said that unless he was paid that day he would hand the man over to the judge. Isaac asked him, if the Gospel commands us not to ask back even what is taken from us by force, how much more should he bear a few days with a man who begged him; and the creditor answered, Leave the Gospel aside for now, father, and went away.

Isaac said to himself that if they would not obey the commands of the holy Gospel, there was nothing he had come there to do, and at once went back to the desert and his first cell, where he stayed until his death. The book places him, from a passage of his thirty-third homily that speaks of the demons’ six thousand years, at the beginning of the seventh millennium from the creation of the world; the entry gives no year of the Christian era. The Greek calendar keeps him with an apolytikion and a kontakion.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 28 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/3534/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026.*
