# Stephen of Sourozh

saint.gr's calendar for 15 December keeps Saint Stephen, archbishop of Sourozh, and says of him only that it has nothing of his life: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Ρώσου Αγίου» — we have no details of the life of the Russian saint. That is the whole of the entry. A name, the see of Sourozh, the word that he is a Russian saint, and a fixed day: the page gives no year, names no place beyond the see, lists no relic and prints no hymn for him.

That he is called a Russian saint is the only thing in the line that places him at all, and this folder's Russian row is nonetheless left undocumented, because the Russian calendar was not read for him. The whole of the entry is the sentence quoted in the attestation.

The Ohrid Prologue, which keeps him on the same menologion day, has what the Greek page said it lacked. He was a Cappadocian by birth, and was brought up at Constantinople with the patriarch Saint [Germanus](/saints/germanus-of-constantinople). He withdrew into solitude and lived hidden from the world; and an angel made him known to Germanus and commanded the patriarch to set him as bishop over the city of Sourozh, the present Sudak in the Crimea, and the patriarch did so.

Zealous Stephen turned many to Christianity. He suffered bitterly under the emperor Leo the Isaurian for the sake of the icons, and foretold to that emperor his swift ruin. After the evil death of the evil emperor he was given back to his own diocese, where he shepherded his flock in a way pleasing to God and died in peace at the end of the eighth century. The book gives no year, names no relic, and prints no hymn for him.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 15 декабря ст. ст., the same menologion day as the other calendars here, and its line is «Свт. Стефана исп., архиеп. Сурожского (VIII).» — the hierarch Stephen the confessor, archbishop of Sourozh, eighth century. That is the century already recorded above, given here without the Serbian book’s «end of», so it widens nothing and narrows nothing.

The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion under his name and see, and both are propers: the troparion has him take up the Cross as a weapon against the reviler of icons and deliver his city, and the kontakion has his relics lying in Sourozh as its glory and its wealth. The troparion’s own words address him as a hieromartyr where its title and the calendar’s line call him a confessor, and the two readings are left as the page has them. The reading the entry links was not opened.
