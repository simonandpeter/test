# Episteme of Emesa

Episteme was a maiden of Emesa in Phoenicia, very beautiful, whom the father of [Galaction](/saints/galaction-of-emesa) betrothed to his son when Galaction was twenty-four. doxologia.ro prints one life for the two of them and prints it twice, once under each name; what it says of her is told inside his. Galaction would visit her but would not give her the customary kiss, and she grieved at it until he told her the reason: she was not baptised, and he would not share with one of unclean faith. If she would renounce the idols and believe, he said, he would kiss her and love her as himself and call her his wife.

She answered that she would do whatever he commanded, and that she believed his God and wished to be baptised. Because the persecution had killed some priests and driven the rest into the desert, Galaction baptised her himself in the river Chifos, having told her to put on white and go there as though to wash; and no one knew of it. She kept her faith hidden from her father, praying in secret in her house.

Eight days after, she told him a wonder: since her baptism she had seen in sleep beautiful palaces where three choirs sang — monks, fair virgins, and comely men with wings of fire — and her heart was glad with the sweetness of that singing. He explained the choirs to her, and she said, would that God gave us also to dance with them. He answered that if they kept their virginity unspotted and renounced the world they would be counted worthy, and she promised him that hour to keep it and to follow him wherever he went. They gave away in secret all they had and left their houses by night, taking with them Eutolmius, the baptised slave of her father.

On the mountain called Publion she was sent to a small house of four aged solitaries, whose eldest was a deaconess ninety years in the monastic habit, while Galaction stayed in the men's monastery. There, the life says, she fell in no way behind her brother, and the two were as two lamps burning with love before God. Before the soldiers came she dreamed that she stood in a royal palace with Galaction and a most radiant emperor crowned them both; the abbot read the dream to her as the Kingdom, and Christ, and the reward of a martyr's death, and bade her not be afraid.

When the soldiers seized Galaction, she saw it from the height of the mountain and fell weeping at the deaconess's feet, begging to be let go after him: through him she had known Christ, his hands had washed her in baptism, he was her betrothed, her brother, her teacher and the guardian of her virginity, and she could not be parted from him in this age or the next. The deaconess blessed her and sent her out as the first martyr Thecla went, and she ran and cried after the soldiers until they took her too.

Before the governor she stood with Galaction, and with him she was hung up and torn; their hands were cut off, then their feet, then their tongues, so that they were silent with their mouths but did not cease crying out with their hearts; and at the last they were taken out of the court and beheaded, and their bodies left unburied. Eutolmius, following at a distance, took the relics away by night, buried them with honour, and wrote the life and the passion that the Romanian calendar prints. The page gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 5 November, and dates the pair: saint.gr puts them in the third century, under the emperor Decius, and the beheading about the year 250. Its account of her is short and is the same story the Romanian life tells at length — Galaction, grown up, married a beautiful girl, Episteme, whom he himself drew to Christ, and their life was spent in the service of the word of God and of their neighbour until the persecution of Decius broke out. Then he went to a monastery of Mount Sinai and she to a women's community; and when the governor Ursus had seized him, she ran to the persecutors and begged them to take her as well, to strengthen her husband. The distich the page prints for them turns on exactly that: the sword kills a wedded pair who never came together, who had loved a joining of souls instead.
