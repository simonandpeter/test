# Narses, companion of Jonah and Barachisius

saint.gr's calendar for 29 Μαρτίου prints one line for eleven martyrs — «Άγιοι Ιωνάς, Βαραχήσιος και οι συν αυτοίς Ζανιθάς, Λάζαρος, Μαρουθάς, Ναρσής, Ηλίας, Μάρης, Άβιβος, Σιμιάθης και Σάββας (ή Σώθα)» — the holy Jonah and Barachisius and, with them, Zanithas, Lazarus, Marouthas, Narses, Elias, Mares, Abibus, Simiathes and Sabbas, or Sotha. The nine were in prison before the two ascetics reached them, and the page tells all eleven passions as one.

[Jonah](/saints/jonah-martyr-29-march) and [Barachisius](/saints/barachisius) were ascetics, and were martyred about the year 330, when Shapur was king of the Persians and Constantine the Great of the Byzantines. They left the monastery where they lived as monks and went to a small town called Marbiabokh, or Marmiabokh, and there visited the nine martyrs who were being held in the prison and encouraged them for their martyrdom. The two were seized on the spot and brought with them before three Persian magistrates, Masdrath, Siro and Marmises, who advised Jonah and Barachisius to deny Christ and to worship the fire, the water and the sun. They did not obey; they were bound and beaten without pity, dragged over rough rods and left out in the frost all night, and then Jonah's fingers and toes were cut off, he was bound tight to a tree, sawn through the middle and thrown into a pit, while Barachisius was dragged naked through thorns, thrown into a pit, and boiling pitch was poured down his throat.

Of the nine themselves the page says only that they were tortured just as harshly and were put to a most bitter death, and that their relics were bought by a devout Christian named Audeisotes for five hundred miliarisia, Persian coins, who buried them reverently. Their own couplet is on their number: «Χριστοῦ ὑπετμηθέντες ἄνδρες ἐννέα, Σύνεισιν ἤδη τάξεσι ταῖς ἐννέα» — nine men cut down for Christ are now in company with the nine ranks of heaven. Narses the page names fourth of the nine; the note it attaches to his name, that a great general of Justinian bore it, is an item about the word and two centuries away from this martyr.

The Russian church calendar keeps the whole company one day earlier than the Greek, on 28 марта ст. ст., and heads it «Мчч. Ионы и Варахисия и других с ними (ок. 330)» — of Jonah and Barachisius and the others with them. Its account puts the two brothers in the village of Ясы in Persia under king Sapor, whose reign it gives as 310 to 331, and says that when they heard that Christians were being tormented in the town of Варавох they went there, found these nine already in the prison, and urged them to hold firmly to the faith of Christ to the end. The two were seized for it. That is all the Russian source says of the nine, and it does not say how or when they died.
