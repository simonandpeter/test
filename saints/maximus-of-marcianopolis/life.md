# Maximus of Marcianopolis

Maximus and [Asclepiodote](/saints/asclepiodote-of-marcianopolis) were
leading citizens of Marcianopolis in Thrace whose Christian life had drawn
many to baptism. When Tiris, governor of Thrace, came through the city in
the persecution of Maximian Galerius, between 305 and 311, hunting
Christians, he summoned the two and demanded they abandon the faith; they
refused and were beaten, and a devout man named
[Theodotus](/saints/theodotus-of-marcianopolis), who reproached the
governor for his cruelty, was seized too, hung on a tree and torn with iron
hooks. The three were thrown into prison and then carried along on the
governor’s circuit for two weeks. At Adrianople he had their bodies scorched
with white-hot plates, and in the middle of it they heard a voice from
heaven telling them to hold out; after several days they were thrown to the
beasts in the circus, but the she-bear loosed on Maximus and Theodotus
nuzzled them, and the bull Asclepiodote was tied to would not move from the
spot. Tiris went on, and at the village of Saltis, short of Philippopolis,
urged them once more to renounce Christ; when they would not, he had them
beheaded. He was struck by lightning where he sat on the judgement seat.

That is the Orthodox Church in America’s account. The Greek synaxarion’s is
shorter and differs in the manner: scourged, their hands and feet cut off,
imprisoned and at last beheaded — in one woman and two young men, its verse
says, there was one fervour of heart towards the cutting. The Greek calendar
keeps the three on 17 September; the Russian calendar keeps them on the
15th of its own reckoning, the day the Orthodox Church in America prints.

*After the life printed by the Orthodox Church in America for 15 September —
[the day’s lives](https://www.oca.org/saints/all-lives/2026/09/15); the Greek
entry is the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 17 Σεπτεμβρίου —
[the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2454/saint.aspx); read 5 September 2026.*
