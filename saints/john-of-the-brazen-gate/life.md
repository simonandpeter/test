# John of the Brazen Gate

When Leo the Isaurian, called also Conon, took the sceptre of the Greek empire, he raised a persecution against the Church of God and invented a new heresy against the icons: he called the holy icons idols, and those who venerated them with faith idolaters. He ordered that the icons everywhere — out of the churches of God, out of every house and every human dwelling — be thrown down, broken and burnt or cast into the water; and those who would not consent to his impiety he drove into distant exile, or tormented and killed in many ways. First of all he put the most holy Germanus, Patriarch of Constantinople, off his throne with dishonour and blows for standing against him, and set up in his place one Anastasius, a heretic of his own mind.

In Constantinople there was a gate called the Brazen Gate, built in the days of Constantine the Great, and above it a bronze image of the Saviour that had stood there many years. The emperor and the patriarch ordered it taken down. A ladder was set against it and a soldier of the rank of spatharios climbed up; and the people gathered there, men and women, were moved with zeal for the true faith, seized the ladder with the soldier on it, threw it down, and put him to death.

When the emperor heard of it he sent armed soldiers out against the faithful people without warning, and they cut down many, men and women, old and young, whose number the Lord alone knows; and the chief of them they took alive. Their names were these: [Julian](/saints/julian-of-the-brazen-gate), [Marcian](/saints/marcian-of-the-brazen-gate), John, [James](/saints/james-of-the-brazen-gate), [Alexius](/saints/alexius-of-the-brazen-gate), [Demetrius](/saints/demetrius-of-the-brazen-gate), [Phocas](/saints/phocas-of-the-brazen-gate), [Peter](/saints/peter-of-the-brazen-gate) and [Leontius](/saints/leontius-of-the-brazen-gate). These they beat with staves without mercy and threw into prison, and the saints were held in chains and cruelly beaten for eighty days, each of them given five hundred blows a day; and strengthened with the power of Christ they endured bravely, and their bodies did not fail.

Seeing this, the tyrant ordered their faces burnt with heated irons, and then that they be brought out into the streets and killed with the sword; and so they finished their suffering. One of the noble women, [Maria the Patrician](/saints/maria-the-patrician), was beheaded together with them for the same faithful honouring of the holy icons, and their bodies were thrown into the depth of the sea. When these martyrs were seized, the nun Theodosia was seized with them, having had a part in the throwing down of the ladder; she received the martyr's crown before them, and doxologia.ro keeps her memory on 29 mai.

This folder is John's. The Romanian calendar counts him among the ten kept on 9 august, and the life it prints gives him no story of his own beyond the name in that list and the eighty days, the irons and the sword the whole company shared. Nothing else of him was read.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day, 9 August, and saint.gr's one page for the ten tells the occasion differently from the Romanian life above. It sets the martyrdom in the reign of the iconoclast emperor Leo the Third, who by his edict of 728 forbade the hanging of holy icons altogether, not only in the churches but in any other place; the patriarch Germanus resisted the edict, the emperor convened an assembly which condemned the patriarch's stand, Germanus resigned, and his place was taken by his own synkellos and pupil Anastasius, who began at once to carry the edict out.

Then, the page says, an officer went most provocatively to the building that served as a kind of forecourt of the palace and was called the Chalke Gate because its roof was of bronze, and began with an axe to tear down the icon of Christ that was on it. The Orthodox people, enraged at the crime, threw the impious officer down from the ladder he stood on, and he was killed by the fall. The emperor then sent his guard, which killed many of the loudly protesting crowd, and among them were these ten martyrs. Of the eighty days in prison, the heated irons and the sword that the Romanian life recounts, saint.gr says nothing at all.

The page names the ten in a single line, «Ιουλιανός, Μαρκιανός, Ιωάννης, Ιάκωβος, Αλέξιος, Δημήτριος, Φώτιος, Πέτρος, Λεόντιος και Μαρία η Πατρικία», and prints the two distichs the Greek books give the day, one «Eις τους εννέα Mάρτυρας» and one «Eις την Mαρίαν». It prints no hymn of any kind and no year of death. This folder is the third of that line, «Ιωάννης».
