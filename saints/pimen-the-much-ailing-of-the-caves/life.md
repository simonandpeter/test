# Pimen the Much-Ailing of the Caves

saint.gr's calendar for 7 August names the venerable Pimen, the one in the cave, the much-suffering, and sets his death in its own head line as 1110. Of the life behind that year it says one sentence and no more: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον Βίο του Οσίου παρά μόνο ότι ήταν Ρώσος μοναχός που κοιμήθηκε το 1110 μ.Χ.» — we have no details of the venerable one's life, except that he was a Russian monk who fell asleep in the year 1110.

The epithets are the whole of his portrait on this page: the cave he lived in, and the long suffering his name there carries. The same day's listing holds a second Russian Pimen, the faster, whom the page dates to the fourteenth century and keeps as an entry of his own; the two are not one man. saint.gr prints no hymn to either of them.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him the fourth entry of its Julian 7 August, and it is the first life this folder has had: the Greek calendar gave it a line. He was sickly from childhood, it says, and from childhood longed for the monastic life. Brought to the Caves Lavra at Kyiv to be healed, he stayed there until his death. He prayed to God for sickness rather than for health.

By night angels appeared to him and tonsured him a monk, and on that occasion they told him that he would be ill until death and would be made well just before it; and so it was. He lay twenty years in his bed. He worked wonders in his lifetime and was unusually clear-sighted. Before his death he rose from the bed completely well, at once prepared a grave for himself, and reposed in the Lord in 1110 — the year this folder already held, now given it a second time.
