# Leonides, father of Origen

Leonides was a leading citizen of Alexandria and a learned Christian philosopher. He and his wife had seven children, and the eldest of them became so famous a Christian scholar that Leonides was often called simply «tatăl lui Origen», the father of Origen. He gave his brilliant son his first lessons in worldly learning himself, and led him on from them to the study of the holy Scriptures.

Origen came to know the Scriptures so well, the page says, that as a grown man he could recite long passages by heart, and could set verses of the Old and the New Testament side by side on the strength of key words. Leonides and his wife gave all their children a fine Christian upbringing, in which family prayer, the observances of their faith and the love of God had the first place.

The family lived through a time of great persecution of the Christians, and when the persecution was at its worst Leonides was seized by order of Laetus, the governor of Egypt. It was soon clear that he would suffer. Origen, then only seventeen, was eager to go with his father to martyrdom for Christ; but his mother could not bear to lose her husband and her firstborn together, and found an unusual way of stopping him — she locked up all his clothes, and so kept him from leaving the house.

While Leonides was in prison Origen wrote him a moving letter urging him to receive martyrdom with joy, and adding: «Ai grijă, stăpâne, ca nu cumva de dragul nostru să-ți schimbi gândul» — take care, my lord, not to change your mind for our sake. Leonides was beheaded in the year 202. The state not only put him to death but confiscated all his property, leaving his wife and children in great poverty; yet they were rich, the page ends, in the memory of their good husband and father, who had given his life bravely for Jesus Christ, and counted themselves happy to be a martyr's wife and a martyr's sons.
