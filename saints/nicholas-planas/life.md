# Nicholas Planas

Nicholas was born on the island of Naxos of devout parents and received the name Nicholas at his baptism. From his earliest childhood he had a marked leaning towards the things of God, and was often found in the chapel of Saint Nicholas that stood on his parents' land, where, wrapped in a sheet, he would spend long stretches singing over every church hymn he knew.

When his father died, Nicholas being nineteen, he moved with his mother and his sister to Athens; at twenty-eight he married and had a child. He was ordained deacon in 1879 and priest in 1884. After his wife's departure to eternity he served the Liturgy daily for fifty years, from eight in the morning until two or three in the afternoon, and in all that time was never ill. Worn out, he would go to the houses of those close to him and rest a little in an armchair; at nine in the evening he would reach one of the churches and begin the vigil.

He ate once a day, in the evening only, and in all the fasts without oil; at the feasts he sang their supplicatory canons until the leave-taking. His joy and his life were to glorify God day and night, with liturgies, vigils, vespers, supplications, blessings of water, anointings and memorials; and the little churches where he served were for many a consolation and a refuge.

For years together he carried about with him the lists of names given him each day. Out of a measureless love for the departed he kept lists yellowed with age, always adding more, and read them all with a father's care at every Liturgy. He helped poor families, widows and orphans — young widows above all, because poverty pushed them towards ruin — and young men to pay for their studies; and the taxi drivers carried him for nothing, knowing that whoever drove the holy father had a good day's earnings.

From everything he did and said it was plain that he had no notion of his own holiness, for the tears for his sins never left his eyes, although he tried to live hidden and alone. He bore every hurt from men with long patience, silence and prayer, and God often worked wonders through this ascetic full of simplicity, who made of the heart of Athens a true desert and filled it with the fragrance of prayer. He departed to eternity in 1932; his body was found incorrupt in due time, and he was numbered among the saints on 23 July 1992.
