# Charitina of Lithuania

She was a princess of the Lithuanian kingdom, and she spent her life in a Russian city: the Russian calendar's own phrase for her is «кн. Литовской, в Новгороде подвизавшейся» — of Lithuania, who struggled at Novgorod. The house was the women's monastery of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, built on Sinichya hill. Having decided to give her life to the Lord she took the monastic habit there, and for the virtue of her life she was made abbess of the house; until her death she was to all the sisters a pattern of humility, purity and strict abstinence. She died in 1281 and was buried in the monastery's own church of Peter and Paul.

The Iconographic Original the calendar quotes keeps both halves of her, the royal and the monastic, and is worth having because it is the only description of her person: «Святая и праведная Харитина, начальница Петровского девичьего монастыря, что в Новеграде. Родом бысть королевства Литовского; подобием простовласа девица, в единой свите без мантии» — head of the Peter convent in Novgorod; of the Lithuanian kingdom by birth; in likeness a maiden with her hair loose, in a single shift without a mantle.

**The same day keeps a second Charitina** and the two are not one woman: the martyr of Amisus in Pontus, whom the Greek and the Romanian calendars also keep, died in 304, nearly a thousand years before this one. The Russian page prints them nine lines apart and distinguishes them by title, «мц.» against «прп.»
