# Vladimir the Presbyter

Vladimir Ionovich Tsedrinsky was born in Nizhny Novgorod province and began his service to the Church in the Omsk diocese; in 1909, already a priest, he was moved to the Turkestan diocese as district observer of parish schools and missionary, and afterwards served at the cathedral of St Nicholas in Lepsinsk in the Semirechye region, its rector from 6 July 1912 and later dean of the Lepsinsk district. It was his report — that the peasant union of the district had resolved to take the clergy’s land and stop payment for services — that moved Bishop Innokenty of Turkestan to publish an appeal to all true Orthodox Christians and Russian citizens to care for the Church and ease the priests’ lot.

On a Sunday of the Dormition Fast in 1918 he preached to his flock, urging them to end the fratricidal strife and be faithful sons of the Church and the fatherland. On 27 August, after the Liturgy, he was arrested by Red Army men, who set him between two horses and drove them out of town to the cemetery, where before his parishioners’ eyes they hacked him to death with sabres. The faithful carried his body into the church, priests from the neighbouring villages sang the funeral, and he was buried in the Lepsinsk cemetery beside the place of his death; panikhidas were sung there on the day each year while the region still had priests, and afterwards the people kept the grave. His relics were found on 2 July 2001 and rest in the church of St John the Theologian in Taldykorgan. He was glorified at the Jubilee Council of 2000. The Sretensky calendar prints no life for him and gives 1920 as the year of his death; azbyka.ru, following the Orthodox Encyclopaedia, gives 1918, and both are recorded here.
