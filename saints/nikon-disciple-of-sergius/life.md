# Nikon, disciple of Sergius

saint.gr's calendar for 17 November keeps «Όσιος Νίκων ο θαυματουργός μαθητής του Αγίου Σεργίου» — Nikon the wonderworker, disciple of Saint Sergius — and dates his falling asleep 1427. The venerable Nikon, the page says, was a disciple of Saint [Sergius](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh), and it sends the reader to the twenty-fifth of September for him. Then it says plainly that we have no other details of the Russian venerable's life except that he fell asleep in 1427.

The one other thing it records is where he lies: the relics of the saint are at the Lavra of the Holy Trinity and Saint Sergius at Moscow. It prints no hymn for him and names nobody else beside him. So this folder keeps a discipleship, a year and a resting place, and says that the calendar has nothing further — the epithet wonderworker included, for which the page gives no miracle of its own.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 17 November and prints two lives behind one index. He was born at Yuryev-Polsky, and when he came to [Sergius of Radonezh](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) asking to be tonsured he was sent away to a disciple of his for testing, and was not received at once there either: only the boy’s refusal to go away got him the habit. Ordained priest when he came of age, he returned, was set to serve the brethren, and spent his days in the monastery’s business and his nights in prayer, until his teacher took him into his own cell and, six months before his own death, named him his successor.

After the house was burnt he was told in a vision not to grieve over its ruin, which would not lie waste but spread further; and when the brethren came back to a place turned to ashes he set them to new work. A wooden church of the Life-giving Trinity was consecrated in 1411 on the day of his teacher’s falling asleep, and on 5 July 1422, while the trenches for the foundations of a stone church over that grave were being dug, the incorrupt relics were found. He brought the best iconographers of the day to adorn the new church. Shown in a last vision the place of his own rest, he gave the brethren their instructions, communicated, blessed them and died on 17 November 1426, and was buried close to his teacher’s shrine, divided from it by one wall of stone.
