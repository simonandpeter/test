# Xenia of Kalamata

Xenia was born at Kalamata in the Peloponnese in the year 291. Her parents were called Nicholas and Despoina, saint.gr says; they were devout Christians and came from the eastern parts of Italy, and because of the continual and harsh persecutions of the Christians in those years they took refuge at Kalamata and settled on a farm outside the town, her father being a farmer. From childhood she adorned her soul with fasting, self-restraint, silence, regular prayer, modesty of speech, tears and vigils, and she helped the poor and the widows and the orphans with all her strength.

Dometianos, the prefect of Kalamata, met her once by chance and was dazzled by her beauty and wanted to make her his wife. Xenia refused firmly to change her faith and become the wife of a pagan ruler; and Dometianos then tortured her in the most dreadful way, and when he saw that he could not change her mind he beheaded her at last, on 3 May in the year 318. After her death, by the grace of God, she worked a great many wonders.

The page's couplet keeps something the body does not: «Ξενία τρίτῃ καμίνῳ βλήθη τμηθεῖσα» — on the third Xenia was cast into a furnace, and beheaded. Both readings are printed, and saint.gr does not reconcile them. Its apolytikion names what she is asked for: the grace to undo sorceries, to drive out demons and to heal diseases. A church in her honour was raised west of Kalamata in 1993 and is a chapel of the parish of the Holy Trinity there.
