# George I

George I, patriarch of Constantinople, died in 686, and almost nothing is
known of him before his election or after the council that was the great
event of his patriarchate. He was a presbyter and sacristan of St Sophia
when, at the emperor Constantine IV’s direction, in November or December
679, he was raised to the throne in place of the exiled Theodore I — who
took it again after George’s death — the emperor choosing him to ease a
reconciliation with the Church of Rome after the breach of 672 over
Monothelitism.

Soon after his enthronement, at the emperor’s insistence, he convened the
Sixth Ecumenical Council at Constantinople, which sat from 7 November 680 to
16 September 681. He and Macarius I of Antioch were called on to justify the
official Monothelite doctrine that Byzantium had held for more than fifty
years against the charges of the Roman legates, and did so from the first
five councils, the authority of the patriarchs Sergius, Pyrrhus, Paul II and
Peter and of Pope Honorius, Cyril of Alexandria and other patristic texts;
at the seventh session, on 13 February 681, the legates in the emperor’s
presence demanded their answer to the letters of Pope Agatho on the dispute,
and at the eighth, on 7 March, George declared his agreement with Agatho,
and Pope Vitalian’s name went into the diptychs of the Church of
Constantinople, which ended the schism of East and West.

At the sixteenth session he asked the legates not to insist on
anathematising the patriarchs Sergius, Pyrrhus, Paul and Peter who had held
Monothelitism; the papal representatives, backed by the council, refused the
concession and he submitted. On 16 September 681 the council adopted the
Orthodox confession of two wills and two energies in Christ, which he signed
after the Roman legates, and he signed also the council’s letter to Agatho.
He died in February 686.

The Sretensky calendar, whose line this entry stood under, gives his years
as 678 to 683 and pairs him with [John
V](/saints/john-v-patriarch-of-constantinople), his predecessor but one,
both under Constantine Pogonatus; the Russian calendar keeps the two on 18
August of the old style, 31 August.

The Greek calendar keeps the two patriarchs together on 18 August and gives both of them their years in the see. saint.gr says that the patriarch John V succeeded Thomas II in the year 668 and was patriarch until 674; that before this, as a presbyter of the Church of Constantinople, he had served as protekdikos and chartophylax of the Patriarchate and as skeuophylax of Hagia Sophia; that he was marked out for his Orthodox mind and shepherded the church peacefully under Constantine Pogonatos; and that he was a lover of mankind and no lover of money, living with great simplicity, and always saying that nothing honours a cleric, and a hierarch most of all, so much as the simplicity of his life and the spending of his income on the hungry and the naked.

Of George I it says that he was patriarch from 678 to 683, that before this he had been synkellos of the Patriarchate and skeuophylax of Hagia Sophia, that the Sixth Ecumenical Council, which anathematised the Monothelite heretics, was assembled at Constantinople in his time, and that of his character it was written «Νομεὺς ἀγαθὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ νόμων φύλαξ» — a good shepherd, a keeper of the laws of God. The page's own distich for George has him dying with the words «Ὡς κελεύεις Χριστέ μου … ἔρχομαι κληθεὶς ἄνω», as you command, my Christ, I come, being called above; the one for John says the faces of the Church were downcast, not bearing the loss of him. saint.gr prints no hymn for either.
