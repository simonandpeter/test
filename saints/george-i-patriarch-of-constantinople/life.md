# George I

George I, patriarch of Constantinople, died in 686, and almost nothing is
known of him before his election or after the council that was the great
event of his patriarchate. He was a presbyter and sacristan of St Sophia
when, at the emperor Constantine IV’s direction, in November or December
679, he was raised to the throne in place of the exiled Theodore I — who
took it again after George’s death — the emperor choosing him to ease a
reconciliation with the Church of Rome after the breach of 672 over
Monothelitism. Soon after his enthronement, at the emperor’s insistence, he
convened the Sixth Ecumenical Council at Constantinople, which sat from 7
November 680 to 16 September 681. He and Macarius I of Antioch were called
on to justify the official Monothelite doctrine that Byzantium had held for
more than fifty years against the charges of the Roman legates, and did so
from the first five councils, the authority of the patriarchs Sergius,
Pyrrhus, Paul II and Peter and of Pope Honorius, Cyril of Alexandria and
other patristic texts; at the seventh session, on 13 February 681, the
legates in the emperor’s presence demanded their answer to the letters of
Pope Agatho on the dispute, and at the eighth, on 7 March, George declared
his agreement with Agatho, and Pope Vitalian’s name went into the diptychs
of the Church of Constantinople, which ended the schism of East and West. At
the sixteenth session he asked the legates not to insist on anathematising
the patriarchs Sergius, Pyrrhus, Paul and Peter who had held Monothelitism;
the papal representatives, backed by the council, refused the concession and
he submitted. On 16 September 681 the council adopted the Orthodox
confession of two wills and two energies in Christ, which he signed after
the Roman legates, and he signed also the council’s letter to Agatho. He
died in February 686. The Sretensky calendar, whose line this entry stood
under, gives his years as 678 to 683 and pairs him with [John
V](/saints/john-v-patriarch-of-constantinople), his predecessor but one,
both under Constantine Pogonatus; the Russian calendar keeps the two on 18
August of the old style, 31 August.
