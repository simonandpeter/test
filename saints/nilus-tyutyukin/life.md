# Nilus (Tyutyukin)

He was born on 4 May 1871 at Olyavidovo in the Dmitrov district of the Moscow province, to the peasants Theodore and Anna Tyutyukin, and was named Nicholas at his baptism. Out of the village school he went into Pozdnyakov's mill at Dmitrov, first as an apprentice and then as a weaver, and afterwards worked at Zimin's weaving mill at Orekhovo-Zuyevo. In 1901 he left for Moscow and served in one of its churches.

Having chosen the monastic way he entered the Joseph of Volokolamsk monastery in 1904 and was received there as a novice two years later; in 1907 he was tonsured with the name Nilus. He was made the house's steward in 1909, ordained hierodeacon in 1910, and in 1913 appointed acting dean and ordained hieromonk, and confirmed in the deanery. The monastery was closed in 1920. For the first years after the closing he served in the churches of the Volokolamsk district, from 1925 in the church of the Nativity of the Mother of God at Timoshevo, and from 1931 in the church of the Saviour Not Made by Hands at Kiyevo in the Dmitrov district, a parish of six villages and the settlement at the Lobnya railway station.

On 21 February 1938 the chairman of the village soviet wrote the NKVD a character sheet on him: the priest Nilus Theodorovich Tyutyukin had all along carried on anti-Soviet work among the people, had lately been saying that the collective farms were the old bondage over again as under the landlords, and had sent his whole church council out to collect signatures against the closing of the church when the soviet and the party cell called a meeting on anti-religious propaganda. Discipline in the collective farm weakened day by day under his influence, the chairman concluded, and therefore he must be isolated from the territory of the Kiyevo village soviet as a dangerous element. The NKVD opened a case against him the same day, and he was arrested on 28 February on a charge of counter-revolutionary activity.

The investigator asked him on what grounds he had spread his influence among the collective farmers, proposing that they leave the farms in a body and telling them that everything they had worked for would be taken from them. He answered that he had never spoken to the collective farmers or to the faithful about leaving the farms, nor told the farmers not to work in them. They confronted him with one of the false witnesses and he turned aside every charge brought against him, and he did not admit himself guilty at any later questioning either. An NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot on 11 March 1938. He was shot on 20 March 1938 and buried in an unmarked common grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow. That civil day is the Julian 7 March on which the calendar keeps him.
