# Audiesus of Bethchaschar

saint.gr says that all these holy martyrs came of Persia and contended in the years of the emperor Constantine the Great, whose reign it dates 306 to 337, and of the Persian king Shapur II, whose reign it dates 309 to 379. Audiesus was bishop in the city of Bethchaschar, and he was denounced by his own nephew: the charge was that he, and Abdas the bishop of the city of Chaschar, and thirty-eight other Christians — sixteen presbyters, nine deacons, six monks and seven virgins — were slandering the Persian religion in order to win people over to Jesus Christ.

Under examination they did not deny that they taught the word of God, and said they held it their duty to draw people out of the nets of delusion by the legitimate means of teaching. Then Arseth, the king's brother, gave orders and they were bound with ropes so tightly that their bones broke; and this went on for seven days. Arseth then supposed their courage would have failed and invited them to eat food offered to idols. They refused, and were strengthened the more when the brave athlete of Christ Abdas, who had been arrested at that moment, was added to their company.

Then their beheading was ordered, and so all of them received the unfading crown of eternal glory, the page says, in 375. Of Audiesus himself it prints one synaxarion verse besides: that having given himself up to the sword, he was counted worthy to see the great Jesus. It names none of the thirty-eight, gives no place of death and prints no hymn.
