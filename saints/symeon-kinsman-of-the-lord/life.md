# Symeon, kinsman of the Lord

Symeon was a kinsman of the Lord, the son of Cleopas, who was the brother of Joseph the Betrothed. The life sets out the reckoning at length and names its authorities for it — Eusebius of Caesarea in Palestine, George Cedrenus and Nicephorus Callistus Xanthopoulos — who agree that Joseph and Cleopas were brothers by the same father and mother, Joseph the elder and Cleopas the younger, and that after Joseph came back from Egypt Cleopas took to wife a daughter of Joseph named Mary, who bore Symeon.

The life then argues the point against the other reading, that Joseph took the wife of Cleopas: Joseph died before the Lord's baptism, whereas Cleopas was alive after the Resurrection and saw the Lord at Emmaus with Luke, and was alive again after Pentecost and ended his life a martyr; so the elder cannot have survived to take the younger's widow. And the Gospel of John, at the Cross, says only «Maria a lui Cleopa» — Mary of Cleopas — not Mary the daughter of Cleopas; she is his wife, and Symeon's mother.

Coming of age and hearing of the Lord's miracles, Symeon believed in Him; and after the voluntary Passion and the Ascension he was numbered in the company of the seventy apostles, going through cities and villages, teaching, working wonders, lighting the peoples with the light of the faith and undoing the darkness of idolatry.

In that time James, the brother of the Lord and son of Joseph, held the high-priestly throne of Jerusalem as its first bishop; and because he confessed Christ the unbelieving Jews threw him down from the seat of the Church and killed him with a blow of wood on the head. Soon after the killing of James came the scattering of Jerusalem by Titus and Vespasian, as the Lord had foretold.

After the scattering, when the city had begun to be lived in again by those who were left, the disciples of the Lord still alive gathered together with those who were kin to Christ after the flesh, and set Symeon — the son of Cleopas, nephew of Joseph, kinsman of the Lord — as the second bishop of Jerusalem in the place of James. The life says he adorned that throne, being himself a church of the Holy Spirit, and brought back the wandering to Christ.

Many years later, under the reign of Trajan, envious heretics accused him to Atticus, the proconsul of Rome, on two counts: that he was of the house of David by descent, and that he was a Christian. The emperors of Rome were then persecuting the seed of David, searching everywhere so that no trace of that royal line should remain among the Jews, and killing those who believed in Christ so that the gods of the nations might be honoured through the whole world.

So Symeon, both of David's line and a believer in Christ and His kinsman, was seized by the order of the proconsul Atticus. He was old, the life says — more than a hundred years — and after many torments he was nailed to a cross, as the Lord Christ had been, and gave up his soul into the hands of God. The page gives no year, and dates him only by the reign of Trajan.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr sets out his kindred in two accounts rather than one. By Hegesippus he was a son of Clopas, the brother of Joseph, and the brother of Judas; by another version he was a son of Joseph the Betrothed and the brother of [James the Brother of the Lord](/saints/james-the-brother-of-the-lord), whose death it puts in the year 62 and whom Symeon succeeded after the destruction of Jerusalem by the Romans in 70. It insists on one point of the accusation against him: he was denounced to the consul Atticus not by Christian heretics but by Jews, and the charge had two heads — that he was of the line of David, and that he was a Christian. He was seized, harshly tortured, and put to death on a cross in the year 107, at the age of a hundred and twenty; and Trajan, under whom this happened, it dates 98 to 117.

The page adds that a service for Symeon by the hymnographer Theophanes is to be found in the Paris codices, and that some synaxaristes «περιττώς», needlessly, repeat his memory on 18 September as well. Its couplet plays on his title: thou sufferest what is brotherly to the Lord, Symeon, being hung upon a tree, as the Lord's brother.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović states the descent in two lines and argues it nowhere: one of the Seventy apostles, the son of Cleopas, and Cleopas the brother of [Joseph](/saints/joseph-the-betrothed) the Betrothed. Seeing the wonders of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, Symeon believed and was numbered among the Seventy; and with great zeal and courage he preached the Gospel of Christ everywhere through Judea.

When the malicious Jews killed [James](/saints/james-the-brother-of-the-lord), the brother of the Lord and the first bishop of the Church of Jerusalem — pushing him from the height of the temple and striking him on the head with a club — then this Symeon, James's kinsman, was set bishop in Jerusalem. As the second bishop in the Holy City he governed the Church of God with wisdom and with power into deep old age, and he was over a hundred years old when he suffered.

His suffering, the book says, was in this way. In the time of the emperor Trajan a twofold persecution was raised in Palestine, against the descendants of David and against the Christians; and malicious men accused Symeon as the one and as the other both. He endured great torments and at the last was crucified upon a cross, as his Lord had been, whom he had served faithfully on earth. **This book gives no year either**, and it names no proconsul; the 107 this folder carries from the Greek calendar stands as it was, and so does the absence of a year of birth.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this same day and heads him «(107)», the year the Greek page gives. Its name index lists two readings and they do not agree about his age: one has him crucified at a hundred and twenty, which is the figure already recorded here, and the other has him taken when he was already an old man of a hundred. Nothing was altered on the second's account. Between them they add that he was the son of Cleopas, the younger brother of [Joseph the Betrothed](/saints/joseph-the-betrothed); that he came to believe in his maturity, having seen the wonders of the Lord, and was numbered among the Seventy; and that he governed the Church of Jerusalem for forty years, chosen to it by the Christians after the killing of [James the Brother of the Lord](/saints/james-the-brother-of-the-lord), whom this calendar dates to the year 63.

The denunciation, these readings say, was laid before a Roman governor named Аттик in the reign of Trajan, and it was twofold: that he was descended from king David — for the Romans were destroying every descendant of David — and that he confessed the Christian faith. He was seized, tortured a long while, and crucified. This is also the first calendar in this folder to print hymns for him in Slavonic: a troparion, a second troparion and a kontakion, all three naming him.
