# Germanus of Dobrogea

This holy and God-bearing man was born about the year 368 in the north of Dobrogea, of proto-Romanian parents, from the same village as [John Cassian](/saints/john-cassian) and of the same age; the village of Casimcea is held to be the ancestral hearth of both these Daco-Roman saints from the mouths of the Danube. Their parents and forefathers, doxologia's life says, had been Christians since the first century, baptised in the name of the Holy Trinity — perhaps by the apostle Andrew himself and his disciples, who sowed the word of the Gospel about the Black Sea and in Lesser Scythia, the Dobrogea of today.

After learning his letters in his native village and at the bishopric of Tomis, together with the blessed Cassian, with whom he was a friend and a fellow-dweller until death, he went to take in the teaching of the holy fathers in the parts of Constantinople. Then, again together, the two entered the monastic life at one of the monasteries of southern Dobrogea that depended on the famous eparchy of Tomis, where great and God-bearing hierarchs such as Bretanion and Theotimus shone.

Wanting a higher spiritual life in Christ, he went with the blessed Cassian to venerate the venerable fathers who laboured in Egypt, in the valley of the Nile. After visiting the great solitaries there and receiving many helpful words from them, they struggled for a time near the great Egyptian anchorites and learned unceasing prayer from them; then they went to the holy places and venerated the life-giving tomb of our Lord Jesus Christ.

They laboured afterwards for a time in the monasteries of Constantinople, near [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom), who ordained them both — Cassian deacon and Germanus priest. This was about the year 400. In 403, when certain hierarchs and officials of the East, urged on by the empress Eudoxia, rose against Chrysostom and demanded that he be deposed from his rank, Germanus was among those who took the great hierarch's part.

His friend Cassian honoured him so much that in his book of *Conferences with the fathers of the desert* he calls him «the holy father Germanus». He did a great deal for the strengthening of monastic life in Dobrogea, in Italy and in Gaul. After a hard and long spiritual struggle, having reached the measure of holiness, he died at Rome about the years 405 to 415, giving his soul up in peace into the hands of Christ, full of days and of the grace of the Holy Spirit.

For the excellence of his life the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church numbered him among the holy fathers on 20–21 June 1992, with 29 February as the day of his feast, and 28 February in the years that are not leap years.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 28 February, where the Romanian keeps him on 29, and its whole entry is three sentences: that the venerable Germanus of Dobrudja was born about the year 358, that he became a monk and practised his asceticism in Romania in a manner pleasing to God, and that he fell asleep in peace between the years 405 and 415. Its heading prints «(358 - ;)». Those years sit a little earlier than the ones doxologia's life gives, and no date in this folder is changed on their account; what the Greek page adds is the name Dobrudja in its own voice, and the day the Greek church keeps him on.
