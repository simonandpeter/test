# Adrian of Poshekhonye

saint.gr says the venerable Adrian of Poshekhonye lived in Russia in the sixteenth century. He was a monk first at the monastery of St Cornelius in the district of Komel, and in the year 1540 he saw in a vision an aged monk named Bestouz calling him to found a new monastery. With the blessing of his abbot, who put into his hands an icon of the Dormition of the Theotokos, Adrian and his disciple [Leonidas](/saints/leonidas-disciple-of-adrian) left the monastic community they were in.

The page tells what settled the place for them. Robbers came on the icon where it hung in a tree and meant to take it, but a hidden power pushed them back; terrified at the wonder, they left bread and fish below the tree. The monks gave thanks to God for that God-sent gift, and understood that this was the place granted them to build the new monastery on, in honour of the Dormition of the Theotokos.

Ten years after the founding of the house, on the fifth of March 1550, a band of robbers plundered the monastery and tortured and killed its abbot, Adrian. His holy relics were found in the year 1626, and at the order of the patriarch Philaret they were reverently translated to the monastery he had himself founded, in the town of Poshekhonye. The headings of the page give him the year 1549 and his disciple 1550, the reverse of what its own body says; the body's reading is the one kept here, and the day the calendar keeps him on is the day of the killing it names.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 19 November, and its line is a finding of relics and not a death: «Обретение мощей прмч. Адриана Пошехонского, Ярославского (1625).» Its three life pages all head him with three days, «5 марта , 23 мая (Ростов.) , 19 ноября», and the first of those is the day he was killed. He was born at Rostov the Great early in the sixteenth century, of pious parents Gregory and Irene, was tonsured in the house of [Cornelius of Komel](/saints/cornelius-of-komel), and was a skilled painter of icons whose gift helped to adorn it.

Three years after his teacher’s death, with the abbot’s blessing, on 13 September 1540 he took an icon of the Dormition and went out with his disciple into unpeopled places for silence, led by a mysterious elder; and in the Poshekhonye forest, on the bank of the river Votkha, the unknown elder showed them the place for their hermitage and became invisible. The Lord marked the place of the house that was to come, the page says, with the ringing of bells. After some while of solitary struggle he founded there a monastery in honour of the Dormition and became its abbot, under a strict rule which shut the gate to women; and he went on painting icons, withdrawing when he wanted silence to a cell with a chapel a verst off in the thick of the forest.

Much sorrow he had to bear from the villagers round about, who at the devil’s prompting resolved to destroy the monastery. In 1550, in Lent, in the night of the 5th to the 6th of March, armed robbers broke into the house and after torments strangled him, and his body was thrown into the forest; a pious priest Isidore took it, buried it secretly, and planted a rowan over the grave, at which many healings were worked.

His pages do not agree about the relics. One has them found on 19 November 1625 and carried into the house he had founded on 17 December 1625, under the patriarch Philaret; another has them opened on 17 December 1626. The day line gives 1625. **None of that is a year of death**, and this folder’s 1550 is untouched. The grade is not one thing here either: the day line abbreviates «прмч.», a venerable-martyr, and his own name page, 76, heads him «Священномученик Адриан Пошехонский», a hieromartyr. The `types` on this folder are left as they stood and both readings stand here. The patriarch of 1625 is not the metropolitan of Moscow whom this corpus keeps under the same forename and who was born in 1782.
