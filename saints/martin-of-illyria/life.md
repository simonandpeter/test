# Martin of Illyria

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the two of them one heading and three sentences, and that is the whole of what this corpus has for them. They were Slavs, it says. They were persecuted in Illyria by heretics, by the Arians. Sent away into exile, these knights of Orthodoxy ended their earthly life in the fourth century and passed over to the Lord.

The book names neither the place of their exile nor the year of their death, nor a see, a monastery, a rank or a kinship, and it does not say that they were killed — only that their earthly life ended where they had been sent. Nothing here is filled in from anywhere else: no coordinates were taken for Illyria, which is a province and not a town, and the century is recorded as the century the book prints.

No rank word stands anywhere in the entry, so this folder carries none. Exile for the faith is what the Church calls confession, but Исповедник is not a word on this page, and a rank this corpus has not read is a rank it does not store. The Prologue prints no troparion and no kontakion for any of its saints.

He is named in that heading beside [Heraclius of Illyria](/saints/heraclius-of-illyria), and the two are inseparable in the only account this corpus has of either: one heading, one persecution, one exile and one century.
