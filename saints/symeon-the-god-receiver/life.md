# Symeon the God-receiver

Symeon the elder, by the witness of the Gospel, was a righteous and believing man who awaited the consolation of Israel, and the Holy Spirit was upon him; and it was made known to him by God that the true Messiah was shortly to come into the world. The Romanian life tells, on the authority of the old historians, how the knowledge came to him.

When by the command of Ptolemy, king of Egypt, the Law of Moses and all the prophecies were being turned from Hebrew into Greek, seventy wise men of Israel were chosen for the work, and Symeon was among them as one skilled in the divine Scripture. Translating, he was writing out the words of the prophet Isaiah, and when he came to the words *Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son* he doubted, saying that a virgin who had not known a man could not give birth, and took up his knife to scrape the words away.

The angel of the Lord appeared and held his hand, saying: "Do not be unbelieving towards what is written, whose fulfilment thou thyself shalt see; for thou shalt not taste death until thou hast seen Him who shall be born of the pure Virgin, Christ the Lord." Believing the angelic and prophetic words, he waited with longing for the coming of Christ into the world; and he was righteous and blameless in his life, keeping from every evil, sitting beside the temple and praying God to have mercy on His world and to deliver men from the cunning devil.

When our Lord Jesus Christ was born, and forty days were fulfilled, and He was brought into the temple after the custom of the Law in the hands of His most pure Mother, Symeon too came into the temple at the prompting of the Spirit. Looking on the Child who is before the ages, and on the blameless Virgin who had borne Him, he knew that this was the promised Messiah and that she was the Virgin through whom Isaiah's prophecy was to be fulfilled; and he worshipped Him, seeing her ringed with heavenly light and shining with the divine rays.

So with fear and with joy he came near to her, took the Lord in his own hands, and said: *Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart, O Master, according to Thy word, in peace, for mine eyes have seen Thy salvation.* He prophesied of the passion of Christ and of His crucifixion, saying that a sword of grief and sorrow would pass through the soul of the Mother of God when she should see her Son crucified. And so, giving thanks to God, he departed in peace at a great age, for it is written of him that he lived three hundred and sixty years, God lengthening his life so that he might reach the time longed for through all the ages.

Many understand that this Symeon, who received the child Jesus in his hands and blessed Mary the Mother of God and Joseph, was a priest, as [Zacharias](/saints/zacharias-father-of-the-forerunner) was a priest who had received Mary before when she was brought as a child into the temple. Among those who understand it so, the page names [Athanasius the Great](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria), in his book on the one nature of the Father and the Son, Cyril of Jerusalem in his homily on the Meeting of the Lord, and Epiphanius in his teaching on the fathers of the Old Law. But because there are some who do not agree, for the Gospel does not speak of it, the life declines to settle the matter, and so does this page. The calendar keeps him the day after the Meeting, together with the prophetess [Anna, daughter of Phanuel](/saints/anna-the-prophetess), and sings one troparion for them both.
