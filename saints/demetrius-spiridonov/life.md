# Demetrius (Spiridonov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar closes the Julian 16 November with him, «Мч. Димитрия (1938).», and prints no life page behind it; his name page carries a title and a list of days and no text, so the life here is the one the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru prints from the database of the St Tikhon’s university. Demetrius Spiridonovich Spiridonov was born in 1871. Already at the Tauride theological seminary he stood out among his fellows by remarkable gifts, which gave his parents ground for great hopes of him, and in a certain sense he justified them. At the seminary he grew close in spirit to the archpriest Nicholas Mezentsev, and in time that grew into a great friendship which lasted until the martyr’s end of both.

He went on, at the public cost, to the theological academy of St Petersburg and finished it with success. While he studied he regularly helped his younger brother Eleazar, sending him the books he needed, giving him advice and supporting him. He was a man of great religion. His humility and meekness bred in him that astonishing delicacy, free of all suspicion, at which many wondered: he allowed himself not merely no rude or insolent act but no such word, even where a strict rebuke was plainly called for. A mind raised and sunk in the high matters of theology, and a prayerful standing back from the cares of every day, moved him to carry his studies further at the university of Moscow as an auditor.

Back in the Crimea he taught in the Tauride theological seminary. His scholarship was highly thought of, and on the recommendation of Arseny Markevich, the historian and ethnographer of the Crimea, he was taken on 13 December 1907 into the Tauride learned archival commission, which had the best scholars not only of the Crimea in it; on 28 November 1908 he read a brilliant paper there on the question of the martyrdom in the Crimea of the first-century pope of Rome whom this corpus keeps on another day. From 1913 to 1916 he worked in Greece and came to know many Greek bishops and university professors. He published much in the Tauride diocesan gazette on the history of the Church — on the early Christian martyrs, on Novatian and his schism, and a translation out of the Greek of the treatise On Virginity ascribed to that same hieromartyr.

About that time he was married to Anisia Ivanovna. The marriage was a happy one and they had no children. From 1918 to 1922 he taught at Yevpatoria, and from 1919 he was secretary of the council of the Greek society and chairman of the church twenty of the Greek church of the prophet Elijah, whose rector was his brother the priest Eleazar. On 6 August 1922 he was elected deputy chairman of the archival commission. The upheavals of the revolution and the civil war made him look for a quieter place to work, and in 1922 he made an unsuccessful attempt to emigrate to Greece; being wholly outside politics was a matter of principle with him, and in Soviet Russia that was already impossible.

After the official refusal he moved to Simferopol and taught in the Tauride university. When the Crimea was sovietised he left teaching in protest and took a place in the museum of local history, where he worked hard at scholarship and rose to be deputy director with all its scholarly work in his charge, and for a short time director. In those years he kept up a wide correspondence with scholars of other countries — Adolf Harnack at Berlin, Ellis Minns at Cambridge, Alexander Vasiliev in the United States. Men spoke of him always as a most cultivated and most erudite man of deep faith. In 1934 he applied once more to the Greek embassy to leave, on the ground of work he had begun in 1926 on the speech of the Greeks of the Mariupol country, and was refused.

Though his life had by then become like a monk’s he would not take holy orders, and the page is careful that this was neither faint-heartedness nor fear of persecutors who could take his liberty and his life at any time: those who knew him testified to a soft resoluteness in him, and a way of making the decision that was needed and carrying it out without loud assurances and unnoticed by anyone who had no business with it. Directing the museum, he was not afraid to take an active part in the life of the Church. To his arrest he remained not only a parishioner but an active member of the twenty of the Holy Trinity church at Simferopol, whose rector was then the archpriest Mitrofan Vasilkioti, so aged that he could not go beyond the church fence unhelped.

The man who helped the old priest in everything was [Nicholas (Mezentsev)](/saints/nicholas-mezentsev), his own old friend and spiritual guide. More than once he took a hand in the Church’s troubles: when the local atheists were taking down the bells he was among those who wrote to the Greek consul asking protection, and in 1934, when the church was all but closed, his qualified part in it and his fearless stand helped to use every mechanism there was and save the one Orthodox church in the city.

At the beginning of January 1938 the special section of the Crimean regional committee resolved to harden the struggle with religion. On 20 January 1938 he was arrested, and with him the whole church twenty; at about the same time some fifty Greeks connected with the church of the prophet Elijah were arrested at Yevpatoria. The case against the Greeks was built around the most prominent member of that church’s twenty, who was known to the Greek consul in Moscow. He and twenty-nine others were charged under article 58 of the criminal code.

By the decision of a troika of 5 November 1938 he was sentenced to the highest measure of punishment, though he rejected every charge categorically and did not admit his guilt. He was shot on 29 November 1938, the civil day on which the Julian 16 November of that year fell, so that the day of his death and his feast are one day. The friend named above is the Nicholas (Mezentsev) this corpus already keeps: that folder stands on the same calendar’s line for 1 февраля ст. ст., whose name link gives «Николай (Мезенцев), протоиерей» and a death in 1938, and azbyka’s own page for him keeps him on 14 февраля, which is that Julian day in the civil reckoning, and makes him a teacher of the Tauride seminary and afterwards archpriest at Simferopol.
