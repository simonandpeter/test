# Nectarios of Optina

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 29 April reads «Sfântul Nectarie de la Optina», and the page under it has tabs for an akathist, prayers, miracles, icons, places of pilgrimage, monasteries, articles, texts of the saint and photographs — but no Viață. The site's own headings elsewhere on that page call him «Sfântul Cuvios Nectarie de la Optina», and set him among the elders of Optina, for whom it prints a common akathist and a common daily prayer.

What the page does print of his own is a prayer, set under his icon: «Doamne, Iisuse Hristoase, Fiul lui Dumnezeu, Care vei veni să judeci viii și morții, miluiește-ne pe noi păcătoșii, iartă căderile noastre în păcat din această viață și ne acoperă pe noi de fața antihristului în pustia tainică a mântuirii Tale.» — Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, who wilt come to judge the living and the dead, have mercy on us sinners, forgive our falls into sin in this life, and shelter us from the face of antichrist in the secret desert of Thy salvation.

The page gives no year of birth or death, no family name, no account of his coming to Optina and no manner of his end. Nothing further is set down here.

The Greek calendar keeps him too, on 12 May, and begins the story earlier than the Romanian line does. saint.gr gives his name in the world as Nicholas Tikhonov and his birth as 1853 in the town of Yelets in the province of Oryol, of devout and God-loving parents, Basil and Helen; its head line dates him 1853 to 1928. When Nicholas was seven he lost his father, who a little before his death blessed his son with an icon of Saint Nicholas and committed the child to that great saint's guardianship — and the future elder never parted from the icon for the whole of his life. His mother's other children, younger than he, died early of hunger and sickness, and his own bond with her was a warm one of the heart; her prayers, and the strict upbringing she gave him, are what the page says kept him from temptations and misfortunes.

The Russian calendar's own reading of him is the fullest this folder has, and it begins by calling him the last elder of Optina to be chosen by the whole community. He was born in 1853 at Yelets in the province of Oryol, Nikolai Vasilyevich Tikhonov, to a poor family; his father worked in a mill and died early, and his mother, who was strict with him and ruled him by gentleness, died early too, so that he was left an orphan. He worked some years in a rich merchant's shop and spent his free time in church and over the church books, and in 1873 he came to Optina with nothing in his bag but a Gospel.

He became the spiritual son of the elder Анатолий (Зерцалов) and went for counsel to the elder Амвросий, who received him and after one conversation — which he never told anybody — kept him in the skete for good. He looked after the flowers first and then served in the sanctuary, and he lived twenty years in a cell whose door opened into the church without speaking to any of the monks: «для монаха есть только два выхода из келии — в храм да в могилу», for a monk there are only two ways out of his cell, into the church and into the grave. When the brethren complained that he came late and heavy-eyed, the elder answered «Подождите, Николка проспится, всем пригодится» — wait, when Nikolka has slept himself out he will be of use to everybody.

He was tonsured into the mantle on 14 March 1887, made hierodeacon on 19 January 1894 and ordained priest four years later; and though he was already healing the sick and foreseeing what was coming, he hid it under an outward folly. The brotherhood elected him an elder in 1912 and he refused — «Я скудоумен и такой тяготы понести не могу», I am poor-witted and cannot carry such a weight — and took it only out of obedience. He received people in the hut of the elders before him, sometimes leaving books on the table so that those waiting found their answers in them, and saying that it was to the elder Амвросий they had come and that the cell itself spoke for him. After the monastery was closed in 1923 he was arrested, and on coming out of prison he lived at the village of Kholmishchi with a peasant, where his spiritual children still found their way to him. He died in great old age on 29 April 1928 and was buried in the local cemetery; his relics were taken up on 3/16 July 1989, the mantle incorrupt and the bones the colour of amber.
