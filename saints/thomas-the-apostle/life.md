# Thomas

Thomas, called the Twin, was one of the twelve apostles of the Lord. The Greek calendar says he came of a family of fishermen; the Romanian life makes him a man of Paneas, a city of Galilee, who heard the Lord’s preaching, saw his miracles and followed him. After the Resurrection, when the other disciples told him they had seen the Lord, he would not believe them; eight days later the Lord came again to the upper room with Thomas among them, and bade him touch the wounds of the nails and be not faithless but believing. Thomas cried out, “My Lord and my God”, and was answered that those who have not seen and yet have believed are blessed — and so, the Romanian life says, his unbelief made the Church’s faith the firmer.

After Pentecost, the Greek entry says, he preached to the Persians, the Medes and the Indians; the Romanian life says the lot sent him to India, and tells how he went there as a builder with Avan, the merchant of Gundafor, king of India, gave the king’s gold for the palace to the poor, and was imprisoned until the king’s brother died, was shown the palace built for the king in heaven, and came back to life to ask for it, after which both brothers were baptised. It tells too how he came late to the burial of the Theotokos, so that her tomb was opened for him and found empty, and how at Meliapor he drew with his belt a great log that neither men nor elephants could move.

The Greek entry says that King Misdaeus in India seized him for catechising and baptising his son Azanes, his wife Tertia and his daughters Mygdonia and Narca, imprisoned him and gave him to five soldiers, who took him up a mountain and killed him with spears. The Romanian life tells the end at greater length and with other kinships — King Muzdie of Calamina, Mygdonia the wife of his friend Carizie, won to the faith through her kinswoman Sindichia, and Queen Tertiana, Mygdonia’s sister, won after her; red-hot iron plates and a furnace that did not harm him — and says that before the five spears he ordained Sifor priest and the prince Azan deacon; believers later carried his relics secretly to Mesopotamia, and the king, whose son was healed by earth from the empty tomb, was baptised by Sifor. The Greek entry records his head at the monastery of Saint John on Patmos and portions of his relics at the monastery of Saint Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain, in the Coptic Patriarchate of Egypt and in the Syrian Jacobite church at Mosul. Neither page gives a year.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him first on this day and opens with the doubt itself: through his doubting of the Lord's resurrection a fresh confirmation of that wonderful and saving event was got, for the risen Lord appeared to the disciples again in order to convince Thomas — put out thy hand and lay it in my side, and be not unbelieving but believing — and Thomas cried out, my Lord and my God.

After the descent of the Holy Spirit, when the apostles cast lots for where each should go to preach, the lot fell on Thomas to go to India. He grieved a little at having to go to so distant a country, but the Lord appeared to him and gave him courage. In India he turned many to the faith of Christ, great men and poor alike, founded a church there and appointed priests and bishops.

Among others he brought over two sisters, Tertiana and Mygdonia, the wives of two Indian princes; both were tormented by their husbands for the faith, would not live with them after their baptism, and were sent away, and being freed of their marriages they lived God-pleasing lives until their deaths. Dionysius and Pelagia, who had been betrothed to one another, heard the apostolic preaching and did not come together but gave themselves to the ascetic life instead: she ended her life as a martyr for the faith, and he was appointed a bishop by the apostle. The prince Mouzdios, Tertiana's husband, whose wife and son Azanes Thomas had baptised, condemned the apostle to death and sent five soldiers, who ran him through with five spears. None of these the Prologue calls a saint or commemorates on the day, so none of them has a folder here.

Before his own death he too, like the other apostles, was wonderfully carried to Jerusalem for the burial of the Most Holy Theotokos; but arriving late he grieved bitterly, and at his request they opened the tomb of the All-Pure and did not find the body in it, for the Lord had taken His Mother into His heavenly dwellings. And so, the Prologue says, Thomas confirmed the faith in the Lord's resurrection by his unbelief there, and here by his lateness uncovered for us the wonderful glorification of the Mother of God.

The day's reflection in the Prologue is the parable of the palace, told of him. A king of India named Gundaphorus meant to build himself a hall such as the earth had not seen, and his envoy Abban, looking for a master builder, met Thomas by God's providence; Thomas said he was the man and that no one but he could build the king what he wanted, took a great weight of gold for the work and gave it all to the poor as soon as he was out of the king's sight. Asked after two years whether the hall was finished, he answered that all was ready but the roof, took more money and gave that away too, and went about the kingdom on his own business, which was preaching the Gospel.

When the king learned that nothing had been begun he threw Thomas into prison. That night the king's brother died, and an angel took his soul through Paradise and showed him a hall more beautiful than a man's mind can conceive; and when the soul wished to enter, the angel said it could not, for the hall was his brother's, which the apostle Thomas had built for him out of his alms. The soul was returned to the body, and the brother made the king swear to give him what he asked, and then asked for the hall he had in heaven. The king doubted that there could be such a hall until his brother described it in detail; then he believed, brought Thomas out of the prison, heard from his mouth the words of salvation and of eternal life, and was baptised with his brother — and set about new works of mercy, so that he too built himself a beautiful hall in the heavens.
