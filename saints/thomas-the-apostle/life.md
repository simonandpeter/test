# Thomas

Thomas, called the Twin, was one of the twelve apostles of the Lord. The Greek calendar says he came of a family of fishermen; the Romanian life makes him a man of Paneas, a city of Galilee, who heard the Lord’s preaching, saw his miracles and followed him. After the Resurrection, when the other disciples told him they had seen the Lord, he would not believe them; eight days later the Lord came again to the upper room with Thomas among them, and bade him touch the wounds of the nails and be not faithless but believing. Thomas cried out, “My Lord and my God”, and was answered that those who have not seen and yet have believed are blessed — and so, the Romanian life says, his unbelief made the Church’s faith the firmer.

After Pentecost, the Greek entry says, he preached to the Persians, the Medes and the Indians; the Romanian life says the lot sent him to India, and tells how he went there as a builder with Avan, the merchant of Gundafor, king of India, gave the king’s gold for the palace to the poor, and was imprisoned until the king’s brother died, was shown the palace built for the king in heaven, and came back to life to ask for it, after which both brothers were baptised. It tells too how he came late to the burial of the Theotokos, so that her tomb was opened for him and found empty, and how at Meliapor he drew with his belt a great log that neither men nor elephants could move.

The Greek entry says that King Misdaeus in India seized him for catechising and baptising his son Azanes, his wife Tertia and his daughters Mygdonia and Narca, imprisoned him and gave him to five soldiers, who took him up a mountain and killed him with spears. The Romanian life tells the end at greater length and with other kinships — King Muzdie of Calamina, Mygdonia the wife of his friend Carizie, won to the faith through her kinswoman Sindichia, and Queen Tertiana, Mygdonia’s sister, won after her; red-hot iron plates and a furnace that did not harm him — and says that before the five spears he ordained Sifor priest and the prince Azan deacon; believers later carried his relics secretly to Mesopotamia, and the king, whose son was healed by earth from the empty tomb, was baptised by Sifor. The Greek entry records his head at the monastery of Saint John on Patmos and portions of his relics at the monastery of Saint Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain, in the Coptic Patriarchate of Egypt and in the Syrian Jacobite church at Mosul. Neither page gives a year.
