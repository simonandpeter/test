# Apollos of Caesarea

saint.gr's calendar for 8 December keeps six apostles of the Seventy in one entry and names them all: [Sosthenes](/saints/sosthenes-the-apostle), [Cephas](/saints/cephas-the-apostle), Apollos, [Tychicus](/saints/tychicus-the-apostle), [Caesar](/saints/caesar-the-apostle) and [Epaphroditus](/saints/epaphroditus-the-apostle). Apollos, it says, was a learned man of Alexandria, gifted with great power of speech and with knowledge of the Scriptures.

At first he knew only the baptism of John; but afterwards he was informed more exactly about the salvation of man through Jesus Christ by [Aquila](/saints/aquila) and his wife [Priscilla](/saints/priscilla), whom the same calendar keeps on 13 February. Apollos preached boldly inside the synagogue of Ephesus, and then went to Corinth and to the rest of Achaia. In the end, the page says, he was made bishop of Caesarea.

The couplet set over his name plays on the word it shares with the Word: having both grace and speech, Apollos persuaded many to reverence God the Word. The Greek calendar keeps another entry of this name on 10 June, which the corpus already holds; nothing on either page joins or parts them, and that is left as a question and not answered here.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, in a company of seven where the Greek calendar printed six, and it gives each of them a see — though not the one this folder carries: Caesarea it assigns to [Sosthenes](/saints/sosthenes-the-apostle), the first of the seven. Of all of them together it says that they preached the Gospel of Christ with a burning love, suffered torments for his holy name, and passed over into the kingdom of everlasting joy.

It also records its own other days. All seven, it says, are celebrated on 4 January with the rest of the lesser apostles, and Apollos is celebrated separately on 10 September. That last is a harder sentence than it looks: on its own 10 September this book prints an Apollos whom the Greek and Russian calendars call Apelles, and this corpus keeps that man in a folder of his own. Whether the two are one apostle is a question neither calendar settles, and nothing here has been merged on it.

The Russian church calendar keeps him with four of the others on 30 марта ст. ст., and its two readings behind that line disagree about his see. Both say he came of Alexandria and was a man of brilliant learning, and both quote the Acts at length: a Jew named Apollos, eloquent and mighty in the Scriptures, who came to Ephesus knowing only the baptism of John, was taken aside by Aquila and Priscilla and taught the way of the Lord more exactly, and then crossed to Achaia, where he mightily confuted the Jews in public. Both have Corinth as the chief place of his labour, and one of them adds that he preached in Crete at the end of his life. But the longer reading makes him bishop of Caesarea, which is the see this folder is named for, and the shorter makes him bishop of Smyrna. Nothing on either page chooses between them and nothing has been renamed on it.

There is a tangle on the source’s own side worth recording, because it is the same tangle this folder already carries. The index the day links under his name serves, among the readings of the company, a life of a different apostle — «Апостол Апеллий из числа 70-ти», whom it seats at Smyrna and whose days it gives as 4 января, 30 марта, 10 сентября, 31 октября and 8 декабря. The corpus keeps that man in a folder of his own. Whether the Smyrna of the shorter reading is his rather than this apostle’s is a question these pages leave open, and no merge has been made on it.
