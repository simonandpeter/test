# Paul, brother of Pausirius and Theodotion

Paul and his brothers [Pausirius](/saints/pausirius-brother-of-paul) and [Theodotion](/saints/theodotion-brother-of-paul) were brothers in the flesh, and saint.gr places them in the reigns of the emperors Diocletian and Maximian, when Arianus was governor in Cleopatris — the town the page identifies with the modern Suez — in Egypt. Paul and Pausirius became monks while they were still young. Theodotion was drawn away by bad friends, went off into the mountains, and lived there among brigands.

When Diocletian's persecution of the Christians began, Arianus arrested Paul and Pausirius; Paul, the page says, was then thirty-seven years old and Pausirius twenty-five. As soon as Theodotion heard of it he left the mountains and the brigands and came down to see his brothers and to say goodbye to them. By the grace of God his heart was warmed, and he too confessed that he believed in Christ, and then rushed at the tyrant and threw him down from his throne.

At Arianus's order red-hot nails were driven into Theodotion's ribs and belly, and at the last he was beheaded. Paul and Pausirius were thrown into the river, and there the two of them were martyred. The distich saint.gr prints over the pair makes the river itself their witness: «Παυσίριον καὶ Παῦλον ἄμφω συγγόνους, Ποτάμιος ῥοῦς καὶ συνάθλους δεικνύει» — the river's stream shows Pausirius and Paul to be both brothers and fellow contenders.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 24 Ιανουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/01/24/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3515/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
