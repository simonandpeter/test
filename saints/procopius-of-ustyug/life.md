# Procopius of Ustyug

saint.gr's calendar for 8 Ιουλίου says plainly that it has no details of the life of the venerable Procopius, the fool for Christ of Ustyug, apart from one wonder, and then tells it. Once, it says, the venerable one came running through the streets of Ustyug to the church; he knelt before the icon of the Mother of God and cried out, Mother of God, save us, save us. Then he turned to the people who were in the church at that moment and shouted to them to repent and to stop sinning, or else God would stone them. They took him for a madman, beat him and drove him out.

Some time later the sky over the city was covered by an enormous black cloud. All the churches filled with the faithful, and Procopius began to reproach the people and urged them to pray; and they all prayed, and the cloud broke up and the sky cleared. Then all the inhabitants, as though something were pushing them, ran out of the city, and there they saw a vast stretch of ground covered with black stones — meteorites. Had those stones fallen on the city, the page says, not a man would have been left alive.

The stones are there to this day, and the scientists who examined them confirmed that they are indeed meteorites; but how they all came to be in one place they could not explain, and that, the page says, only the servant of God knew, who had foreseen it. He fell asleep in 1303, and his canonisation by the Russian Church took place in 1818. The page gives nothing else of his life and prints no hymn and no synaxarion couplet under his line.
