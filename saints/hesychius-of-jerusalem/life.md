# Hesychius of Jerusalem

Hesychius lived and practised ascesis in Palestine in the fifth century, and saint.gr says he was born and bred of Jerusalem itself. Having studied the Holy Scripture deeply, he grew rich in the knowledge of God; then he withdrew and became a monk, and lived in the desert, visiting the venerable Fathers who were there and gathering from each of them the flowers of virtue like an industrious bee. So much virtue could not escape notice, and the high priest of Jerusalem of that time forced him to be ordained presbyter. Waiting upon the Tomb of the Lord and the other places where Christ endured the Holy Passion for our sake, he drew from them springs of knowledge and wisdom, and so interpreted and made plain every Scripture, to the profit of many.

To this the page adds what its other sources say. Theophanes, in his Chronography, puts the ordination at the moment [Cyril](/saints/cyril-of-alexandria) was raised to the archiepiscopal throne of Alexandria, in 412. Cyril of Scythopolis, who knows the matter from the local tradition, calls him presbyter and teacher of the Church, enlightened, a theologian, a luminary; and he reports that at the consecration of the church of [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-the-great)'s monastery by the patriarch [Juvenal](/saints/juvenal-of-jerusalem), in 428 or 429, Hesychius was in the patriarch's company, to Euthymius's great joy.

He took an active part in the dogmatic struggles of his time at the side of Cyril of Alexandria, whose anti-Nestorian policy he followed; that much, the page says, may be gathered from the portion of his Ecclesiastical History quoted in the Acts of the Fifth Ecumenical Council of 553. Having become known and admired by all, and serving God in every way, he fell asleep in peace and went up with joy to the Lord. His tomb was still being shown about the year 570 at the eastern gate of Jerusalem, where there was a chapel in his honour, where liturgical gatherings were held and gifts were distributed to the poor.
