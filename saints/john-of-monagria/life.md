# John of Monagria

saint.gr's calendar names him and gives him one sentence. The line for 4 June reads «Άγιος Ιωάννης Οσιομάρτυρας ηγούμενος Μονής Μοναγρίας», Saint John the venerable martyr, abbot of the monastery of Monagria, and the page behind it says this: according to Saint Nikodemos, Saint John was abbot of the monastery of Monagria, and after they had put him inside a sack they threw him into the sea, and so he received the crown of martyrdom.

The page adds one thing more, under the heading of holy relics: a piece of the saint's skull is at the monastery of the Panagia Amasgou in Cyprus. Its couplet plays on the sack and the sea: though a sack should hold him, though the place of the deep should hide him, may the place of heaven receive John. There is no year here, no emperor, no accusation and no account of who drowned him — which for an abbot drowned in a sack is what a reader will want most — and there is no hymn.
