# Nicholas (Raevsky)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him sixth of fifteen on 21 октября ст. ст., in a line of eleven priests, two deacons and two monks of 1937. He was born on 2 March 1885 in the town of Rzhev in Tver province, where his father Vasily Savvich Raevsky was a psalm-reader. His mother died when he was seven; in 1897 his father moved with the children to the village of Bereznikovo in the Tver uyezd to read at the church there, and in March 1899 he died too.

The boy finished the elementary school, a year of the town school and then the church town school, and in 1899 was sent to Bereznikovo as psalm-reader in his dead father's place. In 1906 he was moved to Turginovo, served there a little over half a year, and in November of that year was called up; he did his military service at Warsaw in the 182nd reserve infantry regiment. In 1910 he came back to Rzhev as psalm-reader of the Protection church, a year later went to Staritsa as bursar of the theological school, and in 1913 was ordained deacon. Two years later he was sent to the village of Osulskoye in the Rzhev uyezd, where he served as deacon for fifteen years.

Those were the years in which, as the calendar's life has it, not a page of history but Russia itself was being turned over — the end of the monarchy, the revolution, the civil war, the seizure of church valuables, collectivisation, and with each of them fresh arrests of priests. It never entered his head to leave the service of the Church, and on 20 January 1930 he was ordained priest to the church of Kokosha in the Rzhev district. The persecution grew; churches were closed on one pretext or another or handed to the Renovationists, who passed them on to the state to shut.

In 1936 he was moved to Baranya Gora, which the authorities were also trying to close, and he began to look for somewhere else to serve. In August 1937 the parishioners of Borzyni wrote inviting him; he visited, served there, and wrote back agreeing, saying that the people treasured their church and loved it and would not refuse to petition and to give, and that he greatly feared the parish would be taken by the Renovationists, which came to the same thing as closing it. On 9 October he petitioned the archbishop for the transfer.

By then it was too late. On 8 October the acting head of the Borzyni NKVD office had already written to his district superior that an unknown priest had appeared in the village on St Sergius's day, had stayed with a nun, had taken part in the service and had gathered the believers afterwards; that he had been identified as the priest of Baranya Gora, unregistered under his passport; and that he had told two party members who came to the nun's lodging on the pretext of renting a room that if it were not for the Soviet power they would make better monks and be of greater use. He was arrested the next day and taken to the prison at Osovo.

At his interrogation he was asked about relatives abroad, and had none; about relatives in the Soviet Union, and named his sons, a cousin who kept his house, and a brother Leonid who had been a priest from 1921 to 1932, had been sentenced to five years for he could not say what, and was now somewhere in Eastern Siberia. He denied belonging to any organisation.

He did say that he had called people to the services and taught them how to keep their church from being closed; that after a service at Kashuyevo he had told his hostess over tea that there was no use working in the collective farm because the state would take the grain anyway; and that he had told people to go to church on a feast day rather than into the fields. He admitted being aggrieved in his own soul at the taxes laid on him, which there had been none of under the tsar, but said he had never spoken his grievance aloud, and would not admit counter-revolutionary agitation.

That same day the investigator went looking for villagers who would testify against him, and a shop bookkeeper did. On the strength of such statements the indictment was drawn up on 26 October; the troika condemned him on 1 November and he was shot on 3 November 1937. He was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000. The day page prints troparia and kontakia for other saints it heads and nothing under his name.
