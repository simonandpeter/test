# Sophronius of Jerusalem

Sophronius — called by the name of whole wisdom — was born at Damascus of right-believing and fully wise parents, Plinthos and Myro, citizens of note. From his youth he lived up to his name, loving both the spiritual wisdom and the outward, and keeping his virginity whole from his mother's womb. He learned the outward philosophy first, and was for that called “sophist”, a title held in much honour in those days; then, wanting the spiritual wisdom as well, he went about the monasteries and the hermitages, gathering from the fathers what profited his soul.

Coming to the Holy City of Jerusalem he entered the community of the great Theodosius, and there found the monk John, called Moschus and Eviratus, a priest by rank and practised in both wisdoms. Sophronius joined himself to him as a son to a father and a disciple to a teacher, and followed him in everything to the end of that man's life, going about the monasteries and the deserts with him, visiting the holy fathers and writing their lives. The book the two of them made is the Limonarion, the Garden of Flowers, which the seventh council names.

When the Persians overran Palestine the two left for the parts of great Antioch, and when the same wars came near there, for Egypt and Alexandria, gathering everywhere what they saw and heard of the holy fathers. Sophronius was still untonsured when he came to Alexandria; his teacher's own book calls him “my lord Sophronius the sophist” and tells how an elder in a lavra outside the city blessed his wish to leave the world. He was tonsured afterwards by his teacher.

In a sickness of which he was ready to die he saw virgins dancing before him and saying that Sophronius had come and was now crowned. Recovering, he laboured the more; and when the heresy of Severus was spreading in Egypt the two of them withstood the heretics, being wise and practised in the divine Scripture. For this the most holy patriarch of Alexandria, John the Merciful, loved them as true friends, helpers against the heretics and comforters in sorrow.

The wood of the life-giving Cross, carried off to Persia together with the patriarch Zacharias, was given back after fourteen years on the peace Heraclius made; Zacharias took his throne again and died soon after, and Modestus held it two years after him. Then Sophronius was chosen patriarch of Jerusalem. In his time the heresy of the Monothelites arose, who confess one will and one operation in the person of Christ in his two natures; Cyrus of Alexandria spread it most, and Sergius of Constantinople followed him, and Pyrrhus after him.

Sophronius gathered a council to himself, anathematised that heresy and sent the council's confirmed letters everywhere; the letters were afterwards read at the sixth ecumenical council and received by the fathers as Orthodox writings. He wrote many other words and teachings useful to the Church, and the lives of some of the saints, among them [Mary of Egypt](/saints/mary-of-egypt). For the heresy set out at length the Romanian life sends its reader to the life of Maximus the Confessor on 21 January.

Then came the barbarians upon Syria and Palestine — not Persians now but Mahometans, who took Damascus first and then besieged the city of God for nearly two years. The patriarch shut himself up in the Holy City with the Christians of Palestine. The sermon he preached to the people on the day of Christ's Nativity during the siege survives, in which, like another Jeremiah, he mourned the wasting of the holy places, and most of all that he could not keep the feast at Bethlehem, that place being already in the hands of the Hagarenes.

In the second year of the siege, when the Christians were hard pressed, he sent to Omar, the leader of the Hagarenes, and made a covenant of peace with him, asking first that no force or wrong be done to the Christian faith and to the Church of God; Omar promised to keep it whole, and on that word the gates were opened. He came in on foot in old camel-hair clothes, asking where the temple of Solomon was; and Sophronius, going out to meet him and seeing him in such a guise, said that here would be the abomination of desolation foretold by Daniel.

Omar did not keep the covenant, and began to do the Christians of Jerusalem many wrongs. Seeing it, Sophronius mourned continually and prayed God to take his soul from the earth, that he might not see more of the affliction of the Christians, the abomination of desolation and the dishonouring of the holy places. He was heard, and soon ended his life, passing over from the earthly Jerusalem, which is full of tears, to the Jerusalem above. doxologia gives no year for his death.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr fills in the years before Jerusalem. He was born at Damascus in Syria about the year 580, of devout parents Plinthas and Myro, and is called Damaskenos from his birthplace; while still young he visited the Holy Places and was tonsured a monk at the monastery of abba Theodosius, where he was bound in spirit to John Moschus, then living in ascesis there, and learned much from him. In his company he went to Egypt, where he came into the circle of John the Almsgiver, and to Rome. When John Moschus died in 620 Sophronius carried his relic to Jerusalem, buried it at the monastery of the venerable Theodosius, and went back to Alexandria.

There an incurable disease of the eyes took him, and he was healed at the church of the unmercenaries Cyrus and John at Aboukir, a miracle he wrote into an encomium of those saints. He went afterwards to Constantinople, hoping to win the patriarch Sergius I to his own positions against the Monophysites and to state his disagreement with the plan of union which Cyrus of Phasis, patriarch of Alexandria, was preparing to silence the quarrel between the Orthodox and the Monophysites; he failed, and came back disappointed to Jerusalem. saint.gr records his head at the monastery of the Phaneromeni at Chiliomodi in Corinthia.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells how he gathered his learning and what he did with it. He was born at Damascus of notable parents, and having gathered the wisdom of the world was still not satisfied with it, but went to gather purely spiritual wisdom as well. In the lavra of Saint Theodosius he met the monk John Moschus, whom he took for his teacher, and together with him set out to go round the monasteries and the ascetics of Egypt. His watchword, the entry says, was to learn more spiritual wisdom every day; and everything the two of them learned they wrote down and afterwards published in two books under the name of the Limonar, the Flower Garden.

Later they went to Rome, where Moschus died, leaving Sophronius the charge of carrying him either to Sinai or to the lavra of Theodosius. Sophronius fulfilled his teacher's wish and carried the body to the lavra of Theodosius, and then stayed in Jerusalem, which had just at that time been freed from the Persians. He was present at the return of the Honourable Cross out of Persia, which the emperor Heraclius carried into the Holy City on his own back.

After two patriarchs who had the see before him he became patriarch himself, and governed the church with particular wisdom and zeal for ten years. He rose in defence of Orthodoxy against the Monothelite heresy, which he condemned at his own council in Jerusalem before it was condemned at the Sixth Ecumenical Council; he wrote the life of [Mary of Egypt](/saints/mary-of-egypt), composed the order of the Great Blessing of the Waters, and brought some new hymns and chants into various services.

When the Arab caliph Omar took Jerusalem, Sophronius begged him to spare the Christians, which Omar promised him dissemblingly; and when Omar soon began to plunder and ill-treat the Christians of Jerusalem, Sophronius prayed to God with much crying out to take him from among the living on earth, that he might not see the holy places defiled. God heard his prayer, the entry ends, and took him to Himself into His heavenly courts in the year 644 — six years later than the year this folder already holds from the Greek calendar, and the disagreement is recorded and not resolved.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery tells the first half of his life as the Greek does and the second half with dates the Greek page does not give. He was born at Damascus, it says, was marked from his youth by piety and by a love of learning, and did so well in philosophy that men began to call him the Most Wise; but he looked for a higher wisdom in the monasteries and in talk with the hermits, and came to Jerusalem to the house of Theodosius, where he attached himself to the hieromonk John Moschus as his spiritual son and gave himself to him in obedience. The two travelled the monasteries together writing down the lives and the sayings of those labouring in them, and out of those notes was afterwards made their famous book, the «Лимонарь» or Spiritual Meadow, which the Seventh Ecumenical Council thought highly of.

Fleeing the Persian raids, the page goes on, the two left Palestine for Antioch and from there for Egypt, where Sophronius fell gravely ill and resolved then to become a monk; and he brought his friend's body back to Jerusalem to be buried in the house of Theodosius. In 628 the patriarch Zacharias returned from the Persian captivity, and after his death the throne was held two years by Modestus, and at Modestus's repose Sophronius was chosen patriarch.

**The page gives two different dates for the beginning of that patriarchate and this folder's interval was widened for the end of it.** Its text says he laboured much for the church of Jerusalem as its president «(634 - 644)», and its own line for the day heads him «(638-644)». Both end at 644, where saint.gr had given 638 for his death outright; so the death this folder records is now the interval 638 to 644 and the note says where each bound comes from. The day page prints a kontakion for him and no troparion.
