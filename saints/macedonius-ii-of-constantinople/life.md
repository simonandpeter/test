# Macedonius II of Constantinople

Macedonius the second was a presbyter and sacristan of Hagia Sophia, saint.gr says, brought up in the piety for which he was known by his own uncle, the patriarch Gennadius; and for that piety he was called to govern the ecumenical throne, in place of the patriarch Euphemius, who had been exiled to Euchaita. The page dates his patriarchate 496 to 511, and says the people bore him a great love.

But the heretical emperor Anastasius Dikoros asked Macedonius for a letter — one that Anastasius had given to Macedonius's predecessor Euphemius, and that Euphemius had passed on to him — in which he had assured them in his own hand that he would keep the dogmas of the Church inviolate, an assurance he had brutally trampled on. Macedonius refused to give it up; and Anastasius in his rage brought him down from the throne and exiled him, first to Chalcedon and then to Euchaita.

But because of the raids of the Huns into Pontus he took refuge at Gangra, and there he died, in the year 517. He was shown a true bishop, the page says, a champion of Orthodoxy and of the Church against imperial arbitrariness. Its couplet says that, having stepped out of the corruptible throne, Macedonius hymns the Godhead with the Seraphim and the Thrones.
