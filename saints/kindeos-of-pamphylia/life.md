# Kindeos of Pamphylia

saint.gr says Kindeos came from the town of Talmenia in the Pamphylia of Side, and that he worked with great energy for the spreading of the Christian faith. For that he was denounced to the eparch Stratonikos and condemned to be burned. Above the notice the page sets a distich: «Oυκ άξιόν τι καύσεως όλως έχων, Σωθείς πυρός τέθνηκας ούτω Kινδέε» — having in you nothing at all deserving of burning, you were saved from the fire and yet so died, Kindeos.

On the road to the execution, it says, the man who was carrying the wood for the fire suddenly fell ill and dropped; the officer of the detachment then ordered one of the soldiers to pick the wood up and carry it, but the brave Christian martyr begged them to lay the wood on his own shoulders, and himself carried the materials of his martyrdom. He showed the same courage in the fire: while they had him up on the wood, and before the flames covered him, he was teaching the bystanders with holy enthusiasm and urging them to come over to the religion of Christ.

At the last, the page says, while he was giving up his soul burning in the flames, he won another great victory: the priest of the idols, astonished before such a death, believed in Christ together with his wife — so that it might be shown once more how unconquerable the Christian truth is when it is preached with self-denial. The page gives the single year 290 beside the name of his town, no emperor, and no hymn.
