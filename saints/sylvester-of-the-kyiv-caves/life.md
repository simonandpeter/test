# Sylvester of the Kyiv Caves

The Greek calendar keeps Sylvester the Russian on 2 January, and saint.gr's whole account of him is two sentences. He practised the ascetic life in the Lavra of Kyiv in the twelfth century, and he served as hegumen of the monastery of Vydubychi in Kyiv. He was the man who continued the work of the venerable Nestor the Chronicler, whom the same calendar keeps on 27 October, and he fell asleep in peace.

Nothing else is printed there — no birthplace, no year of birth or death, no account of what he wrote. The page names the Orthodox Church in America's calendar among its sources. What is recorded here is what the Greek calendar records: a monk of the Caves, an abbot at Vydubychi, and the hand that carried on a chronicle.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 2 января old style and holds two readings of him, which agree. He lived and struggled in the Kyiv Caves monastery in the twelfth century; he was hegumen of the monastery of St Michael at Vydubychi; he carried on the chronicle of the venerable [Nestor](/saints/nestor-the-chronicler); and nine lives of the saints of the Caves were written by him. He was buried in the Near, or Antonian, caves.

The second reading adds one thing the first does not. In the service to the fathers who rest in the Near Caves, the ninth ode of the canon calls Sylvester blessed and says he has the wonderful gift of driving off the assaults of the demons. His memory, both pages note, is kept also on 28 сентября and on the second Sunday of Great Lent, which are the days of the fathers of the Near Caves together.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 2 Ιανουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/01/02/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/22/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026; and after the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 2 января ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260102.html) and its two lives, [one](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life6838.htm) and [the other](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life139.htm), read 3 October 2026.*
