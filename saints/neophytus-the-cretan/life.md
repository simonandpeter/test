# Neophytus the Cretan

Neophytus was a Cretan — the Romanian calendar and his troparion both call him so, the troparion naming him "the shoot of Crete" — and doxologia's account of him begins with his being called into Wallachia by the prince Constantin Mavrocordat, in his third reign, to be the teacher of his children. In January 1737 he was consecrated titular metropolitan of Myra in Lycia, and a year later, "by the counsel of all the honourable boyars and of all the church clergy", he was chosen metropolitan of Wallachia.

Though he was of another people and another tongue than his flock, he worked without rest for the spiritual and material good of the Romanian people, and the page sets him beside his predecessor [Antim Ivireanul](/saints/antim-ivireanul). He supported the full introduction of Romanian into the services, re-establishing the press of the Metropolia at Bucharest, where he published fourteen service books in several editions and other books of instruction, most of them in the people's language. Wishing to help the Orthodox under Ottoman rule, he assisted Patriarch Sylvester of Antioch in printing an Arabic Psalter at Bucharest in 1747.

He founded and endowed the library of the Metropolia, built it a proper house and left it his own rich collection of printed books and manuscripts by will. He supported the hesychast revival begun by Saint Basil of Poiana Mărului, calling him to set out his teaching before the patriarchs Matthew of Alexandria, Sylvester of Antioch and Parthenios of Jerusalem, then at Bucharest, who bore witness to its Orthodoxy. In 1739 he addressed a pastoral letter to priests, monks and all the faithful at the beginning of Great Lent, urging more frequent confession and communion with the confessor's blessing. In 1746 and 1747 he made two pastoral journeys through the counties of his eparchy and part of that of Râmnic, visiting churches and monasteries, guiding priests and people, serving, consecrating and ordaining.

He put an end to the reckoning of years "from the creation of the world" and settled the counting of years from the Nativity of Christ, following the calculation of the monk [Dionysius Exiguus](/saints/dionysius-exiguus). He was the chief supporter of the abolition of *rumânie*, the serfdom that bound the peasants to the boyars on whose estates they lived, and was the first to free all the peasants on the estates of the Metropolia. His pastoral zeal showed again in his support of the people against the Phanariot prince Matei Ghica: the chroniclers say he put himself at their head and went to the sultan's envoy to complain of the prince's abuses.

Angered by this, Matei Ghica secretly ordered him poisoned, and soon after, on 16 June 1753, Metropolitan Neophytus passed to the Lord as a hierarch and martyr, after nearly fifteen years of shepherding, and was buried beside the metropolitan cathedral in Bucharest.
