# Adrian of Monza

Most of what is known of him, saint.gr says, rests on one chronicle: an account of the beginning of the founding of the monastery of the Annunciation on the river Monza, in the region of Kostroma, together with the wonderful events bound up with the two founders, Abraham and Therapon. It was drawn up about the middle of the seventeenth century by a monk of the house. The historian Klyuchevsky gives 1644 as the date of its composition and holds that the writer was the monastery's third hegumen, who remained anonymous.

According to that chronicle he lived at the end of the sixteenth century and the beginning of the seventeenth, in Russia. He was born at Kostroma and baptised Amos. While still young, in the course of an illness, he had a dream that opened his life to him: he saw a church standing alone between two rivers, and heard a voice saying to him, «Αυτός είναι ο τόπος σου» — this is your place. Healed, he slipped away in secret from his parents, who wanted to marry him off.

He came first to the monastery of Tolga at Yaroslavl, which had been founded, the page says, by the venerable Gennadius and was dedicated to Christ the Saviour; there he was clothed as a monk and given the name Adrian. After some years of ascetic labour he went to the region of Vologda, to the monastery of the Saviour near Lake Kuben, and then to the hermitage founded by [Paul of Obnora](/saints/paul-of-obnora), the disciple of Sergius, in the forests of Komel. There he made a deep friendship with the elder Pachnoutios, to whom he told his wish to find again the church he had seen in the dream; and to Pachnoutios in his sleep an unknown monk showed the place where Adrian would find it — a desert between the banks of the river Monza, in the Kostroma country.

The two wished to live the ascetic life together in that remote place, where there was an abandoned church dedicated to the Annunciation of the Theotokos. But in 1595 Pachnoutios was appointed by the tsar's decree archimandrite of the Chudov monastery at Moscow, the monastery of the Miracle of the Archangel Michael; and in the same year Adrian reached the lonely, quiet place with three other monks. The dangers and difficulties of the spring floods forced him to move the church and the monastic community to a safer site, and the unknown monk appeared to him again to show him where to build it.

A little later there came to them an elder named Therapon, who had been living in those parts as a hermit, in isolation and harsh asceticism; and Adrian recognised in his face the monk who had appeared to him so mysteriously. Therapon lived in the community under the hegumen Adrian for rather more than two years, until his death. Adrian himself, having lived in a manner pleasing to God, reposed in peace in 1619.
