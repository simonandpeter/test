# Elias (Nikolayevich)

saint.gr says the hieromartyr Elias, whose patronymic it gives as Nikolayevich, was born in some village of Moscow in the nineteenth century. He studied at the Theological Academy of Moscow and married a devout woman, Eugenia; then he was ordained priest, and served in the small church of a poorhouse and in the parish of Saint Nicholas Tolmachev in Moscow, before the October revolution of 1917 broke out. He was a most reverent priest, the page says, and his church was a beacon of spiritual light to many of the faithful; he was a married man, but he lived an ascetic's life.

It was in 1932 that the secret Soviet police arrested him and put him in prison, and they exiled him to the region of the river Krasnaya Vishera. His wife Eugenia passed the whole night in prayer and tears, but towards morning she fell asleep, and then she saw the Mother of God in her sleep, who told her not to be afraid.

Two years later she visited him in the place of his exile and brought him a Gospel and a small phial of holy water. The guards snatched the Gospel away at once; and when they asked her what was in the phial, she answered that for them it was plain water, but for her and her husband it was something holy — their medicine. He looked, the page says, as though he had been tortured. They would not let him serve the liturgy, and that grieved him beyond imagining.

He began then to tell her his martyrdom. When they were moving him and many others to the place of exile they were forced to walk over snow that had melted at the surface; the thin layer of ice broke under their feet and the convicts sank into the snow to the waist. Soaked to the bone, having eaten and drunk nothing all day, they were made to pass the night inside a hut, and the exhausted men fell on the floor at once and slept like dead men. Only he stayed awake; and in the deep of midnight a cry was heard out of the depths of his heart: Lord, why have you forsaken me? I served you so faithfully. My whole life I dedicated to you. How many times did I read the Akathist hymn and the canons. With how much reverence did I serve in the Church. Why, Lord, have you forsaken me, and why do I suffer so much? Most holy Mother of God, holy hierarch Nicholas, holy father Seraphim, all you saints of God — after all my prayers to you, why am I tormented so?

Suddenly, the page says, a divine visitation like a flame touched his aching soul and flooded it with a comfort not of this world. The light of faith secretly lit his heart and kindled in him a love for Christ inexpressible and unconquerable, of which, as the Apostle Paul says, it is not lawful for a man to speak. When day broke he was a new man, born again as though he had been baptised in fire. As he said goodbye to Eugenia he told her: you know, my heart burns for Christ. I think I came here to understand that there is nothing better at all, nothing more wonderful, than he is. I should like to die for him.

When she reached Moscow again she learned that a fire had broken out in the concentration camp, and that he had been consumed by it, together with eleven other Christians. The page gives the year of his death only in its heading, as 1934.
