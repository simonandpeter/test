# Zephaniah

Zephaniah was from mount Sabaratha, of the tribe of Simeon; his father was called Cushi and his uncle Gedaliah, and his forebears were Amariah and Hezekiah. He was, as his name is interpreted, a seer and a knower of the mysteries of God: keeping his mind always pure and occupied with the thought of God, he was granted divine revelations and knew many of God's mysteries that were to be shown in the last days, and spoke beforehand of their fulfilment.

He prophesied of the pulling down and laying waste of Jerusalem, of the scattering of the Jews and of the coming of Christ, and after that of the turning of the nations to Christ. Then he died and was buried in his own house, awaiting the common resurrection of all. He was small-bearded and round of face.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr sets him in a century and a reign: he lived in the seventh century before Christ, under king Josiah. He is the ninth of the so-called minor prophets, and he came of the tribe of Simeon or, by other accounts, of Levi — the page gives both and settles neither. His name, it says, means God hides, that is, guards and protects.

His prophetic book is divided into three short chapters. In the first he threatens the Jews who had turned aside from the Lord into idolatry; in the second he foretells the destruction of Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod, Damascus, Ethiopia, Assyria and other countries besides; in the third he reproves Jerusalem for her corruption, and yet calls her illustrious, as the cradle of the redemption of the human race. At the end he greets with exultation the coming appearance of the Lord in Zion. The page quotes him on the lawless — «Εξάρω τους ανόμους από προσώπου της γης, λέγει Κύριος» — and says that the prophet Zephaniah died in peace and was buried in the place of his fathers.
