# Macarius the Egyptian

Macarius was of Egypt, from a village called Ptinapar. His parents bore the names of the oldest of the saints: his father Abraham, who held the rank of priest, and his mother Sarah. They were childless and had withdrawn from the life of marriage while remaining one in spirit, and they adorned their days with abstinence and fasting, with prayer and vigil, with almsgiving and the receiving of strangers, until a sudden inroad of barbarians into Egypt stripped them of their goods and they thought to leave the country.

The patriarch Abraham appeared to the father in the night, doxologia.ro's life says, and counselled him not to leave Egypt altogether but only to move to the village of Ptinapar, and not to keep apart from his wife, since God meant to bless them with fruit as he had blessed Abraham himself with a son in old age. The village was not far from the desert of Nitria, and the life reads the move as God's own arrangement, so that the son who was to be born might the more easily come to love the desert. Later, when the father lay ill and expecting death, an angel told him that his wife would bear a son named for blessedness, who would be a dwelling of the Holy Spirit and live on earth as an angel; and he rose up well. So Sarah bore a son and they called him Macarius, which is interpreted *blessed*.

When he had grown and learned the divine books his parents pressed him to marry, and, though he wished only that unstained bride which is the blessed life, he gave way to their will. A journey with neighbours to Mount Nitria, where the saltpetre is dug that gives the mountain its name, showed him the desert. Fleeing the honour men began to give him, he went first to Nitria and then to [Anthony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great), whose close disciple he became and with whom he stayed a long while, learning the perfect life and following his father in everything.

At forty he received from God the gift of healings and of prophecy and authority over unclean spirits, was raised to the priesthood, and became the guide of the fathers living in Scetis. The life fills its pages with his sayings and his miracles, and ends with the account Serapion, who wrote his life, gives of his death: two came to him, Anthony the guide of the desert and Pachomius the teacher of the monastic life, to tell him that in nine days he would pass to the life to come; and as his soul went up through the heavenly gates the demons wept and cried out that he had escaped them, and he answered aloud that he had escaped their devices indeed, walled about by the power of his Christ.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, with Macarius of Alexandria, and saint.gr fills in what the Romanian page left out. He was born in 301 in a village of Upper Egypt and lived in the years of Theodosius the Great; at thirty he withdrew to the desert of Nitria and into Syria, where he remained sixty whole years, and because he advanced in the virtues despite his youth he was called «παιδαριογέρων», the boy-elder. In the desert he came to know [Anthony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great) and became his disciple. At forty he was ordained presbyter, and was counted worthy of the gifts of healing the sick and of prophecy. He dug out with his own hands an underground gallery some hundred metres long, running from his cell to a cave he widened at its end, so that when crowds came and troubled him he could go down unnoticed and hide where none could find him. In his old age he was exiled to an islet of the Nile by Lucius, the Arian bishop of Alexandria, and he fell asleep in peace at the age of ninety in the year 391.
