# Theodosius of Antioch

Theodosius came from Antioch in Syria and was the child of rich and distinguished parents, but a warm godliness and a holy longing to take the road of the monastic commonwealth moved him, and he left for Cilicia. As soon as he found, in the mountains of the city of Rhosus, a wooded ravine, he built there a very small hut and set up his dwelling in it.

In that place he lived alone and carried on his spiritual and ascetic contests with fasts and vigils. Sometimes he would go down to the neighbouring villages, looking for souls to whom he could bring the comfort of faith and hope.

When the Saracens overran those parts he came back to Antioch, where he built a small hut and lived in it with other brothers. His synaxarion, the page says, records that he slept on the ground, wore a garment of hair, and carried heavy irons on his neck, his waist and his hands. God, who saw his ascetic struggle and heard his prayers, gave him the gift of working wonders. He fell asleep in holiness and in peace.

The page gives him no year and no century, and prints no hymn. Over him it sets a couplet: having walked the narrow path, Theodosius treads the broad path of Eden.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 11 января ст. ст. and tells the same story with other details. It says that he left his noble parents' wealthy house while still young and settled in a small cell on the shore of the gulf of Issus, near the city of Rhosus; that he wore a hair shirt and heavy iron fetters, slept on the bare ground and let his hair grow until it covered his feet; and that he worked at a vegetable garden and at plaiting ropes. In his own country he founded a monastery, which it names Skupel, teaching the monks to love bodily labour as well as the spiritual struggle, and he was especially careful of travellers.

Christians and pagans alike knew of him, the Russian account says, and seafarers in danger would call for the help of the God «of Theodosius»; it happened that the waves were stilled at his name alone, and robbers feared him and honoured him and asked his prayers. Fleeing the fame men gave him, he went to live near the village of Marathon and founded the Marathonian cenobium there, and in it he ended his days in peace, about the year 412.
