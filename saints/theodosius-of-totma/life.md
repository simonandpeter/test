# Theodosius of Totma

Theodosius Sumorin of Totma was born in Vologda around 1530 and brought up
from youth in Christian piety and the fear of God. At his parents’
insistence he married, but family life did not draw him from God — he went
diligently to church and prayed much at home, especially at night — and
after the death of his parents and his wife he withdrew to the Prilutsky
monastery near Vologda, where he carried water, chopped wood, ground flour
and baked bread. Sent by the igumen to Totma to oversee the monastery’s
salt-works, he asked leave of Tsar Ivan Vasilievich and the blessing of
Archbishop Nikandr to found a monastery near the town, and was appointed
superior of the new house, which a charter of 1554 freed from every tax;
he also restored the Ephrem hermitage at Totma and set up a brotherhood in
it. Superior of two monasteries, he went on living as an ascetic, wearing
down his body with chains and a hair shirt and, under the schema-monk’s
cowl, an iron cap; and loving spiritual reading he bought many books for
the monastery. He died in 1568 and was buried in the monastery he had
founded, where wonders were worked at his tomb; on 2 September 1796, during
the rebuilding of the Ascension church, his relics were found incorrupt,
and they were glorified on 28 January 1798, the day of his death. The
Russian calendar keeps on 2 September (Julian) the finding of his relics.
