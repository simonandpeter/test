# Basilissa of Rome, with Anastasia

Basilissa and [Anastasia](/saints/anastasia-of-rome-15-april) were of the great city of Rome, well-born and rich, and disciples of the holy Apostles. When the Apostles had been put to death the two women came by night, gathered up their holy relics and buried them; they were denounced for it to the emperor Nero, who put them in prison first of all.

When they were brought out and said that they remained in the confession of Christ, they were hung up, and their breasts and their hands and their feet and their tongues were cut off; last of all their heads were struck off with the sword.

That is the whole of the notice, which the Romanian calendar prints twice over, once under each woman's name. It carries no date but the name of Nero and no troparion. Two other women of this name are kept in this corpus — Basilissa of Nicomedia, the child martyred under Diocletian, and Basilissa of Sirmium — and neither is this Roman woman.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and fills the notice out. saint.gr says the two women were rich and of Rome, under Nero, whose reign it dates 54 to 68, and that they were an exception among their own: they escaped the corruption and the estrangement that was flooding the upper ranks of Roman society, and lived in purity and virginity. They received the faith of Christ while they were young, and by distributing what they had became the support of the Christians of Rome. It is saint.gr, and not the Romanian notice, that names the relics they buried as those of the chief Apostles Peter and Paul. Soon afterwards they were arrested on the charge of belonging to a band of Christian saboteurs; they confessed openly that they were Christians, and after various tortures were beheaded at the last, gaining the bright crown of martyrdom.

Its couplet has the two of them as ewe-lambs: «Ἀμνοῦ Θεοῦ σφάττουσιν ἀμνάδας δύο, Ἀναστασίαν καὶ Βασίλισσαν ἅμα» — they slay two ewe-lambs of the Lamb of God, Anastasia and Basilissa together. The page adds that her relics are kept in the Roman Catholic basilica of Santa Maria della Pace at Rome, and it prints no hymn for her.
