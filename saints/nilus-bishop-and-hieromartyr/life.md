# Nilus

[Peleus](/saints/peleus-bishop-and-hieromartyr) and Nilus came from Egypt. They presented themselves boldly as Christians, with Patermuthius, Elias and a hundred other Christians, before the pagan king; they suffered horrible torments, their right eyes were put out, and at last they were burned alive — what great epic, the verse asks, for Peleus, who went in with Nilus into the midst of the fire? The Greek calendar keeps the bishops Peleus and Nilus on 17 September, and in separate entries the glorious Patermuthius and Elias, martyred by fire with them, and the hundred Egyptian martyrs beheaded with one eagerness.

The Russian calendar keeps them on 17 September old style with the presbyter [Zeno](/saints/zeno-companion-of-peleus) and others, 151 in all, about 310. Its life says they suffered under the emperor Galerius Maximian (305–311): most of them were Egyptians and some Palestinians, and Firmilian, the governor of Palestine, seized 156 Christians, whose eyes were put out and the sinews of their legs cut, and who were tortured in other ways; a hundred were beheaded and the rest burned.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps the 156 martyrs, all of them Egyptians, who suffered for Christ in 310, some by the sword and some by fire; among them were the two old bishops Peleus and Nilus, the priest Zeno and the two glorious men Patermuthius and Elias. It adds that the bishop Silvanus suffered with them, and the famous blind elder John, who knew the Scriptures by heart and recited them at the gatherings of the Christians.
