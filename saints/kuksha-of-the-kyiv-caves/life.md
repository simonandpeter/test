# Kuksha of the Kyiv Caves

The hieromartyr Kuksha and Pimen the Faster died after 1114. Simon, bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal, wrote of Kuksha in his letter to Polycarp, archimandrite of the Caves: how can I worthily glorify the holy men who were in the holy monastery of the Caves, where pagans were baptised and became monks and Jews received the holy faith? — but I cannot keep silent about the blessed hieromartyr and monk of this monastery Kuksha, of whom all know that he drove out demons, baptised the Vyatichi, brought down rain, dried up a lake, worked many other wonders and after many torments was killed with his disciple Nikon. Pimen the Faster foresaw his death: standing in the midst of the great church of the Caves he said aloud, our brother Kuksha was killed at dawn — and having said it, the Prologue adds, himself expired, in 1113. The Vyatichi among whom Kuksha preached and died lived along the Oka, in the lands of Oryol and Kaluga, pagans whose foul customs the chronicler Nestor deplored, living so “even to the present day”, strangers to the law of God; Kuksha preached among them in the days of Theoctistus, bishop of Chernigov (1113–1123), and was buried, like Pimen the Faster, in the Near Caves, whose synaxis of venerable fathers the Russian calendar keeps on 28 September. The Russian calendar keeps the three on 27 August (Julian), with a troparion and kontakion; the Serbian calendar keeps Kuksha and Pimen the Faster from the Prologue.
