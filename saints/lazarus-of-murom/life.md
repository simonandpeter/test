# Lazarus of Murom

saint.gr keeps two ascetics of the Murmansk country together on this day, and of the two it is Lazarus whose life it tells. In his own autobiography, the page says, he calls himself a Roman, born at Constantinople about the year 1286. In his own city he became a monk under the guidance of the elder Athanasius Diskotes, the founder of many monasteries; eight years later he came under the guidance of the bishop Basil of Cappadocia. In 1343 that bishop, wishing to strengthen the Russian Church, sent Lazarus north as an iconographer, with other monks and with gifts, to St Basil the archbishop of Novgorod.

He made a copy of the icon of the Sophia, the Wisdom of God, for the bishopric of Cappadocia, and drew up a report on the churches and monasteries of Novgorod. When the two men met, the hierarch of Novgorod bowed to the monk and blessed him to stay in a monastery he had built; and Lazarus served him faithfully for ten years, until his death in 1352. Grieved at losing both his guides — letters had already brought him word of the bishop Basil of Cappadocia's death — he thought of going home. But the hierarch Basil appeared to him in a dream and told him to go north, towards the sea, to the island in Lake Onega; and a little later his first guide ordered him in a dream to the same place, and to found a monastery there.

He could not reach the island at once. For a long time the man who owned it, a Novgorod merchant named Ivan, would not let him come; the monk prayed hard to the Mother of God and to St John the Forerunner and wept at St Basil's tomb, until at last Basil appeared to the merchant in a dream and ordered him to give the island «to our friend Lazarus», so that the Mother of God might be honoured there.

He came to the blessed place alone and set up a cross, a hut and a little chapel. The Lop and the Chud who lived on the island soon heard of him and made him suffer a great deal: they burnt his hut, did what damage they could, beat him, drove him off the island and hunted him to kill him. But God and the Queen of Heaven guarded him. On the place where the hut had burnt, the icon of the Dormition of the Mother of God appeared to him again, untouched by the fire — the very icon with which he had been blessed when he became a monk — and out of it a command was heard: the unbelieving people will become believing, and there will be one Church and one flock of Christ; found in this place a church of the Dormition of the Mother of God.

A while later the chief of the Lop came to him and begged him to heal a child born blind. Lazarus healed the blind child after prayer and a sprinkling with holy water; and then the evil men left the island, and the healed child's father became a monk, and all his sons were baptised. From that time people began to come to him from far off, and he baptised them and tonsured them; even his own countrymen came to him from Constantinople, the monks Eleazar, Eumenius and Nazarius, whose day the page gives as 4 June and who would later found the monastery of the Forerunner in the Olonets country.

Visiting Novgorod, he received from the bishop [Moses](/saints/moses-of-novgorod) the blessing to build a monastery, together with an antimension and some church vessels. A church was raised in honour of the Dormition of the Mother of God, the first in all that coastal country, and a church of the Raising of Lazarus, and even a wooden church of St John the Forerunner with a refectory. The Dormition monastery of Murom was built up and strengthened by the zeal of its abbot to the end of his life. The time of his death was revealed to him in a dream by his faithful patron, St Basil of Novgorod; having chosen a worthy successor, the Athonite elder Theodosius, and having received the Holy Mysteries, he fell asleep in the Lord on 8 March 1391, at the age of a hundred and five, and was buried in a chapel beside the church of the Dormition. His life was written by the elder Theodosius out of Lazarus's own telling. The other man the day keeps with him, [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-murom), was abbot of this same monastery a lifetime later.

The Russian calendar gives him the fullest life of the four and the one that says how a Greek of Constantinople came to Lake Onega. He took the monastic habit in his own city, at the Vysokogorsky monastery, from the elder Athanasius Disketos, a builder of many houses, and then spent eight years under the direction of a bishop Basil of Caesarea. In 1343 that bishop, wishing to be strengthened by the blessing of the Russian Church, sent him as a man who knew iconography, with icons and gifts, to [Basil of Novgorod](/saints/basil-of-novgorod): he was to make for the diocese of Caesarea a copy of the great Novgorod holy thing, the icon of Sophia the Wisdom of God, and to write a description of the churches and monasteries of Novgorod. The hierarch of Novgorod met him by bowing to the ground before his guest, and blessed him to stay in the monastery he had built. Lazarus served him faithfully for ten years, and when the archpastor died in 1352 he «с своими руками одел в готовые одеяния святое тело и много пролил слез» — clothed the holy body with his own hands and shed many tears.

Grieved to have lost both his teachers, for he had learnt of the bishop of Caesarea's death earlier by letter, he thought of going home; but the hierarch of Novgorod appeared to him in a dream and told him to go north to the sea, to the island of Much on Lake Onega, and soon his first teacher, the bishop of Caesarea, commanded him in a dream to go to the same place and found a house there. He could not reach those lands at once: the island's owner, a Novgorod governor named Ivan, would not give it up for a long time, and gave way only when the hierarch Basil himself appeared to him in a terrible aspect and ordered the island given to «другу нашему Лазарю», our friend Lazarus, because there the name of the Mother of God would be glorified.

He came to the blessed place alone and set up a cross, a hut and a small chapel. The Lapps and the Chud who lived about the lake soon heard of him, and he had much to suffer from them: they burnt his hut, harmed him as they could, beat him more than once, drove him off the island and hunted him to kill him. On the site of the burnt hut the icon of the Dormition with which he had been blessed at his tonsure appeared to him, saved from the fire by a wonder, and a voice of command came from it: the unbelieving people shall become believing, there shall be one Church and one flock of Christ; set a church on this place in honour of the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God. Another time he saw the same place blessed by a woman shining with gold, to whom comely men were bowing. Soon the elder of the Lapps came to him and asked him to heal a child born blind, saying that then they would leave the island as his servants commanded — and Lazarus understood that the servants were the angels of heaven, and gave thanks. He healed the blind child by praying over it and sprinkling it with holy water; after that the evil people left the island, the healed child's father became a monk in the end, and all his sons were baptised.

From then on many came to him from far places, and he baptised them and gave them the habit; three monks of his own country came from Constantinople, who were afterwards the founders of the monastery of the Forerunner in the Olonets country. Having been to Novgorod he received from the bishop [Moses of Novgorod](/saints/moses-of-novgorod) the blessing to found a monastery, and an antimension and holy vessels, and there were built a church of the Dormition, the first in the whole of the Pomor country, a church of the Raising of Lazarus, and a wooden church of St John the Forerunner with a refectory. He ordered and strengthened the house into deep old age. The time of his end was shown him in a vision by his faithful protector the hierarch of Novgorod; having chosen with the brotherhood a worthy successor, an Athonite elder, and communicated of the holy and life-giving Mysteries and blessed them all, he departed to the Lord on 8 March 1391 at the age of 105, and was buried in a chapel beside the church of the Dormition. His life was written down by that same elder from the saint's own words.
