# Lazarus of Murom

saint.gr keeps two ascetics of the Murmansk country together on this day, and of the two it is Lazarus whose life it tells. In his own autobiography, the page says, he calls himself a Roman, born at Constantinople about the year 1286. In his own city he became a monk under the guidance of the elder Athanasius Diskotes, the founder of many monasteries; eight years later he came under the guidance of the bishop Basil of Cappadocia. In 1343 that bishop, wishing to strengthen the Russian Church, sent Lazarus north as an iconographer, with other monks and with gifts, to St Basil the archbishop of Novgorod.

He made a copy of the icon of the Sophia, the Wisdom of God, for the bishopric of Cappadocia, and drew up a report on the churches and monasteries of Novgorod. When the two men met, the hierarch of Novgorod bowed to the monk and blessed him to stay in a monastery he had built; and Lazarus served him faithfully for ten years, until his death in 1352. Grieved at losing both his guides — letters had already brought him word of the bishop Basil of Cappadocia's death — he thought of going home. But the hierarch Basil appeared to him in a dream and told him to go north, towards the sea, to the island in Lake Onega; and a little later his first guide ordered him in a dream to the same place, and to found a monastery there.

He could not reach the island at once. For a long time the man who owned it, a Novgorod merchant named Ivan, would not let him come; the monk prayed hard to the Mother of God and to St John the Forerunner and wept at St Basil's tomb, until at last Basil appeared to the merchant in a dream and ordered him to give the island «to our friend Lazarus», so that the Mother of God might be honoured there.

He came to the blessed place alone and set up a cross, a hut and a little chapel. The Lop and the Chud who lived on the island soon heard of him and made him suffer a great deal: they burnt his hut, did what damage they could, beat him, drove him off the island and hunted him to kill him. But God and the Queen of Heaven guarded him. On the place where the hut had burnt, the icon of the Dormition of the Mother of God appeared to him again, untouched by the fire — the very icon with which he had been blessed when he became a monk — and out of it a command was heard: the unbelieving people will become believing, and there will be one Church and one flock of Christ; found in this place a church of the Dormition of the Mother of God.

A while later the chief of the Lop came to him and begged him to heal a child born blind. Lazarus healed the blind child after prayer and a sprinkling with holy water; and then the evil men left the island, and the healed child's father became a monk, and all his sons were baptised. From that time people began to come to him from far off, and he baptised them and tonsured them; even his own countrymen came to him from Constantinople, the monks Eleazar, Eumenius and Nazarius, whose day the page gives as 4 June and who would later found the monastery of the Forerunner in the Olonets country.

Visiting Novgorod, he received from the bishop [Moses](/saints/moses-of-novgorod) the blessing to build a monastery, together with an antimension and some church vessels. A church was raised in honour of the Dormition of the Mother of God, the first in all that coastal country, and a church of the Raising of Lazarus, and even a wooden church of St John the Forerunner with a refectory. The Dormition monastery of Murom was built up and strengthened by the zeal of its abbot to the end of his life. The time of his death was revealed to him in a dream by his faithful patron, St Basil of Novgorod; having chosen a worthy successor, the Athonite elder Theodosius, and having received the Holy Mysteries, he fell asleep in the Lord on 8 March 1391, at the age of a hundred and five, and was buried in a chapel beside the church of the Dormition. His life was written by the elder Theodosius out of Lazarus's own telling. The other man the day keeps with him, [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-murom), was abbot of this same monastery a lifetime later.
