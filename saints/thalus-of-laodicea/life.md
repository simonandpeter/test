# Thalus of Laodicea

In the days of the emperors Diocletian and Maximian, when Asclepius was governor in Laodicea, a great persecution was raised against the Christians. The holy martyrs Trophimus and Thalus were taken and stoned for a long while, but God kept them and they were untouched. Seeing it, the magistrate and those who were with him were put to shame, and let them live a while without torment.

Being denounced again, they were brought to trial; and there, confessing boldly before everyone that Christ is the true God, mocking the idols of the pagans and rebuking the tyrants, they moved them to anger. For this they were stripped altogether, hung upon wood, and their bodies deeply torn. But the saints prayed to God on the one hand and reviled the Greek gods on the other, and so filled the magistrate with rage that he resolved to make an end of them, and ordered them crucified.

As they were led to the place of crucifixion a great crowd of people followed them; and while they were being crucified they prayed and spoke to the people what builds up the soul. The crowd pressed to touch the bodies of the saints: some gathering the drops of their blood, some a cloth, some a piece of metal that was upon them, and some one thing and some another. Blessing them and wishing them well, the martyrs gave up their holy souls to God.

Some of the faithful took their remains, anointed them with myrrh, wrapped them in linen and laid them in the church. The wife of Asclepius came there too, and smelling the balsam that came out of the martyrs' reliquary, spread over it a covering of very great price. A while after this, Zosimus and Artemius, two devout and believing men of the same city as the saints, took the reliquary with the holy relics and carried it to their own city, Stratonice, about a mile away, and laid it in a tomb cut in a rock.

doxologia's calendar for 11 martie prints Thalus and [Trophimus](/saints/trophimus-of-laodicea) as two lines of its own, and the page behind each of them carries this one life; this folder is Thalus. The life names the emperors Diocletian and Maximian and the governor Asclepius, but gives no year, and doxologia prints no hymn for them.

The Greek calendar keeps the two of them together on the same day and says more of the martyrdom. saint.gr puts it under Diocletian and makes them natives of Stratonike, or Stratonikeia, in the province of Caria in Asia Minor. In the persecution they were arrested for confessing Christ and were stoned for a good while, and by the working of divine grace remained unhurt; seeing it, Asklepios, the governor of Laodicea, let them go.

Later they were taken again and pressed to deny Christ, and confessed their faith with still more courage, mocking the idols and their patrons in front of everybody. That angered the tyrant, who ordered them bound naked to a wooden frame and their flesh torn, and at the last they were martyred by death on a cross. Devout Christians took their holy relics and buried them in a holy place, and the wife of the governor Asklepios came and sprinkled the case with myrrh and spread a precious sheet over it; and two God-fearing countrymen of theirs, Zosimos and Artemios, carried the case home to Stratonike and treasured it a mile outside the city, at the place called Latomeia.
