# Theophanes of Antioch

Theophanes, a righteous man who came from Antioch, was the son of parents without faith and without piety; yet in bringing him up they could not drive out the child's early longing for Christ and for the faith. When he was fifteen he married, obeying his parents' firm urging, and was married three years, until death took the young woman's life. Being hindered by nothing else now, Theophanes hurried to the Church of Christ, and there received the mystery of holy baptism. Then he built himself a narrow cell near Antioch, and this unworldly man shut himself up in his poor shelter, cleansed himself of the passions — those desires and impulses that master the soul — and strove by every means to reach in himself the height of perfect virtue.

In time he learned that a harlot named [Pansemne](/saints/pansemne-of-antioch) lived in the city, who was dragging many young men into the mire. Wounded in his heart both by the woman's misfortune and by that of the men who admired her, Theophanes began to pray from the depth of his soul; and wishing to give himself more perfectly to God, he begged the Lord to show him the divine will in this matter that was wearing at him. Being counted worthy of divine enlightenment, he submitted to the divine plan, which wisely led to Pansemne's redemption.

So he took off his rough hair garment and clothed himself in a costly robe. Then he went to visit his father, and revealing his intention of taking a second wife he was gladly given ten gold coins. Theophanes, whose name means "the seeing of God", went to the house of the well-known harlot in order to change her life. When he came into the house of ill fame he was invited to eat and to drink with the woman; and after they had talked pleasantly a while he asked her how long she had followed that indecent trade. Not feeling herself insulted in the least, she said twelve years, and added that of all her lovers Theophanes was by far the handsomest.

Unwounded by the compliment, seeking only to keep the purity of his soul and of his body, he went on: "I wish to live with thee in an honourable marriage." Pansemne, honoured indeed by so wonderful an offer, thought it a true proof of respect towards her, a shameless prostitute. When she had agreed, Theophanes gave her the earnest of ten gold coins and told her to hurry the preparations for the wedding. Then he left the city and went to his cell, and made his preparations for her too, building her a cell not far from his own. Not long after, he came back to the city to visit her; but this time he set her a condition: they could live together only if she embraced the faith in Christ and became a Christian. Hearing it, Pansemne at first thought it hard to fulfil, and decided to think about it for a time.

What followed is told in the life of Pansemne, who was baptised and went to the cell he had built for her. She lived only forty months after her baptism, and was taken to her Lord on the very day on which the righteous and wonder-working Theophanes was called to the company of his fathers. The page gives no year, no place of his death and no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 June, and saint.gr's account gives no year at all. It says he came from Antioch and was born of idolatrous parents; that at the age of only fifteen he married, and that after three years of married life his wife died. He then withdrew to a small cell, having first been catechised and baptised a Christian, and there lived a holy life. When he learnt that a certain harlot named Pansemne was drawing many into the death of sin, he took off his poor clothes, dressed himself in bright apparel and went to his father, telling him that he wished to marry again and needed money for it; his father was glad and gave him gold coins enough for the wedding. As soon as Theophanes had the gold he went and found the beautiful Pansemne, and when they had eaten and drunk together he asked her whether she would be his wife — on the one condition that she become a Christian. The page adds the two verses of the synaxarion for them: that having practised asceticism together, Theophanes and Pansemne share alike in the vision of God.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the Julian 10 June, the same menologion day as the Romanian and the Greek, and its line gives him something neither of those gave: a year. «Прп. Феофана Антиохийского (369).» Where the Romanian and Greek pages left this folder with no interval at all, that line puts his death in 369. It is recorded here and not in the folder's dates, because a year taken off a day line alone, with the life page behind that line left unread, is not a year this corpus asserts; the reader who opens that page can settle it.
