# Cyril of Turov

Cyril was born of rich parents in the third decade of the twelfth century in the town of Turov, on the river Pripyat. Very early, and with a fervent zeal, he studied the sacred books of the Church and the Fathers, and he learned the Greek language as well. When he came of age he refused his father's inheritance and left the things of the world, and he was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Saints Boris and Gleb at Turov.

He practised fasting and unceasing prayer very hard, saint.gr says, and taught obedience in his own way: he used to say that the monk who does not obey his abbot does not complete his monastic promise, and so cannot be saved. Three of his writings on the monastic life and the manner of monks have in fact come down to us. After some time he lived as a stylite and gave himself deeply to Holy Scripture, and many men came to him for advice in the spiritual life and for guidance.

Because of the holiness of his life the ascetic was elected bishop of the town of Turov, always conscious of the high hierarchical office to which the Lord had called him. In the year 1169 he took part in a synod and reproved the bishop Theodore, who held the see of Vladimir and Suzdalia and wished to be separated from the canonical jurisdiction of the metropolitan district of Kyiv.

His love of stillness and of solitude led him to resign the episcopal throne, and so he gave himself entirely to asceticism and to the writing of spiritual works. He fell asleep in peace in the year 1183. His contemporaries reckoned him the Chrysostom of the Church of Russia; but he wrote of himself, humbly: «Δεν είμαι θεριστής, παρά μαζεύω τα δεμάτια των σιτηρών» — I am no reaper, I only gather up the sheaves of the corn.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this day and prints two readings of him. Both say he was born at Turov on the Pripyat, in the thirties of the twelfth century, of rich parents; that he read the Scriptures from childhood and had Greeks among his teachers as well as Russians; that he gave up his inheritance and was tonsured in the «Туровском Борисоглебском монастыре» at Turov, where he taught the monks that one who is not under obedience to his abbot does not keep his vow and cannot be saved. Three of his writings on the monastic life survive, one of them a «Сказание о черноризском чине от Ветхого Закона и от Нового» which may belong to those years. Afterwards he shut himself up on a pillar and «многа Божественна Писания изложи», set out much of the divine Scriptures, and many came to him for counsel.
