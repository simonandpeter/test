# Boris (Bogolepov)

Boris Ivanovich Bogolepov was born in Moscow on 18 June 1889, finished the Moscow seminary and was ordained priest. From July 1936 he served at the Trinity church at Korobino in the Tver region, living with his wife Catherine, his mother Maria, and the two youngest of his four daughters and one son, Andrew, thirteen, and Eugenia, eight. In January 1937 the village soviet demanded a year’s taxes in meat and money he did not have; he refused, was sentenced to a year and a half of camp, appealed, and was left at liberty. In April he was tried again, for burying a parishioner before her husband had the death certificate and for baptizing children without registration, and told the judge that to baptize was his first duty and he would go on doing it, whatever was done to him; he was sentenced to six months, and appealed again. When the district forbade processions to houses, on the pretext of scarlet fever, he went on serving molebens in the villages when the peasants asked him.

In late July he did not answer a summons to report for the camp, and after Stalin’s order came into force he was arrested. The village soviet’s chairman reported that he went from house to house with icons without leave, called Soviet propaganda empty chatter, took a harvest shift away for a feast, and collected money to repair the church. He was questioned on 8 September; on 27 September the NKVD troika sentenced him to death, and he was shot a few days later, on 1 October 1937.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 18 September old style, and he is also remembered on 26 January with the new martyrs.
