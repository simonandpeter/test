# Erastus

Erastus is one of the seventy apostles. doxologia.ro's notice of him is two sentences long: that he is of the Seventy and that the Apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) mentions him in the Epistle to the Romans; and that he was first steward of the holy Church of Jerusalem, and afterwards, being bishop of Paneas, went over to the Lord.

That is the whole of it. The page gives no year, no manner of death and nothing of his work in either place; and the hymn it prints for him is a common of the apostles, headed «Troparul Sfinților Apostoli Olimp, Rodion, Sosipatru, Erast, Terțiu, Cvart» for the six of them together and naming none of them in its text.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 November, in one entry with four others of the Seventy. saint.gr says all five belonged to the Lord's seventy apostles, whose synaxis it puts on 4 January; that all are named in Romans 16; and that most of them were bishops who kept excellently the command of 1 Peter 5, to shepherd the flock of God willingly and not for gain, becoming patterns for it rather than lording it over it. Of Erastus it says that he governed the bishopric of Neas in the same way as Sosipater governed Iconium — the page's spelling of the see, which is not the folder's. His distich makes a lover of him: «Ἐρῶν Ἔραστος μανικῶς τοῦ Κυρίου, / Σύνεστιν αὐτῷ καὶ χαρὰν χαίρει ξένην» — Erastus, loving the Lord to madness, is with him and rejoices with a strange joy.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day, in one line of six of the Seventy, and closes that line «(I)» — the first century, which is the first date this folder has carried. Its life page makes the four of the six who were not companions of the chief apostles disciples of the apostle whose letter to the Romans greets them, and quotes the greeting that names him: «Приветствует вас Ераст, городской казнохранитель, и брат Кварт» (Рим. 16, 23).

Of his own course it says that he was first a deacon and the treasurer of the Church of Jerusalem, and afterwards a bishop in Paneas — the see this folder already held from the Greek calendar, which the Russian page spells out in its own title for him, «епископ Панеадский». The name page adds the synaxis of the Seventy on 4 января and nothing else.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps six of the Seventy under one heading on the Julian 10 November and gives each of them a see or a death in a single clause. His is an office and then a see, and both agree with what this folder records: he was first the steward of the church of Jerusalem, and afterwards bishop in Paneas of Palestine. It closes on all six together — they fought the spiritual war, were victors, and were crowned with the crowns of glory.

The entry gives no year for any of them, so the first century this folder holds stands as it was. It says of the last three names in the heading that the book keeps them on other days of its own as well, which it prints: 8 April, 28 April and 30 October.
