# Xenia (Petrukhina)

She and [Anna (Gorokhova)](/saints/anna-gorokhova) were novices of the Dormition Brusensky monastery in the town of Kolomna in the Moscow province, and the one life the calendar has for them is one life for both. Xenia was born in 1897 at Chanki in the Kolomna district, the daughter of the peasant Simeon Petrukhin, and entered the Dormition house as a novice in 1913. In 1919, when the godless power began closing the monasteries, she went back to her native village and set about helping at the church of the Entrance of the Mother of God; at first she lived in the watchman's cottage beside it and then with people she knew, earning her bread by quilting blankets. Anna, a year older, had been born in the same village and entered the same monastery in 1914, and in 1919 she too went home, to keep house for her aged father.

In the persecution of the monastics at the beginning of the thirties both were arrested, on 22 May 1931, and shut in the Kolomna prison. At the questioning they confirmed that they had gone with other believers to the church of the Entrance, that they had gathered about the church and talked among themselves about the food supply and the harvest, and that they had said that the Lord gave neither harvest nor provisions nor goods because of men's sins, and that all of it came of godlessness and of mockery of the holy Church. On 10 June 1931 an OGPU troika sentenced them to three years of exile in Kazakhstan and they were sent by convoy to Karaganda.

In 1934, their term over, they came home. Xenia went to work as a nurse at the medical station by Golutvino railway halt and Anna took work in the collective farm, but they did not give up their needlework, the quilting of blankets, and they went to church as before; and soon the next wave of the persecution of the Russian Church overtook them. The duty witnesses called in testified that although since their return from exile the novices went visiting almost nobody and had even taken Soviet work for cover, they still kept company with the local priest, that many people came to them from their own village and from others, and that at home they carried on anti-Soviet propaganda.

The two were arrested on 5 March 1938 and shut in the Kolomna prison. Some days later the investigator questioned them. Having asked what they did and what they had been arrested for before, he asked whether they knew certain named local people; and when they answered that they knew them, he declared that the novices had been engaged in counter-revolutionary activity among the collective farmers and read them the duty witnesses' statements. Xenia and Anna rejected those statements flatly, saying that they had engaged neither in anti-Soviet activity nor in agitation against the government among the people. The investigation closed on 10 March, and on 12 March 1938 an NKVD troika sentenced them to be shot. They were sent from Kolomna to the Taganka prison in Moscow, where on 17 March the prison photographer took their photographs for the executioners. They were shot on 20 March 1938 and buried in an unmarked common grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow, which civil day is the Julian 7 March the calendar keeps them on.
