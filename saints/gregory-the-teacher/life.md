# Gregory the Teacher

Gregory was born at Bucharest in 1765 and was metropolitan of Wallachia between 1823 and 1834. After studying in the well-known schools of Bucharest he became a disciple of Saint Paisius Velichkovsky and was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Neamț. Wanting to go further, and at Paisius's own urging, the young monk lived for a time on Mount Athos; and on returning to the country he settled at the monastery of Căldărușani, which Saint George, abbot of Cernica and Căldărușani, had reorganized.

In 1823 he was called to Bucharest by the prince Grigorie Dimitrie Ghica and chosen metropolitan of Wallachia. As chief shepherd he kept up an untiring pastoral and missionary work, and a social and cultural one besides: he named bishops to the vacant sees of Argeș, Râmnic and Buzău, and founded a school of theology in each of those eparchial centres.

His whole life and labour in the service of the Church, doxologia's life says, was an example of holiness and of sacrificial love for those he shepherded. Many poor people, widows and orphans lived on what he gave them — food, shelter and books of instruction — and at his death there remained a great quantity of books made ready to be given away to schoolchildren.

His work in the metropolitan's chair was broken off by the Russian administration set up in the Romanian Principalities, and he spent more than four years in exile at Chișinău, at Buzău and at Căldărușani. He took particular care for the restoration of the metropolitan cathedral in Bucharest, and for the translation and printing in Romanian of the Lives of the Saints, which he held to be so useful for the spiritual formation and the enlightening of the faithful.

For his life, his work and his spiritual labour he has remained in the history of the Church and of the Romanian people as the foremost occupant of the metropolitan see of Wallachia in the nineteenth century, known by the byname «Dascălul», the Teacher. The page that doxologia gives as his troparion is a recording, and prints only the refrain «Sfinte Ierarhe Grigorie, roagă-te lui Dumnezeu pentru noi!»; no troparion text is printed, so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 22 June, and calls him ο Βατοπαιδινός, of Vatopedi, beside the Romanian byname Dascălul. saint.gr tells the same life: born into a blessed family at Bucharest in 1765 and given the name George at his baptism, George Minculescu studied in the most famous schools of the age, among them the princely Academy of St Sabbas, and came away with an excellent education and a serious theological culture. With two fellow students and friends he left for the monastery of Neamț, then guided by the great Paisius Velichkovsky, and in 1790 was tonsured a monk with the name Gregory; knowing Latin and Greek, he was given the obedience of translating some of the writings of the holy fathers. After some years he was sent with his friend the monk Gerontius to Bucharest, where the care of the metropolitan library was entrusted to him, and from there he went on to the Holy Mountain. The page adds what the Romanian source does not: that his relics lie buried at the monastery of Căldărușani in Romania, and that he was glorified on 21 May 2006, the feast of the patron saints Constantine and Helen, during a liturgy served by the patriarch Teoctist with most of the Holy Synod of the Romanian Church and representatives of the other Orthodox Churches.
