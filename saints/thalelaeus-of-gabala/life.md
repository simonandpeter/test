# Thalelaeus of Gabala

Thalelaeus was of the country of Cilicia. Despising the vanity of this world he went first to the monastery of Saint Sabbas, and loving the hermit's way in the monastic life he took the angelic habit upon himself; for the goodness of his life he was found worthy of the rank of priest. Some years afterwards he went to a city of Syria called Gabala, which lay under the metropolis of Laodicea.

About twenty stadia from that city stood a great mound and an old idol-temple, an ancient Greek burial ground, with a crowd of idols in it. The place was dreaded by everyone who passed, for the demons there did not only frighten people with apparitions and lying visions but killed men and harmed many people and beasts. Hearing of it, Thalelaeus went and settled there, made himself a small hut, and gave himself to the hermit's labours and hardships, praying without ceasing day and night.

The demons would not endure such a neighbour. They gathered all their dreadful camps, fell upon him in great rage, terrified him with disordered voices and threatened him with death, and strove with all their crooked powers to drive him out of a place that had long been theirs. Strengthening and fencing himself with faith in God, with prayer and with the sign of the Cross, he stood like a brave and unconquered soldier on the day of battle and mocked the weakness of their strength; and in the end, with the help of the Most High, they fled in shame.

Having driven the demons off, he made himself a narrow cell, not to the measure of his height but two cubits high and one cubit wide, in which he could neither stand nor sit upright, so that, being a big man, he kept his head always bent to his knees. He had spent nearly ten years in that narrowness when Theodoret, bishop of Cyrus, came to visit him and found him gathering profit from the holy Gospel; and when the bishop asked why he had chosen such a life, he answered as one guilty who had need of it, and the bishop went away much helped.

God gave him the gift of wonders as well, to heal every sickness and infirmity in men and in beasts alike, so that many came to him as to a healing spring and got health of body and of soul. The Greeks of the villages round about turned to Christ God, and the idolaters still left among the Christians in all the borders of Gabala — a great many at that time — were wholly enlightened with the faith through his wonders. With the help of the newly baptised he destroyed the pagan temple, pulled down the Greek burial ground, cleansed the place of those old works of the devil, and built a church there in the name of all the holy martyrs.

He lived out his remaining days in his accustomed labours and went to God in deep old age. Of him, the page adds, Sophronius of Jerusalem writes in the *Leimonarion* that Abba Peter, priest of the lavra of Saint Sabbas, told how Abba Thalelaeus the Cilician passed seventy years in the monastic habit without ever ceasing from weeping, and said always to everyone: «God has given us this time, brothers, for repentance, and if we lose it we shall be asked a great deal about it.» The page gives no year, and prints no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and tells how he fished for souls. saint.gr says Thalelaeus came from Cilicia and practised his asceticism outside the city of Gabala in Syria, where there stood a pagan temple that many streamed to; and he turned that to account, working at the enlightening of the pagans and the drawing of many of them to the Christian faith. He was full of humility and never gave himself airs over his spiritual achievements — but he was also formidably resourceful when it came to bringing souls near to Christ. Once he built himself a strange hanging bed, and word of it went round the whole region, so that many pagans came to see him; and from up there Thalelaeus would start a conversation with them and so cast his spiritual nets, which caught many souls and saved them. By that means, the page says, he managed to christianise a whole city, Gabala, and became its spiritual father by the grace of Jesus Christ. Its couplet has him come brightly to heaven crowned with olive shoots, the virtues, which is his name over again.
