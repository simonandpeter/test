# Joasaph

Joasaph was born at Priluki in Poltava province on 8 September 1705, the
feast of the Nativity of the Theotokos, and named Joachim at baptism, of the
old and devout Little Russian family of the Gorlenkos. In 1712 his father
gave the seven-year-old to the Kyiv Academy, within whose walls he felt the
pull of the monastic life; for seven years he tested himself, and at last
opened his mind to his parents, who long begged their firstborn not to take
the tonsure — but in 1725, secretly from them, he took the rasophore as
Hilarion at the Mezhigorsky monastery in Kyiv, and on 21 November 1727,
as he finished the academy, the mantle as Joasaph at the Kyiv Brotherhood
monastery. A year later Archbishop Barlaam Vonatovich made him hierodeacon
and kept him as a teacher at the academy; Barlaam’s successor Raphael
Zaborovsky saw the young ascetic’s gifts, made him examiner of the
archdiocese, in November 1734 ordained him hieromonk and moved him to the
St Sophia archbishop’s house, and made him a member of the consistory —
where, as examiner, he worked hard at the moral failings of the parish
clergy, learned the needs of the priests and the strengths and weaknesses
of the diocese, and showed the all-round practical gifts that went with
great inner struggle; his surviving work, The Battle of the Seven Honoured
Virtues with the Seven Deadly Sins, shows how fast he climbed. On 24 June
1737 he was made igumen of the Transfiguration monastery of Mgar, once a
bulwark of Orthodoxy against the Unia, and gave all his strength to
restoring it; the relics of Athanasius, patriarch of Constantinople, the
wonderworker of Lubny, lay there, and several times the patriarch appeared
to him as a sign of his protection. Metropolitan Raphael made him
archimandrite in 1744; at the end of that year he was called to Moscow and
by the Synod’s order made deputy superior of the Trinity-St Sergius Lavra,
where much strength was needed to rebuild after a fire. On 2 June 1748 he
was consecrated bishop of Belgorod in the Peter and Paul cathedral at
Petersburg. As bishop he watched strictly over the piety and state of the
churches, the right celebration of the services and above all the morals of
his flock, gave great attention to the education of the clergy and their
keeping of the rule and the church’s traditions, and, as before, spared
neither strength nor health. On the eve of his death he forbade his
cell-attendant Stephen to seek ordination, warning that disobedience would
bring him an early death, and told another, Basil, that he would be a
deacon and never reach the priesthood — and so it turned out. He died on
10 December 1754, and was glorified on 4 September 1911; the Russian
calendar keeps on 4 September (Julian) the finding of his relics that year,
with troparia and a kontakion.
