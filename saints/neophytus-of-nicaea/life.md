# Neophytus of Nicaea

Neophytus was born at Nicaea in Bithynia to Theodore and Florentia, Christians who brought him up in the faith. The life says that when he was near ten years old and had begun his lessons the grace of God settled on him: he would take the poor boys of his school home with him, divide among them the food his parents gave him for the midday meal, and go hungry himself. At the east gate of the city he traced a cross with his finger and prayed, and when he struck a stone in the wall with his hand water came out of it as from a spring, and his schoolfellows drank. He told them to say nothing, and for a year nobody knew.

In the second year his mother was shown it in a dream, and when she prayed to be told more plainly a white dove came down and spoke to her son with a human voice; at that she fell down dead of fright. Neophytus met his father at the gate, said she was only sleeping, took her by the hand and raised her; and many in the city who saw it believed. Afterwards the dove called him out of his father's house and led him to Mount Olympus, to a cave where he told a lion to find another lodging. He lived there, fed by an angel, went home once to bury his parents and give what they left to the poor, and returned until he was fifteen.

The page sets what follows under the tyrants Diocletian and Maximian, when Decius and Uar governed Bithynia. At a public sacrifice at Nicaea angels set Neophytus down in the middle of the market place; he rebuked Decius before the crowd and was hung on a beam and beaten, torn with iron claws to the bone, and shut three days in a heated furnace, from which he came out unhurt. A bear, a she-bear and a great lion were loosed on him in the arena and would not touch him; the lion, the life says, was the one he had sent out of the cave on Olympus. At the last his body was pierced through with iron spikes, and a barbarian ran him through the breast with a spear. He died on 21 January, aged fifteen years and four months.
