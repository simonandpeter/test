# Sergius (Makhaev)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him third of twenty-four and gives no life; the life here is the one azbyka.ru prints for the civil 2 декабря, out of fond.ru and the Moscow diocese’s volume of lives for November. He was born on 6 September 1874 in the family of a priest, Constantine Georgievich Makhaev, who served at the church of the Saviour in the village of Usovo in the Moscow district, where the estate of the grand duke Sergius Alexandrovich lay. He finished the Vifansky seminary in the first class in June 1895 and was appointed psalmist at the Moscow church of Saint Nicholas in Kosheli.

From the first he showed himself a teacher and a zealous preacher. While still a psalmist he taught, without pay, at the Nikolo-Myasnitsky parish school, and then Russian in the senior division of the Sergievsky school in the Rogozh quarter. In those years he married Lyudmila Sergeyevna Tsvetkova, a Moscow priest’s daughter; they had no children. In 1900 a church of the Iverskaya icon was built at the Moscow Iverskaya community of sisters of mercy, whose honorary patrons were the grand duke Sergius Alexandrovich and the grand duchess Elizabeth Feodorovna, and he was appointed to the priest’s vacancy there, ordained deacon on 15 October 1900 and priest two days later. He served that church nineteen years.

He went on teaching as a law teacher, in the Petrovsko-Serpukhovsky town school and the trade classes of the Moscow society for commercial education, and from 1908 at the school of the merchants’ house of free lodgings on Yakimanka. From the autumn of 1905, at the grand duchess Elizabeth Feodorovna’s asking, he began to teach the law of God to the future sisters of the Iverskaya community. He took them on pilgrimage to the Trinity-Sergius Lavra and talked with them of the spiritual life, and two books of 1914 came of it: «Беседы пастыря с сестрами милосердия» and «Подвижницы милосердия».

When the community’s detachment came back from the Far East in 1905 he met them in the church with a word that had little of the usual congratulation in it. War, he said, is a wilderness too — a wilderness of the spirit, where the higher strivings of men towards union in love are trodden down; and as great ascetics had shone in the wilderness, so had many shone in this one. Then he asked them what fruit the sight had borne in themselves: whether the constant sight of death had taught them to be dead to the flesh and alive to God, and whether they had learnt to be ready to give their whole life to the work of love and get for it, perhaps, insult, persecution, sickness and death.

In 1924, when his church was handed to a group of the Union of Regeneration, he brought the militia to it and the Renovationists were taken off to have a protocol drawn up; their community then wrote to the Moscow Soviet asking for measures against what it called «махаевщина». He petitioned the metropolitan Peter (Polyansky) to attach his parishioners and his clergy to the church of Saint Maron in Stary Pany, was arrested the same year and held three months, and served at Saint Maron’s as second priest until it closed in 1930.

In 1937 he was made rector of the Theophany cathedral at Noginsk. In August the whole parish council was arrested, and to keep the church open he got a new body of twenty together; some withdrew from it in the arrests, and this was afterwards turned into a charge that he had pressed the faithful. He was arrested on 22 November 1937 and questioned the same day: he had carried on no counter-revolutionary work, had drawn nobody into the twenty by deceit, had never voiced terrorist intentions, and did not admit the charge. The troika sentenced him on 25 November, and he was shot on 2 December 1937 and buried in a common unmarked grave at the Butovo range.
