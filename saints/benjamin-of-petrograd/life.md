# Benjamin of Petrograd

Vasily Kazansky was born in 1873 on the Nimenga churchyard in the Andreyevo district of the Kargopol uezd, now in the Arkhangelsk region, the son of the priest Pavel and of Maria Kazansky. His parents raised him in piety, and he came to love reading the lives of the saints, admiring their labours and sorrowing that in the world he was born into he was denied the chance to suffer for the Orthodox faith. After the Petrozavodsk seminary he entered the St Petersburg Theological Academy.

As a student he worked in the society for the spread of religious and moral enlightenment, holding talks among working men. In 1895 he was tonsured with the name Benjamin and ordained hierodeacon, and the next year priest. Graduating in 1897 a candidate of theology, he taught Scripture at the Riga seminary, then inspected the Kholm and the St Petersburg seminaries, and after being raised archimandrite in 1902 was rector of the Samara and then of the St Petersburg seminary.

On 24 January 1910 he was consecrated bishop of Gdov and vicar of St Petersburg. He was seen in the poorest and furthest quarters of the capital, in a plain cassock without the marks of his rank, baptising a child or giving the last rites, and his flock called him «our father Benjamin». In March 1917 he was elected archbishop of Petrograd and Ladoga, and on 13 August, on the eve of the Council, metropolitan of Petrograd and Gdov. «I stand for a free Church,» he said; «it must be a stranger to politics.»

When the famine of 1921 brought the campaign against church valuables to Petrograd in March 1922, he blessed the handing over of everything not used in worship, saying «We shall give it all up ourselves». The authorities declared instead that the valuables would be taken formally as state property. A letter in the Petrograd Pravda of 24 March from twelve organisers of the renovationist schism accused the clergy loyal to the Patriarch of resisting the seizure and of conspiracy against the state; he was arrested on 29 May, and on 10 June the hearing opened with eighty-six others in the dock.

Before the tribunal he was as he always was, simple and calm and pleading the innocence of others. «I do not know what you will declare to me in your sentence, life or death,» he said, «but whatever you proclaim in it, I shall with the same reverence turn my eyes upward, make upon myself the sign of the cross and say: Glory to Thee, O Lord God, for all things.» Sentence was given on 5 July 1922, and in the night of 12 to 13 August he was shot on the edge of Petrograd together with [Sergius (Shein)](/saints/sergius-shein), [Yuri (Novitsky)](/saints/yuri-novitsky) and [John (Kovsharov)](/saints/john-kovsharov). A cross stands over the symbolic grave of the Russian new martyrs in the brotherhood cemetery of the Lavra in Petrograd.
