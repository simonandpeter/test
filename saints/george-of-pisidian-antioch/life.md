# George of Pisidian Antioch

George, the confessor of Christ, lived in the years of the iconoclasts. Loving Christ from his youth, he was tonsured a monk, and for his many labours and struggles in the Church he became, the page says, a dwelling and a house of the Holy Spirit. He was made bishop of Antioch in Pisidia, and shepherded well the flock entrusted to him.

When the heresy of the icon-fighters had grown strong and all the bishops were called by an imperial letter to the city of Constantine, this hierarch went up with the rest. He stood bravely for the right faith, telling the emperor to submit to the ordinances of the Church and of the councils of the holy fathers, the teachers of Orthodoxy, and to follow their faith rather than be carried away to strange and various teachings, as the apostle Paul commands.

He too was pressed to renounce the veneration of the holy icons, and he would not yield. For that he was driven from his seat, and in his banishment he departed to the Lord. The notice is the whole of what the page prints: it gives no year, does not name the emperor who summoned the bishops, and carries no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's notice names what the Romanian one left unnamed. George lived in the years of the iconoclasm, it says, and was elected bishop of Pisidia. Called with other bishops to Constantinople, where the abolition of the holy icons was to be imposed, at the council of 754, he resisted stoutly the orders of the icon-fighting rulers and their impiety. For that he was exiled by the emperor Constantine V, called Copronymus, whose reign the page dates 741 to 775, and in exile he fell asleep. For his struggles on behalf of the Orthodox faith he was named the Confessor. The page gives him no year of birth or death, no relic and no hymn.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day as the Romanian and the Greek, and **sets his life a human lifetime later than they do.** Its line heads him «(IX)», the ninth century, and its reading has him live in the time of iconoclasm, take the monastic habit in his young years, become renowned for holiness of life and be set as bishop of Pisidian Antioch; and then, during the persecution of the holy icons under the emperor «Лев Армянин», whose reign it dates 813 to 820, come to Constantinople and at a council of bishops denounce the iconoclast heresy to its face, urging the emperor to abandon it. When he refused to take the icons out of his church at the emperor’s order he was sent into confinement, and the page closes «(+ ок. 813 - 820)».

That is the second iconoclasm. This folder’s `floruit`, 754 to 775, was inferred from a Greek page that put him at the iconoclast council of 754 and had him exiled by Constantine V Copronymus, whose reign it dated 741 to 775 — the first iconoclasm, some sixty years earlier. The shape of the two accounts is otherwise the same in every particular: the monastic beginning, the see, the refusal to remove the icons, the exile, the death in exile. **Two traditions, not one misreading, and nothing was chosen between them**; the interval already on the folder was left exactly as the Greek wave wrote it and the Russian years are set down here in words. The author’s queue is where this belongs.
