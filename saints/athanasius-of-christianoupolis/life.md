# Athanasius the New of Christianoupolis

Athanasius was born at Karytaina in Gortynia about the year 1640 — or, saint.gr says, by other accounts at Corfu in 1664 — and his name in the world was Anastasios Korfinos. His parents were called Andreas and Euphrosyne and had three other children. The page supposes he learnt his first letters in his birthplace and went on to the famous school of the Philosophou monastery, and later, as a cleric, to Constantinople; it says plainly that this is supposition.

When Anastasios came to the age for marriage his parents, against his own wish for the monastic life, insisted on marrying him off. His father betrothed him at Patras, without asking his consent, to the daughter of a rich landowner, and sent him to Nauplion to buy the wedding goods. He obeyed, and on the road stopped at the little church of the Mother of God at Vidoni, near the village of Syrna, and asked for God's light. At Nauplion, having bought what was needed, he made the great decision: the night before he was to leave for Karytaina, tormented by his thoughts, he saw in sleep the Mother of God with the Forerunner, and she called him by the name he was later to take as a monk — a chosen vessel and a servant of my Son I desire you to become, Athanasius; send back your servants with the bridal garments to your father, and let the girl be joined to another man, and go yourself to Constantinople.

So it was. He sent the servants back, went to Constantinople, became a monk under the name Athanasius, and was ordained deacon and then presbyter. Under the first patriarchate of the Ecumenical Patriarch Jacob he was consecrated Metropolitan of Christianoupolis, most honoured and exarch of all Arcadia, in succession to the metropolitan Eugenios; the page puts the consecration at the end of 1680 or the beginning of 1681, because that April is the first time he appears, signing a letter of excommunication as a member of the Holy Synod at Constantinople. The title points to Christianoupolis, today the village of Christianoi, but the real seat of the metropolis, it says, must safely be taken to be the town of Kyparissia.

The state of his province was desperate in money, in church order and in morals. He set at once to the work of it: his first care was to find suitable young men for the priesthood, and to that end he set up schools for their elementary liturgical and other training, and at the same time gave up every one of the customary payments made to a bishop for his own and his see's keep. Believing the Orthodox Church to be the institution that keeps the genuine faith in Christ and the bond that holds the enslaved Greeks together, and the churches to be their point of meeting, he saw to their repair and upkeep so far as money and Turkish permission allowed, and he cared for the monasteries as hearths of salvation and centres of enlightenment and charity. To his flock he was a true bishop and an imitator of Christ, and his love for orphans, widows, the helpless old, the persecuted and the wronged was, the page says, without equal.

God granted him to work signs and wonders in his earthly life and after his falling asleep. It is recorded that when he served the liturgy, at the moment he came out at the Beautiful Gate to say «Lord, Lord, look down from heaven and behold», the faithful saw a shining star before his mouth. Having shepherded his flock in a manner pleasing to God, he fell asleep after a short illness in 1707 or 1708, or by other accounts in 1735; and a few years later, between 1710 and 1713, he was exhumed, and the greater part of the holy relic was found undissolved and streaming myrrh. It is kept at the Prodromos monastery of Gortynia.
