# Macarius (Smirnov)

He was Michael Yefimyevich Smirnov in the world, born on 1 April 1877 in the village of Melekhovo in the St Petersburg province to the peasants Euthymius and Anna. His parents took him with them on their visits to the monasteries, and in the end the wish ripened in him to leave the world and live in one. He finished a theological seminary, was tonsured with the name Macarius and ordained priest; in what year, the reading says plainly, is not known.

The monastery where he had struggled lasted until 1928. After it was closed he served as a priest in a parish church, and in 1933 the authorities laid taxes on him he could not meet and sentenced him for not paying them to a year in a corrective labour camp. Coming out of the camp he served in the church of the village of Krasnaya Polyana, where he arrived in the summer of 1937, not long before his last arrest.

The duty witnesses, the secretary of the village soviet and the chairman of the village cooperative, were questioned on 19 December 1937. Questioned himself, he allowed exactly what he had done and no more: once after a service he had asked the faithful to collect money to repair the church, or it would be closed; and in 1934 he had told them not to let the bells be taken down. Agitation against the state loan he denied; counter-revolutionary agitation he denied; calling the people to rise against the closing of the church he denied, saying again that what he had told them was that the church needed repairing or it would be shut.

On 27 December a troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot the next day but one, on 29 December 1937. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
