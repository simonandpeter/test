# Zacharias, son of Barachias

Doxologia's life of this prophet is a single short paragraph, and this is the whole of what it says. His name, it begins, is interpreted "the remembrance of God". He was of the people of Israel and of the tribe of Levi, the son of Barachias, and he was born in Gilead. He came out of the land of the Chaldeans already an old man, and while he was there he prophesied many things to the people of Israel and worked many signs in proof of them.

He told Josedech that he would beget a son and that the son would be a priest of the Lord in Jerusalem; and he blessed Salathiel concerning his son, saying to him: «Thou shalt beget a son and shalt call his name Zerubbabel.» In the days of Cyrus, king of the Persians, he gave him a sign concerning Croesus, king of the Lydians.

He prophesied also of the sacking of Jerusalem and of its end, and of the beginning and the end of the nations; then of the pulling down of the temple, down to the last, and of the ceasing of the prophets, of the Jewish priesthood, of the sabbaths, and concerning the twofold judgement. At the last, having prophesied many other things besides, he fell asleep in peace at a great age. The page gives no year, and this is a different Zacharias from Zacharias the father of the Forerunner, whom the calendars keep in September.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and fills the line out. It makes him the eleventh of the twelve lesser prophets of the Old Testament, of the race of Israel and the tribe of Levi, born in the city of Gilead in Palestine during the Babylonian captivity; his name, it says, means in Greek the memory of God, that is, the one whom God remembers. It names him son of Barachias and grandson of Addo. He and the prophet [Haggai](/saints/haggai-the-prophet) were the two who roused the Jews to rebuild the temple at Jerusalem when they came back to Judaea in 537 or 536 before Christ. The page reports as an opinion, not a fact, that he belonged to a priestly family and was a priest himself; and it says that by the Jewish tradition he and Haggai were members of the Great Synagogue that fixed the canon of the books of the Old Testament, that they worked at the ordering of the sacred service, and that they composed or revised psalms. It credits him with two prophecies in particular: of the Lord's entry into Jerusalem on the Sunday of the Palms, and of the sum the chief priests paid Judas as the price of the betrayal of his Teacher. He fell asleep in a deep old age and was buried near the tomb of Haggai. The emperor Theodosius the Great, between 379 and 395, built a church dedicated to him at the monastery of Saint Domnika in Constantinople, and another stood on the mountain of Auxentius, in a place that was called the Theatre. Its couplet has him see the minds as horses, and ride on them to the height of the heavens.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the same day of the menologion, 8 февраля ст. ст., and counts him among the twelve lesser prophets: «Прор. Захарии Серповидца из 12-ти (ок. 520 до Р.Х.).» It sets his time at about five hundred and twenty years before Christ, which no other calendar read for this page gives, and it calls him the sickle-seer.
