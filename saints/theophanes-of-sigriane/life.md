# Theophanes of Sigriane

Theophanes's homeland was Constantinople, and he was born of honourable parents, Isaac and Theodotia. Isaac was a kinsman of the emperor Leo the Isaurian (717–740) and of Constantine Copronymus (740–775), and was for that called “the Isaurian”, being of the same imperial stock and of the parts of Isauria; he held the highest offices of the imperial palace. Those were the years of the iconoclast heresy and of a great persecution of the right-believing, and Isaac and his wife kept the right faith secretly, for fear.

They were found worthy to be the parents of such a son, and named him Theophanes, which means “shown forth by God” — partly because he was born at the feast of the Theophany, and partly because God chose him from his mother's womb, as once the prophet Jeremiah, and hallowed him and showed him a great light of the Church. His father lived only three years after his birth, and before dying made a will putting the boy and his mother into the care of the emperor Constantine Copronymus, as of a kinsman.

The child grew in years and in understanding, learning the divine Scripture and good manners. When he reached twelve, a senator who had a daughter of ten, seeing the boy handsome, wise, very rich and a kinsman of the emperor, sought him for her; the widow Theodotia agreed, and the emperor consenting, the children were betrothed before the years fit for marriage, the wedding being put off until they were both grown. Copronymus died, and Leo his son, called Chazar after his mother (775–780), reigned after him; and before the wedding Theodotia died, leaving her son wealth beyond counting.

Theophanes had in his house a God-fearing and wise servant whom he loved and kept as a good counsellor in everything; and urged by that man's profitable talk, and led above all by the Spirit of God, he loved chastity and considered how he might keep his virginity. On the wedding night he set it before his bride: that broad is the gate and wide the way that leads to destruction, and that it were better for them to live in one soul in virginity, keeping the appearance of a marriage for a while because her father was a hard man, and living under that appearance as brother and sister until God should give them occasion to go, he to a monastery of men and she to one of virgins.

The bride took the words like good ground taking good seed, and answered gladly that she knew the Saviour's words in the Gospel, that whoever will not leave father and mother, wife, children, brothers and sisters, houses and villages, and take up his cross and follow him, is not worthy of him; that what he wished she wished, and that they should live in secret in the virginity they had kept until the Lord should bring them into the monastic order. He marvelled at her understanding and fell to the ground and gave thanks.

They spent that whole night in prayer, and at dawn slept a little, and both saw the same dream: a young man with a shining face, smiling at them and saying with love that the Lord had accepted their intention and had sent him to seal them for such a life as they had now agreed upon, that they might give themselves to him whole and unblemished; and he signed them both all over with the sign of the Cross and became invisible. Waking, they told each other the dream, and smelled an unspeakable sweet fragrance, which filled not only the chamber but the whole house, and was smelled by many.

So that holy pair lived in the flesh as angels, burning like two lampstands before God with the flame of the love of God, and pouring out like two olive trees the oil of mercy; for they gave alms without ceasing every day and shared out their goods to the destitute without sparing. When after a time her father learned how his son-in-law and his daughter were living, in virginity and giving their riches to the poor, he was deeply grieved. The Romanian life is long, and tells at length how Theophanes came at last into the monastic order and to the mountain of Sigriane.

In his old age the emperor sent to him with offers — that his monastery should be raised in stone and enriched with all abundance, and he himself honoured above all men, and his kinsmen given great rank — and with threats, that if he set himself against them he would bring great dishonour upon himself. He answered through the messengers that he had no need of the riches of this world, for if in his youth he had left gold and silver and great goods for the love of Christ, should he desire them in his old age? And that for his monastery and his kinsmen God, who provides, is greater than emperors and nobles; and why should the emperor frighten him with chastisements, as one frightens a small child with a rod? Let him prepare every kind of torment and kindle a fire: though he could not walk by himself, he would leap into the fire for the right faith.

The emperor marvelled at that fearless boldness and set one John, a sophist quick in words, to dispute with him; and that man too was left as though dumb before the God-speaking mouth of the venerable father and went back beaten and ashamed. Then, filled with anger, the emperor had Theophanes shut into a dark and narrow house beside the palace called Eleutherius, with guards set over him; and there, old and sick, he spent two years, urged daily, now with enticements and now with chastisements, to fight against the holy icons, and ill-treated and slandered by heretics sent to him on purpose.

Once the emperor sent to him with enticements, that he might sign the rejection of the icons; and he wrote back that the emperor should know who gave him his kingdom, by whom kings reign and tyrants hold the earth; that God, who is uncircumscribed, yet willed to be circumcised, taking our human nature, like us in all things but sin, and through that deified nature raised the dead and gave light to the blind. Since we receive the book of the Gospel with honour and believe what is written in it, he asked, how shall we be condemned for receiving and honouring the same Gospel words set out in icons — by which even the unlettered, looking with their eyes, see the wonders of Christ and his willing passion and glorify the Lord who suffered for us? And he asked what council had ever declared that honouring the holy icons is a sin.

Reading that letter the emperor was filled with unspeakable anger, and at once sent a harsh officer to the parts of Sigriane to pull down and burn the venerable man's monastery, which was called the Great Dwelling, and to drive out his disciples with merciless beating. Another, as harsh, he sent to the saint in the dark prison where he had been kept two years: he stripped the old man, whose body was worn with much fasting and long sickness, and beat him without pity on the back and the belly, giving him three hundred strokes, and shut him again in the same bonds. Next day the same man took him out and beat him again, and then sent him into exile on the island of Samothrace, which the saint had foreseen and told his attendant of some days before.

There Theophanes lived only twenty-three days, and passed from his earthly banishment to the remembrance of heaven, adorned with the crown of a confessor's suffering; and his honoured body was laid there in a wooden coffin. God glorified him not only in his life but after his death, giving his relics healing power, so that a multitude of the sick were healed by touching his coffin. And after the ill-believing emperor Leo the Armenian was killed, the saint's banished disciples returned to their own dwelling on the mountain of Sigriane, adorned the burnt monastery again, and brought their father's holy relics from Samothrace to the Great Dwelling and laid them in the church with honour. doxologia gives no year for his death.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and fixes some things the Romanian life leaves open. saint.gr heads him 760 to 815 and names his parents Isaac and Theodotia; it has him orphaned of his father at the age of eight, his mother taking up the hard work of his rearing and his schooling. At her entreaty he married young, the devout and wealthy Megalo; and that marriage, which stood against the monastic life he wanted, was dissolved — his wife became a nun at the monastery of Prinkipos and was renamed Irene, and he took refuge in 781 in a monastery near the mountain of Sigriane, the Polichnion. Of Sigriane itself the page will not decide: it was either in Media or, more probably, at Mytilene, by the cape of Sigri.

From that monastery, as a learned and virtuous monk, he was called out with other distinguished abbots — Plato of the monastery of Sakkoudion, the monks Nikephoros and Niketas of the monastery of Medikion, the monk Christophoros and others. The page keeps for him the lines «Θεόφανες, φάνηθι πιστοῖς προστάτης, / Τιμῶσι πιστῶς σὸν μετ' εἰρήνης τέλος», and prints an apolytikion, a kontakion, an oikos and a kathisma for him.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović begins with his name. He was called Sigrian, it says, because of the place Sigria where he was born; and he was a kinsman of the emperor Leo the Isaurian and of Leo's son Copronymus. He had an enormous fortune and great splendour, and all of it lost its value for him when Christ the Lord became king in his soul. He resisted his own marriage, and when he was forced into it all the same he managed to persuade his bride that they should live chastely as brother and sister; and as soon as his parents died she went into one monastery and he into another.

His own monastery was in the Sigrian hills in the region of Cyzicus, and there the once famous and wealthy Theophanes lived like the poorest of beggars, so that everyone marvelled at the change in him. Having become renowned for the strength of his faith, his abstinence and his wisdom, he was called to the Seventh Ecumenical Council at Nicaea, where the veneration of the icons was established. For his great purity and chastity God gave him the gift of wonderworking, and he healed every sickness and above all mania and madness; he prayed to God for all the sick and the afflicted and helped them by his prayer. Only when he himself fell ill, and was ill a long time, he would not pray to God for his own recovery, but bore it with thanksgiving.

When the persecution of the icons began again under Leo the Armenian, Theophanes was brought to Constantinople and thrown into prison, where he passed two years in hardship, torment and humiliation. Then the emperor sent him into banishment on the island of Samothrace, which he had foreseen in his spirit beforehand and told the jailers; and when he reached Samothrace he lived only twenty-three days more and departed to his Lord and Creator, to receive the crown of glory he had earned. The entry gives no year, and no other source read for him gives one either.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery tells the parts of his life the Greek page passes over. He was born at Constantinople of a devout and noble family, and his father was a kinsman of the emperor Leo the Isaurian, whose reign it gives as 717 to 741; the father died three years after Theophanes was born and left the family in the emperor's own care, so that the boy grew up at court and became a dignitary under Leo the Khazar, 775 to 780. His position obliged him to marry, and by agreement with his bride he kept his chastity, because the wish for the monastic habit was ripening in him; and visiting the monasteries of the Sigrian country in Asia Minor with his wife he met a clear-sighted elder, Gregory Strategius, who foretold what would come of them.

At fifty, the page says, he fell gravely ill and suffered cruelly until his death, and on his deathbed he worked without stopping at the Chronography, the history of the Christian Church from 285 to 813 which is still a valuable source for it. When Leo the Armenian came to the throne, 813 to 820, and he was already a very old man, the iconoclasm began again; he was pressed to take the heresy, stood firm against it, and was shut in prison, and his house of the Great Village was burned. After twenty-three days in that prison the confessor died, in the year 818 — three years later than the year this folder held from the Greek, so the interval is now 815 to 818 and chooses neither. After the death of the impious emperor the Great Village was rebuilt and his relics were carried there.
