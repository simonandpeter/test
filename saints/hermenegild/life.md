# Hermenegild

saint.gr's calendar for 1 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Ερμινίγγελδος», and spells him twice more in the notice and the epigram. He was the son of Leovigild, king of the Visigoths, the page says, and that king and his whole nation had gone over to the heresy of Arius. Hermenegild, however, was taught the Orthodox faith by the bishop Leander, on a journey of his.

When he came home it became known that he had renounced Arianism and become a Christian. This distressed his father, who tried to turn him back with entreaties and with threats; and when he got nowhere, he denounced his own son as a Christian, and so they imprisoned him. Hermenegild was tortured a great deal but stayed faithful to the Lord, and his death was therefore ordered: soldiers came into his cell and killed him kneeling, as he was praying, about the year 585.

The epigram the page sets over him puts the two baptisms side by side: Hermenegild, washed clean of the dye of error, undergoes slaughter and is dyed in the shellfish-purple of his own gore. No relics are recorded and no hymn is printed. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 1 November as well, «Мч. Ерминингельда, царевича Готфского (586)» — the martyr Hermenegild, prince of the Goths, 586 — and its index of names repeats those words. Its short life is five sentences and all of them are about the faith rather than the killing: he left the Arian heresy and was converted to Orthodoxy, and his father the king, an Arian, could bring him back to the old faith neither by kindness nor by threats and gave the order for his execution.

What this calendar has and the Greek page does not is the sequel. The prince's firmness in the faith and his courage in the face of death made the king repent of what he had done; not daring to accept Orthodoxy himself, he charged the holy bishop [Leander](/saints/leander-of-seville) with bringing his heir, Hermenegild's brother, into the true faith, and that brother, when he became king, established Orthodoxy throughout the country. The year moves by one: this line prints 586 where saint.gr wrote about 585, and the folder now holds both.
