# Anastasia Șaguna, mother of Andrei Șaguna

Anastasia was born in 1785 and was given by her parents a careful upbringing, in the fear of God, in honesty and in steadfastness in the faith of her forebears. She married young, and at eighteen became a mother, bearing Evreta, the brother of the future metropolitan; then a daughter, Ecaterina, in 1806; and then the child Anastasiu — the future metropolitan [Andrei Șaguna](/saints/andrei-saguna) — on 20 December 1808, at Miskolc.

Her life took a hard turn when her husband, Naum Șaguna, pressed by money and by circumstance, went over to Catholicism. The choice set off a long struggle in her and about her, and she resolved to defend her faith and above all the souls of her children with all her stubbornness, being convinced that the holiest religion is the one her parents and forebears had worshipped in.

Helped by her father, Mihail, she fought a bitter battle against the authorities and the Catholic officials, and above all against the Catholic archbishop of Miskolc. She refused to give way to the pressure and managed to hide the children in the house of her uncle, the merchant Atanasie Grabovski, in Pest, where in the Orthodox setting of the Greek school and of the "Greco-Wallachian" church they could be formed in the Orthodox faith; and she sent memoranda to the authorities in Pest, explaining what she was doing and asking insistently that the little ones not be taken by the Catholic authorities.

For all her efforts the authorities made her move with the children to Miskolc and raise them under Catholic tutelage, yet she kept a deep hold on them. She gave them Orthodox teaching at home, took them to the Romanian church in the town, and day after day breathed into them the love of God and of the traditions of their people; and that upbringing, built on sacrifice and prayer, told decisively on her son Anastasiu. In 1816 she went as far as Vienna to ask the emperor's help in the fight for her children's faith, and though she never managed to meet him, her unbroken effort was a rare proof of a mother's devotion and of spiritual courage. Even after the authorities gave the children into the Catholic archbishop's care, she stayed beside them and went on forming them in Orthodoxy.

Her sacrifice was answered. All her children, schooled at first in Catholic schools, declared when they were grown that they wished to return to the Orthodox Church; and the public event of 29 December 1826, when Andrei Șaguna formally declared that wish, was reckoned by the historian Ioan Lupaș as important as the day of his birth.

She departed to the Lord in January 1836, fifty-one years old, and was buried on 17 January in the Kerepesi cemetery in Pest, in the Grabovski family crypt. Her son, by then metropolitan, raised over her a stone cross with the inscription: "To his much-loved mother Anastasia, to his most prized brother Vreta, and to his sweet sister Ecaterina. Andreiu Șaguna, Bishop of Ardeal, raises this monument, 1849." She was canonised by the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church in its session of 1 July 2025, under the title "Saint Anastasia Șaguna, mother of Saint Andrei Șaguna the Hierarch", with her commemoration on 1 December.
