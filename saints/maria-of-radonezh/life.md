# Maria of Radonezh, mother of Sergius

The Russian calendar keeps her with her husband in one line — «Прпп. схимонаха Кирилла и схимонахини Марии, родителей прп. Сергия Радонежского» — and the page that carries their life heads itself with her alone: «Мария, мать Сергия Радонежского». What is told of her is told through her son.

Before he was born, at the Liturgy, she and those praying with her heard the child in her womb cry out three times: before the reading of the Gospel, during the Cherubic hymn, and when the priest said «Святая святым». She and [Cyril](/saints/cyril-of-radonezh) felt this as a great mercy, and their piety wanted it answered by something done rather than something felt, so she vowed the child to God as Hannah had vowed Samuel. He was born and named Bartholomew. From his first days he astonished them by fasting: on Wednesdays and Fridays he would not take her milk, nor on any day when she had eaten meat. When she saw it, she gave up meat herself.

The two of them were known for keeping the church's rules strictly, for helping the poor, and above all for the apostle's charge about hospitality to strangers, which they taught their children to keep: never let a travelling monk or a tired stranger pass the door. The page says plainly that no detailed account of their life has survived.

About 1328 they left Rostov for Radonezh, and in old age they went three versts on to the Khotkovo monastery of the Protection, which then housed both men and women, to take the angelic habit — the monastic spirit having reached the parents from the son. They had little time in it: in 1337 they departed in peace, and [Sergius](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) buried them there. On 3 April 1992 the Council of Bishops glorified them both.

*After the Russian church calendar's [28 September](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260928.html) and its [life of the two of them](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life6670.htm); read 4 October 2026.*
