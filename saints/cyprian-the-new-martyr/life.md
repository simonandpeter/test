# Cyprian of Kletzos

Cyprian was born in the village of Kletzos in the district of Litza and Agrafa in northern Greece, and showed a calling to the priesthood early. After he had been ordained, Father Cyprian went to the Holy Mountain, where he grew in the spiritual life and became a worthy monk. But before long he was seized by the desire to be a martyr.

He went to the city of Thessalonica and presented himself before the kadi, where he preached Christ boldly and urged all who were present to give up Islam and follow the divine Nazarene. Those who witnessed the scene thought that Cyprian had lost his mind, and the servants there fell on him and threw him out.

His desire to give his life for Christ did not lessen, and it carried him to Constantinople, where he went boldly into the palace and met the grand vizier Kara Mustafa, to whom he preached the Christian faith. The vizier, taken by surprise, answered by inviting Cyprian to accept the religion of Islam, and got nowhere; so Cyprian was passed on to the mufti, to whom he preached the same saving news.

Losing his patience altogether, the grand vizier condemned him to death, which Cyprian received with joy, and he was beheaded in the quarter of the Phanar in Constantinople, the place to which the Ecumenical Patriarchate had moved in the year 1601. So the hieromonk Cyprian of Kletzos in Agrafa gave his life for the love of Jesus Christ in the city of Constantinople on Sunday, 5 July, in the year 1679. The page prints no troparion for him; it cites Nomikos Michael Vaporis, *Mărturisitori ai lui Hristos* (Sophia, Bucharest, 2002), pages 156–157.
