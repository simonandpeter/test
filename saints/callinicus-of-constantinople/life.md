# Callinicus

Callinicus I succeeded Paul III on the throne of Constantinople and held it from 693 to 705. Before that he had been a presbyter of the Great Church and keeper of the vessels at the church of Blachernae; the chronicler Ephraemius calls him a man distinguished in word and in life.

His patriarchate fell under Justinian II. Wanting to build a lavish fountain beside the palace, the emperor chose the site of a church of the Theotokos and demanded that Callinicus give a blessing for its demolition. The Church has prayers for the raising of churches, Callinicus answered, and none for pulling them down; pressed, he said only: “Glory to you, O Christ, who always bear with us and endure.” In 695 Justinian was overthrown by the general Leontius, mutilated in the Hippodrome and exiled to Cherson. When he came back in 705, brought in by Bulgars and Slavs, the patriarch was his first victim: condemned without trial, blinded, and sent into exile in Rome.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 23 August (Julian). Its life has him first a presbyter at the church of the Theotokos at Blachernae, raised to the throne in 693 after the death of Paul; when the cruel Justinian II demanded a blessing for demolishing the church beside his new palace he answered that he had prayers only for the building of churches, not their destruction, and when it was pulled down cried with tears: glory to you, Lord, who endure all things. Justinian, overthrown, exiled to Cherson and his nose cut off, came back after ten years with an army, swore before the Cross, the Gospel and the Holy Mysteries to harm no one, and breaking the oath at once beheaded the emperor Leontius’s successor and had Callinicus seized, blinded, his tongue and nose cut off, and walled up alive in a stone wall at Rome; after forty days the wall fell away and he was found alive, though barely breathing from exhaustion, and died four days later, in 705. Peter and Paul appeared to Pope John VI in a light sleep and bade him bury him in the church of the Apostles.
