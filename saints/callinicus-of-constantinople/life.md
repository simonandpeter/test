# Callinicus, Patriarch of Constantinople

Callinicus I succeeded Paul III on the throne of Constantinople and held it from 693 to 705. Before that he had been a presbyter of the Great Church and keeper of the vessels at the church of Blachernae; the chronicler Ephraemius calls him a man distinguished in word and in life.

His patriarchate fell under Justinian II. Wanting to build a lavish fountain beside the palace, the emperor chose the site of a church of the Theotokos and demanded that Callinicus give a blessing for its demolition. The Church has prayers for the raising of churches, Callinicus answered, and none for pulling them down; pressed, he said only: “Glory to you, O Christ, who always bear with us and endure.” In 695 Justinian was overthrown by the general Leontius, mutilated in the Hippodrome and exiled to Cherson. When he came back in 705, brought in by Bulgars and Slavs, the patriarch was his first victim: condemned without trial, blinded, and sent into exile in Rome.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 23 August — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/853/saint.aspx); read 23 August 2026.*
