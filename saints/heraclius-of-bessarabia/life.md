# Heraclius of Bessarabia

Heraclius was born on 11 March 1893 at Pojorâta in Suceava, and was given the name Ioan at his baptism. He fought in the front line in the War of Reunification and was gravely wounded, in danger of losing his left hand; but God, the Romanian life says, worked a wonder and healed him. In 1917, when he had been released from captivity, he kept the promise he had made to God while he was wounded and stayed in Bessarabia.

He was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Hârbovăț in the district of Călărași, made a monk in 1920, ordained deacon in 1925 and hieromonk in 1927. From 1926 to 1930 he followed the courses of the Faculty of Theology in Chișinău, where his scholarly, journalistic and spiritual work was noticed and he was valued by its learned professors. His contemporaries knew him as a great orator and a skilled confessor, merciful and generous to strangers, devoted to his mission, a speaker of languages — Russian, Gagauz, German and others — a translator and a publicist, a man who knew the Scriptures, a good singer and a good administrator.

In 1940, when the Romanian administration withdrew, he was appointed vicar of the eparchy and held things in balance until the monastery was dissolved three months later, going then as priest to the parish of Nisporeni. From the parish he sent help to the monks of the monasteries of Noul Neamț and Hâncu, giving them money and food, until the return of the Romanian Army in 1941. After the liberation of Bessarabia he returned to Hârbovăț and took part in its renovation and in bringing back the monks whom the Soviets had driven out.

Between 1941 and 1944 he served as exarch of the monasteries of the Archdiocese of Chișinău, and in March 1944 he was named eparchial vicar for the evacuated regions of the Archdiocese. On 2 August 1945 he was arrested by the Supreme Soviet, and on 17 December 1945 sentenced to eight years of forced labour in Siberia for propaganda, agitation and calls to overthrow the Soviet power, under article 58, paragraph 10 — a form, the life says, by which clergy and monks were condemned without any real accusation being brought, simply because they were Romanian Orthodox and so, by the party's ideology, enemies of the people.

In the camp he went on, like the other clergy, helping and encouraging the deported, hearing confessions and keeping the flame of the faith alive, devoted to the service he had taken on. He did not deny the faith and did not give way under pressure, and that, the life says, is what helped him survive. He came back to Bessarabia in 1953, and fulfilled the obedience of cashier at the monastery of Țigănești and of guide at Noul Neamț until that monastery was closed in 1962.

The last part of his life he spent in the care of a spiritual son in the village of Chițcani, staying near the monastery until he went to the Lord on 16 August 1964. He was buried in the village cemetery under the close watch of the communists. On 19 October 1992 he was rehabilitated by the Prosecutor General of the Republic of Moldova. doxologia.ro prints a sung troparion of his, but no text of one.
