# Gerasimus of Vologda

saint.gr says the venerable Gerasimus of Vologda was born in Russia in the twelfth century. He was an ascetic first at the monastery of Glushen, and afterwards at the monastery of Glinek, near Kyiv; and on the nineteenth of August 1147 he came into the region of Vologda, where he carried on the God-loving and ascetic life he had begun. He fell asleep in peace in the year 1178.

That is the whole of the notice. It gives no place of birth and no year for it, no parents, no elder, no office in either house, no foundation of his own at Vologda, no wonder, no relic, no distich and no hymn; and it does not say why he left Kyiv for the north.

The same Greek day keeps [Gerasimus of the Jordan](/saints/gerasimus-of-the-jordan), a fifth-century ascetic of Palestine, and the two are told in wholly unrelated terms; the corpus keeps four other men of this name besides — of Kefalonia, of Sourvia, of Tismana, and the patriarch Palladas — and none of them is named on this page.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, and here the Сретенский monastery's calendar gives nearly everything the Greek notice withholds. He was tonsured at the Киевская Гнилецкая Успенская обитель, a house which, the longer of the two lives says, grew up at the caves where Феодосий of the Kyiv Caves used to withdraw for the Great Fast; he passed his days there in unceasing labour and deep prayer of the heart, and out of obedience to the brethren he accepted the rank of hieromonk, which is why that type is added here.

Grown to spiritual maturity he went to the north of Rus and came to the river Vologda, on the nineteenth of August 1147, and blessed the settlement that had arisen on its right bank, «прорицая ту быти граду великому», foretelling that a great city should stand there. He chose for himself the dense forest cut off from the settlement by the small Кайсаров brook, built a hut and gave himself to prayer and work, and there he raised a church in the name of the Most Holy Trinity and beside it the first and oldest monastery of the northern parts of Rus, the Троицкий Кайсаровский.

He enlightened the Vologda country with the Gospel, bringing some out of paganism and strengthening others in the faith, and reposed on 4 March 1178, which the calendar notes was the day of his own tonsure and of his name saint. After his death his relics became a spring of healings, and he would appear himself to sick people who had never known him and send them to his tomb with a promise of healing. The day page prints a troparion and a kontakion for him under his full name. In the troparion it prints «по преставлении своем многими чудесы исцелений просил еси», where the sense of the line asks for просиял; the text is copied as the page has it and the English renders the sense, that he shone forth with many wonders of healing.
