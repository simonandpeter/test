# Timothy of Prusa

The city of Prusa is in the parts of Bithynia and borders on Phrygia and Mysia, being near Mount Olympus. Saint Timothy was bishop in that city, and for his purity and holiness he received from God the gift of working wonders. A dragon had nested under a cypress in a cave and was harming the beasts and the people; and Timothy killed it by prayer and by the sign of the holy cross, throwing into its mouth the covering with which the consecrated bread is covered on the divine altar.

He raised an emperor named Aretas, who lay of a very heavy sickness and was near the gates of hell; and likewise he turned a certain empress back from death to life, and brought her out of the darkness of idol-worship into the light of the holy faith. He healed all sicknesses with the gift given him, and did many other wonders by the power of Christ; he lit up his flock with his teaching and turned many of the unbelieving to the knowledge of the true God.

When Julian the Apostate came to the empire — who, having denied Christ, worshipped the idols and began to persecute the Church of Christ cruelly — Timothy laboured the more in preaching the word of God, strengthening the faithful in the faith and rebuking the unbelieving for their error. Julian, hearing of him, sent to seize him and had him thrown into prison; and while the saint sat bound, many came to him, and he taught them about Christ, the true God.

Julian was told of this, that the Christian bishop, even in bonds, was teaching his faith to those who came to him, and sent to stop him preaching the name of Jesus. But Timothy did not obey the pagan and lawless emperor, and went on doing fearlessly what befitted him. When the emperor heard again that he was preaching the name of Christ without fear, he was angry and sent an executioner, who cut off his head in the prison. The faithful took his holy body and buried it with honour; and from his grave healings of infirmities and of sicknesses are given, to the glory of Christ our God. The page gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 June. saint.gr says he was martyred in the years of Julian the Apostate, 360 to 363, and that the synaxaria relate how in 361 he killed a dragon by a miracle, which had its lair between Prusa and the hot waters; the head of the page brackets him «(; - 361)». For the miracle the site quotes Nikodemos the Hagiorite at length: that the saint had power and grace from God by which he worked various wonders, and so put to death a great dragon which lived in a cave on the public road between the hot springs and the city, where a most graceful and beautiful cypress was planted; that the dragon, having in it the malice of the spiritual and man-slaying dragon the devil, came out and poisoned with its breath alone not only the men who passed that way but the cattle and beasts too, so that the road was untravelled and none dared go by it. A Christian queen living at Apollonias in Bithynia came to the district of Prusa; the brethren dwelling nearby put certain blessings and gifts inside a sacred vestment and sent them to her by the hand of Timothy. When he reached the cypress the dragon rose against him; the saint put the blessings into the corner of his mantle, wrapped and tightened that sacred vestment, then slung it by hand upon the dragon and went on his way. Having given the blessings to the queen he turned back, and — the wonder — saw the dragon dead, and glorified God; and taking up the vestment he went to his metropolis. When Julian heard of him he sent an envoy to the bishop to make him deny Christ; and because Timothy stood firm in his faith, Julian had him beheaded. The page adds that a church of Saint Timothy stood within the hospital «ἐν τῷ Δευτέρῳ» of Constantinople.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 10 June, the menologion day both the other calendars keep, and its five lines are the same story with one new reason in it. For his great purity of soul, it says, God gave him the gift of wonderworking, so that he healed every sickness and every affliction among men — where the Greek page grounds the same gift in a different story, the dragon between Prusa and the hot waters, which this book does not have at all.

The rest agrees. Under the wicked emperor Julian, who denied Christ, Timothy was thrown into prison; the faithful visited him there too, to hear wise instruction from their chief shepherd; and when Julian learned of it he ordered the executioner to cut off his head in the prison, in 362, where the Greek page gives 361 and brackets the martyrdom in the reign from 360 to 363. The folder now holds both years, which both fall inside that reign. His soul went to Paradise, the entry ends, and his relics remained wonderworking, helping men and showing forth the power of God.
