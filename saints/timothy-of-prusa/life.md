# Timothy of Prusa

The city of Prusa is in the parts of Bithynia and borders on Phrygia and Mysia, being near Mount Olympus. Saint Timothy was bishop in that city, and for his purity and holiness he received from God the gift of working wonders. A dragon had nested under a cypress in a cave and was harming the beasts and the people; and Timothy killed it by prayer and by the sign of the holy cross, throwing into its mouth the covering with which the consecrated bread is covered on the divine altar.

He raised an emperor named Aretas, who lay of a very heavy sickness and was near the gates of hell; and likewise he turned a certain empress back from death to life, and brought her out of the darkness of idol-worship into the light of the holy faith. He healed all sicknesses with the gift given him, and did many other wonders by the power of Christ; he lit up his flock with his teaching and turned many of the unbelieving to the knowledge of the true God.

When Julian the Apostate came to the empire — who, having denied Christ, worshipped the idols and began to persecute the Church of Christ cruelly — Timothy laboured the more in preaching the word of God, strengthening the faithful in the faith and rebuking the unbelieving for their error. Julian, hearing of him, sent to seize him and had him thrown into prison; and while the saint sat bound, many came to him, and he taught them about Christ, the true God.

Julian was told of this, that the Christian bishop, even in bonds, was teaching his faith to those who came to him, and sent to stop him preaching the name of Jesus. But Timothy did not obey the pagan and lawless emperor, and went on doing fearlessly what befitted him. When the emperor heard again that he was preaching the name of Christ without fear, he was angry and sent an executioner, who cut off his head in the prison. The faithful took his holy body and buried it with honour; and from his grave healings of infirmities and of sicknesses are given, to the glory of Christ our God. The page gives no year.
