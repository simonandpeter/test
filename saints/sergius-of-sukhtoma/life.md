# Sergius of Sukhtoma

Sergius of Sukhtoma, called Stephen in the world, was born in the city of Kazan in Russia of God-loving and pious parents. For three years he travelled as a pilgrim to the holy monasteries of Palestine and of Greece, and at last, after his return to Novgorod, he decided to settle in the monastery of Solovki. In 1603 he was tonsured a monk by the archimandrite Isaiah, who afterwards painted his icon.

Immediately after his tonsure he began a hard spiritual struggle and gave himself to unceasing prayer, and saint.gr says that God endowed him with the gift of foresight. He fell asleep in peace in 1609. The page holds nothing further about him: no monastery of his own, no relics and no hymn.

The Russian calendar gives him two readings and they agree on the shape of his life and differ on one point of it. He was Стефан in the world and was born at Kazan. Both have him strict in fasting from childhood, the first adding that as an infant he took barley wort instead of his mother's milk. Wanting to know the monastic life, he went on foot to Palestine and Constantinople and was on Athos, and spent three years, the second reading says, about the holy places of Palestine and Greece learning the monastic life; then he came home by way of Great Novgorod and the Solovki monastery, preaching the word of God along the whole road and teaching men to live righteously.

In 1603 he came to the Shukhtoma country, now in the Vologda region, and it is here the two readings part: the first has him tonsured in the Череповец Resurrection monastery at the hand of the abbot Исаия and then withdraw to the Shukhtoma house, the second has him take the great schema in that same Череповец monastery from the archimandrite Исаия, who afterwards painted his icon. Having taken it, he laid a hard rule on himself, spending day and night without sleep at prayer on his knees. In his own lifetime he became famous for many wonders and had the gift of prophecy, and he died on 19 May 1609, the day of this feast.
