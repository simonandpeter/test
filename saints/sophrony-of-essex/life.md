# Sophrony of Essex

Sergei Simeonovich Sakharov was born on 23 September 1896 into a family of Orthodox believers in Moscow. As a child, doxologia says, he prayed a great deal and read as much, and he knew the books of Gogol, Turgenev, Tolstoy, Dostoevsky and Pushkin well. Gifted by God with a great talent for art, he entered the Moscow Academy of Arts in 1915 and afterwards the School of Painting, Sculpture and Architecture; for him art was a way of passing beyond present reality and of finding and living the eternal beauty.

He was twice imprisoned by the Bolshevik police in the Lubyanka. Because he did not rightly understand Christian love, which he took to be a narrow thing, at only seventeen he sank into the mysticism of the Eastern religions. In 1921 he left Russia for the West to perfect his art, stayed a short while in Italy and in Berlin, and in 1922 settled for good in Paris, where he exhibited in several shows and drew the notice of the local press.

He came to feel art powerless to express the purity his soul wanted, and could not make his mind understand the mystery of death; and on Great Saturday of 1924 he turned back to Christianity and gave up the Eastern ideas. On the eve of Pascha, after communion, God visited him at last and gave him to behold the uncreated light of His Kingdom. He enrolled in 1925 at the Saint Sergius Orthodox Theological Institute in Paris, and in the same year left his studies and set out for the Holy Mountain.

In 1926, wanting to learn pure prayer, he entered the Russian brotherhood of the monastery of Saint Panteleimon, the Russikon, and became the disciple of [Silouan the Athonite](/saints/silouan-the-athonite), who was then no more than a simple monk. Silouan had no systematic theology, the page says, but lived theology at its highest reaches, and it was through Sophrony that his theology took a systematic form. He was ordained deacon in 1930 by Saint Nicholas Velimirović.

He settled in the end in England, at the monastery he founded in Essex, which by the blessing of Patriarch Alexy of Russia passed under the Ecumenical Patriarchate and was later raised to stavropegion. There he began to publish Silouan's work and to bring out books on his theology: A Monk of Mount Athos in 1973, Wisdom from Mount Athos in 1975, with the elder's sayings and journal notes, then His Life is Mine in 1977 and We Shall See Him As He Is in 1985.

In 1987 the Ecumenical Patriarchate canonised Silouan, and the work God had given Sophrony — to make Silouan known and to guide souls in pure prayer and right faith — was drawing to its end; his brotherhood already numbered twenty-five. He died on 11 July 1993, close to ninety-seven years old.

The law required an underground crypt before he could be buried inside the monastery, and a little before his death, when the brotherhood was anxious at how slowly the building went, he said that he would not die until the crypt was finished. So it happened: he departed on 11 July, at the very time the work was completed, and was buried on the fourteenth.
