# Michael of Chernigov

Michael, son of Vsevolod Olgovich Chermny, was pious and meek from childhood and in poor health, and in 1186, the Russian calendar’s life says, the young prince asked the prayers of Nicetas the Stylite of Pereyaslavl, received a wooden staff from him and was healed at once. In 1223 he sat in the congress of princes at Kyiv on helping the Polovtsians against the advancing Tatars, and after his uncle Mstislav of Chernigov fell at the Kalka he became prince of Chernigov. Novgorod called him to rule it in 1225, which meant peace with the grand prince George Vsevolodovich of Vladimir, whose wife Agafia was Michael’s sister, but he soon went home to Chernigov, telling the Novgorodians that the two lands should be as brothers. In 1227 Metropolitan Cyril reconciled him with Prince Oleg of Kursk, and from 1235 he held the grand prince’s throne at Kyiv. When the Mongols came before Kyiv in 1240 he went to Hungary to rouse King Béla against them, and tried Poland and the German emperor, without success; he came back to the ruined city, lived for a time on an island nearby and then at Chernigov, and in 1245 sent Metropolitan Peter (Akerovich) to the Council of Lyon to call for a crusade against the Horde.

When the khan’s envoys came to count the people and lay tribute, and every prince needed the khan’s yarlyk to rule, Michael was told to go to the Horde. His spiritual father, Bishop John, blessed him to go and to be a true confessor there and gave him and his boyar [Theodore](/saints/theodore-boyar-of-chernigov) the Holy Gifts for the road. Arriving in 1246, they were ordered to pass through a fire and bow to the sun and the fire — the second life says to bow southward to the grave of Genghis Khan, to the fire and to the felt idols — and Michael answered that a Christian bows only to God, the Creator of the world, not to what he made. Batu sent word through Eldega that they would die unless they obeyed; Michael said he was ready to bow to the khan, to whom God had given power over earthly kingdoms, but not to idols. The second life adds that his grandson Prince Boris and the boyars of Rostov begged him to save himself and offered to take the penance on themselves, and that he threw off his princely fur coat, saying he would not destroy his soul, and away with the glory of this perishing world. While his answer went to the khan the two sang psalms and received the Gifts; then he was beaten until the ground ran with blood, trampled, and beheaded by an apostate named Daman, his last words, in the second life, being that he was a Christian. Theodore was killed after him in the same way. Their bodies were thrown to the dogs and kept unharmed for several days until Christians buried them secretly; they were later taken to Chernigov, on 14 February 1572 to Moscow at the wish of Ivan the Terrible, in 1770 to the Sretensky cathedral and on 21 November 1774 to the Archangel cathedral of the Kremlin. Their life and service were written in the mid-sixteenth century by the monk Zinovy of Otensk.

The Russian calendar keeps Michael and Theodore on 20 September old style as martyrs, confessors and wonderworkers; its day prints 1245 and both its lives 1246. Its life names among his descendants his daughter Euphrosyne of Suzdal and his grandson [Oleg of Bryansk](/saints/oleg-of-bryansk), kept the same day. The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, gives the two a line together: Prince Michael went to the Tatar horde with his boyar Theodore at Batu’s call, and because they would not pass through the fire and bow to the idols, as the Tatar custom was, they were beheaded in 1244; their relics, witnesses of their martyrdom, rest in the Archangel church in Moscow.
