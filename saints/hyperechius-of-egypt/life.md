# Hyperechius of Egypt

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives almost no life. The whole of what it prints for these ten men is a single sentence: the holy martyrs Marcian, Nicander, Hyperechius, Apollo, Leonidas, Arius, George, Selenias, Irenaeus and Pambo suffered for Christ and for the faith that is in him, and, being tormented by the ruler of Egypt with hunger, with thirst and with cold, they finished the struggle of martyrdom.

That is all. The page gives no country, no age, no year and no place of death, prints no troparion, and says nothing that tells one of the ten from another. The epithet “of Egypt” in the English name here is this corpus's own, taken from the one thing the page says about a place: that it was the ruler of Egypt who tormented them.

Of Hyperechius the calendar says only his name, which stands third among the ten on its line for 5 iunie. His companions are [Marcian](/saints/marcian-of-egypt), [Nicander](/saints/nicander-of-egypt), [Apollo](/saints/apollo-of-egypt), [Leonidas](/saints/leonidas-of-egypt), [Arius](/saints/arius-of-egypt), [George](/saints/george-of-egypt), [Selenias](/saints/selenias-of-egypt), [Irenaeus](/saints/irenaeus-of-egypt) and [Pambo](/saints/pambo-of-egypt).

The Greek calendar keeps the ten on the same day and gives them one page and one story. saint.gr says they were together a band of Christians who knew not only how to believe but how to suffer for Christ, readily and gladly. They were denounced to the idolatrous eparch of Alexandria and confessed their faith boldly. He hoped in vain that he might frighten at least the women among them into offering sacrifice to the idols; promises and threats broke against the steadiness of the women's faith, and he was quickly taught that the Cross can bring out women braver than men. Then the eparch ordered that they all be put to death by hunger and thirst. Whole days they stayed without food and naked, exposed to the cold of winter, while the soldiers most provokingly ate and drank the best of food in front of them; but they faced the frightful and more-than-human trial with warm and unceasing prayer to Christ, and at the last, men and women alike, they died unshaken in their faith, singing the name of Jesus Christ. The couplet the page prints says the same in two lines: to ten contestants hungering and thirsting God gives delights that are never used up.
