# George of Karatzasou

George, also called Hadji-George, was the son of Christian parents from Philadelphia, who left his home town for Karatzasou near Heliopolis and worked there as a saddler. At a gathering George was at, a drunken man fell and was killed, and the judge, as the custom was, demanded the fine from the townspeople; George refused to pay it and in his anger said that he would not pay because he was a Turk. The Turks seized him at once and circumcised him.

Repenting afterwards, he went to Mount Athos and stayed there for some time, then came back to Karatzasou and boldly confessed his faith in Christ before the judge. The judge’s flattery did not move him, and he bore a long series of dreadful tortures bravely, saying: whatever you do to me, I no longer deny my faith; a Christian I was born, a Christian I am, and a Christian I want to die. He was beheaded on 2 October 1794 — or, by Otto Meinardus’s reckoning, on 2 October 1752.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 2 October.
