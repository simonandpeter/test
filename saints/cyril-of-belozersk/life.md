# Cyril of Belozersk

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 9 июня and prints two readings of him behind one index, a short one and a long one that contains it. In the world he was Kosma, born at Moscow of pious parents. He was orphaned young and lived with a kinsman, the boyar Timothy Vasilyevich Velyaminov, who was okolnichy at the court of the grand prince Dmitry Donskoy; and because the life of the world weighed on the young man, it was at the asking of [Stephen of Makhrishche](/saints/stephen-of-makhrishche) that the boyar let him go to the Simonov monastery, where he was tonsured with the name Cyril.

His obediences there he bore under an elder named Michael, afterwards bishop of Smolensk. The elder read the Psalter through the night while Cyril made his prostrations, and at the first stroke of the bell he went to matins. He asked leave to eat only every second or third day and was refused it: he was blessed to eat every day with the brethren and never to the full. In the bakery he carried water, split wood and handed out the bread, and from there he was moved to the kitchen, where he laboured nine years and said to himself, looking into the fire, "See, Cyril, that thou fall not into the fire that is everlasting." He could not eat bread without tears of thanksgiving.

He was tonsured by [Theodore of Rostov](/saints/theodore-of-rostov), and when [Sergius of Radonezh](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) came to Simonov he would go to Cyril before any of the others and talk with him in love. To escape the esteem of men Cyril took for a while to playing the fool, and when the superior punished the breach of order with forty days of bread and water he bore it gladly; but the experienced elders saw through his hiding and made him take the rank of hieromonk against his will. In 1390, when Theodore was consecrated archbishop of Rostov, the brethren chose Cyril archimandrite of the house.

The rich and the notable then began to come for his teaching, and that troubled him, so that for all the brethren's asking he would not stay superior but shut himself up in his old cell, and when the visitors found him there too he went out to old Simonovo. Reading the akathist as he always did before the icon of the Mother of God the Hodigitria, he heard a voice: "Go to Beloozero; there is thy place." He went into that country, then wild and thinly peopled, and walked a long while looking for the place he had been shown.

By the hill of Myaura near lake Siverskoye he set up a cross with his companion [Therapon of the White Lake](/saints/therapon-of-the-white-lake) and dug himself a cell in the earth. Therapon soon withdrew elsewhere, and for some years Cyril laboured alone underground. Once, heavy with a strange sleep, he lay down under a pine and had barely closed his eyes when a voice cried "Run, Cyril!" — he sprang away and the pine came down, and he made a cross out of its wood. A peasant tried to set his cell alight and could not, however he went about it, and confessed the sin in tears, and Cyril tonsured him a monk. Brethren came from Simonov, and others asking for the habit, and the elder understood that his silence was over; in 1397 he built a church in honour of the Dormition of the Mother of God.

When the brotherhood had grown he gave the house a rule of common life and hallowed it by his own example. Nobody dared talk in church or leave it before the service ended, and they came to the Gospel in order of seniority; each sat in his own place at table and the refectory was silent, and from it each went to his cell without speaking. No letter or gift could be received unshown to him and none written without his blessing. The money was in the monastery treasury and nobody owned anything; the cells were not locked and held nothing but icons and books. Late in his life the boyar Roman offered the house a village with a deed of gift, and Cyril reasoned that if the house held villages there would be cares about land and settlers and the monastic silence would be broken, and refused it.

God gave him foresight and healings. A certain Theodore, who had entered out of love for him, came to hate him so that he could not look at him and was making to leave; he came to the cell and, seeing the grey hairs, could not get a word out for shame, and Cyril said to him, "Grieve not, my brother: everyone is mistaken in me, thou alone knowest the truth and the whole of my unworthiness; I am indeed a good-for-nothing sinner" — and blessed him, and the thought never troubled him again. Once the wine for the liturgy ran out and he had an empty vessel brought to him and it was found full. In the famine he gave bread to all who needed it and it did not come to an end, where the stores were commonly barely enough for the brethren. He stilled a storm on the lake that was threatening the fishermen, and foretold that none of the brethren would die before his own repose though the plague was raging, and that many would follow after him.

His last service he celebrated on the day of the Holy Trinity. Charging the brethren to keep love among themselves, he fell asleep in the ninetieth year of his life on 9 June 1427, which the long reading notes was the day of his namesake «святителя Кирилла, архиепископа Александрийского». In the first year after his death thirty of the fifty-three brethren died, and he often appeared to the rest in sleep with help and instruction. The veneration of him was general not later than 1447 or 1448, and his life was written at the charge of the metropolitan Theodosius and the grand prince Vasily Vasilyevich by the hieromonk Pachomius Logothete, who came to the Cyril monastery in 1462 and found many eyewitnesses and disciples of his still alive, among them [Martinian of White Lake](/saints/martinian-of-white-lake), who was governing the Therapontov monastery then.

He loved learning and planted that love in his disciples: by the inventory of 1635 the monastery counted more than two thousand books, and sixteen of them were "the wonderworker Cyril's own". Three letters of his to Russian princes have come down, and the calendar calls them notable patterns of spiritual guidance, of love, of peaceableness and of consolation. The day page prints a troparion and a kontakion over him.

This is **not** the Cyril this corpus already keeps under the English name of the White Lake. That folder is the calendar's «Кирилл Новоезерский», Cyril of New Lake, who died in 1532 and is kept on 4 февраля ст. ст.; its own Russian row says in so many words that the English name it carries is the name of this man, the better-known Cyril of Belozersk who died in 1427, and calls that a naming question. The two are a century apart, with two lakes, two houses and two days, and nothing here joins them.
