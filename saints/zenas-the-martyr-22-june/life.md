# Zenas (22 June)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 22 iunie reads «Sfântul Mucenic Zina», and his page carries the day, an icon, the epistle appointed for the day and the gospel read with it, and nothing else — no country, no century, no manner of death, no year and no hymn. The English form here is a choice; the calendar prints only Zina.

The one thing the page adds to the name is the title of the icon it shows, «Sfinții Mucenici Zinon și Zina; Sfântul Ierarh Eusebie» — the martyrs Zeno and Zenas with the hierarch Eusebius. The calendar keeps all three on this day, and that is as far as the source goes; nothing here joins them further than the icon's own caption does.

The readings set for the day are 2 Timothy 2:1–10 and John 15:17–27 with 16:1–2, the apostle's charge to endure hardship and the Lord's word that the world will hate His own.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 22 June, and saint.gr gives the life doxologia.ro did not — and with it the thing the Romanian page left open, that the two bare names it printed for the day are a master and his servant. It says the two of them lived at the beginning of the fourth century; that [Zeno](/saints/zeno-the-martyr-22-june) was a rich Christian of Philadelphia in Arabia, rich in piety as well as in money, and that Zenas was his servant, and that what bound them was more a brotherly love, as it binds all the members of the Church. When Zeno, burning to give himself wholly to the Gospel, shared out his possessions among the poor and freed all his slaves, Zenas refused to leave his former master and begged to stay with him, and was kept as a friend and a brother in Christ. In the year 303 the eparch Maximus declared a persecution at Philadelphia, and when Zeno was beaten and imprisoned for confronting him, Zenas ran to the eparch and begged him to set his master free. The eparch imprisoned him too, and a few days later had them both beheaded.
