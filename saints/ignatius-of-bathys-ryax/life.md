# Ignatius of Bathys Ryax

Ignatius came from the second province of Cappadocia and lived in the reigns of Nicephorus II Phocas (963–969) and John I Tzimisces (969–976), the Greek synaxarion says. Given to God from childhood, he went to the monastery of Bathys Ryax — at Triglia, near today’s Mudanya in Asia Minor — and learned the whole strictness of the ascetic life there from the venerable Basil, its abbot and founder. Having reached great heights of virtue he was ordained reader, then subdeacon, deacon and presbyter, and became abbot of the monastery himself, and brought it great progress, both material and spiritual.

When the civil authorities once sought to make use of the monastery’s money, he protected its property by his firm stand. He died on the road near Amorium — or, as others say and the entry thinks more likely, at Armoutli — returning once from Constantinople, and a year later his body was brought back to the monastery he had laboured for so much.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 27 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2519/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026.*
