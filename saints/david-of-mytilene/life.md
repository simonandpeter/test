# David of Mytilene

At the beginning of the eighth century, saint.gr says, there lived at Mytilene Adrian and Konstanto, who had seven children, of whom five became monks; three of those were David, [Symeon](/saints/symeon-the-new-of-mytilene) and their brother George. David was the firstborn, and was born in the year 717 or 718. He learned a little of his letters, and at sixteen, while he was pasturing his father's sheep in a great storm, he saw in a vision Saint Anthony calling him to the monastic life — telling him in so many words to go over to Mount Ida in Asia Minor, which lies opposite Lesbos and a little to the north, and to be a monk there.

David accepted the great Anthony's counsel with much readiness and joy, came to Asia Minor and lived on Mount Ida inside a cave in great asceticism, eating wild herbs; and there he lived thirty years. Again by a vision he was told to go to the bishop of Gargara to be ordained deacon by him and afterwards presbyter. He went back to Mount Ida, and there, at the direction of an angel he saw in a vision, he built a church of the holy Kerykos and Ioulitta and a monastery, in which a great many monks very soon gathered.

Ten years later, his father having died, his mother came to see him, bringing with her the youngest of her children, Symeon, then eight years old; Symeon stayed with his brother, and their mother went back to Mytilene and died a little after. Symeon grew up in David's monastery, became a monk at twenty-two and was ordained priest at twenty-eight; and two years after that David died, at the age of sixty-six, having foreseen his own death and told his brother to go back to Mytilene.

The page gives no year for that death, only the age, and nothing is inferred from it here. It prints no apolytikion and no kontakion for the three brothers.
