# Elisha

Elisha was of the city of Abel-meholah, the son of Shaphat, of the tribe of Reuben. Doxologia's life says that a wonder was given at his birth: the golden heifer at Shiloh, to which the Israelites who had turned back to idols brought their sacrifices, bellowed at the hour he was born, though it had no soul in it, and the sound carried as far as Jerusalem; and a priest filled with the Holy Spirit said that a great prophet of God had been born that day, who would break the carved idols and shatter the mighty. When he was grown he kept his virginity, that he might be the more pleasing to God.

His call came while he was ploughing with twelve yoke of oxen, and Elijah was on Horeb speaking with God, and was sent to anoint him prophet in his own place. Elijah cast his sheepskin over him and told him the will of the Lord; Elisha left everything at once, asking only to take his parents' blessing. He killed the yoke he had been ploughing with, cut up the wooden plough for fuel, roasted the meat and set it before the people who were there, took the blessing of his father and mother, and ran after Elijah and served him.

When God took Elijah up in a whirlwind of fire, he bade Elisha ask what gift he would, and Elisha asked nothing of this world. Elijah told him that if he saw him taken the request would be granted; and as they walked and talked a chariot with horses of fire came between them, and Elisha cried out, "My father, my father, the chariot of Israel and its horsemen!", and wept and tore his clothes, and the sheepskin fell to him. The sons of the prophets at Jericho saw it and said, "The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha." At Jericho, where the water was bad and the ground barren, he called for a new vessel with salt in it, poured it into the springs, and said that the Lord had healed the waters.

The life goes on through the works that came of that gift: the Shunammite woman who made a little upper room for him with a bed, a table, a chair and a lamp, because she knew the man who passed so often through her city was holy; Naaman the Syrian commander, sent by a captive Israelite girl's word, washing seven times in the Jordan and coming up with the flesh of a child; Gehazi, who ran after Naaman's gifts and took Naaman's leprosy; the borrowed axe-head that fell into the Jordan and was raised; the Syrian camp that fled leaving the road strewn with clothes and weapons.

At the end he told the king to shoot an arrow eastwards towards Syria, laying his own hands on the king's, and said the arrow was the Lord's deliverance; then he bade him strike the ground, and the king struck three times and stopped, and the prophet was grieved, saying that had he struck five or seven times he would have trodden Syria down altogether. So Elisha died and was buried with honour. In that same year a dead man was being carried to burial at Samaria when raiders from Moab broke into the land; the buriers threw the body into Elisha's grave and fled, and when it touched his bones the man rose living to his feet and ran into the city.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 14 June, and saint.gr's account gives no year. It says he was the son of the great landowner Shaphat, from the village of Aelmouth or Abelmeola; that we meet Elisha in the Old Testament as the servant of the prophet [Elijah](/saints/elijah-the-tishbite), whom the page sends the reader to find on 20 Ιουλίου; and that when Elijah departed to the Lord in that wonderful manner, Elisha carried on his prophetic work, and with many wonders. As an example it tells the story of the widow's oil: a woman came to him who had just lost her husband, saying that her husband was dead, and that Elisha knew he had feared God, and yet a pitiless creditor of his had now come and, because they had nothing to pay him, was demanding to take her sons as his slaves. Elisha was moved and asked her what she had in her house; she answered, nothing, only one vessel of oil; and he told her to go and borrow from her neighbours as many empty vessels as she could, then to shut the door of her house and, together with her sons, to fill those vessels. The page's hymns keep to the same theme: they call him a true interpreter of the Saviour, say that he inherited the Tishbite's grace and excelled in wonders and in very many mighty works, and that having received a double grace from the Spirit he was shown a wonderful prophet to all the ends of the earth.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 14 June, the same menologion day the folder’s other two rows carry, and sets him about nine hundred years before Christ. When the Lord meant to take the aged prophet [Elijah](/saints/elijah-the-tishbite) to himself, he revealed to him that he had appointed Elisha, son of Shaphat, of the tribe of Reuben, from the city of Abel-meholah, to be his successor in the prophetic office. Elijah told him the Lord’s will, threw his own mantle over him, and asked of God a double prophetic grace for him. Elisha left his house and his kindred at once and followed him.

When the Lord took Elijah up in the chariot of fire, Elisha stayed to carry the prophetic office on, and with greater power than Elijah’s. In purity and zeal he was the equal of the greatest of the prophets, and in the wonder-working power given him by God he surpassed them all. He divided the water of the Jordan, as had been done long before at the Red Sea; he made the bitter water at Jericho fit to drink; he brought water down into dug trenches in the war with the Moabites; he multiplied the oil in a poor widow’s pots; he raised the dead son of the woman of Shunem.

He fed a hundred men with twenty little loaves; he healed the commander Naaman of leprosy, and brought that leprosy down on his own servant Gehazi for the love of silver; he blinded a whole Syrian army and put another to flight; and he foretold many things, both to the people and to single men. He reposed, this book says, in a deep old age, and gives no year for it.
