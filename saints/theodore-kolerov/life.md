# Theodore (Kolerov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar gives the Julian 16 November to him and to the two men killed with him: «Сщмч. Феодора пресвитера и с ним мчч. Анании и Михаила (1929).» Theodore Xenophontovich Kolerov was born in 1882 in the village of Semyonovskoye in the province of Tver, the son of the priest Xenophon Zakharyevich and of his wife Elizaveta Ivanovna. He finished the Tver theological seminary in 1905 and was ordained to the village of Klyuchevoye; in 1911 he was moved to Stolbovo, and a year after that he became rector of the newly built church of the Transfiguration in the town of Kimry.

He was young for the charge and the town took to him quickly. The longer life says he knew every one of his parishioners and knew what ailed each of them in body or in soul; that the wives of hard drinkers would send for him, and that his talk and still more his prayer with the man often held him back; and that he was stopped in the street by people wanting a word and gave them the time. When the first war came he and his wife Anna Mikhailovna led the town’s society for the help of the front, sewing clothing for the army. At Pascha and the great feasts he went round the houses of all his parishioners, and went on going round after the Soviet power had forbidden it.

He loved church singing and brought known singers to the Transfiguration church, and the concerts were as much charity as teaching: Moscow was starving, artists who had lost every means of living were going hungry, and after the singing the parishioners brought them whatever food they had. In September 1919 the provincial Cheka wired every district town to take the more notable inhabitants as hostages; he was among those arrested, on the charge of having organised a spiritual concert without permission. He was let out soon after, but the furniture was taken from him and then a large contribution laid on him under threat of arrest.

In 1922, when word of the renovationists reached Kimry in the newspapers, he went to Moscow to see for himself what the congress of clergy would do. Only members of the «Живая Церковь» group were to be admitted, and to get in he put his name down as one of them — curiosity, says the life, getting the better of good sense. He was made an agent for the Kimry and Kashin districts and held two meetings of clergy, at Kimry on 31 August and at Kashin on 5 September. When the renovationists split at the end of September he was appalled, broke off every relation with them, wrote out his confession, and brought his repentance to the bishop of Tver, who first forbade him to serve and in time lifted the ban.

In 1927 the town council of Kimry petitioned to have the Transfiguration church closed, and in July 1928 the petition was granted; the order itself came in May 1929. He told his parishioners of it and set the last service for 19 May. The church was full. He read the order out after the service and called the faithful to obey it, and they would not: the next day a crowd turned the inventory commission away from the doors and kept watch about the church for three days, as many as three hundred at a time, with young men among them shouting things meant to provoke. He came out to the people more than once and asked them to give up the church before the innocent suffered.

When they dispersed he was arrested, with the church warden [Ananias (Boikov)](/saints/ananias-boikov) and [Michael (Boldakov)](/saints/michael-boldakov); twenty were charged in all. They were brought down the Volga by night on an open barge to Kimry, where the trial opened on 20 October with correspondents of the Moscow papers invited and admission by ticket, so that not one relative of the accused was let into the hall. None of them admitted guilt. On 27 October five were sentenced to be shot — the priest, the warden, Michael, and two members of the church council whose sentences were afterwards cancelled.

He was moved to the Taganka prison in Moscow while the appeal ran, and allowed to see his family daily. On 20 November he was told the sentence stood, and he wrote in the margin of the canon book he had with him «20/XI, 21/XI, 22/XI — плач три дня», three days’ weeping; at three in the morning of the night of the 23rd he wrote «Читаю акафист Иисусу Сладчайшему». The morning papers of 29 November announced that the three had been shot, and he was still alive: that evening he was given a last visit with his wife and his son. When the guard came for him he wrote on the first page of the canon book «29/XI — 11 ч. ночи», and at eleven that night he was shot.

The two hosts grade him differently and no office is written here. The calendar’s line calls him simply «пресвитер» and azbyka.ru heads its page «Священномученик Феодор Колеров, пресвитер», while the Sretensky life is titled «Священномученик протоиерей Феодор Ксенофонтович Колеров» and the calendar’s own name link «Феодор (Колеров), протоиерей, сщмч.» — archpriest. He was made a priest of the Russian church in 1905, which both hosts say, and that much is written in `types`. He was glorified as a locally venerated saint of the Tver diocese in 1996 and for the whole church at the council of August 2000.
