# Patermuthius the Egyptian

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery prints two entries for 9 июля ст. ст., each naming a Patermuthius and a Coprius, and they are four different men. The fourth entry is his: «Прпп. Патермуфия и Коприя Египетских (V).» — the venerable Patermuthius and Coprius of Egypt, of the fifth century — where the third is «Прмчч. Патермуфия , Коприя и мч. Александра (361-363).», the venerable-martyrs under Julian the Apostate. Each of the four has a name page of its own, and each page gives this one day.

His reading is three sentences. «Патермуфий сначала был язычником и атаманом шайки разбойников, но потом раскаялся, крестился и удалился в пустыню.» — he was a pagan at first and the chief of a band of robbers, and then he repented, was baptised and withdrew into the desert. The whole of the rest of his life he gave to serving the sick and burying the dead, and for his labour and his ascetic striving he received from God the gift of working wonders.

What is known of him is known because somebody wrote it down: [Coprius](/saints/copres-the-egyptian), a presbyter, was an eyewitness of his labours and set down his life and his wonders, and told his own life in turn to the presbyter Rufinus, who passed it to Palladius the bishop of Helenopolis, who put the story in his Lausiac History. So the calendar's two sentences about him come at third hand and say so, which is more than most of its lines do.

He is not the Patermuthius of the day's other entry, who was an Egyptian hermit beheaded under Julian two hundred years before, and whose folder is held in this corpus from the Greek calendar's 17 December. The calendar tells the two apart by one abbreviation — «прп.» on his name page 1745, «прмч.» on the other's 1744 — and by nothing else, the forename and the epithet being the same.
