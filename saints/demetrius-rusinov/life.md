# Demetrius (Rusinov)

Ананьевского, Константина , Димитрия , Нестора , Феодора , Константина , Виктора , Илии , Павла пресвитеров, Иосифа диакона и прмч. Алексия (1937).» One year stands for all eleven, «Сщмчч.» heads the ten clergy and «прмч.» the monk who closes the line, and the grades it prints sort them into a bishop, eight presbyters, a deacon and a hieromonk. The name link behind his place supplies the calendar's own nominative with the surname the line leaves out, «Димитрий (Русинов), протоиерей, сщмч.», and that is where the name form in this folder is read from.

**days.pravoslavie.ru gives this company nothing but that line.** Each of the eleven name pages carries a title, a list of days and no text at all, and not one of them has a `/Life/` link of any kind; the day page's hymns are the two martyrs' of 284–305, the venerable Matrona's, the Mother of God's before the Скоропослушница icon and the wonderworker of Aegina's, and nothing is sung under any of these eleven names. The life below is azbyka.ru's, read in the in-app browser because that host answers no fetcher, and the paragraphs are this corpus's own.

He was born in 1871 in the village of Kurkino in the Moscow country, the son of a priest who was afterwards given many church awards for long and diligent service and raised archpriest himself. In 1893 Дмитрий Владимирович finished the Moscow theological seminary. Where he served after his ordination is not known; the witnesses' depositions say that until 1924 he served in the church of the Three Hierarchs at Kobyakovo in the Ozery district of the Moscow region.

When that church was closed he was moved to the church of the Transfiguration of the Lord at Boyarkino in the same district, and was raised archpriest. He was arrested on 28 October 1937 on a charge of counter-revolutionary agitation and held in the prison at Kolomna. Told that the investigation had information that he was carrying on such agitation among the people of the two villages he had served, he answered «Виновным в проведении контрреволюционных разговоров я себя не признаю».

A troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot on 19 November 1937. He was shot on 21 November 1937 and buried in an unmarked common grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow. **The calendar keeps him on 9 ноября ст. ст., which is the civil 22 November, and his life has him shot on 21 November 1937** — the day before, 8 ноября old style. The difference is one day and not the thirteen of the style conversion, so it is neither a second feast nor an arithmetic slip that could be read away: five of the eleven died on the 22nd and six on the 21st, and the calendar holds all eleven on the one day. The attestation's day is the day page that listed him, as the brief requires, and the death above is the date his own life gives.

**He is given no relation to the ten others the line names.** Four of them — the company of Semipalatinsk — are tied to one another by their own pages and to nobody else; of the six that are not, his is one of five lives that end at Butovo on 21 November 1937 without naming each other, under sentences passed by troikas sitting on different days over different cases. A shared line, a shared class, a shared year and even a shared grave are not a company unless a page sentences the men together, and none of these pages does.
