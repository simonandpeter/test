# Job of Moscow

saint.gr's calendar for 19 June names Job, metropolitan of Moscow, and its page calls him the first patriarch of Moscow. He was born about 1530 at Staritsa, the page says, and fell asleep in 1607; and then it adds that we have no other details of the saint's life. Under the heading it notes that the relics of the saint are in the cathedral church of the Dormition of the Mother of God in the Kremlin.

The day's line gives him the rank of metropolitan and the page's own sentence gives him that of patriarch, and both are recorded here as the source has them. The page also links a separate commemoration, the translation of the relics of Saint Job metropolitan of Moscow, which belongs to another day and is not this entry. Beyond the two years, the birthplace and the relics there is nothing in it: no epigram, no hymn and no account of his patriarchate.

**The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 5 October with the synaxis of the hierarchs of Moscow**, seventeen names in one line under one polyeleos service, marked «(Моск. Свт.)» and not expanded: Peter, Theognostus, Alexis, Cyprian, Photius, Jonah, Gerontius, Joasaph, Macarius, Philip, Job, Hermogenes, Tikhon, Peter, Philaret, Innocent and Macarius — two and a half centuries of metropolitans, then the patriarchs, then the metropolitans of Moscow and Kolomna, ending with a man shot in 1937. He is the eleventh of them and the first patriarch on the line. His index entry carries three further days — 19 June for his repose, 5 April for the translation of his relics, and «26 января (интронизация)», his enthronement, which for the first patriarch of Moscow is the feast one would expect it to keep.

**The same calendar keeps a second commemoration of him on the Julian 5 April, and it is the translation of his relics**: «Перенесение мощей свт. Иова , патриарха Московского и всея Руси (1652).». His name page gave that day without a year, and the day page gives the year — 1652, when the relics were brought from Staritsa to the Dormition cathedral in Moscow. A translation of relics is a feast and not a folder, and one row per church is the schema, so the 5 October row stands and this day is recorded in its note. **What the day does add to the folder is his service**: it prints a troparion in глас 5 and a kontakion in глас 7, both of them his own, where saint.gr printed no hymn at all.

Behind the line is a Russian life, and it is fuller than the Greek one this folder was written from. Patriarch Job was «в миру Иоанн», born in the thirties of the sixteenth century at Staritsa in the province of Tver, to townspeople; his boyhood was spent in the Dormition monastery there, where his father had given him to be brought up, and there he was tonsured and given the name Job. About 1569 «царь Иоанн Грозный» visited the house, noticed the monk, and soon had him raised archimandrite. He was superior of the Simonov Dormition monastery in Moscow from 1571 to 1572 and of the Novospassky from 1575 to 1580, bishop of Kolomna in 1581, archbishop of Rostov the Great in 1586 and metropolitan of Moscow in 1587.

He was named patriarch on 23 January 1589 in the presence of «патриарха Константинопольского Иеремии» and solemnly installed on 26 January — the day his name page keeps as «интронизация». He served the liturgy every day and knew the Gospel, the Psalter and the Apostle by heart; in his days, his biographer wrote, no man was found like him, in appearance or in manner or in voice or in order or in walk or in question or in answer. With the death of «царя Феодора Иоанновича» in 1598 the male line of the Rurikids ended and the Time of Troubles began.

Old and ill, the patriarch wrote denunciatory letters naming the pretender by his real name, the runaway monk «Григория Отрепьева», an impostor; they sobered many, but he had already secured the support of Poland and the Vatican by promising to bring in the union. In January 1605 the patriarch anathematised him and the traitors who held with him. On 13 April 1605 «царь Борис Годунов» died suddenly; Moscow rose, and the city was given up to the pretender and the Poles. The patriarch refused to swear allegiance and was deposed.

The pretender’s servants broke into the Dormition cathedral of the Kremlin to kill him. He was praying at that moment before the Vladimir icon of the Mother of God; they tore the episcopal vestments off him and would not let him finish the liturgy. He suffered many insults and was exiled to the Dormition monastery at Staritsa, and at the end of his days he blessed «митрополита Казанского Ермогена» for the patriarchate. Two years later, on 19 June 1607, he reposed and was buried in that monastery; in 1652 his relics were translated to Moscow, to the Dormition cathedral, which is the commemoration this day keeps. He was glorified in 1989.
