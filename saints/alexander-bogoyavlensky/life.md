# Alexander (Bogoyavlensky)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him ninth of fifteen on 21 октября ст. ст., in a line of eleven priests, two deacons and two monks of 1937. He was born in 1879 in the village of Kukarevo in the Ostashkov uyezd of Tver province, the son of a psalm-reader, Lev Bogoyavlensky. He finished the theological school and was ordained priest in a year the calendar's life says is not known. In the persecution of the late twenties he was arrested for not paying a tax set impossibly high, which served its purpose twice over: the priest was taken and the church, left without one, fell silent.

He was sentenced to two years in a corrective labour camp and came out of it a thoroughly sick man, and still did not leave the service of the altar. In March 1937 he was appointed to the St Nicholas church in the village of Gryadtsy in the Toropets district of the Tver region. Within two months it was plain that this church too would be closed, and he set about assembling the twenty — the body of laymen that alone could legally hold a church and deal with the state. He went from house to house to make the list, because a meeting in the church itself would at once have been called an illegal assembly aimed at overthrowing the government.

Being new in the village he did not know everyone, and asked those he did not know whether they believed in God; if they said they did, he asked them to join. At the end of September an NKVD officer, collecting what was needed for the arrest, questioned the men whose posts made them afraid to contradict him, the collective farm's chairman and brigade leader. They testified that a plenum of the village soviet on 27 September had resolved that the priest's preaching, his services, his going round the houses of the village and the hamlets about it, his baptising of the newborn and burying of the dead created an unsettled mood that stood in the way of the state's campaigns.

The officers then read the list of the twenty and went to each member in turn, to press at least some of them into leaving it and declaring that the priest had enrolled them by deceit. Three signed such statements, saying they had never been members and would not answer for the church. The chairman of the parish council, broken by the investigators' threats, gave up the church's defence at his interrogation and signed what they wrote for him: that the priest had come to his house in May and made him sign, that he had not known he was being put down as chairman and did not wish to be a member at all, and that the priest had told him that if he did not believe God would punish him.

He was arrested on 9 October and taken to the prison at Toropets, and on 11 October was interrogated. He answered that he had conducted no agitation against the Soviet power: living at Gryadtsy, he had held the service in the church as a priest and gone round the villages to baptise the newborn and bury the dead. The investigator read out the collective farm officers' statements that he had called for taxes in kind not to be paid and had spoken of a war that would end in the government's defeat. He would not admit it, saying that when he met people in the villages he had spoken only of praying to God; and he denied distributing leaflets, saying that when he came to Gryadtsy the parishioners had told him such leaflets had gone round the place before him.

The case was closed the same day and sent to the NKVD troika, which condemned him on 1 November. He was shot two days later, on 3 November 1937, and was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000. The day page prints troparia and kontakia for other saints it heads and nothing under his name.
