# Acacius the New of Neochorion

Acacius was born at Neochorion in Macedonia, near Thessalonica, in the eighteenth century, the eldest child of a family of Bulgarian peasants, and was baptised Athanasius. When he was nine the family moved to the town of Serres, and he was apprenticed to a shoemaker who beat him often. One night in Holy Week, after a hard beating, he was wandering the streets when two Muslim women comforted him, took him home and fed him; and, showing him pity, they urged him to leave Christ, the bread that came down from heaven. They brought the boy to Yusuf Bey, who adopted him, gave him a Muslim name and had him circumcised, and he lived nine years in that house.

At first Yusuf Bey's wife treated him with a mother's love, but the love turned later into a carnal desire. As Joseph the martyr refused the advances of Potiphar's wife, the life says, so Athanasius refused hers; and she told her husband that Athanasius had tried to force her. His Turkish father threw him out of the house, and the young man went back to Thessalonica to find his true parents. His mother told him it was too dangerous for him to stay, and he went to the Holy Mountain.

He lived a while at Hilandar and then at other monasteries. He confessed his apostasy to Father Nicholas of the monastery of Xenophontos, who read the appointed prayers over him and received him back into the Church by chrismation. He returned to Hilandar for a year and then went to Iviron, and it was while he was living at Iviron that he learned of the martyrdom of [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-of-dimitsana) and Ignatius. The middle of the life was not read through here.

At the end he was seized, beaten and thrown into prison, having trodden on what was set before him and spat on it; that night he was promised riches and high place if he returned to Islam, and when he refused they began to beat him again. The next day he was brought before the vizier and told his story, and was taken back to prison, where Father Gregory managed to have a small box containing the Holy Mysteries brought to him, and he communicated. Soon after, he was taken to the place called Parmak Kapi and beheaded. He gave his life for Christ on 1 May 1816, at six in the evening; devout Christians bought his body back from the Turks, Father Gregory carried it to the Holy Mountain, and the relics were buried at Iviron in a church dedicated to Saints Ignatius and Euthymius.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's notice adds the childhood and the letter. He was Athanasius in the world, born in 1792 at Neochori, today Asvestochori of Thessalonica; his parents moved to Serres in 1805 for a living and handed the nine-year-old to a shoemaker to learn the trade, and it was the man's cruelty, and two Ottoman women who watched it and promised the boy a better life, that brought him to deny his faith on a Good Friday. His stepmother then fell in love with him as Potiphar's wife did with Joseph, the page says, and when he would not yield she slandered him to his adoptive father, who drove him out; and so he got back to his own parents at Thessalonica, who had left Serres as soon as they heard of his apostasy.

On the Holy Mountain he came at last to the Skete of the Forerunner, into the company of the elder Nikephoros, who gave him as a novice to the elder Acacius to be prepared for martyrdom, as he had prepared the monk-martyrs [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-of-dimitsana) and Ignatius before him. Tonsured and renamed Acacius, he set out for Constantinople on 10 April with the monk Gregory, who had gone with those two as well, and he walked to the martyrdom with joy. Before leaving he wrote to his elder and his brethren: he asked their prayers, told them he had arrived in the reigning city on 24 April, and bade them, when they heard of his blessed end, to thank the Lord and keep the whole week in gladness of soul — «Αὔριο λοιπόν Παρασκευὴ 28 Ἀπριλίου μέλλω νὰ κινήσω εἰς τὸν δρόμον τῆς ἀθλήσεως καὶ εἴθε οἱ ἅγιες εὐχές σας νὰ μὲ βοηθήσουν».

On Saturday 29 April he received the Mysteries, put on Turkish clothes, and confessed before the court that he had come back to the faith of his fathers; and he was imprisoned, flattered, threatened and tortured, and kept his mind. The Communion that the captain's brother carried in to him secretly, with the monk Gregory's blessing, from the church of the Panagia Kataphiane, strengthened him for the end, and he was put to the sword in 1816 at a place called Daktyloporta. On the third day Gregory bought the body back with money collected from the grocers of Galata and brought it by way of Prinkipos to the little harbour of Iviron on 9 May, and it was buried at the Kalyve of Saint Nicholas in the chapel of the monk-martyrs Euthymius and Ignatius, before the icon of the Mother of God, as he had wished. His head is now at the monastery of Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain and part of his relics at the Skete of the Forerunner. The Church keeps the three of them together at that skete on 1 May.

The Serbian church keeps him on this same menologion day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells him in five sentences. He was of the village of Neochori near Thessalonica, it says, and it was being much beaten by his own master at Serres that made him turn Turk. Afterwards, as a penitent and a monk, he lived at Hilandar; and his poor but Christ-loving mother counselled him: „Као што си се добровољно одрекао Господа, тако сад мораш добровољно и храбро примити мучеништво за слаткога Исуса“ — as you denied the Lord of your own will, so now you must of your own will and bravely receive martyrdom for sweet Jesus.

The son obeyed his mother and, with the blessing of the fathers of the Holy Mountain, went to Constantinople, where he was beheaded by the Turks on the first of May 1816; and his head is kept in the monastery of Panteleimon. **That year and that day are doxologia's own, to the day and the city**, so nothing in this folder's dates was touched; this book is simply a second one behind the 1816 that page argues for against the printings of 1815.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day with the two other new martyrs of the Holy Mountain, and the life it serves is his, told in the manner of the Prologue. At nine years old he was given to a shoemaker to learn the trade, who beat him daily; and wishing to be free of a visible tyrant he fell into the claws of the invisible one. Weakened by the beatings he was comforted by two Ottoman women, whose cunning kindness brought the boy, who had lost both his will and his patience, to deny Christ. He spent the next nine years in a rich house in every comfort and luxury, and at eighteen he went to Thessalonica and left the Muslim faith.

Blessing him to withdraw to the Holy Mountain his mother said to him: since thou hast of thine own will denied Jesus Christ, so must thou with boldness confess him before the Turks — and the account says that the pious mother did not spare her son's body to save his soul, and that he wished the same himself. Practising ascesis in the monasteries of Chilandar and Iviron he learnt of [Euthymius of Dimitsana](/saints/euthymius-of-dimitsana) and [Ignatius of Stara Zagora](/saints/ignatius-of-stara-zagora), who had suffered not long before, and wished to become like them.

When his time came he dressed in Turkish clothes, went to the Ottoman Porte, stepped onto the porch of the court of judgment and confessed Christ the true God, cursed Mohammed, tore the green band from his head and trampled it before them all. He was beheaded. The life closes by saying that the holy heads of the three are now kept in the Russian house of the Great Martyr Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain.
