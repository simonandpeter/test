# Acacius the New of Neochorion

Acacius was born at Neochorion in Macedonia, near Thessalonica, in the eighteenth century, the eldest child of a family of Bulgarian peasants, and was baptised Athanasius. When he was nine the family moved to the town of Serres, and he was apprenticed to a shoemaker who beat him often. One night in Holy Week, after a hard beating, he was wandering the streets when two Muslim women comforted him, took him home and fed him; and, showing him pity, they urged him to leave Christ, the bread that came down from heaven. They brought the boy to Yusuf Bey, who adopted him, gave him a Muslim name and had him circumcised, and he lived nine years in that house.

At first Yusuf Bey's wife treated him with a mother's love, but the love turned later into a carnal desire. As Joseph the martyr refused the advances of Potiphar's wife, the life says, so Athanasius refused hers; and she told her husband that Athanasius had tried to force her. His Turkish father threw him out of the house, and the young man went back to Thessalonica to find his true parents. His mother told him it was too dangerous for him to stay, and he went to the Holy Mountain.

He lived a while at Hilandar and then at other monasteries. He confessed his apostasy to Father Nicholas of the monastery of Xenophontos, who read the appointed prayers over him and received him back into the Church by chrismation. He returned to Hilandar for a year and then went to Iviron, and it was while he was living at Iviron that he learned of the martyrdom of [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-of-dimitsana) and Ignatius. The middle of the life was not read through here.

At the end he was seized, beaten and thrown into prison, having trodden on what was set before him and spat on it; that night he was promised riches and high place if he returned to Islam, and when he refused they began to beat him again. The next day he was brought before the vizier and told his story, and was taken back to prison, where Father Gregory managed to have a small box containing the Holy Mysteries brought to him, and he communicated. Soon after, he was taken to the place called Parmak Kapi and beheaded. He gave his life for Christ on 1 May 1816, at six in the evening; devout Christians bought his body back from the Turks, Father Gregory carried it to the Holy Mountain, and the relics were buried at Iviron in a church dedicated to Saints Ignatius and Euthymius.
