# Kuksha of Odessa

Kuksha was born Cosmas on 12 (25) January 1875 at Arbuzinka in Nikolaev province, to devout parents, Cyril and Kharitina. In 1895 he went with pilgrims to the Holy Land, and after half a year in Jerusalem stopped at Athos on the way home; in 1896 he came back to the Mountain as a novice of the Russian monastery of St Panteleimon, was tonsured a rassophore as Constantine and, on 23 March 1904, a monk as Xenophon. In 1913 he became a monk of the Kyiv Caves Lavra. At fifty-six he fell so ill that he was thought to be dying, and on 8 April 1931 he was tonsured into the schema with the name of the hieromartyr Kuksha, whose relics lie in the Near Caves. He was ordained hierodeacon on 3 April 1934 and hieromonk on 3 May, and after the Lavra was closed served in Kyiv, at the church in the Voskresenskaya Slobodka, until 1938.

Then came the camps. At sixty-three he was felling timber, fourteen hours a day on scant and bad food, and in the spring of 1943, his term served, he was freed on the feast of St George and sent into exile in a village near Kungur. The exile ended in 1947, and he went back to the Kyiv Caves. In 1951 he was moved to the Pochaev Lavra, where he kept the wonderworking icon while monks and pilgrims venerated it, and everyone who came tried to make their confession to him. On 19 July 1960 he came to the Dormition monastery at Odessa, where he spent the last four years of his life. In October 1964 he fell and broke his hip, lay on the cold, damp ground, caught pneumonia, and died on 11 (24) December 1964.

Azbyka’s summary counts eight years in camps and exile, and calls him an elder, a clairvoyant and a consoler of thousands, glorified by many wonders after his death. The Sretensky calendar keeps him on 16 September old style as a venerable confessor, and has no life of its own.
