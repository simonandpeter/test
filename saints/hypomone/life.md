# Hypomone

Hypomone is one of those saints whom the imperial and the ruling families gave to the Orthodox Church. By the name she bore in the world she was Helena Dragaš Palaiologina, daughter of Constantine Dragaš, ruler of the Slavs, and wife of the Byzantine emperor Manuel II Palaiologos. The name Hypomone, which means “patience”, she received with her tonsure into the monastic life, after the emperor Manuel's passing.

She was born about 1372 into the family of Constantine Dragaš, one of the rulers and heirs of the Serbian kingdom of Stefan Dušan; many of her forebears, the Romanian page says, were saints. She was brought up in the Byzantine manner, and besides her secular education was raised in the Orthodox faith of her fathers, showing a deep faith early, which guided and lit her through a life full of trials.

She was about nineteen when she married Manuel II Palaiologos, in 1390, only a few days before he became emperor. Her new life proved full of trials from the beginning, given the critical political state of the empire; but she passed through it all by the grace of God, with unshaken faith. As empress she loved the people, and was like a mother whom anyone might approach for comfort and for a word that builds up the soul; everything she did was accompanied by prayer. The philosopher George Plethon writes that she was known for her wisdom and justice, and showed “intelligence and courage, more than any other woman of her time”.

She was faithful to her husband Manuel, and lived thirty-five years at his side, the two of them working the virtues in words and deeds. God gave them eight children. Of the six sons two came to the imperial throne, John VIII and Constantine XI, the last emperor of Byzantium; Theodore, Demetrios and Thomas became rulers of Mistra, and Andronikos of Thessalonica. The two daughters died young. She raised the eight in the love of Christ, planting in them by prayers and tears a love of virtue, and three of them became monks: Theodore and Andronikos were tonsured at the monastery of the Pantokrator, and Demetrios at Didymoteicho.

The emperor Manuel left the throne to his son John VIII and, two weeks before his death, was tonsured a monk at the Pantokrator with the name Matthew. After his death the empress became a nun, in 1425, in the monastery of Saint Martha, and there received the name Hypomone. She lived in that monastery twenty-five years; and though she had been empress she helped in all the work of the house with the other nuns, making herself obedient in everything to the Queen of Heaven.

She founded a house for the old which bore the name “The Hope of the Hopeless”, within the monastery of Saint John, where the relics of Saint Patapios of Thebes lay — a saint to whom she bore a particular zeal. God ordered it that she should not live to see the last tragic moments of the empire, calling her to himself on 13 March 1450, three years before Constantinople fell to the Ottoman Turks. Her son Constantine XI, the last Byzantine emperor, died at the fall of the city on 29 May 1453.

She was buried in the monastery of the Pantokrator in Constantinople. After the Turks took the city in 1453, Angelis Notaras, a nephew of hers, moved the relics of Saint Patapios to a cave on Mount Geraneia, at Loutraki in Corinth; an icon of Patapios was painted in that cave with Hypomone beside him, the painter marking in that way the honour she had borne him, and her head was brought there too. In 1952 father Nektarios Marmarinos founded on that spot the monastery of Saint Patapios at Loutraki, where her honoured head is kept to this day. doxologia prints no hymn for her.
