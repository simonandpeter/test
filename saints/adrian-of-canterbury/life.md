# Adrian of Canterbury

Adrian came out of Africa, and was abbot of the monastery of Nerida, which stood at Naples in Italy. When the see of Canterbury fell vacant, Pope Vitalian — whom saint.gr dates 657 to 672 — offered it to him twice, and twice he refused it, pointing instead to the Greek monk [Theodore of Tarsus](/saints/theodore-of-tarsus-archbishop-of-canterbury) in Cilicia as the man fit for it. The Pope agreed, on the one condition that Adrian should go with Theodore to Britain as his fellow worker.

When the two of them had arrived, Adrian was appointed master of the school of Saint Augustine at Canterbury, and the school was raised up under his hand. He drew a great many pupils to himself, and he taught them theology, Greek, Latin, poetry and astronomy.

Marked out for his virtues and for the spirit that was in him, he fell asleep in peace in the year 710. saint.gr prints no hymn for him.
