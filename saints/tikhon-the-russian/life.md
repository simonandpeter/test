# Tikhon the Russian

Doxologia.ro keeps him on 10 September, and the life it prints is the one [Paisios the Athonite](/saints/paisios-the-athonite) wrote of his own elder. He was born in Russia, at Novaia Mihailovsk, in 1884, and was called Timotei in the world; his parents, Pavel and Elena, were devout people, and the page says it was natural that their child should inherit their reverence. They saw his zeal but would not bless him for the monastery while he was still strong-limbed and high-spirited, wanting him to grow in judgement first, and gave him leave instead to visit monasteries for three years, from seventeen to twenty.

In those years he passed through some two hundred monasteries of Russia. Worn out with walking, he would still avoid their hospitality so as not to burden anyone. In one province, where the bread was rye and smelt foul to him, he could eat nothing at all and grew desperate; he drew aside and prayed with a child's simplicity that the Mother of God would help him, for he would die on the road before he became a monk. A young woman with a shining face appeared, gave him a white loaf and vanished.

He went back to thank the baker, who had already turned him away, and was cursed for mocking: the man had neither wife nor daughter. Long after, a monk showed him a book of the wonderworking icons of Russia, and when he came to the Mother of God of the Kremlin his heart leapt and his eyes filled, and he said, «Aceasta mi-a dat pâinea cea albă» — this is the one who gave me the white bread. From that time, the life says, he felt her near him as a child feels its mother.

After Russia he went to venerate Sinai, where he stayed two months, and then to the Holy Places, living for a while as a hermit beyond the Jordan. He found no stillness there, the page says, because the worldly spirit of the age had reached even the desert and the holy places, and so he was obliged to leave for the Holy Mountain. His first obedience was at the Kelli of Bourazeri, where he spent five years; finding no quiet there either, for the many Russian pilgrims, he took a blessing and went to Karoulia.

Fifteen years he lived as a hermit at Karoulia, between Kavsokalyvia and Saint Anne's, in hard asceticism. His only occupations were prostrations and bows made with the prayer «Doamne Iisuse…» and spiritual reading; he borrowed books from the monasteries, and the fathers there gave him as a blessing the rusks left over from their tables, for which he remembered them in his prayers. From Karoulia he came to the edge of Kapsala, above Kaliagra, to a kellion on the land of Stavronikita, where he nursed an old man and, when the old man died, stayed on alone with his blessing.

The grace in him made him known, and the grieving came to him for counsel. Others begged him to be ordained, so that he might help them further through confession and give them absolution, and it was that need, the life says, and nothing of his own wanting, that made him consent. His kellion had no church and he had no money, so he prayed and set out for Karyes; before he arrived, the elder of the Skete of the Prophet Elias called him over and gave him dollars a good Christian in America had sent for a monk with no chapel of his own.

He dedicated the little church to the Holy Cross, both out of reverence for it and to avoid a patronal festival, since on the Exaltation one fasts and the day is a day of mourning. He kept the dry diet of his youth into old age, thinking cooking a waste of time; when he could no longer rise from a prostration he tied a thick rope to the ceiling and pulled himself up by it. He communicated daily, and at the Cherubic Hymn, the life says, he would be caught away. He dug his own grave and would not have it opened.

In 1968 he foresaw his death and spoke of it constantly. After the Dormition he took to his bed and drank only water. On the afternoon of 7 September 1968, the eve of the Nativity of the Mother of God, he said to Paisios that they would have love unto the ages, that the disciple should pray from here and he from heaven, and that he hoped for God's mercy because in sixty years of monasticism he had never stopped saying «Doamne Iisuse Hristoase, miluiește-mă!». Three days later, on 10 September, he reposed. The page prints no troparion for him.
