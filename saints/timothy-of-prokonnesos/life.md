# Timothy of Prokonnesos

saint.gr says that Timothy lived in the middle of the sixth century, in the reigns of Justin the Thracian and of his nephew Justinian the Great. On account of his great virtue he was made bishop of Prokonnesos, or Proikonnesos, which today is called Marmaras; and his pastoral duties he carried out excellently through his gentleness and through prayer.

Once, the page says, he even healed the daughter of the emperor Justinian of a demon. He reposed in peace on the first of August. Later the empress Theodora, as a token of gratitude to the saint, built a monastery in his name at the place where his holy relic was found; and near there, the page adds, a spring of holy water was found as well. The couplet of the synaxarion puts his byname in two lines: «Τὸν Τιμόθεον, τὸν Προκοννήσου θύτην, / Τιμᾷ Θεὸς πρίν, νῦν τε θαυματουργίαις» — Timothy, the priest of Prokonnesos, God honoured before and honours now with wonder-workings. The page prints no hymn.
