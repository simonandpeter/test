# Attalus the Wonderworker

saint.gr says he shone in the monastic ranks and took care to know the law of God so as to keep it with every attention and exactness; that he knew how to worship in truth and to pray in spirit; and that he was exceedingly merciful and exceedingly temperate. He never ate twice in the day, and it was his pleasure to eat once in two days. In sleep he was more temperate still. The bed, he used to say, is a kind of coffin, and so more hours of sleep are more hours of deadness.

And while his ascetic life and his look were so hard, there nested within him a very tender soul: every morning the birds came to him without fear, and together they sent up the dawn's doxology to God the maker. God gave Attalus the power of working wonders too, and his prayer healed the sicknesses of bodies and of spirits. When he had a foreknowledge of his death he asked, while he was still living, for the last kiss from his fellow monks, and death found him in the midst of a spiritual light. The page's couplet says that if Attalus was a wonderworker while he lived it is no strange thing that he is a wonderworker afterwards, and dust alone.
