# Haggai

doxologia.ro prints a short notice of him, and this is the whole of it. Haggai was of the tribe of Levi, born in Babylon in the time of the captivity of the Jews. While still young he came up from Babylon to Jerusalem, and prophesied together with the prophet Zacharias for thirty-six years, four hundred and seventy years before the incarnation of the Lord Christ. He prophesied of the people's return from the captivity, and saw in part the building of the temple renewed by Zerubbabel after the return from the captivity of Babylon.

Then he reposed in peace and was buried with honour near the graves of the priests, since he too was of the priestly family. The page adds a description of him: he was bald and old, with a round beard, honoured and renowned for virtue, and loved and honoured by all as a great and glorious prophet. It closes by saying what his name means, that it is interpreted as feast, or as the one who keeps the feast.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr sets him in his book. It calls him the tenth of the minor prophets, says his prophetic book is divided into two chapters and is plain and severe in style, and makes him the man who, together with the prophet Zacharias, rekindled the zeal of the Jews for the rebuilding of the temple of Solomon. Because he came of a levitical family, it says, he was buried when he died in the graves of the priests. And it prints what he counsels the priests, out of his own second chapter: so it is with all the works of their hands, and whoever touches there shall be defiled — a priest, the page glosses, who is defiled by bribes taken early in the morning, or by other sins and disobediences, will defile the altar if he touches it, and his offering will be reckoned a defilement.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, prints the same figure for his prophesying that the Romanian life does — four hundred and seventy years before Christ — and adds what neither other calendar gave. He was born in Babylon, it says, during the captivity of Israel, and he was of the tribe of Levi. He visited Jerusalem as a young man.

There he urged Zerubbabel and Jesus the priest to rebuild the temple of the Lord at Jerusalem, and prophesied to that temple a greater glory than Solomon's former one: "The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former, saith the Lord of hosts." The book cites the verse as Haggai 2:9 and gives its reason, that in that new temple the Lord the Saviour would appear. He lived to see one part of the temple built by Zerubbabel; and he died in old age, and was laid with his fathers.
