# Macarius

Macarius was born in Constantinople and lost his parents as a boy. He read the word of God with such zeal and was so filled with it that he resolved to give his whole life to God, and entered the monastery of Pelecete in Bithynia, whose igumen was then the famous ascetic Hilarion; after Hilarion’s death the brethren chose Macarius igumen with one voice. In the reigns of Leo V the Armenian (813–820) and Michael II the Stammerer (820–829) he suffered as a confessor for the veneration of the holy icons, and was exiled to the island of Aphousia, where he died around 830.

Doxologia's life, which keeps him on 1 aprilie, adds that he was called Christopher before he came to Pelecete and laid that name down for Macarius; that God healed incurable sufferings through him and sent rain from heaven at his prayer, so that crowds came to him and went home mended in body or in soul or in both. Patriarch Tarasius of Constantinople sent for him to heal the patrician Paul, whom the physicians had given up, and then his wife as well, and blessed him afterwards and made him a servant of the Lord. It names Leo the Armenian, who gave the icons to fire and water and sent the patriarch Nicephorus into exile, as the emperor who put him to torture and kept him in prison to the end of that reign, and Michael the Stammerer, who brought him out, coaxed and threatened him through others, could not bend him, and banished him under guard to the island of Aphusia, where he worked miracles and departed to the Lord.
