# Theodore Trichinas

Theodore, called Trichinas, which is to say the Hairy, was born and brought up in the city of Saint Constantine, the son of rich parents. Leaving his parents, his wealth and the honour of his house for God, he went into a hermit monastery and became a monk. His life there was so hard, and he so weakened his body, that his face was like a dead man's: every night he stood at prayer and fought the frost, and he never covered his head.

He wore nothing but a rough garment of hair, and from this he was called the Hairy; and the hermit monastery in which he kept that severe life was afterwards called Trichina, the Hairy, after him. He received from God power over the demons, and having worked many miracles he departed to the Lord.

He worked them after his departure as well, for from his holy relics there flowed a sweet-smelling myrrh of healing, by which, the page says, all sicknesses were cured and the demons driven away. No year is given for his birth or his death.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's account turns the hair shirt outward. Theodore came from Constantinople, born of rich and devout parents, and from a young age followed the monastic life in the monastery which was called after him the monastery of Trichinas; he was named Trichinas because he wore garments of hair. His life was frugal and full of self-restraint, the page says, for the sake of giving as much as he could to those who suffered, and he gave his whole life to the service of the poor and the sick.

He lived among deprived people whose needs were great, and so he urged many rich men to lay out what goods they could; many of them gave him money and necessities in quantity, and he distributed them with much discernment — first to those in greatest need, to orphans, to poor widows and to sick heads of families. He did good, it says, until the last day of his life. At night he always found time for prayer and for study. He fell asleep in peace and received from God the grace that his tomb should well up fragrant myrrh, so that those who ran there in faith and reverence received health of soul and of body.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells the same life in a few sentences that fix both his birth and his by-name. He was a man of Constantinople and the son of rich parents; while still a young man he left his parents, his home and his wealth, and settled in a desert monastery in Thrace. There he gave himself to the heaviest labours: he slept on stones, so as to have less sleep, went always bareheaded, and clothed himself in a single garment of haircloth — and for that garment he was called Трихин, the Hairy, or Кострет, Haircloth.

For his great self-mortification for the salvation of his soul, the entry says, God endowed him with a great gift of wonderworking, both in his life and after his death; he reposed in peace about the year 400, and his body was shown to be myrrh-streaming. That „око 400. год.“ is the first year this folder has carried, because neither of the calendars read for it before gave him one.

The Russian calendar adds a family, a province and a reason for the name. He was born, it says, into a rich Constantinopolitan house, and in his youth withdrew to a desert monastery in Thrace and took the monastic habit. He was a strict faster and wore nothing but one coarse, prickly hair-shirt, and from that he was surnamed Трихина, the Hair-shirted; the monastery in which he struggled took the same name from him. In his lifetime he worked many wonders and healings, and after his death a healing myrrh flowed from his holy relics which cured many sicknesses and drove out unclean spirits.

One thing the page says plainly and it is worth recording against the year on its own line: «Время жизни преподобного Феодора неизвестно» — the time of this saint’s life is unknown. The line that heads him on the day page closes with «(400)», the same approximate year the Ohrid Prologue gives him, and the life behind that line declines to give any. The interval on this folder was not narrowed and was not widened either, and both readings stand here side by side.
