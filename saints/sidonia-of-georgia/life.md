# Sidonia of Georgia

The Romanian calendar keeps Sidonia of Georgia, and its page for her carries the story from the Georgian Paterikon as Abiathar told it to Saint Nina. When Herod reigned in Judea, the Jews of Mtskheta and all Kartli heard that Persian nobles had come to Jerusalem seeking a newborn king of David’s line, had found him in a cave at Bethlehem and brought him gold, frankincense and myrrh.

About thirty years later Abiathar’s great-grandfather Elioz had a letter from Annas, the high priest at Jerusalem, saying that the one the Persians had worshipped was grown and preached that he was the Christ, and must die by the law of Moses. Elioz’s mother, a devout old woman of the line of the high priest Eli, let him go but begged him not to join those who meant to kill him. Elioz was present at the Crucifixion, and at that hour in Mtskheta his mother felt blows in her heart like a hammer driving nails, cried out that the kingdom of Israel had fallen, and died.

Elioz got the Lord’s tunic from the soldier who had won it by lot and brought it to Mtskheta. When he showed it to his sister Sidonia she kissed it with tears, pressed it to her breast and fell dead. No one could take the garment from her hands, not even king Anterkie, who came with his courtiers when he heard; Elioz buried his sister with the tunic and hid the grave so well that no one knows it to this day. Some say it lies in the middle of the royal garden, where a cedar that sprang up of itself after her death draws many who believe it holds great power. Nina, hearing the story, went by night to pray by the cedar, and was shown in visions that the place was holy and would be glorified. The page gives no year.

*After doxologia.ro, [Sfânta Sidonia și Cămașa lui Hristos](https://doxologia.ro/sfanta-sidonia-camasa-lui-hristos), after the Patericul georgian (Egumenița), pp. 27–29; read 17 September 2026.*
