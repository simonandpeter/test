# Philosophos Ornatsky

saint.gr says the hieromartyr Philosophos Nikolayevich Ornatsky lived in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. In 1885 he finished the theological academy of Saint Petersburg and married Helen Zaozerkov. He was ordained presbyter and worked as a pastor, building up an enormous work of charity and mission, and he was joined in spirit to Patriarch Tikhon. Through the first world war he stood beside the wounded soldiers and their families.

His two sons were officers. [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-son-of-philosophos) served with senior rank in the ninth battalion of the Russian army, and [Boris](/saints/boris-son-of-philosophos) had been appointed head of the twenty-third artillery brigade and fought bravely on the Austro-Hungarian front.

After the revolution of 1917 the hieromartyr Philosophos was arrested, on 9 August 1918, together with his sons, by men of the state security, who took them to the prisons of Kronstadt. There they were executed by shooting, and so gave the witness of their faith in our Lord and God. That is the whole of the page: it gives no year of birth for any of the three, records no wonder, and prints no hymn.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 31 мая ст. ст. with his two sons and gives him a long life, where the source this folder was made from gave a few lines. He was born on 21 May 1860 at the churchyard of Novaya Yerga in the Cherepovets district of the Novgorod province, in the family of a village priest; one of his brothers was married to a niece of the righteous Иоанн of Kronstadt. He studied at the Kirillov theological school and then at the Novgorod seminary, and in 1885 finished the St Petersburg Theological Academy with the degree of candidate of theology. That summer he married Елена Заозерская, daughter of a former subdeacon of the metropolitan Исидор, and was soon ordained priest.

From 1892 to 1912 he was rector of the church attached to the Expedition for the Preparation of State Papers. For twenty-six years he was chairman of the Society for the Spread of Religious and Moral Enlightenment in the Spirit of the Orthodox Church, and withstood the anti-church currents of the time successfully. In 1893 he was elected to the St Petersburg city Duma for the clergy and served until 1917; he had a part in setting up night shelters, orphanages and almshouses in the city, and by his efforts twelve churches were raised in St Petersburg and round it, the largest of them the church of the Resurrection of Christ by the Warsaw station. Having ten children of his own, he lived very modestly: none of the many public offices he bore for the glory of God brought him any means, and though huge sums passed through his hands as chairman of church-building committees he gave private lessons to feed his family. He was known as editor and censor of several of the capital’s spiritual journals, and for nearly twenty years he was the spiritual son of Иоанн of Kronstadt, who often came to his house and blessed all his undertakings, and who trusted him to carry his own correspondence with Феофан the Recluse.

In 1913 he was made rector of the Kazan cathedral in St Petersburg. During the First World War he gave up his flat for a hospital for wounded soldiers and moved with his family into a small official lodging, and went out himself more than once to the fighting districts with transports of what the soldiers needed. His elder sons went to that war: Николай, born in 1886, a military doctor with the Ninth Russian Army, and Борис, born in 1887, a staff captain of the 23rd artillery brigade and a graduate of the Konstantinovsky artillery school, who fought on the Austro-Hungarian front. His gift for preaching drew those who looked for a living word, and he called his flock more than once not to take in the dissolving ideas of Bolshevism, and told the intelligentsia «Нашей интеллигенции надо стать русскою» — our intelligentsia must become Russian.

During the revolution the husband of his wife’s sister, the archpriest Пётр Скипетров, was shot before his eyes, and he preached at the funeral and rebuked the Bolsheviks fearlessly. In January 1918, when that priest was killed in the Lavra, he organised the defence of the holy things of the «Александро-Невской Лавры», bringing processions of the cross to it from every church in the capital. On 9 August 1918 he was arrested with his two elder sons, and at the arrest he was altogether unmoved and calm; his parishioners gathered in a crowd of many thousands and went along the Nevsky Prospect to the Cheka on Gorokhovaya demanding their pastor’s release, and the Chekists received a delegation of the faithful and promised treacherously to do what they asked. That same night he was moved to the prison of Kronstadt. About 30 October 1918, the calendar says — it gives the date as a guess — he was taken with his sons and thirty other imprisoned officers to be shot, and read the prayers for the departing aloud over the condemned on the way. Where it happened the calendar does not know: at Kronstadt by one guess, by another near the Gulf of Finland between Ligovo and Oranienbaum, and the bodies, it seems, were thrown into the gulf. The three were numbered among the saints at the jubilee council of August 2000.
