# Asclas of the Thebaid

Asclas — the page gives his name in three forms, «ή Ακλάς ή Ασκαλάς» — came from the Thebaid in Egypt. He worked most actively for the Christian faith, and for that he was denounced to the prefect Arrianus. When he refused to sacrifice to the idols his sides were opened and his wounds were burned with lighted torches; and when even so his resolve stayed unbending, Arrianus had a great stone tied to the martyr's body and threw him into the waters of the Nile, where he met his death.

The couplet the synaxarion sets over him plays his Egyptian tongue against the fiery tongues of Pentecost: «Aιγύπτιος την γλώσσαν Aσκλάς πυρ φέρει, Δι’ ένθεον κήρυγμα γλωσσών εμπύρων» — Asclas, an Egyptian in tongue, bears fire, for the God-inspired preaching of tongues of fire. The page gives him no year, no emperor and no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 20 May, spells his name Askalon — which is the third of the three forms the Greek page prints — and gives the wonder that page leaves out. He suffered at Antinoe, a city of Egypt, in the time of Diocletian. He was flogged, scraped and burned with candles, and stayed unshaken in the faith to the end. When his torturer Arrianus was crossing the Nile by boat, Asclas stopped the boat by prayer in midstream and would not let it move until Arrianus had written that he believed in Christ as the one and almighty God; but Arrianus put the wonder down to magic, forgot what he had written, and went on tormenting the man of God.

At the last they tied a stone to Asclas’s neck and threw him into the Nile. On the third day the Christians found his body on the bank with the stone still at its neck — as the holy martyr had foretold them before his death — and buried it honourably, in 287. With him, the Prologue says, suffered the holy martyr Leonid. And their torturer Arrianus repented afterwards, believed in Christ with his whole heart and began to confess his faith openly before the pagans; and then the pagans killed him too, so that Arrianus, the one-time tormentor of Christians, was himself counted worthy of a martyr’s crown for Christ.
