# Cosmas of Arabia

saint.gr's calendar for 17 October keeps five saints on one line — «Άγιοι Κοσμάς, Δαμιανός, Λεόντιος, Άνθιμος και Ευπρέπιος οι Ανάργυροι» — and Cosmas is one of them. The page behind the line gives each of the five a heading of its own, his being «Αγιος Κοσμας ο Αναργυρος», sets three epigrams over them, and then prints a single account for all five together.

They were brothers of one another, that account says, in the flesh and in the spirit too, from Arabia — the other four being [Damian](/saints/damian-of-arabia), [Leontius](/saints/leontius-of-arabia), [Anthimus](/saints/anthimus-of-arabia) and [Euprepius](/saints/euprepius-of-arabia). Devout, all five of them, inspired by the same divine zeal and moved by the same love of man, they had not only done many good works by their art of medicine but had also drawn many to the Christian faith. They were denounced for it and arrested, in the persecution under Diocletian, in Lycia, by Lysias the governor at Aegae; and after many tortures they underwent death by beheading.

Of Cosmas in particular the page records nothing apart from that: no year, no birthplace of his own, no order among the brothers, and no hymn. He and Damian share the page's first epigram, which says that it knows these divine champions, the Unmercenaries, as Arabs by race and by the sword. So this folder holds what the calendar holds — a name, a company of brothers, a trade, a province and a day — and the five stand as five folders because the calendar names them one by one.

These are the unmercenaries of **Arabia**, and they are not the pair the Church keeps on 1 July nor the pair she keeps on 1 November: [Cosmas](/saints/cosmas-of-rome) and [Damian of Rome](/saints/damian-of-rome) are one pair, [Cosmas](/saints/cosmas-of-asia) and [Damian of Asia](/saints/damian-of-asia) another, and these five brothers out of Arabia are a third company on a third day.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 17 Οκτωβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/10/17/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2540/saint.aspx), which the five share; read 1 October 2026.*
