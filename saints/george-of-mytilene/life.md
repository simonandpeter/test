# George of Mytilene

George loved Christ from his youth, took the monastic life, went through every virtue and gained a humble mind as no one else did. He became a confessor of Christ in the reign of Leo the Isaurian, bearing persecution and torment from the fighters against the icons; and afterwards, in the reign of the right-believing Constantine and Irene, he was raised to the episcopal throne of the island of Lesbos, in the city of Mytilene. He was very merciful, feeding the hungry with a full hand; and for his great abstinence he was made a companion of the angels and shown to be a worker of miracles, healing incurable sicknesses and driving out unclean spirits.

When the reign of Leo the Armenian came, who renewed the war on the holy icons, he suffered persecution for them again, being by then in deep old age. Before that reign and that renewal and his own banishment, certain signs were given in the city which showed beforehand the danger and the trouble that were to come on the Church of Christ. Once, in the holy church of the Great Martyr Theodora near the harbour, at the singing of Vespers, while the people were singing Lord have mercy, the holy cross that stood on the holy table was lifted from its place by an unseen power with a great noise and rose to the vault; then it turned its point downward and fell to the ground.

The people were seized with fear and dread, and lifted their eyes and hands and cried Lord have mercy for a long time, and would not leave the church, expecting a sudden destruction to come on the island of Lesbos. There were present then the venerable Symeon and his younger brother George — that younger George afterwards inherited the throne of this elder one — and Symeon, who had foresight, said to the people with weeping that it would not be as they feared, and that God would not destroy this land utterly.

When the emperor began to press the bishops to come over to his heresy against the icons, George the metropolitan of Mytilene was called with the rest and showed himself a brave soldier of Christ. Many consented to the emperor's bad faith; he, with a rich wisdom, put the emperor to shame, and made the false patriarch Theodotus, who was called Kassiteras, and the other heretics with him, see their own delusion.

The emperor and the false patriarch could not bear his reproofs and banished him to Cherson, setting in his place as metropolitan a certain heretic who, like the boar that comes out of the wood, bit and spoiled the vineyard of Christ, trampling the holy icons and troubling the rational sheep like a beast.

George passed the rest of his days in that exile and worked many miracles by the grace of Christ. When the hour of his death came, a very bright star rose in the sky, announcing his blessed end beforehand; and it was seen in the island of Lesbos too, so that his own rational sheep in Mytilene knew of their shepherd's going to the Lord. God glorified with a bright star the end of one who in his life had been a light to the world by his good works; and after his end, springs of wonderful healings flowed from his relics.

The calendar line calls him bishop of Mytilene and the life calls him metropolitan of the island of Lesbos in the city of Mytilene; both stand in the source. The address of the life page on that site spells the see «Militinei», which is the Romanian for Melitene in Cappadocia and not for Mytilene, but the text of the life names Lesbos and Mytilene throughout, and that is what is followed here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and adds the years and the relics. saint.gr heads him «(; - 821)» and says he lived in the time of the iconoclasts, was brought up in great piety, and from a young age was marked out by his humility and his almsgiving, so that his good works made him a spiritual lamp which the Great Church of Christ set on the lampstand that was the see of Mytilene. He justified the choice, the page says, in the brightest way: every work of the see — the charitable, the enlightening and the teaching — had him at its head, and he laboured to hold up the mind of Orthodoxy and to win back those of his diocese whom iconoclasm had led astray. His struggles were crowned with success, and when he died in 821 his flock honoured him in deep mourning.

Under the patriarch [Methodius of Constantinople](/saints/methodius-of-constantinople), whom saint.gr dates 842 to 847, and after the restoration of Orthodoxy, the relics of many saints who had died in exile were brought home — among them those of [Theophylact of Nicomedia](/saints/theophylact-of-nicomedia), [Theodore the Studite](/saints/theodore-the-studite) and Nicephorus of Constantinople. In the years 846 and 847 George's own body was carried to Mytilene with great honour, and the page quotes his Life for it: all the inhabitants of the island of Mytilene, with the presbyters and the whole clergy, came to where the body of our venerable father and confessor George lay, kept vigil the whole night through, and on the next day took the body with psalms and hymns and brought it to their own island, and laid it with the rest of the fathers, giving glory to the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit.

A reverent tradition kept to this day, the page adds, holds his burial place to be the spot called «Τρία Κυπαρίσσια» (Σαρή Μπαμπά), near the chapel of John the Forerunner. In the eighteenth century his hand was still preserved and honoured at Mytilene; it is the right hand kept now at Skalochori, bearing the inscription «Ἅγιος Γιόργις». saint.gr also keeps his memory again on 1 February, together with that of his brothers Symeon the new stylite and David the monk — a second day the schema's one row per church cannot hold, so it is noted and not held.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and tells it in one short paragraph, with a few things of its own. It was for the great virtue he had gained by long ascetic struggle, it says, that this George was chosen and set as metropolitan of Mytilene, and he governed the flock of reasonable sheep zealously and wisely into his old age. When the persecution arose under Leo the Armenian, who trampled on the holy icons, the old man was called by the emperor himself to Constantinople, to a council of bishops which was to abolish the veneration of the icons at the emperor's wish; and he not only refused to do what the malicious emperor wanted but rose up, with some other brave bishops, in defence of the holy icons. For that he was mocked and banished by the emperor into the region of Cherson, where he passed the remainder of his earthly life in bodily want and in every kind of hardship. He was a wonderworker both in his life and after his death, the Prologue adds, for his great righteousness and for his love towards the Lord Jesus, and it dates his passing to the immortal life at about the year 816 — where the Greek page this folder's date is built on says 821. Neither year is chosen here and the date this folder already holds was not touched.

The life gives the shape of the whole career in five sentences. He led a monastic life from his youth, perfecting himself above all in the virtue of humility of mind; under the emperor the page dates 716 to 741 he suffered persecution from the iconoclasts and received the name of Confessor.

Under the emperor it dates 780 to 797 he was raised to the hierarch’s see of the city of Mytilene on the island of Lesbos. His life shone with chastity and purity and was like the life of angels; he had the gift of wonderworking, drove out unclean spirits and healed incurable diseases, and was marked by mercy, helping all who were in need liberally. At the end of his life, in 815, under the iconoclast emperor the page dates 813 to 820, the archpastor was driven out and exiled to Chersonesus, and there he died after 820. **In the hour of his death a bright star shone out in the sky over the city of Mytilene.**
