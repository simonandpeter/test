# Pachomius (Kedrov)

saint.gr says that the holy hieromartyr Pachomius, Peter Kedrov in the world, was born on 30 July 1876 in the town of Yaransk in the province of Vyatka, and was the elder brother of the holy hieromartyr [Abercius](/saints/abercius-kedrov); their father was the hieromartyr [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-kedrov), whom the same Greek day keeps. Peter was humble and gentle by nature and loved the Church greatly, and after his general studies he went to the theological academy of Kazan, when Bishop Antony Khrapovitsky was rector there.

He was of such simplicity, the page says, that he resolved to fulfil the Lord's commandment literally — that if a thing as precious as your right eye scandalises you, you should pluck it out and throw it away, since it is better to lose one member than that the whole body be cast into hell — and so one night he tried to burn out one of his eyes with a candle. The burn was so serious that surgery was needed.

In 1899 he entered the ranks of the clergy, and in 1916 he was elected bishop of Starodub in the province of Chernigov, and the following year raised to be archbishop of Chernigov. Under the Soviet regime the authorities tried to arrest him one day when he was to serve the Liturgy, but the crowd of Christians prevented them. He had the habit of staying a long while in the sanctuary after the services were done, and it was there that they arrested him the first time; and the thing was repeated many times, and it was this continual nightmare that began to undermine the peace of his soul.

An atheist commission of scientists was to pronounce on the holiness of relics, which they often threw away, and he lived through the scene of their examination of the relics of Saint [Theodosius of Chernigov](/saints/theodosius-of-chernigov) — except that when they were opened he let nobody come near. The authorities took the relics and put them in the museum, and from there the Christians carried them off secretly; and for that Pachomius was arrested. Freed in 1923, he took refuge at the monastery of Saint Daniel in Moscow, where he was arrested and, after being shut in the Butyrki prison, sentenced to three years' exile in a camp for dissidents. In 1927 he signed with his brother the letter against the state religion; he was arrested again and exiled to Kuzema, freed, and taken again in 1930, on charges of revolutionary activity, of inciting priests to resistance and of church activity. His martyric end came in 1937.
