# Paphnutius of Borovsk

The Romanian life begins with Batu, the godless khan of the Tatars, who came into the land of Rus, took its great cities with the multitude of his forces and laid the land waste with sword and fire, and set over it governors of his own people, called in the Polovtsian tongue baskaks. Paphnutius came of that stock: his grandfather was one of those baskaks and stayed to live on the holding entrusted to him in Rus. When Batu died and the sons of Rus were relieved of their fear, the princes took back their own inheritances.

As the peoples called by the name of Christ gathered again in the cities and multiplied, the holy faith began to shine and the Hagarene paganism to fail, for the right-believing princes of Rus forbade the Hagarene governors to come near the Christian faith or to put Christians to death. Then Paphnutius' grandfather received the holy faith and was baptised with the name Martin. Living in the right faith he had a son named Ioan, who when he came of age married a maiden named Fotinia; and their child, baptised Parthenius, is the saint of this day.

They lived on the paternal inheritance in a small village called Cudinov, about three stades from the town of Borovsk. The long body of the life — his tonsure, his abbacy and his miracles — was not read through here, and nothing is set down from it.

He died in 1477, on the first day of May, an hour before sunset. His face, the life says, was not as the dead are, but shone like a light, and all who looked at him thought he was sleeping alive; and there was great weeping among the brothers, who were then ninety-five. The next day, at the first hour, they buried him as he had counselled, so that none of the laity should know of his end and his burial and no disturbance be made in the monastery. When his death was heard of in Borovsk and the towns and villages round about, clergy and laity ran to his house to venerate the relics, and learning on the road that he was already buried, some turned back grieving and most came to the grave and bowed there from morning to evening.
