# Paphnutius of Borovsk

The Romanian life begins with Batu, the godless khan of the Tatars, who came into the land of Rus, took its great cities with the multitude of his forces and laid the land waste with sword and fire, and set over it governors of his own people, called in the Polovtsian tongue baskaks. Paphnutius came of that stock: his grandfather was one of those baskaks and stayed to live on the holding entrusted to him in Rus. When Batu died and the sons of Rus were relieved of their fear, the princes took back their own inheritances.

As the peoples called by the name of Christ gathered again in the cities and multiplied, the holy faith began to shine and the Hagarene paganism to fail, for the right-believing princes of Rus forbade the Hagarene governors to come near the Christian faith or to put Christians to death. Then Paphnutius' grandfather received the holy faith and was baptised with the name Martin. Living in the right faith he had a son named Ioan, who when he came of age married a maiden named Fotinia; and their child, baptised Parthenius, is the saint of this day.

They lived on the paternal inheritance in a small village called Cudinov, about three stades from the town of Borovsk. The long body of the life — his tonsure, his abbacy and his miracles — was not read through here, and nothing is set down from it.

He died in 1477, on the first day of May, an hour before sunset. His face, the life says, was not as the dead are, but shone like a light, and all who looked at him thought he was sleeping alive; and there was great weeping among the brothers, who were then ninety-five. The next day, at the first hour, they buried him as he had counselled, so that none of the laity should know of his end and his burial and no disturbance be made in the monastery. When his death was heard of in Borovsk and the towns and villages round about, clergy and laity ran to his house to venerate the relics, and learning on the road that he was already buried, some turned back grieving and most came to the grave and bowed there from morning to evening.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr adds the dates of his offices and the name he was born with. He was Parthenios in the world, it says, and his father was called John; at twenty he left his father's house secretly for a monastery, and in 1414 he was tonsured at the Pokrovski Vysotski house of Borovsk and named Paphnutius. When the abbot died he was chosen in his place, and in 1426 he was ordained presbyter by Photius, metropolitan of Kyiv. At fifty-one he fell gravely ill, gave up the abbacy and received the great angelic habit; and having recovered, on the feast of the Great Martyr George, 23 April 1444, he left the monastery altogether for the banks of the river Protva, where other monks followed him and a new house grew up, its first care being a stone church built to the Nativity of the Mother of God.

He kept the poorest cell and ate little and plainly, the page says, and out of the monastery's work he chose the heaviest: cutting and carrying wood, digging and watering the garden. What marked him above all was his love of the Church's services. He foresaw his death, prayed a last time, blessed his brethren and fell asleep in peace in 1477; and his relics are kept in the monastery that bears his name in the region of Kaluga in Russia. The page's own opening sentence puts him in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, which none of the years it then gives will bear.

The Serbian church keeps him on this same menologion day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him in four sentences which disagree with the other two accounts three times over. He was the son of a Tatar magnate, it says, who later received the Christian faith — where the Romanian life makes the baskak his grandfather, and where the Serbian sentence leaves it open whether the magnate or the son is the one who came to the faith. In his twentieth year Paphnutius was tonsured, and he lived in the monastery until his ninety-fourth year, when he fell asleep in the Lord.

He was a virgin and an ascetic, this book says, and for that reason also a great wonderworker and a seer of what was hidden. **It ends him in 1478**, where doxologia gives 1477 to the day and the hour and the Greek page gives 1477 as well: two books against one, and neither year chosen, so his death now reads 1477 or 1478. Nothing was computed from the ninety-fourth year, which will not sit with a tonsure in 1414 at the age of twenty and which is also the number the Romanian life gives for the brethren who wept at his burial.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day and fills out the family and the two monasteries. He was born in 1394 in the village of Кудиново near Borovsk and named Парфений at his baptism; his father Иоанн was the son of a Tatar tax-gatherer named Мартин who had been baptised, and his mother was called Фотиния. At twenty he left his father's house and in 1414 was tonsured with the name Пафнутий in the monastery of the Protection of the Mother of God at Высокое. When the superior died the brethren chose him abbot, and about 1426 the metropolitan of Kyiv ordained him priest. He spent thirty years in that house as its superior and its confessor.

In his fifty-first year he fell gravely ill, gave up the abbacy and took the great schema; and when he recovered he left the monastery on 23 April 1444 with one monk and settled on the left bank of the river Протва where the Истерьма runs into it. Brethren gathered quickly, a stone church of the Nativity of the Mother of God was raised in place of the first wooden one, and the best iconographer of that time, Дионисий, worked on its painting with his helpers. His own cell was the poorest of all and he took the worst of the food, eating nothing at all on Mondays and Fridays and keeping to dry food on Wednesdays; of the common work he chose the heaviest, cutting and carrying wood and digging and watering the garden, and he came first to the services.

To this house there came a young man of twenty, Иоанн Санин, whom he tested and then tonsured with the name Иосиф — [Joseph the Wonderworker of Volokolamsk](/saints/joseph-the-wonderworker-of-volokolamsk), whom he blessed for that labour. A week before his death he foretold his end, and having said his last prayer and blessed the brethren he departed to God on 1 May 1477. The account closes by calling him a follower of «преподобный Сергий, игумен Радонежский».
