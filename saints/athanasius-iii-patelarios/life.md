# Athanasius III Patelarios

He was born of God-fearing parents towards the end of the sixteenth century in the town of Rethymno on Crete, and was given the name Alexios at his baptism. From an early age, the life says, he laboured to keep the commandments of the Gospel that Paul the Apostle had first preached on Crete, and read the Lives of the Saints until he burned to follow them. After his father's death he was tonsured at the Sinai metochion at Herakleia — the page allows also the monastery of Saint Catherine on Sinai — and took the name Athanasius.

Wanting a harder life, he spent a while at a monastery in Thessalonica, where he was ordained deacon, and then went to the Holy Mountain, to Esphigmenou, or Xeropotamou, where his obedience was to serve at the refectory. He ate only bread and water, passed the nights in prayer, slept a few hours, and lay summer and winter on the bare ground. After a pilgrimage to the Holy Land and to Sinai he became known for the gift of preaching and for his theological learning.

An old Greek writing, the page says, puts him in Wallachia about 1626 as a teacher, and there he translated the Psalter into the popular Greek. The Patriarch of Constantinople, Cyril Loukaris, called him to the City to support the Orthodox faith, which was under attack from unbelievers and from heretics; and in 1631 he was elected metropolitan of Thessalonica, the see that Gregory Palamas had held three centuries before. From there he sent several letters to the tsar of Russia asking help for his metropolia, which the Turks were oppressing. In March 1634, when Loukaris was sent into exile, Athanasius was set at Constantinople for forty days.

In the end the unbelievers forced him to leave the throne of Thessalonica. Having to gather alms to pay the metropolia's debts, he went to Moldavia, where he found help at the court of the pious prince Vasile Lupu, who sent him to the monastery of Saint Nicholas at Galați. Returning to Constantinople once more, he was elected patriarch again in the summer of 1652, shepherded only two weeks, and had to resign under Turkish pressure and go back to Galați. Old and ill in Russia, he received great gifts from the tsar and from Patriarch Nikon, and on the way back stayed at Lubny in Ukraine, at the Transfiguration monastery of Mgarsk.

Knowing his end beforehand he made a testament dividing the tsar's gifts among monasteries and churches of Moldavia, of the Holy Mountain and of Sinai, and appointed gifts for Saint Nicholas at Galați as well; then he prayed for his flock, for those who had done him good and for those who had persecuted or wronged him, asked forgiveness of all and forgave all, and fell asleep in the Lord on 5 April 1654. Eight years later Paisios Ligarides, metropolitan of Gaza, came to Lubny and saw him in a vision asking to be taken out of the grave; the body was found whole and fragrant, and the relics were laid in the right-hand part of the church on 1 February 1662. His commemoration among the saints was appointed for 2 May. The relics were taken to Kharkiv at the beginning of the twentieth century and set for veneration in the Annunciation cathedral there from 1943, and in the autumn of 2004 a particle came from Kharkiv to Galați.
