# Publius of Zeugma

Publius came of a family of councillors in the city of Zeugma, by the river Euphrates. He divided the whole of his father's property among the poor and withdrew into the mountains, where he dug himself a small cave and lived in it. The report of him went everywhere, and many came to share his ascetic life; he told them to make themselves small cells, and visited them often, in case they should keep anything in the cells that they did not need.

The page's Publius is a hard master of small things. He weighed out their bread on the scales, and if he found more with one of them he called him a slave of the belly and a lover of the body's fill; and if he found that one of them separated the flour from the bran, he told him he was making himself a partner in Sybaritic luxury. At night he would go, without warning, to each one's door; if he found the man at his prayer he went away without a word, but if he heard one of them sleeping he struck the door with his hand and rebuked him.

By that constant visiting, the life says, he raised many disciples and stamped all his own virtues on their souls. Two of them are named — Theotecnus and Aphthonius — who took on themselves the defence and the care of the brethren after Publius had finished his course in peace. The page gives no year, either for his birth or for his death, and prints neither troparion nor kontakion for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same twenty-fifth of January. saint.gr says that Publius came from the city of Zeugma, which was built near the bank of the river Euphrates, and that he was a councillor; that his soul was moved by the loving self-denial of the first Christians, so that he shared out all his possessions among the poor and withdrew to a small hermitage; that a little society of hermit brothers was not slow to form around him, of which he was the guide and the teacher; and that he trained them in piety and self-restraint and brought out of them spiritual men who often served in the protection of Christians and in the defence of the Church. The same calendar keeps on this day [Theodotus](/saints/theodotus-of-the-monastery-of-publius), who became abbot of the monastery that bears Publius's name.
