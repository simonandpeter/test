# Isidore of Seville

saint.gr says Isidore was born between the years 565 and 570 at Cartagena in Spain, where his family had taken refuge from the persecution of the Arian Gothic king Agila. He lost his father as a child and was brought up by his elder brothers, [Leander](/saints/leander-of-seville) — whom the same calendar keeps on 27 February — and Fulgentius, and by his sister Florentina. Leander, who was bishop of Seville, took more than ordinary care over his schooling.

Grown up, he worked beside his teacher and brother, helping his theological and pastoral labour, and busied himself above all with turning the Visigoths from Arianism, standing out stoutly against the wrong-believing king Leovigild. During Leander's exile Isidore carried the weight of the fight against the heresy alone, and defended the Orthodox faith and the true interests of the Spanish Church with courage. When Leovigild's Orthodox son Reccared came to the throne, the people came back to Orthodoxy.

Isidore withdrew to a monastery to give himself wholly to stillness, study and prayer, but not for long: Leander's death made him obey clergy and people and take the throne of Seville as its bishop in the year 600. At the second local council of Seville, in 619, over which he presided, a Monophysite disciple of Severus was overthrown, and the saint healed a blind man by a touch; in 633 he presided over the council of Toledo as well.

The page calls him an outstanding teacher of the Church and its ornament, whom few can be compared with for learning and knowledge, and the great shaper of her order: he held that the sacred rites and services should mirror the majesty of the heavenly hierarchy as far as they could, and is reckoned the founder of the liturgical use that survives still, the Mozarabic. Crowds poured into Seville to hear him, and it was said that his wisdom went beyond the wisdom of Solomon himself. He founded and organised many monasteries and built a theological school for the training of clerics, in which he often taught himself, and nothing in the whole field of knowledge was foreign to him — whence the books he wrote on all the sciences then known, among them the «Ετυμολογίες ή περί της αρχής των πραγμάτων», the Etymologies, or On the origin of things.

When he was full of days and of works of piety he fell ill and felt that his course on earth had come to its end. He shared out all he had among the poor and prepared his departure with prayer and repentance, and he foretold the sorrowful history that lay ahead of the Spanish Church and the sufferings that waited for it. Four days before his falling asleep he asked to be carried into the middle of the cathedral and laid there on ashes, dressed in a plain sticharion, and there he prayed and begged God to forgive him. He fell asleep in peace in the year 636. His relic was laid between the relics of Leander and of his sister Florentina in the cathedral of Seville, and later, the page says, after the schism of the Western Church, his honourable body was taken to the city of León.
