# Apollinaris of Ravenna

In the days of Caesar Claudius the apostle Peter went from Antioch to Rome with many Christians, and preaching there out of the books of the prophets that Jesus is truly the Son of God, come to save mankind, he brought many of the Jews and many of the Romans to believe and be baptised. Not long after, he said to his disciple Apollinaris, who had come with him from Antioch, that he had learned well to know Christ and had been informed of His divine works, and that he should now receive the sanctification of the Holy Spirit and go to the city of Ravenna to preach there the name of Jesus Christ, fearing nothing.

So Peter prayed, and laying his hands on his head made him bishop of the city of Ravenna, saying that the Lord would send His angel to prepare his way and fulfil his askings; and he kissed him and let him go in peace. Coming near Ravenna, Apollinaris went into the house of a soldier named Irenaeus to rest, and told him who he was, whence he came and why, and spoke to him of Jesus Christ and of His divine power.

Irenaeus answered that he had a blind son, and that if there were any power in this preaching he should make him see, and then he too would follow his God. The saint had the blind boy brought, and with the whole household gathered to watch he made the sign of the cross over his eyes and prayed that God would make His Son known in that city, so that not the bodily eyes only but the eyes of the soul might be opened. The boy saw at once, and fell with his parents at the feet of the man of God; and all that house believed and were baptised in the river not far from Ravenna, and the saint lodged there from then on. There was in the city a sick woman named Thecla, the wife of the tribune, whom many skilled physicians had not been able to cure. What the page tells from there is very long, and only its opening and its end are set down here.

At the last, when the people were roused against him, a centurion told him that he ought not yet to die, for his life was greatly needed, and bade him go out by night to the village where the sick lay and stay there until the anger of the people should settle; and at midnight he let him out of the city secretly. But some of the servants of the idols learned of it and ran after him, and overtaking him on the road they beat him and cut him with their swords until it seemed to them that he was dead, and left him and went away.

When day came his disciples went out and found him barely breathing, and carried him into that village among the sick, where he lived only seven days. Before his end the whole church of the faithful gathered to him, and he taught them with many words not to depart from the holy faith of Jesus Christ and to abhor the foulness of the idols. He told them prophetically that many persecutions would come against the Church of Christ, and that after all those hard dangers the idols would perish everywhere and the right faith would rise over the whole world; that there would be Christian emperors, and sacrifice would be offered by Christians to the living God without hindrance. And he added that whoever should remain unmoved in the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ would not die but live for ever.

Saying these things and many others, Apollinaris, the hierarch and martyr of Christ, fell asleep. All the faithful wept for him and laid his honourable body in a stone tomb, burying it deep in the earth for fear of the pagans, lest they take it out and dishonour it. He shepherded the Church of Christ twenty-eight years, one month and four days, and suffered on the twenty-third day of the month of July, Vespasian ruling over the Romans.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 23 July, and saint.gr tells his course with several names the Romanian source did not give. Apollinarius was a disciple and follower of the apostle Peter, whom he followed from Antioch to Rome, and it was Peter himself who ordained him bishop of Ravenna. There he worked with much zeal at spreading the Gospel and brought many idolaters to the light, so that the priests of the idols seized and ill-used him. Later he healed, by the grace of God, Boniface, a distinguished member of the society of Ravenna who was deaf and dumb, and his daughter, who suffered from a demon; and that brought many more idolaters to the faith of Christ, and the priests of the idols seized him again and tortured him hard. Then he withdrew to Aemilia, where he raised the daughter of the patrician Rufinus, whose whole household came to Christ; at which the prefect of the city tortured him and put him on a ship to be exiled. The ship was wrecked, Apollinarius was saved, came ashore in Moesia of Thrace and from there returned to Ravenna. As soon as he arrived he was seized and shut in prison in the charge of a centurion — but the centurion was a Christian and let him go. When the priests of the idols learned of it they sent their men, who caught him on the road and beat him so savagely that his body was disfigured and drowned in blood; they took him for dead and left him, but Christians carried him away and cared for him. Seven days later, having blessed his spiritual children, he gave up his holy soul to God who gives the crown.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 23 July and makes him a disciple of the apostle [Peter](/saints/peter-the-apostle), of the city of Antioch by birth. Saint Peter took him with him out of Antioch to Rome, and at Rome ordained him bishop of the city of Ravenna. Coming to Ravenna, Apollinaris went into the house of a certain soldier Irenaeus, whose son he healed of blindness, and through that turned the whole house to the faith of Christ. In the same way he healed the wife of the commander of Ravenna of some grievous sickness, and baptised all his house too.

At the commander’s wish Apollinaris stayed living in his house, and made a house church there, and remained in that house twelve years, preaching the Gospel and baptising the unbelieving. By the heathen elders he was cruelly tortured more than once, but the all-powerful right hand of God upheld him and saved him. At the last he was condemned to banishment to Illyricum — to the Balkans, the entry glosses.

But the ship they travelled in was broken up by a storm and sank, and of all the passengers only Apollinaris was saved, with two soldiers and three of his clergy. The soldiers, so wonderfully saved, believed in the power of Apollinaris’s God and were baptised. Then Apollinaris set out to preach the Gospel over the whole of the Balkans, coming down as far as the Danube; after that he made for Thrace, where likewise under great pressure he spread the Gospel of the Lord. After three years of work in the Balkans he was driven back to Italy. He came to Ravenna, where all the faithful rejoiced over him beyond measure.

Hearing of it, the heathen elders wrote to the emperor Vespasian about Apollinaris as a magician, and asked him whether they should put him to death as an enemy of their gods. To that the emperor answered that he ought not to be killed but only required to sacrifice to the gods, or else driven out of the city; for, he said, it is not fitting that we should avenge anyone for the gods, since they can avenge themselves on their own enemies if they are angered. But the heathen went against even this imperial order and set on Apollinaris and stabbed him with knives; and of the heavy wounds this servant of God breathed out and passed over into the kingdom of God. His relics rest at Ravenna, in the church of his name.
