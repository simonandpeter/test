# Chrysogonus, Eparch of Thessalonica

saint.gr's calendar for 23 December names Chrysogonus, and what it prints in place of a life is a note about where the memory comes from. He is unknown, it says, to the synaxaristes and to the printed Menaia; his memory is recorded on this day in the Sabaitic Codex 635, in these words — «Χρυσογόνου ἔπαρχου πόλεως Θεσσαλονίκης», of Chrysogonus, eparch of the city of Thessalonica — and it refers the reader to Dmitrievsky's Typika, volume two, page 365. That is the whole of the entry.

So the calendar gives him an office and a city and nothing else: no year, no emperor, no manner of death, no hymn, and no statement that he was a martyr at all. He is not the Chrysogonus the same calendar keeps on 22 December, the Roman teacher of Anastasia the Pharmakolytria who was beheaded under Diocletian; the two pages say nothing of each other, and two entries on one calendar are two commemorations. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence by which saint.gr marks an entry it will not vouch for.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 23 Δεκεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/12/23/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3307/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
