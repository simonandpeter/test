# Martin

Martin, bishop of Rome, was born at Todi in Umbria, in central Italy, and
became Pope at the time when the heresy of the Monothelites was troubling
the Church — into whose nets the Church of Constantinople too had fallen,
the Patriarch Paul II being a champion of Monothelitism together with the
emperor Constans II. Martin, a champion of Orthodoxy, tried by letter and by
clergy sent for the purpose to bring the Patriarch back to the Orthodox
dogma, in vain: Paul, under the emperor’s influence, held to Monothelitism
and exiled Martin’s envoys to various islands, and Constans sent and had
Martin himself seized by guile, brought a prisoner to Constantinople, and
exiled to Cherson, where he died on 16 September 655, having governed his
church six years — and, what matters most, death found him fighting on the
ramparts of Orthodoxy, rightly dividing the word of truth, teaching it
without error. Two bishops exiled to Cherson with him died there after many
hardships. The synaxarion’s verse has him who rejoiced to eat the Saviour’s
flesh rejoicing to be stripped of his own. The Greek synaxarion keeps him on
13 April, and some synaxaria repeat his memory on 20 September with [Maximus
the Confessor](/saints/maximus-the-confessor), which is the line this entry
stands under.

*After the Greek synaxarion’s own page for 13 April — [saint.gr, Άγιος Μαρτίνος πάπας Ρώμης](https://www.saint.gr/261/saint.aspx), read 6 September 2026; its 20 September line was all this entry had until then.*
