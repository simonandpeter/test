# Martin

Martin, bishop of Rome, was born at Todi in Umbria, in central Italy, and
became Pope at the time when the heresy of the Monothelites was troubling
the Church — into whose nets the Church of Constantinople too had fallen,
the Patriarch Paul II being a champion of Monothelitism together with the
emperor Constans II. Martin, a champion of Orthodoxy, tried by letter and by
clergy sent for the purpose to bring the Patriarch back to the Orthodox
dogma, in vain: Paul, under the emperor’s influence, held to Monothelitism
and exiled Martin’s envoys to various islands, and Constans sent and had
Martin himself seized by guile, brought a prisoner to Constantinople, and
exiled to Cherson, where he died on 16 September 655, having governed his
church six years — and, what matters most, death found him fighting on the
ramparts of Orthodoxy, rightly dividing the word of truth, teaching it
without error.

Two bishops exiled to Cherson with him died there after many hardships. The
synaxarion’s verse has him who rejoiced to eat the Saviour’s flesh rejoicing
to be stripped of his own. The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 13 April, and
some synaxaria repeat his memory on 20 September with [Maximus the
Confessor](/saints/maximus-the-confessor), which is the line this entry
stands under.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 14 April, and the Lives it prints add the shape of the quarrel and the end of it. Martin took the see of Rome by the spoken choice of all after the death of Pope Theodore, at a time when the emperors at Constantinople still held old Rome; the Monothelite heresy — that there is only one will in Christ our Lord — had grown out of the earlier heresy of Eutyches, was begun by Cyrus of Alexandria and Sergius of Constantinople with the agreement of the emperor Heraclius, and was carried on by the patriarchs Pyrrhus and Paul, at whose urging the emperor Constans wrote the booklet full of it that he called the *Typos*.

Against it the Orthodox confession holds that in our incarnate Lord and God there are two natures, and likewise two wills and two operations, each nature distinct, in one single person of Christ — for Christ God is not in two separated persons, but is known in the duality of natures without confusion. When the notary came to take Martin away to exile, those in the prison wept, and the saint said to them with a glad face: «Nu plângeți, ci mai bine bucurați-vă împreună cu mine, care mă bucur; căci mă duc în surghiun pentru dreapta credință!» — do not weep, but rather rejoice with me, for I am going into exile for the right faith.

He was put aboard ship and sent into exile at Cherson, where he was tormented with hunger, with hardship and with every kind of want, and after two years went to the Lord. His body was buried outside the city of Cherson, in the church of the Most Holy Theotokos that was called of Blachernae, and his grave was glorious, the Romanian Lives say, for the many healings given there to the sick.

saint.gr keeps him on 13 April as well, where this folder's Greek row records the 20 Σεπτεμβρίου on which the same site keeps him with Maximus the Confessor, and that page adds the reason for his exile and a note about the days. It says Martin, bishop of Rome, was born in central Italy, at Todi in Umbria, and became pope at the time when the heresy of the Monothelites was troubling the Church, when the Church of Constantinople too had fallen into its nets, the patriarch Paul II being a champion of Monothelitism together with the emperor Constans II. Martin, a champion of Orthodoxy, tried by a letter of his and by special clerics sent for the purpose to bring the patriarch Paul back to the Orthodox teaching, and in vain: the patriarch, under the emperor's influence, held to Monothelitism and exiled Martin's envoys to various islands. Constans II then sent men who seized Martin himself by a trick, and having brought him captive to Constantinople they exiled him to Cherson, where he died on 16 September 655 after governing his church six years — death finding him, the page says, still fighting on the ramparts of Orthodoxy, rightly dividing the word of truth. Two more bishops were exiled with him to Cherson, and died there after many hardships; the page names neither of them.

About the days it is explicit and hedged: «Η μνήμη του, από ορισμένους Συναξαριστές, επαναλαμβάνεται στις 20 Σεπτεμβρίου» — his memory is repeated, by certain synaxaristes, on 20 September. So the 20 Σεπτεμβρίου this folder's Greek row already holds and this 13 Απριλίου are the same man on two Greek days, and the row was left where it stands.
