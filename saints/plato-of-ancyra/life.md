# Plato of Ancyra

Doxologia's life gives him as from the parts of Galatia, from the city of Ancyra, a brother of the Martyr Antiochus, born of Christian parents and brought up in the right faith. Before he had come to full age he showed himself perfect in good works, for he was a wise man: young in years, the page says, but old in wisdom. He preached Christ, the everlasting God, with boldness, and taught people to know the truth and turn from the deceit of idols to God.

For that he was seized by the unbelievers and brought to judgement before the governor Agrippinus, who was then in the temple of Zeus and sat there to try the confessor of Christ. Looking at him he said: All the world rejoices in its gods, and thou, wanderer, why dost thou flee from them? The saint answered that it was they who had wandered, in leaving the God who made heaven and earth and all their adornment.

The long trial that follows is torments and answers, and at its end he was condemned to the sword. Before he died he prayed with his hands raised to heaven, thanking Christ that His holy name had defended His servant from the enemy's cunning and had given him the grace to finish well the contest he had endured; and asking Him to receive his soul in peace. Then, bowing his head, he said to the soldier who was to kill him: Do, friend, what thou art commanded. The page gives no year for any of it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 18 November, and gives his city, his brother and his judge. saint.gr says Plato lived at the end of the third century, came from Ancyra of Galatia in Asia Minor, and was the brother of the martyr Antiochus. Young still, the idolaters arrested him because he was proclaiming his faith in Jesus Christ, and brought him before the governor Agrippinus.

Agrippinus, seeing the beauty of the young man and knowing that he had property, tried to draw him with flatteries; but Plato refused and went on proclaiming his faith in the one and only God. When the governor saw that enticement would not make him change, he threatened him with torments, and Plato stayed firm; so Agrippinus ordered him flogged without mercy and then tortured with red-hot rods. He kept his whole faith and did not stop confessing Jesus Christ, and for that his beheading was ordered, in 306. The page records fragments of his relics at the monasteries of Mega Spileo of Kalavryta, of the Life-giving Spring on Poros and of the Phaneromene on Salamis.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 18 November, «Мч. Платона (302 или 306).», and its life page makes him the brother of [Antiochus the Physician](/saints/antiochus-the-physician), whose memory it puts on 16 July, born at Ancyra in Galatia into a pious family. While still a youth he left home and went about the cities preaching the word of God to the heathen, astonishing those who heard him by the persuasiveness and the beauty of his speech and by a deep knowledge of Greek learning. For that preaching he was seized and brought into the temple of Zeus to be judged by the governor Agrippinus.

The judge first tried flattery, assuring the young man that he might rival the great philosopher Plato in intelligence if he would worship the gods of the heathen; the saint answered that the wisdom of the philosopher, great as it is, passes and is bounded, while the true, eternal and boundless wisdom lies in the teaching of the Gospel. Then the judge promised him his own beautiful daughter in marriage for a denial and threatened torment and death for a refusal, and the saint answered that he chose a death in time for the sake of a life that is eternal. He was beaten without mercy and sent to prison, and on the way he turned to the crowd gathered by the temple and called on them all not to fall away from the Christian faith.

Seven days later he was brought again before Agrippinus in the temple of Zeus, where the instruments were ready: boiling cauldrons, red-hot iron, sharp hooks. Offered the choice of sacrificing or of feeling them, he refused again firmly to bow to the idols, and after the torments was thrown into prison and kept there without food or water for eighteen days. Seeing that this had not shaken him either, they offered him life and freedom for no more than the words «велик бог Аполлон». «Не хочу согрешить и словом» — I will not sin even with a word — the martyr answered, and by the order of Agrippinus he was beheaded.

The Охридски пролог places him at Ancyra in Galatia, which it glosses as today's Ankara, the capital of Turkey, and makes him a Christian by birth and upbringing who showed great perfection in every virtue while still a young man. He did not hide his faith in Christ the Lord but preached it openly, reproaching the idolaters for bowing to dead creatures instead of the living Creator; for which he was brought to trial before the governor Agrippinus and much examined and tortured by him. When the governor began advising him to escape death and keep his life by worshipping the idols, Plato answered that there are two deaths, one in time and one eternal, and so also two lives, one short and one without end.

Then Agrippinus laid dreadful torments on him. Among others he had red-hot iron balls set on his bared body, and after that strips cut from his skin. Torture me yet more cruelly, the martyr called out to his torturer, that your inhumanity and my endurance may both be seen plainly. When the torturer brought up Plato the philosopher to him as a heathen teacher of that name, the martyr said that he was not like Plato nor Plato like him except in the name alone: I teach and learn the wisdom which is Christ's, and he is a teacher of that wisdom which is folly before God.

After that he was thrown into prison, where he passed eighteen days without food or water. When the guards marvelled that he could live so long fasting, he told them: you are filled with meat and I with holy prayers; you are gladdened by wine and I by Christ, the true vine. He was beheaded with the sword at last and received the crown of eternal glory. This book sets that about the year 266, which is thirty-six years and more before the «302 or 306» the folder holds from two other calendars and falls outside that interval altogether; the interval is left exactly as it was and the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved.
