# Rufus (22 October)

saint.gr's calendar for 22 Οκτωβρίου names a venerable Rufus and describes him in one sentence: a wise ascetic of the desert, who died in peace, whose sayings are to be found in the «Παράδεισος των Πατέρων», the Paradise of the Fathers. The couplet over the entry says that Rufus left earth and life most gladly, for he had reckoned both life and earth a dream.

The page then quotes Nicodemus the Hagiorite at length for the one saying that is certainly his. Rufus was asked what stillness is and what good comes of it, and the elder answered that stillness is to sit in one's cell with knowledge and the fear of God, abstaining from the memory of wrongs and from high-mindedness; that such stillness, being the mother of all the virtues, guards the monk from the enemy's burning arrows and does not let him be wounded by them; and he told his questioner to acquire it, remembering the hour of his own death.

The same passage carries a second saying of his: that the man who sits under the obedience of a spiritual father has a greater reward than the man who sits in the desert and keeps stillness alone. One of the fathers, it goes on, saw four ranks in heaven — the man who is ill and gives thanks to God, the man who receives strangers and stands and serves them, the man who keeps stillness in the desert and sees no one, and the man who is under an elder's obedience and submits to him for God's sake — and the last had the greater glory, because the others keep a will of their own while he has given up all his own wills and hangs on his elder's only.
