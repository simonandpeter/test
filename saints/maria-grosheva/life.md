# Maria (Grosheva)

She was born in 1876 at Varyukovka in the Yegoryevsk district of the Ryazan province, to the peasants Naum and Platonida Groshev, who had four daughters and a son; her sister [Matrona (Grosheva)](/saints/matrona-grosheva) was born there in 1882. The calendar keeps the two of them on one day and the one life it has is one life for both. Three of the daughters, Maria, Matrona and Pelagia, went in their youth to an elder whom many honoured for his ascetic life and his foresight and asked him how they should live. To Maria and Matrona he said: «В монастырь, в монастырь...» — to the monastery, to the monastery; and to Pelagia, to an ungodly family, to an ungodly family, marry.

In 1909 the two sisters entered the Alexander and Maria monastery, ten versts from Yegoryevsk, as novices; Maria was set to the sewing and Matrona to the monastery farm. Pelagia married into a family of complete unbelievers, and her patience and her mildness brought her husband in the end to the faith, so that he went to church and even sang in the choir. After the monastery was closed the sisters went home, lived a while in their father's house, and then moved into the watchman's cottage beside the two churches of the Kazan icon and the great-martyr Paraskeve at Tugoles in the Shatura district, one of stone and one of wood, both set on a high hill in a sea of pine. They lived there twenty years. They had a garden and a cow; they baked the bread for the church, served in the sanctuary, cleaned the church, and earned what else they needed by quilting blankets.

The village loved them for their gentle and open ways. They taught their nieces from childhood to pray, and one of them to read the Psalter, and when every church in the neighbourhood had been closed that niece read the Psalter over the dead. The chairman of the village soviet, who hated the church bitterly, would come out onto the road whenever a service began and try to frighten the peasants going to it away. When the parish priest was arrested and the police came to strip the church, the warden would not give up the keys, and the second time, arrested, he threw them into the snow on the road where Pelagia found them by arrangement with him; the sisters carried some of the holy vessels secretly from the church into their cell. After the church was shut they walked the fifteen kilometres to the Kazan church at Petrovskoye, and before every service they urged the peasant women not to leave off praying and not to lose heart but to come with them to the house of God.

In November 1937 the two priests and the psalmist of Tugoles were arrested and the services there stopped, and from then on the sisters were the only women left in the neighbourhood who could read the Psalter over a dead kinsman, instruct in the faith and teach a person to pray. In February 1938 the arrests began again, and with almost no priests left at liberty it was laymen who were taken. On 15 February the chairman of the village soviet wrote, as a false witness against them, that they were hostile to the Soviet power and the party, that on feasts they went from house to house among the collective farmers and held services in some of them, and that they said: tomorrow is a feast of the Lord, better to go to church and pray to God than to work in the collective farm. Eight of the farm women, he wrote, went the fifteen kilometres to Petrovskoye instead of working, and when they were asked why they did not work they answered that it was better to pray to God or He would punish them all.

The sisters were arrested on 26 February 1938 and shut in the prison at Yegoryevsk. Asked whether she had called the farm women to her house and held services there, Maria answered that there had been no such thing, but that farmers had come in to talk or to borrow something needed for a dead person, a pall for instance, and that she read the Psalter over the dead herself. Asked about the church books, crosses, chalices and vestments found in her house at the search, she said that they belonged to the church and had been kept there since the priests were arrested and the church closed, and that neither she nor her sister had taken on the performing of any rite except the reading of the Psalter. She did not admit herself guilty of anti-Soviet activity. On 11 March 1938 an NKVD troika sentenced the two of them to be shot and they were taken to the Taganka prison in Moscow. They were shot on 20 March 1938 and buried in an unmarked common grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow, which civil day is the Julian 7 March the calendar keeps them on.
