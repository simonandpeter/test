# Emilian of Durostorum

In the reign of the pagan emperor Julian, who had gone far from God, a cruel persecution of the Christians went up from him like a whirlwind and a storm, so that the whole world was troubled. He sent harsh governors through every land and city of the Roman power with orders that all Christians, of every tribe and family, men and women of every age, be given over without mercy to a bitter death by torment; and with that unjust order the God-hating emperor broke the just laws and filled the East and the West with the blood of the Christians, which he shed without sparing, raging in anger and enmity against Christ and against His servants.

In the city of Durostorum, in the country of Moesia, he set a merciless officer named Capitolinus, full of deceit and darkened in mind, a destroyer of men and a lover of idols. Coming to that city he went first into the temple of the idols and sacrificed to the demons and bowed down to the lifeless images. On another day he came out to the place of judgement in the theatre and sat on a high seat in great pride and menace, to frighten the Christians, and, setting out the imperial order, asked closely whether there were anyone in the city who opposed the gods and confessed himself a Christian. The citizens assured him on oath that there was no such man among them, and that all of them honoured the gods every day with worship and sacrifices; and Capitolinus was delighted and called all the chief men of the city to dine with him.

The Romanian page's account of the whole passion is a long one and the middle of it is left here unsummarised. At the end of it Emilian, standing in the fire, signed himself with the sign of the holy cross, blessed God, prayed as long as he wished, and said: Lord Jesus Christ, receive my spirit. With that he lay down and fell asleep in the Lord, the fire being by now burnt out; and his body was left unharmed by the flames, so that not even the hairs of his head were burnt.

Those of the citizens who were secretly Christians went to the governor's wife, who likewise held the Christian faith in secret, and told her everything about the saint, urging her to ask her husband for the martyr's body for burial, since by a wonder it had not been harmed by the fire. She asked her husband, and he told her to take it without fear. So the faithful took the body of Emilian, wrapped it in clean linen, anointed it with spices and buried it with honour, with psalms and singing, in a place called Gezidina, about three stadia from the city of Durostorum.

The page closes by saying that the holy martyr Emilian suffered for Christ at the hands of the governor Capitolinus on the eighteenth day of the month of July, Julian the Apostate ruling at Rome over the pagans, while among us our Master Jesus Christ reigns. It gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 18 July, and saint.gr gives the scene the Romanian line did not. It says he was born at Durostolon in Thracian Moesia when Julian the Apostate was emperor, and that he was the slave of a harsh and fanatical idolater — though it adds that other sources make him instead the son of a local officer named Sabbatianos. When his master learned that Emilian believed in Christ he abused him in the foulest words and then flogged him without mercy, warning him that worse would follow if he stayed a Christian; and the punishments and threats, instead of bending him, only fanned the flame of his faith.

The next day he went into a temple of the idols and broke every statue in it with a hammer. The priests seized him and handed him over for trial, and his master ran to the court, cursed him for what he had done and ordered him to deny Christ without argument. Emilian answered him smiling: you may order what you like and I shall obey you, but my faith is outside your rights; in that I acknowledge one Lord only, Jesus Christ, who is my great and everlasting Lord, who has authority over my body and my soul, and I shall never deny Him. The idolater struck him in the face; then, after they had tortured him, they threw him into the fire. His body was buried in great state by the idolater's own wife, who was a secret Christian.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same 18 July, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the whole act and the argument behind it. He was a youth, it says, a servant in the house of the city governor of Durostorum in Thrace. When the apostate emperor began to destroy Christianity with fire and sword throughout the Roman empire, an imperial envoy came to Durostorum too, to kill the Christians — and did not find a single one. Delighted at it, he gave a great banquet to the citizens and ordered a great sacrifice to the idols and rejoicing throughout the city, day and night.

That night Emilian went round the idol temples, the market-places and the streets, and smashed all the idols with a mallet. In the morning there was horror in the city and everybody was looking for the destroyer of their gods, and a peasant was seized who had been seen passing by a temple that morning. Emilian, seeing that an innocent man would suffer, said within himself: If I hide my deed, what good to me is what I did? Shall I not be found before God as the murderer of an innocent man?

So he presented himself to the imperial envoy and confessed it all. Asked in a rage who had persuaded him to do it, he answered: God and my own soul commanded me to break those dead pillars which you call gods. The judge had him scourged, and after the scourging and other torments had him burned in fire. The Prologue dates it to the 18 July of the year 362, which is the first date of any kind this folder has carried.
