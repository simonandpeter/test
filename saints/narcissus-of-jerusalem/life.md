# Narcissus of Jerusalem

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 7 august reads «Sfântul Sfinţit Mucenic Narcis, Patriarhul Ierusalimului» — the holy hieromartyr Narcissus, Patriarch of Jerusalem — and the page behind that line repeats the name and the day and prints nothing else: no narrative, no troparion, no years, no place beyond the see in his title.

So what is recorded here is what the source records: that the Romanian church keeps on 7 August a bishop of Jerusalem named Narcissus, and calls him a hieromartyr. Neither his century nor the manner of his death is stated on the page, and neither is supplied here. The Greek and Russian calendars have not been read for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 7 August, and tells the story the Romanian line does not. Narcissus was shown forth as the thirtieth, or by other reckonings the thirty-first, bishop of Jerusalem counting from the first, who was James the brother of God; and he was a man temperate, loving towards men, apt to teach, and unbending in the performance of his duty, undeterred by the reaction of his malicious enemies.

In a slander once got up against him three false witnesses came forward. The first, to confirm his false deposition, said that if he were lying fire should fall and burn him; the second, that he should be eaten of worms; the third, that he should be blinded. Not long after, the false witnesses were punished as each had asked: the first was burnt by a thunderbolt, the second fell gravely ill and worms ate his leprous body, and the third went blind. Narcissus came up to the throne twice and held the patriarchate twenty-six years in all, and the page says he departed peacefully at the age of a hundred and sixteen.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him the shortest entry of its Julian 7 August and the shortest in this block: one sentence of eleven words, which says that he was beheaded in the time of Antoninus, in the year 213, in the hundred and sixteenth year of his life. That year is the first date this folder has held; neither doxologia nor saint.gr gave it one.

The age is not recorded as a birth. Subtracting a hundred and sixteen from 213 would give a year nothing on the page states, and the sentence is also at odds with itself: the only emperor it names is Antoninus, and no Antoninus reigned in 213 unless the name is being used as the title the later emperors carried. The book’s words are kept as they are and nothing here is reconciled.
