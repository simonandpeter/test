# Barlaam (Popov)

The Сретенский monastery's calendar names him fourteenth in its clause of fifteen for 25 ноября ст. ст. and carries no life for him; azbyka.ru prints two, a short one out of pstgu.ru and a full one out of the Bryansk diocesan site, and **the two do not agree about his arrest**: the short one says he was arrested in the autumn of 1936 and that only the day of his death is known, while the full one dates the arrest to 2 August 1937. Варлаам Петрович Попов was born on 19 June 1880 at Лобаново in the Севск district of the province of Orel, the son of a priest.

He went into the Севск church school in 1891 and the Orel seminary in 1896, and finished it in 1903 after seven years instead of six, probably through illness. He taught in a parish school, married Раиса Григорьевна Прудникова of Севск in 1906, and had six children, one of whom died in infancy. On 11 October 1906 he was appointed to a priest's place at Поздняшовка, was ordained deacon on 8 December and priest on 10 December of that year, and ran the building of the parish school there and then taught in it. In 1917 he was moved to his own village of Лобаново.

In the civil war, when the fighting for Севск was at its worst, a Kuban regiment of the Red Army was quartered in the district and meant to kill him and his family; warned by villagers, he hid, and for four days the peasants hid their priest from them. When the Whites came he began serving again, and at the services commemorated the provisional government, the Patriarch [Tikhon of Moscow](/saints/tikhon-of-moscow) and the local bishop. Three weeks later the Whites withdrew and the peasants dared not shelter him any longer, so he set out with his family for Ukraine by cart and then by train, left them at a convent near Borisovka, and wandered from Aleksandro-Grushevsk to Ekaterinodar and then to Kuban parishes, living on the leavings of a school table, until he came home in April 1920. He printed the whole of that journey himself at an interrogation years later, and the full life quotes it.

From 1920 he served in Севск, from 1924 at the church of the Exaltation of the Cross, and he fought the renovationist schism hard. In April 1925 the renovationist diocesan board of Bryansk removed him from his place and forbade him to serve anywhere in the diocese for not recognising its authority and for open agitation against it; he was arrested that month, let out in June, arrested again in December as a man whose being at liberty hindered the investigation, and let out on 24 December because the investigation had not established that he had spread the rumours. He was raised archpriest by 1930, sat on the deanery council and from 1932 was dean of the Севск district, helped priests coming back from prison, and gave particular attention to the schoolchildren who came to him for confession in winter. He was arrested once more in October 1932 over a charge of keeping gold currency and released in November.

His daughter Галина remembered the church house by the wall, the move to the Exaltation church when people came across the river to ask for him, his rising at about five in the morning to pray before the liturgy, his serving almost every day, his receiving all comers gladly, and the paper Christmas tree ornaments the five children cut out themselves. He was arrested on 2 August 1937 and held in the prison at Севск; on 22 November the troika of the NKVD administration for the Orel region sentenced him to be shot with confiscation of his property. He was shot at Bryansk on 8 December 1937, and his family tried in vain to learn anything of him. He was rehabilitated in 1989 and numbered among the new martyrs by the Holy Synod on 17 July 2006.
