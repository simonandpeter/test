# Anthimus of Arabia

saint.gr's calendar for 17 October keeps five saints on one line — «Άγιοι Κοσμάς, Δαμιανός, Λεόντιος, Άνθιμος και Ευπρέπιος οι Ανάργυροι» — and Anthimus is one of them. The page behind the line gives each of the five a heading of its own, his being «Αγιος Ανθιμος ο Αναργυρος», sets three epigrams over them, and then prints a single account for all five together.

They were brothers of one another, that account says, in the flesh and in the spirit too, from Arabia — the other four being [Cosmas](/saints/cosmas-of-arabia), [Damian](/saints/damian-of-arabia), [Leontius](/saints/leontius-of-arabia) and [Euprepius](/saints/euprepius-of-arabia). Devout, all five of them, inspired by the same divine zeal and moved by the same love of man, they had not only done many good works by their art of medicine but had also drawn many to the Christian faith. They were denounced for it and arrested, in the persecution under Diocletian, in Lycia, by Lysias the governor at Aegae; and after many tortures they underwent death by beheading.

Of Anthimus in particular the page records nothing apart from that: no year, no birthplace of his own, no order among the brothers, and no hymn. He and Euprepius share the page's third epigram, which says that Anthimus and Eutropius, cut down, flower brightly and most becomingly — and note that the epigram writes Εὐτρόπιος where the line and the account write Εὐπρέπιος. So this folder holds what the calendar holds — a name, a company of brothers, a trade, a province and a day — and the five stand as five folders because the calendar names them one by one.

These are the unmercenaries of **Arabia**, and they are not the pair the Church keeps on 1 July nor the pair she keeps on 1 November: [Cosmas](/saints/cosmas-of-rome) and [Damian of Rome](/saints/damian-of-rome) are one pair, [Cosmas](/saints/cosmas-of-asia) and [Damian of Asia](/saints/damian-of-asia) another, and these five brothers out of Arabia are a third company on a third day.


The Russian calendar keeps the five on the same menologion day, 17 October, and gives them the year nobody else gives. Its line is «Мчч. бессребреников Космы и Дамиана, Аравийских, и братий их мчч. Леонтия, Анфима и Евтропия (287 или 303)» — 287 or 303, a choice the calendar sets down without making it. The life it links says that the holy unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian of Arabia went about the cities and villages preaching Christ and healing the sick by the power of Christ, and took no payment for the help they gave.

In Cilicia, it goes on, the pagans seized the holy physicians and brought them to the governor Lysias. When they refused to deny the Christian faith he ordered them beaten inhumanly and then drowned in the sea; but an angel of God carried them out of the deep onto the shore. Then the pagans beheaded them. Together with the holy physicians their brothers Leontius, Anthimus and Euprepius were put to death. The page closes with a warning in its own parenthesis: these are not to be confused with the other unmercenaries of the same names, whose memories fall on 1 November and 1 July.

The Serbian calendar keeps them on the same day and begins with that same warning, told out. There are three sets of holy physicians called Cosmas and Damian, the Ohrid Prologue says: one pair fell asleep in peace and are kept on 1 November, another were stoned at Rome and are kept on 1 July, and these third were from Arabia, and it is of these that the present day speaks. They were physicians by trade, and when they received the faith of Christ they healed the sick in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, and became famous on every side for their wonderworking cures.

The Prologue names the city where the Russian life names only the province. Spiteful pagans seized them and took them before the prince Lysias in the city of Aegae; and because the holy brothers would not deny Christ at any price they were thrown first into the sea and then into the fire, and God Almighty saved them from the flood and from the flame, an angel of the Lord appearing to them. The pagan prince put it down to some sorcery of theirs, and they answered him: we neither know any sorcery nor need any, but we have the power of Christ, which saves both us and everyone who calls upon the most holy name of Christ.

Then they were struck with stones, and the stones rebounded from them; they were shot at with arrows, and the arrows rebounded from them; and at the last they were beheaded with the sword. With them suffered, and received the crown of glory, the holy Leontius, Anthimus and Euprepius. They suffered in the time of Diocletian and Maximian, the Prologue says, dating them by the reign where the Russian calendar offers two years; and many wonders were shown from their holy relics as they had been from them living.
