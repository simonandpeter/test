# Sergius, companion of Bacchus

Sergius and [Bacchus](/saints/bacchus-companion-of-sergius) were Romans of senatorial rank, the first men at the court of the emperor Maximian, who loved them for their skill and their courage in war. The Romanian life says that no one could come near the emperor or ask anything of him except through these two trusted counsellors, and that they held an honour nobody else at court held. They kept their Christianity hidden for fear of him, until men who envied them denounced them as Christians who had given up the worship of the idols.

The emperor would not believe it, and tested them: he went with the whole court to sacrifice in the temple of the idol Zeus, and the two stayed outside at prayer. Brought in by force and refusing to eat what had been offered, they were stripped of the marks of their rank, dressed in women's clothes, collared with iron and led through the city to be laughed at. Maximian then argued with them in private and promised them greater honour, and when that failed he sent them east to the governor Antiochus, a man who owed his office to their own petition.

Antiochus had Bacchus beaten to death and Sergius kept in prison, where he wept for his friend. He was then shod with shoes driven full of nails and made to run before the governor's carriage, and in the night an angel healed his feet, so that his torturer took the healing for sorcery. At the city of Rosaf he was pressed once more to sacrifice, refused, asked leave to pray, heard a voice calling him, and was beheaded; the faithful of the place buried him.

Men from the city of Sur came by night to carry the body away, and a pillar of fire rose from the grave until they understood that he would not leave the ground where he had died. They built him a tomb of stone, a church was raised at Rosaf in his name, and fifteen bishops of the country round about translated his relics into it and appointed his memory for the seventh of October, the day he was killed. The Romanian life adds that each year on that day the wild beasts of the desert came to the place and harmed nothing.

The Greek calendar keeps the two on the same day, 7 October, and fills the account out. saint.gr says that they served in the military ranks of the emperor Maximian, that they were marked out both by great courage in the field and by sober living, and that for it the emperor gave them the dignities of primicerius of the school of the Kentelioi and of secunducerius. When he learned that his two chosen soldiers were Christians he would not believe it, and arranged sacrifices in an idol temple and summoned them to be present; they refused and confessed Christ, and he had the insignia of their offices stripped from them.

They were then mocked, paraded and sent to a harsh duke of the East, Antiochus, who scourged Bacchus to death. To Sergius, whom he had once favoured, he offered his life if he would deny Christ, and Sergius answered with the Apostle's words, «Ἐμοὶ τὸ ζῆν Χριστὸς καὶ τὸ ἀποθανεὶν κέρδος» — to me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain — and was beheaded at once. In 547 the emperor Justinian raised a splendid church to the two martyrs in Constantinople, the one called «Μικρή Αγία Σοφία», and laid their relics in it; saint.gr adds that their skulls are at the monastery of Curtea de Argeș in Romania.

The Russian calendar keeps the two on its own 7 October and gives them the head of the day, «Мчч. Сергия и Вакха (290-303)». The day's one rank icon stands immediately before that line — «Совершается служба на шесть», a service of six stichera — and the day's service block names this pair and nobody else, «7 октября: Святых мучеников Сергия и Вакха». Its index of names calls him «Сергий Римлянин, Росафский, мч.», Sergius the Roman, of Rosaf, which is the city the Romanian life has him beheaded at and buried in. The Russian prints a span of years where neither of the other two calendars prints any, 290–303, and this folder now carries it.

The day sets out a troparion in tone 5 and a kontakion in tone 2. The troparion calls the two of them the adornment of Christ's passion-bearers and the eyes of his Church and asks them to enlighten the eyes of our souls. The kontakion is the Greek one in Slavonic, the same text and the same tone, and it ends «добро и красно еже быти с Богом» — good and fair it is to be with God — where the Greek has «τὸ συνεῖναι Χριστῷ», to be with Christ.
