# Ammon the Egyptian

Ammon came from Egypt, the Greek synaxarion says. When his parents died an uncle brought him up and made him marry, at twenty-two. On the wedding night, when the couple had withdrawn to the bridal chamber, Ammon opened the Scriptures and read the passage of the letter to the Corinthians where the Apostle speaks of the troubles and cares of marriage and of the undistracted prayer of those who give themselves to the Lord in virginity, and keeping the apostle’s words to the letter — that those who have wives be as though they had none — the two resolved to live in virginity and to withdraw together to a desert place for fasting and prayer. They went to the desert of Nitria, some way from Alexandria, and settled in a small hut; but they soon saw that it was not right to put themselves in temptation by living together, man and woman, and parted so that each could live as an ascetic alone.

Ammon went out into the desert, where there were no monasteries yet, built two vaulted cells, and for twenty-two years lived a life equal to the angels’, knowing neither oil nor wine and eating only dry bread, and that every two or three days. Many brothers gathered round him; when a newcomer arrived Ammon gave him his own cell and all he had, and the others brought the new companion provisions in secret, so that brotherly love was the first law of the growing community, and in some years Nitria became a true city.

When some brothers wished to build their cells farther off for more quiet, [Anthony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great) came to visit him, and after their spare meal at the ninth hour the two walked into the desert until sunset and set up a cross where they stopped, so that brothers from Nitria could reach those living there after the ninth-hour meal in time, and those setting out from there would keep their stillness. So began the desert of the Cells, about nineteen kilometres from Nitria, where some years later about six hundred monks lived, each in his cell.

Ammon and Anthony were bound by so deep a love in God that when Ammon gave up his soul, Anthony, on his mountain fourteen days’ walk away, broke off his talk with some young monks, fell into ecstasy, and saw Ammon’s soul going up to heaven while angels sang hymns of joy. Among the sayings the Spirit gave Ammon the entry sets this: bear with every man as God bears with you. Its heading gives his life as 294–357. The Greek calendar keeps him on 4 October.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 4 October and gives the year of death this folder had none for: «Прп. Аммона (ок. 350)», about 350, against the birth year of 294 the Greek supplied. Its index of names prints his name twice over and adds the place, «Аммон (Аммун) Нитрийский, Египетский» — Ammun, of Nitria, of Egypt. A second Ammon stands on the same page, the recluse of the Kyiv Caves, which is why that one's line names the caves and this one's does not.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him third on this day and tells the marriage differently. It makes him an Egyptian by birth and a vine-dresser by trade, married by his relatives against his own will; from the first day he called his wife his sister and counselled her to keep her virginity with him for the sake of the greater treasure in heaven, and so he lived with her eighteen full years. Then, by agreement, she founded a women's monastery in their own house and he went away to the desert of Nitria and gave himself to the hermit's life. The Greek entry instead has the two go to Nitria together and part there, seeing that it was not right to live as man and woman under one roof.

Because of the purity of his heart, the Prologue says, he received great gifts of insight and of wonderworking from God. A man and his wife brought him their raving son to be healed by prayer, and he refused outright; when they pressed him long he said that the sickness and the health of their child were in their own hands — give that widow back the ox you stole from her, and he named her, and your son will be well. Horrified at such insight, the parents confessed their sin and promised to return the ox as soon as they got home; then Ammon prayed, and the child was well.

He was a close friend of [Antony the Great](/saints/anthony-the-great), and when Ammon ended his course in Nitria about the year 350 Antony saw his soul in the heights from his own cell and said to the brethren: today the abba Ammon has departed; I see his holy soul, how the angels are carrying it to heaven. The Prologue's year is the one the Russian calendar also gives.
