# Dionysius I of Constantinople

saint.gr's calendar for 23 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Διονύσιος Α' Πατριάρχης Κωνσταντινούπολης» and gives him a full notice. He was born at Dimitsana in the Peloponnese and was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Mangana in Constantinople, where he was distinguished in virtue and piety under the guidance of his spiritual father, [Mark Eugenicus](/saints/mark-of-ephesus), metropolitan of Ephesus, who ordained him deacon and then presbyter.

Taken captive by the Turks at Adrianople, he was bought out by the lord Kyritzis and was ordained metropolitan of Philippopolis by the Ecumenical Patriarch Gennadius Scholarius, whose patriarchate the page dates 1454 to 1456. In January 1467 he went up to the patriarchal throne of Constantinople, and he had the protection of the Christian stepmother of the sultan Mehmed II, the lady Mara; he was patriarch until 1472.

He was slandered with having been circumcised and turned Turk during his captivity at Adrianople, but proved the charge false before a tribunal of hierarchs and of the people, showing his flesh to the whole congregation, who all saw, in the page's words, his cleanness and his virginity. He withdrew then to the monastery of Eikosiphoinissa at Drama, where he lived also after his second patriarchate, which the page dates 1488 to 1490, until his death in 1492, and of which he is reckoned the second founder.

His relics are at that monastery still, the page says. A service and a canon for him were written by his contemporary Manuel the Corinthian, the Great Rhetor of the Great Church, and another canon by Chrysanthos, protosyngellos of Xanthi; the page prints neither of them.
