# John Chrysostom

Trained in rhetoric at Antioch under the pagan orator Libanius, John spent some
years as a hermit before returning to the city, where his preaching earned him
the name by which he is now known — *chrysostomos*, golden-mouthed. His homilies
survive in greater quantity than those of any other Greek father.

Made archbishop of Constantinople in 398, largely against his will, he set about
reducing the expenses of his household, disciplining the clergy and preaching
against the luxury of the court. This was less popular than his preaching in
Antioch had been. He was deposed at the Synod of the Oak in 403 on charges
assembled by Theophilus of Alexandria, recalled after a few days, and exiled
again the following year. He died in 407 being marched to a still more distant
place of banishment.

The liturgy most commonly celebrated in the Byzantine rite bears his name,
though its present form is considerably later than he is.
