# John (Romashkin)

John Romashkin was born in 1891 to a peasant family, and served as psalmist and then priest in the Nizhny Novgorod region, at the village of Subbotino. He was arrested repeatedly: in 1928 he was sentenced to three years’ exile for counter-revolutionary agitation; in 1934 he was arrested for resisting the removal of the bells, and released; in 1935 for undermining collective-farm discipline, and the case was dropped. On 8 September 1937 the clergy and Orthodox laity of the Gagino district were arrested, ten people in all, none of whom admitted guilt; they were charged with having organised on 6 July a solemn service in which four priests and a crowd of believers took part, and though no political sermon was preached the service itself was given the meaning of an anti-state act, because it took the peasants from work on the collective farm. On 17 September 1937 eight of them were sentenced to be shot and shot — John with the priests Nicholas Khvoshchev, Alexander Nikolsky and Peter Lebedinsky and the laymen Basil Yezhov, Peter Lonskov, Stephen Mityushkin and Alexander Blokhin. He was numbered among the saints in 2000, and the Russian calendar keeps him on 4 September (Julian).
