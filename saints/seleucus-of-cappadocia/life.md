# Seleucus of Cappadocia

Seleucus — Selevchie, as doxologia's life writes the name — was one of the twelve martyrs of Caesarea in Palestine, who suffered in the reign of Diocletian. He was from the parts of Cappadocia, a brave soldier and a famous one in the companies of Rome, and he had confessed Christ at the beginning of the persecution against the Christians and been beaten hard for it. Stripped of his rank and his honour and driven out of his own city, he took to serving the sick who were thrown out into the streets, and to caring for orphans and the poor.

He was standing in the crowd at Caesarea while Porphyrius, the young servant of [Pamphilus](/saints/pamphilus-of-caesarea), was being burned at the stake for asking aloud that the martyrs' bodies be buried. When Porphyrius was dead, Seleucus ran after the condemned who were being led out to the sword, found them still alive and asking their executioners for time to pray, and told Pamphilus how his servant had ended; and Pamphilus rejoiced and gave thanks to God.

Then Seleucus made his own last reverence to the martyrs as they bent their heads under the sword. The soldiers saw it, knew him for a Christian, seized him and brought him to the governor, who ordered him beheaded at once. So, the life says, he went rejoicing to Christ the Master, having shared in the lot of the martyrs and the confessors. Doxologia gives him no year beyond the reign, and prints no troparion.
