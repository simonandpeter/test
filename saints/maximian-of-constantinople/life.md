# Maximian of Constantinople

Maximian lived in the years of the emperor Theodosius II, called the Younger, whose reign saint.gr dates 408 to 450. He came from old Rome, and from there he came to Constantinople. There he shone by his virtue and by the strictness of his life, and, being besides clever and widely learned, he was ordained priest by the patriarch Sisinnius.

When Sisinnius died he was succeeded by the heretic Nestorius, against whose heretical opinions Maximian reacted strongly. After Nestorius was exiled at last, Maximian came up to the throne by the vote of emperor and people, on 25 October 431. Under his patriarchate peace prevailed in the Church, without scandals and without heretical disturbances.

So having shepherded his flock well for two years and five months, he died peacefully on 12 April 434 — a day the page does not reconcile with the 21 April on which it keeps his memory. His synaxis, it says, is held at the church of the Holy Apostles. The page's couplet turns his name about: Maximian, not the all-devouring wolf, but the nourisher of the Church, is dead.

The Russian calendar gives him a birthplace, a trade of charity and the exact day of his enthronement. He was born at Rome, it says, of rich and devout parents, and at his own cost he made tombs for the burial of the dead who had been renowned for holiness of life. He was a simple man and loved to live far from the bustle of the world. At Constantinople the patriarch Сисиний (426–427) ordained him presbyter for the purity and virtue of his life; and after the heretic Несторий (428–431) had been deposed from the throne of Constantinople, the monk-presbyter was raised to the patriarchal throne on 25 October 431, in the reign of the emperor Феодосий Младший (408–450).

Its last sentence is where the folder and the calendar part company over a day. It says that the holy patriarch Maximian reposed in peace on 12 April 434, on Great Thursday — and the day page that keeps him, and his own name page, both put him on the twenty-first of April. Nine days are not the thirteen of the style conversion and nothing on either page repeats the twelfth as a feast, so the day page stands and he was not moved; the year, 434, is the one this folder already had.
