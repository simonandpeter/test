# Anastasius the Sinaite

Anastasius of Sinai was a monk of the seventh century who left the world and everything in it, and the page reckons him among the fathers of the Church. It is careful to say at once that he is not to be confused with Anastasius the patriarch of Antioch, who was also called the Sinaite and is kept on this same 20 April; the two are separate men, and this corpus keeps them as two. Besides today, he is commemorated with the assembly of the venerable fathers of Sinai on Bright Tuesday.

He was born at Alexandria in Egypt in the first half of the seventh century. While still young he took up his cross according to the Lord's commandment, received the monastic tonsure, and followed the Lord willingly. Desiring a harder ascetic life, he went first to Jerusalem to venerate the holy places, and came from there to Mount Sinai, where he found a company of monks living in severe asceticism and stayed to serve them.

So he came to a deep humility, and received from God great gifts of knowledge and of wisdom. He wrote lives of the holy fathers and composed a number of homilies, and in the end became abbot of the whole monastic community of Mount Sinai. He departed to the Lord in peace, in great old age, some time after the year 700.

Of his counsels the page gives one, on those who receive the holy Mysteries: that if a man has some small human sin, quickly forgiven — a theft of the mind, a sin of the tongue or the hearing or the sight, or one of vainglory or sorrow or anger — then once he has reproached himself for it and confessed it before the Lord he may receive; and that for such men the receiving is unto the forgiveness of sins.
