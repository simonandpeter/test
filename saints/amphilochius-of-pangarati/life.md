# Amphilochius of Pângărați

Amphilochius was born in 1487 somewhere in the north of Moldavia and began
the monastic life very young at Moldovița, from which he came to Pângărați
in 1508; there he was chosen by all as superior, and led the community for
fifty-six years. The hieromonk Anastasius of Moldovița, who knew him, says
of him: from childhood he laboured in the monastic life, witnessed by all
for his good deeds; he was a very skilled writer and knew the craft of
working wood, and was honoured and favoured by all the princes, though in
book-learning he showed himself a simple man; and to old age he governed
his life well, in hunger and thirst, in patience and labours, in every
spiritual striving, and from the demons bore many assaults until death, and
finished his life well, giving dust to dust and going the long road of the
fathers. His labours drew the care of Demetrius, the monastery’s patron,
who asked the prince Alexander Lăpușneanu by a divine appearance to build at
Pângărați a church in his name on the site of the old wooden one the Turks
had burned. While the voivode was at the market town of Piatra, on
22 October at midnight, a fair young man in white appeared to him in a dream
and said: rise, voivode, and go quickly to Pângărați, where a hermit
Amphilochius has lived nearly fifty years, and build a church in my name on
the place where I had one built by old Stephen the voivode in the days of
the elder Simeon; asked who he was, he answered, I am Demetrius the martyr
and soldier of Christ, imprisoned and tortured and pierced in the side at
Thessalonica under Maximian, whose name is glorified to the ends of the
world, and in this land I have no church but in the wilderness. The prince
came at once with all his boyars to the hermit and told him the vision —
though Scripture and the fathers teach us not to believe dreams, he said, I
gathered my boyars and philosophers and all judged it from God — and the
hermit said the same, and the two wept and talked long of the salvation of
the soul and the vanity of this world, which passes like a shadow, and of
how pious emperors had built holy monasteries and gathered the seven
councils; and after the weeping and the spiritual talk the voivode set a
great royal table for the elder, and asked him whether monks had lived on
that spot before him, and Amphilochius told him at length the life of
[Simeon](/saints/simeon-of-pangarati) the priest, the founder. So the
monastery was built and finished in the martyr’s name, and consecrated by
Gregory, metropolitan of Suceava, in 1560 at the prince’s command.

In 1566, knowing his end was near, he gathered his spiritual sons, gave
them his last word, kissed them in tears, left his disciple the hieromonk
Theodoritus as igumen in his place, and withdrew to Moldovița, the
monastery of his tonsure, where he took the great angelic schema under the
name Enoch — as one whose heart and longing were always lifted to heaven —
and added labours to labours for four years more. In 1570, learning from
the Holy Spirit the hour of his death, he received the Mysteries and gave
his soul in peace into the arms of Christ on 7 September, at the seventh
hour of the night, in the days of Bogdan the voivode; all this, Anastasius
writes, the elder Amphilochius told me himself, a great gift having been
given him before his departure, for he told me the day and the hour of it,
and let no one disbelieve it, for all of it is true — I have written it so
that it should not be forgotten after this generation. Honoured as a saint
in his lifetime, he was kept from the day of his death by the fathers of
both monasteries; the Romanian calendar keeps him on 7 September with
Simeon the founder, with a shared troparion.
