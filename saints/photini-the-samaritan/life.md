# Photini the Samaritan

The holy great martyr Photini, doxologia's life says, is the Samaritan woman of whom the Evangelist John tells in the Gospel: she who spoke with our God Jesus Christ at Jacob's well and believed in Him. After the Lord's ascension and the descent of the Holy Spirit on the apostles at Pentecost she was baptised by the apostles, together with two sons of hers and five sisters, and followed them, preaching faith in Christ from place to place and from country to country, and turning many servants of idols from ungodliness and making them Christians.

In the days of Nero a great persecution rose against the Christians, and after the apostles Peter and Paul had made their confession the persecutors hunted down their disciples and all who believed in Christ, labouring in vain to wipe the name of Christ out of the world; but the more they persecuted the faith, the more it grew, for the Lord had said that the gates of hell should not prevail against it.

Photini and her younger son were then at Carthage, in Africa, preaching the Gospel boldly. Her elder son Victor was a soldier in the Roman army, and because of great deeds in the war the Romans fought against the Arabs, who were harrying and plundering their lands, Nero had made him a commander and governor of the whole of Italy; not knowing he was a Christian, the emperor sent him to torment the Christians there.

Sebastian, the duke of Italy, told him plainly that he knew him for a Christian, and his mother and his brother Josiah with him, disciples of the apostle Peter, and advised him to do what the emperor had ordered so as not to put his own life in danger, and to warn his mother and his brother to stop preaching. Victor answered that he wished to do the will of Christ, the true God, the heavenly and deathless King; as for Nero's order to torment the Christians, he did not even want to hear it, much less carry it out.

The end, as the page tells it, was a long series of torments. The other martyrs, Sebastian, Photinos and Josiah, were mutilated, flayed, and thrown into an old bath. The five sisters of Photini were brought before the tyrant, their breasts cut off and their skin taken; and when the servants came to flay Photida she would not be held by anyone, but stood alone with great courage while they stripped the skin from her body, so that even the tyrant wondered at her endurance. Then he had two treetops bent down in his garden, bound her to them, and let them go, and she was torn in two and gave her soul into the hands of God.

The rest were put to the sword. Photini, drawn out of the dry well into which she had been thrown, was shut up in prison; and because she was left alone and had not been crowned with the others she grieved and prayed to God about it. He appeared to her, sealed her three times with the sign of the precious and life-giving cross, and made her whole of all her wounds; and after many days, praising and blessing God, she gave her honoured and holy soul into His hands. The page gives no year, and its troparion tab carries a recording and a single invocation, «Sfântă Muceniță Fotini, roagă-te lui Dumnezeu pentru noi!», and no hymn text.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, as a great-martyr, and names the company that suffered with her. saint.gr says she came from the Samaritan city of Sychar, and that the first notices of her are in the fourth chapter of the Gospel according to John; that every noon she went outside the city to the well called Jacob's and filled her water-jar, and that there one day she met Jesus Christ, who disclosed her whole life to her and told her that he himself is «τό ὕδωρ τό ζῶν», the living water, the inexhaustible spring of the Holy Spirit. That spiritual water the Lord gave to the Samaritan woman, who was baptised among the first women of Samaria and was named Photini. From then on she gave herself to the spreading of the Gospel in Africa and at Rome, and at Rome she was put to death by the emperor Nero when he learnt that she had made Christians of his daughter Domnina and some of her serving-women.

With her, the page says, there suffered her sons and her five sisters, and the calendar prints each of them on the same day: her sons [Photinos](/saints/photinos-son-of-photini) and [Joses](/saints/joses-son-of-photini), and her sisters [Anatole](/saints/anatole-sister-of-photini), [Photo](/saints/photo-sister-of-photini), [Photis](/saints/photis-sister-of-photini), [Paraskeve](/saints/paraskeve-sister-of-photini) and [Kyriake](/saints/kyriake-sister-of-photini). The second apolytikion on her page sings all eight of them together by name. Her head, saint.gr adds, is at the monastery of Gregoriou on the Holy Mountain, one of her feet and a shoulder-blade at Iviron, a forearm with the flesh on it at Pantokratoros, and fragments at the monasteries of Agia Lavra of Kalavryta, Geromeri of Filiates, Ntaou Pentelis and Kykkos in Cyprus.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 20 March, where the Romanian and the Greek keep her on 26 February, and tells her story in a single short entry. She is the Samaritan woman who had the rare good fortune, it says, of speaking with the Lord Christ himself at Jacob's well by Shechem, and it gives the Gospel reference for it. Having believed in the Lord she afterwards went out to preach his Gospel, with her two sons — whom the Prologue names Victor and Josiah, the first of them the son the Greek calendar and this corpus call [Photinos](/saints/photinos-son-of-photini) and the second [Joses](/saints/joses-son-of-photini) — and with her five sisters, [Anatole](/saints/anatole-sister-of-photini), [Photo](/saints/photo-sister-of-photini), [Photis](/saints/photis-sister-of-photini), [Paraskeve](/saints/paraskeve-sister-of-photini) and [Kyriake](/saints/kyriake-sister-of-photini).

They had come to Carthage in Africa. But they were condemned and brought to Rome, in the reign of the emperor Nero, and thrown into prison. By God's providence Nero's daughter Domnina came into contact with Saint Photina there and was converted by her to the faith of Christ. After the imprisonment they all suffered for Christ; and Photina, who at a well had first been lit up by the light of the truth, was thrown into a well, where she died and went to the immortal kingdom of Christ. The reign is the only date the entry gives, and it is the one this folder already records.
