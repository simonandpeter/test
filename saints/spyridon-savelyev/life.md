# Spyridon (Savelyev)

Spyridon Savelyev was born in 1875 in the village of Pikachi in Ploskosh district of Tver province, the son of the peasant Savely Savelyev, and served as a private in the First World War in 1916–1917. The authorities took almost everything he owned, leaving him only his cottage and bathhouse, and in 1935 sentenced him to six years for failing the sowing plan; he farmed on his own, outside the collective farm.

In July 1937, a few weeks after Stalin’s telegram ordering the arrest and shooting of former kulaks through the NKVD troikas, the head of the Ploskosh district NKVD in Tver region drew up a note for the arrest of the priest Andrew Bystrov and the peasant Basil Vinogradov: they had been deprived of voting rights and arrested before, knew one another and visited each other, and Bystrov was a priest. The village soviet described the priest’s pre-revolutionary land and livestock and charged him with gathering kulaks for secret meetings against the collective farm. Basil was arrested on 23 July and Andrew the next day, and both were taken to the prison at Toropets.

On 11 and 12 August the peasants Sergius Mikhailov and Spyridon Savelyev were arrested with them, and Semyon Nikandrov besides. All denied under questioning that they had ever agitated or met in secret; the priest said that he had spoken only of religious rites, as the new constitution allowed, and that if anyone had spoken against the authorities he would have told them that authority must be obeyed, for all authority is from God. Though there was no evidence of any crime, the case was sent to the troika, which on 3 October sentenced the priest and the three laymen to be shot; they were shot on 7 October 1937, and Semyon Nikandrov, sixty-seven, was given ten years in a labour camp. They were glorified by the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
