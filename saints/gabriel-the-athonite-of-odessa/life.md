# Gabriel the Athonite of Odessa

Gabriel was born on 8 January 1849 in the province of Kiev, of poor parents, and because the day of his birth was the day the Church keeps the memory of Saint George of Choziba he was given the name George at his baptism. At twelve he was left an orphan. His teachers helped him to schooling in the village, where he showed most diligence in the study of the word of God; he loved always to read the Scriptures and spiritual books, and had a clear mind and a clear memory.

In a grave illness he promised God that if he recovered he would visit the holy places of the Kiev country, and he soon kept the promise. Seeing the Kiev Lavra and the other monasteries, he was astonished to stand where the saints of the Lavra, of whom he had read in books, had struggled, and the desire rose in him to walk after them, to imitate their labours, to give up everything and devote his life to the monastic work. Going to Kiev a second time he asked the brothers of the Theophany skete to take him as a novice, and he was entrusted to Father Bonifatie to learn the monastic life. Imitating the elders of the desert, the young novice gave himself to hard labours and unceasing prayer.

In 1867 he went to Jerusalem, where he venerated the life-giving Tomb of the Lord and the other holy places, and the following year he reached Mount Athos. There he was amazed at the struggle and holiness of the monks, and was seized with a great desire to stay in the Garden of the Mother of God. He entered as a novice at the Skete of Saint Elias, whose abbot was Father Paisie, because the brothers there kept a stricter rule; and he was marked out among them by his gentleness and humility, preferring solitude and quiet. In 1869 he was tonsured with the name Gabriel, after the Archangel; in 1874 he was ordained hierodeacon, and in 1876 hieromonk.

That same year he was made steward of the skete, and given the obedience of going each year into Russia as captain of the ship belonging to the Skete of the Holy Prophet Elias. The ship was like a small monastery, with a little church in it and everything needed for the monastic life, and he kept that obedience year after year, fetching what the skete needed to live. In 1891 the Patriarch of Constantinople, Joachim III, raised him to archimandrite and gave him the government of the Skete of Saint Elias, after the repose of the Hieroschemamonk Tobie. Having high spiritual gifts from God, he led the life of the monastery skilfully, teaching the brothers the need of keeping the monastic rules strictly.

In 1894 he built at Odessa a fine cathedral in the Byzantine manner, with three altars, for the pilgrims who prepared there to go on to the Holy Mountain, and a small hostel for them in its courtyard; the church was consecrated on 22 December 1896. The page says he departed to the Lord on 19 October 1894, after a life full of labours, at Novonikolaevsk, as he was passing by ship on his way to the Skete of the Holy Prophet Elias — a date the same page's account of the consecration does not agree with, and both are left here as it prints them. His body was brought to Odessa on 2 November and buried in the crypt of the cathedral he had built.

His holy relics were uncovered on 22 July 1994 with the blessing of Metropolitan Agathangel of Odessa, and were laid in the cathedral of the Holy Prophet Elias. He was canonised by the Orthodox Church of Russia on 30 November 2017, with 22 July for the day of his commemoration.
