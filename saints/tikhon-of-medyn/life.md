# Tikhon of Medyn

days.pravoslavie.ru’s calendar names him on 16 июня ст. ст. as «Прп. Тихона Медынского, Калужского (1492).», and behind the line it keeps two readings of him. He lived in the fifteenth century, and the fuller reading says it is supposed that he came from Kiev. In his youth he came to Moscow and was tonsured a monk, by tradition in the Чудов monastery; and after a while, for love of solitude, he withdrew to an empty place seventeen versts from Калуга and fifteen from Медынь, whose names afterwards became part of his own, on the right bank of the small river Вепрейка.

He settled in thick forest, in the hollow of a giant oak which stood for nearly four centuries after his death, until a storm broke it in the early 1830s; in 1838 the abbot Геронтий of the house he had founded built a chapel over the great stump that was left. His food was wild herbs, and his drink the water of a healing well he dug himself at the river’s source, which is called to this day «кладезь преподобнаго Тихона». Word of his life drew disciples, and brethren gathered about him.

The prince Василий Ярославич, who owned those places, came upon his dwelling while hunting and ordered him off his lands at once, and insulting the hermit raised a whip at him. The lifted hand went numb on the instant and stayed still. Brought to his senses by the punishment, the prince repented, asked the monk’s forgiveness, was healed at his prayer, and then begged him to stay in his land for good and to set up a house there for his disciples, promising to want them nothing.

He founded the monastery in honour of the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God and was its superior, governed it into deep old age, and died in 1492, having received the great schema; his body was buried in the cathedral church of the house he had founded, and the all-Russian celebration of his memory was established at the council of 1584. The fuller reading adds that the famous standing on the Угра of 1480, which ended the Tatar yoke, happened near the place of his labours in his last years; that a wooden church of the Life-giving Spring was built over his well in 1887, where a record of the chief healings was kept through the nineteenth century; and that of his disciples the most renowned was Никифор Калужский, supposed to have succeeded him in the house.
