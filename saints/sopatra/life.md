# Sopatra

Sopatra was the daughter of the emperor Maurice, in whose time saint.gr places her and [Eustalia](/saints/eustalia), about the year 584. Eustalia had come from Rome, where she spent an inherited fortune on works of charity and served the girls who needed help because they had fallen into sin, and then went to Constantinople; and there she became known for her benefactions and her spiritual learning, so that many young women visited her at the monastery that housed her.

Sopatra was one of those young women, and she preferred the monastic life to the riches of the palace, and became a nun beside Eustalia. When Eustalia went first to the Lord, Sopatra carried on her work worthily to the end of her own life. That is the whole of what the page records of her: no year of death, no monastery of her own founding, no burial, and no hymn.

Her distich sets her before the throne: «Σωπάτρα Πατρὸς Πνεύματός τε καὶ Λόγου, / Θρόνῳ παρέστη, δοῦσα γῇ τὸ σαρκίον» — Sopatra stood before the throne of the Father and the Spirit and the Word, having given her poor flesh to the earth.

The Russian calendar's joint life for the two women gives this folder its first date and names her father: she was the daughter of the emperor Maurice, whose reign it dates 582 to 602. Drawn to the monastic life, she met [Eustalia](/saints/eustalia) in the church of the Mother of God at Blachernae, and after that conversation resolved to leave the world for good and to give her will wholly over to her as her teacher. The palace her pious father had given his daughter became by degrees a monastery known for the strictness of its rule. Eustolia died in 610 and she about 625.
