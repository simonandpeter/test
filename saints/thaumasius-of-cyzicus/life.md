# Thaumasius of Cyzicus

Cyzicus, says the life, was an old and famous city in the part of Asia called Lesser Mysia, on the sea of the Hellespont that divides Asia from Europe, next to the Troad through which the feet of the teacher of the nations, the apostle Paul, had passed; and it was from Paul, the chosen vessel, that the city received the seed of the word of God. Bright as it was with the light of the faith, it had at first few Christians, because of the emperors who reigned before Constantine the Great and who sent out dreadful orders and pagan governors to force all who confessed the name of Christ to worship idols, and to punish with torment and death those who would not.

Many hid themselves then, fleeing into the mountains and the desert, and others lived among the pagans and kept their faith secret; but those who loved Christ more confessed His name openly and gave themselves up of their own will. Such, the life says, were nine brave men, unmoved in the faith as the mountain of Sion and burning with zeal for God like Elias, whose names are these: [Theognis](/saints/theognis-of-cyzicus), [Rufus](/saints/rufus-of-cyzicus), [Antipater](/saints/antipater-of-cyzicus), [Theostichus](/saints/theostichus-of-cyzicus), [Artemas](/saints/artemas-of-cyzicus), [Magnus](/saints/magnus-of-cyzicus), [Theodotus](/saints/theodotus-of-cyzicus), Thaumasius and [Philemon](/saints/philemon-of-cyzicus). They were from different places and countries, and they came together at Cyzicus.

Caring nothing for the terrible imperial orders or the fear of the tyrants, they glorified Christ and preached Him with all boldness as the one God, maker and holder of all things, and they rebuked the Greek error that honoured demons instead of God, the creature instead of the Creator, the dead instead of the living. Seeing such boldness, the servants of the devil seized them like wolves taking sheep, bound them and brought them before the governor of the city.

They stood before the pagan rulers with firm souls, and were worn down with many kinds of torment and punishment, thrown into prison and brought out and tortured again, to make them deny Christ and sacrifice to the idols. They never departed from the Lord, and rebuked and shamed the rulers for their empty worship; so after further torments their heads were struck off, and their bodies were buried in that same place. The page gives no year, and says only that it was before the reign of Constantine the Great.

Some years later, when Constantine had been enlightened in baptism and the persecution had ceased, the Christians of Cyzicus took the nine bodies up out of the ground, found them uncorrupted, laid them in a new reliquary and built a church in their name. Many healings followed at the relics — a well-known man was freed of dropsy by touching the reliquary, and whoever was held by any illness was healed at a touch — and seeing them, a multitude of the unbelieving people turned to Christ, until almost the whole city had received the faith, broke its idols and raised churches in their place.

The Greek calendar keeps the nine on 28 Απριλίου, a day earlier than the Romanian, and saint.gr's whole notice of them is one paragraph. They came, it says, from different places, but they were seized all together at Cyzicus in the time of the persecutions. Brought before the local governor they showed a wonderful bravery and defended their faith with boldness and courage; and for that reason, and so that their strength might be broken, they were thrown into prison. There, without water and without bread, they prayed and glorified their Lord, who had counted them worthy to suffer for Him, and each gave courage to the other.

When the governor asked them out of the prison one last time whether they persisted in believing in Christ, they answered him all «εν ενί στόματι και μία καρδία», with one mouth and one heart, that they preferred martyrdom to denying the Maker and Creator and Saviour of the world; and beside himself with rage he ordered them beheaded at once, making them a present of the heavenly glory. The page gives no year and names no emperor. Its couplet calls them an image of the nine immaterial Orders, nine men whose heads were cut off, and unlike the Romanian source it prints an apolytikion of the nine together. saint.gr heads him «Άγιος Θαυμάσιος Μάρτυρας Εν Κυζίκω».

The Serbian church keeps the nine on this day, with the Romanian calendar and not with the Greek, which keeps them a day earlier; and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives them the century that neither of the other two would name. These nine brave martyrs, warmed by the love of Christ, would not offer sacrifice to the idols nor deny Christ the Lord, and for that they were bitterly tortured and at the last beheaded with the sword.

In the time of the emperor [Constantine](/saints/constantine-the-great) a church was built at Cyzicus in their honour, and their incorrupt relics were laid in it, and countless healings happened at them. All of them, the book says, despised everything temporal for the sake of the eternal and everything corruptible for the sake of the incorruptible, and the Lord therefore brought them into His eternal dwellings and crowned them with crowns of unfading glory. **They suffered and were glorified in the third century**, which is the first date this folder has carried: doxologia says only that it was before Constantine's reign, and saint.gr gives no year and names no emperor at all.

The Russian calendar keeps the nine together on 29 апреля and gives them the one life it has for all of them, which says more about who they were than about what was done to them. Cyzicus stood in Asia Minor on the shore of the Dardanelles, and Christianity had begun to spread there from the preaching of the apostle Paul; but the heathen persecutions drove some Christians out of the city and made the rest keep the faith secretly, so that by the end of the third century Cyzicus was largely heathen again, though it still had a church. The nine came from different places and were of different ages — «и юные, как святой Антипатр, и престарелые, как святой Руф» — and of different standing: there were soldiers among them, and villagers, and townsmen, and clergy. What they had in common was the faith and a burning wish to spread and strengthen it.

Finding themselves in Cyzicus they confessed Christ boldly and reproved the heathen impiety without fear, and were seized and brought before the governor of the city. For several days they were tortured, shut in prison and brought out again with the offer of freedom for a denial, and they went on glorifying the Name of Christ; and all nine were beheaded with the sword and buried near the city.

When «Константин Великий» came to rule the eastern half of the empire in 324 and the persecutions stopped, the Christians of Cyzicus took their incorrupt bodies out of the ground and laid them in a church built in their honour, where the sick were healed at the relics and the mad came back to their reason, and many of the heathen were converted. Under «Юлиан Отступник» the heathen of Cyzicus complained to the emperor that the Christians had wrecked their temples; he ordered the temples rebuilt and the bishop «Елевсий» imprisoned, and after that emperor's death the bishop was freed and the light of the faith shone in Cyzicus again.

The page closes in Russia. A monastery in honour of the nine was built near Kazan by a hierodeacon «Стефан», who brought part of their relics with him from Palestine, in the hope of deliverance by their intercession from sickness and especially from the fever that was raging in Kazan in 1687 — «ибо, пишет святитель Димитрий Ростовский, этим святым далась обильная благодать ко прогнанию трясавичных болезней», for to these saints was given abundant grace to drive away the shaking sicknesses. That hierarch wrote the service to the nine, described their sufferings, and composed a homily for their day; and the troparion the calendar prints asks them for exactly that healing.
