# Nicephorus the Leper

Nicephorus was born Nicholas Tzanakakis in 1890, the son of devout peasants, in the village of Sirikari in the district of Chania on Crete. His parents died while he was still a child and left him in his grandfather's care. At thirteen he moved to Chania and began work as a barber's apprentice, and soon afterwards the first signs of Hansen's disease appeared on him.

Because leprosy was a contagious sickness that inspired fear and horror, the sick were then shut up by force in the leper colony on the island of Spinalonga. Nicholas escaped from there to Egypt and worked for a time with a barber in Alexandria; and when the disease went on advancing, a cleric told him of the Lovokomeio, a hospice for lepers on the island of Chios, and he fled again, this time to Chios.

At twenty-four, in 1914, he reached the hospice, which was kept by the hieromonk Anthimos Vagianos, known later as [Anthimos of Chios](/saints/anthimos-of-chios). In its chapel of Saint Lazarus, where there was a wonderworking icon of the Mother of God the Hearkening, Nicholas found an air that opened his heart to faith and gave him a longing for a life of prayer; after two years Anthimos judged the young man ready and tonsured him with the name Nicephorus.

His disease advanced — no treatment for leprosy was found before 1947 — but he held to obedience, to fasting and to prayer, and worked in the hospice garden. He became inseparable from his spiritual father, and later wrote down the wonders of Anthimos that he had seen with his own eyes. He was first chanter of the hospice church until the disease took his sight. In 1957 the hospice was closed and he was moved, at nearly sixty-seven, to the leprosarium of Saint Barbara at Aigaleo west of Athens; his body and his eyes had been deeply harmed by then. He went to the Lord on 4 January 1964, at the age of seventy-four.
