# Misdas

saint.gr names six men together for this day and tells one story for all of them. [Aphrodisius](/saints/aphrodisius-of-scythopolis), Misdas, [Macrobius](/saints/macrobius-of-scythopolis), [Valerian](/saints/valerian-4-may), [Leontius](/saints/leontius-of-scythopolis) and [Antoninus](/saints/antoninus-of-scythopolis) were martyrs, it says, and with them others suffered at Scythopolis in Syria. Nothing on the page gives their country, their trades, their years or the emperor under whom they died.

These saints presented themselves of their own accord before the ruler of their country and reproached him most sharply for his inhuman doings against the Christians. Then they smashed the altars of the idols to pieces, and so provoked the fury of the idolaters, who set on them with stones and clubs and killed them all. The Christians took up their bodies, buried them with honours, and raised a church over them; and their synaxis used to be kept at the apostoleion of James the Brother of God, which stands inside the venerable house of the Mother of God at the Chalkoprateia.

Of Misdas the page says less than of any of the other five. It gives him a head line of his own, «Αγιος Μισδας ο Μαρτυρας», and then nothing: no couplet in the verses that follow, and not even the gloss of the name's meaning that it prints for Aphrodisius, Macrobius, Leontius and Antoninus. His name and his company are the whole record.
