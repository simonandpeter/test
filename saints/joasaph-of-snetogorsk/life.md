# Joasaph of Snetogorsk

saint.gr keeps two abbots of Pskov together on this day and gives them three sentences. The venerable martyrs [Basil](/saints/basil-of-mirozh) and Joasaph, it says, lived in the thirteenth century in the region of Pskov in Russia; Basil was abbot of the monastery of the Transfiguration of the Saviour of Mirozh, and Joasaph abbot of the monastery of the Nativity of the Theotokos of Mount Snatna; and the saints were martyred in the year 1299, when the Germans attacked Pskov and the monasteries of the region. Because the day's line names both, each is kept here as a folder of his own.

Of this one, beyond that, the page records nothing: no place of birth and no year for it, no family, no elder, no account of his years in the house, no manner of death beyond the raid it happened in, no burial and no relic. It prints no distich over either man and gives no hymn. The day's line and the body of the page spell his house two ways, Snetogorsk and Mount Snatna, and the display name here follows the day's line.

The corpus keeps seven other men of this name — of Alaska, of Belgorod, of India, of Kubensk, of Meteora, of Zographou, and a presbyter killed in 1918 — and none of them stands on this day or is named on this page.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, off one life shared with [Basil](/saints/basil-of-mirozh), and that life carries much the Greek notice does not. He was abbot of the house of the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos on the Снятная гора, and by some Pskov synodics its founder as well; the strict cenobitic rule he laid down there filled the monks' life with prayer, abstinence and work, and almost ninety years after his death the archbishop Дионисий of Suzdal reproduced those rules in a new charter for the same monastery. Two later ascetics of Pskov, Евфросин and Савва Крыпецкий, began their own labours in his house. The Russian day's line for 4 марта names only this abbot, where the Greek line of the same day names both men.

The two houses stood outside the city walls and had no defence. On 4 March 1299 the Germans fell on Pskov and set the Mirozh and Snetogorye monasteries alight, and the two abbots died in the fire in their churches together with other monks; many monks of other houses perished in the city as well, and women and children, while the men, the chronicler remarks, the Lord preserved by the prayers of the holy venerable martyrs, and under their prince Довмонт-Тимофей they went out and broke the attackers on the bank of the Пскова, beside the church of the holy apostles. Довмонт afterwards built a stone church at Снетогорье in place of the burnt one out of his own just property, and did much to restore the monastic life in the ruined houses.

The glorification followed soon after at Pskov, and the calendar sets down a disagreement in its own sources which it does not resolve: a Pskov Prologue on parchment of the fourteenth or fifteenth century puts their memory under 5 March, while the Pskov chronicle and the old Pskov synodics give 4 March as the day of their blessed repose, and 4 March is the day kept now. Both abbots were buried in the churches of their houses under a bushel, but his head and a part of his relics were kept openly, in a reliquary of their own in the church at Snetogorye. Among those who suffered with the two the chronicle names a presbyter Иосиф and the Prologue a presbyter Константин; whether that is two names for one man or two men, nothing read decides, and neither has a folder here.
