# Terence, husband of Neonilla

The blessed Terence, doxologia's life says, was a right-believing Christian, and married in lawful marriage [Neonilla](/saints/neonilla-wife-of-terence), who held the same faith as he did. They had seven sons — [Sarbelus](/saints/sarbelus-son-of-terence), [Photus](/saints/photus-son-of-terence), [Theodulus](/saints/theodulus-son-of-terence), [Vilas](/saints/vilas-son-of-terence), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-son-of-terence), [Nitus](/saints/nitus-son-of-terence) and [Eunicius](/saints/eunicius-son-of-terence) — and brought them up in the right faith.

Then all of them were taken by the pagans, parents and children alike, and brought before a lawless court, where they confessed Christ and reviled the idols. For this they were tortured beyond measure and then hung up; and while they were being tortured their wounds were sprinkled with vinegar and burned with fire, and the saints prayed quietly for one another and comforted one another.

God did not overlook their prayers, but sent his holy angels, who loosed them from their bonds and healed their wounds. The pagans, seeing them loosed and their wounds healed, were terrified, and gave them to the beasts to be eaten; but they suffered no harm, because at God's command the beasts changed their cruelty for the gentleness of sheep. Then they were thrown into a cauldron of boiling pitch, and at once the fire went out and the pitch grew cold. Seeing that the tortures would not touch them, the pagans cut off their heads with the sword, and so they came to their end.

The Greek calendar keeps the whole household on the same day, 28 October, and saint.gr's notice is about the family rather than about any one of them. It calls them a true «κατ᾿ οἶκον ἐκκλησία», a church in the house, citing 1 Corinthians 16.19: a Christian family with one soul, one heart and one mind. When the persecution began, word came to the parents that they were in danger of arrest, and they asked themselves whether to go far away to protect their children or to stay and await martyrdom bravely. The five sons and the two daughters gave the decisive answer: why flee, when the persecution had spread everywhere, and when their going would sow panic among the Christians there? The Church, they said, is not strengthened by fugitives but by martyrs. So all of them stayed, confessed Christ, and died together by beheading.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps the household on the same day and tells it in one line — «Мчч. Терентия и Неониллы и чад их» and then the seven children by name — with a separate name link behind each of the nine. This one reads «Терентий Сирийский, мч.», which adds to the bare forename the province neither the Romanian nor the Greek page names: Syria. That line also carries the year the other two calendars left out, «ок. 249-250», and it is the household's year and not this one person's. The day page prints two hymns for them, a common of martyrs under their name and a kontakion of their own that calls on Terence the most wise and his consort to heal the sicknesses of our souls.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 28 October and gives him three sentences. He was from Syria, it says — which is the toponymic the Russian calendar attaches to his name — and he suffered for the faith of Christ together with his wife and his seven children. After many torments, during which the power of God was made manifest, all of them were beheaded with the sword. The book names none of the nine and adds no year, so this paragraph adds no name and no date either; the one thing in it the other calendars did not carry is that the power of God showed itself while they were being tortured.
