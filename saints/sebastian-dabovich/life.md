# Sebastian (Dabovic)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar closes the Julian 17 November with him, «Прп. Севастиана Джексонского (1940) (Серб.).», and prints no life page behind it; his name page carries a title and a list of days and no text. The life here is the one the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru prints for the civil 30 November. He was born in 1863 at San Francisco, in a family of Orthodox emigrants from Serbia, and was Jovan Dabovic in the world. That page prints two lives and they give the day of his birth differently, 9 June in the short one and 9 July in the full one, so only the year is recorded here.

In 1884 he entered the theological academy at St Petersburg; the shorter life says he studied at the academies of Kiev and of St Petersburg both. Four years later, in 1887, he was tonsured a monk there with the name Sebastian and made a deacon. When his education was finished he returned to the United States and served at San Francisco, and in 1892 he was ordained priest and became a missionary on the western coast; the short life names the bishop of the Aleutians and Alaska who ordained him, under whose omophorion he opened a wide work of teaching, mission and pastoral care in Washington, California and Minneapolis.

He founded several churches in Washington and California. In 1894 he built one at Jackson in California for the Serbian community there, dedicated to the Serbian archbishop Sava, and around it the first Serbian national parish in America was formed. Afterwards he worked in the administration of the North American mission under [Tikhon of Moscow](/saints/tikhon-of-moscow), then its bishop and later patriarch, preaching in English, Serbian and Russian. In the course of his life he crossed the Atlantic fifteen times and the Pacific nine, for he had to be in Serbia and in Russia, and to make missionary journeys to Japan.

In 1905, already an igumen, he was appointed dean of the Serbian churches, and in the same year, by a decree of the Synod, head of the newly founded Serbian spiritual mission, which acted within the Aleutian and North American diocese; for his labours for the Church he was raised archimandrite that year. In 1910 he left for Serbia, where he served as a chaplain through the Second Balkan war of 1912 and 1913 and the First World War of 1914 to 1918, and from 1936 he lived at the monastery of Zica.

He died there on 30 November 1940 — the civil day on which the Julian 17 November of that year fell, so that the day of his death and his feast are one day. His funeral was served by [Nicholas Velimirovich](/saints/nicholas-velimirovich). In 2007 his relics were reburied in the church at Jackson in California which he had founded. In 2015 he was numbered among the saints: by a decision of the Bishops’ Council of the Serbian Orthodox Church on 29 May, and the glorification itself was served on 4 September in the cathedral of St Stephen at Alhambra in California by the Serbian patriarch Irinej. The calendar marks his line «(Серб.)», Serbian.
