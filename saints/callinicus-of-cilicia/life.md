# Callinicus of Cilicia

Callinicus was born in the parts of Cilicia and brought up in the right Christian faith. When he had come to the age of a grown man he saw many people denying Christ the Master and holding to paganism: darkened by the devil's deceit they believed in stones that had no breath and offered foul sacrifices to idols. Callinicus wept for their perishing and began to teach them openly the knowledge of the truth, turning the Greeks from their error to Christ God.

So he went through many cities and villages preaching the word of God, and came also to Ancyra, the city of Galatia, seeking the salvation of men's souls. He spent a long time there and laboured at the good news of Christ, and won many to God; and then he was taken by the unbelieving and brought to judgement before a nobleman named Sacerdon, a most foul man and a hot servant of the devils, an enemy of Christ and a cruel tormentor of Christians. All the pagans cried out to that judge that this was Callinicus, who had come from other countries into their city and was teaching the people not to sacrifice to the gods nor bow to them, and was leading many after his deceit. The judge looked at him sharply and began to question him in a terrible voice, asking how he dared, a stranger, to stir up the peoples and teach them to leave the gods who had made the world and whom the emperor honoured.

What was done to him after that is longer than what is given here. At the last the soldiers who led him were unwilling to put to death Callinicus their benefactor, who had saved them on the road; but fearing death themselves at the governor's hand they carried out his command against their will. They heated a furnace and set the martyr near the flame of it as it burned; and he, rejoicing and glad, signed himself with the sign of the Cross and prayed: I thank You, heavenly Father, that You have made me worthy of this hour, in which I die for Your holy name; receive my spirit in peace, and put Your enemies to shame, Almighty God.

Saying this he went into the fire, into the middle of the furnace, and lying down he gave his holy soul into the hands of God. When the furnace had gone out his honourable body was found unhurt by the fire, and the faithful took it and buried it with the honour due to it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 29 July, and tells the story with other places in it. saint.gr says Callinicus came from Cilicia, that he was pious and virtuous, and that he had the catechising of the heathen for his life's work, with their salvation in view. When he had reached Egypt, fanatical idolaters rose against him, seized him and brought him to the governor Sacerdon — the same judge the Romanian account names, though that one has him taken at Ancyra in Galatia. The governor played the hypocrite and made a show of pitying him, and to bend his resolve told him of supposed cases of once brave Christians who had denied their faith when they came face to face with the hard torments.

Callinicus saw through the pretence, smiled, and said: do not put off, prefect, taking the measure of the power with which Christ arms his true believers; make ready quickly all your instruments of punishment, fire, swords, wheels, knives, whips and whatever other hard torment you have, for all of these and more and harder besides I long for, for the love of Christ. The prefect did indeed scourge him hard. He shod him in shoes that had nails in them and made him run as far as the city of Gangra, eighty stadia off; he tore his flesh with iron claws, and half dead as he was tied him behind a wild horse that dragged him for many kilometres; and such was the prefect's rage that before Callinicus could draw his last breath he threw him into the fire. His synaxis, saint.gr adds, was kept near the bridge of Justinian, and near a place called Petrion.
