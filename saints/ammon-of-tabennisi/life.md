# Ammon of Tabennisi

The Ohrid Prologue gives Ammon four sentences and no more. It calls him a great ascetic of the fifth century, and sets him at the head of the Tabennisiot monastery in upper Egypt, where, it says, some three thousand monks lived under his direction. To that it adds that he was given the grace of working wonders and of seeing what was hidden from other men.

Of his teaching the Prologue keeps one saying. A monk came to him for counsel, and he answered that a man should be like an offender in prison, who asks without ceasing when the judge will come — so question yourself, and in fear. The calendar gives no year of his birth or of his death, no account of how he came to the monastery, and no name for him but Ammon.
