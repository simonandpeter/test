# Demetrius (Morozov)

Demetrius Mikhailovich Morozov was born in 1875 in the village of
Melnikovo in the Yaransk district of Vyatka province, a peasant, and
served in the army from 1911 to 1914. On 27 November 1936 he was arrested
on a charge of taking part in a counter-revolutionary group of churchmen,
and on 27 March 1937 the special board of the NKVD sentenced him to five
years in the camps; he was held in the prisons of Yaransk and Kirov and
later of Petropavlovsk in northern Kazakhstan, and on 16 May 1937 moved to
the Ortau section of the Karaganda camp. On 7 August 1937 a report on
Demetrius Morozov and [Peter Bordan](/saints/peter-martyr-1937-26-august)
reached the camp’s commissioner: the prisoners Morozov and Bordan do no
work, but systematically carry on anti-Soviet agitation; they hold that
the Soviet power is of Antichrist, built on violence, and that one must
serve only God; they sang “God save the Tsar”, “O Lord, save thy people”
and other prayers; they do not work in the camp, declaring that they will
not work for Antichrist — Bordan more guarded in his judgements, but by a
skilful psychological approach drawn into admitting that on religious
grounds he would not work in Soviet institutions; from morning to night
they demonstratively hold church services in the presence of the other
prisoners, and declare that God sent this power as a punishment for our
sins, that the time is the last, that aeroplanes, radio, motor-cars and
the rest are the invention of the devil and Antichrist, that every
technical invention is aimed at destroying people, and that one can live
without working — we have lived nine months without working and by God’s
help have not died; and because of them six or seven men do not go out to
work. On 22 August 1937 the two were arrested, on 31 August the special
troika of the Karaganda NKVD sentenced them to be shot, and on 3 September
1937 — 26 August of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps
him — they were shot. The jubilee Council of Bishops of 2000 numbered them
among the saints; the Russian calendar also keeps him in the synaxis of
the saints of Vyatka.
