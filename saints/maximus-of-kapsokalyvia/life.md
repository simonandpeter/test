# Maximus of Kapsokalyvia

Maximus, whom doxologia's life places in the year 1320, was from Lampsacus, of good family, his parents believing and virtuous people who, being childless, begged God with tears for a son; and God heard them and gave them this one, whom they named Manuel at his baptism. They counted him a gift of God, as he was, and raised him with much love and care and taught him the holy books, and when he was old enough they brought him into the church of the Most Holy Theotokos, where he sang with a sweet voice and a divine longing and prayed with humility for his own salvation. He seemed another Samuel, growing in age and in grace, loved and praised by all, for he had no childish thoughts but a man's mind from the beginning of his years.

He used to go often to the venerable old men who kept silence nearby, to hear from them what profits the soul, and to serve them when he was free. The divine longing burning in him, he would strip off his own clothes and put them on the poor and go shivering, and give bread away in secret to the hungry; and to hide what he did he played the madman before his parents and his kinsmen, though his goodness would not stay hidden. When his parents forgot that they had given him to God and made ready to marry him, at seventeen years of age he left them and the world and everything in it, and went to the mountain called Ganos, where he put on the monastic habit and was called Maximus in place of Manuel, under a skilled elder named Mark.

When Mark died, Maximus went through Macedonia and the mountains near it looking for another elder as good, and found holy men on Mount Papikion who lived on the hills, in caves and desert places, with nothing about them but the old rags they wore. After a long time with them he went to Constantinople, and having venerated the relics laid up there he came to the church of the Theotokos called Hodegetria to see the wonders worked in it; and he was so astonished at them that he stayed the whole night in the church, barefoot, bareheaded, in a single hair shirt, so that everyone took him for a madman. He was playing the fool for Christ, as the great Andrew had done.

The emperor Andronicus Palaeologus (1282-1328) heard of him, called him to the palace and talked with him before many people; and Maximus answered him out of Gregory the Theologian, as his habit was, and out of the Scriptures. The practised rhetors marvelled that a man who had never learned grammar knew such things, though he set his words down without art; and the Grand Logothete of the Kanikleion said to those present, "The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau". Hearing it, Maximus left at once, calling them empty of mind, and never went back to the court. But he went often to Athanasius, then patriarch, whom he called the new Chrysostom, and he lived in the courts of the church of the Theotokos at Blachernae, in hunger and thirst and vigil, praying and weeping through the nights and playing the fool by day, so that the wind of men's approval should not shake the fruit off his virtue.

Afterwards he went to Thessalonica to venerate the myrrh-streaming Demetrius, and from there to the Holy Mountain of Athos, going round the monasteries; and there he took to burning the hut he lived in whenever he moved on, which is what his Romanian name says of him. Gregory the Sinaite, who had settled at the skete called Magoula and was a wonderful teacher of stillness and of prayer, sent disciples to fetch him; they could not find him, for it was winter and he was living in the caves in the thickets of the desert, and he came to them himself at the cell of Saint Mamas. Gregory was astonished at what he said and would not call him a man but an earthly angel, and begged him to stop burning his hut and to settle in one place and bear more fruit, now that old age had come on him and death often comes untimely.

He spent fourteen years in the cave near the Panaghia where his hut stood, and then came down near the Great Lavra, only so as to hear the bells, which he called the spiritual instruments; and there he made himself a small hut, and in it he stayed until the end of his life.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and dates his death to 1365. saint.gr gives his name in the world as Manuel and his birth at the city of Lampsacus, of devout parents who brought him up in the discipline and admonition of the Lord; he was tonsured a monk at a monastery on Mount Ganos, and after short stays at Constantinople and Thessalonica came at last to the monastery of the Lavra on the Holy Mountain. Because he moved so often, the page says, he used to burn his hut down, so as to practise a complete possessionlessness — and it is from that, «Καυσοκαλύβης», the hut-burner, that his name comes. He fell asleep peacefully in 1365, at the age of ninety-five.
