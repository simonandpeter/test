# Nicholas (Shirogorov)

Nicholas was born on 9 November 1904 in the village of Zdekhovo in the
Bogorodsk district of Moscow province, the son of the priest Demetrius
Shirogorov, finished four classes of the village school and two of the
church school, and got no further, since the godless who had come to power
closed every Orthodox school first of all. Called up for a time in the civil
war into the Red Army’s rear militia, he was ordained deacon after his
discharge and sent to the church of the Nativity of Christ at Yamkino in the
Noginsk district. In the summer of 1937, when the wholesale and merciless
persecution of the Church began, the Noginsk NKVD decided to arrest him for
having said, after the arrest of a number of churchmen: believing citizens,
the Soviet power persecutes the Christian religion and puts innocent priests
in prison, and you pass it over in silence; we must demand in an organised
way that the Soviet power stop this persecution of the faith; the communists
write that there is freedom of religion, and in fact they arrest believers
and priests every day; if we declare an organised protest we shall save our
Christian faith from the antichrists. He was arrested on 26 November 1937
with his rector, the archpriest Nicholas Pokrovsky, and the psalmist Michael
Amelyushkin, and put in the Noginsk prison; questioned the next day he said
he had done no counter-revolutionary work, that he had dealings with the
priest and the psalmist but no anti-Soviet activity with them, and none
aimed at wrecking the election meetings. On 1 December 1937 the troika gave
him ten years in the camps, and he was sent to the Bezymyanka camp near
Samara, where he died on 24 September 1942 and was buried in an unknown
grave. The life is by the priest Maxim Maksimov; the Church keeps him on 11
September of the old style, 24 September, and in the synaxis of the Moscow
saints.
