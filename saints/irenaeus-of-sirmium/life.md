# Irenaeus of Sirmium

Irenaeus suffered in the persecution of Diocletian and Maximian (284–305).
He was a presbyter, bringing up his children with his wife in Christian
piety and much respected for his learning and the strictness of his life,
and was afterwards made bishop of Sirmium in Pannonia, the capital of the
province — a pure and active pastor, the Greek synaxarion says, full of
sincere love for his flock and ready to give his life for it after the Good
Shepherd.

For his fervent preaching of the faith, and, the synaxarion says, for
drawing pagans to the religion of Jesus, he was seized and brought before
the governor Probus; asked whether the charge was true, he prayed in front
of him instead of answering, and for refusing to deny Christ and sacrifice
to the gods he was given over to torture. His parents, his relatives and his
friends watched it and tried to persuade him to yield, and he stayed
unmoved; after cruel torments he lay long in prison, and Probus tried to
break him by urging him to spare his life for his sons’ sake.

My sons believe in God, he answered, who will take care of them; and nothing
will make me deny my Christ. The governor ordered him thrown into the river.
He was led to the bridge over the Sava, knelt, and prayed for the last time
to the Lord for the flock of the church of Sirmium; then he was beheaded and
his body thrown into the river — his head, the Greek synaxarion says, which
gives the year as 288. The Greek calendar keeps him on 23 August, and the
Russian calendar on 26 March of the old style, 8 April, counting him among
the saints of Serbia, Sirmium being Sremska Mitrovica.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 6 aprilie, and adds nothing to the account: doxologia's page for him carries no life tab and no troparion, only his name, the day and the appointed readings. Its line names him in full as the holy hieromartyr Irenaeus, bishop of Sirmium, which agrees with the Greek entry in the see and in the rank, and it puts him two days before the 8 April on which the Russian calendar's 26 March of the old style falls.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 26 March — a third day for a man the Romanian keeps on 6 April and the Greek on 23 August — and gives the scene at his trial. It supposes that he was a Slav, and that before his episcopate he was married and had children. He suffered for Christ in the time of Maximian. Under the heavy torments his own kinsfolk stood about him and begged him weeping to spare himself and them, which is to say to deny Christ; but this wonderful hieromartyr, the Prologue says, loved wounds for Christ more than all the goods of this world.

Since he would not renounce the faith on any terms, the prince Probus ordered him thrown from a bridge into the river Sava, and there this shepherd of Christ's flock came to his end and passed over among the citizens of heaven. The entry dates it 304, which falls inside the interval this folder already holds, so the interval stands as it was. At the same time, it says, a gardener of Srem called Serenus suffered under the same Probus, and an Afer at Regium; neither of those men was read as any folder in this corpus and neither takes anything here.
