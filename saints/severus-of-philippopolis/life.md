# Severus of Philippopolis

Severus, [Memnon](/saints/memnon-the-centurion) and thirty-seven martyrs with them suffered at Philippopolis in Thrace under Diocletian. For his firm and fearless confession of faith in Christ Severus was raked with iron hooks; then red-hot rings were put on the fingers of his hands and a red-hot iron belt about his waist, and after these torments he was beheaded. When the governor learned that Severus had turned the centurion Memnon to Christ, he ordered him tortured too: Memnon was stretched and three strips cut from the skin of his back, and with him suffered thirty-seven more. All of them had their hands and feet cut off and were thrown into a burning furnace, in 304.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 20 August, the same menologion day on which the Russian calendar names him with Memnon the centurion. Doxologia.ro names him and gives no life: the page its calendar links for him carries neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab, so the line «Sfântul Mucenic Sever» is the whole of what it prints, and it does not print Memnon on that day.

The Greek calendar keeps the two on the same day, 20 August, and gives Severus a birthplace and parents. saint.gr says he was from Side in Pamphylia, the son of Petronius the Thracian and of Mygdonia, and that when he came to Philippopolis and saw the Thirty-seven Holy Martyrs contending bravely for Christ he too confessed with boldness the one and true God. At once they tore his flesh with red-hot iron claws, then sawed him, then put a red-hot iron belt on him, and at the end beheaded him.

Memnon, the page says, suffered like tortures, having confessed Christ with the same daring; and him in the end they burned alive inside a lit furnace. The page gives each a distich: Severus found worthily, by the sword, bright prizes for a suffering endured by the sword, and of Memnon it says «Ἐχει τὸ πῦρ σε πρὸς βραχὺν Μέμνον χρόνον, Μένει δὲ σε στέφανος ἐς ἀεὶ μένων» — the fire holds you, Memnon, for a short time, but a crown awaits you that remains for ever. The company whose example drew them out, the Thirty-seven martyred at Bizye in Thrace, has its own entry on the same day, and that entry's two lists of their names do not agree with each other.

*After the life printed by the Sretensky calendar (days.pravoslavie.ru) — [the entry](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life4484.htm); read 23 August 2026; and doxologia.ro's calendar for 20 august — [the day](https://doxologia.ro/20-august), whose page for him carries no life, read 24 September 2026; and saint.gr's calendar for 20 Αυγούστου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/08/20/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2301/saint.aspx), read 1 October 2026.*
