# Phousik, companion of Simeon of Persia

saint.gr adds him to the company at the last moment. King Shapur II, on hearing that the imprisoned bishop [Simeon](/saints/simeon-of-persia) had brought the apostate [Gothazat](/saints/gothazat-companion-of-simeon) back to Christ, had the two of them beheaded, and with them another 1150 Christians of the church there together with the presbyter [Audelas](/saints/audelas-companion-of-simeon). Phousik was the king's own kouropalates, a secret Christian; and when he saw one of the 1150 showing some agitation, he went up to him and urged him to stay faithful to the end.

When Shapur learnt of that, the page says, he ordered him killed in the harshest way there was: they cut out his tongue, and then they flayed him alive. His couplet turns the flaying into a garment: «Τὸ δέρμα Φουσὶκ ἐκδεδάρθω μου λέγει, Χιτὼν ὑφανθεὶς τοῦ Σατανᾶ τῇ κρόκῃ» — let my skin be flayed off, says Phousik, a tunic woven with Satan's weft. The page gives him no year, no family and no country of his own beyond the Persian court.
