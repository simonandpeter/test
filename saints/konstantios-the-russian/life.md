# Konstantios the Russian

saint.gr's calendar for 26 December keeps Konstantios the Russian, a new hieromartyr. He was born at Novgorod in Russia and was tonsured a monk in the Lavra of the Caves of Kyiv. He served as parish priest in the holy church of Hagia Sophia of Kyiv, and afterwards as priest of the church of the Russian embassy at Constantinople. He was unusually well educated and knew Russian, Turkish and Greek; everybody held him in great respect and sincere esteem, and he was marked out for a bishopric.

During the Russo-Turkish war he came to the Holy Mountain and lived as a monk at the monastery of the Great Lavra. After a pilgrimage to Jerusalem he returned to the Great Lavra and was joined there with the former bishop of Arta, Neophytos, and with the man who was to write his life, papa-Ionas the Kausokalyvite. When peace was made between Russia and Turkey the saint went back again to Constantinople and stayed on as priest at the same embassy.

There, for reasons the page says are unknown, he fell into a quarrel with the Russian ambassador; and whether out of fear or out of anger, he presented himself before the Sultan and denied Christ. For that act he met with great honours and attentions from the Turks. A few days later he felt the greatness of his fall, wept bitterly in repentance and longed for martyrdom. So he threw off the Turkish clothes, put on a worn cassock, presented himself before the Sultan, confessed Christ with courage and renounced the religion of Mohammed.

Without any process of law the Turks took the martyr and beheaded him in front of the palace of the Sultan, in the year 1742, or, as the same page allows, 1743. The memoir of the hieromartyr Konstantios was drawn up by his good friend papa-Ionas the Kausokalyvite, and is contained in the New Martyrologion. No hymn is printed for him, and the feast is marked fixed.
