# Athenogenes of Pedachthoe

In the days of Diocletian, the pagan emperor of Rome, there was a cruel persecution of the Christians, and a governor named Philomarchus came to Sebaste, a city of Armenia. He gathered a crowd of idol-servants and bade them crown themselves and sacrifice to their gods with trumpets, cymbals, lutes and flutes; then, sitting at the tribunal before the people, he had the herald call the citizens and the men of standing to come and sacrifice by the emperor's order. The whole crowd cried out that they were Christians and would not bow to idols, and the governor had the soldiers kill them, and many died for Christ.

After that a commander of the troops named Nicholas came to the governor and told him of a Christian bishop, Athenogenes, who was leading many into his faith and living in the village of Pedachthoe. The governor sent soldiers at once. Athenogenes was living in fasting and prayer with ten disciples in a little monastery near that village, in a quiet place apart, but he was away on the monastery's business when the soldiers came, so they seized his disciples, bound them and took them to Sebaste, and then searched everywhere for him and did not find him.

Coming back and not finding his brethren, he went into the prayer house and, looking at the holy cross there, said with tears: Where are the brethren I entrusted to you? Who will make the accustomed prayers with me? Where are the beautiful olive branches? He walked about the monastery in great grief, not understanding where they had gone. A doe he had lately fed from his own hands ran up to him and did him reverence and kissed his hands; and then a man he knew met him and asked where he had been when the governor's soldiers took his disciples away bound to be tortured.

Hearing it, the saint was filled with joy and, looking up to heaven, thanked Christ for calling His chosen servants to the heavenly kingdom. He let the doe go into the wilderness and went in haste to Sebaste after his disciples. Coming into the governor's court he cried out with a loud voice: Torturer, why have you robbed me and plundered my church? God see the evil you have done and turn His anger on you. The soldiers seized him and brought him in, saying that this was the Athenogenes they had been told to find, and that he was grossly insulting the governor's authority.

The governor had him thrown into the prison where his disciples were held. Seeing his brethren the saint rejoiced greatly and said to them: My sons, may your peace be multiplied and the grace of Christ be with you. Since you have been found pleasing to God, He has called you to the heavenly supper; so take courage and do not fear the torturer's threats, for tomorrow morning the Lord will grant you to finish your contest and to dwell in the joys of paradise with the angels and the companies of the saints.

Next day the governor called them all before him and ordered them to sacrifice, threatening to destroy them as he had destroyed the disobedient before them. Athenogenes answered that those he called destroyed had not perished but were in heaven dancing with the holy angels, and when the governor pressed him again he called him a lawless tormentor and said they were ready to bear all things for their God. Then the ten disciples were stripped, hung up, beaten cruelly and raked with iron combs, and they said as they bore it: We are ready for wounds for the sake of the deathless Christ; we will not deny You, our Master and our God. Seeing their courage unbroken, the governor had them beheaded.

The governor then turned on Athenogenes, and when the martyr said that God had delivered him and would deliver him from his devices he had him beaten cruelly over the ribs. The saint cried, In You, Lord, have I hoped; save me in Your mercy — and a voice was heard from above saying, Take courage, My chosen one; I am with you, I am your God who keeps you. The servants who were torturing him heard it too, and fear took them and their hands went stiff so that they could torture him no further. A friend of the governor named Philip, sitting beside him, said that he had told him these Christians were sorcerers, and that he should destroy this one at once before the onlookers were deceived; and the governor condemned him to the sword.

Athenogenes asked to be taken to his own monastery and killed there, and the governor did not refuse. On the way he sang verses of David's psalms, rejoicing that his soul was to be loosed from his body and pass to the Lord. As he came near, the doe came out of the wilderness to meet him and bowed at his feet, and he said to her: You have lost the brethren, and now you will lose the one who fed you; may the God of the ages not give you and your offspring to the hunters, but may your offspring bring its young here at our memorial, to be slaughtered and eaten to the glory of God by those who come to our feast. The doe wept, and he signed her with the cross and told her to go in peace into the heart of the wilderness.

Then he went into his monastery and prayed that God receive his soul in peace and grant him to be with his brethren, and give His eternal blessing to all who honour his memory with love and swift help in all their needs to all who pray with the naming of his name; and a voice from heaven said, Today you will be with Me and with your disciples in paradise, and all your desire will be fulfilled. Filled with great joy he bowed his head gladly under the sword and was beheaded, and the faithful buried his honoured body in that same place. Christians gathered there every year on the day of his end, and every year the doe he had fed and blessed came at his command to the feast and brought one of her young to be offered, and then went back into the wilderness, and nobody hindered her.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 16 July, and saint.gr gives three things the Romanian life did not. It says his homeland was Sebastia in Cappadocia, and that his learning, his warm faith and his brave works of charity were what made him bishop of Pedachthoe. It gives the governor's name as Philimarchos. And it adds that to Athenogenes we owe the vesper hymn «Φῶς ἱλαρόν», whose text it prints in full.

The third thing is the ten disciples who were taken and beheaded with him, whom doxologia's life leaves unnamed throughout: [Reginus](/saints/reginus-disciple-of-athenogenes), [Maximinus](/saints/maximinus-disciple-of-athenogenes), [Patrophilus](/saints/patrophilus-disciple-of-athenogenes), [Athenogenes](/saints/athenogenes-disciple-of-athenogenes) who bore his master's name, [Antiochus](/saints/antiochus-disciple-of-athenogenes), [Ammon](/saints/ammon-disciple-of-athenogenes), [Theophrastus](/saints/theophrastus-disciple-of-athenogenes), [Cleonicus](/saints/cleonicus-disciple-of-athenogenes), [Peter](/saints/peter-disciple-of-athenogenes) and [Hesychius](/saints/hesychius-disciple-of-athenogenes). The page heads the eleven of them, the bishop and his ten, with the year 311.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same 16 July, but under a different see: the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads him bishop of Sebaste, in Armenia, where this folder is named for Pedachthoe, and its body has the persecutor coming to Sebaste and the monastery standing near that city. Both readings are recorded and neither is preferred. The Prologue says he lived in a monastery near the city with ten of his own disciples, and that in the time of Diocletian a fierce tormentor of Christians named Philomarchus came there and seized and killed many Christians in the city. When he saw the old man and his disciples he told him to sacrifice to the idols, so as not to perish as the others had perished.

Athenogenes answered: O torturer, those you name as perished have not perished, but are in the heavens and rejoice with the angels. Then the Prologue gives a scene no other calendar in this folder has. A doe which the merciful Athenogenes had fed with his own hand ran to him and, seeing him in his distress, shed tears — and the beasts of the mountain, the book says, had more pity on the martyrs of Christ than the pagans had. An angel of God eased the martyrs in their heavy torments; and at the last all were beheaded with the sword, first the priests and fellow-workers of Athenogenes and then Athenogenes himself, and they passed over into their heavenly homeland in the year 311 — the year this folder already held.
