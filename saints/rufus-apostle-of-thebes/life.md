# Rufus of Thebes

Rufus, whom the page also gives as Roufos, is numbered among the seventy apostles. He was bishop in Thebes in Greece, and he is one of those the apostle Paul names in the letter to the Romans. His commemoration in the Orthodox Church is kept on 8 April, and that, with the painter's rule below, is the whole of what doxologia prints for him: no country of birth, no year, no account of his preaching and no manner of death of his own.

The day keeps him with five other apostles — [Agabus](/saints/agabus-the-apostle), [Herodion](/saints/herodion-the-apostle), [Phlegon](/saints/phlegon-of-marathon), [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle) and [Hermes](/saints/hermes-apostle-of-dalmatia) — and the page for Agabus says of all six that they ended on the same day as a result of the torments they were put to. This Rufus is not the archbishop of Thessalonica whom the corpus keeps on 9 September, nor the Rufus who was a son of Paul and Tatta, nor the Rufus of 8 September who suffered with Rufianus; the four are kept apart here.

Dionysius of Fourna, in his Hermeneia, says that the apostle Rufus is to be painted as a man not very old, his hair turning grey, with a broad beard, holding in his hand a rolled scroll, the sign of his apostolic preaching.
