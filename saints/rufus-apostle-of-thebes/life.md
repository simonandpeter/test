# Rufus of Thebes

Rufus, whom the page also gives as Roufos, is numbered among the seventy apostles. He was bishop in Thebes in Greece, and he is one of those the apostle Paul names in the letter to the Romans. His commemoration in the Orthodox Church is kept on 8 April, and that, with the painter's rule below, is the whole of what doxologia prints for him: no country of birth, no year, no account of his preaching and no manner of death of his own.

The day keeps him with five other apostles — [Agabus](/saints/agabus-the-apostle), [Herodion](/saints/herodion-the-apostle), [Phlegon](/saints/phlegon-of-marathon), [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle) and [Hermes](/saints/hermes-apostle-of-dalmatia) — and the page for Agabus says of all six that they ended on the same day as a result of the torments they were put to. This Rufus is not the archbishop of Thessalonica whom the corpus keeps on 9 September, nor the Rufus who was a son of Paul and Tatta, nor the Rufus of 8 September who suffered with Rufianus; the four are kept apart here.

Dionysius of Fourna, in his Hermeneia, says that the apostle Rufus is to be painted as a man not very old, his hair turning grey, with a broad beard, holding in his hand a rolled scroll, the sign of his apostolic preaching.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, in the same company of four, and names the verse he is known by. saint.gr says that Paul mentions Rufus in the letter to the Romans — «Άσπάσασθε Ρούφον τον έκλεκτόν εν Κυρίω και την μητέρα αυτού και έμοϋ» (Romans 16.13), greet Rufus the chosen in the Lord, and his mother and mine — and that he later became bishop of Thebes in Greece. The page's couplet for him takes up the same word: Paul the great apostle calls you, Rufus, truly chosen; O praise well deserved. It gives him no year and does not say how he died.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the six together on this day and gives this one two lines. Saint Rufus was bishop in Thebes of Greece, it says, and the apostle Paul mentions him too: greet Rufus the chosen in the Lord. Both the see and the greeting are already written above, and the entry adds nothing else about him — no year, no account of his preaching and no manner of death of his own. It closes of all six that, like bees of Christ, they carried the honey of the Gospel about into various countries, suffering much for the love of Christ, and that all of them passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ the beloved.

**The Russian church calendar keeps the six of them together on the Julian 8 April**, which is the day the Romanian and the Serbian keep, and its line is «Апп. от 70 Иродиона, Агава, Асинкрита, Руфа, Флегонта, Ерма и иже с ними (I).» — six genitives under one rank word, a century in a Roman numeral, and a company left open at the end, «и иже с ними», *and those with them*, which is a phrase no other calendar read for this folder prints. The six name links behind the line, pages 1871, 46, 282, 1886, 2313 and 791, all lead to one life of the company; three of them give in their own titles the see the day’s line withholds, and those same three close with «сщмч.», hieromartyr, which is a rank none of these folders carries. The rank is set down here and `types` is left as it was.

That life sets them in the number of the seventy apostles chosen by Christ and sent out to preach, points to the synaxis of the Seventy on 4 января, and then disposes of four of them in a single sentence: «апостол Руф, которого приветствует святой апостол Павел в Послании к Римлянам (Рим. 16, 11), был епископом в греческом городе Фивы; апостол Асинкрит (Рим. 16, 14) епископом в Гиркании (Малая Азия); апостол Флегонт – в городе Марафоне (Фракия), апостол Ерм – в Далматии». It closes that all of them bore cruel sufferings for their fearless service to Christ and were counted worthy of a martyr’s end — which is what the Romanian page says of all six and the Greek page says of two.

**Two books now give him Thebes and they cite two different verses for the greeting.** The Russian life says he was bishop «в греческом городе Фивы», in the Greek city of Thebes, which is the see the Romanian page and the Ohrid Prologue both give him; and it puts the greeting of the apostle Paul at **«Рим. 16, 11»**, where the Serbian entry recorded above quotes instead *greet Rufus the chosen in the Lord*, which stands in the epistle two verses later. The same sentence of the same Russian life cites «Рим. 16, 14» for his companion in Hyrcania, so the page is citing verse by verse and not loosely; the two citations are set down as the two books print them and neither is corrected here. The Russian adds no year, no account of his preaching and no manner of death of his own, closing instead with the martyr’s end it gives all six at once.

**The day prints one hymn for the company, and it is the first hymn in Russian this folder has had.** It is a kontakion in the second tone, «Явистеся Христовы ученицы и апостоли всечестнии», and it names all six of them in order before asking them to pray for the forgiveness of the sins of those who sing them. He is named third in it, «Агаве и Руфе», and the folder held no Russian hymn before.
