# Euphrosyne of Mega Spileo

Euphrosyne was from the neighbouring village of Galata, now Zachlarou, in the Peloponnese, and often pastured her father’s flock beside a high rock. Twice she had seen one of the goats climb to the mouth of a cave in the rock and come back with its muzzle wet. Guessing that there was a spring of cool water there, she climbed up to look; and having made her way through the thick brushwood she found herself at the mouth of a very great cave.

Suddenly she heard a voice calling her by name. Looking round, she saw an icon of the Mother of God high on the wall of the cave, and out of the icon a voice told her to wait for the coming of the monks [Simeon](/saints/simeon-of-mega-spileo) and [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-mega-spileo). At that same time, while they were still at Jerusalem, the two young monks had the same dream, in which the Mother of God, attended by the apostles Paul, Andrew and [Luke](/saints/luke-the-evangelist), commanded them to go into Achaia to find an icon of her that Luke had carved there.

Coming into Achaia they prayed for guidance and were told in another dream to go to the river Bouras, two hours away, where they would find a devout shepherdess, Euphrosyne, who would lead them to the icon; and the next morning, as they came near the place, they found her waiting for them. She greeted them and called them by name. Afraid of her apparent sight in the spirit, the fathers hesitated to come near; and seeing their doubt she called upon the name of the Mother of God and struck with her staff the rock on which she stood, and a spring of clear sweet water sprang out of it. That spring remains to this day and bears her name.

When Euphrosyne had told them how she had found the icon, the monks climbed to the cave and found everything as she had said. They took the icon down reverently, carried it out and went back to clean the cave; and having lit a fire to burn the brushwood and the filth, they waited outside for the flames to die. Suddenly a huge serpent, driven out by the smoke, came crawling from the cave as though it had wings, and as the monks and Euphrosyne drew aside from its path a flash of light out of the icon pinned it to the ground.

Going back in they found a table where, by the monastery’s tradition, the evangelist Luke had served the Liturgy and written his Gospel; later they widened the cave and raised a little chapel there with a few cells about it. Euphrosyne built herself a small cell beside the cave, and there she lived as a hermit until her death. Her commemoration is kept every year on 18 October. The page gives no year for her birth or her death, prints no troparion, and nothing further is added here.
