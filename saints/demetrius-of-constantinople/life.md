# Demetrius of Constantinople

Demetrius was handsome in appearance, strong in body and modest in his manners, saint.gr says. At twenty-five he worked at Galata in Constantinople, in a wine shop belonging to a Christian, Hadji Panagiotis. Some of the shop's customers, Mohammedans from Pontus, tried to win him over to their religion; and one day when they had got drunk the wine seller, with the help of the strong Demetrius, threw them out roughly.

In that scuffle one of the Turks was injured, and they denounced Demetrius as the man who had done it. So he was brought before the vizier, and because he could not prove his innocence he was put to this choice: either to become a Mohammedan, or to die. Demetrius held firmly to the Christian faith. Faced with that steady insistence, the vizier ordered him beheaded.

Before the execution he was led again before the vizier, and again he did not give way to the flatteries, the promises and the tortures of the Turks, and least of all to those of his own accusers. So on 27 January 1784 Demetrius was beheaded in front of the wine shop where he had worked. saint.gr keeps him among the new martyrs who suffered after the fall of Constantinople, and prints for him no troparion and no kontakion.
