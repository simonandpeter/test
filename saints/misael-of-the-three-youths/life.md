# Misael, one of the Three Youths

doxologia.ro prints one long life for the three youths and [Daniel](/saints/daniel-the-prophet) together, and Misael is named in it third of the three. He was of the sons of Judah and of royal family, one of the children Nebuchadnezzar had brought to the palace out of the captivity — young men without blemish, fair of face, wise and learned — to stand before the king, to be taught letters and the Chaldean tongue and to be fed for three years from the king's own table and his wine. The chief of the eunuchs renamed the four, and Misael he called Meshach, as he called [Ananias](/saints/ananias-of-the-three-youths) Shadrach, [Azarias](/saints/azarias-of-the-three-youths) Abednego, and Daniel Belteshazzar.

With the other three he would not be defiled from the king's table, and asked instead for the seeds of the earth and water; after ten days their faces were seen much fairer than those of the children who ate the king's food, and the steward set the dishes aside. God gave the four young men sharpness and wisdom, and when they were brought before Nebuchadnezzar he found them ten times wiser than all the enchanters and sorcerers in his kingdom.

In the eighteenth year the king made an image of gold sixty cubits high and six cubits broad and set it in the plain of Dura, and had all the governors and lords of his dominions gathered to its unveiling, with a furnace built in the field for whoever should disobey. When the music sounded all the peoples fell down and worshipped; but Chaldean men accused Ananias, Azarias and Misael to the king, and called before him the three answered that their God whom they served was in the heavens and able to bring them out of the burning furnace and deliver them out of the king's hands, and that they would not serve his gods nor worship the image.

The king's face changed against Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego; he had the furnace heated seven times over and strong men bind the three and throw them in. The fire came out and burnt their accusers, and the three fell bound into the middle of the great fire and walked in the flame, praising God and blessing him. The servants kept feeding the furnace with brushwood, brimstone, pitch, tow and vine-shoots until the flame rose some forty cubits and burnt all the Chaldeans standing round; but the angel of the Lord came down into the furnace and blew the flame away like a breath of dew, and the fire did not touch them or grieve them at all.

Then the three praised and blessed and glorified God — blessed are you, O Lord, God of our fathers, most praised and most exalted for ever — with the rest of the songs written in the book of Daniel. The king wondered at their singing, asked his lords whether three bound men had not been thrown in, and said that he saw four men loosed and unhurt in the middle of the fire, and that the form of the fourth was like the Son of God. He came to the mouth of the furnace and called them out by name as servants of the Most High God.

When they came out, all those who had been summoned saw that the fire had not overcome their bodies, that the hair of their heads was unburnt and that no smell of fire was on them. The king bowed to God before them and blessed the God who had sent his angel to bring his servants out of the evil fire, commanded that any people or tongue blaspheming him should be destroyed and their houses plundered, and honoured the three youths greatly, magnifying them before all and counting them worthy to be set over all the Jews of his kingdom.

They came with Daniel to a great old age. The life says, citing Cyril of Alexandria and others, that a king named Attic afterwards questioned them about the faith and, being reproached for his ungodliness, gave order that their heads be struck off; and that Misael received in his garment the head of Azarias, and that his own head was received by Daniel, whose head was cut off last of all. The heads are said to have joined themselves to their bodies again, and the angel of the Lord to have carried the bodies to Mount Gebal and laid them under a stone; and four hundred years later, at the Resurrection of Christ, these also rose and appeared to many and fell asleep again. doxologia.ro gives no year for him, and this folder gives none.

The Greek calendar keeps the four of them on the same day, and saint.gr tells the furnace with the youths' own words in it. The three young Hebrews, it says, studied at Nebuchadnezzar's imperial court together with [Daniel](/saints/daniel-the-prophet), whom the emperor had renamed Belteshazzar, and were given great place in the state when they came of age. Then Nebuchadnezzar made a golden image of his own and required all his officials and the people to worship it; Daniel was away on a mission, and it was the three youths who did not worship it.

They were denounced to the king at once, and he told them that if they truly had not worshipped it the furnace of fire awaited them. Then the three answered: hear, O king, the God of heaven whom we worship is so strong that he can bring us out of the furnace of fire whole and unharmed and save us from your hands; and even if he does not do it, know that we do not worship your gods and do not bow to your image. And indeed, when they threw them into the fire, the three youths came out whole and unharmed. [Ananias](/saints/ananias-of-the-three-youths) and [Azarias](/saints/azarias-of-the-three-youths) are the other two.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and under one heading with Daniel and the other two youths, and it names all three of them. All four, it says, were of the royal tribe of Judah, and all four were carried into Babylon in the captivity.

When the king made the golden idol in the plain of Dura, the three youths would not bow down to it, and for that they were thrown into the fiery furnace. But an angel of God appeared in the furnace and cooled the fire, so that the youths walked about inside it unhurt by it and sang: "Blessed art thou, O Lord God of our fathers." The king saw that wonder and was horrified; and he brought the youths out of the furnace and heaped great honours upon them.

According to [Cyril of Alexandria](/saints/cyril-of-alexandria), the book says, [Daniel](/saints/daniel-the-prophet) and the three youths reached deep old age in Babylon and were beheaded with the sword for the true faith. His own place in that account is the third: when [Ananias](/saints/ananias-of-the-three-youths) was beheaded, [Azarias](/saints/azarias-of-the-three-youths) spread out his garment and caught his head; then Misael caught the head of Azarias, and Daniel caught his. An angel of God carried their bodies into Judaea, to Mount Gebal, and laid them under a stone. By tradition, these four who pleased God rose at the time of the death of the Lord Christ and appeared to many, and then slept again.
