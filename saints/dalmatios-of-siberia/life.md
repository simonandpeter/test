# Dalmatios of Siberia

saint.gr gives the venerable Dalmatios three lines and no more. He was called Demetrius Ivanovich Mokrinsky in the world, the page says, and was born in the city of Tobolsk in Russia in the seventeenth century; he lived as an ascetic in the monastery of Nevyansk in Siberia, and fell asleep in peace in the year 1724. The day's line calls him «ο εκ Σιβηρίας», the one out of Siberia.

The page adds one thing beyond the life: his relics are kept in the monastery of the Ascension at Kurgan in Siberia. It gives no year for his birth, no monastery he was tonsured in, no foundation of his own and no hymn, and the life says nothing the page does not.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 25 июня ст. ст. as «Прп. Далмата Исетского (1697).», and it is the same man: the article its name page links gives his name in the world as Дмитрий, son of the Cossack ataman Иван Мокринский, which is the Demetrius Ivanovich Mokrinsky of the Greek page above, and it has him tonsured in the Невьянский monastery, the Nevyansk of that page, with his monastery in the Курганская область, where that page puts his relics.

What the Greek page leaves out is almost the whole of him. He was born in 1594 at Берёзов, where his father was in the Tsar's service; the family moved to Тобольск in 1628 and he was made city governor there; he married and had five children, and when his wife died in 1642 he left the world and was tonsured. Not wanting the office of monastery builder that was offered him, he went away to a cave on the river Исеть, at the place the people called Белое Городище, where the Течь runs into it.

The land was a Tatar's, Илигей, who was pressed by Russian trappers to drive the monk off it and twice went to do so; the second time, in 1645, the Mother of God appeared to him on the road and told him not only to leave the hermit in peace but to give him the ground, which he did. The Dormition monastery on that spot is reckoned from 1644. It was burnt in a raid in 1651 and again by Bashkir bands in 1662 and rebuilt both times; its chief treasure was an icon of the Dormition that came unburnt out of the fire.

He sheltered runaway peasants and would not give them up to the authorities, and the house did not accept the liturgical changes of Patriarch Nikon, for which his son, who was its abbot, was deprived and exiled to the Yenisei for a year. Dalmat himself went into seclusion and governed the brethren through him, writing to the diocese at Tobolsk, and reporting that the monks kept watch into the steppe for the movements of the nomads. He died in peace in 1697 in the coffin he had made for himself long before, and was buried in the sanctuary of the monastery church. Helmet and mail that Илигей had given him in token of peace lay by the tomb until the revolution, and recruits prayed there before going to the army. He was recognised a saint of the Kurgan diocese in 2004 and given general veneration on 4 February 2013.
