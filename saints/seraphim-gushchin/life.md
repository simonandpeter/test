# Seraphim (Gushchin)

The Sretensky calendar gives this man four words and no life: his name page 14433 heads him «Серафим (Гущин), прмч.» and carries nothing else. Everything below is azbyka.ru's, which prints Abbot Damascene (Orlovsky)'s life of him out of the Optina volume of 2008. He was born Stephen on 22 December 1872 at the village of Nagulino in the Balakhna district of the Nizhny Novgorod province, the son of a small Volga boat-owner. Taught at first by a private teacher, he took work as a clerk on the railway at Nizhny Novgorod in 1888, was called up in 1893 and served as a non-commissioned officer, and went back to the railway office afterwards.

In 1908 he left the world's service and came to Optina hermitage as a labourer. On 29 October 1911 he was numbered among the brotherhood as a novice, and on 24 May 1915 was tonsured with the name Seraphim. Ordained hierodeacon in 1921, he served in the one church the authorities had left the monks. In 1923 an artel was organised out of the monks and novices to keep the house and the monastic life going, but the lawfulness of a religious labouring body under Soviet power was challenged and the artel closed that same year, its property going to a museum with only a few of the brotherhood allowed to stay. The superior blessed him and one other hieromonk to remain and serve.

The church was closed in June 1924 and the brotherhood moved to Kozelsk, where the town cathedral was still open for services. He was ordained hieromonk that year and served in a church at Likhvin, and from 1 January 1937 in a village church of the Peremyshl district in the Kaluga country — a monk going from church to church as they were closed and destroyed. He was arrested on 7 November 1937, the twentieth anniversary of the seizure of power, and held in a prison of the Cherepet district.

The witnesses called were the duty kind. One said he had told people before the all-union census, which asked about belief, that it was being done to find out how many believers were left in Russia and close the churches that remained, and that they should not write themselves down unbelievers; another, that the chairman of the village soviet had asked him whom he would vote for and he had answered, would he really vote against the religion the communists were oppressing. He was interrogated the day he was arrested and denied all of it: no terrorist talk against communists in July or at any other time, no slander of the Soviet power ever, no agitation against the coming elections to the Supreme Soviet — on that he had said only that it was all one to him, that he did not mean to vote and they would manage without him.

On 11 November 1937 the district NKVD chief passed the file to the troika, and on 19 November the troika sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on the same day as the archbishop [Augustine (Belyaev)](/saints/augustine-belyaev), who was governing the Kaluga diocese, and the archimandrite [Joannicius (Dmitriev)](/saints/joannicius-dmitriev) of Optina — 23 November 1937 — and, his life says, buried as they were in a common unmarked grave. 10 ноября ст. ст. is that civil day. azbyka prints under his name a kontakion of his own and a troparion to the two Optina venerable martyrs together, and both are in this folder with the joint one labelled as such.
