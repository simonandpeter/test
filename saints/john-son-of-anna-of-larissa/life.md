# John, son of Anna of Larissa

saint.gr keeps him on 13 June beside his mother, [Anna of Larissa](/saints/anna-of-larissa), and his whole story is inside hers. She had fled Larissa secretly, because her husband's family could not forgive his father for marrying him to a poor and obscure orphan, and, God guiding her, she came to an uninhabited and barren island of the Adriatic; and there she found she was with child. He was born on that island with nobody to help her, and had lived thirty years there with her when a hieromonk, whose ship from Rome to Constantinople had been forced in by the winds, walked inland and met her.

All those thirty years, she told him, she had been entreating God to send a priest to perfect her son through holy baptism. The hieromonk went back to the ship for the priestly vestment and the holy bread, telling nobody, and returned; and she led him to where her son was and called him out: «έξελθε τοίνυν, τέκνον, και προσκύνησον τον εληλυθότα φωτίσαι σε» — come out then, child, and do reverence to the man who has come to illumine you. Her son obeyed, appeared and did reverence to the priest as she had told him.

At a spring nearby the hieromonk catechised and baptised him, and afterwards served the Liturgy, and both mother and son communicated of the body and blood of the Lord. The page is careful about the name it has given him: the Synaxaria selecta of the Church of Constantinople and the synaxarion of Nikodemos the Hagiorite say he was named John, while Paul of Monemvasia's narrative and Anna's own synaxarion do not hand down the son's name at all. According to the synaxarion in the codex Vaticanus graecus 1558, the two of them, having thanked the priest, gave over their holy souls into the hands of God. The page's couplet keeps them together: mother and son, Anna and John, were both shown to be dwellers of heaven.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him and his mother on the Julian 13 June, the same menologion day the folder’s Greek row carries, and under the same pairing — holy Anna and her son John. It tells the story as hers. [Anna](/saints/anna-of-larissa), an orphan brought up in a great man’s house and married to that man’s son, fled secretly to a far and empty island rather than let her husband stand against his kin, who were pressing him to put her away for her low birth; she came there with child and bore her son on the island, and struggled thirty years in fasting and prayer.

Then, by God’s providence, a hieromonk was put ashore on that island, and he baptised the boy and gave him the name John. That is all this book says of him: no age, where the Greek page says he was about thirty when he was baptised, and no year. It sets the two of them in the fifth century, where the Greek page puts them four hundred years later, in the middle Byzantine period; the folder holds the Greek reading and does not choose between them.
