# Abbas

saint.gr's calendar for 22 Νοεμβρίου names «Όσιος Αββάς», and the couplet it sets over him plays on the name: I honour Abbas fittingly, who is the very likeness of the abbas, as a father of little children. He had been a Hagarene before, the page says, and he came to believe in Christ through a monk, and then became a monk himself.

In the company of that same monk who had made him a Christian he went to the great ascetic [Eusebius](/saints/eusebius-of-syria), who practised his asceticism on a mountain near the village of Asicha, and he stayed with him. After Eusebius's death Abbas succeeded him in the charge of the monastery that had grown up there, and he was a pattern of the strict ascetic to those under him. He lived with great hardness and much self-restraint, and he died in peace.

The page gives him no country of his own beyond that mountain, no year, no century and no hymn, and prints no relics for him.

**The Russian calendar is keeping this folder’s man, and it names him Агавва.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar prints for the Julian 22 November «Прп. Агаввы исмаильтянина (V).» — the Venerable Agabbas the Ishmaelite — and hangs a life of six sentences behind it. He was an Ishmaelite by birth, that page says, and his ascetic labour was in Syria; he was first a novice under «преподобного Евсевия», from whom he learned inward prayer and silence, and afterwards he lived thirty-eight years as a hermit.

**Three things make him this folder and not a new one, and none of them is the name.** He had been a Hagarene, which is what the Russian page means by an Ishmaelite. He attached himself to [Eusebius](/saints/eusebius-of-syria), whom the Greek page puts on the mountain near Asicha and the Russian page names without an epithet. And both calendars keep him on the 22nd of November, the Greek as «Όσιος Αββάς» and the Russian as «Агавва исмаильтянин» — the Slavonic form of Ἀββᾶς. Two readings of one man, and a name-match would have found neither: this folder held no Russian form at all and the Greek Αββάς does not fold onto Агавва. It was the feast that found it.

What the Russian page adds is the manner of those thirty-eight years, and it is mostly a list of things he did without. He never put shoes on. He wore irons about his loins. He never sat down and never lay down, and passed day and night standing or on his knees, praying without ceasing. His life in that labour ended, it says, in a peaceful death. What it does not give — any more than the Greek page did — is a year: the only date on either page is the «(V)» the Russian day line closes his clause with, which is the fifth century, and the thirty-eight years are a length and not a date.
