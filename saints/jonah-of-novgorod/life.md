# Jonah of Novgorod

saint.gr's calendar for 5 Νοεμβρίου names Jonah, the wonderworking archbishop of Novgorod, and says in so many words that it has no life for him: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Αγίου» — we have no details of the saint's life. What the page carries besides the name is the year of his death in its head line, «(; - 1470)», with no year of birth; the see, Novgorod; and the title of wonderworker that the day's line gives him.

So nothing is recorded here of his birth, his monastery, his election, his years in the see or his burial, and the page prints no couplet and no hymn for him. He is one of the Russian saints the Greek calendar keeps, and the Greek page is the only source read for this folder; what the Russian calendar makes of his day has not been checked.

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 5 ноября ст. ст. keeps him as «Свт. Ионы , архиеп. Новгородского (1470).», and behind the line stands an index of two lives, which together give the folder the Russian account the Greek one-liner had not. He was John in the world, born at Novgorod at the end of the fourteenth century and early orphaned; one life has him adopted by a devout widow of the city who had him schooled. While he was still a child the blessed [Michael of Klopsko](/saints/michael-of-klops) met him in the street and foretold that he would be archbishop of Novgorod, and the word was fifty years in coming true.

He was tonsured in the Otenskaya hermitage, fifty versts from the city, and the brethren chose him abbot for his life. In 1458, after the death of [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-of-novgorod), the Novgorodians asked to have him for their archbishop. The pages make him a pastor and a father to his flock and particularly careful of widows and orphans, and say he pleaded more than once for the mutinous Novgorodians before the great prince of Moscow, calming his anger and holding Novgorod back from a union with Lithuania. The metropolitan [Jonah of Moscow](/saints/jonah-of-moscow) was his friend and wished to see him his own successor.

He cared for the schooling of his flock and for the building of churches, and did not forget Otenskaya. In 1463 he built the first church on Novgorod soil in honour of «Преподобного Сергия Радонежского» — a dedication and not a relation, and no row is written for it — and he called to Novgorod the known compiler of lives «Пахомий Логофет», who wrote from local sources the services and the lives of the best-known Novgorod saints. He gave great help to the founding of the Solovki monastery: to [Zosimas](/saints/zosimas-of-solovki) he issued a charter by which, with the city's lay authorities, he made over the whole island of Solovki to the new house.

Feeling his end near he wrote a will asking to be buried at Otenskaya, and on 5 November 1470, having received the Holy Mysteries, he died; the first life adds that his incorrupt relics became famous for many healings. The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion under his name and both are now in this folder, and the second of them calls him «Великаго Новаграда похвала».

*After saint.gr's calendar for 5 Νοεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/11/05/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2899/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 5 ноября ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261105.html) and [the two lives](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/id1043.htm), read 4 October 2026.*
