# Stephen of Perm

Stephen, whom saint.gr calls the enlightener of the city of Perm and the apostle of the Zyrians, was born in 1345 at Ustyug in the province of Vologda, the son of a priest named Symeon and of Mary; it was his devout mother, the page says, who shaped his character more than anyone. He was gifted, and showed an unusual zeal for the liturgical life of the Church very young: he learned to read the sacred books in a short time, and helped his father in the church during the services as canonarch and reader.

He was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Gregory the Theologian at Rostov, which was famous for its library; and because he wished to study the Fathers in their own language, he learned Greek. In his youth, helping his father at the church, he had often talked with the Zyrians, and now, steeped in the Church's tradition, he burned to preach the Gospel of Christ to them. To bring that about he devised an alphabet for their language and translated some of the church books into it, and for that work Arsenius, bishop of Rostov, ordained him deacon.

After thirteen years in the monastery he travelled to Moscow in 1379 to see Gerasimus, bishop of Kolomna, who then had charge of the metropolitan district, and begged him: «Bless me, Elder, that I may go to a pagan land, to Perm. I want to teach the Holy Faith to unbelieving men. I have resolved either to lead them to Christ or to lay down my life for them and for the Lord.» The bishop blessed him gladly, ordained him presbyter, and gave him an antimension, holy myrrh and service books, while the great prince Dimitri gave him a document for safe passage.

From Ustyug he set out along the northern Dvina as far as the meeting of the Vychegda, where the Zyrian settlements lay. He suffered much hardship and privation and bitterness living among idolaters who honoured fire, water, trees, stones and a golden female figure, and who entrusted their lives to sorcerers. Where an idol had once stood a church was built in honour of the Archangel Michael, the conqueror of darkness; the pagan priest, after a hard trial in which the error of the idols was laid bare, refused the light of the Godhead and exiled himself; and Stephen built a church of Saint Nicholas at Vishero in thanksgiving.

In 1383 he was consecrated bishop of Perm. He opened schools beside the churches where the newly baptized studied the sacred texts in the Permian tongue, and taught them what they needed in order to become priests and deacons for their own Church, and how to write their own language. He guarded his flock from the frauds of corrupt officials, gave alms, helped them order their defence against the raids of other tribes, and travelled to Moscow and Novgorod to plead the interests of the Zyrians, who were often oppressed by Russian officials.

Travelling to Moscow on church business in 1390 he passed within about ten kilometres of the monastery of [Sergius of Radonezh](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh), whom he loved ardently and longed to visit, and had no time to. He turned towards the monastery, bowed, and said: «Peace to thee, my spiritual brother.» Sergius, at table with the brethren at that moment, rose, made a prayer, bowed towards the place where Stephen was, and answered: «Rejoice thou also, leader of the flock of Christ. May the peace of God be with thee.»

He founded several monasteries for the Zyrians besides — of the Saviour in the desert of Ulyanov, of Stephanov, and of the Archangel at Ust-Vym and at Yarensk. In 1395 he went to Moscow again on his flock's business, fell ill there, and after a few days fell asleep in peace. His relics were laid in the church of the Transfiguration in the Kremlin. The Zyrians mourned their shepherd bitterly and asked the prince of Moscow and the metropolitan in all sincerity to send the body of their protector back to Perm, but Moscow would not let his relics leave.
