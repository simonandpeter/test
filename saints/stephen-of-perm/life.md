# Stephen of Perm

Stephen, whom saint.gr calls the enlightener of the city of Perm and the apostle of the Zyrians, was born in 1345 at Ustyug in the province of Vologda, the son of a priest named Symeon and of Mary; it was his devout mother, the page says, who shaped his character more than anyone. He was gifted, and showed an unusual zeal for the liturgical life of the Church very young: he learned to read the sacred books in a short time, and helped his father in the church during the services as canonarch and reader.

He was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Gregory the Theologian at Rostov, which was famous for its library; and because he wished to study the Fathers in their own language, he learned Greek. In his youth, helping his father at the church, he had often talked with the Zyrians, and now, steeped in the Church's tradition, he burned to preach the Gospel of Christ to them. To bring that about he devised an alphabet for their language and translated some of the church books into it, and for that work Arsenius, bishop of Rostov, ordained him deacon.

After thirteen years in the monastery he travelled to Moscow in 1379 to see Gerasimus, bishop of Kolomna, who then had charge of the metropolitan district, and begged him: «Bless me, Elder, that I may go to a pagan land, to Perm. I want to teach the Holy Faith to unbelieving men. I have resolved either to lead them to Christ or to lay down my life for them and for the Lord.» The bishop blessed him gladly, ordained him presbyter, and gave him an antimension, holy myrrh and service books, while the great prince Dimitri gave him a document for safe passage.

From Ustyug he set out along the northern Dvina as far as the meeting of the Vychegda, where the Zyrian settlements lay. He suffered much hardship and privation and bitterness living among idolaters who honoured fire, water, trees, stones and a golden female figure, and who entrusted their lives to sorcerers. Where an idol had once stood a church was built in honour of the Archangel Michael, the conqueror of darkness; the pagan priest, after a hard trial in which the error of the idols was laid bare, refused the light of the Godhead and exiled himself; and Stephen built a church of Saint Nicholas at Vishero in thanksgiving.

In 1383 he was consecrated bishop of Perm. He opened schools beside the churches where the newly baptized studied the sacred texts in the Permian tongue, and taught them what they needed in order to become priests and deacons for their own Church, and how to write their own language. He guarded his flock from the frauds of corrupt officials, gave alms, helped them order their defence against the raids of other tribes, and travelled to Moscow and Novgorod to plead the interests of the Zyrians, who were often oppressed by Russian officials.

Travelling to Moscow on church business in 1390 he passed within about ten kilometres of the monastery of [Sergius of Radonezh](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh), whom he loved ardently and longed to visit, and had no time to. He turned towards the monastery, bowed, and said: «Peace to thee, my spiritual brother.» Sergius, at table with the brethren at that moment, rose, made a prayer, bowed towards the place where Stephen was, and answered: «Rejoice thou also, leader of the flock of Christ. May the peace of God be with thee.»

He founded several monasteries for the Zyrians besides — of the Saviour in the desert of Ulyanov, of Stephanov, and of the Archangel at Ust-Vym and at Yarensk. In 1395 he went to Moscow again on his flock's business, fell ill there, and after a few days fell asleep in peace. His relics were laid in the church of the Transfiguration in the Kremlin. The Zyrians mourned their shepherd bitterly and asked the prince of Moscow and the metropolitan in all sincerity to send the body of their protector back to Perm, but Moscow would not let his relics leave.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the mission in its own order and with the one thing the Greek notice leaves out. He was a Russian by origin, given from his childhood to prayer and the thought of God; as a young man he went to Rostov and was tonsured there in the monastery of [Gregory the Theologian](/saints/gregory-the-theologian). Learning of the land of Perm, all overgrown with the weeds of heathenism, he conceived the wish to be a missionary in that country, and gave himself at once to the study of the Permian tongue; and when he had mastered it **he composed an alphabet for it and translated the church books into that language**.

With the blessing of the Metropolitan of Moscow he set out, as a presbyter, upon his apostolic work, and with apostolic zeal began to preach the Gospel in the thick darkness of the Perm heathenism. When he had baptized a few souls he laboured and built in Perm a church of the Holy Annunciation; and when the Church of God there had multiplied he was consecrated bishop. Bearing every labour, torment, malice and humiliation, he managed to scatter the darkness among the heathen of Perm and to shine the light of Christ upon them.

In old age he went once more to Moscow, and there ended his earthly life and passed over to the Lord. **The year this book gives is 1396, where saint.gr gives 1395** and prints it in its heading as well. Both have him die at Moscow in old age on a last journey there, so the disagreement is in the number alone; neither reading was displaced and neither was chosen, and the death this folder carries is now the interval 1395 or 1396, with both pages named in its note.

The Russian calendar, which calls him the enlightener of Perm and the apostle of the Zyrians, tells his whole work at a length no other calendar in this folder approaches. It has him born about 1340 — where the Greek page says 1345, and neither year was altered on the other's account — at Ustyug, to a church servitor named Симеон, and brought up by a devout mother named Мария; he learnt to read the holy books in a single year, helped his father in the church as canonarch and reader, and in his youth often talked with the Zyrians who came there. He took the monastic habit at the Rostov monastery dedicated to [Gregory the Theologian](/saints/gregory-the-theologian), which was famous for its library, and learnt Greek there so as to read the fathers in their own words.

For the enlightening of the Zyrians he made an alphabet for their language and turned several church books into it. The bishop Арсений of Rostov ordained him deacon; then in 1379 he came to Moscow to the bishop Герасим of Kolomna, who was managing the affairs of the metropolitanate, and asked his blessing to go into the heathen country of Perm — he had resolved, he said, either to bring them to Christ or to lay down his head among them for Christ — and was ordained priest, given antimensia, holy chrism and service books, and sent off with letters of protection from the great prince Димитрий Иоаннович. He went down the Northern Dvina from Ustyug to where the Vychegda falls into it, and the Zyrian settlements began.

They revered above all a great birch, huge in girth and height, standing on high ground, to which they would gather and offer the beasts they had killed. He set his cell not far from it and used their gatherings there to teach them; then he felled the birch and burnt it. They gathered to kill him, and he preached to them instead: judge for yourselves whether your gods are strong, when they cannot defend themselves from fire; are they gods, if they are so helpless, and have neither understanding nor hearing nor sight? On the place of the birch he built a church in honour of the Archangel Michael, the caster-down of the spirits of darkness.

The turn came when he put their chief priest Пама to shame. Pama argued that the Christians had one God while his own people had many helpers on land and water, who gave them good hunting and told them secrets in divination; and when the dispute ran long he offered, in proof of his faith, to pass through fire and water, and required the same of Stephen. I do not command the elements, Stephen answered, but the Christian God is great: I will go with thee. Pama shook and begged to be spared a certain death; and when the people said the deceiver should be put to death, Stephen answered that Christ had sent him not to give anyone over to death but to teach, and Pama was driven out. In thanksgiving he built a church on the Vishera in honour of the hierarch Nicholas, and from then the preaching went forward.

In 1383 he was made bishop of Little Perm. He opened schools at the churches where the holy books were studied in the Permian tongue, watched the pupils and weighed their gifts so as to make priests and deacons of them, taught some of them to write in Permian, built churches and set Zyrians over them as priests, and brought in the services in the Zyrian language. He translated the Book of Hours, the Psalter, selected readings from the Gospel and the Apostle, the Paremiinik, the Stichirarion, the Octoechos, several festal services and the divine liturgy. In the years of failed harvest he found the Zyrians bread, delivered them many times from the violence and the bribe-taking of the tax officers, had their dues lightened, defended them against the raids of other peoples, and pleaded for them at Moscow.

The reading sets down one meeting that never took place. In 1390, travelling to Moscow on church business, he longed to see [Sergius of Radonezh](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) and could not for want of time; so ten versts from the monastery he prayed, turned towards it, bowed and said, peace to thee, my brother in the spirit. Sergius, who was sitting at table with the brethren, rose, prayed, bowed towards the road the bishop was travelling and answered: rejoice thou also, shepherd of the flock of Christ, and the peace of God be with thee. A daily prayer to the two of them at the brethren's table keeps the memory of it.

He founded monasteries for the Zyrians as well as churches — the Saviour's at Ulyanovo, one of his own name, the Archangel's at Ust-Vym and the Archangel's at Yarenga. In 1395 he went to Moscow once more on his flock's business and died there, and his body was laid in the monastery called the Saviour behind the Wall, in the church of the Saviour on the Pine in the Moscow Kremlin. The Zyrians wept bitterly for their apostle and pressed the prince and the metropolitan hard to let them have his body back in Perm, but Moscow would not part with it. His glorification began early in the fifteenth century; the life was written in 1472, and the service to him by the hieromonk Пахомий Серб together with the hieromonk Епифаний Премудрый, a disciple of Sergius who had known the bishop well and loved to talk with him.
