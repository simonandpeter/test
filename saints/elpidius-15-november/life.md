# Elpidius (15 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 15 November keeps three men together: Elpidius, [Marcellus](/saints/marcellus-15-november) and [Eustochius](/saints/eustochius-15-november). Elpidius, the page says, was a member of the Senate, and lived when the emperor was Julian the Apostate, whose year it gives as 361. Julian took him and told him to choose between denying Christ and a martyr's death, and Elpidius chose the second without turning aside.

Two colleagues and friends of his walked with him to martyrdom, Marcellus and Eustochius. Boiling water was thrown over their flesh; then the limbs of their bodies were broken with heavy iron rods. The fury of the faithless did not stop there: even in the state they were then in, the three were thrown into the fire, and so gave up their spirits, whole burnt offerings for the holy faith.

The verse over their entry says that it was the hope of the prizes that provoked Elpidius to endure the fire along with his two friends — a play on his name, which is the Greek word for hope. Beyond that the calendar gives the three no city, no year of their own and no hymn, and names nobody else with them.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps the three together on the Julian 15 November and closes their line with the fourth century. Its life agrees with the Greek on the frame and differs from it in nearly everything else. It sets them under the emperor Julian the Apostate, whose reign it gives as 361 to 363; it calls Elpidius a man of importance at the imperial court, where the Greek page had made him a member of the Senate; and it says that he was given up to the emperor’s court of justice as a Christian, and [Marcellus](/saints/marcellus-15-november) and [Eustochius](/saints/eustochius-15-november) with him. The three bore many dreadful torments and died thrown into the fire.

What the Russian life has and the Greek has not is what followed. On the place where the Christians buried the remains of the saints there was a wonderful appearing of Christ with a host of angels, and the Lord raised Elpidius up; the emperor then ordered him seized a second time, and under the torments, at the saint’s prayer, the idols standing not far off fell to dust. More than six thousand of the pagans who saw it turned to Christ, and Elpidius was burnt a second time. From that point the page follows Elpidius alone and says nothing further of the other two. The day prints no hymn under any of the three names.

The Охридски пролог gives the three of them two sentences and no more. They suffered for Christ in the time of Julian the Apostate. Elpidius was a senator; and seeing his torments and the wonders worked in him, six thousand of the heathen came to believe in Christ the Lord. The book names no city, no manner of death and no year beyond the reign, so the fourth century this folder records from the Russian calendar is not narrowed by it, and the senatorial rank it gives him stands here in the life, the corpus having no grade for it.
