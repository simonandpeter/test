# Antonina of Tismana

Antonina stepped into the monastic life at twenty-six, a beautiful young woman in a red velvet dress, with an unusually pleasant voice and a presence of her own. After she entered the monastery she was given the obedience of the pigs, a humble service she fulfilled for forty years, a few hundred metres away from the community. Wanting to take part in the night services, she often came up against the monastery's locked gates, and in the face of that obstacle she chose to turn the place of her obedience into an altar of her own.

She prayed in the forest, where she was seen making hundreds of prostrations. One day she told with joy how she had met in the forest a woman clothed in black, with a mantle and a staff — a hidden meeting, which she took for a vision of the Mother of God. Mother Antonina had a particular devotion to the Mother of God and to Saint Nicodemus of Tismana. Her spiritual life was hidden under a form of folly for Christ, and some of the nuns looked on her as out of the ordinary; she used to gather food under her bed, which spoiled in time and gave off a heavy smell, to the displeasure of those around her.

Against appearances, her soul was rooted in prayer and simplicity. Unlike others, she managed to communicate more often, in spite of many hindrances: she said that a hierarch had told her to come more frequently to the holy Mysteries, and she did it with much courage, and God enlightened her always how to go about it, so that she should not be left without the Eucharist. At one point some fathers from the Holy Mountain came to Romania looking for souls that had made progress; she had not at first been invited to the meeting with them, but came providentially to their notice, and after they had come to know her they were deeply impressed by her spiritual life.

She was not especially valued in the monastery, and did not look for recognition either. She lived with much spiritual contentment, and her words came true every time. Though she saw with the spirit, she made nothing of the gift, but went on living hidden. One winter a huge tree fell on the slope opposite the monastery, and despite the snow and the size of it she was seen pulling at the trunk and making the sign of the cross without stopping; three days later she had got the tree down into the valley by herself and was using it for the fire on which she cooked the pigs' food. Nobody could explain how she had managed, but everyone acknowledged that God alone could have been her support.

She was canonised by the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church at its sitting of 1 July 2025, under the title «Sfânta Cuvioasă Antonina de la Tismana», with her commemoration on 23 December. doxologia.ro gives no year for her birth or her death. The page it keeps for her troparion carries no text, only a note that the hymn was sung by the Tronos psaltic group on 6 February 2026, the day of the general proclamation of the canonisation of the sixteen Romanian women saints.
