# John of Beverley

saint.gr says John — the page gives his English name in Latin letters as John Beverley — was born in the north of England and studied at the famous school of Augustine of Canterbury. When his studies were finished he was tonsured a monk at the monastery of St Hilda at Whitby. Neither of those two has a folder in this corpus, so neither is linked here; the page names them only as the school and the house of his youth.

He was elected bishop of the city the site spells Έξμαμ, Hexham, and was distinguished, it says, for the holiness of his life. After a while he was forced to resign and withdraw into the desert, so that the local Church might be at peace: the bishop of the neighbouring region had laid claim to the canonical jurisdiction of his province. A little later he was appointed archbishop of York — and resigned that too, to give himself to prayer and asceticism and to keep his monastic duties exactly. He fell asleep in peace in the year 721.

That is the whole of the page: two resignations, a school, a monastery, two sees and a year. There is no relic, no hymn and no distich, and no year of birth. The day's line calls him bishop of York and the notice itself archbishop; the office recorded here follows the notice.
