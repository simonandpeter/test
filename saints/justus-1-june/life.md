# Justus (1 June)

Doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 1 iunie reads «Sfântul Mucenic Iust», the holy martyr Justus, and the page the calendar links for him carries no Viață tab and no Tropar tab: it prints his name and the date and nothing more about him. The rank in the line is the whole of what is stated, a man who died a martyr, kept on 1 June.

He is kept apart here from the two men called Iustin whom the same day's calendar prints, both of whom have pages and lives of their own on the site; the name in his line is not theirs, and nothing read here joins him to them. No place, no century and no year is recorded for him, because the source gives none.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and names eight where the Romanian named four. These, saint.gr says, received the martyrs' crowns together with [Justin the philosopher and apologist](/saints/justin-the-philosopher), whom the same calendar keeps on this day, in the persecution against the Christians under Marcus Aurelius. They belonged to the brave phalanx of the Christians of Rome, where only genuine Christian courage could keep a man from being dismayed by the power of the idol-worship that the palace and the army upheld; and having confessed Christ repeatedly they were condemned to die by beheading, and all of them underwent it untroubled and joyful, in the conviction that the immortal goods of heaven awaited them. The four the Greek adds are [Charito](/saints/charito) the virgin, [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june) and [Paion](/saints/paion). Over Justus and Justin together it prints a distich: the sword had not yet cut Justus down, and Justin was already bowing his head.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the whole company in one heading of eight names: [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), [Charito](/saints/charito), [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june), [Paeon](/saints/paion), [Valerian](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin) and Justus. All of them suffered at Rome, it says, in the time of Marcus Aurelius and of Rusticus the eparch. When Rusticus asked whether they thought that if they died for Christ they would receive a reward in heaven, Justin answered for them all: we do not think, we know. After that they were all beheaded, in the year 163, and passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ God.

He is named eighth and last, and that is the reading of the day for him. This folder was written off a Romanian calendar line that gave his name, his rank and nothing else, and says so: no place, no century, no year, and nothing joining him to the two men called Justin whom the same day keeps. This book joins him to the company outright, naming him in one heading with the other seven and in one sentence of beheading with them, and gives him the year 163, recorded here as the death.
