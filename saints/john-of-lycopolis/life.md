# John of Lyko

John was famous for his virtues and so wonderful that, although he contended in the company of great ascetics, he surpassed them by his angelic manner of life. He lived in the fourth century and dwelt in the parts of the Thebaid near the city of Lyko — Lyko, saint.gr explains, was a city of the Thebaid, the region of Upper Egypt whose capital was Thebes.

He came to know most of Satan's traps, and by his great virtue, his asceticism and the grace of God he got past them. God counted him worthy also of the gift of healing sicknesses by wonder, and he departed in peace.

The Greek calendar keeps on this same day another John, the disciple of Barsanuphius who was called the Prophet, and saint.gr sets a couplet over this one that turns on the coincidence: where else would it be right to place the John who is in Lyko, if not here, together with John? The page prints no hymn for him.

The Russian calendar's reading is much the fuller and gives the whole course of his life. He was born at the beginning of the fourth century, lived in the city of Lycopolis in Middle Egypt and was a carpenter. At twenty-five he left for a monastery and was tonsured, and for fifteen years he laboured in one house after another; then, wanting to be wholly alone, he withdrew into the Thebaid, to the mountain the page calls the Wolf Mountain. There he passed fifty years and never once left the place of his struggle, speaking with those who came through a small window through which he also took the plain food they brought him.

After thirty years of that enclosure he was given the gift of foresight. He foretold to the emperor the page names «Феодосий Великий», whose reign it gives as 379 to 395, his victory over his adversaries «Максим» and «Евгений» and a war won against the Gauls; to many who visited he foretold what would come of their lives and gave them counsel; and he gave out blessed oil to the sick who came, who were healed of various diseases when they were anointed with it. He foretold to the monk «Палладий», who wrote his life, that he would be a bishop, and so it fell out: «Палладий» was made bishop in Bithynia in Asia Minor.

In his counsels he laid humility before everything. Imitate the virtuous life of the holy fathers so far as your strength goes, he told them, and if you accomplish it all, do not trust in yourselves and do not boast of it; there have been many who reached perfection in virtue, grew proud of it and fell from the height into the pit. Watch carefully, he said: are your prayers fervent, is the purity of your heart unbroken, is your mind taken up with other thoughts while you pray, have you renounced the world with your whole soul, do you go about inspecting other men's virtues while you boast of your own? There is nothing more unreasonable than to talk with God with the lips while the mind is far away from him. And he set the man who, abiding always in the thought of God, rises from what is material to what is not, above even the man who practises virtue in the world with hospitality and alms. He lived to a great age and departed to the Lord in 395, ninety years old.

The Greek entry behind this folder and the Russian one are read here as one man: the city of Lyko and Lycopolis are the same city, the Wolf Mountain is that city's name turned into Russian, and the century, the region, the manner of life and the gift of healing agree. The identification rests on the city and not on the day, and the two calendars keep him on different days.
