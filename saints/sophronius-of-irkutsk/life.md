# Sophronius of Irkutsk

Sophronius, whose name in the world saint.gr gives as Stephen Kristalevsky, was born on 25 December 1703 in Ukraine, near the district of Chernigov, of devout parents. From his childhood, the page says, he loved the Church and the monastic life; he developed a considerable missionary work, and he was elected bishop of the city of Irkutsk. He fell asleep in peace in the year 1771, on the second day of Pascha.

What the page tells at length is what happened to his body. While the decision of the Holy Synod about the burial of the holy relic was awaited, the remains lay unburied for six months without suffering the least change; and that, together with the reputation of his strict ascetic life, drew crowds of the faithful, who venerated the relic as the body of a saint of God. The relics were saved, the page says, by a wonder from the fire that utterly destroyed the cathedral of Irkutsk on 18 April 1917, and they work a multitude of miracles.

The proclamation of his canonisation was made by the Russian Church on 23 April 1918. The page links him to the Synaxis of all the Saints of Siberia, and prints no hymn. Of his missionary work among the peoples of eastern Siberia it says only that it was considerable; that, and everything else a Russian source would carry, is another reader's to bring, since none was read for this folder.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the day of his repose and tells his life where saint.gr gave the outline. In the world he was Стефан Кристаллевский, born on 25 December 1703 at the small town of Березань in the Переяславский district of the Полтавская province, the son of devout parents of whom his father was a clerk in orders. He was sent to the seminary of Переяслав, and on finishing it entered the Красногорский Покровский monastery in the diocese of Полтава; after three years as a novice he was tonsured, was soon ordained priest, and having passed through every degree of obedience and having the whole of a seminary education was made abbot of that house, which he governed for thirteen years.

His manner of life, his strictness and his learning became known to the empress Elizabeth, and by her will he was moved to the Александро-Невская Lavra as its deputy superior, where he served eleven years. Coming from the quiet of Красногорск to the Lavra of the capital he felt the difference in the temper of the monks at once, and he built up the hermitage the page calls «Троице-Сергиева пустынь», both to rest his own soul in and to give the Lavra’s monks a place of obedience away from the noise of the city. He tried to bring the strict monastic rule into the life of the Lavra itself and set the example of it, in the services and at the table alike. It was the empress who then proposed to the Holy Synod that he be sent to the see of Irkutsk; he was consecrated on 18 April 1753 and reached Irkutsk only on 20 March 1754, the road taking about four months and the rest of the time going on the choosing of men he could rely on in so distant a place. He was the third bishop of Irkutsk, and the see had stood empty seven years before him.

He died on 30 марта 1771, on the second day of Pascha, and his body lay six months unburied while the Synod’s decision was awaited and was found not to have corrupted. His relics were examined as incorrupt in 1833, 1854, 1870 and 1909; a fire in the Bogoyavlensky cathedral of Irkutsk on 18 April 1917 left only his bones, and the veneration grew rather than lessened for it, with more than eighteen thousand signatures gathered for his glorification, which the Council of bishops decreed. His own counsel to the clergy and missionaries of Irkutsk the page sets at the head of the life: warm the world of men with the works of preaching and with the lamp of love, for only from love is love kindled, and it brings with it all zeal for God.
