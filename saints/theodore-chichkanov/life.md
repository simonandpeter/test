# Theodore (Chichkanov)

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 9 ноября ст. ст. carries eleven names on one line and he is the fifth of them: «Сщмчч. Парфения , еп. Ананьевского, Константина , Димитрия , Нестора , Феодора , Константина , Виктора , Илии , Павла пресвитеров, Иосифа диакона и прмч. Алексия (1937).» One year stands for all eleven, «Сщмчч.» heads the ten clergy and «прмч.» the monk who closes the line, and the grades it prints sort them into a bishop, eight presbyters, a deacon and a hieromonk. The name link behind his place supplies the calendar's own nominative with the surname the line leaves out, «Феодор (Чичканов), протоиерей, сщмч.», and that is where the name form in this folder is read from.

**days.pravoslavie.ru gives this company nothing but that line.** Each of the eleven name pages carries a title, a list of days and no text at all, and not one of them has a `/Life/` link of any kind; the day page's hymns are the two martyrs' of 284–305, the venerable Matrona's, the Mother of God's before the Скоропослушница icon and the wonderworker of Aegina's, and nothing is sung under any of these eleven names. The life below is azbyka.ru's, read in the in-app browser because that host answers no fetcher, and the paragraphs are this corpus's own.

He was born on 10 February 1884 in a small village of the Barnaul country, the son of a village clerk who was also psalmist in the church where the boy was baptised. There were two other children. Having a good voice he sang in the choir, and in his free time he liked to read. He was educated at the Barnaul theological school and then at the Tomsk seminary, and on finishing the course in 1905 he took work in the Omsk consistory, where he stayed until July 1909 and where he met the daughter of a priest whom he married that year.

A daughter was born in 1912, and half a year later he fell ill with tuberculosis and had to spend a year in the Crimea for treatment. It was there that he settled in his wish to become a priest. He was ordained on 22 July 1913 by the bishop of Omsk and Akmolinsk and sent to the church at Dolon in the Semipalatinsk country, and from 1915 he served in one of the churches of the city of Semipalatinsk. Two more daughters were born; in 1921 scarlet fever took the elder of his children, the baby and the grandmother who had raised his wife.

His daughter's recollections, which azbyka prints at length, are of a two-roomed flat with a great cupboard full of books out of which her father prepared his sermons, of parents who read much and discussed what they read, of a household at prayer and fast and a small prayer book with the morning and evening prayers marked in her father's hand, of the whole family at church with her brother holding the bishop's staff and she holding the music, and of a father who in his free time soled the family's felt boots in winter and sewed their slippers in summer.

When the Znamensky cathedral was closed on 27 November 1931 he became a priest of the cathedral of the Ascension. The Church was then laid under a heavy tax and most of his pay went to it every month; ration cards were brought in and «служителям культа» were not entitled to them, so he took extra work as a bookkeeper to pay the tax and keep the family. He was called in to the NKVD and came back from it gloomy, asking them to ask him nothing: «Я вам все равно ничего не скажу».

In 1937 a wave of arrests went through Semipalatinsk on a conspiracy of churchmen fabricated by the NKVD. The first taken, in August, were [Alexander (Shchukin)](/saints/alexander-shchukin), the archbishop of the city, and the abbess of its ruined Znamensky convent; then came the priests, and they came for him on 19 November 1937. From that day his family could learn nothing of his fate for nineteen years.

The decision of the troika of the regional administration, dated 19 November 1937, sentenced six men as members of a counter-revolutionary spy organisation of churchmen to be shot, and the sentence was carried out on 22 November 1937 in the city of Semipalatinsk. Four of the six were numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia by the jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000, and he is one of them. 9 ноября ст. ст. is the civil 22 November, so the calendar keeps him on the very day he was shot.

**These four are a company and the pages say so in as many words.** Черепанов's life names the collective case he shared with [Nestor (Panin)](/saints/nestor-panin), [Theodore (Chichkanov)](/saints/theodore-chichkanov) and [Victor (Klimov)](/saints/victor-klimov); Климов's life says he was shot «вместе со священномучениками прот. Нестором Паниным, прот. Феодором Чичкановым и свящ. Константином Черепановым» on the strength of one decision of the troika of 19 November 1937; and Чичканов's life prints the decision itself, naming six men sentenced as members of one fabricated organisation and carrying the sentence out on 22 November 1937 at Semipalatinsk. One case, one sentence, one day, one city: `related` rows are written between all four, both ways.
