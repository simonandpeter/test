# Alexis Toth

Alexis was born in the Austro-Hungarian Empire in 1854. His father was a Greek Catholic priest and his uncle a bishop, and after a hard preparation he was himself ordained a Greek Catholic priest in 1878. He taught Church History and Canon Law at the Catholic seminary in Prešov. In 1889 he came to the United States and served as pastor of the Greek Catholic church of Saint Mary in Minneapolis; and in 1891 he and the faithful of that church were received into the Orthodox Church by Bishop Vladimir, metropolitan of the Aleutian Islands.

He gave his time to the sick and the infirm and helped the needy without ceasing, and published many articles to catechise his people. In 1893 he moved from Minneapolis to Wilkes-Barre in Pennsylvania, where he brought many Greek Catholics back to the Orthodox faith. He passed to the Lord at the age of fifty-six, on 7 May 1909; seven years later his relics were moved to Saint Tikhon's monastery, where they remain. He was canonised and numbered among the saints of the Orthodox Church in May 1994. This, in full, is what doxologia.ro prints about him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the day of his death and calls him the Defender of the Orthodox faith in America. He was born in Austria-Hungary on 18 March 1854, saint.gr says, of a poor Carpatho-Russian family which, like many in that empire, was bound to the Uniates: his father and his brother were priests and his uncle a bishop among them, and the page sets each of those words in quotation marks of its own. He was excellently educated and learned many languages, married Rosalie Mihalich, the daughter of a priest, and was ordained on 18 April 1878; his wife died soon after and then his only child, and he bore those trials, the page says, with the patience of Job.

He was made secretary to the bishop of Prešov and put in charge of the diocesan administration and of an orphanage, and taught church history and canon law in the seminary there — knowledge that served him greatly in his later life in America. He took up priestly duties in a Uniate parish in Minneapolis, in Minnesota, and through various ecclesiastical troubles decided to turn to the Russian Orthodox bishop Vladimir; and on 25 March 1891 the priest Alexis Toth and 3,614 of his parishioners came into the Orthodox Church and the faith of their forefathers, crying out for joy, Glory to thee, O God, for his great mercy.

His example encouraged hundreds of other Uniates. He exposed the teaching that had misled his people with a bold preaching, but was discreet enough not to breed intolerance in his flock, and he wrote a number of works on the Orthodox faith and the Orthodox life. To meet his own needs he had to work in a bakery, and though his money was little he did not fail to give alms, sharing it with clergy worse off than himself and helping to build churches and to educate students of theology. In 1907 he refused a nomination to the episcopate and proposed a younger man for it. By 1909, when he fell asleep, thousands of Carpatho-Russian and Galician Uniates had returned to Orthodoxy. He reposed in peace on Friday 7 May 1909, and his relics are kept at the monastery of Saint Tikhon in South Canaan, Pennsylvania.
