# The Hundred Egyptian Martyrs

These are the hundred beheaded with the bishops
[Peleus](/saints/peleus-bishop-and-hieromartyr) and
[Nilus](/saints/nilus-bishop-and-hieromartyr). Of the hundred and fifty-six
who suffered under Maximian Galerius, between 305 and 311, at the hands of
Firmilian, governor of Palestine — Egyptians for the most part, with some
Palestinians among them, their eyes put out and the tendons of their feet
cut first — a hundred were beheaded, and the rest, the two bishops, the
presbyter Zeno and the noblemen
[Patermuthius](/saints/patermuthius-the-martyr) and
[Elias](/saints/elias-martyr-with-patermuthius), were burned. So the
Orthodox Church in America tells it. The Greek synaxarion keeps the hundred
on 17 September with a verse alone — a tenfold ten of Egyptian martyrs have
their heads cut off with one eagerness.

The Russian calendar keeps, on 17 September old style, the Egyptian bishops Peleus and Nilus, the presbyter [Zeno](/saints/zeno-companion-of-peleus), Patermuthius, Elias and others, 151 in all, about 310. Its life says that Firmilian, the governor of Palestine, seized 156 Christians under Galerius Maximian (305–311), most of them Egyptians and some Palestinians, and that after torture a hundred were beheaded and the rest burned; the hundred beheaded are the hundred this folder keeps. The Russian heading does not number them apart.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps the 156 martyrs, all of them Egyptians, who suffered in 310, some by the sword and some by fire, with the bishops Peleus and Nilus, the priest Zeno, Patermuthius and Elias among them, and the bishop Silvanus and the blind elder John, who knew the Scriptures by heart, with them.
