# Agapitus of Markushevo

Agapitus of Markushevo lived in Russia in the sixteenth century. He became a monk first in the monastery of the holy Boris and Gleb of Solvychegodsk. In 1576, when he fell gravely ill, the icon of Saint Nicholas the Wonderworker that is called the Velikoretskaya appeared to him and gave him a charge: to go to the river Markuza and found a new monastery there. Healed in the same moment, he carried the charge out, building first a wooden chapel and then, in 1578, two churches dedicated with reverence to Saint Nicholas and to Saint Procopius of Uglich.

In that same year he went to Moscow to ask the tsar for a piece of ground and leave to build a mill on the river Lokhta, by which the monastery would keep itself; and so it was done. But the people of the neighbouring village of Kalinin, fearing that their own lands would be annexed to the monastery, conspired to kill him. Led by a blasphemous peasant named Bogdan Lyachov, they set on him near the mill on 21 May 1584, killed him, and threw his relic into the river Ustyuga. The monks found the honourable body and buried it with reverence in a chapel raised between the monastery's two churches.

The page ends with how his life came to be written. In 1712 Barnabas, bishop of Kholmogory, visited the monastery and gathered material about the life of Agapitus and about all the miracles of the icon of Saint Nicholas; and in 1715, at that same bishop's order, the life of the venerable-martyr Agapitus was composed.
