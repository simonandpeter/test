# Theophan (Ilminsky)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 11 декабря and gives him a life. He was born on 26 September 1867, Sergei Petrovich Ilminsky in the world, into the family of a church reader named Peter and his wife Akilina, in the village of Sodom in the Saratov province. His father died soon after, and the boy was left to his mother, a deeply religious, gentle and humble woman; a large part in bringing him up was taken by his uncle, a village archpriest named Dimitri.

He remembered that uncle with love afterwards, saying he had been left «in the care of an uncle who took my father’s place … a gentle and humble village pastor, fifty years in one and the same remote, out-of-the-way village. It was with him that I learnt to share both the joy and the grief of the people». Whole generations had passed before that priest in christenings, weddings and burials, and the people loved him, he said, not only his parishioners but the villages round about; and the way that love was earned was simply that he was good and gentle in the manner of the Gospel, never oppressed anyone and never refused a man who asked.

Of his own years as a bishop the page says that he rebuked the god-fighting Bolsheviks without fear. When in 1918 the authorities showed an interest in the lands of the Trinity monastery, he answered that he feared a more terrible Judgement and that without the authority of the ruling hierarch he had no right to tell them what belonged to the monastery. He organised and led crowded processions of the cross against the persecution of the Church and the robbing of monasteries. When in March 1918 some priests, their dean at their head, published a declaration of loyalty and friendliness towards the Bolsheviks, he answered it with sorrow and bewilderment: pastors of the Church, servants of «the ideals of Christianity», express «loyalty» to men of violence and robbers — you ought as pastors, as the salt of the earth and the light of the world, to have pronounced your moral judgement upon them.

On 17 June 1918, after the arrest and execution of [Andronicus of Perm](/saints/andronicus-of-perm), he came to Perm and took over the administration of that diocese; and at the end of the summer he was himself arrested. A few days before Perm was freed by Kolchak’s army on 23 December 1918, the Bolsheviks put him to elaborate torments. On 11 December 1918, in a frost of thirty degrees, they plunged him again and again into an ice-hole in the river Kama; his body was covered with ice two fingers thick and the martyr was still alive, and then his executioners simply drowned him. Two priests and five laymen were drowned with him, and the calendar names none of the seven. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the jubilee council of August 2000.

About nineteen hundred characters in the middle of that page were not read, so what he did between his uncle’s village and his episcopate is not set down here.
