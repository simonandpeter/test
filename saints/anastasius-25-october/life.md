# Anastasius (25 October)

saint.gr's calendar for 25 Οκτωβρίου names Anastasius and gives him a short notice without a name of place or a year. This martyr presented himself of his own will before the tyrants, the page says, and confessed Christ with courage; and because he stayed firm in that confession they beheaded him and threw him into the sea. The couplet above asks what it could write for him beyond the grace of Christ, for whose sake he hurried to die by the sword.

Then a pious woman, when the sea gave the body up, took the honoured relic and buried it with honours, and built a church there in the saint's name; and his relic, by the grace of God, worked many wonders. The page names neither the tyrants nor the woman nor the place of the church, gives no year, and prints no hymn.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this same day, 25 октября ст. ст., and fills in nearly everything the Greek notice left out. He lived in the third century in the city of Aquileia in northern Italy, and it was for missionary work in the city of Salona in Dalmatia that he was seized and brought before a judge. There, boldly and without wavering, he confessed Christ as the true God and the Creator of the whole creation, and by the court's decision he was condemned to death.

The pagans threw his body into the sea. A righteous Christian woman, a rich matron whom this page names where the Greek one did not, found the body of the martyr and buried it with honours in her own house church; and his relics were marked by many wonders. The page gives him a second day besides this one, 5 декабря, and prints no hymn for him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 25 October and gives him a trade that none of the folder’s other books does: he was a fuller of cloth, and a zealous Christian. In the time of Diocletian’s persecution of the Christians this man of God presented himself of his own accord to the tormenting judge in the Dalmatian city of Salona, and confessed his faith in Christ. He was inhumanly tortured and killed. His body was thrown into the sea, but afterwards it was taken out and honourably buried. The persecution is named and no year is: an emperor is not a source for a year, and this folder’s third century stands as it was.
