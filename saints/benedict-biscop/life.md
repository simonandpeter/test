# Benedict Biscop

Benedict came of a noble Anglo-Saxon family and served at the court of Oswiu, king of Northumbria. At twenty-five he left the world and became a monk; he went to Rome, and was taught the monastic polity there by the fathers who were living it. At the command of Pope Vitalian, whom saint.gr dates 657 to 672, he went with [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-tarsus-archbishop-of-canterbury), who came from Tarsus in Cilicia, to Britain, and was appointed abbot there.

Afterwards, at Wearmouth, he founded monasteries which stood under his spiritual direction. He called craftsmen from abroad to teach the English the art of building churches, and he raised the first stone churches among them. The most important of his disciples, the page says, is Saint Bede, 673 to 735, the famous author of the church history of England.

Benedict fell asleep, venerably, in peace in the year 690. The calendar prints for him no hymn.
