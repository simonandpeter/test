# George of Nea Ephesus

saint.gr says the new martyr George was born of a Samian father at Nea Ephesus in 1756. He took the married life and had children. But in July 1798, being in a state of drunkenness, he was drawn into Islam and denied Christ; and because he felt shame for what he had done, he renounced Islam again, left Ephesus and came to Samos.

While he was away the Christians began to build a church at Ephesus, having first got leave for it from Constantinople. The Turks, taking it hard that a church of the Orthodox was going up, and by imperial licence at that, put it about that the Christians had killed George as an apostate from their faith and had hidden his body in the foundations of the rising church. But George was found later and brought to Ephesus by force, where the Turks pressed him to return to the Mohammedan religion. He managed to be got away to Samos again, was seized there afresh and taken to prison, and was set free once more by the mediation of the elders of Samos.

Meanwhile the disturbances and disorders at Ephesus went on. Then George, having repented and come to himself, took the decision of martyrdom. He sent his family away to keep them from the fanaticism of the Turks, and presented himself before the Turkish judge, to whom he confessed his faith in Christ. The Turks began with flatteries and then with threats; and when they saw the saint's steadiness in the piety of his fathers, they beheaded him. It was the year 1801, on a Friday.

The Christians took his holy relic and buried it reverently in the tomb of the new martyr [Polydorus](/saints/polydorus-the-neomartyr), whom the same calendar keeps on 3 September, and one of the page's megalynaria names Polydorus and Markos as his fellow-contenders. The page gives him no family names and no village on Samos, and names his head as kept at the Metropolis of Samos, Ikaria and Korseai.
