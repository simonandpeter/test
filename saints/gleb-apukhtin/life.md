# Gleb (Apukhtin)

Gleb Semyonovich Apukhtin was born in 1885 at Oboyan in Kursk province, a
peasant’s son, and finished the Oboyan town school in 1902. When he was
ordained is not known exactly, but he was a deacon before the revolution;
from 1919 to 1920 he was a mobilised private of the 13th Army at Lugansk,
and in 1925 he was ordained priest and began to serve at Borovoye in the
Nizhnyaya Usman district of Voronezh province. In 1929 he was arrested for
the first time, for non-payment of tax, and served five years of exile at
Kansk in Western Siberia and then in the Kalinovka district of Kiev region,
returning to Borovoye in 1934. On 28 March 1935 he was arrested again, for
systematic anti-Soviet and defeatist agitation, insulting the leaders of the
government, and resisting the removal of the church bells — in the church
lodge and on his visits to houses, said the indictment — and given ten
years, first in the Michurinsk camp and then in the Volga camp in the Uglich
district of Yaroslavl region, where he worked as a courier; the
administration tried to make him an informer on the other prisoners, and he
would not, and became instead himself the victim of slander and
denunciation. In 1937 he was arrested in the camp for refusing work,
counter-revolutionary agitation among the prisoners, wrecking the Volga
construction, praising the tsarist order and spreading rumours of war and
the fall of the Soviet power; the file says that he had tried to worm
himself into the confidence of the third section by professing willingness
to help uncover counter-revolutionary formations in the camp, and had in
fact fed it false information to draw attention away from the real culprits.
The group case carried his name, and [Eugene Popov](/saints/eugene-popov)
and [Peter Grigoriev](/saints/peter-grigoriev) died in it with him. On 22
September 1937 the troika of the Yaroslavl NKVD sentenced him to death, and
on 23 September 1937 he was shot. He is kept on 10 September of the old
style, 23 September, and in the synaxes of the Rostov-Yaroslavl saints and
of the Voronezh metropolia.
