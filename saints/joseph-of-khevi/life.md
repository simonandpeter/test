# Joseph of Khevi

Little is known of Joseph. The Romanian life says as much in its first sentence, and then gives what tradition holds: that he came from Khevi in the north of Georgia and served as the priest of that village. The people of Khevi were fighters, and steady in the faith besides; their churches and monasteries are remembered both for their beauty and for the hard road to them, built high in the mountains on purpose, so that whoever wished to reach them should have to labour for it.

On the flank of Mount Kazbegi stands the monastery of the Holy Trinity, where the cross of Saint Nina was kept for a time, and above it, on the frozen slope, the cave called Bethlehem, which can be reached only by chains let down from above. The chronicle called the Life of Kartli says the cave was used to hide holy vessels and the treasures of the Church, and Georgian tradition holds that the tent of Abraham and the manger of the Lord were kept in it for several centuries.

Joseph was known for his love of the holy vessels, for the strictness with which he kept the fasts, and for his virtues; the life says God gave him foresight and the working of miracles. He fell asleep in peace in 1763. The page cites the great Synaxarion for October published at Iasi in 2015.
