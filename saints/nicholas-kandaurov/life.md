# Nicholas (Kandaurov)

Nicholas Kandaurov was born on 21 January 1886 in the Cossack settlement of Barsukovskaya in the Kuban, the son of a soldier, Andrew Kandaurov. On his mother's side, the Russian life says, many of Anna Alexandrovna's forebears had been priests; on his father's all had been soldiers, mostly officers, and almost all had fought in the many wars Russia waged in defence of her independence. Andrew Kandaurov rose to high officer's rank, was decorated twice for action, and on leaving the service was made inspector of popular education for the North Caucasus district.

At the turn of the century a wave of revolutionary terror went over Russia, in which many officials and officers of the police and the army were killed, and bystanders with them. Andrew Kandaurov was killed by terrorists in 1898; they admitted afterwards that the killing had been senseless and accidental, and came to his widow to offer her compensation. The life's account of the son's upbringing and of his years as a priest is long, and this entry does not retell it.

The end it tells in detail. On a January evening he was sitting with a candle talking to his children about something happy when a loud knock came at the door; it swung open, the candle went out, one of the children lit the lamp, and a man stood in the doorway in a greatcoat belted over a holster, who shouted roughly, does Kandaurov live here. Children, this is the end, said father Nicholas; he grew gravely attentive, but his peaceable and gentle mood did not change, and he said a warm goodbye to them all as he went. He was quiet through the search, and although it was January and cold outside he took nothing warm with him but a quilted jacket.

He was held first in the prison at Kolomna and then in Moscow. He was questioned the next day, accused of anti-Soviet agitation and of spreading counter-revolutionary rumours, and did not admit the charge. The case was closed the same day, the investigator drew up the indictment and sent it to the troika, and on 2 February the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot. The archpriest Nicholas Kandaurov was shot on 17 February 1938 and buried in an unknown common grave. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000 for veneration throughout the Church.
