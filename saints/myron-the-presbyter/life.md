# Myron of Cyzicus

Myron was a presbyter in Achaia in the third century, of a rich and distinguished family — meek and merciful to people, the life says, but brave in defending his flock. At the feast of the Nativity, as he was celebrating the service, the local governor Antipater came into the church with soldiers to seize the worshippers and lead them to torture; Myron stood up for his people and rebuked the governor for his cruelty, and was given over to torment: hanged and raked with iron combs, then thrown into a red-hot furnace, where the Lord kept him whole while a hundred and fifty of the bystanders were scorched by the fire that burst out of it.

Pressed to worship the idols he refused, and the governor ordered strips cut from his skin from head to foot; the martyr seized one such strip and struck the judge in the face with it, and Antipater, beside himself, took his sword and killed himself. Myron was taken to Cyzicus and beheaded there, in 250, under Decius. The Prologue tells it the same way for the Serbian calendar, adding the angel who strengthened him in the fire.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 17 August. Doxologia.ro's life sets him in Achaia in the reign of Decius, under the governor Antipater: a man of honourable family and wealthy, of good habits and warm love towards God and towards men. On the day of the Nativity of Christ, when Antipater came into the church to seize the Christians gathered there, Myron rebuked him to his face; he was taken, hung on a beam, raked with iron claws and burned, and when he was thrown into a heated furnace the fire came out of it and burned a hundred and fifty of the pagan servants standing round, while he stood alive and unhurt in the midst of it, blessing God. His skin was then flayed from him in strips from the shoulders down, and he tore one strip off and threw it in the governor's face; he was raked again and given to the beasts, which did not touch him. Antipater, shamed, killed himself with his own hands, and Myron was taken to the city of Cyzicus and there beheaded.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same menologion day and gives the scene of his trial. saint.gr says Myron was martyred when Decius was emperor, in 250; that coming of a rich family he could have lived at ease with every earthly good he might wish, but that his great love for Christ made him be ordained a priest. He gave himself wholly to his pastoral duty, teaching, admonishing and helping every single member of his flock, and caring daily for the poor, the widows and the orphans.

Once, the page goes on, the eparch of Achaia Antipatros came to the place where Myron served and arrested many Christians. To force Myron to change his faith he brought the flock before him and told him that if he denied Christ he would set them all free. Myron smiled and answered: if it were for the salvation of my spiritual children I would gladly give my life; but it is not that; let them answer for themselves. Then all of them cried out together: «Όχι. Μια ανθρώπινη ψυχή είναι ασύγκριτα πολυτιμότερη από μύρια σώματα και από τον κόσμο όλο» — no; one human soul is beyond comparison more precious than ten thousand bodies and than the whole world; which of us, then, would consent that our spiritual father should lose his soul, so that our own flesh might live a little longer in this passing world?

The eparch, enraged at the answer, tortured Myron in a dreadful manner and at last beheaded him. saint.gr adds that he is the patron of Antikythera, and that a portion of his holy relic is at the monastery of Kykkos in Cyprus; and its third verse counts out the day of the month, that a sharp sword cut Myron on the seventeenth.
