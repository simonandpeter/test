# Raisa (Irais) of Alexandria

Irais — Raisa — lived at Alexandria. Going once to a spring to draw water she saw a ship at the shore full of men, women, clergy and monks in chains for confessing the faith of Christ; throwing down her water-jar she joined the prisoners of Christ of her own will, and was put in chains with them. When the ship came to the Egyptian city of Antinopolis she was the first to suffer cruel torture and was beheaded, and after her the other martyrs sealed their confession of Christ with their blood, about 308. The Russian calendar keeps her on 5 September and again on 23 September (Julian).

The Greek synaxarion keeps her on 23 September as Raïs the virgin, and tells it otherwise. She came from the city of Batan (or Taman) in Egypt, the daughter of one Peter, and became a nun at twelve. Going once to the spring for water with other virgins, she saw a crowd of Christians whom the governor Lucian held in bonds, and went and joined them. The jailer advised her to go away, so as not to lose her life with the rest; instead she presented herself boldly before the governor, laughed at his gods and, because he mocked Christ, spat in his face. At once she was tortured horribly, and at last beheaded. The entry’s verse has her, longing to see the beauty of God, give the beauty of her flesh to the sword.
