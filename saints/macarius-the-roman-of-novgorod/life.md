# Macarius the Roman of Novgorod

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 19 января ст. ст. as «Прп. Макария Римлянина, Новгородского (XVI-XVII)», and gives him a short life. He was born at Rome at the end of the fifteenth century to godly parents and brought up strictly in the faith; then, leaving Rome and what the page calls its spiritual discord, «покинув Рим с его духовным разладом», he reached Great Novgorod.

There he entered the Свирский monastery and was made a monk, and with his elder's blessing he settled for silence on an islet of the river Lenzi. He kept strict vigils and fasts and unceasing prayer, eating berries and herbs, and lived no little time in complete solitude. His struggles, the page says, drew a particular grace to the place, which showed itself now as a pillar of fire and now as a fragrant smoke spreading over the countryside, and it drew men who wanted the spirit of his instruction and the monastic life.

On the site of his struggles he founded a house and became its first abbot. Governing it some years, he received from the Lord the gifts of foresight and of wonderworking. His end was peaceful and fell on 15 August, in the second half of the sixteenth century, and his body was buried in the Dormition church he had built; the calendar's own bracket on the day line reads «(XVI-XVII)», which is wider than its life page, and both readings are recorded here.

He is not the earlier Roman of the same name whom the Greek and Romanian calendars keep on 23 October, a fourth-century ascetic and the son of a Roman senator. The two share the city, the epithet and a name, and nothing else; this calendar's own epithet for the later man is «Новгородский», and it keeps only him on this day. The day page prints four hymns for him, two troparia and two kontakia.
