# Cyril of Kazan

saint.gr's calendar for 26 January names Cyril, metropolitan of Kazan, and says in as many words that it has no life to give. The whole of the page's account of him is this: that he bore witness as a martyr in the year 1937, and that «Δεν έχουμε περισσότερες λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Αγίου» — we have no further details of the saint's life.

So what is recorded here is what the Greek calendar records: the day, the name, the see in his title, the year in which he was killed, and the page's own admission that it knows no more. It gives no year of birth, says nothing of where or how he died, and prints no troparion and no kontakion. The Russian calendar, which keeps the new martyrs of that decade with their own dossiers, is where this entry could be finished; it has now been read, and the rest of this life is what it gives.

**The Russian calendar has now been read, and it gives him a dossier.** days.pravoslavie.ru's line for 7 ноября ст. ст. heads fifteen names with his: «Сщмч. Кирилла , митр. Казанского, Михаила , Александра , Александра , Михаила , Александра , Николая , Алексия , Павла , Василия , Павлина пресвитеров, Иоанна и Вениамина диаконов, мч. Николая , мц. Елисаветы (1937).» — and the name link behind «Кирилла» supplies the calendar's own nominative with the surname and the full see, «Святитель Кирилл (Смирнов), митрополит Казанский, сщмч.», while the life page behind it titles him «митрополит Казанский и Свияжский». He is the only one of the fifteen with a life, and the day he is kept on is, that life says, the day he was shot.

In the world he was Константин Иларионович Смирнов, born on 26 April 1863 at Kronstadt in the St Petersburg province, in a psalm-reader's family. He finished the St Petersburg Theological Academy in 1887 with the degree of candidate of theology, was ordained priest on 21 November of that year to the Resurrection church of the Temperance Society in St Petersburg, and taught religion at the Elisavetpol gymnasium and the second gymnasium of the capital at the same time.

From 1900 he was rector of the Holy Trinity cemetery church at Kronstadt, and he served fifteen years as a priest. **What ended that was a death in his own house**: little «Ольга» died in agony from a swallowed needle, and then his wife, who did not survive the grief; and the sudden double loss moved the thirty-eight-year-old father Константин to take the monastic habit in 1902. He was soon made head of the Urmia mission in Persia and raised archimandrite.

On 6 August 1904 he was consecrated bishop of Gdov, vicar of the St Petersburg diocese. Wanting the people to take a fuller part in the services, he introduced congregational singing at the «Александро-Невская Лавра»; and «святой праведный Иоанн Кронштадтский», who knew him from his years at Kronstadt, asked before his death that this young bishop should bury him. From 1908 to 1928 he had the Tambov and Shatsk diocese, travelling into its remotest parts as often as he could; he was raised archbishop in 1913, and in 1914 the glorification of «святитель Питирим Тамбовский» was his work.

Two sentences of the page show the man's cast of mind. In the cholera year 1909 the sanitary commission ordered that only boiled water be blessed at Theophany; he refused, and blessed the river. And the same conviction stands in one of his last letters, of 8 March 1937: «Всё, что не от веры — грех» — everything that is not of faith is sin. In February 1917 he warned from the ambo that they were about to write a page of their own history that the next generation would read with the colour of shame in their faces, and would want to tear out: «но нет таких ножниц, которыми можно было бы вырезать что-либо из памяти истории».

At the Local Council he was put forward second among the twenty-five first-elected candidates for the patriarchate, and he was elected a member of the Holy Synod under «святейший Патриарх Тихон». On 19 March 1918 he was appointed to the metropolitan see of Tiflis and Baku and made exarch of the Caucasus, but he never managed to travel there. In 1919 he was arrested in Moscow on a charge of «контрреволюционной агитации путём рассылки воззваний и сношения с Колчаком и Деникиным» and imprisoned by the Cheka.

Released in 1920, he was appointed to the Kazan and Sviyazhsk see — and arrested again a month later, in Kazan, for having left Moscow without the Cheka's permission. He was sentenced to five years in a camp and sat in the Taganka prison in one cell with «архиепископом Феодором (Поздеевским)» and «епископом Гурием (Степановым)». An amnesty freed him in 1921 and he went back to Kazan; in 1922 he was arrested once more and exiled to Ust-Sysolsk, the present Syktyvkar, after another Moscow imprisonment — where he shared a cell with «священномучеником Фаддеем» and wrote with him an address to the faithful about the Living Church that they managed to send round Russia.

Then Ust-Kulom, where «епископ Афанасий (Сахаров)» was with him, and then the town of Kotelnichi in the Vyatka country. The head of a G.P.U. department, «Е. А. Тучков», had him brought from Ust-Kulom to Moscow to be talked into a compromise, and the attempt failed. Back from exile in 1924, he met the patriarch in Moscow and successfully argued him out of reconciliation with the Renovationist «В. Красницкий», which the G.P.U. was pressing on him with a promise to let the bishops out of prison. «Ваше Святейшество, о нас, архиереях, не думайте. Мы теперь только и годны на тюрьмы...» — and the patriarch struck the name off the paper he had already signed.

By the patriarch's testamentary disposition of 25 December 1924 he was named first candidate for the office of patriarchal locum tenens; but he was sent into exile again, so that when the patriarch died he could not take it up. **In 1926 the episcopate thought of electing a patriarch secretly, and seventy-two bishops' signatures went under his election, against one for «митрополит Сергий (Страгородский)».** He was elected, and there was no enthronement, the G.P.U. having learnt of it. Тучков said he would allow it on one condition, that the new patriarch follow his instructions in appointing bishops, and the answer was: «Евгений Алексеевич, вы — не пушка, а я — не снаряд, при помощи которого вы надеетесь уничтожить Русскую Церковь».

A wave of arrests followed. He was taken in exile, held in the prison at Vyatka, given three more years and sent to the outpost of Khantayka in the Turukhansk district of the Krasnoyarsk country, and afterwards to Yeniseisk. **After the Declaration of 1927 he separated himself from communion with «митрополит Сергий»**, not wishing to share in what his conscience «признала греховным»; he saw in the Provisional Synod a threat to the wholeness of the patriarchal order and its replacement by collegial government, and he held it senseless and harmful to keep a central church authority at such a price. He urged the bishops instead to follow the patriarch's decree of 20 November 1920 and build local self-government, to give a Council an account of it under better conditions.

From May to November 1929 he wrote to «митрополит Сергий» trying to argue him off the road of compromise; the answer was a threat of canonical penalty in the name of church discipline, and his reply to that is the longest thing the page quotes: that discipline keeps its force only while it is a real reflection of the hierarchical conscience of the conciliar Church and can never stand in that conscience's place. In December 1929 Sergius put him on trial before the bishops and dismissed him from the government of the Kazan see.

From 1932 he was in exile in the Turukhansk country, and the page stops to say what that was: half the year is night, broken only by the northern lights, and for half the year the people there are cut off from the world — no letters, no newspapers, no parcels; the frost reaches sixty degrees; then a short polar summer and myriads of tormenting midges, scurvy, and nothing one needs to live. The exiled bishops were settled in small villages far from one another and could not meet. Only with «священномучеником епископом Дамаскиным (Цедриком)» did he manage a short while together, and from then on they were friends for good.

Released in August 1933 he lived a short time at Gzhatsk, where clergy of his own mind pressed him hard to claim his rights and take up the government of the Church; he held it impossible until he had fully understood the position. In 1934 he came to Moscow and presented himself at the patriarchate: the guard set there barred him, and the tall, once mighty metropolitan put him aside and stepped into Sergius's study. He came out a few moments later, and it was their last meeting. That summer he was arrested at Gzhatsk for «контрреволюционную деятельность», held in the inner isolator of the Butyrka prison and given three years of exile, which he served at Yany-Kurgan in South Kazakhstan — not far from «митрополит Иосиф (Петровых)», so that two old metropolitans had at least the comfort of being able to meet.

**Where he stood on the schism he set out himself, and it is finer than a slogan.** He allowed not going to the «сергианских» churches as a protest, while condemning the railing of unwise zealots against the services celebrated in them; for himself he allowed confession to a «сергиевскому» priest only at the point of death. «Совершённую им подмену власти, конечно, нельзя назвать отпадением от Церкви, но это есть, несомненно, тягчайший грех падения. Совершителей греха я не назову безблагодатными, но участвовать с ними в причащении не стану...» To Sergius himself he wrote that his abstention was directed wholly at him and not at the ordinary clergy, still less at the laity. By 1934 he was writing that those who understood the untruth in Sergianism and did not resist it showed «преступное равнодушие к поруганию Церкви». The page says his position hardened in his last years.

He was arrested in exile on 7 July 1937 and held in the prison at Chimkent. At the interrogation, accused of heading all the counter-revolutionary clergy, he bore himself with great courage and took the whole of the charge on himself. On 6 November the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot. **On 7 (20 new style) November 1937 he was shot in the Fox ravine outside Chimkent, together with «священномучениками митрополитом Иосифом (Петровых) и епископом Евгением (Кобрановым)».** He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of August 2000.

**Two things this page gives and this folder cannot hold.** The life titles him «митрополит Казанский и Свияжский», of Kazan **and Sviyazhsk**, where the office above reads Metropolitan of Kazan off the day line's «митр. Казанского»; the fuller see is reported and not written, an upgrade being unable to set that field. And the two bishops the page says were shot with him, «Иосиф (Петровых)» and «Евгений (Кобранов)», have no folder in this corpus, so the one relation this page does assert cannot be written either way; the fourteen others on his own line, whom nothing says died with him, get no relation on purpose.
