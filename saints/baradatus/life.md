# Baradatus

The venerable Baradatus came of Alexandria, and at a young age he chose the life of the desert. When Theodotus, patriarch of Antioch, whose years saint.gr gives as 418 to 427, learned of his rare virtues, he took care to draw him out of his ascetic life in order to use him in the many-sided social and charitable work of the church.

Baradatus accepted the offer, the page says, and in the seasons of fasting went about the cities, and by his holy life breathed reverence into men for God the creator and for his commandments, and breathed patience into them for the hard turns of life. So, in a manner befitting a holy man, he fell asleep on one of those journeys.

The distich over him turns on the apostle's word: having put his members to death on earth, as Paul says, Baradatus had a share of life in heaven. The page gives no year for his death and prints no hymn.

The Russian calendar keeps the three Syrian hermits together on this day. Baradatus, it says, began his hermit's labour in a hut in the country round Antioch, and afterwards built himself a stone cell on a mountain so narrow and so low that he could only be in it bent over; it had neither window nor door, and through chinks deliberately left in it came the wind, the rain and the cold, while in summer it was no shelter from the heat.

After many years a patriarch who came to see him persuaded him to leave that cramped house. He went then into a new confinement: covered from head to foot in a leather tunic with a small opening to breathe through, he prayed standing, with his hands lifted up to heaven. The grace of God upheld him in his labours and cleansed his heart of the passions, and people began to come to him for counsel in the spirit, which he gave them with deep humility; having reached many gifts of grace, he departed to the Lord in peace.

The older account the same page quotes says that he answers everyone who comes to him better and more strongly than those who have studied the dialectic of Aristotle, and that standing at that height of virtue he will not let his mind be lifted up, but forces it to crawl low at the foot of the mountain, knowing what harm a mind inflamed with pride can do a man's soul. The Russian summary names the patriarch who visited him «Александрийский Патриарх Феодорит», where the dating already recorded above reads the visitor otherwise; the disagreement is set down and not settled here.
