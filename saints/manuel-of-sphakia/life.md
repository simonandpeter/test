# Manuel of Sphakia

This new martyr was born at Sphakia in Crete, of devout parents. While he was still small he was taken captive by the Turks, was forcibly made a Muslim and stayed afterwards in their service. But he found his opportunity and escaped from his masters' house, and went to Mykonos, where he made his confession and lived as a Christian should. There he married and had six children.

His wife betrayed her marriage honour; and he moved with his six children into another house without putting her to shame. But the brother of that faithless wife, who served in the Turkish fleet, denounced Manuel to the Turkish captain, saying that although he had become a Muslim he had gone back to the Christian religion. When he was arrested by the captain, Manuel confessed his faith in Christ with courage; and after many tortures he was handed over to the Turkish admiral Koutzouk, who was at Chios.

The admiral examined him and gave sentence of death. The admiral's servants took Manuel and led him to the place of execution, which was called the Old Spring; and while the martyr bent his head the executioner appointed to kill him lost his nerve, threw down the sword and went off. Then a petty officer seized the sword, and when after many blows at the martyr's neck he could not behead him, he threw him down and cut his throat in the most horrible way, like a sheep.

All this happened at Chios on 15 March 1792, a Monday, at four in the afternoon. The Turks bound his honourable relic with boulders and threw it into the sea. A manuscript account of this saint's martyrdom, the page adds, is in the library of the monastery of Xenophon on the Holy Mountain; and the two lines it keeps for him call him a sheep led to the fold above.
