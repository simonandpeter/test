# Bassos of the Lavra of Pskov

saint.gr's calendar for 29 Μαρτίου names him last of three venerable fathers, «Όσιοι Ιωνάς, Μάρκος και Βάσσος», and the page behind the line has one sentence for all three: the venerable fathers Mark, Jonah and Bassos lived and practised ascesis in the Lavra of Pskov in Russia during the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, and fell asleep in holiness and peace. Of the three he is the one the page says least about, for it does not even gloss his name as it glosses the other two.

What the source keeps is a house, a nation, two centuries and a peaceful death. There is no year of birth and none of death, no office, no abbacy, no teacher, no disciple, no wonder, no relic and no hymn, and no sentence in which he acts. The monastery is recorded in the only form the source gives it, saint.gr's Greek «Λαύρα του Πσκωφ», and no Russian page was read to spell it at home.

He is kept with [Mark](/saints/mark-of-the-lavra-of-pskov) and [Jonah](/saints/jonah-of-the-lavra-of-pskov), which is the only way the source keeps him. He is not either of the two the corpus might confuse him with — Bassa of Edessa and Bassa of Tyre — who are both women and both martyrs, and neither of whom has anything to do with Pskov.

The Russian church calendar keeps this house’s first fathers on 29 марта ст. ст., and the life it links is one life for three of them. Nobody knows when the first hermits settled by the brook Каменец in the natural caves of the hill the country people called the holy hill; the monastery chronicle keeps the account of hunters from Изборск who came to the edge of the hill with their father and heard what seemed to be church singing, sung in order and with reverence, with no singers to be seen and the air full of the fragrance of incense.

In 1472 a peasant named Иван Дементьев was felling timber on the brow of the hill; one of the trees rolled down, tearing up the earth and the other trees with it, and the landslip opened the mouth of a cave over which was written «Богом зданная пещера», the cave built by God. To that place came a priest called Иоанн, surnamed «Шестник», the settler: a man of the Moscow country who had served the Russians of Юрьев, which is Tartu now, and who fled with his family to Pskov in 1470 from the persecution of the German Catholics. Hearing that his fellow priest «Исидор» had died a martyr, he resolved to withdraw into the newly found cave and to found a monastery there, on the very border with Livonia, as a bulwark of Orthodoxy.

Soon after they came, the priest’s wife fell ill, received the monastic habit with the name Васса, and died. Her righteousness was attested at once. Her husband and her spiritual father buried her in the wall of the cave built by God, and in the night her coffin was «изставлен из земли невидимою Божиею силою», set up out of the earth by an unseen power of God. Thinking they had not finished the burial rite, the two sang it over her a second time and buried the body again, and in the morning it was once more above the ground; and then it was understood to be a sign. The coffin was set in the cave on the left-hand side. The Russian source gives no year for her death, and none is written here.
