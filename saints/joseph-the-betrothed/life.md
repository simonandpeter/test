# Joseph the Betrothed

The righteous Joseph was born a son of Jacob, of the descendants of King David, and was a carpenter by trade, the trade that Jesus would later learn from him. When he was betrothed to Mary he was already of a respectable age. The Protoevangelium of James tells how the betrothed of the Virgin Mary was chosen, saying that, in witness of his honour and his righteousness, he was the only one among the widowers of Israel whose staff blossomed, and so was shown worthy of that betrothal.

Joseph shows his goodness and his blamelessness also in his attitude to the Virgin Mary's pregnancy. Not only does he make no legal denunciation, which under the Law of Moses would have led to Mary's being punished with death, but he thinks not to make her pregnancy known and to flee secretly, wishing to avoid shaming her; for in such cases the woman, even when she was not accused, was left with a stained reputation in the eyes of men.

Joseph listens to the voice of the angel who appears to him in a dream and tells him not to fear to take Mary as his wife, since the child conceived in her was of the Holy Spirit, and to give the child the name Jesus — which means Saviour — since he would save the world from its sins.

Joseph goes with Mary from Nazareth in Galilee to Bethlehem in Judaea, for the census of the people ordered by the emperor Octavian Augustus, because he was of the family of David; and there Mary gives birth to the child, the Lord Jesus Christ. According to the Gospel of Saint Luke, Joseph then accompanies the Mother of God and the Child to Jerusalem, for him to receive circumcision after the Law of Moses, and they returned to Jerusalem every year for the Jewish Passover.

Saint Matthew the Evangelist recalls one more episode after the Lord's birth. Hearing of the birth of a new king in Israel, Herod, the king of Judaea, has all the children up to two years old in the city of Bethlehem and its surroundings slaughtered; and an angel appears again in a dream to the righteous Joseph and tells him to go with his family into Egypt, to escape the rage of Herod, so that he goes for a time with Mary and Jesus into Egypt. After a while, being in Egypt, he is told by the angel in a dream that Herod the Great had died.

He is not present at the wedding at Cana of Galilee, where the Mother of God was, nor at the Crucifixion of the Lord beside her. That Jesus on the Cross entrusted his mother Mary to the care of Saint [John the Evangelist](/saints/john-the-theologian) has also been taken to suggest that Joseph was already dead at that moment, and that he and Mary had no other children who might have cared for the Mother of God; and besides that, it was Saint [Joseph of Arimathea](/saints/joseph-of-arimathea) who asked for the body of Jesus, a task that would have fallen to the righteous Joseph if he had still been alive.

The Gospels speak sometimes of the «brothers» of Jesus, among whom Iacov, Iust, Iuda and Simon are mentioned, and of some sisters. A tradition of the second century says that these brothers and sisters came of a previous marriage of Joseph's, with a woman named Salomeea, he being a widower before his betrothal to the Virgin Mary; so that they would have been his stepbrothers and stepsisters. Joseph was also the elder brother of Cleopa, who was likewise married to a woman named Maria, probably the same as the one called «Mary the mother of Joses»; so that the «brothers» of Jesus may rather have been cousins, the Greek word adelphos, translated «brother», being used within the extended family too.

In many icons of the Nativity, Joseph is shown being tempted by the devil, painted as an old man with folded wings, to break off the betrothal. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul și Dreptul Iosif, Logodnicul Sfintei Fecioare Maria» and gives no year for his birth or his death.
