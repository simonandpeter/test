# Eugene (Popov)

Eugene Alexeyevich Popov was born on 10 January 1879 in the village of
Khlevnoye in Voronezh province, now in the Zadonsk district of Lipetsk
region, the son of a psalmist who became a priest after the revolution and
died in 1920; his brothers Leonid and John also became priests in the years
of the revolution. After the parish school he finished the Zadonsk church
school in 1899, served as psalmist, married Eudocia, and had a son Michael.
In the years of revolution, when the Voronezh diocese needed priests ready
to witness to the Gospel to their last breath, he went knowingly into the
priesthood, to the Transfiguration church of the settlement of Olkhovatka in
the south of the province; the campaign for the seizure of church valuables
reached it almost at once, and though he called on his parishioners to save
the sacred vessels from desecration, everything with precious metal or
stones in it was taken. In 1922 Archbishop Tikhon (Vasilevsky) of Voronezh
went over to the Renovationist Higher Church Administration, cut off as the
whole diocese was from the imprisoned Patriarch, and most of the clergy,
including those of Olkhovatka and the villages round, followed him; when the
Patriarch was freed within the year and denounced the schism, Father Eugene
was among the few who returned at once under the Patriarch’s archbishop
Vladimir (Shimkovich), and thereafter stood firm against the Renovationist
clergy, calling them to repentance and his people to loyalty to the
Patriarch as the visible sign of the Church’s unity. At the end of the
twenties he was widowed. In 1930, in the persecution that came with
collectivisation, he was arrested for anti-Soviet agitation and hoarding
silver coin — his stand against Renovationism not the least of the reasons —
and exiled by the OGPU troika for three years to the Urals. Back in 1932, he
lived briefly at Krasnodar and Popovka, then in the autumn of 1933 at
Gorokhovka in the Kalitva district, where he urged the believers not to let
grain be poured into the Transfiguration church there, and at the end of
1933 returned to Olkhovatka, serving from time to time at Gorokhovka. He had
no house of his own and lived first with the chairwoman of the parish
council and then with Daria Burlutskaya until his arrest; the church at
Olkhovatka had been “temporarily taken for grain” with the believers’
supposed consent, so he served mostly in believers’ houses, and they kept
him; a priest with a conviction could hardly get the registration without
which the authorities allowed no services, but he pressed the believers to
petition for the church to be emptied of grain and reopened, which they did
in the summer of 1934 and were refused. On 12 November 1935, on the eve of
the “wholesale liquidation of religious associations of every orientation”,
he was arrested for anti-Soviet agitation and on 14 May 1936 given five
years in the camps by the special college of the Voronezh regional court at
Rossosh, admitting nothing; from the prisons of Rossosh and Michurinsk he
reached the Nerekhta section of the Volga camp in Yaroslavl region on 30
October 1936, and worked for a time as watchman at the power station. In
August 1937, in the great terror, a new case was opened on a denunciation
that he agitated among the prisoners, spread slanderous rumours about the
regime of the construction, refused work and called others to refuse, and he
was joined to the group case of the priests [Gleb
Apukhtin](/saints/gleb-apukhtin), [Peter Grigoriev](/saints/peter-grigoriev)
and others accused of an underground anti-Soviet group; he admitted nothing.
On 22 September 1937 the troika of the Yaroslavl NKVD sentenced him to death
— for counter-revolutionary agitation, malicious refusal of work, condoling
on the shooting of enemies of the people, and abetting the theft of
materials as watchman — and he was shot on 23 September 1937 and buried in
the common grave of the Nerekhta section of the camp. Glorified in 2000, he
is kept on 10 September of the old style, 23 September, and in the synaxes
of the Rostov-Yaroslavl saints and of the Voronezh metropolia.
