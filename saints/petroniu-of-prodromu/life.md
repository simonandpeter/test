# Petroniu of Prodromu

He was born on 23 May 1916 at Fărcașa in the district of Neamț, to a family of devout peasants, and was given the name Petru at his baptism. His parents were Ion and Olimpiada Tănase, whom doxologia's life names as a saint in her own right; they had eight children, six girls and two boys, and Petru was the seventh. His mother was a living example of struggle and of love towards God and towards her neighbour, and from her he took his zeal for spiritual things while he was still small.

At fourteen, in 1930, he entered the monastery of Neamț as a novice, spent a year of apprenticeship at the Vovidenia skete, and then went to the school of chanters at Neamț, where he came to know Ioan Iacob the Chozebite and profited much from his company. From 1935 to 1941 he was at the monastic seminary of Cernica, and from 1941 at the Central Seminary in Bucharest. On 14 August 1942 he was tonsured at Neamț, in the old hesychast lavra of Saint Paisius, and given the name Petroniu.

That September he joined the brotherhood of the Antim monastery in Bucharest to go on with his theology, enrolled a year later in the Faculty of Mathematics as well, and worked in the patriarchal administration. He was ordained hierodeacon on 22 September 1942, appointed to serve the patriarchal cathedral on 15 December 1947, and ordained priest on 26 December 1947, being made protosynghel at the end of the same liturgy. From 1946 to 1948 he was secretary to Patriarch Nicodim Munteanu. At Antim he took part in the meetings of the Burning Bush, and so came to know Father Ioan Kulâghin, who had learned the prayer of the heart in the Paisian brotherhood at Optina.

When Patriarch Justinian founded the higher monastic seminary at Neamț and sent the best-prepared monks of Antim to teach there, Petroniu was among them, and so returned to the house of his tonsure; on 5 May 1952 he was made a confessor, a power he was shaken by all his life. The regime then closed the seminary, and he went to Slatina, to the brotherhood led by Cleopa of Sihăstria, where he organised the chanters and ran the monastic school; after Cleopa's arrest and withdrawal into the wilderness the council of the monastery elected him abbot, and he led it from 1957 to 1959. He begged them in tears not to lay it on him, counting himself unworthy, and took it only at their insistence.

In May 1959, before the decree that drove the monks out of the monasteries, the authorities forced him to leave Slatina. He withdrew to Broșteni, to a sister's house, and worked as a forestry labourer until 1961, when he settled at the monastery of Curtea de Argeș and was taken on, with difficulty, as watchman and cashier-secretary. For a long time the authorities harried and insulted him and would not give him leave to live there; only Patriarch Justinian's personal intervention settled it, on 9 May 1963, when he was formally enrolled in that brotherhood and given back the right to serve at the altar that had been taken from him. In 1965, after many struggles, he fell ill with chronic hepatitis and withdrew to Sihăstria.

In 1975 he made a pilgrimage to the Holy Land and from there to the Holy Mountain, where he was much helped by meeting Dionisie of Colciu and other Romanian and Greek ascetics. In 1976 Patriarch Justinian charged him to gather five monks and go to Athos to help the brotherhood of the skete of Prodromu; there he established the daily liturgy and the seven hours and set about restoring the buildings. On 15 October 1984 the brotherhood elected him abbot against his will, and he governed the Romanian skete for twenty-seven years, until the beginning of February 2011, when he stepped down at ninety-five for reasons of health. In his time the skete came back to life, the brotherhood passed forty, and the church and enclosure were restored.

He was a retreating, very humble man who preached more by what he did than by what he said, and he loved the church so much that he would say a Christian should prefer going to church to lying in bed even when ill. In his last years his bodily sufferings were hard to bear, and he never complained. Until the last day he could come to church he would not sit in the stall during the liturgy, but stood outside it so that self-love should not overcome him; in the refectory he never ate all that was put on his plate. He was silent, said the Jesus Prayer continually, avoided going to other monasteries for feasts or memorials — the cell, he said, both teaches the monk and saves him — and answered every letter sent to him. He remembered no wrong done to him and forgave easily. He ended this life on 22 February 2011.
