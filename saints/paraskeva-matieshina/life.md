# Paraskeva (Matieshina)

**The one name on this day that is not a martyr: a confessor who outlived the camps by eighteen years and died of her illnesses.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar closes 22 ноября ст. ст. with a sentence to itself — «Прп. Параскевы исп (1952).» — and carries no life for her; the life here is what azbyka.ru prints for the civil 5 декабря out of fond.ru, a long life signed by the archpriest Максим Максимов. She was born on 28 October 1888 in the village of Песчаное in the Каменец-Подольский district of the province of Подолия, to a peasant family, Фёдор and Матрона Матиешины. The house was religious and patriarchal, and she remembered of her childhood that «по вечерам мы не знали другого времяпрепровождения, как чтение Священного Писания или житий святых, и не представляли, что может быть иначе» — of an evening they knew no other way of passing the time than reading the Scriptures or the lives of the saints, and did not imagine it could be otherwise.

**The first thing she promised God she promised at three years old.** They began teaching her to fast, and at first she would not believe that in the Great Fast she must give up the milk she loved; she asked for it and was fretful, and a day later fell badly ill and felt the connection between the illness and her unwillingness, and understood that the fast was a serious thing. Then she made her first promise to God: never to drink milk in a fast. Two days later she was well, and from that time she fasted always, easily and gladly. At about the same age her love of the services and of church singing showed itself: once in church in the Great Fast the singing of «Аллилуиа» made so deep an impression on her that she wept when it ended, and at home she sang it to her mother from memory and remembered it all her life. Her grandfather Амвросий taught her her letters out of the Bible, and she came to know the Scriptures very well, loving best the epistles of the apostles and the Gospel of John.

**She wanted the monastery early and her grandmother made her earn it.** Her mother died when she was eight, and after her the grandfather and a younger sister, so that she became the chief worker of the house after her father; going out to work together they liked to sing psalms on the road. The grandmother would not let her think of a monastery; a suitor was found, and she explained to the young man gently that she would not marry because she meant to enter a monastery — and the young man followed her example, went to Athos and was tonsured there. Told she would never bear the hardships of monastic life, she set herself to fast hard and to work on herself in order to be ready for privation, and even tormented herself, putting splinters in her shoes to learn to bear pain. At last the grandmother let her go, being convinced that life in the world would do her granddaughter no good.

**She found the monastery of her dream at the second attempt, and she had seen it in a dream before she found it.** Learning the address of a missionary monastery that took sisters without a money contribution, she wrote and, having leave to come, left home with a friend in 1907; almost without money, lightly dressed, at times hungry, having borne much, the two girls reached by a miracle the Теолинский monastery of the Transfiguration in the Августов district of the Сувалки province, twenty-five versts from the station of Гродно, and were received as novices. Before she entered she had asked the Lord to show her where to go, and had seen in a sleep an icon of the Mother of God shining with a blinding light, standing cornerwise on the solea and ringed with a blue band bearing the words «В этом монастыре ты будешь жить». At Теолин she found nothing of the kind. Some years later, going home on leave, she turned aside to the Турковичский monastery of the diocese of Варшава and Холм, where there was a wonderworking icon; the journey was hard, she was worn out and fell ill and had to stay — and there, when the honoured icon was carried into the winter church and set slantwise on the solea, she came in and knew what she had seen in the dream. In 1912 she was received among the sisters of that house.

**The war carried her house to Дмитров and left her there.** In 1914, when the Germans took Poland, the monastery was evacuated to Moscow and then to Дмитров; in 1918, the war over, it went back to Турковичи, and the novice Параскева was left behind to send on the monastery property after those who had gone. The Турковичский house soon ceased to exist, and she stayed at Дмитров, took a lodging on the edge of the town in the village of Подлипичье and served in the local church of the Kazan icon. A peasant asked her to teach his daughter church singing; others joined, and the girls’ choir grew fast to sixty and became two choirs. She taught them not singing only but the Law of God and how to live as Christians, thought too much tenderness in bringing up slackened the soul, was outwardly somewhat severe with her pupils, and loved them as her own daughters.

**In 1920 she became the spiritual daughter of the bishop of Дмитров, whom the corpus keeps as [Seraphim](/saints/seraphim-of-dmitrov), and his hand is on everything after.** He led her strictly and spoke of her to others with respect. In 1921 he tonsured her to the rassophore without changing her name and assigned her to the Спасо-Влахернский monastery; and when the parishioners of the Kazan church petitioned to have her back at Дмитров to go on teaching their children’s reading and singing, promising her lodging and food, he wrote on 25 July 1921 a resolution granting it «Согласно усиленной просьбе крестьян, в целях укрепления веры и Слова Божия», for the strengthening of the faith and the Word of God, keeping her at the same time among the sisters of the house. **On 27 November 1921 he ordained her, by the ancient rite, to the diaconate** — «посвящена владыкой по древнему чину в диакониссы, на алтарное служение», for service at the altar — and from then she served in the Kazan church of Подлипичье, where he very often served himself and near which he lived. Her diary, which the life quotes at length, calls him «мудрый старец», her wise elder, and «этот второй кроткий Давид», this second meek David, who struck her Goliath in the head.

**She was forbidden to teach in 1924 and went on anyway; the monastery was closed in 1928 and it changed nothing.** Called to the OGPU in 1924 she was ordered to stop the classes; she stopped them and did not stop caring for the girls or directing the choir, and in 1928, ten years after the choir began, wrote them a word of thanks that the life prints whole — thanking them for love, obedience, humility, patience, constancy and work, saying that she saw in herself first of all many faults and many gross errors over those ten years, and that neither the slander raised against her nor the reproaches that often fell on them because of her had shaken their faithfulness. On Great Friday, 20 April 1929, keeping the blessing of her авва to read the Lord’s Prayer a hundred and fifty times on the night of the Passion, she spent the whole night in prayer, and understood then for the first time that «куплена Ценою, что все члены мои назнаменованы печатью страданий Христа».

**Arrested in 1931, five years in Kazakhstan, and two visions of the apostles.** On 27 May 1931 she was arrested; she told the investigator that she was deprived of the vote as a nun, that she had entered a monastery in 1912 in the province of Холм, that the sisters were all moved to Moscow in 1914 and that she came to Дмитров in 1918, took a lodging, served in the church, sang in the choir and taught children church singing, that in 1924 she was called to the OGPU and forbidden to teach girls church singing and had ended the classes from then and was to the choir as a director, and «Себя я в антисоветской деятельности виновной не признаю». On 28 June 1931 the troika of the OGPU sentenced her to five years of exile in Kazakhstan. Before she left she was granted a vision of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, who blessed her and her companions. She was taken first to Alma-Ata and set to work near the town as cook to a large working gang of exiles, who called her sister for the care and compassion she showed them; moved into Alma-Ata, worn out by months of eating whatever came, she would not take state work. There she met unexpectedly her own spiritual father, sent to the same place in August 1932, and for the three months he lived in the town could be comforted by praying with him and by his counsel. She was then moved to Барабинск, living mostly on flat cakes the exiles baked from flour of their own grinding, out of ears gleaned in the fields after harvest.

**The second vision promised her the keys, and she got them.** The exile ended in 1935 and she settled, much weakened and ill, at Astrakhan, then moved to the town of Кирсанов in the Тамбов region; her former pupils, hearing of her illnesses, called her to Дмитров to be near her own places. There the war found her, and the hunger with it. She fell ill of an abscess of the lungs and suffered heavily; the medicines did not help and her state was thought hopeless, and she was making ready to die when one night she was granted a second vision of the apostles Peter and Paul: Peter promised her recovery and, holding out the keys that were in his hands, said «Ты еще будешь этими ключами отворять церковные двери в Подлипичье» — you will yet open the church doors at Подлипичье with these keys. From that day, against the doctors’ expectation, she began to mend. Strong again, she went to her relatives in Ukraine, who received her warmly, and then returned to Дмитров, where she worked again in the Kazan church of Подлипичье, unlocking it in the mornings, reading, singing and directing her choir.

**She died on the feast of the Entry, and the two sources give two years for it.** Her health failed again, the abscess returned, arthritis and furunculosis came with it; she tried to be treated with humility, was easier for a time, but the illness did not pass, and she could get to church more and more rarely. She lost her lodging in the town and had to move to the village of Бирлово, where nobody could give a sick woman the care she needed; a paralysis came on her and she was left wholly helpless, suffering very greatly, above all from the sores and the bedsores. The life closes that she «скончалась в день праздника Введения во храм Пресвятой Богородицы 4 декабря 1953 года» and was buried in the cemetery of Дмитров, where days.pravoslavie.ru prints «(1952)» — and that all her life of many sorrows she carried out what she had been taught and always taught others: «Будьте смиренны, Христу Спасу подобны», be humble, like Christ the Saviour. **Her relics were found on 11 September 2001** and solemnly translated on 30 September from the Борисоглебский monastery of Дмитров to the Спасо-Влахернский convent in the village of Деденёво — a finding of relics and not a death, which is why it is here and in no `dates` field.
