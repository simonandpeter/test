# Heraclius of Athens

saint.gr keeps eight martyrs on one page for 18 May, all of them martyred, it says, in the years of the emperor Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251. Heraclius, [Paulinus](/saints/paulinus-of-athens) and [Benedimus](/saints/benedimus-of-athens) were from Athens, and there they fought a hard fight against the error of the idols and against the philosophers who made war on the Christian religion. They were seized, tortured, and at the last beheaded.

The couplet the page prints for him plays on his name and the labours of Heracles: one contest of yours, Heraclius, towards the sword, covers over all the labours of Heracles. Of the others on the page it says that Peter was of Lampsacus and was crushed with chains and staves, and that Paul and Andrew, Mesopotamians and soldiers of Decius, became soldiers of Christ at Athens and were stoned with Dionysius and Christina. The company's own verse calls them an eightfold band of victors raised from earth to the city above.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 18 May in an entry of three sentences, headed for Heraclius, [Paulinus](/saints/paulinus-of-athens) and [Benedimus](/saints/benedimus-of-athens). They were Athenians, it says; they suffered for the faith in the time of Decius; and for the name of Christ they were burned in a fiery furnace. That last is not what the Greek page says, which has the three of them seized, tortured and at the last beheaded; both readings stand here as their books print them. The Prologue names the reign and gives it no bounds, so no year is taken from it. The heading names him first of the three.

The Russian calendar's notice of the three is four sentences and they share it. They suffered for Christ at Athens, where they preached Christ to the pagans and urged them to leave the worship of senseless idols. These chosen ones of God were handed over to judgement together with their own disciples, who had taken the true faith from them; the page names none of the disciples. After many tortures all of them were thrown into a heated furnace, and in it they gave up their souls to God. The calendar gives no governor, no emperor and no year in its text, and the year its day line prints falls inside the bracket this folder already carried.
