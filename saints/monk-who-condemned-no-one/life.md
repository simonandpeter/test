# The Monk Who Condemned No One

The Ohrid Prologue's second entry for this day is the commemoration of a monk whose name it does not give. Its heading is only „Спомен на монаха“, the commemoration of a monk, and the sentence beneath it says at once what he is remembered for: that he died joyfully, because in the whole of his life he had condemned nobody. The entry is blunt about what he was otherwise, and does not soften it in order to praise him — he was lazy, it says, careless and unprayerful — but through his whole life he judged no man.

When he came to die he was cheerful, and the brethren asked him how a man with so many sins could be dying cheerful. He answered that he had just then seen the angels, and that they had shown him a sheet of paper with his many sins written out on it; and that he had said to them: the Lord has said, judge not, that ye be not judged. I never judged anybody, and I hope in the mercy of God that He will not judge me. At that, he said, the angels tore the paper up. The monks who heard him were astonished, and took the lesson.

That is the whole of the entry. It gives him no name, no country and no monastery, no year and no century; it does not say under whom he lived, who his abbot was, or where he was buried, and it prints no hymn. The Prologue is the only one of the four calendars read for him, and because it names him nowhere he could not be looked for in the other three by name. For the same reason this folder stores no form of his name in any language.

*After the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 30. март ст. ст. — [the day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-04-12&prolog=1); read 4 October 2026.*
