# Christina of Tyre

In the city of Tyre there was a man of great family named Urban who held the governorship, and he had a daughter, a young virgin, who from the years of her youth knew God her Maker and laid down her soul for Him, bearing many cruel torments manfully. When she was born her parents named her Christina — not, the life says, out of a pagan understanding, but by the providence of God, for the name itself was a kind of prophecy, that this girl, coming of age, would be a Christian, a servant, a bride and a martyr of Christ.

At eleven years old her beauty was such that there was no girl like her, and her father, wishing to keep her from being seen, appointed her a dwelling in the highest part of the palace, gave her good serving-women, brought in his gods of gold and silver and ordered her to worship them and to offer them incense every day. Many rich and well-born men, hearing of her beauty, asked her of her father for their sons, and Urban answered that he would give his daughter to no man in marriage but would make a gift of her to his gods, since the merciful gods had loved her greatly and she would live for them and serve them always.

But Christina grew in years and in understanding, and came to the knowledge of the truth, God lighting her mind with His grace: looking out of her window at the sky and its lights she knew the Maker, and thinking of the senselessness and emptiness of the soulless idols she would neither burn incense to them nor bow to them, but looked eastward and wept, saying to herself, how long shall the hearts of men stay so darkened, and their unknowing thought be under cloud, and not look toward the Lord God who made heaven and earth and adorned it with an unseen adornment such as this?

So she began to worship the true God who dwells in heaven, and to pray Him with tears to make Himself known to her, that she might know perfectly the One for love of whom her heart had caught fire. After many days of prayer and fasting she was granted a visitation of God's mercy: the angel of the Lord appeared to her, signed her with the sign of the Cross, called her the bride of Christ, made her wise in the knowledge of God, and told her of her suffering to come, that she would be tortured by three tormentors, for the One God in Trinity. He strengthened her for the struggle and gave her clean bread to eat, for she was hungry with fasting. After that she rejoiced in spirit, stretched out her prayers more warmly to the Lord, and, filling with zeal, broke the idols of gold and silver in pieces one by one and threw them down out of her window into the street, where next day the passers-by gathered up the broken gold and silver for themselves.

Then the governor Urban came up into the high palace to visit his daughter and to bow to his gods, and not seeing his idols asked where they were; and she was silent. What followed is very long, and the whole of the middle of it is passed over here: three tormentors in turn, of whom her own father was the first, and torments that did not kill her.

At the last a great sorcerer who knew how to charm all living things was brought, and ordered to gather by his charms a multitude of snakes, vipers and scorpions and loose them against the martyr to kill her with their poisoned bites. They were loosed on her, and not one of them hurt her, though they crawled and coiled about her body; and while the sorcerer stood near urging them on with his whisperings, they turned at God's command upon him and killed him with their bites. Then the saint said to the crawling things: I charge you, snakes and vipers and scorpions, in the name of Jesus Christ, to go each to your own place and hurt nobody. And they scattered at once, each going where it belonged.

An hour later Christina had pity on the dead man, prayed to the Lord, raised him, and made him a Christian; and not he only, but many who saw such glorious wonders believed in the Lord. The governor in his anger ordered the virgin's breasts to be cut off, and milk ran from the wounds instead of blood; then he had her tongue cut out, which glorified Christ God. Even after that she spoke clearly, and taking up the severed tongue she threw it in the governor's face, striking him in the eye, and said: Pagan, eat my limbs! He was blinded by that blow, while she blessed God greatly and reviled the idols and their servants. Unable to bear any more of it, and angry for the blinding of his eyes, he gave her over to death; and being pierced by the soldiers with sharpened irons the spotless virgin, the Great Martyr of Christ Christina, gave her honourable and holy soul into the hands of her Lord.

In her, the life ends, God showed His great power, in that three cruel tormentors could not overcome this one girl. A man of her own kin who believed in Christ took her suffering body and buried it with honour. These things were done while the pagan Severus reigned over the Romans and the Greeks.
