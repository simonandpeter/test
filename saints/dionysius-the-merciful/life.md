# Dionysius the Merciful

saint.gr begins the notice of Dionysius the Merciful, metropolitan of Larissa and founder of the monastery of St Nicholas Anapafsas at Meteora, by saying what does not exist: no hagiological texts survive for him, no services, no synaxaria, no life. What survives instead is a picture and a chronicle, and the page builds him out of those two.

The picture is a wall-painting of 1627 in the left aisle of the church of the Holy Unmercenaries at Trikala, where seven holy archbishops of Larissa are painted in chronological order from left to right: Thomas Gorianites, Cyprian the Wonderworker, Anthony the Most Learned and New Theologian, Bessarion, Dionysius the Merciful, Mark the Hesychast, and Bessarion of the Saviour. The inscription reads «ο Άγιος Διονύσιος ο Ελεήμων Αρχιεπίσκοπος [λαρίσης]», and the page takes it as witness that he was held a saint by the conscience of his own flock and numbered among the local saints. The surname Merciful, it says, proves in its turn how much he gave, in the Church's work and in the city's, as a compassionate servant of those of low estate and a comforter of those who suffered.

From the order of the seven the page reckons that his episcopate must fall after 1489 or 1490 and before 1499, and that it was certainly short; about 1499 he resigned his office and Mark the Hesychast took his place, after which he withdrew and lived as a monk at St Nicholas Anapafsas, of which he was the later founder. The Chronicle of Meteora, written shortly after 1529, mentions him many times: it was he who first gave the title of abbot to the hieromonk Ioasaph, «father» of the monastery of the Transfiguration of Meteora — a man with no connection to the royal founder of the same name, John Uroš Palaiologos, the monk Ioasaph — and he who afterwards ordained him bishop of Phanari. He fell asleep in holiness and peace on Great Thursday of the year 1510.
