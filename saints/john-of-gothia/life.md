# John of Gothia

John, bishop of Gothia, was from the country of the Scythians. When he had come to the measure of spiritual maturity and was to be named to the hierarch's throne, his people sent him to the bishop of Iberia, that is of Georgia, and he received consecration from him, because the iconoclast heresy was still in the Greek parts and there was no orthodox hand to be had there.

After the death of the iconoclast emperors he went to Constantinople and spoke with the empress Irene, and preached the Orthodox faith. In his own country he endured many dangers, because his own people wanted to sell him to the nobles of the Lazi.

He passed to the Lord in the city of Amastris in Pontus, and his honourable relics were brought by ship to the monastery of the Holy Apostles and laid there. That is the whole of what doxologia's life prints for him: no year of birth, no year of death, no hymn, and no account of his years in the see beyond the dangers it names.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 26 June, and fills in what the Romanian life leaves out. saint.gr says he was born at the beginning of the eighth century, when Leo the Isaurian was emperor, and came from the parts of the Crimea; that his parents were called Leo and Photine and passed their piety on to their son; and that he was marked from childhood for his virtuous life and his zeal for the faith. In the years of the iconoclasm, it says, he kept church communion rather with Rome, which that wound of the East did not touch. As bishop of Gothia he was a teacher and a lover of men, and remained simple, humble-minded, poor and without love of money, a brother to the priests and a father to his laity. Some bloody rising forced the good shepherd to take refuge, with many Christians, at Amastris on the Black Sea; there he stayed four years and died in 780, doing good to the last moment, and clergy and people buried him with great honours.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 19 May, where the Romanian and Greek calendars keep him on 26 June, and it tells his exile differently at every point but one. He was bishop in Georgia, it says — where the Romanian life has him only sent to the bishop of Iberia, that is of Georgia, to be consecrated — and when the Tatar kagan there began to torment the Christians he withdrew for four years among the Goths in Bessarabia, where the Greek page has him take refuge at Amastris on the Black Sea. The four years are the same four years in both books. It adds a parenthesis of its own: that the Gothic diocese was formed in the time of Constantine the Great.

Hearing of the kagan’s death he returned to his service, the Prologue says, and guided his flock zealously and in a way pleasing to God — where the Romanian life has him pass to the Lord at Amastris in Pontus and the Greek has him die there. Before his death he said: in forty days I go to be judged with the kagan, which the book glosses itself, that through death he goes before the throne of God. And so it was: on the fortieth day he departed and went to the Lord. He fell asleep in peace in the eighth century.
