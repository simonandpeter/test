# Varus

Varus was a soldier in Egypt in the reign of the emperor Maximian, and for fear of the idolaters he kept his Christianity hidden. When the order came into those parts that Christians who would not sacrifice be killed, he began to go by night to the prisons where the confessors were held. He bought the guards with gold, kissed the martyrs' chains, washed their blood and bound up their wounds, brought them food, and asked them to beg mercy for him from the Lord.

Seven Christian teachers were taken out of the wilderness, beaten and thrown into prison, and Varus came to them by night. He loosed their bonds and set food before them, for they had gone eight days without any; and falling at their feet he said that he wished to suffer for Christ as they did, but had not the strength, and trembled at the torments he saw them bear.

They answered him that no man reaps who has not sown and no man is crowned who has not suffered, and they bade him travel the martyr's road with them. He stayed in the prison the whole of that night. In the morning the officers found him sitting among the chained men and warned him that he would lose his rank and his life; he told them he was ready to die with the Christians.

One of the seven had died in the night of his wounds. When the governor counted six where there had been seven, Varus stood out and said that he was the seventh, and would pay what the dead man owed. He was beaten with rods, torn on the ribs with iron claws, nailed head downward to a beam, flayed and cut; and after five hours of it he gave up his soul. His body was thrown outside the city among the carcasses of beasts.

There [Cleopatra](/saints/cleopatra), a widow of Palestine whose husband had commanded soldiers in Egypt, took it up by night and buried it in her own chamber. Years afterwards she carried the relics home to the village of Edra near Tabor and built a church over them; and the martyr appeared to her holding her dead son John, both of them shining like the sun, and told her that he had asked God to enrol the boy in the army of the heavenly Emperor.
