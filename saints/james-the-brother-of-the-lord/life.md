# James the Brother of the Lord

James was the son of Joseph, the betrothed of the Most Pure Virgin. From his youth he loved a hard life: he never ate cooked food or oil but only bread, never tasted wine or any strong drink but only water, never went into the bath, and threw off all bodily ease. He wore a rough hair garment on his body and spent the nights in prayer, sleeping very little, and from his frequent bending of the knees the skin had grown hard as a camel's. He kept his virginity unspotted to the end.

The Romanian life explains why he is called the brother of the Lord. Joseph, it says, had four sons by his first wife Salome, the daughter of Haggai the brother of Zacharias the priest, the father of John the Baptist: James, Simeon, Judas and Joses. He had three daughters also: Esther, Mary and Salome, who was the wife of Zebedee and the mother of the apostle [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian). When his first wife died, Joseph raised his children in the fear of the Lord, and being an old and wise man was given the Virgin Mary in betrothal by the priests.

He was the first bishop of Jerusalem, and held that see thirty years. The life's account of his end is the one the Church knows: he was set on a height and told to speak against Christ before the people, and spoke for Him instead; the crowd cried out that the just man had been deceived, and threw him down.

He was badly hurt by the fall but still alive, and raising himself on his knees he lifted his hands and prayed: "Lord, forgive them this sin, for they know not what they do." They stoned him where he knelt. A priest of the sons of Rechab cried out to them to stop — the just man prays for you and you kill him — and then a man ran up with a club and struck him on the head with all his strength, so that his brains came out, and he gave up his spirit. He was buried near the temple, and the faithful mourned him greatly. He was sixty-six years old.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 23 October, and saint.gr sets down both what the tradition says of his kinship and its own reserve about it. The tradition holds, it says, that he was one of the sons of Joseph by another wife, and that this is why he was called the brother of the Lord; but his relationship to the Lord «δεν είναι ομόφωνα καθορισμένη», is not unanimously settled. What is fact, the page goes on, is that James the Brother of God became the first bishop of Jerusalem and that it was he who wrote the first Divine Liturgy of the Christian Church. He shepherded the Church of Jerusalem with justice, with a brave tenderness and with steadiness in the faith, and that roused the malice of those who opposed him: they seized him, threw him down from the pinnacle of the Temple, and while he was still alive finished him with a savage blow of a club to the head. The page names the Catholic Epistle in the New Testament as his work, and quotes out of it the counsel that one should become a doer of the word of God and not a hearer only, deceiving oneself. It adds that his memory is kept again on the Sunday after the Nativity of Christ, together with David the Prophet and Joseph the Betrothed, and that his synaxis used to be held at his own church inside the sanctuary of the Most Holy Theotokos at the Chalkoprateia.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this same day, 23 октября ст. ст., and files three readings of his life under one index. They open from the Epistle to the Galatians, where he is counted among the pillars of the Church, and they say that the risen Lord appeared to him and set him over the church of Jerusalem. So his work was unlike the other apostles': he did not travel preaching but taught and served in the one city, and he presided at the apostolic council there in the year 51, where the proposal he made became the council's decision.

The Russian account of his end adds the political reason for its timing. The Jewish leaders feared the whole people would turn to Christ, and used the gap between the departure of the procurator Festus and the arrival of Albinus; the high priest then was a Sadducee named Ananus. They brought him onto the portico of the temple before a great crowd and asked him, with a few flattering words first, to tell them about the crucified one. He answered aloud that He sits in heaven at the right hand of the Power and will come again on the clouds of heaven, and the Christians in the crowd cried out «Осанна Сыну Давидову!»

The pages add what the other calendars do not. He had been a Nazirite from childhood, under the vow that kept a man from wine and from cutting his hair. Many Christians were killed with him. The Jewish historian Josephus, listing the causes of the fall of Jerusalem, says that God punished the people among other things for the killing of the just James. His catholic epistle was written shortly before his death to comfort the converted Jews in the sufferings ahead of them and to warn them against the error that faith alone can save a man, and the Church ascribes to him the oldest order of the Divine Liturgy. In 1853 the patriarch of Alexandria sent part of his relics to Moscow, and the calendar is careful to distinguish him from the two other apostles of his name, whose days it keeps in April and in October.
