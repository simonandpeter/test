# Paul the Presbyter (4 September)

Paul Vasilevsky was born on 22 June 1881 in the village of Karabuzino in the
Kesova Gora district of Tver province, into the family of a church servant,
Ivan Vasilevsky; the family was large, nine children, and when Paul was five
his father died and an uncle took him in. He finished the church school; in
1916 he was called up and served until 1918 in the 177th reserve infantry
regiment quartered at Novgorod; demobilised, he came home, served as
psalmist, married Anna Mikheyeva, the daughter of devout peasants who had
thirteen children of their own and had taken in a fourteenth, and was
ordained to the church of the village of Khobotskoye, where his
father-in-law bought the family a house. Five children were born to them
and all died young but the last, a daughter, Tamara. Later he served at the
St Nicholas church of Dobrynino in the Maksatikha district, and though his
time there was short the people had come to love him. In 1937 Russia was in
effect under martial law, with countless innocent people arrested, and the
cases were often run not by the local men but by soldiers of the NKVD
troops, who made the arrests, conducted the investigation and took part in
the shootings. He was arrested on 6 August at six in the evening, just back
with his wife and daughter from the forest, where they had been gathering
mushrooms and berries for the winter: three NKVD men turned the house over,
took nothing, and would not let his wife and daughter see him even to the
edge of the village. They walked him off, arresting as they went through
the other villages, to Dymtsevo, where they arrested a very old priest,
Michael Kosukhin, who could not walk, and there the prisoners were put in a
lorry and driven to prison. The investigation took four days: two of
questioning the peasants of the Dobrynino parish, some of them already under
arrest themselves, and on the third, 9 August, the priest. Tell us of your
anti-Soviet and counter-revolutionary activity — I have done none. How do
you regard going from house to house collecting signatures for a procession
with the icon during the spring sowing? — I deny disrupting the sowing; if
there was anything it was only at Dubishchi, where I had been allowed to go
round in the morning, and in some of the farmers’ houses adults stayed
behind, though I had not asked them to. What is your connection with
Voinov? — He is my parishioner; his children sang in the church. What did
you say in church about war? — I never spoke of war in church; I think we
shall have no war soon, of which I am convinced, though I have told no one.
The next day, 10 August, without leaving the village, the NKVD lieutenant
closed the investigation, holding the priest’s guilt in anti-state activity
proven though he had admitted none, and sent the case to the troika, which
on 13 September ordered him shot; he was shot a few days later, on
17 September 1937 — 4 September of the old style, the day the Russian
calendar keeps him, with the bishops Gregory and Sergius and their company.
The Jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000 numbered him among the new
martyrs and confessors of Russia.
