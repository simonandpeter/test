# Joseph of Thessalonica

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its page for him carries the feast line 15 Iulie and nothing else — no Viață tab and no Tropar tab — so all that is recorded here is what the calendar's own line for the day says: «Sfântul Ierarh Iosif, Arhiepiscopul Tesalonicului», the holy hierarch Joseph, Archbishop of Thessalonica.

No year, no place beyond the see, and no account of his life were found on the Romanian pages read for this day, and nothing has been supplied from elsewhere. The Serbian calendar has not been checked for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him a day earlier, on 14 July, and saint.gr tells the life the Romanian page withheld. It says he lived in the years of Leo V the Iconoclast (813-820), that his parents were the devout Photinos and Theoktiste, and that his brother was [Theodore the Studite](/saints/theodore-the-studite); because of his praiseworthy life he was chosen archbishop of Thessalonica. After some time in the see he was recalled to Constantinople by imperial order, presented himself to the emperor, and confessed with courage that he reverenced and honoured the holy icons. For that he was ill-used, imprisoned, and exiled to an island; afterwards the impious emperor Theophilos made him taste harder sufferings and darker prisons, and in hunger and thirst and many afflictions he departed for eternal life in 832.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this day as «Свт. Иосифа Студита, архиеп. Солунского (830)», and the life behind that line begins with his brother: he was the brother of [Theodore the Studite](/saints/theodore-the-studite), and struggled together with him at the monastery of Sakkoudion under the venerable abbot whose memory the same calendar keeps on 5 April. For that ascetic life he was chosen archbishop of Thessalonica by one voice.

With his brother he spoke against the unlawful marriage of the emperor Constantine, who reigned from 780 to 797, and for that he was tortured and shut up in prison on a desert island. The emperor Michael Rangabe, who reigned from 811 to 813, let him out. Under Leo the Armenian, from 813 to 820, he suffered again with his brother, this time for the honouring of the holy icons: in prison he was put to torture and would not be moved, and when the iconoclast emperor required his signature to an iconoclast confession of faith he refused, and was thrown into a second and a foul prison for it.

Under Michael the Stammerer, who reigned from 820 to 829, he was released with the other monks who had suffered for the icons, and he spent his last years in the monastery of Studion, where he reposed in 830. He is known as a writer of church hymns: the triodia and stichera of the Lenten Triodion are his, and the penitential canon for the Sunday of the Prodigal Son, and other hymns besides. He wrote several homilies for the feasts, of which the best known is the one on the Exaltation of the Honourable Cross of the Lord. The calendar prints no hymn under his name on this day.
