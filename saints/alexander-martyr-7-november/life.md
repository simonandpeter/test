# Alexander (7 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 7 Νοεμβρίου keeps a martyr Alexander from Thessalonica, and its notice is two sentences. He lived in the years of the emperor Maximian, which the page dates to 298, and came from Thessalonica. He was brought before the tyrant's tribunal because he had kicked over the altar of the idols and scattered the things sacrificed upon it; beheading was ordered at once, and so he received the glorious crown of martyrdom.

There is nothing else of him on the page: no family, no trade, no trial beyond the single charge, no burial and no hymn. The couplet it prints turns his city into a tree and his contest into its branches: «Τῆς Θετταλῶν γῆς Ἀλέξανδρος φὺς κλάδος, / Ἀθλήσεως ἥπλωσεν ἐκτμηθεὶς κλάδους» — Alexander, a branch grown of the Thessalian land, being cut off spread out the branches of his contest.

The archbishop Alexander of Thessalonica whom the same calendar keeps on 28 May is another man and another entry.
