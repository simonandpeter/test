# Ieria

saint.gr begins with what is missing: for those who study the life of Saint Ieria, or Iereia, there is no synaxarion memoir. From the Synaxarion of the Church of Constantinople, of the tenth century, down to the modern editions of the synaxarists, the page says, we know that she was perfected in peace and that her memory is appointed to be honoured on 3 June each year. The only surviving reference to her person is the Menologion's couplet: being a priestess of the party of the wise, you did not remain outside the Lord's bridal chamber.

What else the page has it takes from the life of the venerable virgin martyr Febronia, kept on 25 June. From that life it draws this: at Nisibis of Mesopotamia, in the reign of Diocletian, there was a cenobitic convent of fifty nuns under the abbess [Bryaine](/saints/bryaine-the-venerable), and there her niece Febronia, beautiful and virtuous and then twenty years old, lived as an ascetic. Ieria, a young woman of noble family and a pagan, once visited the convent and asked to speak with the famous Febronia. The conversation bred such compunction in Ieria's soul that she stayed awake the whole night pouring out rivers of tears; and going home she told her household what Febronia was, and proposed that the family should follow Christianity together.

When the Roman eparch Selenos fell upon Bryaine's convent the nuns were scattered, and only Febronia and Thomais stayed with her. Febronia's immovable mind on the first principles of Christianity and of monasticism was the cause of her being led to dreadful tortures. Ieria was present at that moment and watched Febronia's bloody passion from close by; her sensitive heart could not bear it, and she protested with cries and defended the saint. She struggled in vain: Selenos, with greater cruelty, ordered the martyr's body cut limb from limb and at the last beheaded.

Selenos's nephew Lysimachos carried the relics to the convent, where the abbess and the nuns, returned now to their asceticism, received them with lamentation. The unjust martyrdom of Febronia made Ieria's faith a giant, and she hurried to be baptised with her household, and then was tonsured a nun in Bryaine's convent, where she too was perfected in peace, beside the wonderworking relics of the dearly loved sister and spiritual guide who had brought her there.

The page then sets out where her name survives in the hymns: in the Triodion, at the matins of Cheesefare Saturday, in the seventh ode of the canon of the holy fathers, where the venerable women are named together — Bryaine who bore Christ, with the divine Febronia, and Thomais, and Ieria, and Platonis, and Melanthia with them; and in the service of all the venerable women composed by the elder Gerasimos Mikragiannanites in 1968, approved by the Holy Synod of the Church of Greece on 7 November of that year, whose own synaxarion entry reads: on this day, the memory of our venerable mother Ieria — a living sacrifice you were brought to God, all-revered Ieria, by your asceticism.
