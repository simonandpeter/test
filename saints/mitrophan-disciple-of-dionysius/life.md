# Mitrophan, disciple of Dionysius the Rhetor

Mitrophan is known from the same short page as his teacher, and doxologia says of both of them that it is not known when or where they were born. He was living at the Stoudios monastery in Constantinople when [Dionysius the Rhetor](/saints/dionysius-the-rhetor) was tonsured there, and the two left together for the Holy Mountain, seeking a better spiritual life: first a cell near Karyes, given to prayer, asceticism and watchfulness, and then, for more stillness, a cave in the rocky desert of Little Saint Anne.

With his elder's blessing Mitrophan went out into the region of Chalkidiki and heard the confessions of the Christians there regularly, guiding them in Jesus Christ; and when that obedience was finished he returned to the Holy Mountain. From one of those villages, Isvoros, he brought back a story he had heard from a grieving man whose three children had all died: the Lord, to comfort him, had taken his spirit from him in a vision and shown him his children at rest and rejoicing in a place of light, and when he came to himself he ate, and told it gladly to those with him.

That account is kept to this day in the manuscript called the Kouvaras, in the library of the Saint Anne skete, together with the writings of Dionysius. Mitrophan departed to eternity not long after his elder, who died on 6 October 1606; the page gives no year for his own death, and the two are honoured together on 9 July.
