# Sergius (Mechev)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 24 декабря ст. ст. as «Сщмч. Сергия пресвитера (1942)», and its life page is long where most of the lines of these years have none at all. He was born in Moscow on 17 September 1892, the son of [Alexis (Mechev) of Moscow](/saints/alexis-mechev), rector of the church of St Nicholas in Klenniki on the Maroseyka, who wished to have his son for his successor but would not press him and let him take a secular education instead.

He left the gymnasium in 1910 with a silver medal, began medicine at Moscow University and moved to the historical and philological faculty, which he finished in 1917. In 1914 he served as a medical orderly on a hospital train at the Western front, and met there the woman he married, who was nursing too. The decision to take orders came of a journey to Optina in 1918, where two elders blessed it, and he was ordained deacon and then priest in April 1919 in the Danilov monastery.

He served beside his father on the Maroseyka, a parish people called a monastery in the world — not for walls but for a congregation bound as a family by love and one spiritual guidance. He was an uncompromising man: after the Declaration of 1927 he would not commemorate the metropolitan who had issued it and would not accept the church's prayer for the godless. He was arrested and exiled in 1929, the Maroseyka church was closed in 1932, and a second arrest in 1933 brought five years of camps — a sawmill on Lake Kubenskoye, the river Sheleksa, Ust-Pinega, the Svir camps, and a dam near Rybinsk.

On the Sheleksa he went very hungry and was robbed constantly by the criminals; his spiritual daughters, visiting, saw that for all his wasting every trace of irritability had gone out of him and there was not one reproach. Freed in 1937, he settled near Kalinin, worked in a clinic and served the Liturgy secretly at home. A bishop to whom he opened the position of his community betrayed him at the trial; he refused to hide in Central Asia rather than leave his spiritual children, and wandered a year without papers. In the summer of 1941 villagers near Tutayev took him for a German spy and gave him up. He was shot in the Yaroslavl prison in the night of 24 December 1941 old style, the 6th of January 1942 in the new, after four months of interrogation under which, the page says, he bore himself with great courage and worked so that nobody from his community should suffer.
