# Protasius of Milan

Gervasius and Protasius were brothers of Milan. Their father, the page says, taught them the true faith in his own house and gave up his spirit into God’s hands three days later; and when the brothers were left orphaned they sold the house, divided all their property among the poor and set their slaves free. Then they shut themselves up alone in a house and laboured there ten years in fasting, in prayer and in reading; and in the eleventh year they began to suffer for Christ in prison, under Anulinus the governor.

While they lay in prison [Nazarius](/saints/nazarius-of-milan), who had come from Rome to Milan and spent his inheritance on the poor and on the confessors, went often to comfort them, and loved them greatly, for he saw them full of the divine love and ready at any moment to give their souls for the Lord; he wished to suffer and to die together with them, and for visiting them he was himself beaten and driven out of the city. Long afterwards he and his disciple [Celsus](/saints/celsus-disciple-of-nazarius) came back and found the two brothers still alive in that prison.

Ambrose, bishop of Milan, wrote to the brethren throughout Italy how the relics were found. In the forty days of the fast, being at prayer one night, he saw in a kind of sleep two young men in white raiment lifting up their hands in prayer, and when he woke they were gone. He fasted and prayed that if it were a deceit it might be driven off and if it were true it might be shown more plainly; and on the second night, at cockcrow, he saw them again, and on the third a third young man with them who resembled the Apostle Paul, and who told him that these had heard the apostle’s words, left the world and its riches, lived ten years in Milan serving God, and were counted worthy to be martyrs of Christ.

When the count Astasius was going from Milan to the war against the Moravians, the priests of the idols met him on the road and told him that if he wished to return victorious he must force the two prisoners to sacrifice. He ordered [Gervasius](/saints/gervasius-of-milan) to be beaten with rods of lead until he died, and under that beating Gervasius made his end. Having the body carried out, Astasius said to Protasius, „Wretch, spare your life and do not go mad like your brother”.

Protasius answered that he did not know which of them was the wretch, himself who did not fear the count, or the count who feared him; and when Astasius asked how he could be said to fear him, the saint said that he feared him because he would not submit and sacrifice to the idols, for if the count did not fear him he would not press him to it. He himself did not fear the count, despised his threats, reckoned his idols so much refuse, and worshipped only the God who reigns in heaven.

He told the count at the end that he did not revile him: his own Lord had not reviled those who crucified Him but prayed for them, saying that they did not know what they did, and so the count did not know what he did, and the martyr pitied him — but let him finish quickly what he had begun, that he might see his Saviour together with his beloved brother. Then Astasius ordered Protasius to be cut down with the sword.

Philip and his son took the bodies away by night and buried them in their house in a marble coffer, hoping by the martyrs’ prayers to find mercy themselves, as Philip’s own little book relates. When the relics were taken out of the ground many sick were healed, devils were driven out of men and the blind saw; and a blind man of the city named Severus had only to touch the edge of the garments that lay upon them, and his sight at once grew clear. The page names Nero, Anulinus and Astasius, and gives no year, and none is supplied here.

The Greek calendar keeps the four of them on the same day, 14 October, and saint.gr tells how they came together. The parents of Nazarius, Africanus and Perpetua, are said to have been disciples of the Apostle Peter, who drew them to the faith and baptised them; their son was orphaned young in a corrupt society and kept pure and blameless, and at twenty he divided what he had among the poor and set out preaching. At Mediolanum he met two devout men, Protasius and Gervasius, and the three of them together won many by the divine word, from the crowd and from the higher classes alike. Then Nazarius went into Gaul, where among those who believed was a young soul, Celsus. When he returned to Mediolanum he was arrested; Protasius and Gervasius had been taken before him; and the eparch Anoulinos, seeing that they would not change their faith, beheaded them all, together with the young Celsus. The page adds that the relics of Protasius, and the head and the rest of the relics of Gervasius, are at the Roman Catholic Ambrosian Basilica of Milan.

The Russian calendar keeps the four of them on 14 October too, and gives the only date any calendar gives them: the reign of Nero, 54 to 68. He and Gervasius were twins, born at Mediolanum to rich Roman citizens, Vitalis and Valeria, who were themselves tortured to death for the Christian faith. Left orphans, the brothers gave their property away to the poor, set their slaves at liberty and gave themselves to fasting and prayer, and the pagans shut them in prison for confessing Christ. Nazarius found them there and eased what they suffered until he was beaten and driven out of the city for it.

When Nazarius and Celsus came back to Milan and visited the brothers in prison, the visit was reported to Nero, who had the two of them beheaded; the brothers were executed soon after. Many years later, in the reign of the emperor Theodosius, Ambrose, bishop of Mediolanum, uncovered the martyrs' relics by a revelation from above, and they were carried in solemn procession into the cathedral of Milan and became famous for many healings.
