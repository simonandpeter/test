# Zosimas (Trubachev)

The one life the 1938 line carries is his. He was born on 24 December 1893 at Puchuga on the Northern Dvina, in the Solvychegodsk district of the Vologda province, the son of a village deacon, and of four brothers he alone went on from the church school at Nikolsk to the Vologda seminary and from there, in 1914, to the Moscow Theological Academy. Kindness, responsiveness and a love of the church and its services were what the page says marked his calling out early.

He had an ear and a singing voice, learned to conduct a choir while still at seminary and stood in for the precentor; at the Academy he was one of its best precentors and directed the second academic choir. His years there, 1914 to 1918, were spent under the guidance of confessors of the Trinity-Sergius Lavra and of the Smolensk Zosimova hermitage, and his teachers' friendship fixed his purpose. A year before finishing he married Klavdia Sankova, a daughter of Sergiev Posad. In 1918 he wrote his candidate's dissertation on the cosmic element in worship, and in April of that year Patriarch Tikhon ordained him priest in the Trinity cathedral of the Lavra.

His first years of priesthood were at Podosinovets in the Veliky Ustyug diocese, as rector of the church of the Nativity of the Mother of God: he visited the sick in the farthest villages of the parish, held Sunday conversations with the people, took in a drowned fisherman's orphaned daughter, and nearly died of typhus in 1921. In 1922 the campaign to seize church valuables brought him his first arrest. Later, at Ivanovo, he restored a church and led the last Paschal procession the Soviet years allowed there; in 1928 he was arrested again and exiled three years to the Vologda province, then sent to a labour camp on the lumber works. Permitted in 1932 to live at Yuryev-Polsky but not to serve, he worked on a state farm and directed the singing in the church of the Unmercenaries.

In 1934 the exile ended and he was given the Kazan cathedral at Maloyaroslavets, as rector and dean. He was arrested there for the fourth and last time on 26 January 1938, charged with counter-revolutionary activity that consisted of collections taken in his church for the children of a recently arrested bishop. He admitted none of it. A troika of the NKVD for the Moscow region sentenced him to be shot on 19 February, and on 13 (26) February 1938 he was shot and buried in the common grave pit of the Butovo range outside Moscow. The Jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000 numbered him among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for veneration throughout the church.
