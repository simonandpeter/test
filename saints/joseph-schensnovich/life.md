# Joseph (Schensnovich)

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 9 ноября ст. ст. carries eleven names on one line and he is the tenth of them: «Сщмчч. Парфения , еп. Ананьевского, Константина , Димитрия , Нестора , Феодора , Константина , Виктора , Илии , Павла пресвитеров, Иосифа диакона и прмч. Алексия (1937).» One year stands for all eleven, «Сщмчч.» heads the ten clergy and «прмч.» the monk who closes the line, and the grades it prints sort them into a bishop, eight presbyters, a deacon and a hieromonk. The name link behind his place supplies the calendar's own nominative with the surname the line leaves out, «Иосиф (Сченснович), диакон, сщмч.», and that is where the name form in this folder is read from.

**days.pravoslavie.ru gives this company nothing but that line.** Each of the eleven name pages carries a title, a list of days and no text at all, and not one of them has a `/Life/` link of any kind; the day page's hymns are the two martyrs' of 284–305, the venerable Matrona's, the Mother of God's before the Скоропослушница icon and the wonderworker of Aegina's, and nothing is sung under any of these eleven names. The life below is azbyka.ru's, read in the in-app browser because that host answers no fetcher, and the paragraphs are this corpus's own.

He was born on 7 June 1886 at Zhirovitsy in the Slonim country of the Grodno province, the son of a Belarusian peasant, and his family was deeply Orthodox. He finished the Zhirovitsy theological school in 1901 and a psalmists' course at Grodno in 1903, and was appointed psalmist to the church at Veliky Les in the Brest country, where he served until 1915. The war forced him out on 30 August 1915, and at the beginning of 1916 he reached Moscow, where he took a psalmist's place at the church of St Sergius in the Rogozhskaya Sloboda and worked besides in a railway freight office.

In 1918 he left Moscow for the Voronezh country and then for the Kharkov country, where he served as psalmist; in 1921 he finished a pastoral course at Starobelsk, and in 1922 he was psalmist at a village whose name was Счастье, happiness. In 1924 he was ordained deacon to the church at Vysokoye in the Kharkov region and served there five years. In 1929 he was deacon at the church of Peter and Paul by the Yauza gates in Moscow, and from 1930 at the Trinity church in the settlement of Kupavna in the Noginsk district, where that year he was given the double orarion for blameless service.

He and his wife had three children, the youngest newly born. Relatives of his had been left on land that had gone to Poland, and they wrote that they lived tolerably and invited him to come; in 1933 he took his papers to the Polish embassy meaning to go to his brothers, and nothing came of it. On 5 November 1937 the heads of the Moscow regional NKVD wrote out a warrant for his arrest, saying that a group of churchmen at Kupavna was carrying on counter-revolutionary agitation among the people «внедряя в сознание масс веру в сверхъестественные силы».

The warrant charged him by name with visiting the lodgings of certain working women with a counter-revolutionary purpose, propagandising them in a counter-revolutionary church spirit and giving some of them material help, so that one woman who had not gone to church before had become an active churchwoman. Witnesses were questioned on 10 November and one of them said that the deacon compelled his children to go to church and that the family was set extremely religiously under its head's influence.

On 12 November 1937 the authorities arrested the rector of the Trinity church, this deacon and a member of the parish council of twenty, and shut them in the prison at Noginsk. He was questioned the next day. Told that the investigation knew he had carried on counter-revolutionary agitation, he answered that he had spread no provocative rumours and that what he had said of the Soviet Union, which had moved him to ask the Polish embassy for leave to go, was his own feeling and his family's. He said he knew nothing of the other two beyond that one was the rector and lived in the same house and the other went to church, and he did not admit the charge.

On 19 November a troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on 21 November 1937 and buried in an unmarked common grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow. **The calendar keeps him on 9 ноября ст. ст., which is the civil 22 November, and his life has him shot on 21 November 1937** — the day before, 8 ноября old style. The difference is one day and not the thirteen of the style conversion, so it is neither a second feast nor an arithmetic slip that could be read away: five of the eleven died on the 22nd and six on the 21st, and the calendar holds all eleven on the one day. The attestation's day is the day page that listed him, as the brief requires, and the death above is the date his own life gives.

**He is given no relation to the ten others the line names**, for the reason the company of Semipalatinsk makes plain by contrast: those four are tied to one another by their own pages, and nothing on any page read ties this deacon to the bishop, to the four of Semipalatinsk or to the five other men of this line who died at Butovo on the same day he did. The two men arrested with him at Kupavna, who are the only companions his own page gives him, have no folder in this corpus and are named here by their offices rather than taken into it.
