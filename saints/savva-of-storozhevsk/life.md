# Savva of Storozhevsk

The Russian calendar keeps 10 August (Julian) for the second finding and translation of his relics, in 1998; his life is printed with it.

Savva of Storozhevsk and Zvenigorod left the world in early youth and was tonsured by Sergius of Radonezh, one of his first disciples and companions — a lover of silence who avoided conversation, lived in constant labour and in weeping for the poverty of his soul, and was so deep in spiritual wisdom that while still at Sergius’s monastery he was confessor to the whole brotherhood. When the Grand Prince Dimitri Donskoy, in thanks for the victory over Mamai, built a monastery of the Dormition on the river Dubenka, Savva was its igumen by Sergius’s blessing; in 1392, when Nikon withdrew into silence, the brethren of the Lavra begged him to take its igumenate, and tradition puts the bringing-forth of the spring outside the Lavra walls in his time. Prince Yuri Dimitrievich of Zvenigorod, Sergius’s godson, chose him as his confessor and begged him to found a monastery near Zvenigorod “at the place called Storozhi”, the watch-hill that had guarded Moscow; there Savva raised a small wooden church of the Nativity of the Theotokos and a cell, and in 1399 a monastery, digging its well himself and carrying the water on his shoulders. He blessed the prince on campaign that year and foretold his victory. He died in great old age on 3 December 1406; his veneration began at once, a charter of 1539 calls him wonderworker, and the feast was set by the Moscow council of 1547. In the 1480s he appeared to the igumen Dionysius and told him to paint his likeness, which an old monk who had seen him in youth confirmed; Tsar Alexis Mikhailovich, whom tradition says Savva saved from a bear, walked to the monastery on pilgrimage, and the relics were found incorrupt on 19 January 1652.
