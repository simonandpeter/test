# Savva of Storozhevsk

The Russian calendar keeps 10 August (Julian) for the second finding and translation of his relics, in 1998; his life is printed with it.

Savva of Storozhevsk and Zvenigorod left the world in early youth and was tonsured by Sergius of Radonezh, one of his first disciples and companions — a lover of silence who avoided conversation, lived in constant labour and in weeping for the poverty of his soul, and was so deep in spiritual wisdom that while still at Sergius’s monastery he was confessor to the whole brotherhood.

When the Grand Prince Dimitri Donskoy, in thanks for the victory over Mamai, built a monastery of the Dormition on the river Dubenka, Savva was its igumen by Sergius’s blessing; in 1392, when Nikon withdrew into silence, the brethren of the Lavra begged him to take its igumenate, and tradition puts the bringing-forth of the spring outside the Lavra walls in his time.

Prince Yuri Dimitrievich of Zvenigorod, Sergius’s godson, chose him as his confessor and begged him to found a monastery near Zvenigorod “at the place called Storozhi”, the watch-hill that had guarded Moscow; there Savva raised a small wooden church of the Nativity of the Theotokos and a cell, and in 1399 a monastery, digging its well himself and carrying the water on his shoulders. He blessed the prince on campaign that year and foretold his victory. He died in great old age on 3 December 1406; his veneration began at once, a charter of 1539 calls him wonderworker, and the feast was set by the Moscow council of 1547. In the 1480s he appeared to the igumen Dionysius and told him to paint his likeness, which an old monk who had seen him in youth confirmed; Tsar Alexis Mikhailovich, whom tradition says Savva saved from a bear, walked to the monastery on pilgrimage, and the relics were found incorrupt on 19 January 1652.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 3 December, which the Russian calendar had already named as his principal feast while printing his life with the translation of his relics in August. Its whole entry is four lines, and two of them are about what happened after his death: a disciple of Saint Sergius of Radonezh and a great wonderworker, who appeared to many as though alive once he had died, now teaching them, now rebuking them, and now healing them. It puts his passing from this life to a better one in 1406, which is the year this folder's own life gave off the Russian calendar and a year earlier than the one its dates had carried.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the Julian 3 December, which is the day of his repose, and marks his service with the typikon’s sign for a great doxology — the only saint of that day so marked. It gives him the fullest life this folder has. In early youth he left the world and was tonsured by the abbot of Radonezh, whose first disciples and fellow labourers he was among. He loved a silent life, avoided conversation with people and lived in constant work, weeping over the poverty of his soul and remembering the judgement of God; he was to everyone a pattern of simplicity and humility, and had gained such depth of spiritual wisdom that already in his teacher’s monastery he was confessor to the whole brotherhood, an elder honoured and very apt to teach.

When the grand prince, in thanksgiving for a victory, founded a house of the Dormition on the river Dubenka, Savva became its abbot by his teacher’s blessing, keeping the simplicity of his ascetic life: he ate only plant food, wore rough clothing and slept on the floor. In 1392 the brethren of the Lavra, their abbot Nikon having withdrawn into silence, begged him to take the abbacy there; tradition sets the bringing out of a spring of water beyond the Lavra’s walls in his time.

The prince of Zvenigorod, his teacher’s godson, held him in great love and reverence, chose him as confessor, and begged him to stay and found a new house near Zvenigorod at a place called Storozhi, the watch. Seeking a solitary and silent life he accepted, and before an icon of the Mother of God asked her protection for the wild place with tears. On Storozhevsk hill, where a guard had once watched for enemies of Moscow, he built a small wooden church of the Nativity of the Mother of God in 1377 and a little cell for himself not far from it, and in 1399 he set up a monastery there, receiving with love all who sought a silent life.

He laboured much at it. He dug the well under the hill himself and carried the water up on his shoulders, ringed the monastery with a wooden fence, and a verst away, in a ravine, dug himself a cell for silence. In 1399 he blessed the prince as he went out to war and foretold his victory, and by the old man’s prayers the victory came quickly; a stone cathedral church of the Nativity was built by his labours. He died in deep old age on 3 December, in 1406 by this calendar and in 1407 by the reading this folder carried before.

His veneration by the local people began immediately after his death: the healing power flowing from his tomb and his many appearances convinced everybody, and a charter of 1539 already calls him a wonderworker. The sixteenth-century life tells how, about 1480 to 1490, an elder appeared after the evening office to Dionysius, abbot of the house, and said, Dionysius, get up and paint my face on an icon; asked who he was, he answered, I am Savva, the founder of this place. An aged monk of the house who had seen the saint in his own youth described his appearance, and it was exactly as he had appeared to Dionysius, who did as he was told and painted the icon. His commemoration was established at the Moscow council of 1547, and on 19 January 1652 his relics were found incorrupt.

This folder’s `russian` row stands on 10 августа, the second finding and translation of those relics in 1998, because that is the day the corpus read first and a venerated row’s day may not be moved. His name page gives three days — 19 января for the first finding, 10 августа for the second, and 3 декабря — and 3 декабря is the principal one. He therefore does not render on the Russian 3 December, and that is recorded here rather than quietly corrected.
