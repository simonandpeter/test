# Irene (16 April)

saint.gr gives her four sentences and sets her beside a company it lists separately. This saint was martyred at the same time and in the same place as [Leonides](/saints/leonides-of-epidaurus) and the holy women with him, the page says, referring the reader to its own 16 April. Irene was labouring for the enlightening of other women, but she was betrayed to the ruler, arrested and put in prison. Afterwards they cut out her tongue and drew her teeth; and because she stood by her faith, they beheaded her in the end.

Its couplet plays on her name, which is the word for peace: «Εἰρηνικῶς ζήσασα, Μάρτυς Εἰρήνη, Οὐκ εἰρηνικῶς, ἀλλ' ἐκ τοῦ ξίφους θνῄσκεις» — having lived peaceably, martyr Irene, you die not peaceably but by the sword. The page names no city, no ruler and no year, and it prints no hymn; and it is not the Irene the same calendar keeps on this day with her sisters Agape and Chionia.

**The Russian calendar gives her a year, a place and a manner of life, where the Greek gave her four sentences and no year at all.** She lived in Greece together with other Christians in a cave, both of the readings it serves say, spending her time in unceasing prayer. Pagans denounced her, the governor's soldiers seized her and she was shut in prison; and for confessing Christ as the true God without fear she was cruelly tortured — her tongue was cut out, her teeth were knocked out, and at the last her head was struck off with the sword. She suffered at Corinth in the year 258, on the day of holy Pascha.

That settles what the Greek page left open. Its notice said only that she was martyred at the same time and in the same place as the company of this day and then told the tongue, the teeth and the sword; this calendar prints her inside the company's own line, between the seventh woman and «и иных», so the source itself puts her in the company rather than beside it. It also gives her the day: the others went to the sea the day before Pascha, and she was beheaded on Pascha itself.
