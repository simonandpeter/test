# Helen, mother of Constantine

The calendar keeps her with her son on the same day, and the life it prints is one life for the two of them. Helen was the mother of [Constantine the Great](/saints/constantine-the-great), whom she bore to Constas, the emperor in Britain; of her, the page says, Constantine alone was born, and he was the heir of his father's rule. She was living with him in Britain when the Romans sent secretly to beg him to come against Maxentius, and the page says nothing of her own beginnings, her country or her years.

What it tells at length is her journey. When Constantine had built his city and called the council at Nicaea, he sent his mother to Jerusalem with much wealth, as one who loved God greatly, to search for the honourable Cross. Wishing to find the life-giving Cross of the Lord, which the Jews had hidden, she called them all together and questioned them; and at the prayer of the patriarch Macarius a sweet fragrance came out of the place, and the Tomb and the place of the Skull, Golgotha, were shown to the east.

Three crosses were found there, and Macarius had them laid one by one on a dead man being carried past; when the Cross of Christ was laid on him the dead man at once rose. Then Macarius, standing on a high place, lifted the honourable Cross and showed it to the people, who cried out, Lord have mercy; and from that the feast of the Exaltation is kept. The Jew Judas who had shown her the place believed with a multitude of Jews and was baptised with the name Cyriacus, and was afterwards patriarch of Jerusalem and was tortured for Christ under Julian the Apostate.

Helen took with her a part of the holy wood and the holy nails, and put the rest in a silver casket and gave it to the patriarch Macarius to keep for the generations to come. She ordered churches to be built in Jerusalem at the holy places: first the church of the Resurrection, beside the Holy Sepulchre where the Cross had been found, then a church at Gethsemane, where the tomb of the Most Holy Mother of God is, of her honourable Dormition, and eighteen others besides.

When she had adorned them all and endowed them richly, she came to Constantinople, bringing a part of the wood of the life-giving Cross and the holy nails with which the body of Christ was fixed. Not long afterwards, the page says, she passed to God, having pleased Him, and was buried with honour; her son lived ten years and more after her. It gives her no year, either for her birth or for her death.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day as her son and adds a few plain things about her. saint.gr makes her the daughter of an innkeeper of Drepanum in Bithynia and the wife of Constantius Chlorus. It says that a year after the finding of the Honourable Cross, on her way back to Constantinople, she passed through Cyprus, and it names the Cypriot chronicler Leontios Machairas for that. It puts her falling asleep in peace at 327 — «μάλλον», probably, is the page's own word — at the age of eighty, and reports out of Eusebius that she foresaw her death and left her property by will to her son and her grandchildren. Her son carried her honourable relic to Constantinople and buried it in the church of the Holy Apostles, and the page adds that their common synaxis was kept at the Great Church, at the church of the Holy Apostles and at their own church at the cistern of Bonus.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 21 May in one entry with her son, and gives her four sentences of her own. She was the mother of [Constantine](/saints/constantine-the-great) by the emperor Constantius Flor, and his only child by her, though Flor had other children by another wife. The devout mother of the emperor, it says, was full of zeal for the faith of Christ: she visited Jerusalem and found the Honourable Cross of the Lord, and built the church of the Resurrection on Golgotha and many other churches besides throughout the Holy Land.

In her eightieth year this holy woman departed to the Lord, in 327. The book gives her that year and gives her son none, saying only that he outlived her by ten years and reposed in his sixty-fifth year at Nicomedia; nothing is reckoned here from either number.

The Russian church calendar keeps her on 21 мая ст. ст. with her son and gives her two readings, one of her own and one written for the two of them together. It reckons two services to her: that she disposed her son Constantine towards the Christian faith at a time when other youths of noble birth were brought up in the pagan spirit and despised Christianity, and the raising of the Cross of the Lord.

To find the Cross she went to Jerusalem in 326. She was shown that it had been buried in the ground at the place where the pagans had raised a temple in honour of Venus; and when at her order the building was pulled down and they began to dig, they found three crosses and beside them a board with the inscription «Иисус Назарянин, Царь Иудейский» — Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews. To learn on which of the three the Saviour had been crucified they laid them in turn upon a dead man: from two of them no wonder came, but when the third was laid on him the dead man rose, and so the Saviour’s cross was known. When the people heard of the wonder they all wished to see the holy Cross; and the patriarch Макарий of Jerusalem and the empress stood on a high place and set it up — raised it — and the people, seeing the Cross of the Lord, prayed with the words «Господи помилуй!» In memory of that the Church appointed the feast of the Exaltation of the Cross.

The other reading adds that it was the emperor who sent her to Jerusalem for this, with wide powers and great means, and that she searched together with the Patriarch; that she had all the places bound up with the earthly life of the Lord and of his most pure Mother cleared of every trace of paganism and churches raised at them; that she gave the life-giving Cross into the Patriarch’s keeping and took a part of it with her for the emperor; and that having given generous alms at Jerusalem and set out tables for the poor, at which she served herself, she returned to Constantinople and soon afterwards died, in 327. Her own page lists the churches she built in the Holy Land: the Resurrection and the Sepulchre on Golgotha, one on the Mount of Olives where the Lord ascended, one at Bethlehem where he was born in the flesh, and one at Hebron by the oak of Mamre, «где Бог явился Аврааму». For her great services to the Church and her labour in finding the life-giving Cross she is named Equal to the Apostles.
