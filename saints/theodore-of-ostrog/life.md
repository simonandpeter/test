# Theodore, of the Kyiv Caves

Theodore, prince of Ostrog, won his fame by building churches and by defending Orthodoxy in Volhynia against the pressure of Rome. He descended from the equal-to-the-apostles Prince Vladimir through his great-grandson Svyatopolk-Michael, prince of Turov and grand prince of Kyiv. His name first appears in 1386, when King Jagiello of Poland and Vytautas of Lithuania confirmed to him the hereditary district of Ostrog and added Zaslav and Korets; in 1410 he fought in the rout of the Teutonic knights at Grunwald; in 1422, out of sympathy for the Orthodox of Bohemia, he supported the Hussites against the emperor Sigismund, bringing their wagon-fort tactics into the warfare of the region, later the Cossacks’. In 1432, after victories over Polish forces, he made Jagiello protect by law the freedom of Orthodoxy in Volhynia. Prince Švitrigaila, fearing his growing strength, imprisoned him in 1435; the people, who loved him, rose, and he was freed — and made peace with his offender and helped him against the Lithuanian–Polish party. He fought the Tatars in 1438, and in 1440, under King Casimir, held Volodymyr, Dubno and Ostrog as governor, with vast estates in the best parts of Podolia and Volhynia.

All this, with a prince’s power and glory, he left after 1441 and entered the Kyiv Caves monastery, where he was tonsured with the name Theodosius and laboured for his salvation until his death; the year is unknown, but certainly in the second half of the fifteenth century, in deep old age — Solovyov reckoned 1483. He is buried in the Far Caves of Theodosius, and his glorification was evidently by the end of the sixteenth century, for in 1638 the hieromonk Athanasius Kalnofoysky wrote that the venerable Theodore rests openly in the cave of Theodosius, whole in body.
