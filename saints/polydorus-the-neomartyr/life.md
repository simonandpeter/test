# Polydorus of Nicosia

Polydorus, a Cypriot of Nicosia, was raised by devout parents, Loukas and
Lourdanou, learned letters and travelled to Egypt and other lands as a
merchant. In Egypt in 1793 he was taken on as secretary by a Zakynthian
who had renounced Christ, and at a carousal with him got drunk and denied
Christ; coming to himself he repented bitterly, went to Beirut and
confessed to the bishop there, withdrew to a monastery on Mount Lebanon,
where he rested through Great Lent, and then went to Ptolemais — Acre —
where he told the bishop of his longing to be a martyr. At the bishop’s
urging he left for Egypt, but a storm at sea put him ashore at Jaffa; from
there he went to Chios, on 13 June 1793, then to Smyrna, and back again to
Chios, and at last reached New Ephesus, where before the mufti he
confessed Christ and after savage tortures was hanged. It was a Sunday,
3 September 1794. The new martyr’s body was buried near the graves of the
Armenians, and today his skull is kept in the church of St Catherine at
Plaka in Athens. The synaxarion’s verse says God gave him to us, a gift
both great and new, thrice-blessed Polydorus.
