# Nikon the Dry

saint.gr's calendar for 11 December names the venerable Nikon the Dry, he who is in the Cave, and then says in one sentence that it has no details of the Russian venerable's life. The one other thing it prints is where his body is: the relic of the saint lies incorrupt in the Lavra of the Caves at Kyiv. No century, no parentage, no manner of death, no hymn and no synaxaristic couplet is given.

What the entry says is in the line itself and in that note, and it is set down here as they have it: a Russian ascetic whom the Greek calendar calls the Dry, places in the Cave, and whose incorrupt relic it puts at Kyiv. The same calendar keeps another Nikon of those caves on 23 March, the abbot, whom the corpus already holds; that is another entry and nothing here is claimed from it.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, dates his repose to it — 11 December 1101, the first year any source has given this folder — and where the Greek line gave two by-names it gives the whole story. As a monk in the caves of Kyiv he was taken captive by the Tatars, and spent three years in slavery, chained, tortured and mocked. When his kinsmen brought money to buy him from his master he refused it, saying that if the Lord had wanted him to be free he would not have given him into the hands of these lawless men.

Once he told his master that Christ would set him free within three days. The Tatar thought the slave meant to run, and cut the tendons under his knees; but on the third day he was indeed carried to Kyiv in an instant by an invisible hand. After some time that same Tatar came to Kyiv, recognised in him his former slave, repented and was baptised; and the former master became the novice and disciple of his own former slave. He was called the Dry, the book says, for the great emaciation of his body, and he saw far into what was hidden.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps him on this menologion day and prints the account of him this folder had in no form. Nikon the Dry was the son of rich and notable parents and left everything for Christ’s sake, taking the monastic habit at the Kyiv Caves monastery. In 1096, when the khan Bonyak invaded, he was taken captive along with other monks. Expecting a rich ransom for him, the Polovtsian who held him treated him cruelly; and when he refused to be ransomed, his owner began to torment him with hunger, leaving him out in the heat in summer and in the frost in winter.

But he gave thanks to God for everything, and said once to his tormentor that the Lord would bring him back to the monastery by the prayers of its two founders, as a monk who had appeared to him had foretold. The Polovtsian cut the tendons of his legs and set a strengthened guard on him. But on the third day, at the sixth hour, the prisoner suddenly became invisible, and the guards heard only the words «Praise the Lord from the heavens». So he was carried to the church of the Dormition, in time for the divine liturgy; and the brethren crowded round and began asking how he had got there. He wanted to hide the wonder, but they begged him to tell the truth.

He wanted to go on with his labours in the monastery without taking off his fetters, but the abbot said: if the Lord had wished you to stay bound, he would not have brought you out of captivity. Long afterwards his former owner came to the Kyiv Caves monastery and recognised his old prisoner, dried up with hunger and with wounds; and he believed, was baptised, took the habit, and became Nikon’s own novice. Nikon died at the beginning of the twelfth century and was buried in the Near Caves.
