# Nicanor the Deacon

In the days of the holy apostles, after the Lord's Ascension and the receiving of the Holy Spirit, while they had not yet scattered through the whole world to preach Christ and the churches of the faithful in Jerusalem had grown many, seven men full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom were chosen for the service of the diaconate: Stephen, Philip, [Prochorus](/saints/prochorus-the-deacon), Nicanor, [Timon](/saints/timon-the-deacon), [Parmenas](/saints/parmenas-the-deacon) and Nicolas of Antioch. The apostles prayed, laid their hands on them and made them deacons.

Of these, the memory of the first martyr and archdeacon Stephen is kept on 27 December and that of Philip on 11 October; the rest — except Nicolas, who is not written in the number of the saints — the holy Church has been accustomed to keep on this day, although they ended by suffering at different times, preaching Christ apostolically in various places.

On the same day on which the first martyr and archdeacon Stephen was stoned, as it is written in the Acts of the Apostles, there was a great persecution against the Church of Jerusalem, and Nicanor was killed too, with two thousand people who had believed in Christ. The manner of his killing is remembered in the canon: being slaughtered by the Jews like a sheep, he was sacrificed to Christ.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 28 July, and keeps the four deacons together. saint.gr says Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon and Parmenas — apostles of the Seventy — were among the seven chosen deacons of the first Christian Church of Jerusalem, in Acts 6:5, and that their work was to serve and to see to the feeding of the poor members of the Church, the orphans and the widows above all, while they served in the spreading of the divine word as well. Of Nicanor it says that he and Parmenas died at Jerusalem, carrying out their work as deacons, and that they were buried by the Apostles themselves under the mourning of the whole Church, which they had served with so much zeal and success; the Greek page does not say that he was killed. Above the notice it sets a distich for each man, and his own runs: «Tὸ δίκτυόν σου Nικάνωρ Θεοῦ Λόγῳ, Πολλοὺς ἀγρεῦσαν ἐρρύη τέλει βίου» — your net, Nicanor, which caught many for the Word of God, slipped away at the end of your life.
