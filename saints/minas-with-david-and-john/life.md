# Minas, with David and John

saint.gr's whole account of the three is one sentence: «Οι Άγιοι Οσιομάρτυρες Μηνάς, Δαβίδ και Ιωάννης ήταν μοναχοί και τελειώθηκαν τοξευόμενοι» — the holy venerable-martyrs Minas, [David](/saints/david-with-minas-and-john) and [John](/saints/john-with-minas-and-david) were monks and were made perfect by being shot with arrows. The day's line adds nothing but the rank word, οι Οσιομάρτυρες.

Above it the page prints a couplet which is the only other thing said of them: three servants of God, abbas, slayers of the passions, are killed with arrows by slayers of men. Under each of the three names it sets a gloss on the name — Μηνάς from μήνη, the moon, the one bright in the dark as the moon is; Δαβίδ a Hebrew word, the beloved, loving and loved; Ἰωάννης the grace and favour of God towards men — and those glosses belong to the names and not to the men.

Nothing else is told. The page gives no century, no country, no monastery, no persecutor and no year; it does not say whether the three were of one house or only of one death, and it lists no relic and prints no hymn. Because the line names the three and enumerates them, this is one folder for each, and each says only this much.

**The Russian calendar keeps the three on the same menologion day, in one line, and gives them a country, a people and a date the Greek page did not.** Its line is «Прмчч. Мины , Давида и Иоанна (после 636).» — after 636 — and the one life page behind all three names says they ascetised in Palestine and in the seventh century received a martyr's end from the Arabs, who shot them through with arrows; and it explains the date in its own parenthesis, «после 636 года, когда Иерусалим был взят арабами», after the year 636, when Jerusalem was taken by the Arabs.

That is the whole of it, and it is four facts more than this folder held: the province, the manner of death, the persecutors, and a year that is a terminus and not a point. The folder stood undated, and the reading recorded is the one the page states — not earlier than 636 — with the upper bound left at the end of the century the page names, since it says the seventh and nothing narrower.

The name link behind this man's place in the line gives «Мина Палестинский, прмч.», with the Palestinian epithet, which is the first form of his name any calendar this corpus reads has printed beyond the bare Greek forename, and it is stored for that reason. The page names the three together in its heading and tells of them together in its one sentence, and nothing in it distinguishes one from another.
