# James the Persian

James was of the country of the Persians, from the city called Elani, born of Christian parents and brought up in the true faith. He took a Christian wife and lived in much wealth and honour, for he was loved by the emperor of the Persians, Isdegerd, who set him over the imperial palaces. But it happened that he was deceived by the many favours and imperial honours and fell from the faith, so that he sacrificed to the idols and worshipped them together with the emperor.

Hearing of it, his mother and his wife wrote him a letter and sent it in haste: *Wretched man, why have you left God, the King of heaven, for human honour? Doing the pleasure of this passing emperor you have lost the life that does not die; serving this corruptible king you have denied the incorruptible one. You have exchanged truth for a lie, leaving the faith of Christ and taking the devil's deceit.*

*Submitting to a mortal emperor you have overlooked the Judge of the living and the dead. For a man's love you have made yourself unworthy of God's love and fallen from the good hope. We weep and lament for you, that being a son of light you have now become a son of darkness and of hell. And know that if you do not turn to God you have no part with us, for we do not wish to see you again while you worship idols; and when God's wrath overtakes the emperor and his men, it will overtake you with them. So we beg you with tears: turn to Christ.*

The letter was given to James while he was with the emperor outside the city. Reading it he was pricked to the heart, and said within himself: *If my mother and my wife make themselves strangers to me, what will the age to come be to me, when God shall come to judge the living and the dead and to reward each according to his works?* Reading it again carefully he wept much and lamented his sin, and resolved to knock without ceasing at the door of God's mercy.

As he wept, some pagans saw him, knew him for a Christian, and went and told the emperor — the son of Isdegerd, who now reigned. He called him at once and questioned him, and when he heard his confession he was very angry: *Is this the thanks you give for the love of mine you have enjoyed until now — that you set yourself against me and speak evil of my father?* James answered that what he boasted he would do he should do quickly, for his words did not frighten him, being like wind beating on a rock.

So the emperor called his counsellors and friends and took counsel with them what torments to destroy him with; and he gave sentence that, to be a terror to others, James should be cut apart limb by limb. The torturers came, stripped him and stretched out his hands and feet, telling him he had no escape now. Some of his friends wept for him and begged him to spare himself and not to throw away his fine youth, to do the emperor's will and worship his gods and live.

But he opened his mouth and said: *Do not weep for me; weep for yourselves and for your children. For I go into everlasting rest, and you will go to perish in everlasting torments.* Then some of the imperial officers began to beat the torturers, asking why they stood looking at him instead of doing what they had been ordered; and one of them took the saint's right hand and cut off a finger.

The officer, pitying him, told him to spare himself and obey the emperor, since they were all sorry for him, and that for the finger already cut off there would be a remedy. But at each finger the saint gave thanks: at the fourth, *You who receive praise from the four living creatures, receive the suffering of the fourth finger, cut off for the confession of your most holy name*; and when they came to the left hand he said that shepherds who shear the right side of a sheep do not leave the left unshorn, and that he was a sheep of the flock of Christ given into their hands to be shorn.

So the executioner cut off the thumb of the left hand, and James thanked the Lord that he was counted worthy to bring him a sixth finger; and at the eighth he said that the Lord himself had received circumcision on the eighth day according to the law, while he endured the cutting of eight fingers, that being parted from lawless men he might be joined to God. The torturers did not stop, cutting his members from morning to the ninth hour; and all that while a sweet fragrance came from his cut body as from a cypress.

When they ceased, the soldier of Christ, having overcome the devil with his pains, cried out to God: *Holy, Holy, Holy are you, God Almighty, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, praised by the cherubim and glorified by the seraphim; look on me, God of the living and the dead, and hear me who am barely alive. All my members are cut off, and my body lies part dead and part still a little living. I have no feet to stand before you, Lord; no hands to lift in prayer to you, Master; no knees on which to fall and worship you, my Maker. My hands, my feet, my shoulders and my thighs are cut off, and I am thrown down before you, All-seeing one, like a fallen house and like a tree without branches. I beg you then, lover of mankind, do not leave me to the end, but bring my soul out of prison.*

As he prayed, one of those standing by took a knife and cut off his holy head; and so, finishing the contest of his suffering, he gave his soul into the hands of God. His honoured body, broken and cast away, lay on the ground until night; and when night came the faithful gathered his members and buried them all together with honour.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 27 November. saint.gr puts him in the fourth century, under the emperor Arcadius, about the year 395, and says he lived at Bethlada of Persia and came of a distinguished family — where the Romanian account named his city Elani. It tells the story more shortly and in the same shape: that it was his mother and his wife, devout and faithful Christians, whose grief and anger and refusal of all dealings with him brought him back to the straight road; that he then went to the king and confessed before him the one true faith in Christ; and that Isdegerd, astonished at the change, tried to talk him out of it before ordering the torture. It adds nothing about the burial.

The Russian calendar tells the same martyrdom and gives it a court and two reigns. days.pravoslavie.ru has him born in the fourth century into a wealthy and well-born Christian family, married to a Christian wife, bringing up their children to prayer and the Scriptures, and holding high office under the Persian king Izdegerd, whose reign it dates 399 to 420, and under his successor Varahran, 420 to 438. On one campaign, caught by the king's favour and afraid to own himself a Christian, he sacrificed to the idols with the king.

His mother and his wife wrote to him in deep grief, reproaching him and calling him to repentance, and the letter did it: he understood the weight of the sin, saw what it was to be renounced not only by his own household but by God, and wept aloud and begged forgiveness. His companions heard him praying to the Lord Jesus Christ and reported it to the king. At the interrogation he confessed his faith in the one true God and no persuasion could shake him, and the king ordered him put to death by torture.

They laid him on a block and cut off his fingers and his toes one after another, and then his hands and his feet; and through the whole of it, the page says, he did not stop offering thanksgiving to the Lord for giving him the chance to buy back in hard pain the sin he had done. Bleeding, he was beheaded. The calendar closes his line with the year 421, which no other calendar read gives him, and prints him a troparion and a kontakion, the kontakion turning on the wife whose letter brought him back and on a body cut like a vine-branch.

The Охридски пролог has him born in the Persian city of Клапа, or Вилат, of Christian parents, brought up in the Christian faith and married to a Christian wife. The Persian emperor Yazdegerd came to love him for his gifts and his quickness and made him a true nobleman at his court; and flattered by the emperor he was led astray and offered sacrifice to the idols the emperor himself worshipped.

When his mother and his wife heard of it they wrote him a letter of reproach in which they mourned him as an apostate from God and a dead soul, begging him nevertheless at the end of it to repent and return to Christ. Moved by that letter he repented bitterly and confessed his faith in Christ the Lord boldly before the emperor. The enraged emperor condemned him to death, but on these terms: that he be cut away part by part until he breathed his last.

The executioners fulfilled their emperor’s command to the letter and cut off first the fingers of his hands, then the toes of his feet, then the feet, then the hands, then the shoulders, and at the last the head; and at every cut the repentant martyr gave thanks to God. Out of his wounds came a fragrance like that of cypress. So this wonderful man paid off his sin, the book says, and went in soul to Christ his God in the kingdom of heaven.

He suffered about the year 400, this book closes, where the Russian calendar gives a flat 421 and this folder holds that year; the two are not an interval and neither has been moved, for about is the book’s own word and the other is a stated year. His head is at Rome, it adds, and part of his relics in Portugal, where he is kept on 22 May — a local celebration where the relics lie, and not one of the four calendars this corpus records, so nothing in this folder’s attestations stands on that day.
