# Parthenius of Koudoumas

saint.gr says that Parthenius and his brother [Eumenius](/saints/eumenius-of-koudoumas) came from Pitsidia of Pyrgiotissa in Crete, the children of Chariton and Maria Charitaki, and that their monastic bent showed itself from early childhood in several wonders they lived through. Parthenius was born in 1829 and was given the worldly name Nicholas; Eumenius was born in 1846 and was Emmanuel in the world. Nicholas loved quiet from his earliest years, turned away from games, and kept every fast day the Church appoints; unlike his younger brother, who was taught by a schoolmaster of the village, he never took to letters.

Their father died in 1856 and the two began preparing to leave for the monastic life. Several wonders came between, the page says. As a small child Nicholas fell into a deep well, and the villagers who ran to pull him out expected to bring up a dead child and found him entirely unhurt. Later his godfather wanted to take him to sea and make a sailor of him, and he was given to know from God that the ship would go down, so he found an excuse and vanished. The third wonder was the one that won their mother's blessing: she told them to light the oven for the bread, and Nicholas did not light it, and to her complaint answered that she should put the loaves in without fire, to see that God wanted her two sons to be monks. The bread baked, and she glorified God.

They set out in 1858 for the monastery of Odigitria. Nicholas was tonsured after four years of probation, on 27 August 1862, and took the name Nestor; Emmanuel was tonsured in 1865 and took the name Methodius. Nestor was sent by the abbot to the cave church of Martsalo, where he worked on the church and built cells and a cistern, and asked the abbot's blessing to have his brother with him. When the rising of 1866 was declared the Turks plundered as far as Martsalo and destroyed even the icons of the church; the brothers made the damage good and asked the abbot Gerasimus to tonsure them into the great schema, and he came and did it, and Nestor was named Parthenius and Methodius Eumenius. Parthenius wore chains next to his skin and a hair cassock even in the summer months.

A new abbot took against the crowds who came to Martsalo, and after much reproach the brothers left the monastery in 1874 and, after four years of wandering, came to the caves of the Asterousia mountains at Koudoumas. There, in the ruins of the long-abandoned monastery of the Mother of God, Parthenius saw and spoke with the Most Holy Theotokos, who charged him: «μεῖνε ἐδῶ νὰ ἱδρύσεις Μονύδριον… καὶ μὴ φοβοῦ διότι Ἐγὼ θὰ εἶμαι οἰκονόμος» — stay here and found a little monastery, and do not be afraid, for I shall be the steward. They had no money at all, and the people of the district kept them; water was found in a marvellous way and a well opened.

The church was finished in 1895, and is called Theoktistos — built by God — because of the wonder the page tells of it: the stone of the place was unworkable, the builders decided to leave the work unfinished, the fathers begged them for one night more and spent it in prayer, and in the morning dressed stones had come up out of the sea. The two slept two hours a night on a grass mat with a stone for a pillow, never ate meat, and had one hair cassock each, which they washed once a year in the sea and washed themselves. Parthenius was severe about the monastic rule and closed his monastery to women, keeping even the novices outside in the caves around it, and Koudoumas became a great spiritual centre.

In 1905 he fell gravely ill, his body already worn out by his ascesis and long troubled in the stomach. The doctor Alexander Papachatzakis gave him medicine and told him to take strengthening food and above all meat; he refused, and would not even be moved to a proper bed. He called the monks, the ascetics and the novices, admonished them with tears, told them to stay faithful to the tradition and the example he had given them, appointed his brother Eumenius his successor and gave him the little bag with his amulet. He received communion, and then, the page says, his face shone, heavenly melodies were heard, a fragrance spread over the whole hillside, and the Theotokos herself appeared to take his soul; his last words were «Καλῶς ὅρισες, Παναγία μου».
