# Alypius of the Kyiv Caves

Alypius, one of the first and best of Russian iconographers, was tonsured by [Nikon of the Caves](/saints/nikon-of-the-kyiv-caves) and laboured at the Kyiv Caves monastery from his youth. He learned icon-painting from the Greek masters who from 1083 were adorning the monastery’s church of the Dormition, and he painted for nothing: if he heard that the icons in some church had decayed he took them home and restored them without payment, and when he was paid he spent one third on materials, gave one third to the poor and kept one third.

He was never idle, and left his painting only for the divine services; ordained hieromonk, he was known in his lifetime for the gift of miracles — he healed a leper, the Prologue says, and saw an angel of God — and he died in peace in old age, around 1114. The Prologue, which keeps him on the same day for the Serbian calendar, says that in painting the likenesses of the saints on wood he was painting their virtues in his own soul.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 17 August as «Sfântul Alipie, iconarul», the icon-painter. Doxologia.ro's life calls him a follower of the Evangelist Luke, who painted not only the faces of the saints on icons but their goodness in his own soul, and a wonderful physician besides; it says he was given by his parents to the Greek painters who came to adorn the church of the Caves in the days of Vsevolod Yaroslavich of Kyiv and the abbacy of Nikon, and that he was himself a witness of the miracle of the mosaic of the Mother of God in the sanctuary. It ends with the angel who finished an icon for him and then stood by to take him: he gave up his spirit on the seventeenth day of the month of August, and the brethren laid him in the cave of Saint Anthony.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same menologion day and makes his craft the whole of its account of him. saint.gr calls our venerable father Alypius the cave-dweller an imitator of the holy evangelist and first iconographer Luke. This blessed man, it says, painted the figures of the saints wonderfully, while at the same time adorning his own soul with all the virtues; and so God counted him worthy to become a wonderworking physician of souls and bodies.

He was still a layman, the page goes on, when he witnessed the surpassing wonder that happened inside the church of the Caves during its painting: he was then helping the Greek iconographers who were decorating the church of the Theotokos, and saw with his own eyes a dove that strangely appeared. After that wonderful event Alypius stayed in the monastery and received the holy angelic habit from the hands of the abbot, the venerable Nikon, whose memory the page says is kept on 23 March, and went on perfecting himself in the sacred art of iconography. By the grace of God he acquired the power of stamping, on the lifeless painted figures of the saints, all the virtues and divine gifts that had adorned them while they lived; and the Lord gave him that power, saint.gr says, because he practised iconography not for gain.
