# Irenarchus

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 28 noiembrie reads «Sfântul Mucenic Irinarh» — the holy martyr Irenarchus — and the page that stands under his name carries no Viață tab at all, only a troparion in the fourth tone, and that troparion is the common of a martyr with his name set into it. Nothing is said there of his country, his century, the persecution in which he suffered or the manner of his death, and nothing of the kind is written here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr does give the story doxologia.ro had none of. He lived in the time of the emperor Diocletian, it says, and came of Sebastia, born into a family of idolaters and taught to feel rather a loathing for Christians; he served the torturers of the Christians in the persecutions. But his frequent nearness to the martyrs of Christ lit him up by degrees: he saw them meek and at the same time strong and unshakeable in their faith, and he saw the women bear the dreadful tortures with endurance and self-denial, and he wondered at their greatness.

That gradual change in his soul ended in his confessing Christ, at the very hour when seven Christian women were bearing all the punishments and torments most patiently for their faith. The confession cost him his life: he found death by beheading, and underwent it with complete gladness, in the year 303. The listing puts those seven women beside him and does not name them.

The Russian calendar names him of Sebaste, puts a reign where the Greek put a year, and gives him seven companions. Its line is «Мч. Иринарха и святых семи жен (303).» — the martyr Irenarchus and the seven holy women — and its life for him is two sentences: he and the seven women with him suffered in the reign of Diocletian, which it dates 284 to 305, and after the heaviest tortures they were beheaded. The name page behind the line gives him the by-name the line omits, «Иринарх Севастийский», and carries an icon and this one day.

The seven women are not written into this corpus from that line, and the reason is worth stating. The calendar names none of them, so they could only be a company folder like any other unnamed company; but the corpus already keeps `seven-women-of-sebaste`, seven women of the same city with a venerated Russian row of their own on 11 февраля ст. ст., where they stand beside the bishop of Sebaste and are dated about 316 under a different emperor. Two sets of seven women at one city, one under Diocletian and one under Licinius, is a question for a reader with both pages open, and until it is settled no second folder of that name is made here.
