# Dionysius of Corinth

Dionysius was bishop of Corinth and lived in the second century, saint.gr says, about the year 160, in the time of the emperor Marcus Aurelius. According to Meletios he was a man of letters, of wide learning and godly; and he took thought not only for his own flock but for the other churches too, with which he kept up a correspondence by letter. That correspondence the historian Eusebius characterises as divine teaching.

Of his end the page says only that it is said he was martyred by the sword — «Λέγεται ότι μαρτύρησε δια ξίφους» — and it gives no year for it. His skull, it adds under «Ιερά Λείψανα», is at the monastery of Jerusalem at Davleia in Boeotia. Its couplet counts him twice: Dionysius, being one of the sacrificers, went to meet the sword and is one of the martyrs as well; and a second couplet names Gerasimos as the man who wove the hymn for him.

The Охридски пролог gives him two sentences. He was a notable pastor and teacher, it says, and he was beheaded for Christ in the year 182.

That year is the corpus’s first year of death for him. The Greek calendar, which gave this folder its floruit of about 160 in the time of Marcus Aurelius, says plainly that it has no year for the martyrdom; so the death is written beside the floruit here and neither figure has been moved to fit the other. A pastor flourishing about 160 and beheaded in 182, two years after that emperor’s death, is one life. The Serbian book adds nothing else about him at all — no see beyond the one in his title, no account of the teaching, no accuser and no place.
