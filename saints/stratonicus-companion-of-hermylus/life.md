# Stratonicus, companion of Hermylus

Stratonicus kept the prison in which the deacon [Hermylus](/saints/hermylus) was held in the reign of Licinius (307-324), and he was secretly a Christian and Hermylus's friend. It wounded his heart to see his friend under torture, though his spirit was glad at the man's boldness and his strength; but he did not dare of himself to give himself up to such sufferings. What gave him away was pity. When Hermylus was stretched naked and his belly torn open with eagles' claws, Stratonicus began to weep; and some of those standing by told the emperor at once that the guard of the prison was openly a sharer in the Christian error and a friend of the man he guarded, since he pitied him and wept for him.

Called and questioned, he would not lie, being a disciple of the truth, and seeing that the hour of his own contest had come he confessed that he was Hermylus's friend and that he was a Christian; and he called the idols lifeless things and those who worshipped them madmen, and glorified the one God, the maker of heaven and earth. They beat him naked over the whole body with staves, and under the blows he raised his eyes to his friend and said, pray Christ for me, Hermylus, that he give me strength to keep my faith firm and unmoved and to be above all my tormentors. Then he laughed at Licinius and urged him to leave off worshipping blind and lifeless idols and to fear the punishment of the true God. They beat him until he fell silent, weakened by his wounds.

In the prison he prayed that the Lord would not remember his former sins, and the two of them together heard a voice from God: you have finished the course, you have kept the faith, and the crown of righteousness is prepared for you, which you will take in the morning. When the emperor urged him to sacrifice and not suffer as his friend was suffering, he answered that he would be wretched indeed to obey so mad a command, and that he did not wish to stay alive while his friend died for Christ; that true friends should bear danger together and enjoy good things together; and that nothing could be sweeter than to suffer and die for Christ. Licinius sentenced him to the same death, to be drowned in the Danube with Hermylus.

They were led down singing, put into nets and thrown into the deep. On the third day their bodies were found on the bank and were buried with honour some eighteen stadia from the city of Singidunum, which is Belgrade, the two of them laid in one grave, so that as friends they should hold everything in common.
