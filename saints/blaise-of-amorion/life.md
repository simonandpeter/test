# Blaise of Amorion

Blaise came from Amorion in Asia Minor, from the village of Aplatiani, and his name in the world was Basil. At the beginning of the ninth century he left his homeland for Constantinople, where he was ordained deacon at Hagia Sophia by the holy Patriarch Ignatius; his brother was a priest at the same church. After a departure to Bulgaria full of hazards, and a wonderful deliverance out of it, he travelled to Rome.

There he stayed almost twelve years. He was ordained priest, lived a wonderful life in the coenobium of Saint Caesarius, worked miracles, and twice the Mother of God visited him in a vision. He came back and shut himself away in the famous monastery of Studion for four years, where he made friendships with powerful men — with the emperor Leo VI the Wise and with the holy Patriarch Anthony.

About the year 896, «wishing to pass alone through the martyric struggle», he came to Athos with a few of his disciples and built a skete. When he had made it shine and had left one of his disciples as his successor, he was drawn to the more deserted parts of the Mountain; and of that time the life says that those about him were led to a deep peace, rested by the breezes of the Spirit.

After a further twelve years of ascetic labour he went back to the Studion monastery, because people had begun to trouble him. After a sharp fever, and having foreseen his end and served the Liturgy for the last time, he gave his holy soul to his Creator. His life was written about the year 940 by a Studite monk, a disciple of the disciple of Luke, «the best among the fathers» and «the best-loved disciple»; and doxologia.ro's notice adds that his honour as an Athonite is unknown on the Holy Mountain. It takes the account from Moses the Athonite, The Saints of the Holy Mountain (Mygdonia, Karyes, 2008), pages 137–138.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and follows him from one end of the empire to the other. saint.gr has him come of Amorion in Asia Minor, from the village of Aplatiane, his name in the world Basil; at the beginning of the ninth century he left his homeland for Constantinople, where the holy patriarch Ignatius ordained him deacon of Hagia Sophia, and where his brother was a priest at the same church. After an adventurous flight to Bulgaria and a wonderful rescue he travelled to Rome, and stayed there about twelve years: he was ordained priest, lived a life beyond admiration in the coenobium of St Caesarius, worked wonders, and was twice visited in a vision by the Theotokos. Returning, he was a monk for four years in the famous monastery of Studios, where he was bound to powerful men, the emperor Leo VI the Wise and the holy patriarch Antony. About the year 896, «την καταμόνας μαρτυρικήν παλαίστραν διεξελθείν εφιέμενος» — desiring to go through the solitary wrestling-school of martyrdom — he came to Athos with some of his disciples and founded a little monastery; and when he had adorned it and left one of the disciples as his successor, he withdrew to the most deserted parts of the Mountain and gave himself to ascesis and prayer, caring nothing for food. He was, the page says, for the whole of Athos «ως αστήρ διαυγής πάντας καταφωτίζων τοις αυτού προτερήμασιν» — a clear star lighting all of them by his excellences — so that the Mountain and its inhabitants, governed by his exhortation, kept a deep peace. After twelve years of these struggles he went back to Studios, because men had begun to trouble him; and after a high fever, having foreseen his end and served the Liturgy for the last time, he gave up his holy soul to his Maker in the year 909 or 912, and was buried gloriously in the chapel of St George at Studios. His fine life, the page adds, was written about 940 by a Studite monk who was the disciple of his own disciple Luke.
