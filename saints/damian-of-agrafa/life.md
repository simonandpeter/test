# Damian of Agrafa

Damian was born in the village of Mirihovo, in the district of Agrafa in Greece, of devout princes. Drawn to the monastic life from his earliest infancy, he entered the monastery of Philotheou on the Holy Mountain Athos; and later, seeking a harder manner of living, he went to a hermitage, where he grew in the things of the spirit.

Later still he felt himself called to leave the monastery, for the good of others and his own. Going into the distant region of Mount Olympus, he began to preach in villages and market towns, urging people to repentance and to the keeping of God's commandments. But some of the faithful did not receive him gladly, nor receive his word; on the contrary, they accused him of being a charlatan and threatened to beat him.

Leaving that place, he went into the regions of Kissavos, Larissa and Agrafa, where he met with similar trials. Because of those rejections he returned to Kissavos, where with other monks he founded a monastery dedicated to Saint John the Baptist; and he went on preaching there, for his words reached the people who came for the services and for spiritual counsel.

One day, as Damian was going on an errand to a neighbouring village, he was stopped by some Muslims who accused him of preaching against working on Sundays and of upholding the faith of the Orthodox Christians. For that they took him, threw him into prison, beat him hard and put him in chains. When he was brought before the kadi he was given the choice between denying his faith and death; he showed himself unmoved by every attempt to persuade him, and for that was condemned to die.

So they brought him out of the prison and hanged him. While he hung from the rope a Muslim struck him on the head with an axe, cutting the rope at the same stroke; the holy martyr fell to the ground, not yet dead, and the executioners took him and threw him into a fire, and his ashes were thrown into the river Peneios. So Damian the monk, of Mirihovo in Agrafa, received the sweet-smelling crown of martyrdom on 14 February 1568. The page carries no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and fills out the story. saint.gr has him born at Myrichovo of Agrafa, today Agia Triada, about 1510, of pious parents; he left his birthplace for the monastery of Philotheou on the Holy Mountain, was tonsured there, and then withdrew to live near an ascetic called Dometius for nearly three years, where he was counted worthy to hear the Lord's voice telling him not to seek only his own spiritual profit but other men's too. He went from Athos to the region of Olympus and preached repentance, was accused of being a deceiver of the people and a liar, and moved on to Larissa and Kissavos and then to Agrafa, where at Karytsa of the Dolopes he refounded the ruined monastery of the Panagia Pelekiti and was afterwards remembered as its founder. Accused again as a false monk, he left and built a new monastery by the village of Anatoli in honour of the Forerunner. Taken at Voulgarini by the Turkish governor of Larissa on a charge of stopping trade on Sundays and urging the Greeks to stand firm in their faith, he was chained by the feet and the neck and threatened, and when he still confessed Christ the governor ordered him killed and burned; the soldiers struck him on the head with an axe, and he was thrown half-dead into the furnace, and what was left of his relic was thrown into the river Pineios. The page adds that Nikodemos the Hagiorite gives 23 February for the martyrdom, and calls that probably a mistake.
