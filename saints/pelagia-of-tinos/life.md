# Pelagia of Tinos

doxologia.ro's calendar names her and gives no life. Its line for 23 iulie is «Sfânta Cuvioasă Pelaghia din insula Tinos» — the venerable Pelagia of the island of Tinos — and her own page carries the epistle and the gospel of the day, photographs, and links to the monastery of Saint Pelagia on Tinos in Greece as a place of pilgrimage. There is no Viață tab on it and no troparion.

So what the Romanian calendar sets down of her is her rank, venerable and not a martyr; her island, Tinos in the Aegean; and her day, the twenty-third of July. No century, no parentage and no account of her life were read, and none is supplied here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 23 July, and saint.gr says why that day: the memory was appointed for it because it is the day of her vision. It gives her years as 1752 to 1834 and her people by name. Pelagia was the daughter of the priest Nikephoros Negrepontis; her mother came from Tripotamos on Tinos and belonged to the Phrangoulis family; she was born in 1752 in the village of Kampos on Tinos and her worldly name was Loukia, and from various documents it appears she had three more sisters. Her father died when she was twelve, and the hardness of life made her mother send her to Tripotamos, to her somewhat better-off sister; there Loukia stayed three years and often visited her other aunt, a nun at the monastery of Kechrovouni. At fifteen she entered that monastery as a novice under the care of her aunt, the nun Pelagia, and in time was made a nun herself with the same name.

As a nun she gave herself soul and body to the worship of God and to the relief of the suffering, and her purity, her self-denial and her hidden life made her, the page says, the chosen vessel to whom the Mother of God revealed where her holy icon lay. On Sunday 9 July 1822 she saw in her sleep a stately lady with a halo, who told her how she had suffered buried so many years under the soil, and bade her go to the monastery's steward and tell him of her wish that her ruined buried palace in the field of Antonios Doxaras be uncovered. Pelagia understood on waking that the lady was the Theotokos and the palace her church, but doubted that such a thing could come to an obscure and humble woman, and said nothing. The same lady came again on 16 July with the same charge, and a third time on Sunday 23 July, grieved now and stern and asking why her charge had been ignored; and that day Pelagia went to the abbess, who knew her virtuous life and believed her, and to the steward, and so to the metropolitan of Tinos, who called the people of the island together to help with money or with labour.

The digging began in early September 1822 and uncovered the ancient temple of Dionysus and the church of Saint John the Forerunner, but no trace of an icon, and the people slowly abandoned the work and mocked Pelagia as a dreamer while the plague was reaping the island. With tears she asked the help of the Mother of God, who then revealed the exact spot; and on 30 January 1823, at the place she pointed out, the pick of Demetrios Vlassis struck the wonderworking icon. The page says she worked a number of wonders before her death and after it, that she died on 28 April 1834 and was buried in the monastery's church of the Taxiarchai, that she was declared a saint by a synodical patriarchal act on 11 September 1970, and that a great church was built in her name in 1973, where her holy head is kept and venerated today.
