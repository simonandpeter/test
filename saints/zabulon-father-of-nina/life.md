# Zabulon, father of Nina

Zabulon was a Cappadocian, a soldier of note and full of piety, who left his own country to offer his service to the emperor Maximian. He came to Rome, soon won the emperor's favour, and was made a commander of the army. In those days the Franks rose against the Romans, crossed the Alps, advanced along the river Po and pitched their camp there; and Zabulon at the emperor's order went against them, defeated them with God's help, and took many prisoners, the leader of the rising among them.

Maximian rewarded the army and condemned the rebels to death. Before they were killed the prisoners asked Zabulon to baptise them in the name of his God, who had given him so much power; and he not only baptised them but persuaded the emperor to set them free. The newly baptised took him home with them to preach Christianity in their own country, and for ten days the priests baptised the people on the banks of the river Gadamar.

Leaving the priests to finish the work, Zabulon went to venerate the Holy Places. There the deaconess of Bethlehem counselled the bishop of Jerusalem to give his sister [Susanna](/saints/susanna-mother-of-nina) in marriage to Zabulon, this famous and God-fearing commander of armies who had helped the Franks to know Christ. The counsel seemed good to the bishop, the marriage was soon made, and the two went home to his father's country, Cappadocia; and of that blessed pair was born [Nina](/saints/nina-of-georgia), the enlightener of Georgia.

When Nina was twelve she went up with her parents to Jerusalem, and there Zabulon, burning with love for God, wished to consecrate himself to Him and become a hermit. With his wife's consent and the bishop's blessing he took leave of his daughter in tears and entrusted her to the Lord, the Father of orphans and the Protector of widows. Fear nothing at all, my child, he told her; follow with zeal the example of Mary Magdalene and of the other Mary, the sister of Lazarus, and if you love Christ as they did His grace will not leave you.

Then he went away and was lost to sight in the desert of the Jordan. The place of his struggles and of his death, the page says, remained unknown. It gives him no year, either for his birth or for his end, and names the book it is drawing on: the Georgian Paterikon.

The Greek calendar keeps the pair on the same day and adds nothing to what is here. saint.gr's whole account of them is one sentence — «Οι Άγιοι Ζαβουλών και Σωσσάνη ήταν οι γονείς της Αγίας Νίνας της Ισαποστόλου», Zabulon and Susanna were the parents of Saint Nina Equal-to-the-Apostles — followed by a cross-reference to her own day, 14 Ιανουαρίου. It gives them no country, no years, no account of their lives and no death, and prints no hymn to either of them. The one thing it does add is a spelling: it writes her name Σωσσάνη.

The Russian church calendar keeps him and his wife together on 20 мая ст. ст. — «Прпп. Завулона и Сосанны , родителей равноапостольной Нины (III-IV)» — and gives them no life page of their own: the entry’s link is the index of Nina’s lives, and what the calendar says of him is said there. He was a kinsman of the great-martyr Георгий, of a noble house and of pious parents, and stood in favour with the emperor Maximian, whose reign the page dates 284 to 305. Being in the emperor’s military service, and a Christian, he worked for the release of Gallic prisoners who had received Christianity.

His daughter was twelve years old when he and his wife came with her to Jerusalem, where she was their only child. There, by the agreement of both of them and with the blessing of the Patriarch, he gave his own life to the service of God in the deserts of Jordan, while his wife was set as a deaconess at the church of the Holy Sepulchre and their daughter’s upbringing was given over to a devout elderly woman named Нианфора. The shorter of the calendar’s two readings puts the same thing in one clause: «отец ее Завулон ушел в пустынники» — her father Zabulon went off among the hermits. The calendar prints «(III-IV)» against the two of them and no year anywhere, and none has been written here.
