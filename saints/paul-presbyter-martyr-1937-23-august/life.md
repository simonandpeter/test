# Paul (Gaidai)

Paul Ignatievich Gaidai was born on 15 January 1886 in Bessarabia, at Izmail — the case files disagree, some naming Galați in Romania, but his relatives at Izmail have documents that he was baptised in the Edinoverie church there. In his youth he had the spiritual counsel of John of Kronstadt, who charged him: go to the archpriest Jonah (Atamansky) at Odessa and follow in his steps; after John’s death he did so, came to Odessa and served as psalmist in a town church, meaning to become a monk, but Father Jonah did not bless him for monasticism and found him a bride, a devout maiden of noble family, Kapitolina Dmitrievna, then about fifty while Paul was little over twenty, married them and blessed them to live as brother and sister; they later took in two girls, Stesha and Vera. Soon he was ordained deacon and priest and began to serve at the church of Peter and Paul in Odessa, drawing the hearts of the Orthodox by his preaching, his warm living faith, his boundless love for people and tireless work — and the authorities, noticing it, exiled him from Odessa to a distant farmstead and then to the village of Kapaklievka, where he celebrated the liturgy daily, served molebens for the sick and exorcised the possessed, and the sick came from all the towns around and were healed by his prayers. On 30 April 1929 he was arrested for practising healing, deceit to rouse superstition for gain and counter-revolutionary activity, and exiled three years to Turukhansk in the Krasnoyarsk country, where his wife, the girls and some twenty spiritual children followed him, living by gathering wild onion and burning lime; freed in 1933 he moved to Leningrad and served at the church of St George, but was soon arrested again and held in the Kresty — the calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. Exiled again at Akmolinsk, in 1937 he was charged with counter-revolutionary agitation and sentenced to be shot, and was executed on 5 September 1937; the Russian calendar keeps him on 23 August (Julian).
