# Irene of Lesvos

Irene was twelve years old, the daughter of Basil the headman of Thermi on Lesvos, when the Turks put down the Christian rising of 1463 and fell upon the village and the monastery where [Raphael](/saints/raphael-of-lesvos) was abbot. The life calls the children of that day the smaller torches that burned beside the great ones: her brother Raphael, a child of eleven months, was torn from his mother's arms and trampled, and Irene was taken with the rest of her household.

Her torturers first cut off one of her hands and threw it down in front of her parents, hoping to get something out of them about the rising. Then they forced the girl's mouth open and poured boiling water into it, and at the last they burned her alive in a great clay jar; that jar, doxologia's life says, is kept to this day in the monastery on the hill of Karyes. Her mother Maria was tied to a tree and died of the suffering, her father Basil was tortured and stabbed, Theodore the village schoolmaster was beheaded, and an orphan girl of fifteen named Helen was killed with them.

It was Irene, the page says, who showed in dreams where to dig, and the digging on the spot she gave uncovered a gold engolpion with the face of Christ the Almighty on it. Her relics lie in the lower church of the monastery at Karyes together with those of the deacon [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-of-lesvos), and the troparion calls her «trandafir al fecioriei», the rose of virginity.
