# James of Rostov

saint.gr keeps him on 27 November and gives him seven words of life. Its head line reads «Άγιος Ιάκωβος ο θαυματουργός Επίσκοπος Ροστοβίας (; - 1392)» — James the wonderworker, bishop of Rostov — and the page says of him only that he was bishop of Rostov, that he fell asleep in the year 1392, and «Δεν έχουμε άλλες λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Αγίου»: we have no further details of the saint's life.

So nothing else is recorded here. The page gives him no birth, no country, no account of his episcopate and no miracle, though it calls him wonderworker; it prints neither apolytikion nor kontakion, and no distich. The only other thing on it is a link to the synaxis of all the saints who shone in Rostov and Yaroslavl, which is a feast and not a part of his life.

What the Greek calendar attests is that a wonderworking bishop of Rostov of that name is kept on this day and that he died in 1392. Anything more would have to come from a Russian source this folder has not read.

The Russian calendar has the story the Greek line left out. By the local tradition he was tonsured at the Копырский monastery on the river Ухтома, eighty kilometres from Rostov, and was its abbot a long while before he was made bishop of Rostov in 1385. A woman condemned to death was brought before him; following the Saviour's own example he told whoever thought himself sinless to throw the first stone at her, and sent her away to repentance instead. The prince and the Rostov boyars were angry at that judgement and drove him out of the city.

Going out, he came to lake Неро, spread his hierarch's mantle on the water, signed himself with the cross and sailed on it as in a boat. About a verst and a half from the city he came ashore, and there the monastery would stand. The prince and the people repented and asked his pardon; he forgave them, the page says, and did not go back. On the lake shore he built himself a cell and a small church of the Conception of the Mother of God by the righteous Anna, and that was the beginning of the Зачатьевский Иаковлевский monastery. He died there on 27 November 1392, which is the day the calendar keeps him on.

The page then does something calendars rarely do: it takes a story away. It is widely held, it says, that he fought an iconoclast heresy at Rostov at the end of the fourteenth century — but his ancient life has not survived, the hagiographers who tell it drew on his service instead, and that service, surviving in sixteenth and seventeenth century copies, was put together out of the service to a second-century bishop who fought a first-century heretic and the service to an eighth-century hierarch who stood against an iconoclast emperor. The day page also prints two hymns of his own, in the fourth and the eighth tones, beside a troparion and kontakion to the hierarchs of Rostov together.
