# Stephen of Dečani

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 11 noiembrie reads «Sfântul Ștefan de la Decani, regele Serbiei», and the page it points to has no Viață tab and no Tropar tab: it carries the name, the day and the readings appointed for the day, and nothing else.

What the calendar's own line does say is that he was king of Serbia and is named from Dečani. It gives no century, no year, no parentage, no manner of death, and no hymn. He is not the Stefan the First-Crowned the corpus already keeps; nothing on this page joins him to that king.

The Serbian calendar would be the natural place to find him documented, and this reading did not open it; that row is left `undocumented` with the omission named.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 11 ноября ст. ст. and prints him as a Serb, «Мч. Стефана Дечанского (ок. 1336) (Серб.).», with four hymns of his own — and its own life, the first this folder has had from any source. He was the eldest son of [Stephen Milutin](/saints/stephen-milutin), and the page says his earthly life was a martyr's as his end was: his father, set on by the boy's stepmother, sent him away to the Pantocrator monastery in Constantinople with an order that he be blinded on the road.

[Nicholas of Myra](/saints/nicholas-of-myra) appeared to him and said «Не бойся, Стефан, твои очи поручены мне и в надлежащее время я верну их тебе» — fear not, Stephen, your eyes are entrusted to me and in due time I shall give them back to you. He spent five years in the strictest asceticism, keeping it as a perfect monk would, and became famous for his wisdom; on his advice, the page says, the emperor drove a heretic out of the capital.

With his sight wonderfully restored he returned to his own Serbia and ruled there ten years. He built the monastery of Dečani, one of the loveliest monuments of Serbian piety, where his incorrupt relics are honoured — the page adds, and it is its own closing remark — even by the local Muslims. **The year of his death is the first this folder has carried and the two Russian hosts disagree about it**, so both their figures are in the interval and neither is chosen.

*After doxologia.ro's calendar for 11 noiembrie — [the day](https://doxologia.ro/11-noiembrie) and [his page](https://doxologia.ro/sfantul-stefan-de-la-decani-regele-serbiei); read 25 September 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery, 11 ноября ст. ст. — [the day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20261111.html) and [the life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life6934.htm), read 5 October 2026.*
