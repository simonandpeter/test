# Domnina of Antioch

Domnina was from Antioch, the Greek synaxarion says, and embraced the Christian faith with her two daughters, [Berenice](/saints/berenice-daughter-of-domnina) and [Prosdoce](/saints/prosdoce-daughter-of-domnina); John Chrysostom, it adds, spoke in their praise and wrote down their life. Her husband was a pagan, and using the harshness of Diocletian’s persecution he tried to make them deny Christ, so the three fled to Edessa in northern Mesopotamia. He learned where they were hiding and came with soldiers to take them. On the way back they stopped for the night; the soldiers slept and the father kept watch over his wife and daughters, and, thinking that the soldiers would dishonour them, he set them the condition that to be let go they must throw themselves into the river and drown. They accepted, prayed, asked the Lord’s forgiveness, walked with a steady step into the river and so ended their lives.

The Romanian synaxarion tells it differently: out of godly zeal the mother and her daughters — Verina and Prosdoca in its spelling — left their homes and kin and wandered from place to place as far as Edessa, where the husband and father came on them suddenly with servants and caught them like game. They were taken to Hierapolis, where a river ran round the place, and while their guards gave themselves to drink the three slipped away, prayed in haste, threw themselves into the water and were drowned by its current. The Greek calendar keeps the three together on 4 October, and the Romanian keeps each on her own line the same day.
