# Alexius (Zadvornov)

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 9 ноября ст. ст. carries eleven names on one line and he is the eleventh and last of them: «Сщмчч. Парфения , еп. Ананьевского, Константина , Димитрия , Нестора , Феодора , Константина , Виктора , Илии , Павла пресвитеров, Иосифа диакона и прмч. Алексия (1937).» One year stands for all eleven, «Сщмчч.» heads the ten clergy and «прмч.» the monk who closes the line, and the grades it prints sort them into a bishop, eight presbyters, a deacon and a hieromonk. The name link behind his place supplies the calendar's own nominative with the surname the line leaves out, «Алексий (Задворнов), иеромонах, прмч.», and that is where the name form in this folder is read from.

**days.pravoslavie.ru gives this company nothing but that line.** Each of the eleven name pages carries a title, a list of days and no text at all, and not one of them has a `/Life/` link of any kind; the day page's hymns are the two martyrs' of 284–305, the venerable Matrona's, the Mother of God's before the Скоропослушница icon and the wonderworker of Aegina's, and nothing is sung under any of these eleven names. The life below is azbyka.ru's, read in the in-app browser because that host answers no fetcher, and the paragraphs are this corpus's own.

**He is the one man of the eleven whom the line classes apart.** The other ten are «Сщмчч.», hieromartyrs, and the line gives them a bishop's see or the word presbyter or deacon; he is «прмч.», a venerable-martyr, and the name link calls him a hieromonk. Алексей Семенович Задворнов was born in 1882 in the village of Korepovo in the Borisoglebsk country of the Yaroslavl province, in a peasant family, and had his first schooling in the village school.

In 1901 he entered the Yugskaya Dorofeeva hermitage in that province as a novice and took the monastic habit there. In 1912 he was appointed to the church of the Athanasius convent in the same province and ordained hierodeacon, and later priest. In 1922 he was sentenced to a year's imprisonment for concealing the monastery's valuables; he came back to the monastery in 1923 and stayed until it was closed in 1924, and for five years after that he served in a chapel near it.

When the authorities closed the chapel too he moved to the village of Zakedye in the Borisoglebsk district. On 1 April 1934 he was arrested and charged with counter-revolutionary activity; under questioning he admitted that he was an opponent of the Soviet power, and he was sentenced to five years in the labour camps and served the sentence at Dmitlag in the Moscow region.

On 1 November 1937 he was arrested in the camp on the evidence of witnesses who charged him with «осуждении Конституции, цитировании слов из Библии, недовольстве условиями жизни в лагере» — condemning the constitution, quoting words out of the Bible, and discontent with the conditions of life in the camp. To that he said that he «рассматривает лагерь как монастырь, всем доволен и за все благодарит Бога»: that he looked on the camp as a monastery, was content with everything and thanked God for everything. He was sentenced to be shot as a «служитель религиозного культа».

He was shot on 21 November. **The calendar keeps him on 9 ноября ст. ст., which is the civil 22 November, and his life has him shot on 21 November 1937** — the day before, 8 ноября old style. The difference is one day and not the thirteen of the style conversion, so it is neither a second feast nor an arithmetic slip that could be read away: five of the eleven died on the 22nd and six on the 21st, and the calendar holds all eleven on the one day. The attestation's day is the day page that listed him, as the brief requires, and the death above is the date his own life gives. He was glorified by the Council of Bishops of the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000.

**He is given no relation to the ten others the line names.** Four of them — the company of Semipalatinsk — are tied to one another by their own pages and to nobody else; of the six that are not, five lives end at Butovo on 21 November 1937 and no one of them names another, under sentences passed by troikas sitting on different days over different cases. A shared line, a shared class, a shared year and even a shared grave are not a company unless a page sentences the men together, and none of these pages does.
