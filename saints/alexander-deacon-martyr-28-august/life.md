# Alexander (Medvedev)

Alexander Medvedev (1889–1918) served as deacon in the village of
Bolshe-Trifonskoye in the Irbit district; in the years of persecution he
went on serving despite the danger, and was killed without trial by Red
Guards on 10 September 1918 at the station of Yegorshino — they cut him
down with sabres. He was born in 1889, studied at the Ekaterinburg school
of psalmists without finishing it, and began early to serve in church,
first as a sacristan, and from 1 July 1910 was allowed to act as psalmist
at the church of John the Forerunner at Bolshe-Trifonskoye; just before the
revolution Alexander Nilovich was ordained deacon, and served there until
his martyr’s death. Most of the villagers had strong farms and were
well-off — kulaks, as the Bolsheviks called them — and the October
revolution found no sympathy with them; the committee of the poor set up
by the Bolsheviks to carry out requisitions of food and property met
resistance from the mass of the villagers. Committees of the poor were
being formed in the villages, on which Lenin was staking a great deal,
wrote V. P. Anichkov, once manager of the Ekaterinburg branch of the
Volga-Kama bank, in his memoir of the revolution and the civil war — and
of whom did they consist? of idlers and drunkards, and nothing else; could
one expect success, under such rulers, for a communism founded on the
abolition of private property, when all that riff-raff would only plunder
property, not to hand it to the treasury but for their own ends? The
peasants of Bolshe-Trifonskoye said they had no need of a committee of the
poor, since they held the poor to be loafers and idlers, and refused to
obey it or to hand over grain and property for the needs of the Red Army.
For the peasants’ refusal to recognise Soviet power the Bolsheviks blamed
the clergy, and did all they could to undermine the Church’s authority in
the people’s eyes, declaring that the people had no need of religion and
calling it an opium that only drugged the people’s mind; churches were
desecrated everywhere and reprisals taken on the clergy. The young
deacon — twenty-seven, the calendar says — went on with his service at
Bolshe-Trifonskoye through the reprisals against the clergy that ran
without pause all the summer of 1918, serving with the priest Peter
Snezhnitsky, who likewise had not left his parish in that terrible time;
and on 28 August 1918 — 10 September of the new style — he was killed
without trial or investigation by Red Guards at Yegorshino station, cut
to pieces with sabres. By decision of the Holy Synod of 17 July 2002 he
was glorified in the Synaxis of the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia;
the Russian calendar keeps him on 28 August (Julian), and in the synaxis
of the saints of Ekaterinburg.
