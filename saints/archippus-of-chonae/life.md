# Archippus of Chonae

Archippus, the son of devout Christians of Hierapolis, came at ten years old to venerate at the church of the Archangel Michael — built, the Russian calendar says, by a man of Laodicea in thanks for his dumb daughter’s healing at its spring — and stayed at the church for good as its sacristan, leading a harsh ascetic life in constant fasting and prayer, and persuading many pagans who came to the holy spring to be baptised and leave their impiety for the one true God and Saviour Jesus Christ. The stubborn pagans, led by the idols’ priests, tried more than once to kill him, and the Lord saved him from their hands each time; at last they resolved to destroy the church and him with it by flooding the place where it stood and the healing spring flowed, turning a river against it. Seeing the preparations Archippus resolved not to leave the holy place and prayed earnestly to God and the Archangel for the church and the spring; the Lord heard him, and he witnessed the great miracle of the Archangel at Chonae, who opened a cleft in the rock beside the church into which the diverted river sank — whence the place was called Chonae, the sinking. Delivered from death, he lived at the church without leaving it to a ripe old age and died in peace at seventy. The calendars keep him with the memory of the miracle on 6 September (Julian); the Russian calendar prints the Archangel’s troparion and kontakion with the day.
