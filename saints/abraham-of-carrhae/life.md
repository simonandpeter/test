# Abraham of Carrhae

This venerable man lived in the time of Theodosius the Great and was of the city of Cyrrhus, where he was born, grew up and gathered the riches of the hermit's life and of virtue. With such vigil and standing all night, and with such fasting, did he mortify his body that he remained many years without moving, unable to walk. Doxologia's life of him is one paragraph, and what follows is the whole of it.

Learning that near Mount Lebanon there was a place full of idols, he went there, took a house on rent, rested the first three days, and on the fourth went out quietly. He was seized by the idolaters of the place and buried in the dust, and then ordered to go far away. But it happened that the collectors of the tax came just then, who were beating the inhabitants without mercy for the imperial dues; and the saint, taking pity, paid those dues to the collectors and so saved his own tormentors from the beating. Seeing it, all of them marvelled at the venerable man's love of mankind.

For that cause they became Christians, built a church at once and forced him to be their priest. Having been made priest he stayed there three years, guided them well in the honouring of God and strengthened them, and then went back to his cell, leaving another priest in his place. Shining with such good deeds, pleasing to God, he came to be bishop of Carrhae — a city, the page says, in Palestine, and full of idols. Going there with countless labours and with God-inspired teachings, he turned its inhabitants to the honouring of God in a short time and brought them to the Lord, teaching them first by what he did.

The emperor Theodosius, informed about him, called him to Constantinople; and going there and living a little while, he gave up his soul into the hands of God. The most right-believing emperor Theodosius sent his body with great honour to the city of Carrhae. The page gives no year, and it carries no hymn for him.
