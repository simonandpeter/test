# Silvanus of Gaza

Silvanus was from Gaza, a gentle man, whole in the faith, a priest of the church of Gaza and old in years. Standing upright before the people of Caesarea he was bitterly beaten and his ribs were raked. That is how doxologia.ro begins and very nearly ends its account of him: three sentences in all, with no emperor named, no governor and no year.

He was then condemned to labour at the copper mines of Zoora; and having been got away from there by the faithful, he was counted worthy to be made a bishop. Coming to deep old age and to weakness, for he was worn down by sickness, he was beheaded by the worshippers of idols.

The calendar’s line for the day names him «Sfântul Silvan, Episcopul Gazei», and the life on his page calls him «Sfântul Mucenic Silvan». The page prints no troparion for him, and nothing further is added here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 14 October, and adds two things doxologia does not. saint.gr says Silvanus came from Gaza, a city of Palestine, lived in the time of Diocletian, and was a man gentle, harmless and advanced in years, who had earlier been a soldier; and that it was for the purity of his life that he was made a presbyter in the church of Gaza. It tells the rest as the Romanian calendar does — he stood with courage before the unbelieving people of Caesarea in Palestine and reproved them for their impiety, the crowd beat him hard and broke his ribs, and he was condemned to labour at the mines at the place called Zoora, where at the urging of the people he became a steward. A little later, by reason of old age and of sickness, he was beheaded by the idolaters. And it closes with the company the Greek calendar keeps beside him: with Saint Silvanus, it says, forty other martyrs were beheaded as well, some of them from Egypt and some from Palestine.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day and is the only one of the three to say anything of his life. He came from the city of Gaza, its three sentences run, and was a presbyter there; for his zeal in spreading the faith of Christ he was sent to labour in the mines of the Palestinian town of Phaeno, and for all the torments he suffered there he did not deny Christ. Afterwards he received a martyr's end: he was beheaded together with forty Christian soldiers. It dates him to the fourth century, where the other two calendars give no date at all.

The Serbian church keeps him on a different day from the other three: the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him on the fourth of May, where the Russian, Romanian and Greek calendars all keep him on the fourteenth of October. Its whole entry is two sentences. He was first in military service, it says, but later, driven by the force of his own faith, he crossed over into the spiritual service. Accused of converting pagans into Christians, he was first bitterly tortured and then beheaded together with forty other soldiers, „и тако сви посташе грађани небески“ — and so they all became citizens of heaven. It names none of the forty.

**This book gives the year as 311**, where the century recorded in this folder comes from the Russian calendar's bare «(IV)» after his name. The two do not disagree — 311 is inside the fourth century — but the recorded interval was deliberately left as it stands, because narrowing a sourced interval is not a reader's act even when the narrower reading contradicts nothing. It also calls him **bishop** of Gaza, with the Romanian calendar and against the Russian line, which makes him a presbyter.
