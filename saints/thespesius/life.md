# Thespesius

saint.gr says Thespesius lived in the years of the emperor Alexander Severus, in the year 222, and came from Cappadocia. Because he confessed Christ he was arrested by Simplicius, the governor of Cappadocia, and was taken to the temple of the idols to sacrifice to them. Thespesius not only would not sacrifice, but mocked the idols; and for that they hung him up and tore his flesh.

Then they put him into a cauldron of boiling water, and by the grace of God Thespesius came out of it unharmed. Next they brought him again to the temple of the idols to offer sacrifice, but when he came near the altar he threw it down. At once they put him into a cauldron of boiling oil and pitch and fat, and after he had stayed in it two days he came out unhurt, without a single burn on his body; and at that many of the Greeks were drawn to the faith of Christ.

At the last they took him outside the city and beheaded him, and so his blessed soul went up crowned to the heavens. The page's distich says that Thespesius — whose name means one who speaks like God — was truly shown to be that, in fact and by the sword. It prints no hymn for him, and no year for his death.
