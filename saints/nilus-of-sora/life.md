# Nilus of Sora

He was born in 1433 into a good family of Moscow that loved Christ, and wishing to take the good yoke of the ascetic struggle he became a monk in the monastery of Saint Cyril at the White Lake. Afterwards he withdrew into stillness and made himself a wooden cell, where he laboured as a great hermit. Hearing of the purity of his life, many young men came to him and became monks, and that hermitage grew famous in the north of Russia.

Twice a week, on Wednesday and on Saturday evening, all the brothers gathered for an all-night vigil; in the morning they heard the Liturgy and communicated of the most pure Mysteries, and then each returned to his cell, thinking on holy things and praying without ceasing. They fed themselves by the work of their hands, did not receive lay people and above all not women, and avoided heavy farm work, speaking unceasingly with Christ. So Nilus became known through all Russia as a great spiritual father.

In 1503 he argued at a council that the monasteries should be released from the administration of the villages and the great agricultural estates given to them, which draw monks away from prayer and from a pure life in Christ; on the contrary he urged all to the hesychast life and to the practice of unceasing prayer. This, the page says, troubled the Orthodox Church of Russia for a long while.

In old age he withdrew to the stillness he loved, in the hermitage he had founded, which was called Sorska and was famous in all northern Russia, and gave his last years to the study of Holy Scripture and of the writings of the Fathers. Seeing himself old and burdened with days he composed a testament for his burial, in which he wrote, among other things: after my death, throw my body into a waste place, for the animals and the birds of heaven to tear, for it has sinned so much against God that it is unworthy of burial. On 7 May 1508 he gave his soul into the hands of Christ, and his disciples wept much for him.
