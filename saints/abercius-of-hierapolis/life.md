# Abercius of Hierapolis

Abercius was bishop of Hierapolis in the reign of Marcus Aurelius, the son of Antoninus, at a time when there were few Christians in that city and a countless multitude of idolaters. At a great pagan festival he wept to see men leaving God for the work of men's hands, and prayed in his chamber that the city be brought out of darkness. Sleeping after the prayer, he saw a young man fairer than the sons of men, who gave him a staff and said: “Abercius, go now in My name, and with this staff break the leaders of error.”

He rose about the ninth hour of the night, took up a great piece of wood, and went to the temple of Apollo, where the sacrifices of the day before were still standing, and broke the idols in it. The city rose against him; but he preached to them, and they began to come over, and he taught and baptised them. He healed the sick and drove out unclean spirits; at a place called Seliște he knelt by a river and prayed, and after a thunderclap out of a clear sky a spring of hot water ran where his knees had been, for the healing of those who should wash in it.

A demon whom he had driven out boasted that he would take him to Rome, and threw him down so that he cut his ankle on a stone. On the seventh night the Lord appeared and told him he would go to Rome, that His name might be better known there; and so it was. He healed the emperor's daughter of the enemy that tormented her, and the emperor, who did not want to let him go again for fear the demon return, gave him at his asking a written grant of corn for the poor of Hierapolis.

From Rome he was sent in a vision into Syria, and he went through many lands and cities, preaching, healing and driving out demons, so that all called him equal to the apostles, for nobody but the apostles had gone round so many countries. Then he came home, and the whole city — small and great, men, women and children — ran out to meet him. Three measures of wheat a year were given to the country people out of the imperial taxes on the strength of his letter, until Julian the Apostate destroyed both the grant and the letter; and baths were built at the hot springs by the emperor's order and his own care.

He spent the rest of his years in piety and righteousness, keeping his flock well, and knew his end beforehand, for the Lord appeared and told him the time was near to rest from his labours. He called his flock, told them his life was ending, taught them to hold to faith, unwavering hope and love unfeigned, made his grave ready, gave peace and a blessing to all, and gave up his soul, being seventy-two years old. The stone he had brought from Rome was laid with great labour on his grave, and healings were worked there and at the hot springs.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 22 October, and saint.gr adds what his office cost him and what it did not. The blamelessness of his life and the fruitfulness of his teaching, it says, moved his flock to compel him to become bishop of Hierapolis in Phrygia, and the dignity did not lessen his zeal: he used to say that it is not enough for a man to seem a ruler, he must really be one — that is, he must increase his service and his labours, because the Gospel says that whoever wishes to be first shall be last of all and the servant of all. God gave him the gift of many wonders for it. He healed the daughter of the king of Rome of an evil spirit and brought hot waters out of the earth; he preached in all the cities of Syria and Mesopotamia, and afterwards went into Lycaonia, Pisidia and the province of the Phrygians. He was called equal to the apostles, the page says, because he travelled and preached as the chief apostles of Christ did; and he died in peace at seventy-two years of age.
