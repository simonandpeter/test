# Job of Ushchelye

He was a monk of the Solovki monastery and the founder of a house at Ushchelye, in the province of Arkhangelsk, on the river Mezen. The reading says how poor that foundation was at its beginning: the first monks who gathered to him lived in the houses of their own lay relatives, there being nothing else to live in. Only after the tsar Michael Fyodorovich granted the house land with fishing rights did he build a church and cells for the brethren.

On 5 August 1628, while the brethren were out at the haymaking, robbers fell on the monastery and came to him demanding that he open its treasures. After dreadful torments they cut off his head. The reading closes by saying that his relics became renowned for wonders.

The two readings the source hangs behind his name differ in one word, and it is the word that settles what he was: the fuller of them heads him «Преподобномученик», the venerable-martyr, and the shorter «Преподобный», the venerable. The day line itself has «Прп.», the shorter grade, while the manner of death it does not mention is what the fuller grade is for.
