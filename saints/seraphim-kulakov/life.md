# Seraphim (Kulakov)

Seraphim, in the world Nicholas, was the son of Jacob Kulakov, a peasant of the Galich district of Kostroma province. He was drawn to the church from childhood and learned to read and write there, and when he was found to have a good voice the choir became his chief obedience, which he kept gladly. At twenty-four he entered a monastery with his father’s blessing: from 1898 he laboured in the Nikolo-Prilutsky monastery, as its novice from the next year, and after five years in its choir and cell obediences he was moved to the Koryazhma monastery of St Nicholas, which he reached in 1904 at thirty. He was tonsured there on 14 March 1904 with the name Seraphim, ordained hierodeacon two years later and hieromonk on 1 July 1907, and gave himself wholly to the service of God.

After October 1917 a commandant sent by the new authorities lived in the Koryazhma monastery. On 23 September 1918 an illiterate villager told the Solvychegodsk Cheka that suspicious people were hiding in the monastery, and on 30 September the military commissar and members of the commission came there, demanded that its administration hand over every hiding place and the money, and recorded the refusal as resistance to the Soviet power. The commandant wrote all the answers on one sheet: [Paul](/saints/paul-moiseev) said no to every question and that nothing more was in the monastery; [Nicodemus](/saints/nicodemus-shchapkov) admitted nothing; Seraphim said he had been in charge of nothing; and [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-sobolev) said he took no part in the monastery’s affairs and lived apart. The commission counted Seraphim, who had no part in the monastery’s business, and the retired Theodosius among its administration, and ordered the four shot as ringleaders of a hostile band. No money was found, and without waiting for the sentence to be confirmed they were shot at two in the afternoon of 30 September 1918, on a deserted island in the Vychegda on the road from the monastery: Theodosius in his seventy-seventh year, Paul in his seventieth, Nicodemus at sixty-five and Seraphim at forty-five.

The Russian calendar keeps the four on 17 September old style.
