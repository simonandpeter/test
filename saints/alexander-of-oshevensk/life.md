# Alexander of Oshevensk

Alexander of Oshevensk, Alexis in the world, was born on 17 March 1427 in the Vyzeozero region of Russia, to the family of Nikephoros and Photine Oshevensk. He was the last of five children and came into the world, the page says, thanks to his parents' fervent prayers: the Virgin and [Cyril of the White Lake](/saints/cyril-of-the-white-lake) had appeared to his mother and promised her the birth of a child. Though Alexis was the youngest son, his parents hoped that he would be the one to stand by them in their old age.

Reaching his youth he learned to read and write, being prepared to become a resourceful landholder; and at eighteen his parents tried to marry him to a rich bride they had chosen, but Alexis got their promise that he might first go and pray at the monastery of St Cyril. There he stayed. The abbot, having noticed the young novice's humility, proposed that he become a monk; but Alexis refused, wanting first to test himself, and so lived six years in obedience and in service to the community, studying the Scriptures and the works of the holy Fathers. After that long trial he was tonsured and received the name Alexander.

Meanwhile his parents had moved to the village of Volosovo, thirty kilometres from the town of Kargopol, in the country of the river Onega; and his father Nikephoros, by leave of John, the prince of Novgorod, had founded a village on the banks of the river Kuryuga, which he afterwards named Oshevensk. The monk Alexander asked the abbot's leave to go and see his parents, wishing to ask their forgiveness and their blessing on the hermit's life he had chosen. The abbot did not grant it at once, warning him of the dangers of the solitary life, but Alexander pressed to be let go — fearing, in truth, that he might fall into the sin of arrogance, since he already enjoyed among the brethren the reputation of an ascetic — and in the end he received the blessing.

His father, happy at the meeting with his son, proposed that he settle along the river Kuryuga, and promised to help him build a monastery in the wilderness. Alexander accepted, set up a cross on a spot as the sign of the monastery to come, and promised to remain there to the end of his life. He lived twenty-seven years more in the monastery he had founded, and fell asleep in peace in the year 1479.

After his repose the monastery began to decline, although — the page says — he did not cease to protect it. One day a servant of the monastery named Mark saw a vision in his sleep: the monastery was teeming with life. An elder with white hair, who was a bishop, was blessing with a cross those who worked at a building; another elder with a long beard was sprinkling holy water, and a third, of middling height and with light brown hair, was censing, while a fourth elder, a young man, watched them from a distance.

The third of them was Alexander himself, and he explained that the elders helping were St Nicholas and St Cyril, and that the young man standing apart was Maximus, the monastery's sacristan, who a little while before had become a monk, and who afterwards — by Alexander's own prophecy — would be abbot until the year 1525, and would cultivate in the monastery its old spirituality.
