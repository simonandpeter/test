# Alexander of Oshevensk

Alexander of Oshevensk, Alexis in the world, was born on 17 March 1427 in the Vyzeozero region of Russia, to the family of Nikephoros and Photine Oshevensk. He was the last of five children and came into the world, the page says, thanks to his parents' fervent prayers: the Virgin and [Cyril of the White Lake](/saints/cyril-of-the-white-lake) had appeared to his mother and promised her the birth of a child. Though Alexis was the youngest son, his parents hoped that he would be the one to stand by them in their old age.

Reaching his youth he learned to read and write, being prepared to become a resourceful landholder; and at eighteen his parents tried to marry him to a rich bride they had chosen, but Alexis got their promise that he might first go and pray at the monastery of St Cyril. There he stayed. The abbot, having noticed the young novice's humility, proposed that he become a monk; but Alexis refused, wanting first to test himself, and so lived six years in obedience and in service to the community, studying the Scriptures and the works of the holy Fathers. After that long trial he was tonsured and received the name Alexander.

Meanwhile his parents had moved to the village of Volosovo, thirty kilometres from the town of Kargopol, in the country of the river Onega; and his father Nikephoros, by leave of John, the prince of Novgorod, had founded a village on the banks of the river Kuryuga, which he afterwards named Oshevensk. The monk Alexander asked the abbot's leave to go and see his parents, wishing to ask their forgiveness and their blessing on the hermit's life he had chosen. The abbot did not grant it at once, warning him of the dangers of the solitary life, but Alexander pressed to be let go — fearing, in truth, that he might fall into the sin of arrogance, since he already enjoyed among the brethren the reputation of an ascetic — and in the end he received the blessing.

His father, happy at the meeting with his son, proposed that he settle along the river Kuryuga, and promised to help him build a monastery in the wilderness. Alexander accepted, set up a cross on a spot as the sign of the monastery to come, and promised to remain there to the end of his life. He lived twenty-seven years more in the monastery he had founded, and fell asleep in peace in the year 1479.

After his repose the monastery began to decline, although — the page says — he did not cease to protect it. One day a servant of the monastery named Mark saw a vision in his sleep: the monastery was teeming with life. An elder with white hair, who was a bishop, was blessing with a cross those who worked at a building; another elder with a long beard was sprinkling holy water, and a third, of middling height and with light brown hair, was censing, while a fourth elder, a young man, watched them from a distance.

The third of them was Alexander himself, and he explained that the elders helping were St Nicholas and St Cyril, and that the young man standing apart was Maximus, the monastery's sacristan, who a little while before had become a monk, and who afterwards — by Alexander's own prophecy — would be abbot until the year 1525, and would cultivate in the monastery its old spirituality.

The Russian calendar tells his whole life, and it begins before he was born. He was the fifth son of a rich farmer, Никифор Ошевен, and his wife Фотиния, born on 17 March 1427 eighty versts from Beloozero in the Vysheozero volost, a few months before the repose of [Cyril of the White Lake](/saints/cyril-of-the-white-lake); the Mother of God had appeared to Фотиния together with Cyril and promised her a son at Cyril’s intercession. In the world he was called Алексей. He was taught his letters as a boy and groomed to be a capable householder, and at eighteen they tried to marry him; with his parents’ leave he went to pray at the Kirillo-Belozersky monastery and stayed there.

The abbot loved him for his humility and soon offered him the tonsure, but he refused, meaning to test himself: he served the brethren six years as a novice, studying the Scriptures, and only then was tonsured. By that time his parents had moved to the village of Волосово, thirty versts down the Onega from Kargopol, and his father had obtained from a Novgorod boyar a place to settle on the river Чурьюга, which came to be called the Oshevensk sloboda. Twice he asked the abbot’s leave to take his parents’ last blessing and go to a solitary life, and twice he was warned of the danger of it; at the third asking, when he told how a wondrous voice had called him to found a house, the abbot let him go with icons of the Hodegetria and of святителя Николая Чудотворца.

He set a cross at the place and vowed to remain there to the end of his life. Архиепископ Иона of Novgorod (1459–1470) ordained him presbyter and set him abbot; the church was consecrated in the name of святителя Николая; a boyaress and her son would have given the monastery their whole volost and he took charters only for the land he needed. His rule required complete silence in church and at table while the lives were read, and no idleness in the cell, the Jesus prayer or the psalms during obedience. «Братия, — he said, — пусть не страшат нас труды и скорби пустыни»: it is by sorrows that men enter the Kingdom, and God is love and loves the humble.

Two of his nephews were tonsured in his monastery, which angered one of his brothers, Амвросий, whom he softened by meekness — and then the nephews cooled in their zeal and left. Grief for them broke his health, and he lay unable to lift a hand or his head or to speak a word. In that helplessness he prayed to Кирилл Белозерский, his patron, who appeared in white robes and signed him with the cross, saying: do not grieve, brother, I will pray and thou shalt be well; only forget not thy vow and leave not this place, and I will help thee. He woke healthy and went to church in the morning, and told the brethren of the visit. He laboured twenty-seven years in the house he had founded and reposed in peace on 20 April 1479.

The fuller of the two readings goes on past his death. The monastery began to fall quickly into decay, and a servant named Марк saw in a dream the house full of people, with Alexander himself censing among the builders while святитель Николай blessed with a cross and Кирилл sprinkled holy water, and a young man watching from a distance — the deacon Матфей, who was soon tonsured as Максим, chosen abbot as Alexander had foretold, set in office by архиепископ Сергий of Novgorod (1483–1485), and governed until 1525. When the new church of святителя Николая was built his incorrupt relics were found, and his image was painted then as he had appeared and as an elder who had known him described him: of middle height, with a dry face and sunken cheeks, a small and thin beard, and grey in fair hair.
