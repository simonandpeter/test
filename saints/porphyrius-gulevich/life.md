# Porphyrius (Gulevich)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him first of twenty-four and gives no life; the life here is the one the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru prints for the civil 2 декабря, out of pstgu.ru. Polycarp Vasilyevich Gulevich was born on 26 February 1864 at Tokarevka in the Litin district of the province of Podolia, in the family of a priest. He finished the Podolian ecclesiastical seminary in 1885 and was ordained on 22 October 1886, the feast of the Kazan icon. From 1914 to 1928 he was dean and rector of the cathedral at Olgopol and taught the law of God in the local gymnasium, and the page says the words of the Lord about whoever shall do and teach the commandments had sunk into his heart.

After 1917 he gathered round himself those who grieved for the faith, spoke against the Renovationist movement and in defence of Patriarch Tikhon, and passed leaflets among his flock calling them to stay faithful to the Patriarch. His wife died in 1927 and he was tonsured with the name Porphyrius. That year the Kharkov OGPU called him from Olgopol: a letter of his to an abbot named Ignatius had reached them, on the decree of the deputy patriarchal locum tenens ending the commemoration of exiled diocesan bishops. «Невозможно отказаться от сосланных епископов, это все равно, что отказаться от самих себя», he had written.

The journey turned out well for him. He came to know the church life of Kharkov and grew close to the abbot Barsanuphius (Yurchenko) and the priests Gregory Seletsky and Basil Podgorny, and there he met the archbishop of Kharkov, Constantine (Dyakov). It was at Kharkov, the page judges, that his consecration was decided. On 25 June 1928 he was consecrated bishop of Krivoy Rog by Constantine of Kharkov, Onuphrius (Gagalyuk) of Elisavetgrad and Paul (Kratirov) of Yalta, who was then himself in exile at Kharkov. He was sixty-four, and served often and preached at every service.

In September 1930 he was moved to the see of Zinovievsk, and on 11 August 1931, after the arrest of archbishop Arsenius (Smolenets), to the Crimean see, where he stayed until his own arrest. He was one of the first to speak openly of the famine then over the south of Russia and Ukraine, Crimea with it. He helped the exiled bishop Gabriel (Krasnovsky), who was ill with tuberculosis, to a place first at the Toplovsky monastery and then at Bakhchisaray, and kept up with him through the priest Nicholas Bychkov. A warden of the Symferopol church of All Saints, Nicholas Tainov, was an informer, and his report of the bishop survives: he was «активным церковным реакционером».

He was arrested with his helper the archpriest Nicholas Kazansky in 1933 and let out after two months. An enormous tax was then laid on the diocesan administration, out of all proportion to its income; he wrote to Moscow and had it reduced. He walked to the services at the Transfiguration cathedral of Symferopol, and witnesses said it was easy to pray with him, that everything else fell away. On 9 October 1936 he was arrested again with ten clergy, charged among other things with blessing prayers for men already sentenced. He did not deny it: «Ко мне обращались некоторые верующие с просьбой помолиться за осужденных». Four months in the Symferopol prison, and on 3 January 1937 five years of administrative exile in Kazakhstan.

He was sent in February 1937 to the station of Ush-Tobe in the Alma-Ata region, where the exiled bishop Macarius (Karmazin) was already living, and Macarius took him into his house. The Crimean communities of Symferopol, Yalta and Kerch sent him parcels, and that was all he had to live on. The two bishops lived shut away, at prayer, and now and then served the liturgy secretly at home. On 20 November 1937 both were arrested. Asked at his one interrogation to tell of his counter-revolutionary work he answered: «Я контрреволюционной агитации никогда не проводил». The troika sentenced him to death on 1 December, and he was shot on 2 December 1937; the burial place of those shot is the Zhanalyk range in the Talgar district.
