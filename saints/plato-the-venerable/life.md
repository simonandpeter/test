# Plato

Doxologia.ro's calendar for 4 aprilie names him and gives no life. The line is «Sfântul Cuvios Platon», and his own page on the site carries no life tab, no troparion and no text beyond the name, the date and the readings appointed for the day. There is no country, no century, no monastery and nothing of what he did.

The one thing the source asserts besides the day is that he was a monastic: «Cuvios» is the Romanian for the rank the Greek calendars give as ὅσιος, and the readings set beside his name are the monastic pair — the fruit of the Spirit from Galatians, and the easy yoke from Matthew. No epithet is added to the name here, because the page gives none and an epithet would be an identification this entry has not read.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives the whole career. saint.gr calls him famous for the strictness of his life and a teacher of asceticism, born at Constantinople in 732 of noble and wealthy parents, Stergios and Euphemia. He was at first an imperial notary in the palace, but renounced the world and went to Olympus in Asia Minor, where he became a monk at the monastery of the Symbols. Then he came back to Constantinople, took his nephews Theodore and Joseph — the second of them the later metropolitan of Thessalonica — and the rest of his family with him, and withdrew to the parts of Prousa, where on an estate of his own he built the monastery called Sakkoudion and became its abbot. Falling ill, he handed the abbacy to his nephew [Theodore](/saints/theodore-the-studite).

The barbarian raids forced him and his spiritual flock back to the reigning city, where the monastery of Stoudios, then deserted, was given him to live in; he rebuilt it and became its abbot. By the severity of his character, the page says unhappily, he came into conflict with the emperor Constantine VI over his marriage to Theodote, and was exiled. He was recalled, took an active part against the iconoclasts, and with his nephews Theodore and [Joseph](/saints/joseph-archbishop-of-thessalonica) bore one exile and hardship after another until he died on 4 April 814.

**The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 5 April**, where the Romanian and the Greek keep him on the 4th, and its line is «Прп. Платона , исп. Студийского (814).» — venerable Plato, confessor, of Studion, 814. The name link, page 1804, gives the fullest form of all three books, «Платон Студийский, игумен, исповедник», abbot and confessor together. Its life is the first narrative this folder has had from a Russian hand, and on one point it contradicts the Greek: it gives the year of birth as 735, not 732, and names the parents «Сергия и Евфимии» where saint.gr names Stergios and Euphemia. Both years are now held and neither is chosen.

Orphaned early, he was brought up by relatives who gave him a good education; grown, he managed the estate left him by his parents, was abstemious and hard-working, and made a large fortune by his own labour — and then gave away all of it, freed his servants, and withdrew to the monastery called «в Символех» near mount Olympus. His prayer, his industry and his meekness won him the brethren’s love; in the hours free from prayer he copied service books and made collections out of the works of the holy fathers. When the superior of the house died in 770 the brethren chose him abbot, although he was only thirty-five.

After the emperor Constantine Copronymus died in 775 he went to Constantinople. **He refused the metropolitanate of Nicomedia** — which no other book read for this folder mentions — and in 782 withdrew with his nephews [Theodore](/saints/theodore-the-studite) and [Joseph](/saints/joseph-archbishop-of-thessalonica) to the desert place Sakkoudion, where they built a church on the mountain in honour of the apostle «Иоанна Богослова» and founded the monastery he became superior of. When the Seventh Ecumenical Council met at Nicaea in 787 he took an active part in it and refuted the iconoclast heresy there; in his old age he handed the house over to his nephew.

In 795 the emperor forced his wife to take the veil and resolved to marry a kinswoman of his, and a prominent priest of the capital performed the wedding against the patriarch’s prohibition. Plato and his nephew excommunicated the emperor and sent letters about it to all the monks; Plato was imprisoned and his nephew exiled to Thessalonica, and they were freed only when the emperor died in 797. His nephew then became abbot of the Studion house and Plato lived in the same monastery as a simple monk, under his nephew’s obedience. When the next emperor restored the excommunicated priest on his own authority the two denounced that too, and in 807 both were punished again: four years in prison, and Plato released after the emperor’s death in 811.

He lived three more years in labour and prayer and reposed on Lazarus Saturday **in the seventy-ninth year of his life, on 8 April 814** — a day of the month neither of the two calendars that keep him uses, and a figure that squares with the 735 this page gives for his birth and not with the 732 the Greek page gives. It is recorded here and no feast is moved for it. For his fearless stand in defence of the holy icons, the page ends, he received the name of confessor, which is the one word his Russian line adds to his rank.
