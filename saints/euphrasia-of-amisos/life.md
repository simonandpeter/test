# Euphrasia of Amisos

saint.gr's calendar for 20 Μαρτίου names seven women together, and its life page gives each of them a heading of her own, which is why they are kept here as seven folders and not as one company. Euphrasia contended with [Alexandria](/saints/alexandria-of-amisos), [Claudia](/saints/claudia-of-amisos), [Matrona](/saints/matrona-of-amisos), [Juliana](/saints/juliana-of-amisos), [Euphemia](/saints/euphemia-of-amisos) and [Theodora](/saints/theodora-of-amisos) in the years of the impious emperor Maximian (286–305) at Amisos of Pontus — or Aminsos, or Amsos, or Emed, the page offers all four spellings — when a great persecution rose up against those who confessed Christ.

The women were arrested by the ruler of Amisos, who was an idolater, and when they stood before him they confessed that they were Christians and rebuked him with boldness, calling him hard and unjust and an enemy of the truth. The ruler flew into a rage and gave orders that they be set in a public place as a spectacle, where they began to beat them with rods. Then their breasts were cut off with swords, and they were hung up and flayed so far that their entrails showed. Last of all they were thrown into a great furnace of fire, and while they were singing and praying to God they gave up their souls. Their martyrdom, the page says, took place between the years 303 and 305.

Over the seven saint.gr prints a couplet — the number seven, a virgin among numbers, declares the women who were burned — and adds a note of its own explaining it: seven is called virgin because within the first ten numbers it neither produces another nor is produced by one. The page gives Euphrasia no age, no parentage and no words of her own apart from the confession the seven make together, names no relic, and prints no hymn.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 20 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/20/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3926/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
