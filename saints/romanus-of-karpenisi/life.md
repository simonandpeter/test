# Romanus of Karpenisi

saint.gr says the new venerable-martyr Romanus came from the region of Karpenisi, and most probably from the village of Andranova, which today is called Aspropyrgos. He was brought up by parents who were poor and unlettered but devout, and he remained unlettered himself. He came once as a pilgrim to the Holy Land, to the monastery of Saint Sabbas, where he heard accounts of the new martyrs of the faith, and the longing for martyrdom took hold of him.

He went to Thessalonica, where he confessed Jesus Christ before the judge with courage, as true God and saviour of men, and denounced Mohammed as a teller of tales. The tortures, the page says, were dreadful. The commander of the Turkish fleet asked that he be sentenced for life to the oar of the ships; but some Christians bought his freedom for money and sent him to the Holy Mountain, to Kausokalyvia.

On the Holy Mountain he stayed with the venerable Acacius of Kausokalyvia, and prepared himself for martyrdom with prayer and fasting. So with his spiritual father's blessing, a monk now, he set out for the Holy Land to bear witness there; but so that nothing ill should come upon the All-holy Sepulchre he went instead to Constantinople. There he provoked the Turks, and they threw him into a dry well and left him without food for forty days. In the end they beheaded him on 5 or 6 January — others, the page says, on 16 February — of the year 1694.

His holy relic was bought for five hundred piastres by an English ship's captain and carried to England. The handkerchief with his blood, saint.gr adds, is at the monastery of Docheiariou; a service for him is in Codex 61, page 265, of the Kalyve of the Ioasaphaioi at Kausokalyvia, and was published at Volos in 1937. The page closes by saying that the Church keeps his memory also on 16 February.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, where the Greek calendar keeps him on 5 January, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells it at length. A simple unlettered peasant of Karpenisi, he learned of the courage and glory of Christ's martyrs and wanted martyrdom himself; he went to Thessalonica and began praising the faith of Christ in the street and calling Mohammed a teller of fables. The Turks tortured him dreadfully and then sold him to a galley captain, from whom Christians bought him back and sent him to the Holy Mountain, where he became a monk under a famous elder.

He still wanted martyrdom for Christ. With the elder's blessing he went to Constantinople, played the madman and began leading a dog through the Turkish streets; asked what he was doing, he answered that he was feeding that dog as the Christians feed the Turks. They threw him into a dry well, where he spent forty days without bread, and then drew him out and beheaded him. Light came out of his body for three days, the Prologue says; an Englishman took the body and carried it to England, and a monk dipped a towel in the martyr's blood, which is kept to this day at the monastery of Docheiariou. It dates his suffering 1694.
