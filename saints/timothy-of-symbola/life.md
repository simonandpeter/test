# Timothy of Symbola

Near Mount Olympus there was a desert place called Symbola, and a monastery in it, whose archimandrite was the venerable Theoctistus, a man of virtue, by whom Plato the Confessor was also guided into the monastic life. Among that blessed archimandrite's disciples was this venerable Timothy, who took the monastic life from his youth and laboured in fasting, in abstinence and in prayers that lasted all night, putting the leapings of passion wholly to death.

He was without passion and perfect to the end of his life, a virgin in body and in soul, for from his youth he had laid down a rule for his eyes never to look at a woman's face. For this he was counted worthy to receive the gift of healing and power over the demons, and he worked wonders by his prayers, healing every infirmity and driving the wicked spirits out of men.

Afterwards he went from place to place for many years, living alone in the mountains and the wildernesses with God, and cooling his soul with a dew of tears in unceasing prayers. Passing his days in such a life, he came to deep old age and departed to the Lord. Doxologia gives him no year and no century, and the troparion its page prints for him is the common of the venerable fathers.
