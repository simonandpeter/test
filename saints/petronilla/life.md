# Petronilla

saint.gr says the holy martyr Petronilla lived in the first or the third century — the page gives the two and settles neither. In the cemetery of Domitilla at Rome, it adds, there is a fresco dated to the fourth century in which the martyrdom of Saint Petronilla is depicted, and that fresco is the one thing about her the page dates firmly.

She came of a noble family, it says, and was a Christian, and she refused to marry a nobleman named Flassus, because she wished to be dedicated to her Bridegroom Christ. For that reason she was arrested and was perfected in martyrdom. The page names no judge, no emperor and no year, and prints no hymn for her.

It closes by reporting, and then declining, a tradition about her: because of her name, tradition holds that she was the daughter of the Apostle Peter, or a devoted disciple of the Apostle who helped him in his work. But that tradition, saint.gr says, is reported in various Gnostic books of the sixth century, and most scholars and hagiologists do not accept the view. This folder records neither the kinship nor the discipleship for that reason.
