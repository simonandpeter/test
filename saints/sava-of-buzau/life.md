# Sava of Buzău

Sava was born in 334, the son of devout Christians of the Buzău country, at the time when that part of the land was held by the Goths. He grew up under the guidance of the priests Sansalas and Gutticas, and the life praises his devotion, his zeal for prayer, his gentleness, humility, love of truth and steadiness in the Orthodox faith. As he grew he sang the psalms in church and lived a life others could take as a pattern. He was gentle and patient with everyone, quick to obey, simple in his daily living, gathering no property beyond what was needed for clothing and food, temperate in all things, a virgin, fasting daily and persevering in prayer.

When the pagan Goths began to persecute the Christians, their king Athanaric ordered an idol carried on a cart through the towns and villages so that sacrifice might be made to it. Sava urged the Christians not to sacrifice to the idols nor to eat of the meats offered them, since that was to deny the faith in Christ. Before Pascha of the year 372 he wanted to go to another village for the services, but an angel of the Lord appeared to him and told him to return to his own village, preparing him for martyrdom. Not long after, confessing himself a Christian, he was seized by the pagan Goths.

When those leading him reached the bank of the river, they took counsel among themselves: let us set this innocent man free, for how would Atharid ever know? But Sava said to them, «De ce vorbiţi deşertăciuni şi nu împliniţi ceea ce vi s-a poruncit?» — why do you talk emptily and not do what you were ordered? I see what you cannot see: the angels are standing here in glory, come to receive me. So they brought him down into the water, giving thanks and glorifying God as he went, laid a beam across his neck and pushed him into the deep.

After they had drowned him they drew him out, left him unburied and went away. No wild beast touched the body; the brethren took it up and buried it. It was the twelfth day of April in the year 372. Afterwards, following the exchange of letters between [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great) and Bretanion, bishop of Tomis, and with the leave of the Church in Gothia, the governor of Dobrogea, Junius Soranus, sent trustworthy men to take up the relics of the martyr Sava and carry them to Cappadocia, from which the saint's forebears had come before the Goths took them captive.
