# Natalia of Nicomedia

Natalia was the wife of [Adrian of Nicomedia](/saints/adrian-of-nicomedia), who at twenty-eight declared himself a Christian after seeing twenty-three Christians about to be martyred under Maximian. She went to him in prison to stand by him and tell him not to bend, and when he had died under torture, and a downpour had put out the fire that was to burn his body, she buried him.

A little later, the synaxarion says, she was buried beside him, having herself suffered for Christ. The Romanian entry adds that she was born of faithful parents and had kept her faith secret, seeing the cruel persecution of the Christians.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep them the same day, 26 August (Julian), and the Serbian prints their troparion. The Sretensky calendar’s life: Adrian and Natalia married young, only a year before their sufferings — thirteen months, the Prologue says — and lived at Nicomedia in Bithynia under Maximian (305–311), both of noble and rich family, Adrian head of the praetorium and a pagan, Natalia a secret Christian. Beginning the persecution the emperor promised rewards to those who pointed out Christians and brought them to trial; on one denunciation twenty-three Christians hiding in a cave near Nicomedia were taken, tortured, pressed to worship the idols and brought to the court to have their names and answers written down. Adrian, the head of the court, watching with what courage these men bore suffering for the faith and how firmly they confessed Christ, asked what reward they expected from their God for their torments; such a reward, they answered, as we cannot describe and your mind cannot grasp. Fired, he said to the scribes: write me down too, for I also am a Christian and will gladly die for Christ God. The scribes told the emperor, who summoned him — have you too gone mad, and want to perish? go, strike your name from the lists and sacrifice — and Adrian answered, I have not gone mad, I have come to my right mind; and Maximian had him imprisoned. Natalia, learning that her husband suffered for Christ, rejoiced, and hurried to the prison to strengthen him: blessed are you, my lord, that you have believed in Christ; you have gained a great treasure; spare nothing earthly, neither beauty nor youth — he was twenty-eight — nor riches; all earthly things are dust and corruption, and only faith and good works please God. On the surety of the other martyrs Adrian was let out of prison to tell his wife the day of the execution; Natalia thought he had denied Christ and been set free, and would not let him into the house, until he persuaded her that he had not fled the torments but had come to tell her the day of his death. He was cruelly tortured; the emperor advised him to spare himself and call on the gods, and he answered, let your gods say what good they promise me, and then I will worship them — and if they cannot speak, why should I bow to them? Natalia never stopped strengthening her husband, and asked him to make his first prayer to God for her, that she should not be forced to marry a pagan after his death. The torturer ordered the saints’ hands and feet broken on an anvil, and Natalia, afraid that her husband, seeing the others’ sufferings, might waver, asked the executioners to begin with him and herself helped lay his hands and feet on the anvil. They wanted to burn the bodies, but a great storm rose and the furnace went out, and many of the executioners were killed by lightning. Natalia took her husband’s hand and kept it in the house; soon a commander of the army asked the emperor’s leave to marry her, young and rich as she was, and she fled to Byzantium, where Adrian appeared to her in a dream and told her the Lord would soon give her rest — and the bloodless martyr, worn out by what she had suffered, soon went to God. After long torments and imprisonment, the Prologue says, the emperor ordered an anvil brought into the prison and their legs and arms broken with a hammer, and Adrian with the twenty-three honourable men died in the heaviest torments; Natalia carried their relics to Constantinople and buried them there with honour, and after some days Adrian appeared to her all in light and beauty and called her to go to God too, and she gave her spirit to God in peace.
