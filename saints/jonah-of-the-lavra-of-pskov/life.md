# Jonah of the Lavra of Pskov

saint.gr's calendar for 29 Μαρτίου names him first of three venerable fathers, «Όσιοι Ιωνάς, Μάρκος και Βάσσος», and the page behind the line is one sentence about all three: the venerable fathers Mark, Jonah and Bassos lived and practised ascesis in the Lavra of Pskov in Russia during the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, and fell asleep in holiness and peace. The calendar line and the narrative disagree about which of them to name first, and nothing else on the page tells them apart.

So the source gives him a house, a nation, two centuries and a peaceful death, and no year, no office, no teacher, no disciple, no wonder, no relic and no hymn. The note on the name — «ΙΩΝΑΣ (εβραϊκή λέξη) = το αγγελιοφόρο περιστέρι», the messenger dove, the one sent by God — belongs to the word.

Two cautions belong on this folder. The same Greek day keeps another Jonah entirely, the Persian ascetic who was sawn through the middle about the year 330 and who is commemorated with Barachisius; and the corpus already holds Jonahs of Kyiv, of Moscow and of Yashezero. This Jonah is none of them, and is identified only by the Lavra of Pskov and by his two companions, [Mark](/saints/mark-of-the-lavra-of-pskov) and [Bassos](/saints/bassos-of-the-lavra-of-pskov).

The Russian church calendar keeps this house’s first fathers on 29 марта ст. ст., and the life it links is one life for three of them. Nobody knows when the first hermits settled by the brook Каменец in the natural caves of the hill the country people called the holy hill; the monastery chronicle keeps the account of hunters from Изборск who came to the edge of the hill with their father and heard what seemed to be church singing, sung in order and with reverence, with no singers to be seen and the air full of the fragrance of incense.

In 1472 a peasant named Иван Дементьев was felling timber on the brow of the hill; one of the trees rolled down, tearing up the earth and the other trees with it, and the landslip opened the mouth of a cave over which was written «Богом зданная пещера», the cave built by God. To that place came a priest called Иоанн, surnamed «Шестник», the settler: a man of the Moscow country who had served the Russians of Юрьев, which is Tartu now, and who fled with his family to Pskov in 1470 from the persecution of the German Catholics. Hearing that his fellow priest «Исидор» had died a martyr, he resolved to withdraw into the newly found cave and to found a monastery there, on the very border with Livonia, as a bulwark of Orthodoxy.

He is that priest Иоанн. Shaken by the wonder worked over his wife’s coffin, he received the monastic habit with the name Иона and gave himself to the struggle more earnestly than before; he dug out the cave church with his own hands, with two cells on pillars, and went to ask the priests of the Trinity cathedral of Pskov to consecrate it. The day’s line closes him with 1480, which is the year recorded here; the house he founded is the Pskov Caves monastery.
