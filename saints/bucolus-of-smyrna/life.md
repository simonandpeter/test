# Bucolus of Smyrna

doxologia.ro gives Bucolus one short paragraph, and this is the whole of it. God's well-pleasing servant Bucolus was trained from his childhood in innocence and in whole wisdom, and became a vessel of the Holy Spirit. The much-praised disciple whom Christ loved, [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), finding him skilled and worthy, ordained him bishop and a most profitable shepherd for the people of Smyrna.

Enlightened by the Holy Spirit, and set like a lamp on a lampstand, he lit with heavenly light those who sat in the darkness of error and of the deceit of idols; and by holy Baptism he made them sons of light and delivered them from the unseen and savage beasts that sought whom they might swallow. Before his death he committed his rational sheep to the blessed Polycarp, and passed from this temporary life to the everlasting one. When his holy body had been buried, God caused a plant to grow on his grave which gave healing to the sick. The page gives no year for his birth and none for his repose.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and adds three things. It makes him a disciple of St John the Theologian, who himself ordained him bishop of the church of Smyrna. It says that before he left this passing life he ordained as his successor the hieromartyr Polycarp, whom the Greek calendar honours on 23 February, and then fell asleep in holiness and in peace. And it reports out of his synaxarion that as soon as his body was buried a tree sprang up by a wonder of God on the place of his burial, and gave healings to the faithful. A fragment of his relics, the page notes, is at the monastery of St Theodosius at Argos. Its couplet calls him the shepherd of Smyrna and a sacrificer who, dead, is still the sleepless guard of his flock.

The Serbian church keeps him on the Julian 6 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue tells him in five sentences that agree with the other three on the essentials — the disciple of St John the Theologian, ordained by him bishop of the city of Smyrna, who before his own death consecrated the glorious Polycarp his successor and then made a peaceful end and went to the Lord. Two things in it are its own. There were few baptised in Smyrna at that time, and in the darkness of paganism he shone like a clear candle; and of all the virtues that marked him out, the ones it names are gentleness and meekness. It does not carry the healing plant that grew on his grave, and it gives no year.
