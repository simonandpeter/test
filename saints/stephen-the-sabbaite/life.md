# Stephen the Sabbaite

He is called the Sabbaite, doxologia explains, because he took the monastic habit in his youth at the famous monastery of Saint Sabbas in Palestine. He was a nephew of Saint John of Damascus, whose day the page gives as 4 December, and he was born in the year 725; at ten years old he entered the Lavra of Saint Sabbas the Sanctified, whose day it gives as 5 December, and was tonsured a monk there.

He spent his whole life at that monastery, going out at times into the desert to live alone and to give himself to the spiritual struggle. For the quality of his spiritual life, the page says, he was raised to the rank of the hierarchy; it says no more than that about the office and gives no see, so no office is recorded in this folder.

God granted him the gift of healing. He healed a maiden held by a devil by his prayer; he tamed wild animals; and by striking a rock with his staff he brought water out of it, so that one of his disciples might quench his thirst. He passed to the Lord in peace, falling asleep in the year 794, having foretold the day of his death beforehand. His life, the page ends, was written by his disciple Leontius.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 13 July. saint.gr says Stephen was the nephew of John of Damascus, born at Damascus in 725, and that his father was called Theodore Mansour and was John's brother. At ten years old he was brought by his uncle into the Lavra of Saint Sabbas, where he was trained very well in the monastic life for fifteen years; after his uncle's death he withdrew into the desert and gave himself still more to the study of the word of God, and there took up poetry as well, in which he was distinguished and was shown to be one of the greatest poets of the Church. Whatever he made with the talent God had given him, the page says, he dedicated to the glory of God and not to the admiration of men, being moved by the words of Scripture: whatever you do in word or in deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God. Having adorned the Church with his poems he died in peace in 794, or by others in 807. The page adds that his memory is kept again on 28 October, and says plainly that it does not know why there are two: perhaps, it suggests, today is the translation of his relics and 28 October the memory proper. A portion of his relics is at the monastery of Prousos in Eurytania.
