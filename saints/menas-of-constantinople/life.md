# Menas

Menas came from Alexandria and ascended the throne of Constantinople in 536, under Justinian the Great, ordained patriarch by Pope Agapitus of Rome after the deposition of the heretic Anthimus. The Fifth Ecumenical Council (553) was convened in his patriarchate. He was patriarch sixteen years and six months and died in peace, having tended the flock of Christ well.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep him the same day, 25 August (Julian). The Sretensky calendar has him first a presbyter of Constantinople and overseer of the hospice of Sampson the Hospitable under Justinian; after the deposition of the heretic Anthimus he was raised to the patriarchal throne as worthy of the episcopate for his high virtue and firm confession of Orthodoxy, and consecrated by Pope Agapitus, then in the capital. In his patriarchate a wonder happened that became known to the whole city: a Jewish boy went into a church with other children and received the Holy Mysteries, and told his father at home; the father, a glass-blower, in a terrible rage threw the child into the red-hot furnace and said nothing to his wife, who searched three days in tears, calling him, until on the third day he answered her from the furnace and she drew him out unharmed — a most radiant Lady, the boy said, had come to him, cooled the fire and brought him water and food. Menas and Justinian heard of it; the boy and his mother were baptised, but the father hardened himself and would not repent despite the great wonder he had witnessed, and the emperor had him tried as a child-murderer and sentenced to death. Menas governed the Church of Constantinople sixteen years, in which the famous church of Holy Wisdom was consecrated, and died in peace in 552; he governed the Church wisely from 536 to 552, the Prologue says.
