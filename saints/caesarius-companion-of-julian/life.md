# Caesarius, companion of Julian

The Romanian life sets Caesarius in the reign of Claudius at Rome, who had put his own mother to death for her faith in Christ and had no mercy left for Christians. Caesarius came out of Africa to a village the life calls Tarachini, saw the sacrifices offered there, spat on them and trampled them. He was seized, kept three days in prison without food, and handed over to the proconsul.

His hands were tied behind him and he was dragged before the governor's carriage as far as the temple of Apollo. There he prayed, and the temple fell from its foundations with the chief priest of the idols and many others inside it. Leontius, seeing that, fell at the saint's feet, believed in Christ and was baptised before them all; the priest [Julian](/saints/julian-companion-of-caesarius) gave him the holy Mysteries, and he gave up his soul at once, as he had asked in the saint's prayer.

Loxorius the commander then arrested Julian and Caesarius the deacon and ordered them sewn into sacks and thrown into the sea. The saints told him that they would be drowned but that he would be bitten by an evil serpent and lose his soul by a bad death; two days later a great serpent coiled about him by the shore and crushed him, and he lay swollen and pitiable until he died. Their bodies were brought out of the sea and buried near the city by a priest named Eusebius and by Felix, who had been sent by a vision.
