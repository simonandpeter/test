# Calliope

Calliope lived in the middle of the third century after Christ, in the days of the emperor Decius. She was remarkable both for the beauty of her body and for the richness of her gifts of soul and spirit. She firmly refused every offer of marriage, having given her life to the Lord Christ, to the service of his word and to the easing of the sick and the suffering.

In the dreadful persecution the cruel emperor Decius loosed against the Christians, Calliope was seized and brought before the local ruler. He, struck by her beauty, tried to win her over with flatteries and promises, wishing to draw her into the shameful life of sinful pleasures and into idolatry. But with heroic constancy and unshaken courage she boldly confessed her faith in Christ, the only Saviour and Redeemer.

Angered, the tyrant ordered her to be whipped without pity. Then she was cut with knives, and her wounds were burnt. At the last her head was cut off, and so she received the crown of martyrdom. The page gives no year and no place, and its Tropar tab carries only a recording of her troparion sung in Greek, without any printed text, so no hymn is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 8 June, and saint.gr's account adds the reign and the shape of her trial. It says she lived in the middle of the third century after Christ, in the years of the emperor Decius, and was remarkable for her natural beauty as well as for the rich gifts of her soul and spirit; that she rejected every offer of marriage with loathing, because she had given her life to Christ, to the teaching of his divine word and to the service and consolation of her sick and suffering brethren.

In the dreadful persecution which Decius, that dreadful persecutor of the Christians, let loose, Calliope was arrested and brought before the local governor, who admired her beauty and tried by flatteries and promises of every kind to entice her and drag her into the unholy life of soul-destroying pleasures and of crippled idolatry. She held instead to Christ as her Saviour and Redeemer; and the tyrant, enraged, ordered her scourged without mercy, then scored with knives, and her wounds burned. At the last they beheaded her, and so made her a gift of the heavenly and incorruptible glory. The page's couplet says that Calliope, overlooking the beauties of the things made, beholds the beauties of the Maker.
