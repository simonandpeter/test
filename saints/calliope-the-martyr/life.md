# Calliope

Calliope lived in the middle of the third century after Christ, in the days of the emperor Decius. She was remarkable both for the beauty of her body and for the richness of her gifts of soul and spirit. She firmly refused every offer of marriage, having given her life to the Lord Christ, to the service of his word and to the easing of the sick and the suffering.

In the dreadful persecution the cruel emperor Decius loosed against the Christians, Calliope was seized and brought before the local ruler. He, struck by her beauty, tried to win her over with flatteries and promises, wishing to draw her into the shameful life of sinful pleasures and into idolatry. But with heroic constancy and unshaken courage she boldly confessed her faith in Christ, the only Saviour and Redeemer.

Angered, the tyrant ordered her to be whipped without pity. Then she was cut with knives, and her wounds were burnt. At the last her head was cut off, and so she received the crown of martyrdom. The page gives no year and no place, and its Tropar tab carries only a recording of her troparion sung in Greek, without any printed text, so no hymn is recorded here.
