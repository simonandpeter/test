# Or of Nitria

saint.gr says this venerable one kept the ascetic life on the mountain of Nitria. At first, and for a few years, he lived as a hermit, with fasting, with prayer and with the study of the divine word. Then Christians who were looking for a spiritual guide discovered him, and from that time the fame of his holiness spread everywhere, so that thousands of visitors came to him to profit by his teaching and his humility.

Afterwards, the page says, the venerable Or built up brotherhoods and founded monasteries, in which spiritual work and bodily work were both cultivated with much zeal. He had besides the gift of speech, and knew when to be silent and when to speak. He died peacefully at ninety years old, keeping the clearness of his mind to his last breath. That is the whole of the entry: it names no country but the mountain, no emperor and no year.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps him on the Julian 7 August and heads him a hermit of the Thebaid, where saint.gr put his asceticism on the mountain of Nitria; the two places are both in Egypt and the books are not reconciled here. By a very great struggle he reached a great perfection, it says. When he had established and enlightened himself thoroughly in solitude, he then founded several monasteries one after another, and was an excellent spiritual guide and teacher of many monks.

Rufinus, who visited him, describes him so: in his clothing he was like an angel of God, a ninety-year-old elder with a long beard as white as snow, of a very pleasant appearance, and his glance shone with something more than human. He saw the angels of God more than once. He took particular care never to utter an untruth. He had great temptations from the demons, but soberly and bravely overcame them all, and he communicated every day.

A disciple once reminded him that Pascha had come and that it ought to be kept. Hearing that, he went outside, raised his hands to heaven and spent three days in prayer without rest; and then he explained to the disciple that this is what keeping Pascha is for a monk, to raise his mind up and unite it with God. He reposed in deep old age, the entry ends, about 390 — the first year this folder has held.
