# Chrysostom of Smyrna, Hieromartyr (1922)

Chrysostom Kalafatis, the national martyr, was born at Triglia on the Propontis in 1867 to Nicholas Kalafatis and Calliope Lemonidou, who had eight children, four boys and four girls; of the boys the firstborn Eugene, born 1865, and Chrysostom survived, and Eugene stood by his younger brother through all his stormy life and followed him at last to martyrdom. Nicholas knew Ottoman law and represented his fellow-townsmen in the Turkish courts, loved church music and was elected elder of the community; his wife Calliope, a devout woman, vowed Chrysostom to the Panagia on the day of Epiphany 1868, when the metropolitan of Prousa visited Triglia. His first teachers at Triglia were the archimandrite and later metropolitan Ioannikios for church matters, Gazis for Greek, Christophoros Moumouzis for Turkish, Nicholas Hatzichrysaphis for French and Papa-Theodosis for church music; he studied at the theological school of Halki (1884–1891), served as archdeacon to the metropolitan of Mytilene, Constantine Valiadis, who became Ecumenical Patriarch as Constantine V in 1897, was protosyncellus of the Great Church, and in 1902 was consecrated by Patriarch Joachim III metropolitan of Drama (1902–1910), where his struggles against the Bulgarian propaganda and for the national spirit troubled — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here. He was martyred at Smyrna in 1922, and the Greek calendar keeps him with the hierarchs [Gregory of Kydonies](/saints/gregory-of-kydonies), [Ambrose of Moschonisia](/saints/ambrose-of-moschonisia), [Procopius of Iconium](/saints/procopius-of-iconium) and [Euthymius of Zela](/saints/euthymius-of-zela) on the Sunday before the Exaltation; the verse calls him protomartyr, sacrificed on an ill-omened day, a multitude of fellow-athletes following his fate.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 13 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2408/saint.aspx); read 23 August 2026.*
