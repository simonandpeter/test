# Gregory of Antioch

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him one short paragraph, and it opens with his nation: he was an Armenian by birth. He was abbot of the monastery of Pharan, under Mount Sinai, and from that house he was raised to the throne of Antioch — against his own will, the entry says — when the blessed [Anastasius of Antioch](/saints/anastasius-of-antioch) was driven from it.

Of his standing among his contemporaries the Prologue offers one witness, and it is a book rather than an act: the blessed patriarch Sophronius, it says, writes of him very praisingly in his Limonar. The entry names no see for that Sophronius and no date for the book, so nobody in this corpus is identified with him here. What it singles out in Gregory himself is his mercy — he was distinguished above all, it says, by an exceedingly great mercifulness, and particularly towards sinners.

He reposed in the Lord in 593. That is the whole of what this source carries: no parents, no birthplace beyond his nation, no account of his years on the throne and no manner of death. The Prologue is a book of selected lives and not a full menologion, and it prints no troparion and no kontakion for any of its saints; no other calendar was read for him, and the Russian, Romanian and Greek rows of his attestation say so.
