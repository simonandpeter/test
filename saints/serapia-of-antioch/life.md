# Serapia of Antioch

She was a girl of Antioch, and she lived in the house of a certain Savina, a senator’s lady, whom she brought to the faith of Christ. A tormentor of Christians named Вирил heard of her and gave orders, and they brought her before him. Since Serapia stood unshaken in her faith, the tormentor ordered that she be thrown into prison, and sent some young men to pass the night with her there and defile her.

Serapia prayed to God in the prison. When the young men reached the doors of it, an angel of God flashed out before them of a sudden with a sword in his hand, and they fell down as dead, wholly senseless and powerless. The next day the tormentor begged Serapia, and by her prayer she brought the young men back to their senses. Ascribing all this to sorcery, Вирил gave orders, and they first burned this holy virgin with candles and after that beat her with staves.

But as they beat her a piece of one staff broke off, flew back and struck Вирил in the eyes, and he was blinded by it. At the last they took off the head of this handmaid of Christ with a sword, and she gave up her spirit to God. The pious Savina honourably buried her body, and from it healing began to flow to many. Serapia suffered in the time of the emperor Hadrian, who reigned from 117 to 138.

This corpus keeps that Savina as [Sabina](/saints/sabina-of-rome), the Roman noblewoman whose own account says she owed her conversion to her servant Serapia, a Christian from Antioch, and that she was beheaded about the year 126, a month after her servant had suffered the same death for Christ. The Prologue gives the servant her own day and her own entry, and this is it.
