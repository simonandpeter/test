# Amandus of Maastricht

Amandus was born near Aquitaine towards the end of the sixth century and studied theology at Rome. From an early age he was a monk in a monastery on the island of Yeu. Because his father did not want his son to be a monk and put obstacles in his way, he took refuge in the city of Tours, where for fifteen whole years he was shut up in a cell in the city walls.

On a pilgrimage to the tombs of the holy Apostles Peter and Paul at Rome he saw the Apostle Peter in a vision, who revealed to him that it was God's will that he preach the Gospel to the peoples of Belgium. So he returned to the city of Bourges, where he was consecrated bishop, and began his missionary work among the peoples of Ghent, Flanders, the Pyrenees and Gascony.

After a second pilgrimage to the apostles' tombs at Rome he was elected, in the year 647, bishop of the city of Maastricht. There he worked to gentle the manners and customs of the heathen peoples, and many times saved his flock from natural disasters by his prayer. With the blessing of St Martin, Pope of Rome, whom the Greek calendar keeps on 13 April, he called local councils against the heresy of Monotheletism.

After so many years of mission and service he enthroned St Remaclus as his successor and resigned the episcopal throne, but went on with his preaching, which he finished in Gascony; and he fell asleep in holiness and in peace in the monastery of Elnon in the year 680. The page prints no hymn for him and no verses.
