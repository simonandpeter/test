# Domnica of Constantinople

Domnica came to Constantinople from Carthage in the reign of the great Theodosius. She was a Greek by religion, and travelled with four other virgins. A revelation concerning her was given to Nectarius, the archbishop and patriarch of the city, who received her and counted her worthy of holy baptism. She then took the monastic habit and gave herself to fasting and to the labours of the spirit, and the life says that many wonders were worked through her.

Once she saved sailors caught in a storm by pouring blessed oil upon the sea, and the sea grew calm. She had the gift of prophecy: she foresaw the death of the emperor and told him of it; she saw the holy angel come and sanctify the nature of the waters; and other glorious mysteries of God, the life says, were disclosed to her. She came to a great old age, living on into the reigns of Leo and of Zeno, and knowing her departure beforehand, she reposed in peace.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same eighth of January, and saint.gr adds what the Romanian notice does not. She was born at Carthage in Africa, it says, and in 384, when she was still young, came to Constantinople with forty other women of her own age for reasons of her own; the emperor then was Theodosius the Great and the patriarch Nectarius.

As soon as the patriarch met her he recognised her pure intentions, baptised her at once and afterwards made her a nun. In the monastic life she was early marked out for her faith, her zeal, her devotion to works of mercy and above all for the purity of her soul; and that moral purity, joined to a humility the page calls admirable, had such an echo in the conscience of the Church that it ranked her in the choir of the saints. The page's verse says that Domnica, whose mind was set on heaven, left the earth and went up to the heavens, as she had loved to do.
