# Euphemianos of Lysi

saint.gr's calendar for 14 November keeps «Άγιος Ευφημιανός ο θαυματουργός», and calls him one more contestant in the great and difficult struggle known as the struggle of the desert. About the middle of the twelfth century, the page says, the preaching of the Pope's men calling Christians to a campaign to free the Holy Places moved many hearts; among them was Euphemianos, who with many other Greeks left his work and enlisted.

That campaign, which we know as the Second Crusade of 1147 to 1149, broke up before it ever reached Palestine. The Greeks in it, under a leader called Auxentios, decided to go on to Jerusalem by themselves, since Christian Europeans still held it, longing to worship at the holy places where the Lord walked and taught and worked wonders and died. They did it; and afterwards they resolved to scatter into the wastes of the Jordan and live as ascetics. But the Muslims, and the Latins who were there, troubled them continually, so one day they gathered and decided to leave.

They went down to the shore, found a ship sailing for Cyprus and boarded it. At Paphos a heavy storm broke the ship on the rocks; all aboard were saved, sitting on pieces of her timber, and came ashore on the Paphian beaches. There they took counsel and agreed to scatter over the island, each to practise asceticism wherever he found a fit place. Euphemianos went on with [Jonas](/saints/jonas-of-pergamos), who stopped at Pergamos, while he himself settled a little further on, between the villages of Lysi and Trouloi, making his home in a cave.

There in his hermitage he passed his days in fasting, vigils and prayer, and the result, the page says, was immediate: divine grace, which heals what is sick and fills up what is lacking, overshadowed him richly in a very short time, and his holy labours sanctified the place. The calendar's apolytikion calls him the lamp of the venerable and the boast of Lysi, and its megalynarion names him the helper of the Cypriots and the physician of the sick.

The page ends with a question of names. The little church of the saint carries an inscription naming him «Θεμονιανός», and the people round about say Phinianos, Thymianos, Thomianos; but the handwritten service of the saint, and Machairas's list — which gives the names of only sixty-seven of the Alamanoi — both have **Euphemianos**, and the others, the page says, are no more than a deformation of the real name. The chapel was built by one Laurentios, abbot of the monastery of Saint Andronikos, which by the oral tradition of the people of Lysi stood in the village of Arsos in the district of Larnaca.
