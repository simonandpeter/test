# Archippus (Shestakov)

Archippus — Athanasius Shestakov in the world — was born in 1825 in a
merchant family in the settlement of Ezdotskaya in the Stary Oskol
district of Kursk province. His contempt for earthly comfort showed early:
minding his father’s shop he gave goods to the poor for nothing, called
money iron with scorn, slept without a pillow, sought solitude and disliked
good clothes, and suffered for these oddities at home; once he ran away
secretly to Kiev, was caught, beaten and brought back, and later hid in a
relative’s cellar until the family agreed to write him a paper of release.
In 1852 he came to the Glinsk hermitage and worked in the bakery, sleeping
five years on its floor before he was given a cell; when a novice was slack
he gladly finished the man’s work, counting it no feat but a chance
thankfully taken to care for a neighbour; he put a chip of wood in his
mouth as he set to work so as not to talk idly, and drawn into empty talk
answered, forgive me, for God’s sake, I know nothing, and began to say the
Jesus prayer aloud; sometimes he played the fool, spilling flour, pouring
out water, dropping dough on the floor, so as to kill the passion of anger
in others by humbly bearing their reproaches and cuffs, and when he was
blamed unjustly he asked forgiveness, that the enmity the demon suggested
should find no place in a brother’s soul. In 1869 he was tonsured with the
name Archippus; they wanted to make him head of the bakery as its most
experienced hand, and he got out of it by calling himself a fool. In 1885
he took the schema and was freed from the bakery, keeping only the reading
of the Psalter for the monastery’s benefactors — and still often turned up
at night to help the bakers; in the nineties he was the first head of the
Far Skete of Glinsk, and there his gifts showed in their fullness.
Wonderful he is with us, said the brethren — he can hardly walk himself,
and when someone needs comforting he comes running, cheerful, sings about
death, cheers you with a word, and you take heart just to see him. By the
foresight given him he saw the evil one’s designs against many of the
brethren and warned them of the danger in time, sometimes gently, not
teaching but as if asking advice, sometimes in open rebuke, his whole being
breathing a father’s love; [Iliodor](/saints/heliodorus-golovanitsky)
said of him, this is one of the ancient elders living in our time. He died
on 27 September 1896; the Council of Bishops of 2017 entered him in the
calendar of the Russian Church, which keeps him on 9 September of the old
style, 22 September, in the synaxis of the Glinsk fathers.
