# Nicholas (Biryukov)

Nicholas Biryukov was a zealous pastor, demanding of himself and others,
whose service was full of active love for his neighbours and care for the
spiritual and moral upbringing of his flock; he was counted a good
preacher who could explain the Gospel text to his hearers simply and give
them instruction. After the revolution the settled life of the
parishioners began to break up, with disorders, drunkenness, riot, murder
and violence, and he taught his people not to yield to revolutionary
passions, not to go to rallies or take part in demonstrations, to avoid
temptation, to pray more and hope in God; despite the anti-religious bans
of the local authorities he celebrated all the services of the rule, the
processions, and visited his parishioners’ houses with the holy icons. In
1918–1919 a cruel civil war went on in the Kamyshlov district, with
churches wrecked, clergy beaten and peaceful people killed; he blessed his
son to serve in the White army, and in his sermons went on calling his
parishioners to steadfastness in the faith, to the saving of their children
from godlessness and the strengthening of the moral foundations of life.
The denunciations survive: one asked particular attention to the monastery
priest N. Biryukov, who in private talk agreed with the writer on not a
single political question and refused to attend the rallies, and asked the
political department of the 21st division to be told of the consequences;
another reported hearing from citizens of the Kamensky works who had been
at the service that the monastery priest — whose name the writer did not
know — had preached an anti-Soviet sermon, which was inadmissible, and
asked the revolutionary committee, standing guard over the interests of
the working people, to take measures. On 10 August 1919 he was arrested by
the Cheka, and the search of his flat the same day turned up his private
correspondence, including an unsent letter to his son in the White army:
greetings, my dear! how are you, and are you beating the red-rag men,
thieves and swindlers? I hear you stand surety for people you do not know
— be careful, brother; mercy is a good thing and a duty for you, but only
where the criminal’s wrong touches you alone, not where the harm is done
to society, the order, the state; remember Christ’s denunciations of the
scribes and Pharisees, whom he, the all-merciful, angrily calls serpents
and a brood of vipers for leading the whole Jewish people from the way of
truth, and how he used force in driving the traders from the temple, again
to protect the whole people; God give you success and well-being, and God
keep you. On the strength of that letter and the two denunciations it was
resolved to prosecute him for pogromist anti-Soviet and antisemitic
agitation, and he was sentenced to the highest penalty at the end of August
or on 1 or 2 September 1919. The pretext for the charge of antisemitism,
beside the letter, was a sermon on the Gospel words “beware of the leaven
of the Pharisees and Sadducees”, after whose exposition he had added: the
Sadducees taught that there is no resurrection of the dead, and today the
enemies of the people say the same at the rallies — no resurrection of the
dead, no paradise and no hell, no God — and warned his children not to go
to such rallies for fear of temptation; asked by the investigator whom he
meant by enemies of the people, he said: all who go against the Christian
Church I call enemies of the people; in my sermon I pointed out that the
enemies of the people are not only the Jews but all who go against the
Christian Church. Another charge was his refusal during the civil war to
bury a Red Army man, which he explained thus: his death is attested by no
one; besides, I am not a parish priest but a monastery priest, and such
persons I hold excommunicated for the outbursts, blasphemies, beatings of
priests and desecrations of churches the Red Army men did last year —
citing the Local Council’s support of Patriarch Tikhon’s appeal of 19
January 1918, which called the warring to repentance, forbade the
perpetrators of bloody reprisals the mysteries of Christ, anathematised
them, and charged the faithful children of the Church to have no dealings
with such monsters of the human race; though the dead soldier belonged by
birth to the Orthodox Church, his part in the fratricidal strife, the
trampling of holy things and the bloody reprisals brought him under the
Council’s anathemas, and in refusing to bury him the priest broke no canon.
On 2 September 1919 — 20 August of the old style, the day the Russian
calendar keeps him — he was shot by the Ekaterinburg provincial Cheka. The
Council of Bishops of 13–16 August 2000 numbered him among the saints; the
life follows the archpriest Valery Lavrinov’s history of the Ekaterinburg
diocese, and the Russian calendar keeps him also in the synaxis of the
saints of Ekaterinburg.
