# Nicholas (Biryukov)

Nicholas Biryukov was a zealous pastor, demanding of himself and others, whose service was full of active love for his neighbours and care for the spiritual and moral upbringing of his flock; he was counted a good preacher who could explain the Gospel text to his hearers simply and give them instruction. After the revolution the settled life of the parishioners began to break up, with disorders, drunkenness, riot, murder and violence, and he taught his people not to yield to revolutionary passions, not to go to rallies or take part in demonstrations, to avoid temptation, to pray more and hope in God; despite the anti-religious bans of the local authorities he celebrated all the services of the rule, the processions, and visited his parishioners’ houses with the holy icons. In 1918–1919 a cruel civil war went on in the Kamyshlov district — the calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. In 1919 he was arrested for a letter to his son in the White army and a sermon denouncing the God-fighters, and shot at Ekaterinburg on 2 September 1919; he was canonised in 2000, and the Russian calendar keeps him on 20 August (Julian).
