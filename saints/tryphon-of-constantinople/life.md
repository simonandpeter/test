# Tryphon of Constantinople

After the death of the patriarch Stephen II, saint.gr says, the monk Tryphon was chosen as his successor in December of 928. He had been living as a monk in some monastery of Asia Minor and was marked out for his reverence and his holiness, and the Church preferred him because his moral advantages were great. The emperor Romanos Lekapenos also took the side of his election, but with a purpose of his own: he reckoned that after a time it would be easy to persuade Tryphon to resign, so as to raise his own son Theophylact to the patriarchate instead.

For that reason Romanos helped Tryphon in his work for the Church with many alms and gifts to monasteries and to poorhouses. But being impatient, in 931, when his son was barely fifteen, he told the patriarch to resign and let the boy take the throne. Tryphon of course did not agree, because the young prince was under age and the thing would make a fearful scandal and a stain upon the Church.

Then Romanos set on the metropolitan of Caesarea, Theophanes — whom the people called «χοιρινό», the pig, for his shamelessness — and by a trick he got the unsuspecting Tryphon's signature on a blank sheet of paper. Above it they drew up his resignation, and so they contrived to drive him out and to put in his place the sixteen-year-old Theophylact, who left a shameful memory by his scandalous conduct. Tryphon withdrew to a monastery, where he lived in holiness three months more and fell asleep in peace.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day as the Greek calendar and tells the whole of the affair, with a different year at the end of it. Under the emperor Romanus, who ruled Byzantium at the beginning of the tenth century, the emperor's son Theophylact was sixteen years old when the patriarch Стефан died. The emperor had vowed that son to the spiritual calling from infancy and wished to raise him to the patriarchate, but was ashamed to do it while the boy was under age; so the throne was taken by Tryphon, a simple but pure and devout old man, and he held it three years. When the son entered his twentieth year the emperor resolved to be rid of Tryphon at any cost. The man of God would not withdraw of his own will, and for one reason only: he held it a great scandal that so young a man should be raised to a place so responsible and so heavy.

Then, by the intrigue of some evil bishop, the guileless Tryphon's signature was coaxed onto a clean sheet of paper, and above that signature a supposed resignation of the patriarch was written out at the imperial palace, which the emperor published. Great trouble followed in the church, for the people and the clergy stood by the man of God; and the emperor removed the old patriarch by force and sent him to a monastery, and raised his son Theophylact to the patriarchate. Tryphon lived in the monastery as an ascetic two years and five months, and departed to the Lord in 933.

That year, and the length of the retirement with it, disagree with the Greek page, which has him elected in December 928, forced out in 931 and living three months more in a monastery, and gives 931 for his death. Nothing is chosen between them: this folder's death now carries the whole of the disagreement, 931 or 933, with both readings named in its note. Neither page gives a year of birth. The emperor, his son and the dead patriarch are named in the entry and are identified with nobody in this corpus.
