# Basil of Ostrog

Basil was born on 28 December 1610 in the village of Mrkonjić in Herzegovina, of a believing family, and received at baptism the name Stojan, which doxologia.ro spells «Stoian». From childhood, the life says, he showed an uncommon love for God and for the life of the Church, and his parents sent him to the monastery of Zavala, where he was given a sound Christian schooling.

Later he entered the brotherhood of the monastery of Tvrdoš near Trebinje, where he was tonsured a monk and given the name Basil. For his ascetic life, his unceasing prayer and his zeal for the faith he was ordained deacon and priest and then raised to the rank of archimandrite; and wishing to go further in the spiritual life he travelled to Mount Athos and visited many holy places of Orthodoxy.

In 1638, though he had not sought the dignity, he was chosen bishop and then metropolitan of Herzegovina. As a shepherd he defended the Orthodox faith firmly in a difficult time, marked by the pressure of the Ottoman Empire and by attempts to spread Catholic influence in the region; by preaching, by prayer and by his own example he strengthened the faithful and became, the life says, a true defender of Orthodoxy.

After the Turks destroyed the monastery of Tvrdoš he withdrew to the monastery of Ostrog, where he continued his labours and his pastoral work. There he raised houses of prayer and formed a strong monastic hearth, becoming abbot and spiritual guide to many; and his life was marked by humility, fasting, prayer and love for people.

He went to the Lord on 29 April 1671, in his cell at Ostrog. Some years after his death his body was found incorrupt, giving out a sweet smell and becoming a source of many wonders; his relics are kept to this day at the monastery of Ostrog in Montenegro, where thousands of pilgrims, Christian and not, come to pray and to ask his help. The Church keeps him each year on 29 April, honouring him as a great wonderworker and a protector of those in suffering.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's notice fills in his see and his birthplace. He was born of devout parents in 1610 in the district of Popovo in Herzegovina, it says, and out of love for the monastic life he left his father's house and entered the monastery of the Dormition of the Mother of God at Trebinje, where he was tonsured. For his virtue he was elected bishop of Zahumlje and Skenderija, taking the metropolitan throne as successor of the bishop Paul and predecessor of the bishop Nikodemos; and that he might know the Byzantine monastic and hesychast tradition he visited the Holy Mountain. He was a true shepherd for the flock of Christ, and the Lord counted him worthy of the gift of wonderworking.

He fell asleep in peace in 1671 and was buried in the town of Ostrog, in Montenegro, near the border with Herzegovina. One sentence of the Greek page cannot be made to stand with the rest of it: it says he took the metropolitan throne in the second half of the sixteenth century, where the same paragraph, and the page's own heading, give his birth as 1610. The disagreement is the source's and is left as it stands; the years this folder records are the ones both sources give, 1610 and 1671.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović — a Serbian bishop writing of a Serbian saint — gives the fullest account of him read for this folder. He was of Popovo Selo in Herzegovina, of parents simple but devout. From childhood he was filled with love for the Church of God, and when he was grown he went to the Trebinje monastery of the Dormition of the Mother of God and took the monastic habit. As a monk he soon became known for the seriousness and the rarity of his ascetic life, for he laid labour upon labour upon himself, each heavier than the last.

Later he was chosen and consecrated bishop of Zahumlje and Skenderija, against his own will. As a hierarch he lived first in the monastery of Tvrdoš, and from there, as a good shepherd, he strengthened his flock in the Orthodox faith, keeping it „од свирепства турског и лукавства латинског“ — from Turkish ferocity and Latin cunning. When he was too hard pressed by his enemies, and when Tvrdoš was destroyed by the Turks, he moved to Ostrog, and there he struggled firmly, hedging his flock about with his unceasing and warm prayers.

He reposed in peace to the Lord, leaving his relics whole and healing, uncorrupted and wonderworking to this day; and the wonders at his grave, the book says, are beyond number. Christians and Muslims both come to those relics and find healing in their heaviest sicknesses and torments. A great assembly of the people is held at Ostrog every year at Trinity — a moveable observance this corpus has no field for, and so it is recorded here and not in his attestation.

**This book says he fell asleep „у XVI веку“, in the sixteenth century, and so becomes a second source for a century the years in this folder cannot hold.** doxologia's life gives the birth as 28 December 1610 and the death as 29 April 1671; saint.gr puts him on the metropolitan throne in the second half of the sixteenth century while giving that same 1610, which is the contradiction already set out above. Nothing was changed on its account: widening the death to reach the sixteenth century would make this folder say he died before he was born, and narrowing or displacing a sourced year is not a reader's act either. It also puts his birth at Popovo Selo, where doxologia names the village Mrkonjić.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 29 апреля, marks the commemoration «(Серб.)» as one it has from the Serbian church, and serves three readings of him which do not agree about when he lived. The longest begins «Святитель Василий родился в 1610 году» in a village of Herzegovina then under Muslim rule, as it was when the page was written; it has him given as a boy to the nearby Zavala monastery, where a quick mind and a great love of the services and of the writings of the fathers gathered him wisdom about heavenly things and earthly, and tonsured with the name Basil while still a youth, then made hierodeacon and priest. The shortest of the three puts him a hundred years earlier: born «в XVI веке в Поповой волости в Герцеговине» of devout parents, tonsured at the Trebinje house of the Dormition, raised to the see of Zahumlje and Skenderija in the second half of the sixteenth century as the successor of a bishop Pavle and the predecessor of a bishop Nikodim, travelling to Athos to sanctify his soul with the wisdom of the ascetic fathers, and buried at Ostrog in Montenegro on the Herzegovinian border. The folder's two years stand where they stood, and the disagreement is this source's own.
