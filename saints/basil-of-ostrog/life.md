# Basil of Ostrog

Basil was born on 28 December 1610 in the village of Mrkonjić in Herzegovina, of a believing family, and received at baptism the name Stojan, which doxologia.ro spells «Stoian». From childhood, the life says, he showed an uncommon love for God and for the life of the Church, and his parents sent him to the monastery of Zavala, where he was given a sound Christian schooling.

Later he entered the brotherhood of the monastery of Tvrdoš near Trebinje, where he was tonsured a monk and given the name Basil. For his ascetic life, his unceasing prayer and his zeal for the faith he was ordained deacon and priest and then raised to the rank of archimandrite; and wishing to go further in the spiritual life he travelled to Mount Athos and visited many holy places of Orthodoxy.

In 1638, though he had not sought the dignity, he was chosen bishop and then metropolitan of Herzegovina. As a shepherd he defended the Orthodox faith firmly in a difficult time, marked by the pressure of the Ottoman Empire and by attempts to spread Catholic influence in the region; by preaching, by prayer and by his own example he strengthened the faithful and became, the life says, a true defender of Orthodoxy.

After the Turks destroyed the monastery of Tvrdoš he withdrew to the monastery of Ostrog, where he continued his labours and his pastoral work. There he raised houses of prayer and formed a strong monastic hearth, becoming abbot and spiritual guide to many; and his life was marked by humility, fasting, prayer and love for people.

He went to the Lord on 29 April 1671, in his cell at Ostrog. Some years after his death his body was found incorrupt, giving out a sweet smell and becoming a source of many wonders; his relics are kept to this day at the monastery of Ostrog in Montenegro, where thousands of pilgrims, Christian and not, come to pray and to ask his help. The Church keeps him each year on 29 April, honouring him as a great wonderworker and a protector of those in suffering.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's notice fills in his see and his birthplace. He was born of devout parents in 1610 in the district of Popovo in Herzegovina, it says, and out of love for the monastic life he left his father's house and entered the monastery of the Dormition of the Mother of God at Trebinje, where he was tonsured. For his virtue he was elected bishop of Zahumlje and Skenderija, taking the metropolitan throne as successor of the bishop Paul and predecessor of the bishop Nikodemos; and that he might know the Byzantine monastic and hesychast tradition he visited the Holy Mountain. He was a true shepherd for the flock of Christ, and the Lord counted him worthy of the gift of wonderworking.

He fell asleep in peace in 1671 and was buried in the town of Ostrog, in Montenegro, near the border with Herzegovina. One sentence of the Greek page cannot be made to stand with the rest of it: it says he took the metropolitan throne in the second half of the sixteenth century, where the same paragraph, and the page's own heading, give his birth as 1610. The disagreement is the source's and is left as it stands; the years this folder records are the ones both sources give, 1610 and 1671.
