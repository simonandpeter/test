# Macarius (Telegin)

In the world he was Макарий Николаевич Телегин, born in a peasant family of the village of Перемениха in the Бузулукский district of the province of Samara; the page gives him no year of birth, and none is recorded here. He had only an elementary education. From 1897 he lived in Moscow, and until 1914 he served in the Чудов monastery in the Kremlin; from the beginning of the First World War he was an army priest at a hospital on the Austrian front and then at the staff of the First Don Cossack brigade, and from 1917 he was a cleric of the Крестовская church of the patriarchal podvorye at Самотёка.

In March 1922 he was arrested in the case of the confiscation of church valuables, after declaring his protest to the commission and calling its members robbers and men of violence. At the trial he said plainly that he was a monarchist by conviction. A priest who shared his cell told afterwards that he was cheerful, and said: «Жду не дождусь встречи с Господом моим Христом» — I can hardly wait for the meeting with my Lord Christ. The verdict of 8 May named him among nineteen who, it said, had openly opposed the confiscation, and that this opposition had expressed itself in the beating of Red Army men.

The public trial of fifty of the arrested had opened on 26 April in the hall of the Polytechnic Museum under the chairmanship of the tribunal's president Бек. On 8 May the Moscow revolutionary tribunal sentenced eleven men to be shot; on 18 May the Politburo struck six names from that list, and five were left: [Christopher (Nadezhdin)](/saints/christopher-nadezhdin), [Alexander (Zaozersky)](/saints/alexander-zaozersky), [Basil (Sokolov, 13 May)](/saints/basil-sokolov-13-may), this hieromonk, and the layman [Sergius (Tikhomirov)](/saints/sergius-tikhomirov).

They were shot at the beginning of June 1922 in what the page calls the ship of the Cheka — the premises of the archive of a former insurance company, where the condemned were shot in the windowless rooms built for its safes in the half-basement — and buried at the Калитниковское cemetery in Moscow. They were canonised in 1998 as locally venerated saints of the Moscow diocese, and numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000. The calendar gives him both a venerable-martyr's rank and a hieromonk's, and both are kept.
