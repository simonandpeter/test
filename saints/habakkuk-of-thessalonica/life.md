# Habakkuk of Thessalonica

saint.gr's calendar for 6 August names the new martyr Habakkuk, and begins by saying what is not known about him. His name is written neither in the Synaxaristes of the Neomartyrs nor in any of the known printed synaxaria, because no account of his martyrdom was preserved. He is known from one line only: a memorandum in codex Ω 89 of the Great Lavra, folio 155, of the seventeenth century, according to which Habakkuk was martyred on 6 August in the year 1628 at Thessalonica.

The page quotes that line: «Κατὰ τὸ ζρλς' ἐν μηνὶ Αὐγούστῳ εἰς τὰς ς' ἐμαρτύρησεν ὁ ὅσιος καὶ νέος μάρτυς ᾿Αββακοὺμ ἐν τῇ μεγαλουπόλει Θεσσαλονίκῃ, εἰς δόξαν καὶ καύχημα τῶν ὀρθοδόξων χριστιανῶν» — in the year 7136, in the month of August, on the sixth, the venerable and new martyr Habakkuk was martyred in the great city of Thessalonica, to the glory and the boast of Orthodox Christians. Nothing in it says how he died, or at whose hands, or how old he was.

From his being called venerable there, the page goes on, it is possible to suppose that Habakkuk was a monk, and most probably one of the Holy Mountain — and that is offered as a supposition and not as a fact, so it is left here as the page leaves it. His name was written for the first time in the Hagiologion of Sophronios Eustratiades, the same man who published the catalogue of the manuscripts of the monastery of the Great Lavra in which the memorandum stands. Pieces of his relics, the page ends, are kept in the monastery of the Genesion in Akarnania.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 6 Αυγούστου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/08/06/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/749/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026.*
