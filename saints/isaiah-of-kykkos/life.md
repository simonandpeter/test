# Isaiah, founder of Kykkos

One of the many famous monasteries of Cyprus is Kykkos, dedicated to the Panagia Eleousa, which keeps the wonderworking icon of the Panagia, painted by tradition by the evangelist Luke — thanks to the efforts and wonderful persistence of an old hermit, Isaiah. Where he was born, who his parents were and what his education, is not known; what is known is that about the eleventh century this holy figure lived in ascesis in a cave in the parts where the great monastery now stands. By tradition, as he prayed on his knees he heard the song of a strange bird that said over and over: Kykkou, Kykkou, the hill will become a monastery, a golden Lady will go in and never come out; the old ascetic heard it but could not grasp its meaning. At that time, as today, many of the people of the plains went up in the summer months to enjoy the coolness of the mountains; the governor of Cyprus, then under the protection of the Byzantine empire, was the duke Manuel Boutoumites — the synaxarion’s life runs on past the point read here.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 10 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/4163/saint.aspx); read 23 August 2026.*
