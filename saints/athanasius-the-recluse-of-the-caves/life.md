# Athanasius the Recluse of the Caves

saint.gr keeps on the second of December a venerable Athanasius whom it calls the Recluse, «ἐν τῷ Σπηλαίῳ» — in the Cave. The whole of its entry for him is one sentence: the venerable Athanasius lived in the Lavra of the Caves and healed those who visited him with faith — «Ο Όσιος Αθανάσιος έζησε στην Λαύρα των Σπηλαίων και θεράπευε όσους τον επισκέπτονταν με πίστη».

Nothing else is there. No century, no name in religion, no abbot, no account of the enclosure he is named for, no single healing told, no relic, no couplet and no hymn. The page does not say which Lavra of the Caves it means, and this folder does not supply one. Three things are attested and no more: the day, the enclosure, and the healings.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 2 December and gives him the whole of what this folder knows about his life, in five sentences and one year. After a long asceticism this holy man died, and the brethren washed him, clothed him and made him ready for burial. He lay dead like that for two days; and then all at once he came alive. When they came to bury him they found him sitting up and weeping.

After that he shut himself into a cell and lived twelve years more, on bread and water alone, without speaking a word to anybody. He fell asleep in the Lord in the year 1176, which is the first year of any kind this folder has carried.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the Julian 2 December and carries two lives of him, which agree. He was a contemporary of Polycarp, archimandrite of the Kyiv Caves monastery, who died in 1182; he was long and heavily ill, and when he died the brethren prepared him for burial. On the third day the abbot and the brethren came to bury him and saw that the dead man had come to life, and was sitting up and weeping. To every question he answered only this: be saved, have obedience in all things to the abbot, repent every hour, pray to our Lord Jesus Christ, to his most pure Mother and to the venerable Antony and Theodosius, that you may end your life well here. Ask no more.

After that he lived twelve years enclosed in a cave and in all that time said not a word to anyone, weeping day and night and tasting a little bread and water only every other day. Only before his end did he gather the brethren and repeat what he had said before, and so he departed peacefully to the Lord, about 1176 — the year the Ohrid Prologue gives too — and was buried in the cave of Antony, which is the Near Caves.

A monk named Babylas, who had suffered for many years from pain and weakness in his legs, was healed at his relics, and the page gives his own words for it: he was lying crying out from the violent pain when the blessed Athanasius came in to him suddenly and said, come to me and I will heal you; and when he wanted to ask how and when he had come, the saint became invisible. He believed the apparition nevertheless, and asked to be carried to the relics, and there he was healed.
