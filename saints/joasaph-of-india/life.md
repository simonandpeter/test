# Joasaph, son of Abenner

What is known of Joasaph comes from the tale called Barlaam, attributed to John of Damascus. In the days of Constantine the Great there was a king in the Indies called Abenner, to whom astrologers said that his newborn son Joasaph would one day embrace the Christian religion. To defeat the prophecy Abenner shut the prince in a remote palace with servants and teachers ordered to tell him nothing of the Christians’ faith, and there Joasaph grew up and was given a great education.

The confinement puzzled him and he demanded its cause; no one would tell him. After many entreaties he persuaded his father to let him go outside under close watch, and on one walk he met two poor old men, gave them generous alms, and they, moved, revealed the secret. From then on he thirsted to learn of the Christian faith, and God blessed him: a Christian priest, Barlaam, got into the palace in the guise of a merchant and taught him. Joasaph was baptised, made his father a Christian too, and later became a monk and taught the Gospel in his own country.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 19 November, second on one line with his teacher and his father, and its life page is titled for the three of them. It has India receive the Christian faith through the preaching of the apostle Thomas, and his father the king an idolater and a cruel persecutor; a star-reader foretold at his birth that he would take the faith his father persecuted, so a separate palace was built for him and orders given that he should hear no word of Christ. Grown to youth, he asked leave to drive out beyond it, saw that there are sufferings and sicknesses and old age and death, and fell into heavy thought on the emptiness of life.

[Barlaam of India](/saints/barlaam-of-india), a hermit of a far desert, learned of him by a revelation of God, came to the city as a merchant with a precious stone that healed sicknesses, set the faith before him in parables and then out of the Gospel, and baptised him. His father tried a disputation in which a sorcerer appeared in the hermit’s likeness, and other means after it, and failed; then gave him half the kingdom. As king he restored Christianity, rebuilt the churches and at the last brought [his father Abenner](/saints/abenner-king-of-india) to baptism. Afterwards he left the kingdom, wandered two years until he found his teacher’s cave, buried him, stayed thirty-five years in the desert and departed to the Lord at sixty; and his successor in the kingdom found the two bodies incorrupt and fragrant and carried them home to a church Joasaph had raised.
