# Hierotheus (Glazkov)

Hierotheus — Nikandr Arsenyevich Glazkov in the world — was born on 27
October 1889 in the village of Pankratovo in the Lezha district of the
Vologda county of Vologda province, to a peasant family. He finished the
course of the zemstvo primary school, and at nineteen, in 1908, under the
igumen Nikon (Chulkov), entered the brotherhood of the Trinity monastery
of Paul of Obnora in the Gryazovets district, which still drew those who
sought the soul’s salvation; there he bore the obediences of baker and
sacristan through a novitiate of some eleven years, and in 1919, at
thirty, was tonsured with the name Hierotheus — hallowed by God — for the
hieromartyr Hierotheus, bishop of Athens. In 1920 Archbishop Alexander
(Trapitsyn) of Vologda ordained him hierodeacon, and from 1921 to 1924 he
was sacristan. The Gryazovets executive committee’s decision of March 1924
to close the monasteries was carried out at Paul’s monastery on 13 April
1924, and the next day all the monks were ordered out and forbidden to
come within five kilometres of it; he took the ruin of his monastery hard,
and wrote in a poem of the evil men who had come and wrecked that fair
garden, tearing everything up by the roots with violence and rudeness, so
that the monastery stood like an orphan. The monks had to find shelter
where they could: in 1925 he was received into the clergy of the town of
Lyubim in Yaroslavl province, ordained hieromonk on 25 May by Sergius
(Melnikov), bishop of Lyubim, and made rector of the cemetery church of
the Entry of the Mother of God, and after three years of zealous service
was given the nabedrennik. In 1930 came a new trial: he was arrested,
imprisoned, and sentenced for anti-Soviet activity to five years in the
camps; when he came back in March 1935 Metropolitan Paul (Borisovsky) of
Yaroslavl and Danilov put him back in his old church, and on 4 March 1936
he was given the gold pectoral cross. A true monk and pastor, a soldier
of Christ faithful unto death, he openly denounced the godless power in
his sermons, prophesying its end and the war to come, gave much attention
to the young, called the faithful to rally round the Church and glorified
the new martyrs the godless power had killed. On 27 July 1937 — the 28th,
the short life says — he was arrested a second time, taken from Lyubim to
the Yaroslavl prison and questioned: an active member of a
counter-revolutionary group, the charge said, who spread rumours of war
and the fall of the Soviet power, agitated among believers and praised
the enemies of the people and the Trotskyites. The investigators demanded
testimony about the clergy he knew, and he flatly refused to give it. On
19 August 1937 the troika of the Yaroslavl NKVD sentenced him to the
highest penalty, and on 4 September 1937 — 22 August of the old style, the
day the Russian calendar keeps him — he was shot at Yaroslavl; his grave
is unknown. He was rehabilitated on 26 April 1989, and on the findings of
the Yaroslavl diocese’s commission the jubilee Council of Bishops of 2000
numbered him among the monk-martyrs in the great host of the new martyrs
and confessors of Russia; on 5 February 2002 researchers from Moscow and
Vologda found a photograph of him at Lyubim, from which an icon was
painted for the Dormition church of Paul’s monastery, and a service was
written that year for his feast. The Russian calendar keeps him also in
the synaxis of the saints of Rostov and Yaroslavl.
