# John (Sofronov)

John Sofronov was born on 1 January 1875 in the village of Pavlovskoye in the Tver province, a peasant's son. He served in the army as a regimental clerk, finished a year of courses at the seminary, and farmed until the persecutions came - it was then, when the Church was being punished, that he asked to serve her and was ordained priest. In 1931 he was sentenced, like most of the clergy, to a year in a labour camp over obligations the state had set him; from 1936 he served in the church of Voloskovo.

The case built against him in 1937 is made of his neighbours' testimony, and what they testified to was his diligence: that he served the liturgy too long on feast days and in the busy season, that he had organised the collection that repaired the church roof, that over a tax of 1,220 roubles he had said, "Oh Russia, what have you come to!" Arrested on 29 July, he answered every accusation the same way through two interrogations held in one day - he admitted no guilt and had conducted no work against anyone. On 20 September the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 23 September 1937. He was glorified among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia in 2000.
