# Cosmas of Zographou

Cosmas was a Bulgarian who fled his parents' house in secret rather than be married, and made for the Holy Mountain. On the road the devil tried to turn him back with a vision of the sea round Athos as a bottomless deep, and long, ardent prayer got him past it. At Zographou he was a novice a long time, then tonsured and made ecclesiarch; at the feast of the Annunciation at Vatopedi the Mother of God let him see her as a Queen attending the services and the refectory, with all the monks ministering to her - the Mistress of the Mountain among her own.

Ordained deacon and then priest, he asked her in prayer how he should be saved, and was answered: silence. He took a blessing and withdrew to a cave as a hermit, where he laboured many years, was given discernment and grace against the demons, and foretold his own end. When he died - in 1323, the life says - the birds themselves gathered at his cave and cried at his burial. After forty days the brethren opened the grave to translate his relics to the monastery, as the custom was, and they were not to be found: God had hidden them.

The Greek calendar makes his family Bulgarian aristocrats and says he learned both Greek and Bulgarian letters. It sets the vision at a feast of Vatopedi, where he saw the Theotokos herself serving the monks, and has the Theotokos, when his abbot meant him for the priesthood, show him in answer to prayer that he might go out into the wilderness. There he heard the confessions of those who came to him and was given foresight, wonders, divine visions and the foreknowledge of his end. At his funeral, it says, quoting an old telling, the wild beasts gathered out of the wilderness and stood round, and when he had been buried each cried out in its own voice in his praise and went back. It calls him the greatest ascetic Zographou has had; his fearsome cell, a cave near the monastery where he fought with the demons, survives. He died in peace on 22 September 1323 and was buried in his cell, and forty days later the relics were sought and not found.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps him on 22 September old style: of a noble Bulgarian family, he fled to the Holy Mountain when his parents meant to marry him, and lived as a hermit and wonderworker in a cave near Zographou, where the Most Holy Theotokos appeared to him more than once. It calls him the greatest ascetic and wonderworker of Zographou, says that the fearful cell in which he fought the demons in silence still stands north-west of the monastery, that he saw in the spirit and described events far off in time and place, and that he died in old age on 22 September 1323.
