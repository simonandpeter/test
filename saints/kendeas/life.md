# Kendeas

Kendeas the wonderworker was one of the many Alaman saints of Cyprus who came to the island from Palestine. At eighteen he went to Jerusalem and became a monk, then withdrew to the desert of the Jordan and lived in a small cave on a steep and trackless height, eating tender shoots of trees as John the Forerunner had. There he began to work wonders: Ananias, a famous ascetic of those parts, begged him to cast the demons out of a local ruler’s son, and after refusing he obeyed and freed the young man. Pressed because of the miracle, he was ordained priest and entered a monastery, but its many visitors drove him back to the desert, until enemies of the faith expelled the hermits living there. After a stormy voyage their ship was wrecked in the harbour of Paphos; all came ashore unharmed and scattered across Cyprus.

Kendeas built his hut on the cliff above the shore at Paphos. The devil fought him there in many ways, the synaxarion says: once he took a man’s shape and fell at his feet asking a blessing, so that the frightened saint fell headlong down the cliff and was unhurt; once he showed him a beautiful woman who begged him to bless her house and there threw off her clothes; once he gave him into the hands of a murderous robber who beat him, stripped him and burned his hut. Crowds came to him for healing and for his words. Wishing to see his fellow ascetic Jonah, who lived near Famagusta, he set out, but was stopped in every village by the sick and, in humility, shut himself in a cave on the road vowing never to come out; an angel then carried Jonah to his cave and back. Doubting whether it had been a dream, he broke his vow, went to Jonah and found the meeting had been real, and returned home.

He healed the sick, brought forth springs where there was no water, often brought rain by his prayer and cast out many demons, and died at a great age. His cave survives near the village of Avgorou, where there is a convent dedicated to him, and a portion of his relics is at the monastery of Kykkos. The Greek entry, after the holy metropolis of Paphos, gives no year.
