# Theodosia, mother of Procopius

Theodosia was the wife of Christopher, a man of senatorial family in the city that was then called Aelia and is Jerusalem. He was a Christian and she held to the Greek religion; and when he died a martyr's death soon after their son was born, she brought the boy up in the worship of the idols, in which she was a zealous servant herself, and had him taught all the outward Greek learning. It was she who took him to Antioch, where Diocletian was, and put him into the emperor's service, where he rose to command — [Procopius](/saints/procopius-of-jerusalem), who was then called Neanias.

When he came back from his first victory she met him and told him to go in and thank her gods, who had helped him; he answered that his own God had helped him. He took her in to the gold and silver idols and asked them which of them had given him the victory, and they were silent; she knelt and asked them herself, by name, and they were silent to her too. Then he broke them in pieces and gave the metal to the poor, and she, forgetting her natural love for her son, went to Antioch and denounced him to the emperor with many tears.

What changed her was watching. Twelve noblewomen who had confessed Christ at her son's prison window were brought out to trial, scourged, hung up, burnt in the sides, their breasts cut off; and Theodosia, standing in the crowd, wept bitterly at their manly endurance, and then the light of the knowledge of the truth rose in her heart and she came out before the governor and cried, I too am a servant of the Crucified, Christ God. The page says this enlightenment came by the prayers of her son, who prayed always for her turning.

The governor was amazed that a woman of her birth should throw away honour, wealth and glory, and asked her who had deceived her into leaving her fathers' gods. She answered that she had been deceived before, by devils, worshipping idols made by men's hands in place of the true God who made heaven and earth; and when he pointed at the tortured women as her deceivers she said they had taught her the truth by the manner of their suffering, for women could not have been made so brave unless the One who strengthened them were the true God.

She was put in prison with them, where her son received her with joy, having known of her conversion by the Holy Spirit. She served the women there, wiping their blood with clean linen and laying healing plasters on their wounds, for she was skilled in medicine; and Procopius taught her the faith and took her out one night to the hidden bishop Leontius, who baptised her.

Brought out again, she was offered mercy on account of her birth and answered that idols are blind, deaf, dumb, and neither walk nor work, and that their worshippers deserve to be like them. The governor had her struck on the mouth, stretched out and beaten with rods and her body torn with iron claws, while the other women sang psalms they had learnt from her son; then all of them were bound with iron chains, led outside the city and beheaded, going to their death, the page says, gladly and as to a wedding feast.
