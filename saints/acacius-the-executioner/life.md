# Acacius, executioner of Paul and Juliana

saint.gr keeps five saints on one page for this day: the brother and sister [Paul](/saints/paul-of-ptolemais) and [Juliana](/saints/juliana-of-ptolemais) of Ptolemais, and with them the three men who were set to torture them. Paul and Juliana had been denounced to the emperor Aurelian as he passed through their city, and when he could not shake their faith he ordered them hard tortured; «και ενώ οι δήμιοι, Στρατόνικος, Κοδράτος και Ακάκιος, τους βασάνιζαν, θαύμασαν την ψυχική τους ανδρεία, ομολόγησαν τον Χριστό και αποκεφαλίστηκαν επί τόπου» — and while the executioners Acacius, [Stratonicus](/saints/stratonicus-the-executioner) and [Codratus](/saints/codratus-the-executioner) were torturing them, they marvelled at the courage of their souls, confessed Christ, and were beheaded on the spot.

That is the whole of what the page records of Acacius: the office he held, the two whose torture converted him, the confession and the beheading. It gives him no country and no city of his own, no year of birth and none of death — the only date on the page is the reign it gives Aurelian, 270 to 275 — no family, no relic and no hymn, and it does not say which of the three spoke first.

The distich the site prints over the three counts them and turns their trade against them: three men who had poured out the blood of many like water pour out the blood of the wise for Christ, by the sword. New executioners, the page says, finished what these three would not, burning the flesh of the brother and sister with lighted torches and then beheading them. The etymology block the page sets over the name reads «(α στερ + κακός) = ο μη έχων κακία, άρα ο ενάρετος», the man without malice and so the virtuous one, which the site prints over an executioner without remark.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 4 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/04/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3792/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
