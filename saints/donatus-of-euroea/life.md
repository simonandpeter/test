# Donatus of Euroea

In the days of the right-believing emperor Theodosius the Great, Donatus was bishop in the city called Evria, in old Epirus. Near that city was a place called Soria with a spring of water in it, and whoever drank of that spring died at once in bitterness. Hearing of it, the bishop went out to the spring with his clergy.

As he came near, a thunderclap was heard, and out of the spring came a great and fearful dragon, which made for the chosen one of God and tried to catch with its tail the legs of the mule the bishop rode. Donatus struck it with the whip he used on the mule as it came up behind him, and the dragon died on the spot; and the clergy about him and the people, seeing the wonder, gathered wood and burnt the beast, lest the air be poisoned, for it was very large.

No one dared drink the water of that spring. So Donatus made a prayer, blessed the spring, drew water first himself and drank; then he told everyone to drink without fear, and they all drank their fill and took no harm, and went home glorifying God.

Going once into another place, dry and waterless, and seeing the people there parched with the great heat and faint with thirst, he dug a small pit with his own hands, prayed to God, and brought out a spring that ran plentifully ever after. He brought water down from heaven too: in a time of heat and great drought he prayed to the Lord, and at once a great rain poured out, watered the ground well, and a plenty of fruits followed on his prayers.

In those times the daughter of the emperor Theodosius was very ill, tormented by an unclean spirit, and the emperor and empress were in great grief for her, for she was their only child. That is where the long middle of the life begins, with the summons of the bishop to the court; and it ends with him praying outside the city for rain on the dry ground.

The rain fell so heavily that the people said a second flood was coming, and the emperor was sorry for the bishop, who had only one garment on and would fall ill; but when Donatus came back into the imperial court his clothes were seen to be perfectly dry, without a trace of wet, for not one drop of rain had fallen on him, and everyone marvelled. The emperor rejoiced over such a pleaser of God, thanking God that in the days of his reign the Lord had sent such a lamp of the world and worker of wonders.

After much talk with him the emperor sent him back to his own see, and gave him everything needed for the building and adorning of the new church the saint wished to raise at the place named before. Donatus went home, built a most beautiful church at the emperor's charge, and prepared his own grave in it; and coming to a full old age, he departed to the Lord. The page gives no year, and dates him only by the reign of Theodosius.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and makes him the patron of Paramythia in Thesprotia. saint.gr sets him under Theodosius the Great, whose reign it dates 379 to 395: born about the year 330 at Euroea and educated at Bouthroton in Epirus, ordained bishop of Euroea at the age of thirty, and hierarch, it says, for sixty years; he took part in the Second Ecumenical Council. Other sources, it adds, hold that he came from the West, the name being widespread there. And it warns that the Latin sources confuse him with his namesake the bishop of Arezzo in Tyrrhenia, who was martyred under Julian the Apostate — an easy confusion, since the bishopric of Euroea belonged ecclesiastically to the West though politically it was Byzantine.

Its telling of the dragon has the detail the Romanian life wants. Near Euroea was a village called Soreia with a spring, and whoever drank of it died. When Donatus learned of it he took other priests with him and went to the spring; and as he arrived a thunderclap was heard, and a dragon that had its lair in the water appeared before him. The saint turned his eyes on the beast, took in his hands the cord with which he used to strike the ass he rode, struck the beast across the back, and it fell dead on the ground. Then he blessed the spring, drank of it first himself, and urged the rest to drink without any fear; and they drank and were glad and went home safe.

The fame of his wonders reached Theodosius, who called him to Constantinople to heal his daughter, who suffered from a demon. Donatus healed her, and the emperor gave him a place at Omphalion in Epirus and money to raise a church; ruins of an ancient church survive there, of the age of the Despotate of Epirus, and the page thinks it likely that the later building stands on the foundations of his, since early Christian material was found in the digging. He died, having driven on to a great old age, probably in 388, and was buried near that church in a monument which tradition says he had prepared himself.

The relics travelled. In 604 the bishop John of Euroea, driven out by the barbarian raids of those years, took refuge with his clergy at Kassiope in Corfu and carried the body of Donatus with him; in 1125, in the time of the crusades, the Venetian doge Domenico Michelli took it to the church of the Panagia and of Donatus at Murano in Venice; and on 29 September 2000 a part of it was brought back from Murano to Paramythia and laid in the great church that bears his name. His memory is honoured particularly in Thesprotia, Preveza and Ioannina, and at Lefkada on 7 August, a day with which a manuscript of the Great Lavra agrees.
