# Theodore of Tamasos

Theodore lived in the first century, the Greek synaxarion says, one of the small missionary band — the others were Heraclidius and Mnason — who from a cave in populous Tamasos first set about scattering the darkness of idolatry. He was born in that great city, famed then beyond Greek lands for its copper and its rich mines. His parents were pagans and his father a maker of statues of the gods, which his son Theonas — his name before baptism — sold in the market to keep them. As a young man Theonas was a close friend of Mnason, whom Luke in the Acts calls an old disciple, and went with him to Rome to settle a dispute between the pagans of Politiko and the village of Pera over which of their gods was the greater. There the two met some of the apostles of the Seventy — which, the entry says, is not known — and heard from them of the new faith, and thirsting for more they went not home but to Jerusalem, to find Peter, the chief of the apostles, and [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian). From these eyewitnesses they learned in detail of the birth, the baptism, the teaching and wonders, the cross, the resurrection and the ascension of Christ, and of his coming again to judge; and when they had been baptised they sailed for Cyprus to meet Paul, Barnabas and Mark and their follower [Heraclidius](/saints/heraclides-of-tamasos), whom the apostles had already taught and baptised, and they stayed with them.

A few days later the apostles left for Paphos. Mnason, the synaxarion says, stayed with his teacher Heraclidius, and Theodore parted from them both and lived as an ascetic, for thirty-eight years, with strict abstinence for his rule, unceasing prayer for his support and humility for his helmet. When he felt his death coming he called Rhodon, of the same missionary band and the third bishop of old Tamasos, and charged him to write down the works of Heraclidius and Mnason for the building up of the faithful, handing him the memoirs he had himself written of the two; then he called some of his spiritual children, counselled them, and gave up his soul peacefully on 4 October. Heraclidius and Mnason and the other brethren buried him with great sorrow in the tomb where his father Chrysippus had been laid before.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 4 October.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 4 Οκτωβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2593/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026.*
