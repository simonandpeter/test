# Benedimus of Athens

saint.gr keeps eight martyrs on one page for 18 May, all of them martyred, it says, in the years of the emperor Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251. [Heraclius](/saints/heraclius-of-athens), [Paulinus](/saints/paulinus-of-athens) and Benedimus were from Athens, and there they fought against the error of the idols and against the philosophers who made war on the Christian religion; they were seized, tortured and at the last beheaded. The page names Benedimus patron of Herakleion, and spells his name Benedimus in its calendar line and Benedemus in its narrative.

The couplet printed for the two of them makes Paulinus speak first: seeing me, Benedimus, your own Paulinus cut down, he says, be cut down with me. Of the others on the page it says that Peter was of Lampsacus and was crushed with chains and staves, and that Paul and Andrew, Mesopotamians and soldiers of Decius, became soldiers of Christ at Athens and were stoned with Dionysius and Christina. The company's verse calls them an eightfold band of victors raised from earth to the city above.
