# Nicetas the Goth

Nicetas came of the nation of the Goths, settled beyond the river Ister — the Danube, the geographer Meletius says, from where it joins the Sava to the Black Sea — in the years of Constantine the Great. From childhood he was taught the holy faith by the Gothic bishop Theophilus, who often reminded him of the apostle’s words: continue in the things you have learned — from a child you have known the holy Scriptures, which are able to make you wise to salvation through faith in Christ Jesus (2 Timothy 3:14–15); and so it was. When the ruler Athanaric seized him and threatened him to make him deny Christ he stayed immovable in what he had learned from childhood, and confessed Christ boldly before the ruler, who in his fury had his bones broken in the most horrible way and then, such was the barbarians’ hatred, had him thrown into the fire, where he died; but the fire, by God’s will, spared his body, which a devout Christian took and kept in a casket; its verse says, you burn, Nicetas, and become victor, or rather a fire-bearing victor, thrown into the furnace on the fifteenth. The Romanian Lives say the holy faith of Christ grew among the barbarians from day to day and Nicetas, of great and glorious family in that land, brought many to Christ; the Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 15 September — the Romanian as Nicetas the Roman, with a troparion and kontakion — and the Greek keeps with him the martyrs who suffered in Gothia with him.
