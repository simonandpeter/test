# Stamatios of Volos

Stamatios was a peasant from the country around Volos in Thessaly. When an
inhuman agha collecting the imperial taxes abused the people, he went with a
few companions to Constantinople to complain to the vizier.

His sharp accusations against the agha offended the sultan’s grandees, and
they had him arrested. First they tried flattery to make him a Turk,
promising wealth, glory and honour; the martyr cried out: “My wealth, glory
and honour is my Christ!” He was tortured and at last beheaded in front of
Hagia Sophia in 1680. So, the Prologue says, this soldier of Christ was
crowned with the martyr’s wreath. The Russian church calendar prints his
page on 16 August of the old style, 29 August, noting that he is not in the
present Russian menologion, and tells it in one line: a man of Volos in
Thessaly, slandered as having accepted Islam, who bravely confessed himself
a Christian and was beheaded at Constantinople in 1680.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same menologion day, and where the Prologue gives a line saint.gr gives the whole trial. The new martyr Stamatios, it says, came of a village of Volos, Agios Georgios, in the province of Demetrias. An agha once came to their district to collect the head-tax, and was very oppressive in gathering it, acting arbitrarily and tyrannising and wronging the Christians. In despair the inhabitants decided to send a deputation to Constantinople, to the Sublime Porte, in the hope of finding justice; and Stamatios was one of its members.

They came before the vizier and began to complain of the agha's injustices; but the vizier, being a friend of the tax-collector, ordered them thrown out with pushing and blows. Some of the deputation, the saint among them, protested loudly at the injustice. Then some Turkish officials standing by, friends of the agha, singled him out and took him to the vizier, bearing false witness and slandering him as having become a Turk and now appearing as a Christian.

The saint denied the charge vehemently before the vizier, who sent him to the judge competent in such cases; and there, examined again, he denied it again and said it was a slander. The judge then said to him: «Κι αν δεν έγινες, γίνε τώρα» — and if you did not become one, become one now. The martyr answered in a great voice that God forbid he should be so foolish as to deny his Christ, and that it was better to die and be with his Christ than to live in this world with ten thousand pleasures and glories.

Seeing his steadiness the judge sent him back to the vizier, who tried flatteries, promises, honours and offices, and even promised to make him his own adjutant. A second time the saint confessed firmly that his wealth and glory and honour was his Christ, who had a dwelling for him in the heavens, and that the vizier's honours were corruptible and vain and perished quickly along with those who pursued them. He was imprisoned and tortured, brought out again some days later, and answered for the last time that though the vizier condemn him to ten thousand deaths he would not deny his Christ.

Then the vizier in rage handed him to the eparch to be put to death, and they beheaded him on 16 August 1680, on a Monday, before the royal palace at Hagia Sophia. His martyrdom was written by the monk Iakovos the Athonite in that same year, and is in codex 805 of the monastery of Vatopedi; a portion of his relic is at the monastery of the Archangels on Pelion in Magnesia.
