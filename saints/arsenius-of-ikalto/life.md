# Arsenius of Ikalto

Arsenius of Ikalto was a translator, a scholar, a hymnographer, a philosopher and a great defender of the Georgian Christian faith. His father was Ibadi Vachnadze, a wise and learned man who spoke Greek fluently. Arsenius directed the academy at the monastery of Ikalto and was the teacher of the holy King David the Builder. Few details of his life have been kept, the page says, but it is known that he lived in the eleventh and twelfth centuries and was a younger contemporary of Saint Ephrem Mtsire, Ephrem the Lesser.

He received both his first and his higher education in Byzantium, at the Mangana monastery, which had been founded by the Byzantine emperor Constantine IX Monomachos. At that academy he completed one of his most important works, a translation of the chronicle of the Byzantine historian George Hamartolos — a history in nine volumes running from Adam to the year 842. At Mangana too he translated into Georgian a volume of dogmatic and polemical writings, which he called his *Dogmatikon*, and in the years that followed works translated by other authors were added to the book.

When his studies at Mangana were finished he moved to the Black Mountains near Antioch, to continue his labours under the guidance of Saint Ephrem the Lesser; and after Ephrem's repose he returned to Mangana to go on with his translations. In 1114 King David the Builder called him back to Georgia, to the Gelati Academy in the west, where he translated the Nomocanon, the Byzantine collection of church law, from the original Greek into Georgian. Afterwards he returned to Kakheti in eastern Georgia, where he founded an academy at the monastery of Ikalto; and he took part in the Council of Ruisi-Urbnisi, called by King David the Builder. He was buried at Ikalto, beside Saint Zenon, the founder of the monastery of Ikalto.
