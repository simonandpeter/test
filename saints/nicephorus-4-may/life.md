# Nicephorus (4 May)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 4 mai reads «Sfântul Cuvios Nichifor» — Nicephorus the venerable — and his page on that site opens no Viață tab: it carries icons, a list of articles about him, and the epistle and gospel appointed for the day, and no text of its own.

So the rank is all that is recorded: a monk, kept by the Romanian calendar on this day. No century, no country, no monastery and no hymn is printed, and none is set down here.

The Serbian church keeps a venerable Nicephorus on this same menologion day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović — unlike doxologia — says who he was. He had first been a Roman Catholic, it says, and afterwards came over into Orthodoxy. He struggled as a monk on the Holy Mountain together with the wise Theoliptos, and he was the teacher of the renowned [Gregory Palamas](/saints/gregory-palamas); and he wrote a work on the prayer of the mind.

His teaching, as this book gives it, is to gather the mind together and compel it to go down into the heart and stay there; and once it is settled there it should not stand empty but pray without ceasing — „Господе Исусе Христе, Сине Божји, помилуј ме!“ — and never fall silent. Through that, he taught, a whole train of virtues comes to dwell in a man, love and joy and peace and the rest, and for their sake every petition he afterwards makes to God will be granted.

**He reposed in peace in the Lord in the fourteenth century**, which is the first date this folder has carried. The identification rests on the day, the rank and the name: doxologia prints only «Sfântul Cuvios Nichifor» for 4 mai and opens no life at all, and this book heads a venerable Nicephorus on the same day. Nothing in the Romanian entry disagrees, because the Romanian entry says nothing.
