# Apostolos the New of Pelion

saint.gr says that Apostolos the New was born at Agios Laurentios of Pelion in 1667. His father was called Kostas Stamatiou and his mother Melo. At fifteen he was left an orphan, and in 1682 he went to Constantinople, where he worked in a tavern. He had already been four years in the City when the following thing happened in his own country: the inhabitants of Agios Laurentios and the district, being harshly oppressed by a heavy and unjust taxation, decided to appeal to the Sultan's commissioners, who had jurisdiction over those villages.

They did obtain some reduction of the tax and went home; but the Voivode not only refused to recognise the commissioners' documents and rejected them as forgeries, he also arrested three of the villagers' committee who had gone to Constantinople, bound them like criminals, took them himself to the City and had them imprisoned as guilty of high treason.

When their countrymen learned of it they at once sent a committee to the City, meaning to appeal to the queen mother herself. Not knowing how or where to apply, they were helped by Apostolos, who knew the Turkish language well; he took the petition himself and gave it to a high officer of the Sultan.

But that man had already received the Voivode's slanders. Enraged, he ordered Apostolos arrested and handed to the Voivode to be punished for his impudence. The Voivode had him chained and demanded four years' head-tax of him for the time he had been away from his village; and being afraid that the rest of the committee would appeal to the queen mother, he thought of releasing the four prisoners after all. A jealous old countryman of the saint, however, grudging that an insignificant and poor boy should be reckoned the benefactor of his birthplace, slandered him as having set the whole business in motion. So the Voivode ordered him tortured to death.

While he was being tortured without pity he managed one day to free one foot and tried to limp away, but the noise of the chains betrayed him. The Voivode came and began striking him with an axe, and the saint said to him: «τι με χτυπάς με τόση σκληροκαρδία; Ή δεν γνωρίζεις ότι είμαι και από σένα και από τους υπηρέτες σου καλύτερος;» — why do you strike me with such hardness of heart? Do you not know that I am better than you and than your servants? That was taken for a confession of Islam, as though he were saying he was a better Mohammedan than they, and the Voivode at once ordered him circumcised. The saint resisted bravely: «εγώ Χριστιανός είμαι και δεν αρνούμαι την αγία μου πίστη».

They tortured him then and shut him in the prison of criminals, and afterwards brought him before the chief religious leader of the Muslims and the other officials, who tried flatteries and promises of office, riches and honours. Because he stayed firm they took him to the vizier, who promised more still. The saint did not even attend to their words, but told them: do not delay and waste your time; whatever you mean to do, do it quickly. Do you want to burn me? Let me gather the wood myself and make the fire ready. Do you want to hang me? Let me prepare the noose with my own hands. Do you want to behead me? Give me the sword and I will sharpen it as much as it needs.

Unable to bear him any longer the vizier ordered him beheaded. They tortured him all that night, and before dawn led him to the place of execution. Some Christians he met on the way he greeted humbly, asking them to forgive him; they understood the reason, followed in fear from a distance, and were eyewitnesses of his end. At the gate of the Yeni mosque towards the Golden Horn he knelt and waited for the executioner, who to make the execution more painful struck him three times on the neck with the sword and then, seizing his hair with his bloody hand, beheaded him. He was nineteen years old.

While the firing party sat a little apart from the holy relic, the page goes on, a star came down from heaven, stood over it and formed a cross; and at the same time a multitude of people appeared and surrounded the martyr. Thinking they were Christians come to steal the body they rushed at them, but on approaching saw nothing but the saint's holy body.

As day broke the executioners feared the Christians would learn what had happened and ask for him, so they threw the body into the sea and took the head to the vizier as proof. The body, instead of sinking, came out of the Golden Horn afloat, and where it came to shore remains unknown. The holy head the Christians he had met asked for through the Patriarchate, put in a silver casket and laid in the church of Saint Demetrius at Tatavla; and later Dositheos of Seleucia, a countryman of the martyr, sent it to his birthplace, to the martyr's own house, which had been built up into a church, after a wonderful prompting from the saint himself.
