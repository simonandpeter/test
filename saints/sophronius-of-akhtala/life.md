# Sophronius

Sophronius was born to devout and virtuous parents in 1738 in the village
of Lotsion in the district of Deraini of the province of Chaldia in Pontus;
his father, a priest, was George Sertaridis, his mother Barbara, and he had
four sisters and a brother Demetrius, also a priest. Symeon at first, from
a young age he nursed a great love for divine things and especially the
monastic life, and still young went as a novice to the monastery of St
George Choutoura, after three months to Soumela, and after three years to
Vazelon, learning at all three the sacred letters and in great measure the
monastic life; there he was tonsured Sophronius and then ordained priest.
After seven years the abbot Ignatius sent him, in 1776, to the mine of
Akhtala in Iberia, where some five hundred metal-workers had settled and
made a village called Dal-ver, precious stones; by his holy life he won the
inhabitants’ respect, and on their own initiative they made him bishop on
29 October 1777, with his seat at the monastery of the Panagia there. He
governed the bishopric until 1794, when a barbarian tribe attacked the mine
and plundered it, having first destroyed everything; Sophronius was taken
captive and sold to a woman of the Latin confession, who set him free and
sent him by sailing-ship to Trebizond. From there he went back to Vazelon,
where he lived as an angel in the flesh; but because of the envy of the
abbot Jeremiah he left for his own homeland, and there breathed his last.
His relics, full of fragrance, were translated to the monastery of Vazelon
in 1824.
