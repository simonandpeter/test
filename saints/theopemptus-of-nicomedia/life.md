# Theopemptus of Nicomedia

In the first year of the persecution he raised against the Christians, the emperor Diocletian (284-305) came from Rome into the city of Nicomedia, made many idols there and worshipped them; and a few days later Theopemptus, the man of God and bishop, was seized before the city and brought before him. He told the emperor that the idols of silver and gold and wood and stone he worshipped were no gods, for they can neither breathe nor speak nor do anything good or ill, and that the heavenly and almighty God made heaven, earth and sea and all that is in them.

The emperor, vexed, told him to stop his many words and sacrifice to the god Apollo. Theopemptus answered that he would never sacrifice to such gods and would not fear his torments, for it is written: fear not those who kill the body and cannot kill the soul; and that having power over his body the emperor might do what he liked. He was ordered burnt alive in a furnace. The soldiers heated it from morning till noon, and the bishop asked them to stop a little, saying he would show the power of the Lord his God for whose holy name the furnace was heated to destroy him; then he threw himself in and sat down in the middle of it, and they went away supposing he would burn at once. At midnight he came out.

Then the magician [Theonas](/saints/theonas-called-synesius) was brought to undo what he called the Christians' spells, and was converted by what he could not undo, falling at the bishop's feet and saying that he was a Christian. In the prison Theopemptus taught him the faith, named him Synesius, which is to say full of understanding, and baptised him there. The two of them, the page says, finished their martyr's contest under the emperor Diocletian at Nicomedia.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same fifth of January, with Theonas beside him. saint.gr dates the edict he answered: Diocletian signed it on 23 January 303, and the bishop Theopemptus was the first to confess Christ crucified and to reproach the emperor for his error, knowing at once what waited for him. The page counts the tortures as a series, each attended by a wonder — a lit oven, out of which he came alive and unhurt; then the putting out of one eye; then deadly poisons, from which the grace of God kept him unharmed, so that in the end they beheaded him. It adds that the bravery, the unshaken faith and the moral brightness in the martyr's face spoke persuasively to the heart of [Theonas](/saints/theonas-called-synesius), the pagan magician who had prepared the poisons.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 5 января old style and tells the whole of it, his and his companion's together. He was bishop in Nicomedia under Diocletian, and speaking out against the worship of idols he defended the faith of Christ. Called before the emperor he refused to do what was required of him and bow to the idol of Apollo. He was thrown into a white-hot furnace and by the power of God remained alive; the emperor went by night with a detachment of soldiers to the furnace and saw him there indeed, living and praying to God. Setting the wonder down to sorcery, Diocletian ordered him starved.

The sorcerer [Theonas](/saints/theonas-called-synesius) was brought to test him. He made a poison, put it in a flat loaf and offered it to the bishop, and it did him no harm at all; a second and stronger poison did no more, and at that the sorcerer himself believed in Christ. He was thrown into the prison with the bishop, who instructed him and baptised him, giving him the name Synesius, which is to say filled with understanding.

In the morning Diocletian called the bishop again and urged him once more to deny Christ; and seeing the unbending firmness of the man, he gave him over to many heavy torments, after which he was beheaded. Theonas, who refused to sacrifice to the idols, was buried alive in a deep ditch. This was at Nicomedia, in the year 303.
