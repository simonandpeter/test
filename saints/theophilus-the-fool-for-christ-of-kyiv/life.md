# Theophilus the Fool for Christ of Kyiv

In October 1788, in the little town of Makhnovka in the province of Kyiv, Euphrosyne, wife of the priest Andrei Gorenkovsky of the church of the Nativity of the Mother of God, bore twins: Thomas and Callinicus. Thomas would not take the breast, turning his face away every time, and to keep him alive his mother had to feed him on boiled potato, radish and carrot, which he took gladly; but the moment a vessel of milk touched his lips he turned away and wept. His mother grew cold towards him, and more than once tried to drown him; each time, the life says, God preserved the child.

His father gave him secretly to a wet-nurse, who fed him on bread soaked in watered honey, cared for him as her own and carried him to church every Sunday to communicate. When the priest felt death near he left the boy with a miller, who passed him to a well-off childless peasant; that benefactor died suddenly, and his widow, wanting to marry again, persuaded the village priest to take the child. Thomas showed no taste for the games of his age: he kept apart, and the church became the dearest shelter he had, so that he was often found praying before its closed doors.

He gave away what little he had — seeing a boy in rags in the street he took off his own shirt and went home in his coat alone, and was beaten for it. He loved the Psalter and learned the psalms he loved by heart. When he was seven the priest began to teach him letters, and soon fell ill and died. The churchwarden, supposing the mother's hatred spent, took him home; Euphrosyne was splitting wood, and threw the axe at him, and its edge split his right shoulder. While the wound healed the warden found that an old widowed priest at the Bratsky monastery in Kyiv was the boy's uncle, and took him there; Thomas was entered at the theological academy and grew up of exemplary conduct.

Struck with an incurable illness, Euphrosyne repented of her cruelty, and not long before her death Thomas came to her house and had the consolation of seeing her at peace with him. "Forgive me, my son," she cried, "forgive me, hard and foolish as I was; my mind was darkened and I did not know the weight of what I did. Do not curse your bad mother, and remember me, a sinner, in your unceasing prayers." With that she held him hard to her breast, signed him with a great cross, and gave up her spirit in peace.

Thomas did what the school asked of him, but felt no call to higher study, and chose the church for his university, training his mind in reading and chanting and in unceasing prayer. When his uncle died he was left without means or shelter; he left the academy in 1810, was made a teacher at Chyhyryn, and, his voice being too weak, was moved the same year to be sacristan at Obukhiv. Soon after, in the middle of the war of 1812, he entered the Kyiv Bratsky monastery as a brother, and served there as baker, cook, assistant to the infirmarian, sacristan and bell-ringer, which last he loved best.

On 11 December 1821 he was tonsured with the name Theodoret; he was made sacristan of the vestry, and on 30 September 1822 ordained hierodeacon. He stayed a hard faster and kept nothing in his cell, and would go two or three days without food in order to give food and money to pilgrims and the poor. On 6 February 1827 he was ordained hieromonk and made steward of the Bratsky monastery; asking to be relieved of the office and to live as a hermit in the caves dug by Theodosius at Lesnyky, and being refused, he took up instead the great cross of folly for Christ.

He set an old coffin in his cell but never slept in it, keeping stores and vessels there; on the day of his tonsure into the great schema, when he received the name Theophilus, he sewed his schema cowl out of scraps of rag and wore it so until his death. Crowds followed him for a single word. The academy's superiors disliked the dirty, ragged monk Theophilus and complained daily to the metropolitan that the curious were disturbing the school, and the blessed one, sharply rebuked, hid in the forest by day and came home after sunset — but the crowd found him there too, and waited on the banks of the Dnieper to walk him back to his cell.

On 1 December 1844, old and weak, he asked to be moved from the Bratsky monastery to the Kyiv Caves Lavra; Metropolitan Philaret instead sent him to the Holosiiv hermitage near Kyiv. His fame drew crowds there, and in 1848 the persecutions began again over his strange way of life: the metropolitan forbade him to serve and allowed him only to communicate each Saturday, vested, "for the salvation of his soul". He was moved to the orchard called Novopasichny, and though the church was far off he never missed a service, reaching it before the first stroke of the bell.

After half a year there he was moved, on 29 April 1849, to the Kytaiv hermitage near Kyiv, and there he did not leave his folly but increased it: walking in the forest he would choose a great stump and spend whole days and nights kneeling on it, sighing at the corruption of the age and praying for the forgiveness of a sinful world. He spun wool, knitted stockings and wove linen, which he gave chiefly to icon painters for their work, and said the Psalter by heart as he worked. His cell was heaped with things and thick with dirt, and asked why, he answered: so that everything around me may remind me without ceasing of the disorder in my soul.

He had the habit of giving visitors some trifle — a shard, a splinter, a rotten apple, a pear, a piece of pie, a cucumber, a rag, a prosphoron, a candle-end — which carried a prophetic hint of what awaited them. Shortly before the end he moved from Kytaiv to Holosiiv at the superior's warm invitation, telling him: be it as you wish; I shall come to you, but remember, I shall return to die in the old nest. So it fell out, and on 15 July, at the metropolitan's order, he was back at Kytaiv. In his last days he came daily to the Lavra to serve an akathist to the Mother of God before the wonderworking icon called of Częstochowa.

In October 1853 he stopped taking food and would only stand at prayer; his legs began to swell and he took no notice, and increased his praying the more. In peace and without trouble he gave his soul into God's hands on 28 October 1853, at five in the afternoon. The news drew crowds to the Kytaiv hermitage from the neighbourhood and from towns further off; the burial procession went round the church of the Holy Trinity, and the coffin was let down into the earth to the chanting of the Trisagion and the tolling of the Kytaiv bells. His relics are kept at the Kytaiv monastery.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 28 October, and says almost nothing of him. saint.gr's line is «Άγιος Ιερομάρτυρας Θεόφιλος, της Λαύρας των Σπηλαίων Κιέβου», and the notice behind it is one sentence and a disclaimer: he lived as a fool for Christ, «Δια Χριστόν σαλός», and «δεν έχουμε άλλες λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Αγίου» — we have no further details of the saint's life. So the Greek page agrees with this folder on the day, on the Caves Lavra of Kyiv and on the folly for Christ, and differs from it in one word: it calls him hieromartyr. It gives no year and prints no hymn.
