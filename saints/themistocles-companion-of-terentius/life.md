# Themistocles, companion of Terentius

saint.gr keeps a company of martyrs at Carthage in Africa on 10 April and names every one of them. Its whole account of the martyrdom is two sentences long: «Οι Άγιοι Μάρτυρες Αφρικανός, Θεόδωρος, Μάξιμος, Πομπήιος και Τερέντιος μαρτύρησαν το έτος 250 μ.Χ., επί αυτοκράτορα Δεκίου (249-251 μ.Χ.) και ηγεμόνος Φουρτουνιανού, στην Καρθαγένη της Αφρικής μαζί με άλλους τριάντα εννέα Χριστιανούς» — [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius), [Theodore](/saints/theodore-companion-of-terentius), [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius), [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius) and [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa) were martyred in the year 250, under the emperor Decius and the governor Fortunianus, at Carthage in Africa, together with another thirty-nine Christians. The page then prints the names of those others, and forty names stand in the list; the day's own line says forty others as well. Both readings are the page's and it reconciles neither.

The martyrdom it tells is one martyrdom for the whole company. They were seized because they were Christians and were tried with many torments: first they were beaten beyond all measure, until their inward parts could be seen; then their hips were pierced with red-hot nails, and vinegar and salt were thrown upon the wounds. But when they had prayed, they destroyed the wooden images, the altars and the temples of the idolaters. At the last, after the dreadful torments, they were all beheaded. The page's couplet for the rest of them says: let us see the contestants they cut down, and how many — a fourfold ten, and others besides.

The page gives each of the company a heading of his own with a gloss on his name, and the names are largely those of the Greek past. It says nothing else of any one of them: no city any of them came from, no trade, no age, no order in the Church, and nothing at all that tells one from another beyond the name. Its three hymns address them all together. Because the page enumerates the company, the corpus keeps one entry for each man, and each entry says only what the page says of him.

The page's heading for him is «Αγιος Θεμιστοκλής», and it glosses the name — from θέμις with κλέος: the glorious defender of what is right. Under the gloss it names who else has borne it: the great Athenian statesman and general, 525 to 461 before Christ. Of the martyr it says nothing beyond that.
