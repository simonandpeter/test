# The Monk-martyrs of Belogorsk (1918)

Archimandrite Varlaam and the brethren of the Belogorsk monastery killed with him in 1918 — the Russian calendar names them: Antony, Sergius, Elijah, Vyacheslav, Joasaph, John, Bessarion, Micah, Matthew, Euthymius, Barnabas, Hermogenes, Arcadius, Euthymius, Marcellus, John, Sergius, Demetrius, Savva, James, Peter, James, Alexander, Theodore, Alexius and Peter.

Varlaam — Vasily Evfimovich Konoplyov — was born in 1858 at the Yugo-Knauf works in the Osa district of Perm province, to a peasant family of priestless Old Believers. Literate at ten, he loved the Scriptures, and for twenty-five years he wrestled with the question of which Church had the grace of the priesthood without which, he saw, there was no salvation, travelling among the Old Believers and praying at night with tears to be shown the way. The turn came at an Old Believer community in Saratov province, on All Saints’ Sunday, when he prayed for a sign — that if the Russian Church had not lost grace by its change of rites, rain should fall in the heat while its clergy sang a moleben on the White Mountain — and on the way home heard that it had. In the 1890s he joined the Orthodox Church by chrismation, with his father, aged seventy-five, his brothers and their families and his elder sister’s household, nineteen in all. In November 1893 he was tonsured a rasophore and settled on the White Mountain, where twelve had gathered by 1894; tonsured Varlaam on 1 February 1894, hierodeacon the next day and hieromonk on 22 February, he was put in charge of the new missionary monastery, later called the Athos of the Urals, where he restored the full order of services and let no service pass without a sermon. A Perm priest wrote in 1913 of his experience, asceticism, strength of spirit and childlike gentleness, of the crowds who came to him from all Perm province and the Trans-Kama country, and of the quarter-century in which the monastery consoled the suffering; its last great solemnity was the consecration of the cathedral in June 1917, with Bishop Andronik of Perm and processions of pilgrims from the villages round about, the churches too small to hold them and the services sung under the open sky.

A year later, in August 1918, the Bolsheviks seized the monastery: Red Army men broke up and defiled the altar of the cathedral, made a latrine of the archimandrite’s cell and a theatre of the icon workshop, where the monastery’s choirboys were made to sing frivolous songs, and many of the monks were killed after savage torture. On 12 (25) August the Red Army men arrested Archimandrite Varlaam and shot him on the road to the district town of Osa; many of the brethren died as martyrs with him — the hieromonks Sergius, Elijah, Vyacheslav, Joasaph, John and Antony among the first the life names.
