# Pancratius of Tauromenium

Pancratius's parents were from the borders of Antioch, and they lived in the time when the Son and Word of God came down to the human race and became man for men. Hearing of the shining wonders the Master Christ was working in Jerusalem, they went there with their son Pancratius, and hearing the Saviour's most sweet teaching they believed in him and were baptised in the name of the Holy Trinity, and returned to their house praising the Lord. They lived a long while in godliness and then died, and Pancratius remained in their house, growing in wisdom and understanding, in the study and the reading of the divine Scriptures.

After the Lord's Ascension the Apostle Peter, preaching the word of God through cities and countries, came also into the parts of Pontus; and finding Pancratius there, Pancratius received him with love as an apostle of Christ the Saviour and gave him and all his household rest enough. Peter spoke a word of teaching and many believed, and he baptised them together with all Pancratius's servants, and ordained a bishop for them, a most wise man and disciple named Maximinus. Pancratius gave his whole estate as alms to the poor, and what was left he made over to his servants, giving them their freedom and saying that he was going with the apostle to preach the holy Gospel.

Peter took him to Antioch, where he found another of the apostles, Marcian, sent out from Jerusalem, who had turned many to the true faith; and after baptising and strengthening those, he said to Marcian and to Pancratius: «My sons, take you also the lot of the episcopate, and go into the parts of the West, to preach Christ as you know how.» Going down to the shore they found two ships out of Sicily ready to sail, and the sailors, seeing the apostles clothed with the glory from above, were reverent towards them, heard the word of God, were overshadowed by the grace of the All-holy Spirit and were all baptised. So Pancratius came to Tauromenium as its bishop.

The long life closes in the voice of the disciple who was given the lot of the episcopate after him and was told to follow his teacher's ordinances. He baptised the governor Bonifatius, who at once left his temporal office, appointed a devout and warlike man governor of the city in his place, cut his hair, put on poor clothes and never again missed the service and rule of the Church. With the gold Bonifatius brought, the disciple built a rich and beautiful church to the glory of God and of the holy Martyr Pancratius, gilded it, painted on it the wonders of the Old and New Scriptures, and painted with great care the icon of his teacher, saying that when he looks at it he seems to be looking at him and speaking with him, and he rejoices. Many and countless wonders were worked at the saint's tomb, healings of the sick and the driving out of demons, which the writer says he has left unwritten for brevity.

How he died is not told in that page; doxologia's life of [Marcellus](/saints/marcellus-of-sicily), which keeps the two of them together on this day, says that Pancratius, made bishop of Tauromenium by Peter, was killed in secret by the pagans. The page gives no year. Doxologia's own page for him carries the feast line «9 Iulie», while its calendar for 9 februarie prints him among the day's saints, and both are recorded here as they stand.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and adds to the page a third bishop, [Philagrius](/saints/philagrius-of-cyprus) of Cyprus, whom nothing but the day joins to him. saint.gr puts all three in the first century, and it makes the relation to Marcellus plain: Marcellus was his father, a follower of Simon the Magician who came to the true faith through the teaching and the miracles of the apostle Peter, and Pancratius followed the apostle with him. He was then made bishop of Tauromenium in Sicily — the modern Taormina, the page glosses — and the three of them fell asleep having borne many trials in their pastoral duties, and a life of daily martyrdom. The page also notes that his memory is kept again on 9 July.
