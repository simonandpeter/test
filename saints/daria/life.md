# Daria

Daria was one of the virgins who served the goddess Athena at Rome: learned, beautiful and very intelligent, the life says, and of an age to marry, having gone through all the books and the whole training in rhetoric. The father of [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) was advised to take her before anyone else did, and his kinsmen went to her and asked her to do two things — to marry his son, and to turn him back from Christianity. She agreed, and learned beguiling words to draw the young man to the love of the flesh and to the gods of the Romans.

She was dressed richly and brought into his chamber; and there, the page says, the soldier of Christ stood like hard diamond, like an unshaken pillar and an unmoved mountain. He told her that if for a short union with a mortal man she had adorned herself so and brought him such honeyed words, she should take far more care to find the love of the immortal Son of God: let her keep her soul pure with her body and adorn her heart as she had adorned her body, and angels would be her companions, apostles her friends, martyrs her neighbours, and Christ her Bridegroom, who was preparing an incorruptible bridal chamber for her in heaven.

Daria was pierced by it, and said that no great bodily desire had brought her there but love for him and his father's tears; and she began to argue for her gods — that men must honour them and not anger them, and that the images are carved so that simple people may see with their eyes whom they should think of with their minds. Chrysanthus answered that gods who need dogs tied to them at night, lest thieves carry them off, and who are nailed down with iron and fastened with lead lest they fall and break, are no guardians; and he went through the deeds their own writers record of Cronos and of Zeus, and said that mighty kings had been called gods by foolish men, and every one of them had perished in his time.

She was won, and the two lived in a spiritual union; and when the city rose against them, saying that their teaching was emptying Rome of marriages, the prefect had them both taken. Chrysanthus was given to the tribune [Claudius](/saints/claudius-husband-of-hilaria), who was converted with his household; and when the emperor Numerian heard of that, he ordered the two of them destroyed. Chrysanthus was thrown into a deep, foul prison, where a heavenly light shone on him and the stench turned to fragrance; Daria was taken to a house of prostitution, and a lion, escaping from its cage, came and stood over her where she lay praying with her face to the ground, and guarded her.

The lion took the men who came in and held them down without killing or wounding them, waiting on her word. She told them that if they believed in Christ they could go out unhurt, and if not, their own gods must save them; and they cried with one voice that whoever did not believe Christ to be the true and living God should not go out alive. She bade the lion let them go, and they ran through the city shouting that there is no God but the Christ whom Daria preaches. The prefect Celerinus had a great fire lit at the doors to burn her with the beast; the lion was frightened and roared, and she told it not to fear, for it would die its own natural death in its own time, and sent it away in peace into the desert, and it went out through the middle of the city and harmed no one.

When the torturers' hands shrivelled at the touch of her, and the wood and the bonds gave way about Chrysanthus, the emperor took it for sorcery and had the two of them carried out of Rome to the Salarian way, thrown into a deep pit, and covered over with stones and earth while they were still alive. Singing and praying they went down; and as they had lived one life together, so they ended it together.

The Greek calendar keeps her with [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) on the same day, gives her a country and fixes the year. saint.gr calls her «η ωραία Δαρεία», the beautiful Daria, says she came from Athens and was an idolatress, and has Polemon give her to his imprisoned son as a wife precisely in order to tear him from the faith. Instead of drawing her husband to idolatry the opposite happened: she believed in Christ herself and was baptised. The two were denounced to the eparch Celerinus, handed to the tribune [Claudius](/saints/claudius-husband-of-hilaria) for punishment, and their endurance under the tortures was what brought Claudius, his wife [Hilaria](/saints/hilaria-wife-of-claudius), his sons [Jason](/saints/jason-son-of-claudius) and [Maurus](/saints/maurus-son-of-claudius) and his whole guard to Christ. Then Chrysanthus and Daria were thrown into a pit and buried alive, in the year 283. The couplet over them says that they live in heaven though they breathed their last, living as they went down into the pit, and that the earth covered a wedded pair on the nineteenth day.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same menologion day and tells the whole of it in one entry. [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) was the only son of a great boyar, Polemius, who had moved from Alexandria to Rome. As the son of rich parents he went through all the schools of the world, with the most learned men for his teachers; but worldly wisdom confused him and left him uncertain what the truth was, and he grieved over it. Then a written copy of the Gospel and of the Acts of the Apostles came into the young man's hands, and reading them he was lit up by the truth. He wanted a teacher, and found one in a priest named Carpophorus, who instructed him and baptised him.

His father was not pleased, and tried everything to turn him from the faith of Christ. Failing in that, the wicked man first tried to corrupt him, shutting him up alone with shameless girls; but Chrysanthus overcame even himself in this and kept his purity. Then his father forced him to marry a pagan girl, Daria — and Chrysanthus counselled her to accept the faith of Christ as well, and to live as brother and sister though married in appearance. When his father died he began to confess Christ freely and to live as a Christian, he and his whole household.

In the reign of the emperor Numerian both he and Daria were terribly tortured for the faith. But their torturer [Claudius](/saints/claudius-husband-of-hilaria) himself, seeing the patience of these honourable martyrs and the wonders shown at their torture, received the faith of Christ with his whole house; and so Claudius was drowned in water, his two sons were beheaded, and his wife, having said a prayer, breathed her last at the place of execution. Daria held out so far under the torments that the heathen shouted: Daria is a goddess! At the last it was ordered that Chrysanthus and Daria be buried under stones in a deep pit, and a church was raised on that place afterwards.

Near that pit was a cave, in which some Christians once gathered for prayer and communion in memory of the holy martyrs Chrysanthus and Daria; the heathen, learning of it, fell on the place and shut the cave up, and so drove those Christians out of this world by death into a better one, where Christ the Lord reigns in eternity. These glorious martyrs, Chrysanthus and Daria, and the rest with them, among whom [Diodorus](/saints/diodorus-the-presbyter) the priest and [Marianus](/saints/marianus-the-deacon) the deacon, suffered for Christ at Rome in 283 and 284 — the years these folders already record.
