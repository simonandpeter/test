# Theodore (Smirnov)

Theodore — Vladimir Alekseevich Smirnov in the world — was born on 17
January 1891 in the village of Kozlovka in the Petrovsk district of
Saratov province, today in the Lopatino district of the Penza region, a
psalmist’s son. He finished the Saratov seminary in 1911 and, in 1916,
the Kazan Academy as a candidate of theology; from 1916 to 1918 he taught
history and other subjects in the secondary schools of Volsk in Saratov
province, in 1918–1919 worked in various offices at Saratov, in 1919–1920
served in the Red Army, in the Don North-Caucasian division, as instructor
of the school and political departments, and in 1921 worked as instructor
of pre-school education in the Saratov river-transport administration. On
4 October 1921 he was ordained priest; sentenced to a year in 1924 and to
three years’ exile at Narym in 1929, on his release he found a priest’s
place at Nikolskoye in the Kuznetsk district of the Penza region, was
tonsured Theodore in 1934, probably in October, moved to Penza in April
1935 — mentioned that year as archimandrite and rector of the Mitrophan
church — and on 23 September 1935 was consecrated bishop of Penza, with
the Mitrophan church for his cathedral, where he drew close to its priest
[Gabriel Arkhangelsky](/saints/gabriel-presbyter-martyr-1937), an old
acquaintance from the Saratov seminary and a zealous pastor of the
Tikhonite line. With no diocesan administration left, he did the vital and
extremely dangerous work of strengthening the spirit of the Orthodox faith
in the districts, sending like-minded Tikhonite priests, Gabriel among
them, into the Bashmakovo, Narovchat, Bednodemyanovsk and other districts
to find Orthodox Christians who would work for the opening of churches
and for the passing of churches from the Renovationists under the lawful
bishop. On 18 October 1936 he was arrested: his attempt to set the Penza
Church in order under the terror was cast as counter-revolutionary
activity — organising from elements repressed by the Soviet power a
counter-revolutionary group to agitate for the overthrow of the Soviet
power and a fascist dictatorship, the usual formula of the pre-war
fabrications — and at the interrogations he bore himself firmly and
categorically denied it. On 5 February 1937, still in custody, he was
retired from the government of the Penza diocese. Five others were in the
case, arrested the same day: the priests I. I. Umov of Bednodemyanovsk and
I. K. Khoroshunov of Narovchat, Gabriel Arkhangelsky, then acting psalmist
at the Mitrophan church and twice convicted before, A. P. Golubev of
Olshanka in the Bashmakovo district, and the retired priest
[Basil Smirnov](/saints/basil-presbyter-martyr-1937-22-august), a
disinfector at the Penza bacteriological institute when arrested. On 7
August 1937 he was condemned as leader of a counter-revolutionary
organisation of reactionary clergy and for anti-Soviet agitation and
sentenced to be shot, and on 4 September 1937 — 22 August of the old style,
the day the Russian calendar keeps him — he was shot at Penza with the
priests Gabriel Arkhangelsky, Basil Smirnov, Irenarch Umov and Andrew
Golubev, Khoroshunov alone getting ten years. On 20 August 2000, at the
close of the jubilee Council of Bishops, the bishop of Penza and the
priests Gabriel Arkhangelsky and Basil Smirnov who suffered with him were
numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia for veneration by
the whole church; the Russian calendar keeps him also in the synaxes of the
saints of Penza and Saratov.
