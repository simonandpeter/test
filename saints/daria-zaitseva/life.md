# Daria (Zaitseva)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names her second of the six women it grades прмц. on the one line it gives to fourteen people of 1938 for the Julian 1 March, and prints no life for her. Daria Petrovna Zaitseva was born in 1870 in the village of Bogoslovo in the Ryazan district of the province of Ryazan, the daughter of the peasant Peter Zaitsev, and at nineteen she entered the Borisoglebsk monastery of Anosino in the Zvenigorod district of the province of Moscow as a novice. She was the oldest of the fourteen on the line by sixteen years.

The house was closed in 1928 and she moved to the village of Kholmy in the Istra district of the Moscow region, where many nuns of monasteries the Soviet power had shut were then living. There she settled in the watchman’s hut by the church of the Icon of the Sign and helped in the church, and in 1934 she was elected its warden. In 1937 a denunciation reached the Istra section of the НКВД saying that a woman of one of the closed monasteries was living at Kholmy and selling lamp oil and Theophany water at the railway station, and the writer demanded that the officers turn their attention to such a disgrace.

The duty witnesses heard in February 1938 said she told people they must pray more to God and that the Lord would punish the Bolsheviks for destroying the faith. She was arrested on 3 March 1938 and held at first in the cell at the police station in the town of Istra, where her first interrogation was taken — for want of enough officers of the НКВД for an operation arresting hundreds of thousands of people, she was questioned by an officer of the criminal investigation department, who required her to tell of the counter-revolutionary agitation she was conducting among the people of Kholmy.

She answered that she was discontented with the Soviet power; that under the tsar’s government life had been incomparably better and after the communists came to power it got worse, the Orthodox were oppressed and priests, deacons and nuns put in prison; and that she had already got her things ready and was waiting to be arrested. In the winter of 1937 women had indeed come to her in the church hut, and she had said, without any malice, that they must pray more to God and ask the Lord to help them be rid of this power and send another.

The power drove out the peasants and the Orthodox, she said, and she was ready to die for the faith and for the little father the tsar; and she did not deny that there had been talk of a war, that other states would come and deliver them from this power. She confirmed to the investigator that she was discontented with the existing order, but only because the churches were being closed and the Orthodox persecuted; for that reason she could not be reconciled to it, and if that was counter-revolutionary activity then she owned it as hers.

On 8 March 1938 the troika sentenced her to be shot and she was moved to the Taganka prison in Moscow. She was shot on 14 March 1938 and buried in a common unmarked grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow. Besides 14 марта her azbyka page keeps her on the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs who suffered at Butovo and the moveable synaxis of the saints of Moscow, and neither is a day this corpus can hold. Her monastic grade is azbyka.ru’s, «послушница», a novice; the calendar’s name link gives her the grade прмц. and no monastic rank, and the office recorded here is the novice of her azbyka heading.
