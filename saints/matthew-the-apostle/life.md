# Matthew

Matthew was the brother of James, son of Alphaeus, and was called to the apostleship from the tax booth. He was the first to write the life of Christ, setting down the Holy Gospel in the Hebrew tongue. He preached Christ throughout all Ethiopia and in the parts of the man-eaters, where, in the city called Myrmene, he passed from this life as a martyr.

Doxologia prints this as one paragraph of its account of the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles, which the Romanian calendar keeps on 30 June. The page gives no year, so none is recorded here.

The Romanian calendar keeps him again on 16 noiembrie, his own day, and it is the only entry it prints for that day. The life doxologia gives there begins at the custom house in Capernaum: the Son of God, passing through, saw a man sitting at the toll named Matthew and said to him, Follow me; and the publican, hearing it not only with the ears of the body but with those of the soul, rose at once from the toll, left everything and followed Christ, and made Him a feast in his own house, where his neighbours and friends and many acquaintances, publicans and sinners, sat down with Christ and His disciples. To the Pharisees who asked why their teacher ate and drank with sinners the Lord answered that the physician is needed by the sick and not the healthy, and that He had come to call not the righteous to repentance but sinners. From that time, the page says, Matthew was a disciple and follower of Christ and was counted worthy of a place among the twelve apostles.

The Greek calendar names him again on 30 Ιουνίου, where saint.gr keeps the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles. The page lists all twelve whom the Lord first chose, Judas Iscariot excepted and the twelfth place filled by the apostle elected in the upper room on the eve of Pentecost, and it says plainly that it sketches each man's life at his own feast and not here. The first of the three apolytikia it prints names him in its roll of the twelve. What the synaxis is for, the page says, is the reminder of the unity the twelve had among themselves and of the manner of life that did so much for the world's rebirth in Christ; and the duty it draws from that is that Christians should move in their tracks with the same warm zeal for spreading the Gospel.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar heads the Julian 16 November with him, his own day: «Апостола и евангелиста Матфея (60).» Its life calls him Levi as well as Matthew and names him the brother of the apostle [James son of Alphaeus](/saints/james-son-of-alphaeus). It has him a publican at Capernaum, a gatherer of the tribute for Rome among a people under the Roman empire, to whom the word «мытарь» sounded as «sinner» and «idolater» did; and it says that at the voice «Иди за Мной» he left his post, repaid fourfold those he had taken from, gave the rest of his property to the poor, and followed the Saviour.

After Pentecost he preached eight years in Palestine, and before leaving for the far countries he wrote his Gospel at the asking of the Jews who stayed in Jerusalem. The page sets the writing in Palestine and in the year 42, in the Hebrew of his own day, afterwards turned into Greek; the Hebrew text has not come down, and the Greek keeps the marks of it. It stands first among the books of the New Testament, and the page reads it in three parts answering to three sides of the Messiah’s service: the Prophet and Lawgiver, the King over the world seen and unseen, and the High Priest who offers the sacrifice for the sins of all.

He went with the good news through Syria, Media, Persia and Parthia, and ended his preaching with a martyr’s death in Ethiopia, among tribes the page calls cannibals of rough custom. There he turned some of the idolaters to the faith, founded a church at Myrmene and set a companion of his own over it as bishop. When he prayed for the conversion of the Ethiopians the Lord appeared to him as a youth and gave him a staff to plant at the church doors, saying that a tree would grow from it and bear fruit and a spring of water run from its root, and that the people, washed in the water and tasting the fruit, would change their wild manner and become kind and gentle.

Carrying the staff to the church he met the wife and the son of [Fulvian](/saints/fulvian-of-ethiopia), the ruler of that country, held by an unclean spirit, and healed them in the name of Jesus Christ; and that wonder turned a multitude more. The ruler would not have his subjects leave the gods, charged him with sorcery and had him laid face down under brushwood and burnt. The fire did him no harm; and when more brushwood and pitch were piled on and twelve idols set about the fire, the flame melted the idols and scorched Fulvian, who begged for mercy, and at the apostle’s prayer it lay down. His body was left unhurt and he went to the Lord in the year 60. What Fulvian did afterwards fills the rest of the page and is in his own folder.

The calendar lists 30 June among his days as well, the synaxis of the Twelve, which this folder already records from the Romanian and Greek calendars; 16 November is the day that named him here. The day prints his troparion in глас 3 and his kontakion in глас 4, both his own and both about the tax-booth he left: the one has him following the Master who called him away from it, the other calls him a merchant beyond compare who brought back the wisdom from on high as his riches.
