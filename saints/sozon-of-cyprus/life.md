# Sozon of Cyprus, the shepherd boy

Sozon is a local saint of Paphos and a martyr of the Church of Cyprus
unknown to the synaxarists, mentioned by the medieval chronicler Leontios
Machairas: Saint Sozon at Plakountoudi, a shepherd boy, whom the Saracens
chased when they burned the icon of the Theotokos at the monastery — and
the icon was printed on the stones and is there to this day; he was holding
the milking-pail with the milk, and stumbled, and the pail broke and the
milk spilled, and it shows to this day; and he went into the cave with the
other children, and they lit a fire and burned them; and they built a
church and laid the holy relics there, and they heal every sickness. By
Machairas, then, when the Saracens came to Cyprus in the seventh century
they burned the icon of the Panagia at the monastery — probably the nearby
Chrysorrogiatissa, or the monastery of the Priests — and the form of the
Panagia was miraculously printed on the stones there, and remained until
Machairas’s own time, the fourteenth century. Sozon was a young shepherd of
Plakountoudi, a medieval settlement near the village of Asprogia in Paphos;
chased by the Saracens, he broke the vessel of milk he was carrying as he
ran, and the prints of the spilt milk showed on the stones. He fled to a
cave where other children were hiding too, and when the Saracens reached
it they set fire to the cave and burned them. The cave, near Asprogia in
the district of Paphos, is called the cave of Saint Sozon; it is very large
but holds nothing now, since it has ceased to be a place of worship. It is
said that in the old days water sprang from inside the cave and ran out at
its mouth and was held to be holy water — by local tradition Saint Sozon’s
water heals rashes — though there is no sign of water there today; water
springs from a much smaller cave in the river-bed below the great one, and
in the old days the faithful also hung clothes on the wild bushes near the
cave. Where the saint was martyred, across from the cave and the river, a
church was later built, in which the bones of the martyr, held to be
wonderworking, were kept; its ruins at Asprogia still stand. His icon is
kept at the monastery of Chrysorrogiatissa, and relics of the saint at the
monastery of Machairas and at Chrysorrogiatissa.
