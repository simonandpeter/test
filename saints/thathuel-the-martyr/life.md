# Thathuel

Thathuel and [Bebaia](/saints/bebaia-the-martyr) were brother and sister and lived in the days of the emperor Hadrian — or, by others, Trajan — in 116. Thathuel was a priest of the demonic delusion and was taught Christianity by a bishop; for this conversion the toparch Augarus had him cruelly scourged, then his eyes put out, then hung by one hand, his belly flayed and his sides burned with fire. At last both received the crown of martyrdom, beheaded. The synaxarion adds a note from Eustratiades’ Hagiologion on the martyrs — the entry runs on past the point read here. The Russian calendar keeps Thathuel and his sister Bebaia on 5 September.

The Russian calendar keeps Thathuel and his sister Bebaia — Thiphael and Thibea or Vivea, as it spells them — on 5 September (Julian), between 98 and 138: they suffered for their bold and successful preaching of Christianity among the pagans; after long and refined tortures Thathuel was hung on a tree and sawn through, and his sister killed with a spear-thrust to the neck.
