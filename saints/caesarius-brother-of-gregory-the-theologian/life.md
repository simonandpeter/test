# Caesarius, brother of Gregory the Theologian

Caesarius was the younger brother of [Gregory the Theologian](/saints/gregory-the-theologian), and was born at Nazianzus in Cappadocia in the year 330. His father was [Gregory](/saints/gregory-the-elder), bishop of Nazianzus, and his mother the most devout [Nonna](/saints/nonna-mother-of-gregory-the-theologian); his elder sister was [Gorgonia](/saints/gorgonia). saint.gr keeps each of them on another day, the Theologian on 25 Ιανουαρίου, his father on 1 Ιανουαρίου, his mother on 5 Αυγούστου and his sister on 23 Φεβρουαρίου.

After his first schooling he followed his elder brother to Caesarea of Cappadocia and to Caesarea of Palestine for higher studies, and then to Alexandria, where he read mathematics, astronomy, philosophy and rhetoric, and above all medicine, which he loved the most. From there he went to Constantinople, where the emperor Constantius and the people received him with honours and he was appointed physician of the palace; and the benefactions he did to everyone there, the page says, were great.

When Julian the Apostate came to power Caesarius would not come to terms with him. He left behind all the honours and offices Julian offered him and went home to Nazianzus, where he practised as a physician and did good to a multitude of his neighbours. When Valens took power in 364 he returned to Constantinople and was made «επιμελητής θησαυρών και ταμίας των δημοσίων χρημάτων», keeper of the treasuries and treasurer of the public moneys, at Nicaea in Bithynia, and there he gave himself to fresh benefactions towards the poor and the suffering.

His ascetic abstinence, with his many cares and trials, told on his health. He fell gravely ill and died on 9 March 368, and his holy relic was carried to Arianzus and laid in a tomb that had been quarried for his parents. The page keeps for him two lines on his brother's funeral oration, «Ὁ Γρηγορίου πρὸς νεκρὸν Καισαρίου, / Γλώττης χαλινοῖ τῆς ἐμῆς λόγους λόγος», and prints no hymn.
