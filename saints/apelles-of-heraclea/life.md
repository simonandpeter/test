# Apelles of Heraclea

doxologia.ro's calendar names Apelles among the apostles it keeps on 31 October, and the life it prints for him is a single sentence: he was bishop in Heraclea, preaching the Gospel of Christ, and after many labours and ascetic struggles gave up his blessed soul into the hands of the Lord. The sentence ends in the plural, the same formula the page sets under several of this day's apostles.

Nothing else is printed about him: no year, no account of his preaching at Heraclea, no manner of death and no burial place, and nobody is named beside him. doxologia.ro prints no separate troparion for him. The hymn on his page is the one it gives to all six apostles it names on this day — Apelles, Stachys, Ampliatus, Urban, Aristobulus and Narcissus together — a short common of apostles in the third tone, asking them to entreat the merciful God for the forgiveness of our offences.

The Greek calendar keeps the six of them together on the same day, 31 October, and saint.gr gives each of them his see. All six belonged to the seventy Apostles of the Lord, it says, and all of them were «Χριστοῦ εὐωδία τῷ Θεῷ ἐν τοῖς σωζομένοις» — a fragrance of Christ, pleasing to God, among those being saved — which it takes from 2 Corinthians 2.15. Of this one the whole account is a sentence: Apelles became bishop of Heraclea, and brought many to the Christian faith. The page gives him no year, no birthplace and no account of his death, and sets over him and three of his companions an epigram that calls them the four-stringed lyre of the Apostles, falling silent when its pegs — their spirits — are taken away.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps the same six apostles of the Seventy on the Julian 31 October, and of him its one life for the six says what the other two calendars said: he was a hierarch in Heraclea. The century is new — the line closes the six with «(I)» — and so are the day's two hymns, the second of which puts his name first of the six.

One thing on that day page is worth saying because it would mislead a reader who followed it. The link behind his name on the line does not go to a page of his name: it goes to one titled «Апостол от 70-ти Аполлос Александриец», whose days include the 8 December on which the Greek calendar keeps an Alexandrian apostle of that like name, whom this corpus holds as a folder of his own, and whose list of lives holds both this day's life of the six and a separate life headed «Апостол Апеллий из числа 70-ти». The page is an index of a name and holds two men. What is read into this folder is therefore the see from the day's life, and the name from the day's line and its kontakion, which sings «Апеллия» and not «Аполлоса».

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 31 October, fifth of six apostles of the Seventy under one heading, and gives him the shortest sentence of the six: the holy Apelles was bishop in Heraclea of Thrace. The province is the one thing this book adds to the folder, which carried the city without it; and he is the only one of the six whom the paragraph attaches to nobody — the others are appointed by Andrew or by Philip, or are a brother of Barnabas. It gives him no year and no death.
