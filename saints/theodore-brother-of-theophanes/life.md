# Theodore, brother of Theophanes

Of those who have wished to suffer for Christ, the life says, some suffered only for the Christian faith, standing against the Greeks who defended their idolatrous error; others laboured for the right faith against Christians indeed, but Christians who did not believe rightly, and suffered no less than the first, and received crowns equal to theirs. One of these is this Theodore. Palestine brought him forth — the land God gave Abraham for an inheritance and rightly called the land of promise, since from there came the company of the prophets and the patriarchs, and Christ himself in the flesh, and the number of the apostles who received God.

The parents of these venerable brothers were of the Holy City of Jerusalem, of good family, whose wealth was the name of Christians. From his early years Theodore gained a great wealth of wisdom: while he was still a child there was no childish mind in him, to attend to children's games or to shows of any kind, but he was always busy in the dwellings of God. No sight pleased him more than to look upon fair and honourable manners, and his boast was to obey his parents; in short, from the first he showed himself a tree bearing good fruit, and from his earliest growth he proved what he would be afterwards.

When he came of age his parents gave him in the monastery of Saint Sabbas to a priest of good works, to learn the knowledge of the divine Scriptures and the fear of God. Theodore had a brother named Teofan, younger in years but his equal in good ways, and both learned together with that priest; but Theodore, being sharp of mind, went through the whole of the book learning in a little while. Wishing to take up a higher learning he left the monastery, and finding an old man adorned with life and with wisdom he attached himself to him and learned the outward philosophy from him perfectly, and then learned the inward one well too, for he was taught by him to overlook the world and all that is in it.

Perfect in both philosophies, he returned to the monastery of Saint Sabbas and became a monk, doing all that is pleasing to God, so that he surpassed the others in his life. For who there was gentler than he, who freer of malice, who more continent, and who kept more carefully the time for speech, for silence and for each thing — so that he neither lost by his silence, nor left unsaid the useful word in its season, nor failed at any monastic labour? Who so mortified his body? Who so restrained his eye, his tongue and the assaults of his thoughts?

For such virtue, by the ordering of God and at the desire of all the brethren who were there, he was ordained priest in Zion by the patriarch; and the house of Saint Sabbas had him as a hard-working bee, gathering diligently the honey of good works. It is said of him too that there was in him something such as there was once in the prophet Jeremiah, to whom it was said: «Before you were born I knew you; before you came out of the womb I sanctified you».

doxologia.ro's life for him is long, and its middle — the brothers' stand against the iconoclast emperors — was not read. What the page prints at the end is that the venerable ones were taken by soldiers and came to Apamea, bearing on their foreheads the sign of their faith in Christ, and were shut up there in prison; and that Theodore, full of days and weakened by many beatings and labours, rested in the Lord on the day of the commemoration of the First Martyr Stephen, leaving in its bonds the body that had suffered so much for Christ.

His brother after the body and after the spirit, the venerable Teofan, weeping for the parting from his brother and singing the burial service over him, laid him in a wooden coffin. It is told that at the time of Theodore's passing a certain elder, great in good works, happened to be there, and heard angelic singing from on high, which was the assurance of his going up to heaven together with the angels.

When the emperor Theophilus died, peace was given to the Church, for the empress and her son, being of the right faith, called all the holy fathers back from banishment and blessed them with much praise. Teofan too, set free from prison, came to Constantinople with the rest, bearing on his forehead the marks of his victory over the wrong believers, and was marked out among the holy fathers as a cherub in the midst of angels.

The holy icons were brought back into the churches with a great festival on the first Sunday of the holy and great Fast, which has been called since then the Sunday of Orthodoxy, in the year of salvation 842; and for it the blessed Teofan wrote a most beautiful canon, which he also sang. He was then chosen metropolitan of the city of Nicaea. So the light of the right faith shone out, and the darkness of the heresy against the holy icons — which had begun a hundred and twenty years before, under the wrong-believing emperor Leo the Isaurian, and had troubled and darkened the Church of God — was driven out altogether by the grace of Christ.

Some time afterwards the relics of the venerable Theodore, the confessor of Christ, were brought from Apamea to Chalcedon, and gave many healings. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Cuvios Teodor Mărturisitorul» and gives no year, and the troparion the site prints for him, in glasul 8, is the common of a venerable teacher with his own name set in it.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 27 December, and fills in his family and his sentence. saint.gr makes him the son of [Jonah](/saints/jonah-the-sabaite), from Palestine, and a disciple together with his brother [Theophanes](/saints/theophanes-of-nicaea) in the monastery of Saint Sabbas. The two came to Constantinople in the years of the emperor Leo V and were confined for the question of the holy icons in a monastery at the mouth of the Black Sea.

Michael the Stammerer brought them back, but they would not buy their quiet with indifference to the Church's affairs or deaden their convictions, so they were confined again near Sosthenion, and under Theophilos the Iconoclast sent to Aphousia. Years of strict watch did not stop them crying out against iconoclasm, and Theophilos had them brought to the City, flogged without mercy, and then branded on their foreheads with red-hot iron — twelve iambic lines, which the page prints whole, calling them vessels of wicked superstition and condemning them as criminals. From that both brothers were called Graptoi, the Branded.

Under the patriarch John VII, in 836 or 837, they were exiled again to Apamea of Bithynia, where Theodore died and was buried by his brother; his relic was afterwards moved to Chalcedon, and a portion of it is kept today in the church of Saint Catherine at Thessaloniki.

The Охридски пролог keeps him on 27. децембар ст. ст., in a heading that names both brothers: „Преподобни мученик Теодор и Теофан Начертани.“ — the venerable martyr Theodore, and Theophanes, the Graptoi. They were brothers by birth, it says, of Palestine, very well taught both in worldly wisdom and in the wisdom of the spirit; monks in the house the book calls «обитељи Светог Саве Освећеног» and afterwards presbyters.

They suffered bitterly for the icons in the time of three emperors, «Лава Јерменина, Михаила Валвоса и Теофила» — Leo the Armenian, Michael the Stammerer and Theophilus. The senseless Theophilus beat them with his own hands, and gave orders that mocking verses be drawn with iron across their faces, «због чега су и прозвати Начертани» — for which they were called the Written-on. Both were thrown into prison in the city of Apamea in Bithynia, and there Theodore ended of his torments and his wounds; [Theophanes](/saints/theophanes-of-nicaea) was set free in the time of Theodora and Michael, and appointed by the patriarch Methodius metropolitan of Nicaea.

«Свети Теодор сконча 833. године» — Saint Theodore ended in the year 833, and that year is not written into this folder, because the Greek calendar that helped make it puts the last exile to Apamea under the patriarch John VII, in 836 or 837, and has him die there. The two cannot both be right; the one is recorded here beside the other, and the folder keeps the reading it had. «Ова дивна браћа пострадаше за Христа, и примише дивну награду од Христа у бесмртном царству светлости» — these wonderful brothers suffered for Christ, and received a wonderful reward from Christ in the immortal kingdom of light.
