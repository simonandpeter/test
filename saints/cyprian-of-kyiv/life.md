# Cyprian

Cyprian was a Serb who lived as an ascetic on Mount Athos, and his learning
and his life brought him to the notice of the patriarch Philotheus of
Constantinople, who consecrated him metropolitan of Kyiv and Lithuania in
1375. A council at Constantinople, wanting to spare the Russian metropolia a
division, decided that on the death of St Alexis he should become
metropolitan of all Rus’; but Moscow’s grand prince gave him many sorrows,
and for years he lived in Lithuania or at Constantinople instead. Only in
1390, under Basil Dimitrievich, was he received as primate at Moscow. There
he set himself to correcting the service books — manuscripts in his own hand
of Slavonic translations survive as witness to the work — and to encouraging
the church’s faith by his pastoral letters; his translating of liturgical
books is what he is remembered for. He died in 1406.

The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 16 September in three lines that say the
same: of Serbian descent, an ascetic of the Holy Mountain, appointed by
Philotheus, asleep in 1406. The Orthodox Church in America keeps the day as
his repose.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 16 September old style as metropolitan of Moscow and all Russia and wonderworker, with a troparion and two kontakia; its life is the account above. The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, calls him a native of Tarnovo but a pupil of the Serbs on Athos, where he gave himself especially to translating and copying books.

The patriarch Philotheus of Constantinople came to know him on the Holy Mountain, took him to himself, and sent him to Kyiv as metropolitan; he bore great sorrows and hardships in that office, held for nearly thirty years, all with a good heart, and did much for the Russian church. Before his death he wrote a letter of farewell, which was read over his grave. He died on 16 September 1406, and his wonderworking relics lie in the Dormition church in Moscow. The Prologue’s Tarnovo stands here beside the Serbian birth the other sources give.

**The Russian church calendar keeps him on the Julian 5 October with the synaxis of the hierarchs of Moscow**, seventeen names in one line under one polyeleos service, marked «(Моск. Свт.)» and not expanded: Peter, Theognostus, Alexis, Cyprian, Photius, Jonah, Gerontius, Joasaph, Macarius, Philip, Job, Hermogenes, Tikhon, Peter, Philaret, Innocent and Macarius — two and a half centuries of metropolitans, then the patriarchs, then the metropolitans of Moscow and Kolomna, ending with a man shot in 1937. He is the fourth of them, and his index entry carries six days in all, two of them Athonite — «7 мая (Русск. Афон.)» and «1 июня (Афон.)».
