# Luke the New

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 11 decembrie reads «Sfântul Cuvios Luca cel Nou», and his own page on that site carries the name, the date, an icon and a list of articles, and no life: no country, no century, no monastery, no account of what he did or when. Two things the line does assert, and they are all this entry rests on: that he was «Cuvios», the Romanian word for a monastic saint, and that he is called «cel Nou», the New — the epithet that distinguishes him from the older saints of his name.

What the page prints beside the name are the readings appointed for his commemoration: the epistle Galatians 5:22-26 and 6:1-2 on the fruit of the Spirit and the bearing of one another's burdens, and the Gospel Matthew 11:27-30, the Lord's easy yoke and light burden. Those are the readings the Romanian calendar gives a monastic saint, and they are the whole of what stands under his name.

Nothing further is set down here because nothing further was read. Another calendar will carry his life; none was consulted for this page, and the three other churches' rows say so.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 11 December, and calls him the New Stylite. saint.gr has him living in the middle of the tenth century and coming from Asia Minor; his parents, Christophoros and Kale, brought him up according to the commands of the Gospel. He served as a soldier, and not only kept his own purity but influenced towards the good the young men who served with him and had an inclination to corruption.

Later Luke became a priest and gave himself to the enlightening of the souls of his parish. After that he went up as an ascetic to Olympus, from there to Constantinople, and then to Chalcedon, where he set his hut on top of a pillar. From that new ascetic base he used to go to various places and preach the word of God, and he worked many wonders. On that pillar Luke spent forty-five whole years, and he died in a marvellous spiritual brightness. The couplet over his name says that the pillar carried Luke up to the height, and Luke carried his mind up to God, towards whom he runs.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, calls him by a different by-name from the other two calendars — the Stylite, where they have the New — and is the only one of the three to date him: having pleased God, he reposed between 970 and 980. He lived at Constantinople in the days of the emperor Constantine Porphyrogenitus, and as a soldier he took part in the war against the Bulgars, in which he saw many thousands of men perish and out of which he came alive and unhurt.

Seeing the finger of God in his own deliverance, he despised all the vanity of the world and withdrew to a pillar near Chalcedon, on which he struggled for a full forty-five years, cleansing his soul of every sinful desire and thought.
