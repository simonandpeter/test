# Alexander of Side

In the reign of Aurelian (270–275), when there was persecution against the Christians, there was in the country of Pamphylia a governor named Antoninus. Coming to Side, a city of Pamphylia, he seized Alexander, the priest of that city, and set him before his judgement seat, tormenting the servant of Christ with many questions and torments. He asked him first who he was, and the saint answered: “I am a Christian, a priest by rank, a shepherd of the flock of Christ.”

Asked where the flock of Christ was, he said that in every part under heaven Christ God had made all the men there are; that those of them who believe in him are sheep of his pasture, and that those who have fallen away from their Maker and serve the creature and the work of men's hands — soulless idols, as the governor did — are strangers to his saved flock, and will be set with the goats on the left hand in the day of God's dread judgement.

The governor said he meant to torment him for two reasons: that he should show him those who believed in Christ, and that he should sacrifice to their gods with worship. Then he asked who Christ was, and the saint said that Christ is the Saviour of the world, the light and the life of those who hope in him. The governor asked how one crucified on a tree and dead in bitterness could be a saviour; and Alexander answered that this is the wonder — that he suffered the Cross and death in the body, as he himself willed, while in his godhead he overcame hell and loosed the bound and put death to death, and rose from the tomb; and that he raised others too, so that many bodies of the saints who had slept rose and entered the Holy City of Jerusalem after his Resurrection.

The governor called him a babbler and a madman, and the saint told him that it was he who was mad, having the eyes of his soul blinded so that he could not look upon the mystery of our salvation. Then Antoninus had him tormented: first beaten with ox-sinews, then stretched on the wheel, then thrown into a cauldron boiling with pitch and oil, then burned in a furnace of fire; and in every one of those torments he was kept unharmed by the grace of Christ.

When they threw him into the burning furnace two wonderful young men were seen dancing with the holy martyr in the flame — they were angels of God, cooling the furnace for him — and the people marvelled that one man had been thrown in and three were seen inside. The governor and those with him called these glorious wonders sorcery, not believing in the almighty strength of Christ. Only one of the tormentor's servants believed, and fell down worshipping the saint from afar and praying to be received by Christ; and the governor at once killed that servant with the sword.

Coming out of the furnace unharmed, Alexander was hung up and torn with iron claws, so much that his whole body was one wound, and those watching said in horror that only his bare bones could be seen, and wondered how he bore it. He was burned again in a great fire and not hurt; then they pierced his belly with iron hooks and drew out his inward parts; then he was given to the beasts for food, and the beasts would not touch him. At the last the head of the hallowed martyr of Christ was struck off.

In that same hour the governor Antoninus, still sitting in judgement, was struck with sickness and tormented by the devilish gods he served; his servants carried him towards his house, crying bitterly, and he cast out his wretched soul before he reached it. After the beheading a certain Eustathius, an honourable man and a Christian in faith, took the much-suffering body of the martyr and buried it with honour, glorifying God. doxologia prints no hymn for him.
