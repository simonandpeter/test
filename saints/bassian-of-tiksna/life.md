# Bassian of Tiksna

Basil, in the world, was a peasant of the Totma country and a tailor by trade. Leaving his family, he was tonsured Bassian by Theodosius of Totma at the Sumorin monastery on the Sukhona, and after some years of labour and obedience there settled in 1594 near Totma by the church of St Nicholas on the river Tiksna - first on the church porch, then in a cell he set up beside it. He attended every service, and for thirteen years wore irons on his body: a heavy chain on his shoulders, an iron hoop about his loins, and under his cowl an iron cap. Beyond his spiritual father he received no one in his cell, and lived on the alms left at his little window.

He reposed on 12 September 1624, and only at his burial was it discovered how heavily he had humbled his flesh. A monastery of the Icon of the Saviour Not-made-by-hands rose later on the place of his labours; his veneration began in 1647, when many were healed at his grave during a plague, and his life was written in 1745 by the hegumen Joseph.

*After the Православный церковный календарь (days.pravoslavie.ru), [Преподобный Вассиан Тиксненский](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life1513.htm); read 30 August 2026.*
