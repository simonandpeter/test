# Procopius of Vyatka

saint.gr's calendar for 21 Νοεμβρίου names «Όσιος Προκόπιος της Βιάτκα ο δια Χριστόν σαλός» and gives him the longest notice of that day — the day is otherwise the Entry of the Theotokos and twenty-seven synaxes of her icons. He was born in 1568 in the village of Koryankiskoi, by the Russian town of Vyatka, to poor farmers called Maximus and Irene. At twelve he was struck by lightning, which left him senseless and in a very bad way; they carried him to the abbot of the monastery of the Dormition, afterwards Saint [Tryphon](/saints/tryphon-of-vyatka), who prayed over him and healed him.

The thing moved him, and he went to a neighbouring village and served the church of Saint Catherine there. When he was twenty his parents wanted to marry him to a girl of their choosing, and to escape the marriage he left for the town of Vyatka and played the madman. Afterwards he decided to take up folly for Christ in earnest: he went about the streets half naked, slept anywhere except in a bed, stopped speaking, and made himself understood only by nods or by signs with his hands. He spoke with one man only, his spiritual father, the priest John of the church of the Ascension, who alone knew of his discipline and alone had heard his voice; and he confessed and communicated every Sunday without fail.

When someone gave him clothing to cover himself or to keep warm, he would wear it a little while out of obedience and then give it to some poor man. He used to visit the hospitals; if he saw a man who was going to get well he set fire to his bedclothes, and if he foresaw that a man would not be healed he wrapped him in his sheets, to put him in mind of his shroud and have him repent while there was still time. He made a good many predictions by various prophetic signs, and they always came true: once, before a great fire broke out, he went up to a church bell-tower and rang the fire alarm for a week.

Another time he walked into the office of the district's chief of police, took his cap and put it on his own head; the commander, who knew him, offered him his place at the desk as a joke, and Procopius took him by the hand and led him into the wing with the prisoners' cells. Within a week the Tsar sent an order for the commander's arrest over some offence of his. The next governor of the town and his wife revered him greatly and took him into their house, where they washed him and dressed him in clean clothes; the holy man, seeing their good intention, accepted their hospitality, but in a few days he was out in the streets again, rolling in the mud, tearing his new clothes and living as before.

Once he went to the church of the Forerunner in a neighbouring town, caught a young man named Cornelius by the arm while he was chanting and dragged him by force in front of the Beautiful Gate into the sanctuary; six years later that young man was ordained a priest. So he lived in the discipline of folly for thirty years, until his peaceful repose in 1627, and he was buried in the monastery of the Dormition of the Theotokos at Vyatka, where his relics lie to this day. After 3 March 1666 he began to be better known, when he healed a woman named Martha who was suffering from a grave illness and to whom he had appeared in a vision. His life, the page ends, was written at the close of the seventeenth century.
