# Theocharis of Cappadocia

In 1740, doxologia.ro's life says, the governor of Asia Minor issued a decree that young Christian men were to be put into camps. Among them was Theocharis, a young Greek of Cappadocia who had been orphaned early. One day he was noticed in the camp by the governor of Nevșehir in Cappadocia, who took a liking to him and brought him home to look after his animals.

His beauty made the governor want him for a son-in-law, and he urged the young Christian to go over to Islam. Theocharis answered boldly: my master, I was born a Christian and I cannot give up the faith of my Saviour and of my parents. The governor took the answer as an insult, threatened him with torture, and then condemned him to death by starvation.

Theocharis went to church to confess and to receive the Holy Mysteries, and then returned to his master. When he confessed his faith again he was thrown into prison without food for several days; he fed on prayer, and was content now and then with a little water. The governor repeated the offer of his daughter's hand, and again Theocharis refused firmly. At last, after fearful torments, they took him out of the city, stoned him and hanged him from a poplar, on 20 August 1740.

In 1923 his right hand was brought to Thessalonica and laid in the church of Saint Catherine, where it is to this day. The Orthodox Church keeps his memory on 20 August. Doxologia.ro prints no troparion for him.
