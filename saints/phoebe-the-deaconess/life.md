# Phoebe the Deaconess

Phoebe the deaconess is the Phoebe whom the apostle Paul commends in the
Epistle to the Romans (16:1–2) — our sister, a deaconess of the church at
Cenchreae, to be received in the Lord as befits the saints and helped in
whatever she needs, for she has been a helper of many and of Paul himself.
That is all the Sretensky calendar says, and it keeps her on 3 September
(Julian). The Greek synaxarion notes that her memory stands for this day
only in the Small Euchologion of the Apostoliki Diakonia (1956), without
further particulars, and nowhere else, and takes her for the Phoebe of the
epistle: a choice soul with many spiritual interests, so that when Paul
visited Cenchreae, the port of Corinth, and preached the new teaching, the
teaching of Christ, the noble Phoebe attached herself to the herald of
Christ with a devotion like Lydia’s at Philippi and Priscilla’s at
Corinth, and became an untiring fellow-worker of the great apostle in the
mission among women. She is the first woman the New Testament names as a
deaconess, an office the apostle entrusted to her and which she proved
worthy of, answering her teacher’s hopes so fully that she took a chief
place in a heart that beat without cease for Christ. Paul surrounded his
faithful fellow-worker of the church of Cenchreae with great trust, and
proved it: when he wrote the Epistle to the Romans at Corinth and it had
to be sent to the church of Rome, he put it in Phoebe’s hands, and she
carried that spiritual treasure safely to the Christians of Rome. Few
souls showed the apostle so great and boundless a devotion, and among
those few the good Phoebe holds a choice place: when her teacher was in
danger at Cenchreae from the enemies of the Gospel she protected him and
kept him wholly from harm, and the apostle noted the deed in the epistle —
for she has been a helper of many, and of myself also — and in the same
letter calls her his sister, his sister in the spirit. A choice
fellow-worker of the great apostle of the nations, then, with a message to
every soul: to aspire to be fellow-workers of the heralds of the Gospel
who walk in Paul’s steps and labour in the mission. The Greek entry gives
her an apolytikion in the first tone, a kontakion in the fourth and a
megalynarion — companion of Paul and godly deacon of the church of God at
Cenchreae, handmaid of Christ full of heavenly virtues, who received the
light of Christ and by the light of her divine works enlightens the church
of Cenchreae; glory to Christ who glorified and crowned her.
