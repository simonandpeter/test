# Kyriaina of Tarsus

saint.gr's calendar for 1 Νοεμβρίου names «Αγίες Κυριαίνα και Ιουλιανή», and its notice is about what the two women did rather than about where they came from. They had given themselves to works of charity and of Christian sisterly love, the page says, and they lived in the years of the emperor Maximian, which it dates 298. Kyriaina's homeland was Tarsus in Cilicia, and that of [Juliana](/saints/juliana-of-rhosus) the city of Rhosus, also in Cilicia.

What the two of them did, the notice goes on, was to look after orphans, to comfort poor widows, and to give their nursing to the sick without taking anything for it. They knew few letters but had great zeal, and they always found a way to hold up the faith of people tried by poverty, or by injustice, or by some other wind of this life; and many times they had been able to bring souls of heathen women into the light of Christ, fishing for them with the net of evangelical patience and goodness.

They were arrested by the governor Marcian and cruelly tortured, without letting go of the treasure of their faith; and they died when they were thrown into the fire, bearing that terrible death with wonderful endurance and self-denial. The epigram the page sets over her name has Kyriaina breathe her last towards the fire, honouring the great Lord of every breath. No hymn is printed, and the page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου».

The Russian church calendar keeps the two women on 1 November too, «Мцц. Кириены и Иулиании (305-311)», and its index of names gives each of them a by-name the Greek page leaves in the prose: «Кириена Тарсийская» and «Иулиания Россонская», of Tarsus and of Rhosus. Its short life sets them under the emperor Maximian Galerius, whose reign it prints as 305 to 311, and has them seized for confessing the Christian faith by the governor of Cilicia.

What it adds is one sentence of its own, and it is about her. She was led around Tarsus stripped and with her head shorn, to be mocked, and only then taken with [Juliana](/saints/juliana-of-rhosus) to Rhosus, where the two of them were given to the fire. The year is now two readings and not one — the Greek page's 298, which falls under Maximian Herculius, against this calendar's reign of Galerius — so the folder's interval holds both and chooses neither.
