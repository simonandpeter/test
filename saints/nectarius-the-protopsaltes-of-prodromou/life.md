# Nectarius the Protopsaltes of Prodromou

Doxologia's life gives him as born in the town of Huși in 1804 and named Nicholas at his baptism. His parents, Vasile and Joița Crețu, had eight children: two boys, who both took the monastic habit at the monastery of Neamț, and six girls, three of whom became nuns at Văratec. After their parents died, Nicholas was left in the care of his brother, the schemamonk Athanasius, who had been eight years a disciple of the venerable George at Cernica near Bucharest.

At Athanasius's urging he went to learn psaltic chant at the school of the Greek hierarch Gregory of Ephesus, at the Golia monastery in Iași, and became one of the best chanters of that school; then he followed his brother to Neamț as a novice. After a time of trial he was tonsured in the little schema with the name Nectarius, and appointed canonarch and chanter at the stall.

The abbot, knowing his humility, decided to ordain him deacon; but feeling himself unworthy of so great an honour, and seeing how the fathers pressed him, he took a blessing and went away with his brother to the monastery of Ciolanu in the Buzău country. Two years later he was asked there too to accept ordination, and so he and Athanasius went on to Căldărușani near Bucharest, where he received the tonsure into the great schema. The two brothers then lived apart at the skete of Cocioc, where [Macarius](/saints/macarius-the-protopsaltes), the great teacher of Romanian psaltic chant, was labouring at that time, and from his life and his teaching they took much profit.

In 1835 they went to the Holy Mountain and lived at the skete of the Annunciation on the land of the monastery of Xenophontos; a year later they went on pilgrimage to Jerusalem and stayed six years at the Holy Places. In 1842 they returned to Athos and settled at the skete of Saint Anna, on the land of the Great Lavra, where there were twenty Romanian cells and great hesychasts; and there Nectarius learned to carve little crosses, to buy his food and pay his dues to the monastery.

Being a good chanter, he was called by many monasteries to sing at the vigils of their feasts, where he astonished everyone and was likened to John Koukouzelis. At one such feast a chanter from outside the Mountain, asked to sing a very beautiful piece, was then obliged to hear Nectarius write it down in notation and sing it back faithfully; and the man was so filled with envy that he offered him a cup of wine with poison in it. Nectarius drank it and at once began to sicken.

He told his brother, and Athanasius took the lamp from the wonderworking icon of the Mother of God the Portress and gave him the oil to drink; and he drank it, saying in great bitterness of soul, Most Holy Virgin, deliver me, they have poisoned me — and she who is the swift helper gave him healing at once. From then on he stopped going to the monasteries' feasts.

About 1854 he and his brother followed the venerable [Niphon](/saints/niphon-of-prodromou), who laboured to raise the Romanian skete of Prodromou. There Nectarius was first in church and sang like an angel, and many came to the services to hear him; the more he was praised the more he humbled himself, looking down, often standing in the stall with his eyes full of tears. At the obediences he was first out, lifting stones for the building. He did not raise his eyes, did not talk much, was never troubled, and if vain talk began he made as though he had forgotten something and went to his cell.

After the great church of Prodromou was consecrated he withdrew to stillness at the cell of Saint Menas in the desert of Vigla, with his brother; and after Athanasius died he settled in a solitary place on the skete's land, by the sea. In 1896 he came through a great trial: going down to the sea to wash a towel he stumbled and fell among the rocks, and lay hurt through a night of rain and wind, until the fathers found him next day and carried him on a mule to the skete's infirmary, where he was tended three months before he recovered. He passed to the eternal things on 18 November 1899.
