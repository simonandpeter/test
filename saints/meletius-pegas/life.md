# Meletius Pegas

Meletius Pegas was patriarch of Alexandria from 1590 to 1601, a great church figure of the sixteenth century who was also locum tenens of the Ecumenical Patriarchate in hard times, 1597–1598. He was born in 1549 at Chandax in Crete to a well-off and devout family, had a notable general education and continued his higher studies at the University of Padua; on his return he embraced the monastic life at the monastery of Angarathos in Crete, whose abbot was Silvester, later patriarch of Alexandria, and as abbot of Angarathos after Silvester’s departure fought zealously against the Latin propaganda.

Soon he is mentioned as cleric and protosyncellus of the patriarchate of Alexandria, and after Silvester’s death was consecrated patriarch of Alexandria by the patriarch Joachim of Antioch on 5 August 1590. He worked with great interest for Orthodox relations and the problems between the churches, reduced the patriarchate’s debts, built a new patriarchal house with the help of Jeremiah, prince of Moldavia and Wallachia, and solved many of the patriarchate’s pressing problems, until his early death at fifty-two in 1601. The Greek calendar keeps him on 13 September.

The Russian calendar keeps him too, and a month away from the Greek: «Свт. Мелетия, патриарха Александрийского (1601)» stands on its 13 October, where saint.gr has him on 13 September. It agrees on the year and on the see and says nothing else — its index of names gives his page a heading, one day and no life, so there is nothing of Crete, of Chandax or of the patriarchate in it that the Greek has not already supplied.
