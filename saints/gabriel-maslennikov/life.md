# Gabriel (Maslennikov)

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 5 ноября ст. ст. names «Сщмч. Гавриила пресвитера (1937).», and unlike most of this calendar's new martyrs he has a long life page of his own. He was born in 1871 in the town of Mikhailov in the Ryazan province, to a blacksmith «Михаил» and his wife «Ольга», who had eleven children and brought them up devoutly. He finished the town school and took up his father's trade, as his elder brother had, and read what he could get, spiritual books above all, to make up for the schooling he felt he lacked.

From 1893 to 1896 he served as a corporal in the 171st regiment at Warsaw, and he fought in the war with Japan, where he was decorated with the cross of St George for courage in the field and taken prisoner. Freed and discharged, he went back to the forge, renting a smithy at Pechersky Vyselki in the Mikhailov district and afterwards at the village of Priluchye, and married «Татьяна Ивановна Борина» of a townsman's family at Kovrov. They had eleven children and five lived.

In 1920 the townspeople of Mikhailov elected him their town head. In the early twenties his sight began to fail fast; the doctors blamed the forge and pressed him to leave it, and an old wish to be a priest woke in him. The persecution did not frighten him, but his age and his want of training did, and he thought himself unworthy of so high a service. A dream in which he buried his own dead mother as a priest decided him.

In 1924 he went to the bishop at Ryazan, was examined on the services, the Scriptures and the chief articles of the faith, and was ordained deacon to the church of the Nativity of the Theotokos at Makovo; in 1925 he was ordained priest to the church of the Protection at Samodurovka, now Solnechnoye. The page says the service was a great consolation to him, that he loved the church and the offices, and that his parishioners felt it and came.

In the early thirties a quota reached the Mikhailov OGPU to arrest a number of the town's inhabitants, priests first. On 11 March 1931 he was arrested together with three women — a psalmist, the warden of the church of the Archangel Michael, and his wife's sister — and the page prints in full an inspectress's own report of how she found the psalmist by crossing herself at the door of a watchman's lodge, kissing a Gospel the nuns gave her, and claiming to carry a secret packet from a priest.

He was questioned on 17 March and answered that collective farms made of poor men who would not work would never be farms, that he had held his religion from childhood as his parents taught him, and that he had become a priest for his soul's comfort; of agitation against the Soviet power and talk of its fall he said he did not admit himself guilty. None of the women admitted anything either. On 25 April 1931 the Troika gave him five years in a camp, commuted to exile to Kazakhstan for the same term.

The page is plain about the investigation: prisoners were beaten without mercy and it was not hidden from their families, and his wife was given his bloodied shirts out of the prison more than once. The policeman who had helped to take him came to her afterwards, fell at her feet and asked forgiveness — a quota for three had come, he said, and of course a priest had to be taken first.

He returned from exile in 1936 to a wrecked house. The family wandered among Orthodox households and then found an empty cottage standing apart with an earthen floor; there was a roof but no money for boards, so he cut sedge in the Oka meadows, dried it, strewed the floor with it and burned it in the stove. He read the spiritual books he had gone without, and was sent to serve the church of the Transfiguration at Vnukovo in the Mikhailov district.

He served there a short time. In the autumn of 1937 a collective farmer and then the farm's chairman testified against him: that priests came to his house in groups, that the farm's discipline fell the day he moved in, that he had persuaded women to go to church for the Dormition during the harvest, that christenings were held in his house, and that he had said to two priests on a bench that the time had grown hard for Christians and that believers were driven into the farms like cattle and worked there for nothing.

He was arrested on 16 October 1937 and held first at Skopin and then at Ryazan. The charge was active counter-revolutionary religious propaganda and close ties with two arrested priests, one of whose families he had helped with money after the arrest. He admitted the visits and a conversation about the new constitution in which, he said, they welcomed the power that now made them electors like other citizens, and he denied the rest twice over.

On 12 November 1937 the NKVD Troika sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on 18 November 1937 and buried in an unmarked grave, and he was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000. The names of the four arrested with him, of the two priests named in the charge and of the farm chairman are left here in the source's own Cyrillic or left out: they are bystanders in his story and no relation is claimed with any saint of this corpus.

The day page prints no hymn under his name. The life page also lists «26 января (Новомуч.)», the synaxis of the new martyrs and confessors of Russia, which is a synaxis and not a second feast; this folder renders on 5 ноября ст. ст. only.
