# Caecilius of Carthage

saint.gr says that Saint Caecilius lived in the third century after Christ and was a presbyter at Carthage in Africa, and that he was the one who led [Cyprian](/saints/cyprian-of-carthage), bishop of Carthage, by the hand into Christianity. Baronius and other historians, the page adds, reckon him a friend of the philosopher Octavius and of the jurist Marcus Minucius Felix.

Having been converted to Christianity, Saint Caecilius contended for the piety of the fathers and for the teaching of the divine Scriptures. That is the whole of the page's account: a century, a city, an office, a famous pupil, two supposed friends out of a historian's reckoning, and a sentence about how he spent himself afterwards. There is no year, no manner of death, no relic and no hymn. The page's own cross-reference for Cyprian points at 2 October, which is not the day this corpus keeps Cyprian of Carthage on; the man named in the page's words is the one linked here.
