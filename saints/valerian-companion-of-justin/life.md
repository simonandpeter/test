# Valerian, companion of Justin

Valerian is the last man doxologia's life questions before the sentence. When the idolaters had published through every land and city that decree against the Christians, that everyone who believes in Christ should be forced to sacrifice to the idols, there were seized the holy martyrs [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), Euelpistus, Hierax, Paeon and Valerian, and a woman named Charito; and they were brought to Rome before Rusticus, the prefect of the city, and set on trial.

The prefect began with Justin. Submit to the gods and to the emperors' commands, he said, and you will not be condemned; and Justin answered that no one who keeps the commands of our Saviour Jesus Christ can ever be condemned. He said he had tried every philosophy and joined himself at last to the true teaching of the Christians: to know one God, the Maker and Fashioner of all things visible and invisible, and to confess our Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God, foretold by the prophets, who is to come and judge the race of men.

Then Rusticus went round the others. Chariton said that with God's help he too was a Christian, and the woman Charito that by the grace of God she was one. Euelpistus said he was a slave of the emperor but a Christian set free by Christ Himself, and that his parents were in Cappadocia. Hierax said that he had been a Christian and would be, that his true father was Christ and his mother the faith, and that he had come out of Iconium in Phrygia. Paeon said he had his good confession from his parents. Last the prefect asked Valerian whether he too was a Christian and had no zeal for their gods; and Valerian answered: I also am a Christian, for I honour and worship the one true God, and your gods I despise.

When the prefect threatened torments without any mercy, Justin answered that they truly desired to endure torments for the Lord Jesus Christ and so be saved, since these win salvation and boldness at His dread judgement; and the others said the same, adding: do quickly what you mean to do, for we are Christians and will not sacrifice to idols. Rusticus gave sentence that those who would not sacrifice and would not obey the imperial command should be beaten with rods and beheaded, as the laws of the Romans command. They were led out, beaten, and cut down with the axe, and some of the faithful took their honoured bodies secretly and buried them in a worthy place.

Doxologia prints this same life on the page of each of the men whose names its calendar carries for the day, and one troparion for them all together with the philosopher Justin. Of the company the life names, only Justin, Chariton and Valerian have a line of their own in the calendar; Euelpistus, Hierax, Paeon and Charito are named here and have no folder. The life gives no year, and none is recorded here.
