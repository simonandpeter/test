# Epimachus of Pelusium

Epimachus was of Egyptian birth and of Christian parents, and from his youth he loved God and wished to serve him alone, so he went out into the desert, following Saint John the Baptist, and spent a long time on the mountain of Pelusium. Having none of the holy fathers for a guide in the hermit's life, he was guided by the Spirit of God, and the love of God was his teacher, teaching him every part of a good life. For who, the life asks, can teach a man virtue better than the love of God?

That love taught the apostles to leave everything and follow the Lord who had made himself poor; it taught the venerable fathers to go about in sheepskins and goatskins, destitute, afflicted and ill-treated; it taught the holy martyrs and women martyrs to suffer eagerly for Christ, saying, "Thee, my Bridegroom, I love, and seeking thee I am in torment." That same love taught Epimachus to bear the labours of the desert, to endure every trouble from the unseen enemies, to live in holiness before God and to be ready to die for him.

After long years of solitude he heard that in Alexandria the Christians were being tortured by the pagans, and that many, fearing the cruelty of the torments, were fleeing to the mountains and hiding in the wilderness, while others were falling from the faith. Kindled with divine zeal, he left the desert and went to Alexandria, meaning to suffer to the shedding of his blood for the confession of Christ.

Seeing in the city the lawlessness of the idolaters, and that the devilish impiety had grown very great while the honour of Christ was diminished and the holiness of the Lord dishonoured with sacrifices, he went boldly into the idol temple in front of everybody, and in the middle of their demonic feast he overturned their sacrifices and threw the idols to the ground and broke them. For this he was seized and taken to the governor Apellianus; and there, seeing the governor sitting in judgement and the Christians being tortured, he set on him with a manly heart, meaning to kill him, and would have killed him had those standing before the governor not stopped him. Such was his zeal for the Lord his God.

The governor marvelled at such boldness in a simple man and ordered him thrown into prison until he should decide by what torments to destroy him. In the prison were a multitude of the faithful shut up for the confession of Christ, and Epimachus strengthened them for the contest with a mouth full of the Holy Spirit. They were so strengthened by his words that not one of them was afraid any more or fell from the faith, but all of them poured out their blood eagerly for the true God and gave up their souls after many torments.

At the last they brought Epimachus out to the cruellest torments, not only for believing in Christ but for having made a disturbance of their feast, and above all for daring to rise against the governor to kill him. First they hung him up and raked him with iron bars, then beat him with stones and broke his bones. In his torments he said: if my Lord Jesus Christ was crucified for me, pierced with the lance and given vinegar to drink, shall I not be a sharer in his sufferings? I want greater torments than these you are giving me. Strike my face, spit on me, put a crown of thorns on my head, give me reeds in my hands and give me gall to drink; make my whole body one wound, nail me to a cross and pierce me with spears. All this my Lord endured, and this I too wish to endure.

While he was being tortured a great crowd stood about and watched. In it was a woman standing near him who had one eye diseased with a white film; and she wept as she watched him suffer, and looked at him without turning away. In those cruel torments, as the martyr's blood was spilled, a drop of it leapt into the woman's diseased eye, and at once the eye was healed and made clear and got its sight like the other; and the woman, having gained her healing from the drop of blood that fell into her eye, cried out, "Great is the God whom this sufferer confesses." After that they cut off his head, and so he gave up his soul into the hands of the Lord for whom he had suffered.
