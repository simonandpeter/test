# George the Cypriot

George came from Cyprus, and having left his own country reached Ptolemais in Palestine — the present Acre — where he was in service to a European consul. Doing his master's errands there, he used often to visit the house of a poor Mohammedan woman who had a young daughter, and to buy eggs from her. Some Turkish neighbours, because George did not buy his eggs from them, slandered him with having had unlawful relations with the young Mohammedan woman, and with their shouting gathered the Turkish mob in front of her house.

George, protesting against the false charge laid on him, was dragged before the judge of the sacred law. That man tried in vain to persuade him to become a Muslim and so escape punishment; and for all the judge's efforts and the mob's flatteries and threats the martyr stayed unmoved in his faith, declaring that he was born a Christian and wished to die a Christian.

Then the judge ordered his death, in the year 1752. The martyr was led to a place near the sea, where the executioners read out his sentence and tried once more with flatteries and promises to get him to turn Muslim. He lifted his chained hands to heaven and cried out with a great voice: «Κύριε, Ιησού Χριστέ, δέξαι το πνεύμα μου και αξίωσέ με της Βασιλείας Σου» — Lord Jesus Christ, receive my spirit and count me worthy of Thy Kingdom. The Turks shot him, and rushing upon him dismembered his honoured body with knives.

Then suddenly a storm arose that shook the sea, and the waves came up to the spot where the holy relic lay. The Turks took fright and drew off, and the Christians took up the martyr's body and buried it in the church of Ptolemais. On 13 April 1967 the venerable relics of the new martyr were carried with honour to Nicosia in Cyprus and laid in the cathedral church of St John.
