# The Six Disciples of Athanasius the Athonite

The disciples of [Athanasius the Athonite](/saints/athanasius-the-athonite) had gone up with him to watch the building work on the church, and all of them together were buried when the dome fell. Athanasius had foreseen his end and had prepared for it. His disciples were monks of virtue who had come from various places, and they were counted worthy to end their lives in the Lord together with their venerable father, in the church of the daily prayers.

Their names have remained unknown. Only one is known: he was called Daniel. Death did not find him at once, as it found the others; the monks who ran to help heard him glorifying God among the fallen stones. Daniel also told of a vision he had had a few days earlier, in which Saint Athanasius led his six disciples into the palaces of heaven. A week later he too was numbered among the blessed disciples of the great venerable one.

Their commemoration is kept immediately after their father's, on 5 July. Doxologia gives the six no names beyond Daniel's and no year, and prints no troparion for them, so none of those is recorded here; the page cites Moses the Athonite, *Sfinţii Sfântului Munte* (Mygdonia, Karyes, 2008), page 163.

The Greek calendar keeps the six with [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-the-athonite) on the same day, 5 July, and names none of them either. saint.gr tells their death as his: the roof of the monastery's church needed rebuilding, and the venerable one, old as he was, went up with other brothers of the house to do the work; the roof gave way and buried him together with the rest of the brothers. Over them it sets the couplet «Ἀθανασίῳ καὶ μαθητῶν ἑξάδι, / Ναοὶ λύονται σωμάτων ναοῦ λύσει» — for Athanasius and the six of his disciples, the temples of their bodies are undone by the undoing of a temple.
