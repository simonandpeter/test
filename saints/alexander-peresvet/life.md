# Alexander Peresvet

Alexander Peresvet was a boyar of Bryansk and, by the Zadonshchina, the poem
of the Don written at the end of the fourteenth century or the middle of the
fifteenth, the brother of [Andrew Oslyabya](/saints/andrew-oslyabya). In
1369 and 1370 he took part in the rising of the principality of Bryansk
against the Lithuanian prince Olgerd, in 1370 went over to the service of
the grand prince Dmitry Ivanovich, afterwards Donskoy, and on the Kulikovo
field in 1380 commanded one of the detachments of the army and fell; by the
Zadonshchina, Oslyabya foretold before the battle the death of his brother
Peresvet and of his own son Jacob. The later redactions of the Tale of the
Battle with Mamai, of the sixteenth to the eighteenth centuries, make him a
monk of the Trinity-Sergius monastery, sent with Andrew Oslyabya to the
battle by Sergius of Radonezh; at the beginning of the eighteenth century a
legend arose that he had been tonsured at the Nativity of the Mother of God
monastery at Old Simonovo near Moscow, where stone sarcophagi were found and
ascribed to the two, an insertion naming them as schemamonks was made in the
monastery’s book of the dead of 1662, and the Dmitrov panikhida of the same
century calls him a monk. When the two were locally canonised is not known;
their names were entered in the calendars in the seventeenth century — the
Menologion of Simon Azaryin in the 1650s has the venerable elders Alexander
and Rodion, called Peresvet and Oslyabya, killed in the battle with Mamai —
and at the end of that century they were counted among the monk-martyrs of
Moscow in the Description of the Russian Saints, and in 1896 in the Trinity
Paterikon among the disciples and companions of Sergius not marked in the
calendars. Tombstones were set over their supposed remains in a stone
chamber under the bell-tower of the Simonovo church, destroyed when the
tower was taken down, raised again in 1794 and given a chapel in the
nineteenth century, destroyed again when the church was closed in 1928 and
found itself inside the Dinamo works, and restored only after the church’s
revival in 1989 at the instance of the painter Pavel Korin; a canopy now
stands over the two tombs, but the remains themselves were not found in the
works, the place having been concreted over many metres deep. In 1981 the
two were included in the synaxis of the Radonezh saints, with their memory
on 7 September, the eve of the Nativity of the Mother of God, the day of the
battle, and the Council of Bishops of 2016 glorified them for the whole
church. The Russian calendar keeps him on 7 September of the old style, 20
September, and in the synaxes of Radonezh, Moscow and Tula.
