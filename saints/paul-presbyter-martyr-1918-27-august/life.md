# Paul (Fokin), Presbyter, Hieromartyr (1918)

Paul Fokin was born in 1883 to a cleric’s family; his mother baked the prosphora. Besides Paul two more sons were raised in the family, who later became priests, and two daughters, Eugenia and Vera; like his brothers he chose the spiritual road and entered the Perm seminary, and after two years married Taisia Vsevolodovna, the daughter of well-off townsfolk, and five children were born to them — a son Nicholas and daughters Lydia, Zinaida, Nina and Eugenia. Leaving the seminary he was ordained deacon in 1900 and served twelve years in the Perm diocese; in March 1912 he was moved to Ekaterinburg, to the cathedral of St Catherine, where he served — the calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. A priest of the church of the Meeting of the Lord in the village of Murzinskoye, after the revolution he was arrested for his sermons and his refusal to give up his pastoral work; in June 1918 he signed an undertaking not to speak against the Soviet power, but was arrested again when his parishioners refused mobilisation into the Red Army, and was shot at Alapaevsk on 9 September 1918. He was numbered among the saints in 2002, and the Russian calendar keeps him on 27 August (Julian).
