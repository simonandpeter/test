# Celsus, son of Marcian

saint.gr keeps on the eighth of January a company martyred in Egypt at the beginning of the fourth century, under Diocletian and under Marcian, governor of Egypt. [Julian](/saints/julian-of-antinoopolis) was from Antinoopolis in Middle Egypt, and his marriage to [Basilissa](/saints/basilissa-of-antinoopolis) gave them no children, but they counted the poor and the orphans as their children. When the persecution was declared in 303 the two went, he to a monastery of men and she to one of women, to escape the violence of the idolaters; but Marcian's hard persecution reached even those houses.

Julian was seized, and he above all, the page says, was cruelly tortured. It was then that Marcian's son Celsus, who respected the Christians, told his father to stop the tortures; his father refused, and Celsus then confessed Christ, and was put to death by his own father. A little later, after inhuman tortures, Julian and Basilissa gave up their souls to Christ the giver of crowns — and with them a priest named [Antonius](/saints/antonius-the-presbyter), some of the executioners, many other men, one [Anastasius](/saints/anastasius-8-january) who had risen from the dead, and twenty soldiers.

The day's line counts twenty jailers and seven brothers in the company as well and names none of them, so they have no folder here. Celsus's own [mother](/saints/the-mother-of-celsus), the governor's wife, has a notice of her own on the same day, which the page leaves nameless. saint.gr adds that the memory of all of them, for a reason it says is unknown, is repeated on 21 June.

Of Celsus the page's verse says that the sword cut off his head, and Antonius's with his.
