# Paul of Aglomazovo

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps thirteen people on 29 октября ст. ст. under one heading and one life page: «Сщмч. Николая пресвитера и с ним. мчч. Агломазовских: Космы, Виктора, Наума, Филиппа, Иоанна, Павла, Андрея, Павла, Василия, Алексия, Иоанна и мц. Агафии (1918)» — a priest and the twelve martyrs of Aglomazovo. The life is one story and this folder holds it, with its own last paragraph; the other twelve folders hold the same story with theirs.

Father Nicholas Probatov was born in 1874 at the village of Ignatyevo near Kadom in the Tambov province, the youngest son of that village's priest. He was schooled at Kasimov and at the Tambov seminary, in such want that his clothes wore through and his fellows laughed at him. Ordained presbyter in 1899 and made second priest at Temirevo, he wanted a parish of his own, and in 1906 was given Aglomazovo, with a wooden church of the Theophany put up in 1779; he renewed its decayed iconostasis in 1910, trained the choir into congregational singing, and said of the service that he had a corner of paradise in his altar. The village had a hundred and fifty houses and more than a thousand parishioners and no school, so he had a wooden building raised in which two hundred children could learn, taught them the law of God himself, and took no payment for services. He owned two pairs of shoes, one for winter and one for summer.

When the war came and the army was short of priests, the bishop of Tambov asked the diocesan clergy to volunteer, and few did — one pleaded illness, another his wife, another his small children. Father Nicholas was ashamed of it: our own soldiers are shedding their blood out there, he said, and we are priests and refuse. He had three children of his own, the eldest fourteen and the youngest two a year old, and he went as regimental priest to the first Bakhmutovsky regiment against the Austrians. There he saw how little faith was left in people — out of the whole regiment not thirty men came to church — and when he came home in 1917 he said to those near him that priests were not wanted here now, and were sooner citizens of heaven than of earth.

In February 1918 the Bolsheviks declared a mobilisation into the Red Guard, and the peasants, who had expected peace, decided instead to march on the nearest district town and break up the Bolshevik authority there. They asked him for a service before they went, and he ended the sermon after it with the words: I bless you to go out against the persecutors of the Church of Christ. They set off with axes and pitchforks twenty-five versts; many lost heart on the road and turned back, some hurried ahead to warn the town, and a machine gun set in the belfry fired a burst into those who came up to it and scattered them. The incident was over, but a punitive detachment was sent to Aglomazovo. He blessed his household to go to the next village and stayed; the peasants told him to run, and he said he had never run and would not run now. His eldest son Alexander would not leave him.

He went out of the house in a padded cassock, saw the detachment coming in the distance and said, shaking his head: the Roman legions are on their way. They quartered themselves in a brick house near the church and put the arrested in its cellar, and the lists were drawn up by the village schoolmaster, a local atheist who disliked the church and the priest. Those arrested were questioned with beatings. They beat Father Nicholas on the heels with ramrods to make him dance, and he said he had never danced before and would not dance before his death. Some of his torturers had been at church not long since and remembered the words of the prayers, and mocked him with them — ardent Intercessor! You prayed to her; why does she not intercede for you? — and to all of it he answered: Christ endured, being without sin, and we endure for our own sins, which made them laugh.

The schoolmaster had put several women on his list, and the commander of the detachment struck the women's names out and left one, Agafya, because she was quite alone and there was nobody to plead for her. Then it was announced that all the arrested would be shot. They confessed before death. At the seventh hour of the evening eighteen were brought up out of the cellar and taken to the river Tsna, divided into two parties and led along the bank to left and to right. The command was given to form up and make ready, the condemned crowded together opposite, and Father Nicholas raised his hands and prayed aloud, saying the words loudly and separately, so that everyone heard: Lord, forgive them, they know not what they do. The volley came; it was quite dark and nobody meant to check who was living, and he gathered his strength, rose with his hands raised and went on praying out loud — it is truly meet to bless you, the Theotokos — and a second volley killed him. Of the eighteen, thirteen were killed; the rest crawled in the night to the nearest huts and were hidden by the villagers, and it is from them that what happened is known.

In the morning the peasants fetched the dead with a cart, and his son went with them. The sand was soaked through with blood and Alexander lifted off the top layer of it and laid it on the cart. The body had already stiffened with the hands raised in that last prayer and the fingers set for a blessing, and as it was carried through the village people looked out of their windows and said: our father blesses us even dead. His widow asked leave to bury him by the church, and was told that a dog had a dog's death and he should be taken to the rubbish tip, and that she might thank them he had not been thrown into the ravine. Two priests of neighbouring parishes came to bury him, one of them a close friend with whom he had agreed beforehand that whichever was killed the other would come; they sang the funeral in his house with the windows hung with black cloth so the detachment should not see in, and buried him deep in the night by the light of a clear moon. The whole company was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia by the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.

He is the first of the two men of this name the line names, sixth in its order. The calendar separates the two Pauls by nothing but position and by giving each his own name page, 10015 for this one and 10016 for the other, which by the settled rule makes them two men. Both links read «Павел, мч.» and nothing more, so the two folders store the same form, and this one carries the tail `-a` in its slug.
