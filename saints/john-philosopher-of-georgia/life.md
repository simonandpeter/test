# John the Philosopher of Georgia

saint.gr says John — Shavteli, the page adds in its own parenthesis — lived between the twelfth and the thirteenth centuries in Georgia. He studied theology, philosophy and history at the academy of Gelati in the north of the country, then became a monk and for many years lived as an ascetic in a solitary cell at the famous monastery of Vardzia in the south. There, the page says, he kept a severe ascetic life, continually given to theological enquiry through prayer, and steeped himself in the interpretation of Holy Scripture; and after long spiritual effort he reached a high degree of perfection and received the gift of the word, which showed itself in his poetry.

At Vardzia, in the years 1210 to 1214, he wrote a notable ode under the title «Δούλος Χριστού», the Servant of Christ, in which is sketched the image of the Christian who is faithful to the Canons of the holy Orthodox Church; in it the monk often calls himself a wanderer and a servant of Christ. A great part of the ode is dedicated to the Georgian emperor St [David the Builder](/saints/david-the-builder), whom the page calls David III the Restorer, and to the Georgian empress [Tamara](/saints/tamara-of-georgia) the Great.

The theological significance of the ode, the page says, is especially clear in those verses where the poet offers prayers in the Name of the Most Holy Trinity, to thank the divine omnipotence and the divine providence that granted men the mystery of the divine economy; and where he speaks of the order in which God created the world he writes, in correspondence with the works of St Dionysius the Areopagite, about the divine and the ecclesiastical hierarchies. He fell asleep in peace in deep old age.

The same Greek day keeps [Eulogius](/saints/eulogius-of-georgia), the fool for Christ, whose page has the two of them together in the queen's company at the battle of Basiani, and it is from that page rather than this one that the one recorded scene of John's life comes.
