# Vadim of Persia

Vadim was of the city the Romanian life spells Vitlaplata, of a very rich family, and when he wished to become a monk he gave the whole of his wealth away to the poor. He built himself a monastery outside the city and lived in it, and the page praises him at length and in general terms: a man full of grace and truth, a chosen vessel, a chosen priest of God who guided many on the way of salvation, in whom greed found no place and pride was humbled. Gold, it says, did not dare come before his face.

He was arrested with seven of his disciples at the time when the forty martyrs of Persia were killed, and by order of the emperor Shapur — «împăratul Savorie» — they were shut up in prison. There he spent four months in chains, beaten hard every day and bearing it, the life says, with courage. In the same prison was a great nobleman named Nirsan, of the city called Aria in the territory of Vidghern, a Christian who had been jailed for refusing to worship the sun and who then lost heart and offered to do whatever the emperor commanded.

Shapur promised Nirsan his freedom and Vadim's whole estate if he would kill Vadim with his own hand. Vadim was taken out of prison to the palace and set before him; Nirsan took a sword, came up to strike, and was seized with trembling and stood like a stone. «Până într-atât a crescut răutatea ta, o, Nirsane», said Vadim — has your wickedness grown so far that you not only deny God but would kill His servant? Nirsan, ashamed and unable, hardened his face at last and struck at the neck, but his hands shook so that it took many blows to finish the beheading, and Vadim stood through them unmoved as a pillar.

The unbelievers standing by marvelled at his endurance and jeered at the murderer as a coward; Nirsan himself, the page says, came to a bad end and died by the sword, his own killer in despair. Vadim's body was thrown outside the city and taken up in secret and buried with honour by the devout. His seven disciples stayed four years in prison until Shapur died, and were then released in peace and allowed to keep their faith. The life closes by dating his martyrdom to the 8th of April, though the calendar keeps him on the 9th.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and heads him «(; - 376)». saint.gr says Vadim lived when the king of Persia was Shapur II, whose reign it gives as 325 to 379; that he was a Persian by birth and of a distinguished family, and left riches and an attractive worldly future in order to serve Christ. He became a priest and worked for the spreading of the Gospel, and gathered about him a band of seven disciples whom he was preparing as labourers of the Christian enlightenment.

He was denounced for drawing many Persians away from the ancestral religion to Christ, and was imprisoned with his disciples for four months; and because in that time he refused to leave his faith, he was condemned with them to be beheaded. The carrying out of the sentence was taken on by a man who had denied Christ before the torments, Nirsan; and although Vadim rebuked him for it, he performed the execution with trembling hands. The divine justice did not leave him unpunished: one day an idolater grew angry with him and killed him with a knife. The page names not one of the seven disciples.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and tells it shorter, with two things of its own. In the time of the Persian king Shapur, it says, Vadim too — the abbot of a monastery and a man famed for his piety — was thrown into prison with seven disciples; and with them in the prison was a certain prince Nirsan, also a Christian. Every day they were brought out and beaten. Nirsan took fright and promised to leave the faith and to worship the sun; and this being pleasing to Shapur, he promised Nirsan, besides the rest, all the property of Vadim's monastery if he would behead Vadim with his own hand. Nirsan agreed to it. With a trembling hand, frightened by the dignified face of the saint, he struck many times with the sword at that holy man's neck before he barely cut it through.

But soon after, the entry says, he fell into despair and ran himself through with the sword, and so received from his own hand the punishment for the murder of a righteous man. That is a second witness for the end the Romanian life gives him — his own killer, in despair — against the Greek page, which has an idolater grow angry with him one day and kill him with a knife; two readings to one, and neither chosen here. Vadim suffered in the year 376, which is the year already written above.

**The Russian calendar keeps him on the same menologion day and disagrees with itself about his rank.** Its line is «Прмч. Вадима архимандрита и 7 учеников его (376).» — Прмч., преподобномученик, a venerable-martyr, which is what this folder’s `types` already carry — and the name link behind that line, name page 330, closes «сщмч.», a hieromartyr: «Вадим Персидский, архимандрит, сщмч.» Both readings are recorded and `types` are untouched. The name page’s nominative with the epithet is the form stored here, the day’s own line giving the forename alone in the genitive, «Вадима», and the bare «Вадим» being the Serbian form this folder already holds.

Its life page adds a city, a mountain and a reign. He was born in the fourth century in the Persian city «Вифлапате», which is the Romanian life’s Vitlaplata under another spelling; he gave part of his estate away, went out into the desert and founded a monastery there at his own cost; and going up a mountain for solitary prayer he was once counted worthy to see the Glory of God. **This page dates Shapur’s reign «(310 - 381)»** where saint.gr gives «(325 - 379 μ.Χ.)» — two readings of one reign, recorded and not reconciled, and this folder’s own year of 376 falls inside both.

Nirsan is placed here more exactly than in the other three: «один из приближенных царя Сапора», one of the intimates of the king, a man who had confessed Christianity and had even borne imprisonment for it before he failed the test. And this page agrees with the Romanian life and the Ohrid Prologue about his end against the Greek entry’s: «терзаемый совестью, он покончил с собой, бросившись на меч» — tormented by conscience he made an end of himself by throwing himself on the sword, where saint.gr has an idolater grow angry with him one day and kill him with a knife. Three readings to one, and still nothing chosen here.

Two smaller differences stand. Where the Romanian life has the seven disciples spend four years in prison after the martyrdom, this page says only that they came out of it after Shapur’s death; and where the Romanian has Vadim four months in chains before the sentence, this page has the same four months and makes him in that time the spiritual leader and support of the Christians living in Persia, which is a thing none of the others says. Under «Сайт прихода» the day page links the parish of «Храм прмч. Вадима Персидского» at Саракташ in the diocese of Orenburg, a church of the Russian church carrying his name.
