# Porphyrius the Mime (4 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 4 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Πορφύριος ο Μίμος», and ends his notice by insisting that he is not the other man of that trade whom the same calendar keeps on 15 September. He lived in the years of the emperor Aurelian, about 270, the page says; he came from Ephesus in Asia Minor, and from a small age had been brought up among the mimes in the theatres. Practising the mime's craft, he often mocked Christian customs and ceremonies — until a divine intervention brought an unlooked-for change.

Porphyrius had a daughter, and he lost her to a sudden death. The loss struck his heart an enormous blow; he left the trade of mime and began to wander about, lamenting. And the comfort came to his heart out of the bosom of the Christians, whom he had mocked so much. Finding his peace there, he was baptised a Christian, and then with courage renounced the idolatrous faith.

When the prefect heard of it he summoned him and ordered him to embrace the idols. Porphyrius refused, and for that he was beheaded. The epigram over him plays on the name: Porphyrius, well dressed by the sword, clothed in a bright purple of blood.

The page's last sentence is the one that matters most for a corpus: the Porphyrius the mime who is kept on 4 November is not to be confused with the Porphyrius the mime kept on 15 September — the two saints are different, since they have a different couplet and lived in different times. The corpus keeps that other man as [Porphyrius the Mime (15 September)](/saints/porphyrius-the-mime-15-september). No hymn is printed here, and the page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου».

*After saint.gr's calendar for 4 Νοεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/11/04/index.aspx) and [his life](https://www.saint.gr/2883/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026.*
