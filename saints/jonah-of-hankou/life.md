# Jonah of Hankou

The Russian calendar's 7 October prints him as «Свт. Ионы , еп. Ханькоуского (1925)» — Jonah, bishop of Hankou — and the life behind that line is the fullest thing on the day. It opens by calling him the first man of the Russian emigration to be acknowledged a righteous one while he still lived: a young missionary bishop, dead in his thirty-seventh year, whose influence on everyone who came near him, Russian or Chinese, was so deep and so beneficent that he was held a saint before his death. He was born at Kaluga in 1888, orphaned early and, the account says, with much grief to bear, and was educated at the Kaluga seminary and the Kazan theological academy. While a student he was under the spiritual care of the great Optina elder Gabriel.

When the academy offered him the chair of the New Testament he refused it as beyond his strength and told his elder so. The elder read the offer as the will of God, ordered him to take the post, and for the haste of the refusal set him three hundred prostrations with the Jesus prayer. In 1918, hunted by the revolutionary authority, he had to leave Kazan. At Perm he was arrested by the soviets and beaten until he lost consciousness — sharing, the life says, the lot of the new confessors of Russia — and was sent for trial to Tyumen. Near Tobolsk he and the others held with him were freed by the White forces.

At Omsk he was made senior priest of the Southern Army, and when the White movement in Siberia was broken he fell back into China with Ataman Dutov's army. There his life began again: service in the Peking ecclesiastical mission, and from 1922 the episcopate in Manchuria. The gifts of the young bishop showed in that work — unresting preaching, pastoral care, and a gift for teaching children. He founded a school that took as many as two hundred, lectured, set the services in order by the typikon, opened an orphanage, and at Harbin founded courses in theology and philosophy.

He was careless of himself in the same measure. The account calls him cheerful, plain and easy with people, and in his own life modest past measure. He fell ill with quinsy in 1925 and went on serving: feverish, he served the Exaltation and the Protection, and with his throat bad he taught his flock the truths of the Gospel almost in a whisper. Rinsing the throat with kerosene poisoned his blood. The doctor came to him at ten at night on 19 October and, having looked at him, told him to confess and communicate quickly.

His spiritual children and the children of the school gathered. He confessed to his confessor Alexis, put on a new cassock and stole, bowed to the ground before the Holy Gifts and received them. Then he sat quietly down at the table and typed his will, in which he asks his children to love one another and begs them not to abandon the little ones. He spoke with every person there, found a kind word for each and asked forgiveness of all. At half past twelve he rose, put on the stole and the cuffs of the Optina elder [Ambrose](/saints/ambrose-of-optina), made prostrations and read the prayers for the departing aloud over himself.

He said what he was to be dressed in, asked to be buried by the monastic rite, and asked for a plain oak cross instead of a monument. Everyone there was in tears and the children were praying, «Боженька, оставь нам Владыку!» — God, leave us our bishop. At half past one he suddenly sprang up from the bed and said, swaying on his feet, «Иду умирать в церковь!» — I am going to the church to die. They talked him out of going. He lay down holding a cross and an icon in his right hand and a lit candle in his left, and three minutes later he died.

The whole population of Manchuria came to the funeral. On the day of his burial he appeared to a boy of ten whose legs were useless, who could neither stand nor walk, and said: «На, возьми мои ноги, они мне больше не нужны, а свои отдай мне» — take my legs, I do not need them any more, and give me yours. The boy woke, stood on his feet and walked to the kitchen door crying for his mother, and the bishop he described was this one. Between 1994 and 1996 his admirers, emigrants from Manchuria living in San Francisco, tried to find his remains in China and bring them to the United States, and could not. He was numbered among the saints on 19 October 1996.
