# Hermas (5 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 5 Νοεμβρίου keeps five of the Seventy Apostles in one entry, and Hermas stands first in it. All of them, the page says, were true shepherds of the rational flock of the Church. Hermas is one of those the Apostle Paul greets in his letter to the Romans, as the page has it, and so are Patrobas, Gaius and Philologus who are kept with him; Linus it names out of the second letter to Timothy.

Hermas was appointed bishop at Philippi in Macedonia. To him Origen, Eusebius, Jerome and others ascribe the writing known as «ὁ ποιμήν», the Shepherd — a work, the page says, that shows the pit into which sin brings a man, and that stirs up strongly the sense of repentance and of peace with God. For a long while it could be read only in its Latin translation; in the nineteenth century the Greek original was found as well.

The Greek books give three of the five one couplet: «Ἑρμᾶς, Πατρόβας καὶ Γάϊος τρεῖς ἅμα, / Ἀπόστολοι θνῄσκουσιν, οἷς τριὰς φίλη» — Hermas, Patrobas and Gaius, three together, die as apostles, to whom the Trinity is dear. The page gives him no year and no account of his death, and prints no hymn for the entry. Those kept beside him are [Patrobas](/saints/patrobas), [Linus](/saints/linus-of-rome), [Gaius](/saints/gaius-of-ephesus) and [Philologus](/saints/philologus-of-sinope).

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar for 5 ноября ст. ст. keeps him as one of five named on a single line, «Апп. от 70-ти Патрова , Ерма , Лина , Гаия , Филолога (I).», and he is the only one of the five with a life page — two, in fact, behind the index `/Life/id792.htm`. They make him a Roman citizen but a Greek by descent, living at the end of the first century, and they both say he was a bishop. The name link behind his place on the line reads «Святой Ерм, апостол от 70-ти» and gives him no see at all.

**The two calendars do not agree about which city he held.** This folder's `office` came from saint.gr and reads Bishop of Philippi in Macedonia; both of these pages say Philippopolis, and the longer adds the province — «Был епископом в Филиппополе Фракийском», in Thrace. Both readings are recorded here and neither is chosen, and the `office` field is left exactly as it was, which is also what the tool that applies an upgrade can do; the disagreement is named in this batch's report and is not a reader's to settle.

Both pages ground him in Scripture: the apostle «Павел» names him among those the Romans are to greet, «приветствуйте Асигкриста, Флегонта, Ерма» (Rom. 16:14). The longer page makes him the author of the book «Пастырь», the Shepherd, which revelations from above moved him to write, and reports what that book says of its own author: that he lived at Rome in the days of the pope «Климент», that he was rich at first and busy with worldly affairs, that he had an evil-tongued wife and vicious sons whom he was not strict enough with, and that God punished him for it with the loss of his wealth.

Not knowing why the disaster had come on him, the page says, he was taught the reason in a series of visions, and those visions and the instructions in them are the content of the book, which in its form belongs among the apocalyptic writings. It had such influence that it was for a time entered into the Bible as a part of it, but did not enter the final canon of Scripture. The shorter page says nothing of the book and instead that he **died a martyr** in the first century, which is why `martyr` is added to this folder's types.

Both pages list «Дни памяти: 4 января (70 ап.) , 8 марта , 5 ноября». The day page that named him is 5 ноября and that is the row; the synaxis of the Seventy and the 8 марта commemoration are recorded as notes on it and this folder renders on 5 ноября ст. ст. only. The 8 марта day page has since been read, and it does keep him: «Ап. Ерма (I)» is its second entry, and the name behind it links the same index page 792 as the 5 ноября line, so the two days are one man by the calendar's own reckoning and not a guess.

The form «Ерм» is now in two folders of this corpus, deliberately. The other is `hermas-of-rome`, a martyr of the second century whom this same calendar keeps on 18 августа ст. ст. with two companions, dragged over sharp stones at Rome: a different day, a different century and a different company, and no identity is proposed between the two.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps all five of them in one entry of four sentences on the Julian 5 November, names the see of each, and gives no year for any. They were of the seventy apostles, it says, and all of them fulfilled the law of Christ with love and passed over into the kingdom of Christ. The see it gives Hermas is not the one this folder carries: bishop in Philippopolis, citing the epistle to the Romans, where the Russian calendar made him bishop of Philippi in Macedonia. Philippopolis stands in Thrace and Philippi in Macedonia, so this is two cities and not two spellings; the folder's recorded office is unchanged and both readings stand.
