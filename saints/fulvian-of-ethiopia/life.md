# Fulvian of Ethiopia

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 16 ноября ст. ст. in a line that carries both his names: «Прав. Фулвиана , кн. Ефиопского, во св. Крещении Матфея (I).» He has no life page of his own. What is told of him is told in the life the day gives to [Matthew the Apostle](/saints/matthew-the-apostle), where he is the ruler of the country in which the apostle ended his preaching, and the page is titled for the two of them together.

He is in that story first as the persecutor. The apostle, carrying to the church the staff the Lord had given him, met Fulvian’s wife and son on the road held by an unclean spirit and healed them in the name of Jesus Christ, and the wonder turned a multitude of the pagans to the Lord. But the ruler would not have his subjects become Christians and leave the gods; he charged the apostle with sorcery and had him put to death, laid face down under brushwood and set alight. The fire did the saint no harm, and when Fulvian had more brushwood piled on and pitch poured over it and twelve idols set around, the flame melted the idols and scorched him.

He begged the saint for mercy and at the apostle’s prayer the fire lay down; and then, the page says, he repented bitterly of what he had done and still did not leave his doubts. He had the body laid in an iron coffin and thrown into the sea, saying that if Matthew’s God kept it in the water as He had kept it in the fire, then that one true God was the God to worship. That same night the apostle appeared in a dream to the bishop he had set over the church and told him to go down to the shore with his clergy and find the body there.

Fulvian came to the shore too, with his retinue. The wave carried the coffin out, it was borne with honour into the church the apostle had built, and he asked the apostle’s forgiveness; and the bishop baptised him with the name Matthew, which he gave him at God’s own command. Soon he gave up his power and became a presbyter, and when the bishop died the apostle appeared to him again and urged him to take charge of the Church of Ethiopia. He accepted the episcopate and laboured much in the preaching of the word of God, carrying on the work of the apostle he calls his heavenly patron.

The calendar’s line calls him righteous and a prince; the title on the name link behind it, name page 2340, calls him «Святитель Фулвиан (в крещении Матфей) Эфиопский, князь, епископ» — a hierarch, a prince and a bishop. Both readings are set down here and neither is chosen over the other. No office is recorded: the see the page gives him is a country and not a city, and this corpus mints no office it cannot print in five languages.
