# Sebastiana of Heraclea

Sebastiana was a disciple of the apostle Paul, the Russian calendar says. In Domitian’s persecution she was tried as a Christian by the governor George at Marcianopolis in Moesia, confessed Christ firmly and was tortured: first beaten, then thrown into a burning furnace, from which she came out unharmed. She was sent on to Heraclea and tried a second time. The governor Pompeian had her hung on a tree and her body scraped with potsherds, and when she did not waver he gave her to the beasts, which did not touch her; at last, on his order, she was beheaded. Her body was thrown into the sea, and angels carried it to the island of Rhodes, which the life places in Thrace, on the Sea of Marmara.

The calendar dates her about 86–96.

The Romanian life, which doxologia.ro prints for 24 octombrie, tells the same story and differs in two details: it calls the governor Sergius rather than George, and it ends with the body's recovery. An angel of the Lord tore open the sack of lead that had been thrown into the sea and brought her body ashore at a place called Risiston, where a noblewoman named Amia, learning of it, wrapped the relics in clean linen with costly spices and laid them in that same place.
