# Leo of Nicaea

In the year 815, under Leo the Armenian the iconoclast, the Bulgarians came down to make war on Byzantium. Their leader was Krum, and they took Adrianople; for three days, saint.gr says, the bloodthirsty slaughtered the Christians, and after Krum's death his successors Doukoumos, and after him Ditzengos, showed the same beastly behaviour to the unhappy Christians. Ditzengos was succeeded in power by Mourtagon — Omurtag.

Of Leo, bishop of Nicaea, the page says one thing and it is the manner of his death: Mourtagon ripped his belly open with a sword. He is named in the day's list after [Manuel](/saints/manuel-of-adrianople) of Adrianople and the two other bishops, and the same list names a second Leo, whom the life calls one of the two generals beheaded by Mourtagon's own hand; that [Leo](/saints/leo-companion-of-manuel) has his own folder here, because the source counts them as two names.

No year of his birth is printed, nothing of his episcopate at Nicaea, and no hymn.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same menologion day, fourth in the company's one line and the bishop among the Leos there, as he is the bishop among them on the Greek day. The two calendars do not agree on his see: the Greek page has him bishop of Nicaea, while the Russian name link calls him Адрианопольский, of Adrianople, and gives his name in two forms, Леонтий and Леон. The place in the list and the rank are the same in both, so the row below is read as his.
