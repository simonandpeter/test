# Nicholas from the Soldiers

saint.gr's calendar for 24 December keeps Nicholas, whom it calls «ὁ ἀπὸ στρατιωτῶν», the one from among the soldiers, and puts beside the name the words «and a profitable narrative». He was a soldier, it says, and took part in the war against the Bulgarians under Nikephoros the Logothete, whose reign it dates 802 to 811. On a march he lodged for the night at an inn; and in the night the innkeeper's daughter came at him with sinful intent, and he held himself in and did not defile his body with the shameful act she was calling and goading him to.

For that he was granted a vision in the night which rewarded his purity. Nikodemos the Hagiorite, whom the page quotes whole, tells it at length: that she came three times and was three times sent away; that the next night he saw a judge seated near him with his right foot laid over his left, who showed him the armies, the Romans on one side and the Bulgarians on the other, and the Romans cutting the Bulgarians down; and that when the judge changed his feet about, the left over the right, Nicholas looked again and saw the Bulgarians cutting down the Romans.

When the slaughter was over the figure told him to look well at the places of the fallen. He saw the whole ground full of the bodies of killed Romans, and among them one green and lovely space the size of one man's bed; and he was told that the bed was his own, that he was to have fallen in it with his fellow soldiers, and that because he had shaken off and beaten the threefold serpent in the night before, he had delivered himself from that death. He woke in fear, turned back a day's journey and prayed on a mountain for the Roman army; and the Bulgarians came upon the Romans by night, unguarded and careless after a victory, and put nearly all of them to the sword together with the emperor Nikephoros.

Remembering the vision, Nicholas gave thanks to God and turned back weeping and lamenting. Then he went to a monastery, received the angelic habit of the monks, and having served God truly for years enough, the page says, he became a most discerning and great father. The short account adds only that he came home from the war safe and unhurt, withdrew to a monastery, and having lived a holy life died in peace. No hymn is printed for him, and the feast is marked fixed.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 24 Δεκεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/12/24/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3226/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
