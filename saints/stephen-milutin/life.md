# Stephen Milutin

Stephen Milutin was the son of Uroš the First and of the princess Helen, and his brother was Dragutin. He fought many wars in defence of the Christian faith and of his people. He fought against the emperor Michael Palaiologos, because that emperor had accepted the unlawful union with Rome and was trying to draw the Balkan peoples, and even the monks of Athos, into acknowledging that impure union as well.

King Milutin fought also in defence of his own borders against Shishman, the chief of the Bulgarians, and against Nogai, the chief of the Tatars. He came out of all his wars the victor, because he put all his hope in the one God alone, before whom he prayed always and fervently.

He founded more than forty churches. Besides those he built on his own estates — the monasteries of Treskavac and of Gračanica, Saint George at Nagoričino, the church of the Mother of God at Skopje, the church at Banjska and others — he built churches outside the borders of Serbia as well: at Thessalonica, at Sofia, at Constantinople, at Jerusalem and on the Holy Mountain of Athos.

He departed to the Lord on the twenty-ninth day of the month of October in the year 1320. His body was shown soon afterwards to be incorrupt and a worker of wonders, and it rests today in the Church of the Saviour in Sofia, the capital of Bulgaria.
