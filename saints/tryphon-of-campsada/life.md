# Tryphon of Campsada

Tryphon was born to right-believing parents at Campsada, a village near Apamea in Phrygia, the Romanian life says, and from infancy God gave him the grace of the Holy Spirit and the gift of wonders: he healed every sickness, and above all had power over demons, which fled from the sufferers as soon as they heard his name. It tells one wonder at length. The emperor Gordian, who worshipped idols but did not persecute the Christians, had a beautiful and learned daughter, Gordiana, and a demon entered her and tormented her without rest, throwing her into fire and water, and no physician could help. The demon itself cried out that no one could drive it out but the young Tryphon, and when the emperor’s men had searched the world and brought many of that name to no purpose, they found him at Campsada, tending geese by a spring, seventeen years old, and brought him to Rome. The demon left the girl before he arrived. The emperor asked to see it with his own eyes, and after six days of fasting and prayer Tryphon made it appear before them all as a black dog with eyes like fire, and made it confess that demons have no power over those who believe in Christ, but only over those who serve their passions. Many believed; Tryphon gave the emperor’s gifts to the poor on his way home and went back to healing the sick.

Under Decius a denunciation reached Aquilinus, eparch of the East, that Tryphon was a Christian who went about healing and turning people to Christ. He did not hide, but gave himself up and went gladly to Aquilinus at Nicaea, where he confessed his name, his homeland and his faith. He was hung up and beaten for three hours without a groan; dragged barefoot in bitter frost behind the eparch’s horse on a hunt; imprisoned; led through the city with sharp nails driven into his feet, in winter snow; and hung up again, beaten with rods and burned with torches, until a light shone from heaven and a crown came down on his head and his torturers fell back in fear. When he still would not sacrifice to Zeus or bow to the emperor’s image, Aquilinus had him sentenced to beheading. He prayed facing east, and the Lord took his soul before the sword fell. The Christians of Nicaea wanted to keep his body to protect their city, but he appeared to them and told them to carry it to Campsada, where he was born, and they did.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 29 September and on 1 February, with a troparion, which is the common of one martyr, and a kontakion. Its life gives no year for his death. Whether he is the Tryphon the Greek calendar keeps on the same day with Trophimus and Dorymedon is a question neither source answers.
