# Tiburtius (18 December)

saint.gr's calendar for 18 December keeps ten people together and names every one of them: Nikostratos and his wife Zoe, Tranquillinus and his wife Marcia, Claudius, Tiburtius, Castor, Castulus, Marcus and Marcellinus. They all suffered, the page says, in the persecution Diocletian set in motion against the Christians, whose years it gives as 284 to 304, and they suffered together with Saint [Sebastian](/saints/sebastian-of-rome), whom the same day's calendar keeps.

Tiburtius was put to death by stoning, as the page's prose has it, while its epigram for him has them cut off his divine head as he cried, you are my God, O Word of God.

That is the whole of what the page gives for this company: the ten names, the one reign, and for each of them the manner of the death. It names no city and no judge, gives no year of birth for any of them and no year inside the reign, lists no relic and prints no hymn of its own; the day's apolytikion stands on Sebastian's page and names Sebastian. The others of the ten here are [Nicostratus](/saints/nicostratus-18-december), [Tranquillinus](/saints/tranquillinus), [Marcia](/saints/marcia-wife-of-tranquillinus), [Claudius](/saints/claudius-18-december), [Castor](/saints/castor-18-december), [Castulus](/saints/castulus-18-december), [Marcus](/saints/marcus-son-of-tranquillinus), [Marcellinus](/saints/marcellinus-son-of-tranquillinus), and [Zoe](/saints/zoe-18-december), whom this corpus already kept from the Romanian calendar.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, inside the heading „Свети мученик Севастијан и други с њим.“ — Sebastian and the others with him — and names him in the body. He was the son of Chromatius, the eparch of Rome, whom [Sebastian](/saints/sebastian-of-rome) healed of the same gout he had healed Tranquillinus of, and who came to baptism with this son of his.

He was taken second of the company, after [Zoe](/saints/zoe-18-december). The judge set hot coals before him and told him to choose life or death: either to throw incense and burn it to the idols, or to stand barefoot on the coals himself. He crossed himself and stood barefoot on them, and was unhurt; and afterwards he was beheaded with the sword. His is the one ordeal in the whole entry that the book tells out at length, and it gives no year for it.
