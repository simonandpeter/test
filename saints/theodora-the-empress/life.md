# Theodora the Empress

On this day, doxologia's life begins, is kept the memory of the holy empress Theodora, who brought back the right faith. She was the wife of the emperor Theophilus, the fighter against the holy icons, but she did not follow her husband in his heresy: keeping the holy icons in secret, she venerated them in right belief. After her husband's death she gathered a council of holy priests and brought the holy icons back into the church.

Then she took pains that the soul of her dead husband should be delivered from its torments by the prayers of many holy fathers — of which, the page says, there is a full account in the Synaxarion for the first Sunday of the great and holy Fast. Having done many good works and pleased God, Saint Theodora left the earthly empire to her son Michael, and passed over to the eternity of heaven.

That is the whole of what the page prints of her. It gives no year, no place of birth or death, and no hymn: the «Tropar» tab holds a sung video whose only words are the invocation «Sfântă împărăteasă Teodora, roagă-te lui Dumnezeu pentru noi!», which is not a troparion with its tone, and so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and gives the dates and the family the Romanian page did not: saint.gr heads her «(815 - 867)», puts her birth at Ebessa in Paphlagonia of the droungarios Marinos and the virtuous Theoktiste, names her sisters Sophia, Maria and Irene and her brothers Bardas and Petronas, and has her married in 830 to the iconoclast emperor Theophilus, keeping and hiding the holy icons in her own rooms all the while. On his death in 842 she took the empire and the guardianship of her under-age son Michael, removed the iconoclast patriarch John and had Methodius elected; the synod of 11 March 843 restored the icons, and the great festival was held on the first Sunday of the Fast, which has been the Sunday of Orthodoxy since. At the end her own son and her brother Bardas shut her up with her daughters Anna, Thekla, Anastasia, Maria and Pulcheria in the monastery of Gastria and had her tonsured; she gave herself there to prayer and ascesis, and her incorrupt relic is kept in the church of the Panagia Speliotissa on Corfu.

The Serbian church keeps her on this day. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović names her the Greek empress, wife of the wicked iconoclast emperor Theophilus, and says that after his death she reigned with her son Michael III and at once restored the veneration of the icons at a council in Constantinople in 842. On that occasion, it adds, the feast of the victory of Orthodoxy was instituted, which is kept to this day on the first Sunday of the Fast.

This holy woman, who deserved well of the Church, gave up her soul to God on 11 February 867. The Prologue closes the entry by setting her reign beside what came of it: that in that time of the full triumph of Orthodoxy over every heresy, by God's wonderful providence, missionaries were sent among the Slavs.
