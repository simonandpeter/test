# Mary of Egypt

Her life is told through the monk Zosimas, who had lived in a Palestinian monastery near Caesarea from childhood and struggled there until he was fifty-three, when he was troubled by the thought that he had reached perfection and had no one left to teach him — was there a monk anywhere, he asked, who could show him an asceticism he had not attained, or who had surpassed him in sobriety and in labour?

An angel came to him and said that he had struggled valiantly, as far as that is in the power of man, but that there is no one who is righteous; and that so he might learn how many other roads lead to salvation, he should leave his own country as Abraham left his father's house, and go to the monastery by the Jordan.

There he found elders practised in contemplation and in struggle, who never spoke an idle word but sang continually and prayed the night through, and he set himself to imitate them. It was from that monastery that he went into the desert and met Mary, whose life he afterwards told.

Doxologia prints the whole of the Life as Saint Sophronius wrote it, opening with his own warning that it is a fearful thing to hide the king's secret and a loss to the soul to keep silent about the works of God, and closing with what happened after Mary died: Zosimas, old and without a tool, could make no impression on the dry ground, and a great lion came and stood by her body and licked her feet. He asked the beast to dig with its claws, and it dug with its forepaws a grave deep enough to cover her; then the lion went off into the inner desert as quietly as a sheep, and Zosimas returned to his monastery and told the monks everything, and they began to keep the day of her death. He himself died in that monastery when he was nearly a hundred, leaving the account unwritten; it passed from mouth to mouth until Sophronius set it down.
