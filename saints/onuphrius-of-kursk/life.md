# Onuphrius (Maksimovich)

Onuphrius, called Antony Maksimovich in the world, was born on 2 April 1889 in the province of Kholm. saint.gr says that in 1915 he finished the ecclesiastical academy of Saint Petersburg and that in 1915 he was ordained presbyter and set over a parish — the page gives the same year for both. On 8 January 1923 he was elected bishop of Odessa, and there, the page says, he was of spiritual profit to the people of the city and to its young people in particular.

He was arrested by the Bolshevik regime in 1924 but released, and passed through the ordeal of arrest again in 1927. In 1934 he was elected bishop of the city of Kursk, in 1935 he was moved to the Urals, and in 1938 he was killed for his activity by the regime's men. The page prints no hymn to him and names no place where he was buried.

The Russian calendar's life page gives his name in the world and his career year by year. He was Антоний Максимович Гагалюк, born on 2 April 1889 in the province of Kholm, and he finished the St Petersburg Theological Academy in 1915. On 8 January 1923 he was consecrated bishop of Elisavetgrad — Kirovograd — as vicar of the Odessa diocese, and the page says that his short service at Krivoy Rog was a triumph of Orthodoxy outright: old and young filled the church where he served to bursting, and the young forgot their amusements and hurried to hear their bishop.

In the summer of 1924 he was arrested, and the people saw the train with their arrested bishop off with weeping, many of them falling on their faces. A year later he was appointed bishop of Elisavetgrad again, and in 1927 arrested again. He was known as a fighter against the Renovationist movement. From 1929 he was bishop of Stary Oskol and from 1933 bishop of Kursk, and on 17 January 1934 he was raised to archbishop of Kursk and Oboyan. From 22 November 1935 he was in exile in the Urals, at Krasnoyarsk, and on 19 May 1938 he was shot.

The page adds that he was glorified as a locally venerated saint by a decision of the Holy Synod of the Ukrainian Orthodox Church of 8 (22) June 1993, and numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for general veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops of the Russian Orthodox Church in August 2000.
