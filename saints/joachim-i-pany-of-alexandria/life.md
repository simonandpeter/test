# Joachim I Pany

Joachim the Pany — the very great — was the first patriarch of Alexandria
after the Turkish conquest of Egypt in 1517, when Sultan Selim I defeated the
Mamelukes and took from them the lordship of Egypt and the caliphate. Born at
Athens in 1448, he was a monk at the monastery of Sinai and at the Lavra of
Sabbas the Sanctified in Palestine, and ascended the ancient apostolic
throne of St Mark in August 1487. He spent himself on the reorganisation of
the Patriarchate with a sharp and practical mind; he sat at the council of
Jerusalem of 1526, at which the so-called Sinaitic question was argued and
settled; he sought and secured support for his hard-pressed throne from the
tsar Basil; and he stoutly resisted the approaches, above all of the
Venetian archbishop of Crete Peter Lando, to consent to the union of the
churches and sit in 1562 at the Roman Catholic council of Trent. That stand
deepened still further the church’s sense of his holiness and of his hold on
the faith of the fathers, and chroniclers of the day record wonders worked
by his faith. He died in extreme old age in 1567, in the hundred and
seventeenth year of his life, having been patriarch for eighty years. His
name was entered in the church’s calendar by a patriarchal and synodal act
of 17 September 2002, under Patriarch Peter VII, and a service composed by
Metropolitan Cyril of Rhodes was published in 2009 under Patriarch Theodore
II in his predecessor’s memory; his precious skull is treasured at the
monastery of St Sabbas. The Greek calendar keeps him on 17 September.
