# Dioscorus (19 April)

Dioscorus was a priest of the idols, saint.gr says, in the years of the emperor Antoninus, and he came to the Christian faith through the wonders of [Theodore of Perga](/saints/theodore-of-perga) and was martyred by fire. That sentence is the whole of the page's account of him: «ο Άγιος Διόσκορος ήταν Ιερέας των ειδώλων στα χρόνια του βασιλιά Άντωνίνου (138-160) και ήλθε στη χριστιανική πίστη διά των θαυμάτων του Θεοδώρου και μαρτύρησε δια πυρός».

The wonders meant are the ones the page tells of Theodore, who came from Perge in Pamphylia and went about destroying idols and their temples. He was stretched on a red-hot gridiron, dragged behind wild horses, thrown into a furnace with the two soldiers [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-socrates) who had believed, imprisoned, and at the last crucified and shot with arrows. His mother [Philippa](/saints/philippa-mother-of-theodore) confessed Christ after his death and was beheaded.

Dioscorus is given no city, no year of his own and no judge, and the page prints no hymn. It dates the reign of Antoninus 138 to 161 where it speaks of Theodore and 138 to 160 where it speaks of Dioscorus, and those two readings are the only chronology it offers for either of them.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the young martyr alone — „Св. муч. Теодор.“ — and then opens its body with „И други с њим.“, *and others with him*, before naming the rest. [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-perga) suffered for the faith of Christ at Perga in Pamphylia in the time of the emperor Antoninus. He was young and fair of face; and when the governor of that region chose him with other youths to be sent for service at the imperial court, he refused and declared that he was a Christian. For that he was tortured with various torments and then thrown on a fire — but water welled up out of the earth and quenched it.

The governor put that down to some magic of Theodore's, and the martyr answered him: this is not the work of my power but of Christ my God; and if you wish to know the power of your gods, lay another fire and throw in one of your own soldiers, and you will know their power and the omnipotence of my God. The governor meant to do it; but the soldiers begged him in fear to throw in the priest Dioscorus instead of them, and Dioscorus begged him to throw in the idol of Zeus and the rest of the idols, for if they are gods they will easily save themselves — which he said because his heart was already turned to Christ, having seen the wonder of the fire.

Learning this, the governor condemned Dioscorus to death and burned him; and he gave Theodore to death too, with the two soldiers [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-socrates), and with [Philippa](/saints/philippa-mother-of-theodore), Theodore's mother. Theodore they stretched on a cross, on which he gave up his spirit only on the third day; Socrates and Dionysius were run through with a spear, and Philippa was beheaded with the sword. All of them, the entry ends, were crowned with the crowns of glory in the kingdom of Christ.

What the entry adds to this folder is who Dioscorus was and how he came to die. He was a жрец, a pagan priest, whom the frightened soldiers offered to the fire in their own place; and he answered by asking that the idol of Zeus be thrown in instead, because, having seen the wonder of the fire, his heart was already turned to Christ. For that the governor condemned him and burned him. **No type was added**: жрец is what he was before, not a rank in the Church, and the `martyr` this folder carries is what the entry makes of him. The entry gives no year for this company, only „у време цара Антонина“, in the time of the emperor Antoninus. More than one Roman emperor bore that name, so no interval was read out of the reign and no date was added here; this folder stays undated, as it was.
