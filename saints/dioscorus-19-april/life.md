# Dioscorus (19 April)

Dioscorus was a priest of the idols, saint.gr says, in the years of the emperor Antoninus, and he came to the Christian faith through the wonders of [Theodore of Perga](/saints/theodore-of-perga) and was martyred by fire. That sentence is the whole of the page's account of him: «ο Άγιος Διόσκορος ήταν Ιερέας των ειδώλων στα χρόνια του βασιλιά Άντωνίνου (138-160) και ήλθε στη χριστιανική πίστη διά των θαυμάτων του Θεοδώρου και μαρτύρησε δια πυρός».

The wonders meant are the ones the page tells of Theodore, who came from Perge in Pamphylia and went about destroying idols and their temples. He was stretched on a red-hot gridiron, dragged behind wild horses, thrown into a furnace with the two soldiers [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-socrates) who had believed, imprisoned, and at the last crucified and shot with arrows. His mother [Philippa](/saints/philippa-mother-of-theodore) confessed Christ after his death and was beheaded.

Dioscorus is given no city, no year of his own and no judge, and the page prints no hymn. It dates the reign of Antoninus 138 to 161 where it speaks of Theodore and 138 to 160 where it speaks of Dioscorus, and those two readings are the only chronology it offers for either of them.
