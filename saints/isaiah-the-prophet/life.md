# Isaiah

Isaiah was of Jerusalem by descent and came of the stock of the kings of Judah. The Romanian life is careful about his father: he was the son of Amoz, not of the prophet Amos, for that Amos was a Tekoan and a herdsman and is honoured among the holy prophets, while this Amoz was not numbered among them but was a kinsman of the king of Jerusalem and the full brother of Amaziah. Growing up in the fear of God and taught in the law of the Lord, Isaiah married lawfully when he came of age and had sons.

His wife the life calls a prophetess for two reasons: because she was a prophet's wife, and because she had herself received the gift of prophecy from God, having served in the temple of the Lord from among the company of virgins before her marriage, with good and holy works and unceasing prayer. Of the sons, the prophecy names Iashuv. Blessed Jerome is quoted for a Hebrew account that Isaiah was, on his mother's side, the grandfather of Manasseh who tortured him, for Manasseh was born of his daughter Ophovia, the wife of King Hezekiah.

He prophesied in the days of Azariah, also called Uzziah, the son of Amaziah; and in the days of Jotham son of Uzziah; and of Ahaz son of Jotham; and of Hezekiah son of Ahaz; and of Manasseh son of Hezekiah, at whose hands he suffered. He was a great wonderworker too: when Jerusalem was threatened with siege by the foreigners and was short of water, he brought a spring out from under Mount Sion by his prayer — Epiphanius and Dorotheus of Tyre are cited for it — and it was called Siloam, that is, sent, because God sent it to the thirsty people for the prophet's prayers; and the spring was wonderful, for it gave water to the Jews and went dry for the foreigners.

After Hezekiah's death Manasseh took the kingdom of Judah at twelve years old and did wickedly: he built temples to the idols and an altar to Baal, defiled the house of the Lord with sacrifices and passed his own sons through the fire. The prophet rebuked him and the people, telling them that by their unclean deeds they had become like Sodom and Gomorrah; and they, with the king, could not bear the rebuke and sought to kill him. He was cut in two with a wooden saw in the field outside Jerusalem, by Manasseh's order, and so ended as a martyr in deep old age, being a hundred and twenty-six years old.

God-fearing men took his honourable body and buried it near that spring of Siloam which he had drawn out by his prayer, so that by his prayers the water of Siloam might flow unceasingly after his death as well. Beside the spring a pool was built, the one to which the Lord sent the man blind from birth to wash the clay from his eyes; and to this day, the page says, that water has a healing power. It gives no year for anything in the account.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and its page adds a great deal the Romanian line did not. He was the son of Amos, saint.gr says, born at Jerusalem about 774 before Christ, the first of the four great prophets and the most brilliant and most great-voiced of them; the name, in Hebrew Yeshayahu, means «God saves». An old rabbinic tradition made his father a brother of Amaziah king of the Jews, and his daughter the wife of king Manasseh — traditions the page itself calls legends rather than historical truths, while allowing that they point to his noble birth. He was married and had two sons, who are named in his own prophecies and who were given symbolic names by God's command: the first Jasoub, which the Seventy render «the remnant shall return», meaning that the Jews left in the captivity would come back to their country; the other Maher-shalal-hash-baz, «spoil quickly, plunder sharply», in token of the coming raid of the Assyrians and Babylonians against Jerusalem.

He was called to his prophetic ministry about 738 before Christ, in the last year of the reign of Uzziah and the first of the reign of Jotham, and he tells the story of that call himself. Standing in the temple he saw the Lord seated on a high throne while the house was flooded with the exceeding light of the divine glory, and the six-winged Seraphim stood about the throne calling and answering one another, glorifying God and saying: holy, holy is the Lord of Sabaoth, the whole earth is full of his glory. Before that majestic sight Isaiah was overcome with awe, thought on his own unworthiness as a man, and cried out that he, a man of unclean lips, had been counted worthy to see the King, the Lord of Sabaoth; and after that humble confession one of the Seraphim took with the tongs a burning coal from the altar where the fragrant incense was burning, touched his lips with it and said: behold, this has touched thy lips, and it shall take away thine iniquities and shall cleanse thee wholly and blot out thy sins from thee.

His work ran on through the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, and perhaps of Manasseh too, by whom, it is said, he was condemned to death and executed with a wooden saw for reproving him publicly for his impiety. The age was a hard one for the kingdom of Israel: the priests were drunkards, the false prophets ran riot, the rulers were thieves, and those hypocrites who fasted and sacrificed while being unjust and unmerciful had multiplied and worked together in the corruption. So his task throughout was to rebuke that sinfulness, to condemn apostasy and idolatry with the utmost severity, to foretell afflictions against a people in revolt, and to call them to repentance and return; and in seasons of plain danger, of invasion and of servitude, to encourage the disheartened, rekindle faith and obedience, and cultivate the hope of deliverance. But what marks him out most strongly, the page says, are his many and most pure prophecies of Christ: enlightened by the All-Holy Spirit he foretold the birth of the Redeemer from a Virgin and his name Emmanuel, which means God with us, for which the Fathers called him the Evangelical Prophet and his prophecies the Gospel according to Isaiah. His holy relic was carried to Constantinople under the emperor Theodosius II, who reigned from 408 to 450, and laid in the church of the holy martyr Laurence near the Blachernae; and a fragment of his skull is at the monastery of Chilandar on the Holy Mountain.

The Ohrid Prologue, read for the Julian 9 May, opens by calling him a man of royal kin: born at Jerusalem of his father Amos, the brother of Amaziah king of Judah. The Greek page had the same kinship but set it down among the rabbinic traditions it calls legends rather than historical truths, and the two readings are left standing side by side here. It has the vision of the Lord of Sabaoth on the heavenly throne among the six-winged Seraphim who sing without ceasing, holy, holy, holy is the Lord of Sabaoth; and it says that he prophesied many things, to single men and to whole nations alike.

One of those prophecies it tells at length, and no other book read for this folder has it. He walked three days wholly naked through the streets of Jerusalem, foretelling the city's imminent fall under Sennacherib king of Assyria, and warning the king and the chiefs of the people not to lean on the help of the Egyptians and the Ethiopians, since those too would soon be subdued by that same Sennacherib, but to lean on the help of God the Most High. That prophecy, it says, like all the rest, was fulfilled to the letter; though it holds his greatest to be those of the incarnation of God, of the conceiving of the All-Holy Virgin, of the one who was to go before the Lord, and of many events in the life of Christ.

For the purity of his heart and his zeal towards God, the book says, this far-seeing man received from above the gift of wonderworking besides, which is how the spring came out from under Mount Sion for the besieged and thirsting city and was called Siloam, that is, sent. Under Manasseh, when he thundered against the heathen customs of the king and the chiefs and set that generation beside Sodom and Gomorrah, the anger of the chiefs and of the people rose against him, and he was seized, led outside Jerusalem and sawn through with a saw. He lived and prophesied, the book closes, seven hundred years before Christ, and it sends the reader to the book of the prophet Isaiah in the Old Testament.
