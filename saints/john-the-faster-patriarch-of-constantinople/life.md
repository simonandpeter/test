# John IV the Faster, Patriarch of Constantinople

John IV the Faster, patriarch of Constantinople from 582 to 595, is known in the Orthodox Church as the compiler of the Penitential Nomocanon, the book of penances, which has come down in several redactions on one foundation: a guide for confessors on how to receive secret confession of secret sins, committed or only intended — where the old canons spoke of public penance for open, convicted sinners, John took the voluntary confession of sins unknown to the world as itself a sign of readiness to be reconciled to God and conscience, and shortened the ancient penances accordingly. The Greek synaxarion says he was marked from childhood by a rare self-restraint and a natural love of fasting, whence his name; born in Constantinople, an engraver by trade as a youth (a goldsmith, the Prologue says), his heart given to divine things and the daily reading of Scripture; the patriarch John III ordained him deacon, and in that office he gave alms to crowds of the poor, then became presbyter, and after the blessed Eutychius was chosen patriarch — he would not accept until a heavenly vision frightened him into it. To his death a great faster, man of prayer and wonderworker, he reposed in 595, and all that was found of his was a wooden spoon, a linen shirt and an old garment. His writings on repentance and confession are famous. All four calendars keep him on 2 September, and again on 30 August with Alexander and Paul the New.
