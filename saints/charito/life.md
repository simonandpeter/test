# Charito

saint.gr's calendar for 1 Ιουνίου prints eight names in one line — [Justus](/saints/justus-1-june), [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), Charito the virgin, [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june), [Paion](/saints/paion) and [Liberianus](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin), whom the page says others call Valerianus — and the life behind the line is one paragraph. These, it says, received the martyrs' crowns together with [Justin the philosopher and apologist](/saints/justin-the-philosopher), whom the same calendar keeps on the same day, in the persecution against the Christians under Marcus Aurelius.

They belonged to the brave phalanx of the Christians of Rome, where only genuine Christian courage could keep a man from being dismayed by the power of the idol-worship that the palace and the army upheld. Having confessed Christ repeatedly, they were condemned to die by beheading; and all of them underwent that martyrdom untroubled and joyful, in the conviction that the immortal goods of heaven awaited them, which the heavenly Father gives to all who have lived on earth in faith and love.

That paragraph is the whole of the account, and it is shared by every folder in the company. Beside it the page prints a short epigram over each name, and Charito's is shared with Chariton: the divine Chariton and Charito the virgin, cut down, have the good fortune of divine graces — a play on the name they have between them.

Of Charito herself the calendar states two things and no more: that she was a virgin, and that she was one of the eight. It gives her no age, no parentage, no year and no separate death, and it prints no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the whole company in one heading of eight names: [Justin](/saints/justin-companion-of-chariton), [Chariton](/saints/chariton-companion-of-justin), Charito, [Euelpistus](/saints/euelpistus), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-1-june), [Paeon](/saints/paion), [Valerian](/saints/valerian-companion-of-justin) and [Justus](/saints/justus-1-june). All of them suffered at Rome, it says, in the time of Marcus Aurelius and of Rusticus the eparch. When Rusticus asked whether they thought that if they died for Christ they would receive a reward in heaven, Justin answered for them all: we do not think, we know. After that they were all beheaded, in the year 163, and passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ God.

She is named third, between Chariton and Euelpistus, and the heading gives her one bare name and no rank of her own; the masculine rank word at its head covers the whole company. Nothing in this book calls her a virgin, so what this folder records of her virginity stays the Greek calendar’s word and not two books’. The year 163 is the first any book has given her and is recorded here as the death.
