# Alexander the Presbyter (22 August)

Alexander Ratkovsky was born in 1870 in the village of Oprosyevo in the
Novorzhev district of Pskov province, the son of a deacon, Matthew
Ratkovsky. He finished the seminary in 1891 and was ordained deacon, and
priest in 1897; in 1906 he was appointed to one of the churches of Toropets,
where he served all his life until his martyr’s death. On 19 March 1931 he
was arrested among other Orthodox — Bishop Tikhon of Velikiye Luki, priests,
deacons, monks and lay people — whom the authorities bundled into an
“organisation” they named “the Strengthening of the Christian Movement”.
Questioned on 2 April, he said: my parents were of the clergy, my father a
deacon in the Novorzhev district, where he died; I came to Toropets in 1906
and serve here still; my family is eight — three sons, Anatoly a priest at
Sevastyanovo, now exiled by the OGPU, Nicholas at Kremenchuk, where he
works I do not know, Vladimir on a state farm in Novgorod province, and
four daughters in Soviet jobs; the clergy and nuns of Toropets I know, and
they are under arrest now, but I would be hard put to name others; I have
kept no particular acquaintance with anyone, living on the edge of the
town, though people came to me on business; more I cannot say. He was
sentenced to three years’ exile in Kazakhstan, came back to Toropets in
1934, and served in the same church, where church life revived with his
return — the people saw in him a confessor of the faith, he restored the
services according to the rule, the nuns sang in the choir and one of them
he made warden. That was enough. The NKVD arrested him on 23 July 1937 and
put him in the Toropets prison, and the next day the investigator asked his
relations with Bishop John (Troyansky) and the other priests — normal, he
said; the bishop had been to his flat and he to the bishop’s, on religious
matters; whether the bishop had handed out sweets to children in his church
to recruit worshippers — yes, he admitted the bishop had given the children
sweets in his church, but he did not consider it anything abnormal; whether
the bishop preached there — yes, the bishop’s sermons were an ordinary
thing, and what they contained he was hard put to say, since at that moment
he was consuming the holy gifts. On 31 August the NKVD troika sentenced him
to be shot, and he was shot on 4 September 1937 — 22 August of the old
style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him, with the bishops John and
Alexius and their company. The Jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000
numbered him among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia.
