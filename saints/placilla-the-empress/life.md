# Placilla (Flacilla) the Empress

Placilla was the wife of Theodosius the Great (379–395), the mother of the
emperors Arcadius and Honorius and of a daughter, Pulcheria, who died in
childhood in 385, and a deeply devout Christian, very charitable. All her
life she cared for the poor and gave without stint to those in need what
they required, and she did not only give money: she visited the poor herself
and nursed the sick with her own hands. Some found that beneath the dignity
of an imperial person and told her that money was enough from her and that
her own labour for beggars was out of place; I know, she answered, that it
befits the imperial rank to give generous help to the needy, but I think it
my duty to give my own labour for the sake of him who was pleased to raise
me to that rank. Having filled her life with the most beneficent activity
she died in peace about 386, and Gregory of Nyssa spoke her funeral oration.
So, having lived as a good Samaritan, the Greek synaxarion says, she died in
peace — leaving the corruptible crown of earth, the verse says, Placilla
found an incorruptible crown in heaven. The Greek calendar keeps her on 14
September, the feast of the Exaltation; the Russian church calendar prints
her page on 14 September of the old style, 27 September, and notes that she
is not in the present Russian menologion.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, calls her the wife of the emperor Theodosius I and a true Christian in mind and deed, above all in helping the poor and the sick. When some told her this did not befit an empress’s dignity, she answered that it became the imperial rank to help with money, but her own labours for the poor she gave to Him who had been pleased to give her that rank. She died in peace, it says, about 400, where the folder’s other sources put her death about 386.
