# Julitta of Caesarea

Julitta came of a very rich family, and for that reason a man of Caesarea in Cappadocia coveted her property. She went to law against him for what was hers and called him before the judge. But the decrees of the emperor Diocletian held Christians to be outside the laws and outside civil rights; and the man, having no way of defending himself, accused Julitta of being a Christian.

The judge, wanting to satisfy himself that it was so, called for fire and incense so that she should sacrifice to the idols. Because she refused to do it, she was thrown into the fire. Her body was found almost whole, so that it is thought she may have died of the smoke. She was buried in the porch of the great church in Caesarea.

That is all doxologia.ro prints of her. The page gives no year and names no other person, and the day's service on that site carries no hymn of hers.
