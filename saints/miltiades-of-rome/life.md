# Miltiades of Rome

saint.gr says that Miltiades, or Melchiades, was pope of Rome from 311 to 314, that he came from Africa, and that he is known as Melchiades the African. It glosses the name from μίλτος, the red dye: the blood-red, the brave — and notes in passing that it was borne by the famous commander-in-chief of the Athenians, the victor at Marathon.

By him, the page says, a synod was convoked at the Lateran in Rome in the year 313, which condemned Donatus and acquitted Caecilian, bishop of Carthage. In his time the edicts of the Roman emperors Galerius, Constantine and Licinius on freedom of religion were issued. The page then adds that Miltiades or Melchiades was martyred, according to the Roman Martyrology, under the emperor Maximinus, whose reign it gives as 307 to 313, and that he was buried on the Appian Way, near the cemetery of Saint Callistus.

Those two statements do not fit together on the page's own numbers: the pontificate it gives runs a year past the reign of the persecutor it names. Because the page settles nothing between them, nothing is settled here either; the martyrdom is reported as the page reports it, without being made a rank, and the only years recorded are the ones it gives for the pontificate. The page prints no couplet and no hymn, and the same day's calendar keeps a second Miltiades among the martyrs of Carthage, who is another man.
