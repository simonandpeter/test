# Ampliatus of Diospolis

doxologia.ro's calendar names Ampliatus among the apostles it keeps on 31 October, and the life it prints for him is three short sentences. He was made bishop by the holy Apostle Andrew; he was bishop in Diospolis; and preaching Christ and destroying idols he raised the Jews and the Greeks against himself, and being killed by them he wove himself a martyr's crown.

Nothing further is given: no year, no account of his preaching, no manner of death and no burial place, and nobody is named beside him but the apostle who ordained him. doxologia.ro prints no separate troparion for him. The hymn on his page is the one it gives to all six apostles it names on this day — Apelles, Stachys, Ampliatus, Urban, Aristobulus and Narcissus together — a short common of apostles in the third tone, asking them to entreat the merciful God for the forgiveness of our offences.

The Greek calendar keeps the six of them together on the same day, 31 October, and saint.gr gives each of them his see — and for this one it gives a different see from the one recorded here. All six belonged to the seventy Apostles of the Lord, the page says, and all were «Χριστοῦ εὐωδία τῷ Θεῷ ἐν τοῖς σωζομένοις», from 2 Corinthians 2.15. Then: «Ο Άγιος Αμπλίας έγινε επίσκοπος Οδυσσουπόλεως και ο Ουρβανός, επίσκοπος Μακεδονίας. Επειδή και οι δύο γκρέμιζαν τα είδωλα, θανατώθηκαν μαρτυρικά» — Amplias became bishop of Odyssoupolis and Urban bishop of Macedonia, and because both of them pulled down the idols both were put to death as martyrs. Odyssoupolis is not Diospolis, and this reading does not decide between the two.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps the same six apostles of the Seventy on the Julian 31 October, and its one life for them gives him the see the other two calendars had already given this folder: he was bishop in the city of Diospolis. It adds that he held it, as Urban held his, with the blessing of the holy Apostle [Andrew the First-Called](/saints/andrew-the-first-called); and it adds the hands that killed the two of them. For the preaching of the Gospel they were given over to a martyr's death by Jews and by Greek pagans. **His name link sets him over Lydda where the life sets him over Diospolis**, which are two names of one city, and the folder keeps the name the other calendars gave it. The day's line closes the six with «(I)», the first century, which is the only date any calendar has given him.
