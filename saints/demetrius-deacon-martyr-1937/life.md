# Demetrius (Troitsky)

Demetrius was born in 1884 in the village of Maksheyevo in the Kolomna
district of Moscow province, the son of the deacon Basil Troitsky. After
finishing the Kolomna church school in 1901 he served as psalmist at the
Transfiguration church of Pochinki in the Egoryevsk district of Ryazan
province, and in 1924 was ordained deacon to that church, the village
having passed by then into the Moscow region. In 1930 a court sentenced
him, for failing to hand over grain, to a year and a half’s banishment
beyond the region; arriving at his place of exile he served as a deacon
there, and returning to Pochinki in 1932 at the end of his term served in
the church again. In January 1933 the authorities arrested the priest of
the Transfiguration church, the deacon and the most active parishioners.
Asked whether he kept up relations with the priest and the people the
inquiry was interested in, whether they met in each other’s houses and
what they talked of, he answered that he knew them and kept up relations
and they met together, but owing to a poor memory he could not remember
the talks and their subjects, and in any case he tried to keep out of
them, and the priest’s political disposition towards the Soviet power he
could not determine. The witnesses said the deacon complained that the
Soviet power had ruined him, taken his house and his orchard — could that
have happened before? of course not; that the communists tormented the
Orthodox people, herded them by force into the collective farm and crushed
them with taxes — though what exactly the deacon had said against the
Soviet power, the witness went on, he could not recall. The witnesses’
answers made the deacon’s complete innocence plain to the OGPU
investigators, and their indictment had to record that “the accused
Troitsky Dmitry Vasilievich did not admit guilt of agitation, while not
denying that he had a connection with the priest … at whose house they
gathered” — which was the whole of his guilt. On 19 May 1933 the OGPU
troika sentenced him to three years’ exile in Kazakhstan, and he was sent
to Alma-Ata.

In 1936 he came back to Pochinki. His family’s house had been taken; his
wife lived in the church lodge as the church’s watchman, and the deacon
settled there too; his health broken by prison and exile, he retired and
went to the church to pray as a parishioner. In 1937 the persecution of
the Russian Church rose again: on 5 September a witness was questioned,
the watchman of the collective farm’s vegetable garden, and a day later
the NKVD wrote out a warrant which said that the deacon Troitsky, living in
the village of Pochinki, figured in the agents’ case “the White-Robed”,
that counter-revolutionary in disposition and keeping up connections with
reactionary clergy he systematically carried on counter-revolutionary
agitation among the population, and quoted him: I suffered much in exile
and lost much health because of the Soviet power; and how many wholly
innocent people still suffer there — people suffer on a single
denunciation; plainly it is not we alone who suffer but the whole people;
when you talk with the collective farmers about their conditions and what
they bear in the collective farm, you are convinced that the
collective-farm peasant’s life under the Soviet power is hard. The next
day, 6 September 1937, he was arrested, put in the Egoryevsk prison and
questioned the same day. Back from exile you again showed yourself an
enemy of the Soviet power; tell how it was! demanded the investigator. —
I said nothing against the Soviet power. — You are not being frank and
are telling untruths. — I answer correctly that I said nothing against
the Soviet power. — Why do you deny counter-revolutionary talk on your
part when a witness confirms it? — The extract from the testimony read to
me I deny; I did not say it. Two days later there was a new interrogation
and a confrontation, to which he did not object; the witness whose words
stood in the warrant was brought in. Do you confirm the witness’s
testimony? — I do not confirm it. After these interrogations he was moved
to one of the Moscow prisons, where on 17 September the investigator
questioned him again. What was your occupation? — I was a deacon, but
lately, because of poor health, I do not work and live without fixed
occupation. — You are charged with anti-Soviet agitation; do you admit
guilt? — No, I do not admit myself guilty of it. On 19 September the NKVD
troika sentenced him to be shot, and on 21 September 1937 the deacon
Demetrius Troitsky was shot and buried in an unknown common grave at the
Butovo range outside Moscow — 8 September of the old style; the Russian
calendar keeps him on 9 September of the old style, 22 September, and in
the synaxes of the new martyrs of Butovo and the saints of Moscow. The
life is by Igumen Damascene (Orlovsky).
