# Stephen Štiljanović

He was born into a devout Christian family in the Serbian town of Župa, on the southern side of Zahumlje, at a time when the Turks raided Serbia constantly and laid it waste. He defended his country as a soldier in the army of the Serbian ruler. When famine came he gave his own bread away to the starving, and the calendar makes a point of refusing to separate the patriot from the Christian: he laboured always, it says, in what is good — almsgiving, purity, prayer, the Orthodox faith, and unfeigned love of his neighbours. He went to the Lord on 4 October 1515.

What happened afterwards is why he has a feast. Some time later the Turks saw a light over his grave, and supposing treasure was hidden there they dug it open and found his body incorrupt. Serbian monks bought the relics from the Turkish pasha and carried them to the monastery of Šišatovac on the Fruška mountain.

The Serbian Church, the Russian calendar says, prays to him as a healer: «Слава во бранех, воине Стефане Щиляновичу, великий целебниче приходящим к тебе с верою» — glory in battles, soldier Stephen Štiljanović, great healer of those who come to you in faith. The Russian keeps him with «(Серб.)» beside his name, marking him as one of the Serbian saints it carries, and his own index entry gives 30 August as his Serbian day. **That is a reason to go to the Serbian calendar for him and not a substitute for it**, so the Serbian row here still stands undocumented.
