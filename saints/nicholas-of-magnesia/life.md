# Nicholas of Magnesia

Nicholas lived with his father Hadji-Kanelos in the town of Yaya Köy. His father was steward of the estates of the town's aga, Kara Osumanoglou, and stood in particular esteem with the Turks there. At twenty-two, being by then married, Nicholas got leave of his father and of the aga to go to Magnesia to settle various pieces of business; and entering the town he was wearing Turkish shoes and a fez on his head.

The servants of the judge of Magnesia, although they knew him, arrested him and brought him before the judge. The judge, pretending for his part not to know him either, asked the martyr whether he wished to embrace the religion of Mohammed, and whether, meaning to do so, he had put on the Turkish shoes and the fez as a sign of that desire. Nicholas answered with spiritual courage: «Ο Θεός να με φυλάξει και να μην γίνει σε εμένα ποτέ να αρνηθώ την πίστη μου. Εγώ τα ρούχα αυτά τα φοράω με δική σας άδεια, επειδή ο πατέρας μου είναι υπηρέτης και δουλευτής δικός σας» — God keep me, and may it never come to pass in me that I deny my faith; I wear these clothes by your own leave, because my father is your servant and your labourer.

The judge then ordered him beaten with rods, and when he saw the young man's unbending mind he gave orders that he be flogged hard. After the flogging came flatteries and great promises of honours and offices, but Nicholas stayed unmoved in his decision to be martyred for his love of Christ. Having borne a third and a fourth flogging he was thrown into prison, where, confessing Christ and rejoicing in his sufferings, he gave up his spirit — in the year 1776, the page says, or by other reckonings 1769 or 1796. Its couplet plays on the errand he came on: Nicholas, seeking to find a marriage, found an incorruptible one in heaven through his wounds.

The Serbian church keeps the two new martyrs together on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović names them both in its heading — „Св. новомуч. Лука и Никола.“ — and gives them one sentence between them: „Дивни младић Лука, кројач по занату, пострада за Христа 1564. год., а Никола 1776. год.“ — the admirable youth Luke, a tailor by trade, suffered for Christ in 1564, and Nicholas in 1776.

Of Nicholas the entry says only the year. **1776 falls inside the interval this folder already carried and agrees with the first of the three years saint.gr prints** — «το έτος 1776 μ.Χ. (κατ' άλλους 1769 μ.Χ ή 1796 μ.Χ)» — so **no date was added and none changed**, and the Prologue's single year was not used to narrow the interval the Greek page's three readings opened. The Greek account's Magnesia, his twenty-two years and his manner of death are not in this book, which gives him one clause beside the tailor.
