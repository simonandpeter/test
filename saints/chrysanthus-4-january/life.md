# Chrysanthus (4 January)

saint.gr keeps Chrysanthus and [Euphemia](/saints/euphemia-4-january) together on the fourth of January, and puts an asterisk against both names. The asterisk is explained at the head of the notice: the saint's existence, or his biographical particulars, or the date of his feast is not cross-checked. That caveat is the page's own and is repeated here because it is the most important thing the page says about him.

What else it says is one sentence: Saint Chrysanthus and Saint Euphemia were martyred for the faith of Christ, but we have no details of their lives. Of Chrysanthus alone there is the two-line verse, which plays on the name — gold-flower — and says that Chrysanthus flowers with a fruit altogether sweetest, one that has shown itself far better than gold. He is not the Chrysanthus the corpus already keeps with Daria on 19 March: this is another entry, on another day, and the page gives it no story at all.
