# Hilaria, wife of Claudius

Hilaria was the wife of the tribune [Claudius](/saints/claudius-husband-of-hilaria), who was set over the torture of [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) at Rome and was converted by what he saw. She was baptised with her husband, with their two sons Jason and Maurus, and with the whole household and his soldiers; and the family lived under the teaching of Chrysanthus, desiring to suffer for Christ.

When the emperor Numerian learned of it he had Claudius drowned in the sea with a stone about his neck, and the sons and the soldiers beheaded. The Christians cleaned out an old tomb at the place of execution and laid the bodies there by night, and Hilaria went to it often and said her prayers beside the relics, at the spot where her two sons lay.

She was seized there by the unbelievers and led away to be questioned. She asked them to let her finish her prayer and then take her wherever they wished; and they let her be, and she knelt, raised her hands and lifted her eyes to heaven and said: Master, Lord Jesus Christ, with all my heart I confess Thee; number me with my sons, who came out of my womb, whom Thou didst call to the struggle for Thee, and who laid down their souls for Thee, their Lord.

So praying, she gave up her spirit into the hands of God. Those who had taken her, seeing her suddenly dead, were moved, and went away leaving with her the two servants who had come with her; and the servants took their mistress's body and buried it with honour beside the old tomb in which the martyrs lay.

The Greek calendar keeps her with her household on the same day, and gives her death twice over. saint.gr has her baptised with her husband [Claudius](/saints/claudius-husband-of-hilaria) and her sons [Jason](/saints/jason-son-of-claudius) and [Maurus](/saints/maurus-son-of-claudius) when the tortures of [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) and [Daria](/saints/daria) left the pair unharmed. After Claudius was drowned and the two boys beheaded with their soldiers, their mother Hilaria took up their relics and buried them; and on one of her visits to their grave the eparch's soldiers seized her and dragged her off to put her to death. She asked them to let her pray first, and in the course of the prayer she breathed her last, and her maidservants took her body and buried it in her sons' tomb. Then the page adds, in its own parenthesis, that according to another tradition she died by beheading — which is what the couplet over her says as well, that Hilaria, her head cut off by the sword, looks most cheerfully upon the face of God, the couplet playing on her name, ἱλαρός, cheerful. Both readings are the page's, and neither has been chosen here.
