# Joseph of Nea Moni

These venerable fathers of Chios lived in the eleventh century. Leaving the world, they withdrew to Mount Provateon on the island of Chios and struggled in a cave in a deep asceticism, eating only bread and drinking water once a week. Standing at prayer in the night, they saw a light shining in the wood at the foot of the mountain; but whenever they went down to see what it meant, the light at once disappeared.

So they set fire to the trees, and saw that one tree alone would not burn, like the bush at Sinai. Coming closer they found it untouched by the fire, and in the tree an icon of the Mother of God without the child Jesus in her arms. They took the holy icon with much reverence back to the cave where they struggled; but it vanished from the cave and returned to the tree in the wood below. Seeing that wonder, they built a small church on that spot to the Mother of God.

When Constantine IX Monomachos, whom the page dates 1000 to 1055, the emperor of the Eastern Roman Empire, was exiled to Mytilene, Joseph and Nikitas consoled him in his trial, and Constantine promised them that if he came to the throne he would grant them whatever they asked. They asked him to raise a beautiful church on the place where the icon of the Mother of God had been found; he promised that he would, and gave them his royal ring in confirmation.

After a time Constantine became emperor, and the fathers visited him and showed him the ring he had given them as his promise of the church of the Mother of God. The emperor remembered it all, and sent the materials needed for the building and workmen fit for the task; and so the monastery of Nea Moni on Chios was raised.

The calendar keeps Joseph with [Nikitas](/saints/nikitas-of-nea-moni) and [John](/saints/john-of-nea-moni), and the page tells the three of them as one story. Of Joseph apart it says only that he was one of the two who comforted the exiled emperor.
