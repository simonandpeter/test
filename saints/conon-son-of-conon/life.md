# Conon, son of Conon

The Ohrid Prologue gives this son and his father three sentences and no more. The father, it says, was already an old man, and the son a youth of seventeen years. In the time of Dometian the two of them were sawn through with a saw for the faith of Christ, and were glorified in the Church on earth and in heaven; and they suffered honourably, the entry ends, in the year 275.

That is the whole of it. The Prologue does not say who Dometian was — it calls him neither emperor nor governor — and it names no city, no province and no judge. It gives neither man a second name, so that the only thing that tells them apart is which of the two was the father, and it prints no hymn. The one kept here is the son, seventeen years old; the father is [Conon](/saints/conon-father-of-conon), under the same heading.

The Russian church keeps the two of them on this same day, on one line, «Прмчч. Конона и сына его Конона (270-275).», and its life is the first full account either folder has carried — and the only source read for him that says what he was. He was seven years old when his widowed father took him out of Иконии in Asia Minor into a monastery, and seventeen when he was brought to the court of the governor Дометиан after his father: «На суд был доставлен и семнадцатилетний сын мученика - диакон Конон», the seventeen-year-old son of the martyr, the deacon Конон. So the ten years between the two ages are the whole of his recorded life before the trial, the deacon's rank is the calendar's own word and is recorded in `office` on that authority, and he is the younger of the pair by a generation and by the only two numbers either page gives.

Flattering persuasion failed on him as it had on his father, and the two were tortured together: stripped and laid on a red-hot iron bed, drenched with scalding oil, thrown into a cauldron of boiling tin, sulphur and pitch, hung head downward and scorched with foul smoke — and kept unharmed, the life says, by a power from heaven. Shamed and furious, their tormentors sentenced them to be sawn apart with a wooden saw. The two asked for time to pray, prayed «Благодарим Тебя, Господи, что сподобил нас пострадать за Имя Твое!» — we thank thee, Lord, that thou hast counted us worthy to suffer for thy name — asked peace for the Church and rest for their own souls, and at a voice from heaven signed themselves with the cross and died. An earthquake threw down every idol temple in the city. The monks buried them secretly in their own monastery; it was in the reign of Aurelian, 270 to 275, which is the span this folder already records, and the relics were afterwards carried into Italy, to the city of Ацерра in Campania.

The grade on the line is прмч., a venerable-martyr, and it is the calendar's own reason for `venerable` beside `martyr` here: he had been a monastery's child and was its deacon, not a soldier or a townsman. **The Russian form kept on this folder, «Конон Иконийский, Младший (сын)», is the title of his own name page, 1264, where his father's is 1265** — two pages, two numbers and two bracketed words, and the one calendar of the four read that tells the two men apart in writing at all. The Serbian book behind both folders gives each of them the bare «Конон» under a single rank word, which is how the gate came to fold them together; the fold is read, and it is two men, a father and a son.

*After the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 6. март ст. ст. — [the day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-03-19&prolog=1); read 4 October 2026; and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery for 6 марта ст. ст. — [the Russian day](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260306.html) and [the life](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Life/life582.htm), which is the only source read that calls him a deacon or gives either age, read 6 October 2026.*
