# Peter Snezhnitsky

Peter Andreyevich Snezhnitsky was born in 1880, or by other records 1879,
studied at the Kamyshlov church school and from 1892 at the Perm seminary,
which he did not finish; having a good voice he was invited to the bishop’s
choir at Yekaterinburg, where he sang some years. In November 1897 he was
made psalmist of the Nativity of the Mother of God church at Shmakovskoye in
the Irbit district, vested in the sticharion in June 1898, and in 1903
ordained deacon for the Elijah church of the Berezovsky works in the
Yekaterinburg district. There he was put to a trial: in the spring of 1907 a
branch of the Union of the Russian People was founded at the works and,
honoured by a telegram from the Emperor, resolved to serve a moleben with a
procession from the church to the parish school, and the priest, the deacon
and the psalmist on duty that day could not take part, each for his own
reason; the branch reported them to Bishop Vladimir (Sokolovsky-Avtonomov)
for offending the religious and patriotic feelings of the faithful, and the
bishop demanded a written explanation. The priest had served the liturgy
from four in the morning and the paschal molebens in the houses and was
exhausted by evening; the psalmist had not known of the service; and the
deacon wrote that he had agreed to serve the moleben in church but could not
walk in the procession, because after the catarrhal inflammation of the
lungs he had suffered in February and March the doctor had absolutely
forbidden him to sing in damp, cold air or in dusty rooms, and that the
agitation had brought on such palpitation, even to spasms of the throat,
that he had barely got home — and that the charge of not sympathising with
the Union was unjust, since he had served the moleben at the branch’s
opening on 18 March with the priest Vyshegorodsky, which the members would
not deny. The explanations were satisfactory enough, but to avoid further
conflict the bishop moved all three, and on 7 August 1907 he went to the
church of the Nizhne-Baranchinsky works; soon after he was ordained priest
for St Nicholas at Rychkovskoye in the Verkhoturye district, and in 1910
made teacher of the law of God at its school. In February 1917 he was moved
to Shogrysh in the Irbit district, and soon to the church of John the
Forerunner at Bolshoye Trifonovo in the same district, a small staff of
priest, deacon and psalmist. In 1918, in the troubles in the Perm country,
he was shot by the Bolsheviks at Yegorshino station. The Holy Synod numbered
him among the saints on 17 July 2002; the Church keeps him on 7 September of
the old style, 20 September, and in the synaxis of the Yekaterinburg saints.
