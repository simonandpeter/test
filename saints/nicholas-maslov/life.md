# Nicholas (Maslov)

Nicholas Ivanovich Maslov was born in 1874 at Tver. He finished the theological school and a course of pastoral studies, and in 1924 he was ordained priest; he served in Tver, in the church of the icon of the Mother of God «The Unburnt Bush» beyond the Volga. When almost every church in the city had been closed, among them the Ascension cathedral, the archbishop [Thaddeus of Tver](/saints/thaddeus-of-tver) came to serve with Father Nicholas there, and at times the whole Orthodox clergy of the city would gather in that churchyard church on the hill.

The persecution of 1937 left almost none of Tver's clergy at liberty. On 3 November 1937 the NKVD arrested him. He was sixty-three, his health was very weak and he was often ill, and perhaps for that reason, the account says, the NKVD chose a camp for him rather than a bullet. He was questioned at once. Asked whom he knew in the city, he named only the priests who had served with him. Asked whether he knew «the former bishop Uspensky», he corrected the questioner: not the former bishop, but the archbishop Thaddeus; he knew him well.

Told that he was a member of a counter-revolutionary fascist-monarchist organisation in Kalinin headed by archbishop Thaddeus, he answered that he had never belonged to any such organisation and did not admit the charge. What he did admit was his own mind: that he was a convinced monarchist, that he believed the Soviet power ill-disposed towards the clergy and oppressive of religion, and that he had said among the faithful that the new constitution gave them nothing, that the churches were still being closed, and that believers should be elected to the Supreme Soviet so that religion might have support there.

After such answers the standing witnesses were called in and signed what the investigator had written for them. On 2 December an NKVD troika sentenced him to ten years in a corrective labour camp. He was ill in prison and grew worse; in July 1938 he was moved from Tver to the prison at Lykoshino, and there, after a long and heavy illness, he died on 17 January 1939. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
