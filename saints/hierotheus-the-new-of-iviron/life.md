# Hierotheus the New of Iviron

Hierotheus was born in 1686 at Kalamata in the Peloponnese, to rich and
devout parents, Demos and Asimina, Nikodemos of the Holy Mountain says; a
pious youth of great ability and diligence, wanting to reach divine wisdom
both in learning and in the monastic life, he studied Latin and Greek
philosophy, and after his parents’ death, meaning to go on with his studies,
went first to the Holy Mountain, famous for its learned men. He was at first
the disciple of a hermit at the cell of St Artemius, then entered the
brotherhood of Iviron, where he was tonsured. Soon, on the monastery’s
business, he went to Constantinople and from there to Wallachia, where the
Lord showed him he should finish the schooling he had broken off; studying
under a Cypriot named Mark, he won by his good character the regard of
Auxentius, metropolitan of Sofia, and was ordained deacon, and after
finishing his studies at Venice he came back to the Holy Mountain and
settled near Iviron in the desert of Chagi. By the witness of his
contemporaries he led the severest hermit’s life, and by unceasing prayer of
Jesus won a high love for his neighbour and the joy-making mourning; at the
petition of the igumen of Iviron he was ordained priest by Metropolitan
James of Neocaesarea, who was living there in retirement. At the request of
the people of Skopelos, who had been left without clergy, the selfless
ascetic broke his solitude and for eight years, with his Athonite disciples
the hieromonk Meletius and the monks Joasaph and Symeon, served the liturgy
for them and preached much; foreseeing his death he withdrew with the three
to the island of Gioura, where criminals were sent for life, and there,
after a short illness, died in 1745. His disciples buried him on the island,
and after three years his precious head was brought to Iviron; by his
prayers many were healed of sickness and of the war of the flesh.
Eustratiades, though, writes in his Hagiologion that Nikodemos placed this
saint in the Church’s hagiologion having found a fanciful life of him,
accompanied by wonders, written by a fanatical friend of Hierotheus. The
Greek calendar keeps him on 13 September with megalynaria, and the Romanian
calendar keeps him the same day, with his cell on the island of Skopelos;
the Russian church calendar prints his life in the synaxis of the Athonite
fathers.
