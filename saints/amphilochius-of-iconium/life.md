# Amphilochius of Iconium

Amphilochius loved God from childhood, became a monk and lived as a hermit. Fleeing the quarrels and disturbances of the world, he stayed alone in a desert place, serving God earnestly, and struggled in a cave for forty years. When the shepherd of the Church of Iconium died, an angel of the Lord appeared to him by night and said: *Amphilochius, go into the city and shepherd the divine sheep.* He would not go.

The second night the angel appeared again and told him to go into the city and shepherd the flock God entrusted to him, and again he did not obey, for what he saw seemed to him a deception, and he said to himself that Satan too can sometimes put on the shape of an angel of light. On the third night the angel came and called him out of his bed. Frightened, he rose and said: *If you are the angel of God, let us both stand at prayer* — and bowing his head he began to sing *Holy, Holy, Holy, Lord of Sabaoth; heaven and earth are full of his glory*, and the angel sang it with him.

Then the angel took him by the right hand and led him to a church nearby, whose doors opened of themselves. Inside he saw a great light and a multitude of men in white garments, who brought him to the altar and put the holy Gospel into his hand, saying *The Lord is with you.* One of them, more honoured and more shining than the rest, said with a loud voice: *Let us all pray* — and then: *The grace of the Holy Spirit appoints our brother Amphilochius bishop of the city of Iconium; let us all pray for him, that the grace of God may come upon him.*

After the prayer they gave him peace and became invisible, and he stood wondering at the vision and at his own consecration. He prayed, committing himself to God's holy will, and at daybreak went out towards his cave — and on the road seven bishops met him, gathered from the cities round Iconium to elect a bishop for the city, who had been commanded by God to seek out the monk Amphilochius. They asked him whether he was Amphilochius, and he answered humbly: *I am Amphilochius the sinner.*

They took him with honour to the church, meaning to ordain him, but he said that since God had willed to show him, unworthy and sinful, to them for the bishopric, it was no longer right to hide God's wonderful works. So he told them how he had been consecrated by angels that night. The bishops marvelled and glorified God, and did not dare to ordain him a second time; with awe they bowed to him, kissed him with love and set him on the throne, in the reign of Valentinian and Valens.

He laboured much against the heretics: at the second Council he contended against Macedonius, the fighter against the Holy Spirit, and against those who held the heresy of Arius. For such zeal for the true faith and for his life full of good works he was famous everywhere and loved by the holy Fathers, and most of all by [Basil the Great](/saints/basil-the-great) and by [Gregory the Theologian](/saints/gregory-the-theologian), who always counted him a friend and sent him letters.

While the pious Theodosius reigned, Amphilochius came to him and begged him to order the Arians driven out of every city. The emperor would not, for fear of stirring up the people, and passed the request over, and the saint went away sorrowful. A few days later he came to the palace again and did a thing worth remembering: he bowed to the emperor on his throne, gave him due honour and greeted him with fine words — and overlooked his son Arcadius, newly raised to the throne and sitting beside his father, giving him no honour at all.

The emperor was very angry, and unable to bear the slighting of his son he ordered Amphilochius put out of the palace in disgrace. Then the saint said to him: *See, emperor, how you cannot bear your son's dishonour, and are angry with me. So God the Father does not bear the dishonour of his Son, but turns away and hates those who blaspheme him, and is angry with those who join themselves to their accursed heresy.*

The emperor understood then why he had given his son no honour — that by that example he showed the Son of God is due equal honour with the Father — and marvelled at the wisdom of the act and the understanding of the words. Rising from his throne he bowed to him and asked his pardon, and at once sent letters through the whole empire that the Arians be driven out of every city by force. So Amphilochius cleansed the Church of Christ of heretics; and having composed many discourses for the true faith and written books, he came to deep old age and rested in the Lord in peace.
