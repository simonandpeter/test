# Demetrius of Rostov

Demetrius, in the world Daniel Savvich Tuptalo, was born in December 1651 at Makarov, near Kyiv, into a pious family, and grew up a deeply believing Christian. From 1662 he studied at the Kyiv-Mohyla college, where he learned Greek and Latin and the classical sciences, and on 9 July 1668 he took monastic vows with the name Demetrius, after the great martyr Demetrius of Thessalonica. Until the spring of 1675 he served his obedience in the Kyiv monastery of St Cyril, where his writing and preaching began, and on 23 May 1675 Lazarus (Baranovich), archbishop of Chernigov, ordained him hieromonk. For some years he preached in the monasteries and churches of Ukraine, Lithuania and Belarus, was abbot of the Maksimov monastery and then of the St Nicholas monastery at Baturin, and in 1684 was called to the Kyiv Caves Lavra, whose archimandrite, his former teacher Barlaam (Yasinsky), gave him the compiling of the Menologion, the lives of the saints for the whole year. That work of twenty years took the rest of his life; by his own witness, the Russian calendar’s life says, his soul was filled with the images of the saints, who strengthened him in it. In 1701, by Peter I’s decree, he was called to Moscow and on 23 March consecrated in the Dormition cathedral of the Kremlin to the metropolitan see of Tobolsk in Siberia, but for the sake of the work and his poor health he was sent instead to Rostov and Yaroslavl, where he arrived on 1 March 1702.

At Rostov he went first to the monastery of Saint Jacob, bishop of Rostov, served the liturgy in its cathedral of the Conception of the Most Holy Theotokos, and before everyone there marked out a place on the right side for his own grave, calling it his rest for ever. He went on caring for the unity of the Russian Church, weakened by the Old Believer schism, and when he died on 28 October 1709 nothing was found in his possession but books and manuscripts. The clergy and people of Rostov asked Stephen (Yavorsky), metropolitan of Ryazan, who came to bury him, to lay him in the city’s cathedral beside his predecessor Joasaph, but Stephen held to his friend’s will. The grave had not been made ready in the month since the death, so a wooden frame was set hastily into it and the body buried there on 25 November, and that, the life says, is what led to the early finding of the relics: in 1752, during repairs to the monastery’s church, the sunken floor was being mended on 21 September and his body was found incorrupt. The place was damp and the oak coffin and the manuscripts in it had rotted, but the body, the omophorion, sakkos, mitre and silk prayer rope were whole. Many healings followed at the relics; the Synod sent Metropolitan Sylvester of Suzdal and Archimandrite Gabriel of the Simonov monastery to examine them, and by its decree of April 1757 — the 29th in the first life, the 22nd in the second — he was numbered among the saints, with feasts on 28 October, the day of his repose, and 21 September, the day his relics were found.

The Russian calendar keeps the finding of the relics on 21 September old style, with his troparion and kontakion; its lives list 23 May (with the saints of Rostov) and 19 July among his days as well. The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, names him bishop of Rostov on the same day and says that his feast is 28 October and that 21 September commemorates the finding of his wonder-working relics in 1752. A third, much longer life on the Russian calendar was not read for this entry.
