# Porphyrius (9 November)

Porphyrius, doxologia.ro's life says, endured many torments for Christ in the reign of Diocletian, taking beatings and wounds over his whole body, and was burnt in cauldrons. At the last he was dragged by wild horses and broken on sharp stones, and gave up his soul to God. His body was taken away secretly by the faithful and laid in the village called Panghianete. That is the whole of the notice, one paragraph long.

The same paragraph is printed, word for word and with only the name changed, under [Onesiphorus](/saints/onesiphorus-martyr-9-november), who is kept the same day; and the two are set together in the heading of the day's hymn, «Troparul Sfinţilor Mucenici Claudiu, Castor, Sempronian şi Nicostrat; al Sfinţilor Mucenici Onisifor şi Porfirie», which marks them off as a company of two beside the company of four. The hymn itself is a common of martyrs and names neither of them.

The page gives no year: it places him by the reign of Diocletian and by nothing else. He is not the Porphyrius of Gaza the corpus already keeps, nor Porphyrius the Mime of 15 September; nothing on this page joins him to either.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 9 November, and tells the two of them as one passion. saint.gr has them living, and dying baptised in their own blood, in the persecution of Diocletian; both served in the charitable works of the Church, and both belonged to the party that searched out by night the bodies of martyrs thrown into the ravines and handed them over for honourable burial. They were discovered and arrested, and pressed to deny the faith, and stayed firm in it without fear of the threats or of the tortures prepared for them. They were then tied behind wild horses and dragged at a gallop through thorns and stones, and when the horses stopped after some hours their bodies were found torn apart and drowned in blood. The page's own commentary sets beside this the Revelation's woman drunk with the blood of the saints, and the judgement that followed on her.

The Russian calendar keeps the two martyrs on the same day and prints one short life for both, which is the first source read for this folder to give a time: they suffered in the persecution under Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305. They were beaten and burned with fire, and then tied to wild horses, which dragged them over the stones until they died; the faithful gathered up what was left of them and buried it with honour. The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion under their two names, the first the common of martyrs and the second naming them, and both are now in this folder.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him with [Onesiphorus](/saints/onesiphorus-martyr-9-november) under one heading and tells the suffering of both in four sentences, which is more of it than this folder held. They were tortured for the name of Christ, it says, in the time of the emperor Diocletian: beaten severely, then burned on an iron grid, and then tied by horses' tails and dragged over stones and thorns until they were torn to pieces, and so gave up their holy souls to God.

It adds what no page this folder cites had given: their relics were buried in Pentapolis. The reign it names is the one the folder already records as the interval of their death, and it prints no year of its own, so nothing is narrowed.
