# Cyriacus of Constantinople

saint.gr's calendar for 27 Οκτωβρίου names Cyriacus, patriarch of Constantinople, and then prints two accounts of him that cannot both be right. According to the website of the Ecumenical Patriarchate, it says, Cyriacus was a presbyter and steward of the Great Church, and succeeded John the Faster on the ecumenical throne in 595; having governed the Church in a manner pleasing to God, he died in peace in 606. Before the twelfth century, the page adds, his memory was kept on 30 October.

But Nicodemus the Hagiorite, it goes on, holds that this is Cyriacus the First — who appears in some patriarchal lists as Cyrillianus — who lived in the third century and shepherded the bishopric of Byzantium as a true and good shepherd for sixteen years. The page sets the two side by side and chooses neither, so this folder carries no years at all; the couplet over the entry says only that Cyriacus, leaving earth and life, comes to the Lord of lords himself.

That is the whole of the entry: no family, no city of birth, no relics and no hymn. What both accounts agree on, and all that is recorded here beyond the name, is that he held the see of Constantinople and that the Greek calendar keeps him on this day.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 27 Οκτωβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/10/27/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2662/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026.*
