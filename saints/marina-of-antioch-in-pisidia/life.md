# Marina of Antioch in Pisidia

Marina was born at Antioch of Pisidia to parents of good family but not of a good faith, darkened with Greek paganism; her father, whose name was Edesius, was a servant of the idols. Her mother died while she was still in swaddling clothes, and her father gave the orphan to a nurse who lived in a village about fifteen stadia from the city. As the child grew she proved beautiful in body and more beautiful in soul, for she was adorned with good understanding and good ways.

In those days there was a great persecution of the Christians, and the priests and clergy, the teachers of the word of God, hid themselves for fear of the torturers — some in the deserts, some in the mountains and caves, and some in the villages among simple people, disguised as poor men; but where they could they taught the holy faith in secret and turned many from the error of the idols to Christianity.

So it happened that Marina, who was then twelve years old, heard from a believing man the word about Christ, the true God: how He was incarnate of the Holy Spirit in the womb of the Most Holy Virgin and was born of her with her virginity kept whole; how He worked many wonders and suffered willingly for the salvation of men, died, rose, ascended into heaven and prepared for those who believe in Him and love Him a life without end, glory and the eternal kingdom.

Hearing this she believed in Christ, and her heart was kindled with divine love, so that she neither thought nor spoke of anything but Jesus Christ, and all her attention was wherever she could hear something said about the true God. As she believed in Him with her heart, so she was not ashamed to confess Him with her mouth, although she was still unbaptised, for she had found no Christian priest to baptise her; and she longed to pour out her blood for Christ and to be baptised in it as in a font, as she had heard that many of the holy martyrs, men and women, had been baptised in their own blood.

Her father Edesius, learning that his daughter had believed in Christ, hated her and reckoned her not a daughter but a stranger; but she set her hope on the heavenly Father. When she had come to her full years, at about fifteen, she went out once to her father's sheep, which were grazing in the field, to see to them; and at that time Olymbrios, the eparch of the parts of the East and a cruel tormentor of the Christians, was travelling to Antioch of Pisidia, and met her on the road.

What follows on the Romanian page is very long, and the account of her interrogation, of her torments and of the visions she was granted is left here unsummarised. At the end of it, at the sight of her endurance, men and women to the number of about fifteen thousand bowed their necks under the sword, and, baptised in their own blood and cleansed of all their sins, entered into the joy of their Lord, crowned with martyrdom.

Then the eparch condemned the martyr herself to be beheaded. Led out to the place of execution, she asked a little time of those who brought her and, turning to the people who had followed her, urged them all to know the one true God who had made them and to flee the deceit of the devil and the ruin of idol-worship. Then she bowed to prayer and prayed at length for them all, and suddenly the earth shook and fear came on everyone, and many fell to the ground, the executioner among them, for Christ with His holy angels appeared from heaven to His bride, calling her into His rest and stretching out His hands to receive her soul.

Filled with unspeakable joy she raised the executioner up and told him to carry out his order quickly, and she bowed her honoured head under the sword and was beheaded, and her soul was taken in the hands of the Lord. So Marina finished her martyr's contest, on the seventeenth day of the month of July. Her passion was written by one who saw it with his own eyes, the servant of God Theotimus, who watched all her torments and was granted to see the visions revealed to her, and gave them to the faithful for their profit.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 17 July, and saint.gr dates what the Romanian life left undated: she was born at Antioch of Pisidia in the years of the emperor Claudius II, in 270. It gives her father's name as Aedesios and calls him a priest of the idols, says her mother died a few days after her birth and that he put her in the care of a Christian woman who taught her Christ, and that at fifteen she told him she was a Christian, at which he struck her out of his family in hatred.

Its eparch has a name, Olymbrios, who admired her beauty and tried every way to make her deny Christ and become his wife, and met the one answer «Είμαι χριστιανή», I am a Christian. It adds to the torments the black dog that the dragon in the prison turned into when she prayed, and the hammer she caught up to beat it about the head and back; and after the second examination the lighted candles he burned her with, whose wounds closed by a wonder, so that many of the bystanders became Christians — which is why, in the face of that, he had her beheaded.

The page follows her relics as well. They were kept at Constantinople until the Latins first took the city in 1204; by other sources they were at Antioch until 908 and were then carried to Italy. Today, it says, they are in Athens, in a church that bears her name, and her hand has been taken to the monastery of Vatopedi on the Holy Mountain. It names her patron of Leros, of Heliopolis and of Schinoudi on Imbros.

The Serbian church keeps her on the same 17 July, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives her the fullest entry of its July. In her twelfth year, it says, she first heard of the Lord Jesus Christ — how He was incarnate of the most pure Virgin, worked many wonders, accepted death on the cross and gloriously rose — and her young heart was set on fire with love for Him, so that she vowed never to marry and longed with her whole soul to suffer for Christ and to be baptised in the blood of martyrdom. Her father came to hate her for her faith and did not consider her his daughter.

The imperial governor, learning from her own mouth that she was a Christian, first wished her to be his wife; and when she refused, he ordered her to bow to the idols. I will not bow nor offer sacrifice to breathless and dead idols, she answered, which neither know themselves nor know whether we honour them or dishonour them; I will not give them the honour that belongs to my Creator alone. He put her to fierce torments, and threw her into prison all wounded and bloody.

In the prison, the Prologue says, the devil appeared to her first in the form of a dreadful serpent which engulfed her head; and when she crossed herself the serpent burst apart and vanished. Then a heavenly light shone round her, and it seemed to her that the walls of the prison had vanished together with the roof, and a cross appeared, shining and high, and on the top of the cross a white dove, from which came a voice: rejoice, Marina, rational dove of Christ, daughter of the Sion above, for the day of thy gladness is come. And she was healed by the power of God of all her wounds and pains.

The second day the judge tortured her in fire and in water, and she bore it all as though in somebody else’s body; and before her death the Lord Jesus appeared to her with angels. She was beheaded, the Prologue says, in the time of the emperor Diocletian — a reign and not a year, so this folder still carries no date of death.

It ends with her relics. One of her hands is at Vatopedi on the Holy Mountain; and in Albania, on the mountain of Langa above Lake Ohrid, there is a monastery of her name with a part of her wonderworking relics, where many wonders have happened and happen still — witnessed, the Prologue says, not only by Christians but by Muslims. So much respect do the Turks have for that holy place, it adds, that they have never been willing to touch either the shrine or the property of the monastery, and for a time a Turk was its trustee.
