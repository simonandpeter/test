# Sergius (Rodakovsky)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him on 8 апреля ст. ст. in four words, «Сщмч. Сергия пресвитера (1933).», and sends the line to a page of twenty-three: «Новомученики, в земле Белорусской просиявшие (23 священномученика Минских)», where his notice is one paragraph of six. The name link behind the line, page 9401, gives the surname and the office the line withholds, «Сергий (Родаковский), протоиерей, сщмч.», and lists two more days beside this one, both of them moveable synaxes — the new martyrs on 26 января and the saints of Belarus on 8 июня — which are synaxes and not written into the feast. **The corpus already holds two of the twenty-three off this same page**, an archimandrite kept on 24 августа and a priest kept on 10 февраля, each read on his own day; this is the third.

azbyka.ru carries a far longer life of him, from the April volume of игумен Дамаскин (Орловский)’s *Жития новомучеников и исповедников Российских ХХ века*, Tver 2006, pages 126 to 128, with the investigation file of the Belarusian KGB cited line by line in its footnotes. Where the two pages can be compared they agree on the shape and differ on four facts, and both readings are set down below rather than averaged.

He was born in 1882 at Житковичи in the province of Minsk — azbyka adds the Мозырский uyezd — the son of a psalomshchik, a church reader, whom azbyka names «Петра Родаковского». In 1904, having finished the Minsk seminary, he was ordained priest and sent to the church of the Dormition in the village Лавришево of the Новогрудский uyezd of the same province. days says he served there ten years; azbyka says he left in 1915 for the town Переславль in the province of Vladimir, which is the one place the two accounts put him that the other does not mention at all.

**Then the war, and a regiment this corpus can name.** He was made priest of a hospital train; days says he then did the obedience of a regimental priest in 1916 and 1917, azbyka that he was sent to the front in 1916 as regimental priest of the 4th Dragoon regiment and served there **until the regiment was disbanded in April 1918**. In that same year, azbyka says, the bishop «Георгий (Ярошевский)» made him rector of the Trinity church in the village Таль of the Бобруйский uyezd and raised him to archpriest; days has only that after the revolution he took charge of the parish at Таль, and names no church and no bishop. The office recorded here is Archpriest, which both the name page and azbyka give him, against the «пресвитера» of the day’s own line.

**The first arrest is for tax, and it is the Russian calendar that has it.** In 1930, days says, he was arrested for not paying an immoderately high income tax levied for serving in a church, and sent to forced labour on procurement. And it prints the thing counted against him as a special fault — a question: **«Почему советская власть не накладывает норм по заготовке на раввинов, а всё на священников?»**, why does the soviet power not lay procurement quotas on rabbis, but all of them on priests. azbyka has neither the year nor the question; the six months of forced labour appear there only inside his own testimony, where he lists them among the troubles sent on him. He came back to his village after six months and went on serving; his house was confiscated and he and his family wandered among the neighbours.

**The charge sheet of 1933 is azbyka’s, and it is a catalogue of hearsay.** He had, it says, carried on active counter-revolutionary work to wreck collectivisation in the village; he had begun the story that the pope of Rome was coming to Belarus and gathering great processions of the cross; in February 1933, carrying out the timber-felling quotas, he had complained of the bad life under the soviet power, saying that the time had come when he too must cart firewood, and what a year he had been suffering; and at the burial of a church warden that February he had told the weeping women not to weep, **that the man had suffered for Christ and died in prison**. The arrest that followed is dated 20 February 1933 by azbyka and placed in March by days, and that is the fourth of the four disagreements.

**His answers are printed at length and they are the answers of a man arguing from the text.** He did not acknowledge himself guilty: knowing the Gospel well, as a clergyman, he found in it no indication of a form of state government, and still less of land settlement, and therefore could not have spoken against collectivisation on the Gospel’s authority — the Gospel speaks of the moral relations of men. He had carried on no agitation as a conscious citizen and Christian; the evidence that he had said the power would soon fall while fishing with a named man was untrue for the plain reason that **he had never fished with him, and therefore the testimony was false**. At a burial he might indeed have said something to men standing with their hats on; that he had added that the soviet power had brought things to this he could not have said, because he would have held that to be agitation.

And on being asked to leave the priesthood: a representative of the district executive committee had come to his flat with a member of the village soviet and offered him work if he would give up the priesthood and serve the soviet power, **and he had answered that, being a believer, he could not put a mask on himself, and that while there were believers and while it was possible he would go on serving as a priest**. He could not consider himself a prophet or say that war was coming soon. He came to church with his wife among the first and went home among the last, so that there was almost never anyone walking with him; the claim that on a named day of April 1932 he had agitated with those walking home beside him he held to be a lie.

**And he gave the reason the villagers would not join the collective, which is a reason about him.** The representatives of the local power, he said, had at the forming of the collective struck at the religious feelings of believers as well, so that many came to think joining it meant giving up the faith — the more so because at that time every trouble, as the saying is, was sent upon him as the representative of the community of believers: an unbearable tax, half again more than his income; a trial; eviction from the house the parishioners had bought in 1927 for the priest’s quarters; **the stripping of his property, so that his last bed and his cast-iron pots were taken, a complaint about which had been laid before the prosecutor and had to that day received no answer**; and six months of forced labour. All this worked on the believers, who looked on him as a martyr for the faith.

He made no difference, he said, between the single farmers and the collective men in the offices he performed for them, and none of those who had joined could complain of being treated otherwise than a single farmer; he looked at both from their moral side, and held the view that a man who had joined must be an unbeliever to be a wrong one. For persuading others, measures of persuasion are allowable and not of compulsion. In conclusion he could say that he considered the accusations raised against him false. On 24 March he was interrogated for the last time: the rumours that the pope of Rome would be travelling had gone about the village, he said, and from whom he had heard them he did not know — **he had only laughed at it** — and he did not acknowledge himself guilty of the charge brought against him.

**On 21 April 1933 a troika of the OGPU sentenced him to be shot.** days closes «Приговор был приведён в исполнение», the sentence was carried out, and gives no date for it; azbyka closes «он был расстрелян и погребен в безвестной могиле», he was shot and buried in an unknown grave, and gives no date for that either. **21 April 1933 is the civil date of the Julian 8 April**, so this calendar keeps him on the day of the sentence, and the stored `death` holds the year alone for that reason. azbyka lists two moveable synaxes beside this day — the new martyrs and confessors of the Russian Church, and the saints of Belarus — which are the same two the Russian name page gives on its own numbers, 26 января and 8 июня, and both are synaxes and not feasts.
