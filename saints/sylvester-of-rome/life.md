# Sylvester of Rome

Sylvester was born in old Rome and brought up in the faith, a disciple of the presbyter Quirinus, a man beloved of God and wise, from whom he learned both letters and good habits. Grown to his full age he was known above all for taking in strangers: out of love for God and for his neighbour he brought them into his own house, washed their feet, fed them and gave them every rest. So he received Timothy, a priest and confessor come to Rome from Antioch preaching the Gospel of the kingdom, and in the year and some months Timothy spent in his house Sylvester was made perfect in faith.

Timothy was seized by Tarquinius, the prefect of the city, held in chains, beaten three times a day, and at last beheaded; Sylvester took his relics by night and buried them in his own house with the fitting hymns. The prefect then called Sylvester in, demanded Timothy's goods of him and pressed him to sacrifice; and when he would not, threatened him with torments. Sylvester answered out of the Gospel that his soul would be required of him that night and that what he boasted he would do would not come to pass. The prefect had him chained and thrown into prison, sat down to his meal, and choked on a fish bone that no remedy could shift; he died at midnight, as the saint had said.

Sylvester was brought out of prison with joy and greatly honoured, by unbelievers as well as by believers, for many of the prefect's household had seen the prophecy fulfilled. At thirty he was ordained deacon by Miltiades, bishop of Rome, and on Miltiades's death he was chosen by all to that see. It was he, doxologia.ro says, who ordered that the first day be called the Day of the Lord, because on it the Lord rose in glory, where the Romans had called it the Day of the Sun; and he laid down that Christians should fast on one Saturday only, that on which Christ died and went down to Hades. He held the see «23 de ani și 10 luni (314-335)».

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same second of January, and saint.gr opens its notice by saying that the Eastern and the Western Church hold a number of saints in common down to the separation, and that Sylvester is one of them. It adds that he was the son of Rufinus, born at Rome, that he was ordained at thirty by Pope Marcellinus and became pope in 314 in succession to Miltiades, that he fought the heresy of Arius and laid down the liturgical rules for the consecration of chrism, and that among his best works was his care for the bodily upkeep of the poorer clergy and of nuns, so that people given to prayer and spiritual service should not be dragged away from it. The page dates his death 335.
