# Ahmed the Calligrapher

Ahmed was born in the seventeenth century into a Muslim family of Constantinople, and died a martyr of the Orthodox Church on 3 May 1682. He was made a copyist in the Great Archives, and afterwards an officer of the Sublime Porte and secretary of the court by the sultan's appointment — the calligrapher's trade by which the calendar still names him.

In his house were two Christian slaves of Russian birth, a young one with whom he lived in fornication and an older one who served him in the house. The older woman he allowed to go to the services at the patriarchal cathedral, and she would bring home the blessed bread and the holy water and share them with the other. Whenever she had eaten them and Ahmed came near her, he smelt a beautiful and indescribable fragrance coming out of her mouth.

When he learned what it was she ate at church, he was filled with the wish to see the cathedral, and asked a priest to prepare him a hidden place during the Liturgy. From it he saw the Patriarch vested in light, and as he blessed the people rays came out of his fingers and rested on the heads of all who were there — but not on his own. That decided him: he was received into the Orthodox faith and baptised in secret.

Being present afterwards at a gathering in the city, he confessed that he was a Christian. He was tortured and sentenced to death, and beheaded on 3 May 1682. The Romanian calendar keeps him on that day and on 24 December.
