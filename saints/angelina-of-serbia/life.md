# Angelina of Serbia

The Охридски пролог gives her in one paragraph, and the greater part of it is her family. She was the daughter of Ђорђе Скендербег, prince of the Albanians, and the wife of Стефан, despot of the Serbs, who was the son of Ђурађ. She bore her husband’s banishment with him and shared every bitterness of his life, in Serbia and in Albania and in Italy alike, and none of the three is a place the book sets her down in for long.

She brought up her two sons, Максим and [John](/saints/john-the-despot), in a true Christian spirit. After her husband’s death she was tonsured and gave herself wholly to prayer, to works of mercy, and to the building and the repairing of churches. A faithful wife, an excellent mother and a perfect Christian, she earned in truth the name „Мајка Ангелина“ — Mother Angelina — by which the people call her still, and the book says so in the present tense.

Her wonderworking relics rest together with those of her righteous husband and of her consecrated sons in the monastery of Крушедол — and the book adds, in a parenthesis of its own, «уколико нису од Турака уништене», if the Turks have not destroyed them. She reposed at the beginning of the sixteenth century and passed into the kingdom that does not die. The entry closes with the book’s own cross-reference, «в. 30. јул», see 30 July; that page was not read here, and nothing above rests on it.

The cross-reference that entry closes with, «в. 30. јул», has now been followed. The Охридски пролог for the Julian 30 July does print her, as its fifth entry and in two sentences: „Преп. мајка Ангелина, деспотица српска.“ — her relics lie in the monastery of Krušedol, and see her life on 12 December. So this book keeps her twice, and her day in this corpus stays the 10 December that carries her life; she does not render on 30 July.

Two things about that second page are worth setting down. Its cross-reference is two days out: it sends the reader to 12 December, and the Prologue's own 12 December page prints „Свети Спиридон Чудотворац, епископ тримифунтски“, „Свештеномученик Александар, епископ јерусалимски“ and „Свети мученик Разумник“ — three saints whose names are left here in the book's own script because none of them belongs to her story — and does not name her, while the 10 December page carries the heading this folder quotes and her whole life. And the 30 July entry names her alone: there is no word on that page of her son the despot, under his own name or inside hers, although his folder was given the same cross-reference out of the 10 December heading.

The Русская Православная Церковь keeps her too. The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery prints her for 1 июля ст. ст. — «Прав. Ангелины , деспотиссы Сербской (XVI).» — which is not the day the Охридски пролог gives her, and both days stand: 1 июля in the Russian calendar and 10. децембар in the Serbian. That calendar grades her righteous where the Serbian reading makes her a nun, heads her name page «Блаженная», and sets her century at the sixteenth, as the Prologue does. Under her name on the day it prints a troparion — the common of a venerable woman, with her name set into its last line — and a kontakion of her own.
