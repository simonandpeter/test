# Macarius of Kalyazin

saint.gr's calendar for 17 Μαρτίου keeps a Russian abbot, Macarius of the monastery of Kalyazin, and heads his page «(1402 - 1483)». In the world he was called Matthew, and he was born in the year 1402 in the village of Kozino in Russia, near the town of Kashin; his father, Basil Koza, was a soldier in the service of Prince Basil II the Dark.

At his parents' insistence, and against his own inclination to the monastic life, he married Helen Yakhontova. Within the next three years his parents and his wife died, and so he entered the monastery of St Nicholas of Klobukov, where he was tonsured a monk. With his abbot's blessing he left that house and lived as an ascetic in a desert place near two lakes in the region of Kashin.

Later he became abbot of the monastery of Kalyazin, and there he carried his spiritual struggle on. He was a simple man, the page says, and would often withdraw into the wilderness to live in stillness; in the forest he talked with the wild beasts, and they took their food from the saint's hand. Macarius fell asleep in peace in the year 1483.

That is the whole of what saint.gr prints. It gives no account of the founding of the Kalyazin house, names no relics beyond setting the finding of them beside him as its own commemoration, and prints neither a couplet nor a hymn under his name; nothing has been added here from any other source or from memory.

The Russian church keeps him on the day of his death, and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery gives the whole of a life the Greek page tells in four lines. In the world he was «Матфей», born in 1400 — two years earlier than the Greek reckoning — in the village of Gribkovo, also called Kozhino, near the town of Kashin, to a boyar's family. He wanted the monastic life from his youth and married at his parents' wish; within a year his parents were dead, and three years after that his wife, and with nothing left to hold him to his old life he was tonsured at the Nikolaev Klobukov house.

For love of solitude he left the town monastery and with seven monks found a place eighteen versts from Kashin, between two lakes near the Volga; there he set up a cross and founded a hermitage. A boyar whose lands lay nearby grew afraid that a monastery would rise and break up his waste ground, and the enemy of salvation sowed such hatred in him that he set about driving them out. The house grew all the same, and so well ordered that an elder who had spent nine years on the Holy Mountain told the brethren he had made that long journey to Athos for nothing, since men could be saved here too, where everything was done as in the coenobia of the Mountain.

He never relaxed his own rule to a great age, and healed the palsied and the possessed while he still lived. He died on 17 марта 1483, and after his death heavy chains were found on him that nobody had known about. His relics were found whole on 26 мая 1521 while trenches were being dug for a new church; the Council of 1547 appointed his commemoration throughout Russia. The page adds that the monastery was sacked by the Poles in 1610, that tsars of the new house prayed there, and that after the Soviet closure and the sacking of the 1930s the relics were carried to Tver, where they rest in the cathedral of the White Trinity. The day page prints a troparion and a kondak for him and both are copied here.
