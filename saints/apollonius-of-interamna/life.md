# Apollonius of Interamna

Saint [Valentine of Interamna](/saints/valentine-of-interamna), bishop of Interamna in Umbria, had from God the gift of healing sicknesses by prayer in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ. In the reign of the emperor Aurelian three young pagans came from Athens to Rome to learn the Roman tongue and the Roman sciences, and lodged in the house of a teacher named Craton. They were [Proculus](/saints/proculus-of-interamna), [Ephebus](/saints/ephebus-of-interamna) and Apollonius.

Craton's son Herimon fell gravely ill, his back bending until his head came down between his knees, and three years of physicians did him no good. At last Craton begged the bishop to pray for the boy. Valentine came to Rome, shut himself in a room with him and prayed the whole night through, and in the morning the young man came out sound. Craton and his whole household believed in Christ and were baptised, and the three students were baptised with them and became their teacher's devoted disciples.

Word of the healing went through Rome, and among the many who turned to Christ was the city prefect's son [Abundius](/saints/abundius-of-interamna). The prefect's anger fell on the bishop. Valentine was pressed to deny Christ and worship the idols, and when he would not he was thrown into prison, where his disciples came to him; the governor then had him brought out and beheaded.

The three took their teacher's body, carried it to Interamna and buried it with honour, and kept their days at his tomb in prayer, drawing both believers and pagans to the true faith. For that they were seized and shut in prison, and the executioners beheaded them by night for fear the people would take them away. Abundius found them already dead and buried them beside the bishop's tomb. The calendar names Apollonius last of the three, and the day page prints no hymn for any of them.
