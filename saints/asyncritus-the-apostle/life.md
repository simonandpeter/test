# Asyncritus

According to the church writings, the page says, Asyncritus was ordained bishop in Heraclea of Pontus, in Asia Minor. He is commemorated together with the apostles [Hermes](/saints/hermes-apostle-of-dalmatia), [Phlegon](/saints/phlegon-of-marathon), [Herodion](/saints/herodion-the-apostle), [Agabus](/saints/agabus-the-apostle) and [Rufus](/saints/rufus-apostle-of-thebes), and named with them in the apostle Paul's letter to the Romans.

That is the whole of what doxologia prints for him. It gives no birthplace, no year, no account of his preaching and no manner of death of his own; the page for Agabus says of all six of them that they ended on the same day as a result of the torments they were put to, and that is the only thing said here about how his life ended. His page carries no painter's rule and no separate troparion beyond the one the six share, in the third tone.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, in the same company of four, and joins his end to Phlegon's. saint.gr says that [Phlegon](/saints/phlegon-of-marathon), who became bishop of Marathon, and Asyncritus, having preached the Gospel in various parts of the world, brought many unbelievers to the true faith; and that in the end they were tortured in various ways by Jews and Greeks, with the result that both died on the same day, and so received from the Lord the heavenly goods laid up for the true labourers of the Gospel. The page's couplet for him says that everyone will praise Asyncritus greatly, for the mouth of Paul greeted him; and it glosses his name as one so far above all things that nothing can be set beside him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the six together on this day, and its heading names Asyncritus among them — but the body of the entry never writes his name. In the place where his notice should stand there is a sentence with no subject: he was bishop in Hyrcania of Asia. It falls between the notice of Rufus, who has already been given Thebes of Greece, and the notice of Phlegon, who is introduced by name and said to be mentioned in the same place of the epistle; and Asyncritus is the only one of the six whom the body otherwise passes over. So the sentence reads as his, by its position and by what is missing from the page, and that is a reading and not a thing the Prologue says. The see it would give him is Hyrcania in Asia, where the Romanian page ordains him bishop in Heraclea of Pontus; both stand here and neither was chosen. The entry closes of all six that, like bees of Christ, they carried the honey of the Gospel about into various countries, suffering much for the love of Christ, and that all of them passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ the beloved.

**The Russian church calendar keeps the six of them together on the Julian 8 April**, which is the day the Romanian and the Serbian keep, and its line is «Апп. от 70 Иродиона, Агава, Асинкрита, Руфа, Флегонта, Ерма и иже с ними (I).» — six genitives under one rank word, a century in a Roman numeral, and a company left open at the end, «и иже с ними», *and those with them*, which is a phrase no other calendar read for this folder prints. The six name links behind the line, pages 1871, 46, 282, 1886, 2313 and 791, all lead to one life of the company; three of them give in their own titles the see the day’s line withholds, and those same three close with «сщмч.», hieromartyr, which is a rank none of these folders carries. The rank is set down here and `types` is left as it was.

That life sets them in the number of the seventy apostles chosen by Christ and sent out to preach, points to the synaxis of the Seventy on 4 января, and then disposes of four of them in a single sentence: «апостол Руф, которого приветствует святой апостол Павел в Послании к Римлянам (Рим. 16, 11), был епископом в греческом городе Фивы; апостол Асинкрит (Рим. 16, 14) епископом в Гиркании (Малая Азия); апостол Флегонт – в городе Марафоне (Фракия), апостол Ерм – в Далматии». It closes that all of them bore cruel sufferings for their fearless service to Christ and were counted worthy of a martyr’s end — which is what the Romanian page says of all six and the Greek page says of two.

**The see this folder had only by position is now given him in a sentence that names him.** The Ohrid Prologue put Hyrcania in Asia in a sentence with no subject, and the reading written above is that the sentence is his because of where it falls and because the body passes him over. The Russian calendar names him twice and both times with the same see: the name page is titled «епископ Гирканский», and the company’s life says «апостол Асинкрит (Рим. 16, 14) епископом в Гиркании (Малая Азия)», with the verse of the epistle beside it. So a second book now says what the Prologue only let be inferred, and the Romanian reading that ordains him in Heraclea of Pontus stands against both; all three are set down and `office` keeps the bare Bishop it had. The name page also carries the one living dedication any of these six pages names, «Храм апостола Асинкрита» at Южа.

**The day prints one hymn for the company, and it is the first hymn in Russian this folder has had.** It is a kontakion in the second tone, «Явистеся Христовы ученицы и апостоли всечестнии», and it names all six of them in order before asking them to pray for the forgiveness of the sins of those who sing them. He is named fourth in it, «Асинкрите, Флегонте со Ермом», and the folder held no Russian hymn before.
