# Angelis of Argos

Angelis the new martyr was martyred on Chios, saint.gr says. He came of Argos in the Peloponnese and lived at Kuşadası, near Ephesus, in Asia Minor, where he worked as a practical physician; he was a quiet man, devout, fond of the services, and merciful. One day at a gathering there happened to be a godless Frenchman who mocked the Christian faith, and Angelis contradicted his arguments boldly, and even proposed that they fight a duel, the Frenchman fully armed and the saint with nothing but a stick, believing that he would beat him by the strength of faith.

The Frenchman accepted, and they put the agreement in writing at the embassy. Angelis ran to his spiritual father, confessed and asked his blessing; the priest tried hard to turn him from it, but Angelis insisted, and at last he was blessed. He kept the whole night awake in prayer. God did not allow a killing: when Angelis, having communicated, appeared before the Frenchman, terror and cowardice took hold of the man, and in front of everybody he abandoned the duel in shame, and the saint was declared the winner.

After that, the page says plainly, he shut himself up and pride ate at him, that he had beaten his opponent by the greatness of his own faith; and the devil, finding his chance, put into him the idea of turning Turk in order to be martyred afterwards. So on the Saturday of Lazarus in 1813 he went to the Turks and asked to become a Muslim. They first drove him off with abuse, then, faced with his insistence, received him; and immediately after his apostasy he began to do senseless things so that he might be taken to court, confess Christ and be martyred. It did not happen so: they drove him off as a madman and sent him to Chios.

On Chios he kept up the strange behaviour, going into every church he met, making prostrations with sobs, striking his head mercilessly on the floor, kissing the icons. He would give money for liturgies and alms to the poor so that they might pray for him, and he asked the Christians to pray that God would bring his contest to an end; if anyone praised him for the wish he grew wild and threatening. He often went to the tomb of Saint Makarios Notaras, the guide of many neomartyrs, and prayed in tears with his arms round the monument, that by that saint's intercessions he might be counted worthy to be martyred.

Having at last understood that he had been tricked by the crafty demon, he recognised his human weakness and repented sincerely, laying all his hope on God; and then God counted him worthy of the thing he had wanted so violently. After six months on Chios, with a heart now broken, he entered the course of martyrdom. One day he shaved his beard and went to the custom-house; and when the Turks asked him in astonishment why, he answered that while he was a Turk he let it grow, because the Turks make much of beards, but now that he had become a Christian again he had cut it off as superfluous and useless.

They tried to bring him to his senses, and when nothing came of it they shut him in irons in the castle and tortured him all night. The next day they took him to the governor of the island, where the aghas had gathered too, and tried promises and threats; they wanted to drag him by force up into the mosque, beating him without pity, but the martyr shouted that it would be better for him to be killed that minute than to go up into the mosque, since he was a Christian again. So they shut him once more in the dark prison with his feet in the stocks; and the next day, the third of December 1813, having failed to change his mind, they led him to the place called Vounaki and beheaded him.

His martyred body lay three days and nights at the place of execution. The Christians tried to get something of the martyr's clothes or of his blood, paying money to the guards, and one Christian offered thousands of piastres to take the holy relic for burial, but the Turks would not have it. Because a priest snatched the saint's honoured head and kissed it in front of them, they hardened, and took up the body with the head and the earth that had been wetted with the blood and threw them into the open sea at twenty-five fathoms; and some Christians who loved the martyr tried that night to bring them up and could not.
