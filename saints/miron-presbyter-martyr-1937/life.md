# Miron (Rzhepik)

Miron was born into a devout Czech Catholic family, to John and Mary
Rzhepik, on 21 April 1885 in the village of Kuchkarovka in the Lutsk
district of Volhynia province, and baptised on 2 May 1885 in the Roman
Catholic cathedral of Lutsk with the name Miroslav, his godparents Joseph
and Mary. In 1887 the whole family joined Orthodoxy in the same city of
Lutsk, and Miroslav was named Miron for the hieromartyr Miron of Cyzicus,
kept on 17/30 August; in 1900 Miron finished the Klevan church school and
entered the Volhynia seminary, which he finished in 1906 in the first
rank with the title of actual student, and from 1906 to 1910 studied at
the Moscow Academy, finishing as a candidate of theology. On 7 September
1910 he was sent to teach at the Tauride seminary; married to Elizabeth
Petrovna, he was ordained priest on 7 October 1912 and sent as catechist
to the Lubny teachers’ seminary in Poltava province, and on 2 September
1915 moved to the Kashin seminary in Tver province to teach Holy Scripture
and act as inspector. After the October coup of 1917 the Soviet power
abolished all the church schools, and from 1918 the Kashin seminary was
made a second-level school, where he taught foreign languages and
technical drawing. In April 1926 he moved to the town of Sergiev, the
present Sergiev Posad, as rector of the Pyatnitsky church in place of the
late archpriest Eugene Vorontsov, and was soon made archpriest; when that
church was closed in 1928 he moved to the church of Peter and Paul, the
Resurrection church. On 5 April 1930 he was arrested among sixty clergy
and laity of the Moscow region and put in the Butyrka; on 15 May 1931 he
was charged with membership of the Zagorsk branch of the
counter-revolutionary organisation “True Christians”, taking part in
illegal meetings in the sanctuary of the church and carrying on
monarchist anti-Soviet agitation, and on 20 May 1931 the troika of the
Moscow OGPU sentenced him under articles 58-10 and 58-11 to ten years in a
labour camp, to be served in the Siblag. Six years of imprisonment, in
which he worked at tree-felling, as a draughtsman and as a clerk, were
beyond measure heavy in spirit and body; from 1934 he was at the Chistyunka
separate camp point in the Kemerovo region, in a barrack with Archbishop
Juvenal (Maslovsky) and a dozen other clergy, and there the new wave of
persecution of 1937 found him, sparing not even those already under long
sentences. On 2 August all the imprisoned clergy were searched, and from
him were taken an antimension with its cloth, a Gospel, a prayer-book, a
service-book, a wooden cross and a perpetual calendar — a paschalion; from
9 August the secret department of the NKVD called in the barrack elders,
and false witnesses were found among the criminals and petty offenders
who reported that the priests held services in the barrack in the
evenings and obstructed the cultural-educational work of the camp point.
On 18 August 1937 he was charged anew with systematically carrying on
anti-Soviet work in the camp and exploiting religious prejudice among the
prisoners and the free workers; at every interrogation he bore himself
firmly and with dignity, betrayed no one and denied his guilt, though he
admitted that he served God in the camp too. On 7 September the troika of
the NKVD for the West Siberian region sentenced him to the highest
penalty, and on 13 September 1937 — 31 August of the old style, the day
the Russian calendar keeps him — he was shot with other priests and
buried in an unknown common grave of the West Siberian region. He was
canonised on 6 October 2006 and joined to the synaxis of the new martyrs
and confessors of Russia; the Russian calendar keeps him also in the
synaxes of the saints of Kuzbass, Moscow, Altai and Volhynia and of the
new martyrs of Radonezh.
