# Maximus the Capitularius

saint.gr's calendar for 22 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Μάξιμος ο Καπιτουλάριος» and gives him two sentences. He was the guard of some prison at Rome, the page says, explaining the epithet as δεσμοφύλακας, a keeper of the bound. He came to believe in Christ through the holy [Valerian](/saints/valerian-husband-of-cecilia) and [Cecilia](/saints/cecilia-of-rome), whom the same calendar keeps on the same day; he confessed his faith, and he suffered a martyr's death — «αἰκισθεὶς τελειοῦται», he was made perfect under the scourging, in the words the page quotes.

The couplet set over the notice turns on his trade: Maximus the guard of the watch is beaten, having been straightway a guard of the commandments of God. The page gives him no country of his own beyond that prison, no year, no emperor and no hymn, and prints no relics for him.

**The Russian calendar keeps the four of them on one clause and one life page.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar prints for the Julian 22 November «Мц. Кикилии (Цецилии) и мчч. Валериана, Тивуртия и Максима (ок. 230).» and hangs a single life behind all four names. That life tells the story this folder already carries, with the same bishop hiding from the persecutors in a cave by the Appian Way, the same four hundred converted in Cecilia’s house, the same three days of heat and smoke and the same executioner who struck and did not kill; it calls the governor «Аммах» where the Western reading calls him Almachius.

What it adds is the whole of what he was and did, where the Greek page gave him a line. He commanded the detachment of soldiers that escorted [Valerian](/saints/valerian-husband-of-cecilia) and [Tiburtius](/saints/tiburtius-brother-of-valerian) out of the city to their execution. Their courage struck him, and he asked them why they were not afraid to die; they answered that they were leaving a temporary life for the sake of an eternal one. He wanted to know the Christian teaching in detail, took the two of them into his own house, and listened to them talk the whole night. [Cecilia of Rome](/saints/cecilia-of-rome) heard of it and came to him with a priest, and he was baptised with his whole family. When the two brothers were beheaded the next day he confessed publicly that he had seen their holy souls going up to heaven, and for that confession he was beaten to death with lashes.
