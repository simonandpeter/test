# Barnabas of Peristerona

saint.gr's calendar for 21 Οκτωβρίου names Barnabas and Hilarion the wonderworkers together, and begins by saying that very little is known of them. Leontios Machairas in his chronicle and Kyprianos in his history place Barnabas and [Hilarion](/saints/hilarion-of-peristerona) among the three hundred so-called Alamanoi who came to Cyprus after the Second Crusade; but that opinion, likely as it looks, collides with what both the tradition and the saints' own synaxarion give, which is that they came from Cappadocia and lived in the reign of Theodosius the Younger.

Both were of noble families, the page says, and served in the emperor's army, where they were marked out for their bearing, their courage and their whole manner of life. In spite of the bright future that opened before them there, the love of Christ made them leave the army and the glory that smiled on them, and they went into the desert, where prayer, the study of the word of God, asceticism and virtue became their work, and where many wonders were done for those who came to hear their counsel.

Their wonders did not stop when their bodies rested in the earth, and the page counts the coming of their relics to Cyprus as one of them. It says Christians fleeing persecution used to shut what they wished to save in a wooden chest, close it with a note and cast it into the sea, and that this is how the relics of Mamas, of Hermogenes of Samos and of these two reached the island. The saints then appeared by night to a pious man of Soloi named Leontios and told him to take his yoke and come to the place called Stomation to bring them in.

He went with his pair of oxen to the shore of Morphou, near where the river Serrachis comes out, and found the closed chest in the sand; but for all his efforts it would not move, as though it had rooted itself in the ground. The next night the saints appeared again and said that they had not meant the yoke of his oxen but the pair of his sons. He fetched the two boys, and father and sons together carried the holy coffin to the place they had been shown, Peristerona, where the wonders began at once and a beautiful church was raised that stands to this day.
