# Anna (Gorokhova)

She was born in 1896 at Chanki in the Kolomna district of the Moscow province, the daughter of the peasant John Gorokhov, and in 1914 entered as a novice the Dormition Brusensky monastery in the town of Kolomna. [Xenia (Petrukhina)](/saints/xenia-petrukhina), born in the same village a year after her, had entered the same house the year before, and the one life the calendar has for the two of them is one life. In 1919, when the godless power began closing the monasteries, Anna went back to her native village to keep house for her aged father, and Xenia went back too, to help at the church of the Entrance of the Mother of God.

In the persecution of the monastics at the beginning of the thirties both were arrested, on 22 May 1931, and shut in the Kolomna prison. At the questioning they confirmed that they had gone with other believers to the church of the Entrance, that they had gathered about the church and talked among themselves about the food supply and the harvest, and that they had said that the Lord gave neither harvest nor provisions nor goods because of men's sins, and that all of it came of godlessness and of mockery of the holy Church. On 10 June 1931 an OGPU troika sentenced them to three years of exile in Kazakhstan and they were sent by convoy to Karaganda.

In 1934, their term over, they came home. Anna took work in the collective farm and Xenia became a nurse at the medical station by Golutvino railway halt, but they did not give up their needlework, the quilting of blankets, and they went to church as before. The duty witnesses called in afterwards testified that although since their return the novices went visiting almost nobody and had even taken Soviet work for cover, they still kept company with the local priest, that many people came to them from their own village and from others, and that at home they carried on anti-Soviet propaganda.

The two were arrested on 5 March 1938 and shut in the Kolomna prison. Some days later the investigator questioned them; having asked what they did and what they had been arrested for before, he asked whether they knew certain named local people, and when they answered that they knew them he declared that the novices had been engaged in counter-revolutionary activity among the collective farmers and read them the duty witnesses' statements. Anna and Xenia rejected those statements flatly, saying that they had engaged neither in anti-Soviet activity nor in agitation against the government among the people. The investigation closed on 10 March, and on 12 March 1938 an NKVD troika sentenced them to be shot. They were sent from Kolomna to the Taganka prison in Moscow, where on 17 March the prison photographer took their photographs for the executioners. They were shot on 20 March 1938 and buried in an unmarked common grave at the Butovo range outside Moscow, which civil day is the Julian 7 March the calendar keeps them on.
