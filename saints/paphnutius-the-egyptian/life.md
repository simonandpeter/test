# Paphnutius the Egyptian

An Egyptian who laboured as an ascetic in the desert, in the persecutions of Diocletian - when the governor Adrian ordered him brought in, he did not wait for the soldiers sent to fetch him but presented himself, to confess Christ and receive the torments. Of the soldiers who tortured him, Dionysius and Callimachus, seeing the power with which God kept the martyr unharmed, believed in Christ themselves and were beheaded. Thrown into prison, he brought forty of the prisoners to Christ, and they were burned alive; released and taken in by a Christian named Nestorius, he led that whole household to a martyr's end; counsellor and strengthener of many, the life counts five hundred and forty-six who confessed Christ through him, some by the sword and some by fire.

The executioners threw him into a river with a stone hung at his neck, and the life records the wonder by which he did not stay there. The Romanian calendar keeps him on this day.

The Greek calendar keeps him as Paphnutius the hieromartyr, «ο Ιεροσολυμήτης», with his life and his hymns on 19 April and again, with a verse on his crucifixion, on 25 September; its account is the same man’s. In the years of Diocletian (284–305) the prefect Arrianus set out to seize him in the deserts of Egypt where he lived, and Paphnutius came to meet him alone. Arrianus had him tortured on the spot until his entrails showed; he prayed, and his wounds closed, so that the two soldiers who had tortured him believed in Christ, and Arrianus beheaded them and put Paphnutius in prison. There he made Christians of forty leading men held for arrears of tax, and when Arrianus burned them all alive Paphnutius vanished from his sight by a wonder.

After bringing many more to the faith he presented himself again, and was ordered cut to pieces; but when Arrianus came to see the body the pieces joined, and Paphnutius, alive before him, asked whether he knew him, and told him that the Lord Jesus Christ worked these wonders to rebuke his impiety, that he was kicking against the goads and worshipping deaf and blind idols. The wonder brought a whole company of soldiers to believe; in despair Arrianus sent him to Rome, where he died on a cross. The Greek calendar keeps him with two apolytikia, the second the common troparion of a hieromartyr, and a kontakion.
