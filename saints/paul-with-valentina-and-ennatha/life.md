# Paul, with Valentina and Ennatha

Doxologia prints one short life for the three martyrs of this day, and Paul stands last in it. Saint [Ennatha](/saints/ennatha-of-gaza) was from the region of Gaza and [Valentina](/saints/valentina-of-caesarea) from Caesarea; when the governor Firmilian sat in judgement, Ennatha was brought before him, confessed that Christ is God and was cruelly tortured, and Valentina, unable to bear the cruelty of it, kicked over the altar that stood beside the judgement seat, with the fire on it, and was tortured in her turn. The governor sentenced the two of them to be burned.

After this, the page says, Saint Paul was brought to suffer. When he had endured a number of torments and had shown himself, by the grace of Christ, to be above the passions, he was condemned to death by the sword. He gave thanks to God and prayed for those of one faith with him, and so received the cutting off of his honourable head, giving up his spirit into the hands of God.

That is everything the page says of him. It gives him no birthplace, no year and no hymn, and his name in the corpus carries the two women's because this life is all there is to tell him apart by.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, with [Ennatha](/saints/ennatha-of-gaza) and [Valentina](/saints/valentina-of-caesarea), on one page, and saint.gr makes his part in it one moment: when both women had been bound and tortured before the governor Firmilian, «τη στιγμή εκείνη, παρουσιάστηκε κάποιος νέος, που ονομαζόταν Παύλος» — at that moment a certain young man came forward, named Paul, and with boldness branded Firmilian's crime. The governor, enraged, ordered Paul beheaded and the two women burnt alive, and so all three took the immortal crowns. The page prints a distich of his own for him: «Ἐκεῖνος οὗτος Παῦλος ὁ Χριστοῦ φίλος, Χριστοῦ πόθῳ τράχηλον ἐκτετμημένος» — this is that Paul, the friend of Christ, his neck cut through for the longing of Christ.
