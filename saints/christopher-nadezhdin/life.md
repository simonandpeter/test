# Christopher (Nadezhdin)

He was born on 21 February 1869 in the family of a priest, in the village of Нижний Белоомут in the Зарайский district of the province of Ryazan. He finished the Zaraysk church school and the Ryazan seminary, and in 1897 the Moscow Theological Academy; from 1901 he served in Moscow, and he was rector of the church of Иоанн Воин on Якиманка. The Russian calendar's name page for him calls him a priest and the life page an archpriest, and both words are set down here because the source gives both.

In the last days of March 1922 he was arrested in the case of the confiscation of church valuables. He did not admit himself guilty, and he refused to name those through whom he had received the Epistle of the patriarch Тихон. The indictment of 8 May added a charge of its own against him: that on 4 March he had openly preached from the ambo a sermon the tribunal called irreligious, on the theme of the perishing and once mighty Russian realm, and had called the people to active resistance.

The public trial of fifty of the arrested opened on 26 April in the hall of the Polytechnic Museum under the chairmanship of the tribunal's president Бек. On 8 May the Moscow revolutionary tribunal sentenced eleven men to be shot; on 18 May the Politburo struck six names from that list, and five were left. Those five were [Christopher](/saints/christopher-nadezhdin)'s own company: with him [Alexander (Zaozersky)](/saints/alexander-zaozersky), [Basil (Sokolov, 13 May)](/saints/basil-sokolov-13-may), the hieromonk [Macarius (Telegin)](/saints/macarius-telegin) and the layman [Sergius (Tikhomirov)](/saints/sergius-tikhomirov).

They were shot at the beginning of June 1922 in what the page calls the ship of the Cheka — the premises of the archive of a former insurance company, where the condemned were shot in the windowless rooms built for its safes in the half-basement — and buried at the Калитниковское cemetery in Moscow. They were canonised in 1998 as locally venerated saints of the Moscow diocese, and numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for veneration throughout the Church at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
