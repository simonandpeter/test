# Hesychius the Sinaite

saint.gr's calendar for 29 Μαρτίου names the venerable Hesychius the Sinaite, and the page behind the line is one sentence: he lived in the seventh century after Christ, he was abbot of the monastery of Sinai, and he fell asleep in holiness and peace. There is no birthplace, no teacher, no disciple, no wonder, no relic and no hymn, and the page names no writing of his, though the monastery he governed was in those years the house of John of the Ladder and of much else that was written there.

This corpus keeps that silence rather than filling it. What can be said is what the source says, and what the source says is a century, an office and a peaceful death. The gloss it prints on the name — «ΗΣΥΧΙΟΣ (από το ήσυχος) = ο ήσυχος, ο γαλήνιος», the quiet, the serene, the maker of calm — is about the word, as is the note beside it about a lexicographer of Alexandria in the fifth century.

One thing needs saying to keep two men apart. The Greek calendar keeps a Hesychius of Jerusalem on the day before this one, 28 March, a presbyter of Jerusalem in the fifth century who commented on the Scriptures; he is not this abbot of Sinai in the seventh, and neither page mentions the other.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 29 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/29/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/106/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
