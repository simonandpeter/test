# Valentine of Interamna

Valentine was bishop of Interamna, a city in the region of Italy called Umbria, and he had from God the gift of healings; curing every infirmity and sickness of men by prayer and by calling on the most holy name of Jesus Christ, he turned unbelievers away from the worship of idols. In his day the pagan emperors held both West and East and persecuted the Christians. Three students from Athens, Greek by birth and by religion — Proculus, Ephebus and Apollonius — came to Rome for learning, and lodged with a famous rhetorician named Craton, skilled in both Latin and Greek.

Craton's son Herimon had fallen gravely ill, and in his sickness his back was so bent that his head lay between his knees and he could not straighten at all. Many physicians were called and none could help him, and he suffered three years without hope from them. Then a tribune named Fronton came to Craton's house, saw the boy, and said that a brother of his own had suffered the like and had been healed by a Christian bishop named Valentine, who lived in the city of Interamna. Craton sent honoured friends of his to fetch him, since the boy could not be carried so far, and Valentine came.

Craton offered half his wealth for the cure. The bishop answered that he marvelled at a wise teacher who did not take his meaning: if Craton himself would believe in Christ and cast off the idols made of wood and stone and put his hope in the one invisible and almighty God, his son would be made well, and the half of his goods that he had promised should be given to the poor. When Craton argued that every man is saved by his own faith and that one man's faith cannot help another, Valentine answered him out of the Scriptures — the centurion's dying servant, the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, Lazarus raised on the fourth day at his sisters' weeping — and out of Pharaoh, whose unbelief brought plagues on all Egypt and lost him his own army.

At that the rhetorician fell at his feet and said that he believed in the God preached to him, who commands sicknesses and death and they flee. Valentine told him that the Christian faith is shown not in words only but in works, and shut himself in with the boy for a night, saying he would not open the door until he had finished his usual prayers and songs of praise. At daybreak he opened it and brought out Herimon whole in body, as though he had never been ill, and gave him to his parents; and Craton with his whole household believed in Christ and was baptised.

The healed boy would not be parted from his physician, and the three students likewise left their outward philosophy and attached themselves to Valentine as his disciples, following his holy life. They brought other scholars of their own age to faith in Christ and to baptism by their urging and by the example of their honourable and prudent lives, so that crowds of students gathered to them and were taught heavenly wisdom. But when Abundius, the son of the city prefect, was baptised and confessed before everyone that he was the servant of Christ, his father and the other senators were filled with anger, seized Valentine, beat him without mercy and pressed him to worship the idols.

He would not do their will, and after cruel torture he was shut in prison, where he rejoiced that he had been counted worthy to suffer for Christ his Lord and boasted with the apostle that he bore the wounds of his Lord on his body. He went on strengthening the disciples who came to him there, and the prefect, hearing of it, was greatly angered, sent by night, had him brought out of the prison and beheaded. So Valentine ended his martyr's struggle.

His disciples Proculus, Ephebus and Apollonius took the honoured body and carried it to the city of Interamna, where they bought a small piece of ground before the city and buried it with honour; and they stayed by the grave day and night in fasting and prayer, praising God. All the believers in the city gathered to them, and many of the unbelievers came and were enlightened by their preaching. After some time Leontius, the governor of the city, arrested the three and put them in prison; and because he saw the people rioting and mourning for them, since they were loved by all, he had them killed by night rather than condemn them by day and risk their being taken out of his hands. They were beheaded in the night. Abundius, who had come to his beloved friends and heard of their killing, grieved bitterly, took their bodies and buried them with honour beside the grave of Valentine his teacher.
