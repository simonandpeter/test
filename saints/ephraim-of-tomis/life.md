# Ephraim of Tomis

Ephraim was the second known bishop of the eparchy of Tomis, who held for a time the pastoral throne of Pontic Dacia, as the Greek and Latin synaxaria attest. He was born south of the Danube of Christian parents who brought him up from childhood in the fear of God. Going to worship at the Holy Places he stayed there, and was made a priest and a server in one of the churches; in the Roman Empire of that time the services were in Greek, Latin, Syriac and Thracian, the Bessian tongue.

Becoming a disciple of Hermon, patriarch of Jerusalem, he was prepared from his youth to preach the Gospel of Christ to the peoples the page calls barbarian, at the mouths of the Danube and around the Euxine Sea. At the beginning of the fourth century Hermon sent him to shepherd the Daco-Roman faithful of the bishopric of Tomis, and he converted many Dacians, Romans, Goths and Scythians, and bought back from death many slaves, whom he then baptized.

In the days of this blessed bishop's pastorate, scores of Daco-Roman, Scythian, Bessian, Thracian, Greek and Cappadocian Christians who had been banished into Pontic Dacia were martyred for the faith in Christ. Their relics the bishop Ephraim would ransom and bury in hidden places, raising churches of wood or of stone above them and appointing priests to serve the Liturgy there by night.

In the great persecution of Diocletian in the years 304 and 305 the blessed bishop was seized, imprisoned and cruelly tortured at Cherson in the Crimea, so that he should deny the true God and worship the idols. Confessing that he was ready to give his life for Jesus Christ, the Saviour of the world, he was beheaded in the year 304, and his memory is kept on 7 March.

doxologia quotes the Synaxarium of the Church of Constantinople at 7 March: that in the reign of Diocletian, Hermon, bishop of Jerusalem, sent Ephraim as bishop into Scythia and [Basileus](/saints/basileus-of-cherson) to Chersonesus. The same page argues against those who would place Ephraim in Great Scythia, since the Life of Saints Epictetus and Astion names a bishop Ephraim at Tomis, in Little Scythia. The life of the bishops of Cherson printed on the same site tells it a little differently, and calls the patriarch who sent them Ephraim rather than Hermon.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and keeps him inside the company of seven. saint.gr sets the mission in the reign of Diocletian and says that the patriarch of Jerusalem Hermon sent the bishop Ephraim into Scythia and the bishop Basileus to Cherson, for the spreading of Christianity; of Ephraim's own end it says one sentence, that while he was teaching the Gospel he was seized by the idolaters and beheaded. Its distich turns on that: not bowing his head to the images, Ephraim rejoices, bowing it to the sword. The page gives him no year, and the reign it names is the setting of the sending rather than a date of his death.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day among the seven bishops of Cherson, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the seven of them one entry. All of them were bishops in Cherson at different times, it says, and all of them suffered at the hands of unbelievers — whether of Jews, or of Greeks, or of Scythians — except Aetherius alone, who died in peace. All of them went out into those wild parts as missionaries, sent by the patriarch of Jerusalem to carry the light of the Gospel; all were tormented, and all suffered for the sake of their Lord.

Of him the entry says one thing: Ephraim was beheaded with the sword. One reading is worth setting down beside it. The Prologue makes all seven bishops in Cherson, where the Romanian calendar from which this folder was first written gives this one the see of Tomis and prints him on a line of his own; the Greek calendar keeps him first of the seven names.

The Russian church keeps all seven of them on this day, on one line — «Священномучеников, в Херсонесе епископствовавших: Василия , Ефрема , Капитона , Евгения , Еферия , Елпидия и Агафодора (IV).» — and its life is the fullest account of the company any of the four calendars read has given. They carried the gospel, it says, into the lands of the northern Black Sea from the Danube to the Dnieper, the Crimea among them, and sealed their apostolate with a martyr's death in the fourth century at Херсонес Таврический. The page sets them in a history: the faith had reached the Crimea, anciently Таврида and subject to the Roman emperors, long before the baptism of Владимир; its beginning there was the preaching of the apostle Андрей Первозванный; and its spread was helped against their will by its own enemies, since the emperors used the place for state criminals, which in the first three centuries meant confessors of Christ — «в правление Траяна (98–117) был сослан на заточение в Инкерманские каменоломни святой Климент, епископ Римский», who went on preaching in the Inkerman quarries and was martyred there. The pagans of the Crimea resisted hard and many preachers died in the struggle.

The episcopal see at Херсонес was founded at the beginning of the fourth century, in a turning time when the city, a base for Roman soldiers, was passing into dependence on Byzantium. In the reign of Diocletian, which the page gives as 284 to 305, and in the year 300 — «то есть еще до начала гонения, которое император начал с 303 года», before the persecution the emperor began in 303 — the patriarch Ермон of Jerusalem, whose years the same sentence gives as 303 to 313, sent many bishops out into various countries for the preaching of the gospel. **The two numbers do not agree and nothing is done about it here**: a patriarch whose reign the page dates from 303 cannot have sent anybody in 300, and both figures are the page's own.

**This calendar calls him of Херсонес where this folder is named for Tomis, and the line is the reason the folder is the right one all the same.** He is named second on the Russian line, among the bishops who held sees at Херсонес; the Greek row already on this folder quotes saint.gr's heading for the same company, and the Romanian row gives him Tomis as his see, which is in `office`. The Russian life has him arrive at Херсонес in 300 with Василий, sent out with many other bishops by the patriarch Ермон of Jerusalem for the preaching of the gospel, and the two of them plant the word of God there. «Затем святитель Ефрем пошел к народам, жившим по Дунаю» — then he went to the peoples living along the Danube, where he bore much affliction and labour in the preaching of Christ; and in the persecution that came he was beheaded. **The page is explicit that only the day of his death is known**: «точно известен лишь день его кончины – 7 марта». So the two sees are one career on this reading, Херсонес first and the Danube after, and the folder's death year of 304 is left exactly as the Romanian and Greek pages gave it, because this page refuses a year for him in so many words.

The preaching of the hieromartyrs, the page ends, established the faith at Херсонес: from the beginning of the fifth century the city was a spiritual centre from which Christianity spread north towards Rus, from the ninth the Russians were settling in it, modern excavation has found more than fifty churches there of the fifth to fourteenth centuries, and in 987 Владимир was baptised in it. «Этот древний город явился колыбелью христианства для Руси» — this ancient city was the cradle of Christianity for Rus.
