# Macedonius the Barley-Eater

The venerable Macedonius took the peaks of the mountains for his wrestling-school and his stadium. He did not settle in one place, but moved continually from one to another, in Phoenicia, in Syria and in Cilicia; and he kept to that way of life, saint.gr says, precisely in order to escape the troubling attentions of the people who came running to him.

For forty-five years he held to it, using neither tent nor hut but living inside a deep pit. When he had grown old he gave way to some who begged him, and built himself a hut, and in it he continued the ascetic contest for another twenty-five years. He received from God the gift of wonderworking, and he fell asleep in peace in the fourth century.

One of his many miracles is this. A woman was brought to him who, by the working of the devil, ate a great quantity of food, and those who brought her begged him to make her well. He had compassion on the girl, and after praying to God he put his right hand into water and made the sign of the Cross over it; and when she had drunk of that water she was well at once.

Theodoret, the page adds, reports that because he lived in pits he was also called the venerable Gouvas, γούβα being the Syriac word for a pit, and that he was called Kritophagos, the barley-eater, because barley was his food. No hymn is printed for him.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same menologion day as «Прп. Македония, сирийского пустынника (ок. 420)», the Syrian hermit, with the same year of his repose, and its name link calls him пресвитер as well. It does not print the barley by which the Greek calendar names him, and it prints no hymn.
