# Paul of Jamnia

Paul was the third of the twelve martyrs who suffered for Christ at Caesarea in Palestine in the reign of Diocletian. The life calls him fervent in faith and burning with zeal for the right belief, and says he came from the town called Jamnia, which had already suffered burning by fire for Christ. The first two of the twelve were [Pamphilus](/saints/pamphilus-of-caesarea), presbyter of Caesarea, and [Valens](/saints/valens-the-deacon), deacon of the church of Aelia.

The three of them were put to various tortures by the governor Urban and then thrown into prison, where they remained two years, until Firmilian succeeded him. In that time five young Egyptians who had escorted a hundred and thirty banished confessors to the mines of Cilicia were taken at the gate of Caesarea, on their way home, for confessing that they were Christians; and on the next day, which was 16 February, they were brought out of prison together with Pamphilus, Valens and Paul.

Firmilian could get nothing from the young men but prophets' names and the Jerusalem above, and had them beheaded. Then he turned to the three older confessors, and, learning how much Urban had already done to them and how long they had lain in prison, he asked only whether they would obey the emperor's command. They would not, and he sentenced them to the sword with the rest. Doxologia gives Paul no year beyond the reign, and prints no troparion for him.
