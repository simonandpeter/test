# Andrei Șaguna

Andrei Șaguna, Anastasie at his baptism, was metropolitan of Ardeal and of Aromanian stock. He was born on 20 December 1808 — 1 January 1809 in the new style — at Miskolc in Hungary, and departed to the Lord on 16/28 June 1873 at Sibiu, being buried at Rășinari. He went to the gymnasia of Miskolc and of Pest, finishing in 1826, read philosophy and law at the university of Pest from 1826 to 1829, and theology at the Orthodox seminary of Vârșeț from 1829 to 1832.

When his theological studies were done he entered the Serbian monastery of Hopovo, where he was tonsured a monk with the name Andrei on 24 October 1833 and ordained hierodeacon in February 1834. He taught at the theological seminary of Karlowitz and was secretary of its archdiocesan consistory from 1834, was ordained hieromonk on 29 June 1837, and served as protosyngellos, assessor of the metropolitanate and acting abbot at the monasteries of Iazac in 1838 and Beșenovo in 1841; he was made archimandrite and abbot of Hopovo in 1842 and of Covil in 1845, and for a time after 1842 taught in the Romanian section of the seminary at Vârșeț.

On 15/27 June 1846 he was named vicar general of the bishopric of Ardeal, with his seat at Sibiu; on 2 December 1847 he was elected bishop, recognised on 5 February 1848 and consecrated at Karlowitz on 18/30 April 1848; and on 12/24 December 1864 he was named archbishop and metropolitan of the re-established metropolitanate of Ardeal, again with his seat at Sibiu. As bishop he had worked for that restoration through memoranda to the court at Vienna, to the Serbian Orthodox patriarch and to the Serbian national church congress at Karlowitz, and through the diocesan synods of clergy and laity he called at Sibiu in 1850, 1860 and 1864.

After the restoration, with suffragan dioceses at Arad and Caransebeș, he called a national church congress of all the Orthodox Romanians of the metropolitanate at Sibiu in the autumn of 1868, which approved the Organic Statute of the Romanian Orthodox Church in Transylvania; sanctioned by the state on 28 May 1869, it governed the Church in Transylvania and the Banat until 1925. Its two principles — autonomy from the state, and synodality, with laymen taking two thirds of the seats beside the clergy's one third — stood behind the statutes of the whole Romanian Orthodox Church in 1925 and in 1948.

In learning he organised Romanian Orthodox primary and secondary schooling in Transylvania and put it under the Church: parish priests were directors of the village school, archpriests inspectors of the schools of their district, and the bishop supreme inspector of all the schools of the diocese. By the end of his pastorate the archdiocese of Sibiu had some eight hundred village schools, an eight-class gymnasium at Brașov founded in 1850, a commercial school there from 1869, and a four-class gymnasium at Brad from 1868. More than twenty-five school books were printed at his urging.

At Sibiu he lengthened the theological course from six months to a year in 1846, and in 1853 founded a theological and pedagogical institute of two sections, buying houses for the school and its boarding house; he had a great number of textbooks printed for students and priests, some written by himself and some by the institute's professors, and sent many young men to specialise at the universities of Austria and Germany on scholarships from funds he had founded. He was decisive in founding ASTRA, the Transylvanian association for Romanian literature and culture, and was its first president from 1861 to 1866; and he began the newspaper Telegraful Român in January 1853, which is printed to this day.

As a public man he had a great part in the years 1848 and 1849: president of the Romanian national assembly on the Field of Liberty at Blaj on 3/15 May 1848, and delegated by it to carry the Romanian demands to the emperor of Austria at Innsbruck and Vienna and then to the government at Pest. In the years that followed he presented memoranda to the emperor again and again; after 1860 he sat in the imperial senate at Vienna, from 1863 to 1865 in the diet of Transylvania, and he was co-president of the Romanians' national political conference of 1861 and of their national congress of 1863, both at Sibiu. He founded the church at Gușterița near Sibiu, and was an honorary member of the Romanian Academic Society from 1871.
