# Dorotheus the Hermit of Egypt

Dorotheus was a native of the Thebaid who spent sixty years in the desert of
Scetis, west of the Nile. Palladius, later bishop of Helenopolis, was his
disciple as a young man and kept the memory of him in the Lausiac History:
how, when his prayers were done, the old man went out in the noonday heat to
gather stones along the shore and build cells for other hermits, and wove
baskets by night to trade for the little he needed. His food was bread and
the sparse desert grass, once a day, with a little water; he never lay down
to sleep, but dozed sometimes over his work or after eating. Once his
disciple came back from the well saying a snake was in it and the water
spoiled. Dorotheus went himself, drew a ladle, signed it with the cross and
drank — where the cross is, he said, the demons do no harm. He died in peace
at a great age.

The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 16 September as a hermit of the fourth
century, and says it has no further details.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 16 September old style as a hermit of the fourth century, and its life tells the same account from Palladius. The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, gives him a full sixty years in one cell in the Thebaid, marked out by uncommon love of labour and by wonders, building cells for new monks by day and weaving mats by night without ever breaking off his prayer and psalmody.
