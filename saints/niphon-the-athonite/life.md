# Niphon the Athonite

saint.gr says he was the son of a priest and was born in the village of Loukovi in the district of Argyrokastro. Young in years, he entered the monastery of Mesopotamos, where he was schooled and was ordained priest. Afterwards he went away to the Holy Mountain, and within the bounds of the Great Lavra, having changed cells a number of times, he came at last to the place called the Vouleuteria, and there he practised asceticism about the year 1330.

He then went and settled at the Transfiguration; but because many monks kept coming, he left that place and went as a disciple to [Maximus of Kapsokalyvia](/saints/maximus-of-kapsokalyvia). After a short time, and with his elder's blessing, he settled in a cave near Kapsokalyvia, where he passed the rest of his life in severe asceticism. He lived, the page says, ninety-six years in all.

That is the whole of the narrative; the page gives him no year of birth, no year of death and no account of his end, and names no wonder. It prints for him five synaxarion verses, two apolytikia, a kontakion, a megalynarion, an oikos and a kathisma, and the first of the verses plays on his name as the site's own gloss would: Nephon is Nephon, watchful, by the very facts, for he ran through his life by the watchfulness of the mind. The apolytikion calls him the offspring of Epirus and the glory of priests.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 14 June, the same menologion day the folder’s Greek row carries, and it is the first of this folder’s books to give that day inside a life. He was born in the region of Argyrokastro, in the village of Lukovo, and his father was a priest. From his youth a longing for solitude and prayer drew him, and that longing brought him at last to the Holy Mountain, where he struggled first in the cave called after one saint and then in the desert called after another — each a dedication and no kinship, and the attestation note names them rather than this paragraph, which is §6’s way with a name that would imply a relation it cannot carry. He would not even eat bread, but fed on grass and roots; and when some envious men accused him of loathing bread he cleared himself of it easily and quickly.

At last he joined himself to [Maximus](/saints/maximus-of-kapsokalyvia) at Kapsokalyvia. For his sincere love of God he was given by God the gift of wonder-working and of clear sight: he healed the sick by prayer and by anointing with oil, and he saw into things that had been done and things that were to come. He foretold of himself that he would die at the Fast of the Apostles.

And when the day of his repose broke he said to the brethren about him: Do not weep, but rejoice, for in me you will have one who prays before God for your salvation. At the last he said: It is time for me to go. And he gave up his holy soul to God on the fourteenth of June, in 1330 — a day and a year together, where the Greek page gave only a year in which he was living on the mountain. The folder now holds both, and takes no year of birth from the ninety-six years that page says he lived in all.
