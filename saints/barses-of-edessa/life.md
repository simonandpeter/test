# Barses

Barses and [Eulogius](/saints/eulogius-of-edessa), bishops of Edessa, and [Protogenes](/saints/protogenes-of-carrhae), bishop of Carrhae, confessors, suffered from the Arians in the second half of the fourth century. The emperor Valens (364–378), wanting to plant the Arian heresy, raised a cruel persecution of the Orthodox; at Edessa he drove Barses, a champion of Orthodoxy, from the episcopal throne and sent him into exile on the island of Aradus, where the Orthodox people received the exiled bishop with great honour; sent further, to Oxyrhynchus in Egypt, he met the same welcome, and was then banished to the very edge of the empire, the distant city of Phaeno, where, worn out by exile, he died in 378.

At Edessa Valens unlawfully set on the see an Arian false bishop named Lupus — wolf — who proved a wolf in name and deed, ravaging the flock of Christ; the Orthodox of Edessa, clergy and laity, stopped going to their church, seized by the Arians, and gathered outside the city to worship in the open. Learning of it the emperor ordered the eparch Modestus to kill all the Orthodox who came to worship outside the city; the eparch pitied the people and warned them not to come, but the faithful did the opposite: burning to die as martyrs for Christ they came, every one of them, to the place where they usually gathered to pray.

Modestus, carrying out the order, set off there with armed soldiers, and on the way saw a woman hurrying to the service with her child so that the two of them should not miss the martyr’s crown; struck, he turned back with his soldiers, and going to Valens persuaded him to revoke the decree that all the Orthodox be killed and to limit it to the clergy.

The clergy, led by the senior presbyter Eulogius, were brought before the emperor, who tried to make them enter communion with the false bishop Lupus, and not one agreed; after that eighty clergy were sent in chains into exile in Thrace, and when the Orthodox met them on the way with great honour as confessors and supplied them with everything they needed, the emperor, hearing of it, ordered the martyrs split up in twos and sent to distant places.

The presbyters Eulogius and Protogenes were sent to the Thebaid city of Antinoë, where by their preaching they turned many idolaters to Christ and baptised them; when Valens perished and Theodosius came to the throne the Orthodox confessors still alive were brought back from exile, and the two returned to Edessa — Eulogius was raised to the see of Edessa in place of Barses, who had died in exile, and Protogenes made bishop of Carrhae in Mesopotamia, and both governed their flocks until their deaths at the end of the fourth century. The Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps them as the holy confessors of Edessa, who suffered much in prisons and banishment for the Orthodox faith under Valens, who held the Arian heresy, and were freed under Theodosius.

doxologia.ro keeps him on 15 octombrie as «Sfântul Ierarh Vars, Episcopul Edesei» and prints a short Romanian life of the same man. It says he shone by his good works not only at Edessa but in Phoenicia and in Egypt and in the Thebaid; that the emperor, whom it names Valentinian where the Sretensky life names Valens, first ordered him to live on the island of Aradus, and then, learning that many people were going out to him, sent him into exile to an Egyptian city called Oxyrhynchus, where his famous name drew everyone as before; and that at the last this worthy priest of heavenly things was exiled to a fort of the barbarians nearby. It adds that his bed is said to stand at Aradus to this day in great honour, and that the sick who lie on it in faith are healed, and that he worked countless wonders before he passed to the Lord.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 15 October, and takes his exile further than the Russian account does. saint.gr says Barses lived after the middle of the fourth century, when the Arian heresy was troubling the Church, and had already been marked out as bishop of Edessa; that passing through Phoenicia, Egypt and the Thebaid he enriched his learning and spread his fame; and that he could not stay out of the quarrel between Orthodoxy and the heresy of the Arians, but defended the Church by his teaching. The emperor Valens, a protector of the heretics, exiled him to the island of Arandos; but the faithful hurried there to hear him, and so they moved him to Oxyrrhynchus in Egypt, and there too a new throng of the Orthodox gathered round the brave bishop. At last Valens shut him in a fortress near Algiers, and there he gave up his last breath, having given his whole life to the service of God, of the Church and of his neighbour. His bed, the page adds, survives at Arandos and is honoured by the local people for the wonders worked through it.
