# Joseph of Arimathea

Joseph of Arimathea was of the tribe of Ephraim. He was a rich man, and a disciple of the Saviour Jesus Christ in secret. It was Joseph who saw to the burial of the Saviour: having asked Pilate for the body, he and Nicodemus took it down from the cross, wrapped it in a white winding sheet, and laid it in a new tomb hewn out of the rock which Joseph had made ready for himself.

That is the whole of what doxologia.ro prints of him. The site gives him a life of four sentences and no more: no year, no word of where he came from beyond Arimathea and the tribe he belonged to, and nothing of what became of him afterwards. Its page for the day carries no troparion of his in text, only one sung on video, whose words are the single petition «Sfinte Iosif, roagă-te lui Dumnezeu pentru noi!» — Saint Joseph, pray to God for us.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 31 July. saint.gr says Joseph of Arimathea was a member of the Jewish council at Jerusalem, a councillor, a friend and a secret disciple of Jesus Christ, and that he came from Arimathea, a small town north-west of Jerusalem. He went to Pilate, it says, and asked him for leave to bury the body of Jesus Christ; and having received it, he and Nicodemus took the body of the Lord down from the cross, wrapped it in linen cloths with choice spices, laid it in a hewn tomb and set a great stone over the door of the tomb — for which it cites Matthew, Mark, Luke and John together. Of his end the page says only that he reposed in peace, and adds in a parenthesis that this was most probably in England. It gives no year, and it prints no hymn.

The Охридски пролог keeps him on the Julian 31 July and adds an episode neither of the other two calendars has. It calls him the noble Joseph, a rich man, a member of the Jewish Sanhedrin and a secret follower of Christ, citing Matthew 27 and John 19 for him; and it says that he took the body of Christ down from the cross together with Nicodemus and laid it in his own tomb.

For doing that, it goes on, he was put in chains by the Jews and thrown into prison. But the risen Lord appeared to him there and assured him of his own resurrection. After that the Jews let him out of the prison and drove him from his homeland; and he went out through the world to preach the Gospel of Christ, and brought that good news to England too, where he fell asleep in the Lord. The Greek page had England only as a parenthesis and most probably; this book says it plainly.
