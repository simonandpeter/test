# Abramius of Rostov

saint.gr's calendar for 29 Οκτωβρίου names «Όσιος Αβράμιος Αρχιμανδρίτης Ροστόβ», and the page behind that line says one thing only: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Οσίου» — we have no details of the venerable one's life. So the Greek calendar keeps an archimandrite of Rostov on this day, and gives nothing of him but his rank and his city.

There is no year on the page, nothing of his monastery, his rule or his death, and no hymn. The same day's listing keeps another man of the same name, [Abraham the Recluse](/saints/abraham-the-recluse), whose niece was Mary; the two are separate entries with separate pages and separate lives, and this one is the Russian. The entry carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands, bare as it is.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery is the first of the four to say anything about him beyond his day and his title. He was Averky in the world, left his parents' house young, took the monastic habit, and settled at Rostov on the shore of lake Nero, where the land was still largely pagan and he worked hard at spreading the true faith. Not far from his cell stood a sanctuary where a stone idol, Veles or Volos, was worshipped and feared by the people of Rostov. In a vision the apostle John the Theologian stood before him and gave him a staff crowned with a cross, and with it he broke the idol; on the site of the sanctuary he founded a house in honour of the Theophany and became its superior, and in memory of the vision he raised a church in the apostle's name. Many pagans were persuaded by him and were baptised.

His influence on children was the greatest of all, the life says: he taught them their letters, instructed them in the law of God, baptised them and tonsured them as monks. He received everyone who came to the house with love, and his own day was prayer and work for the brethren — he split firewood for the bakery, washed the monks' clothes and carried water for the kitchen. He died in great old age and was buried in the church of the Theophany; his relics were found under the great prince Vsevolod, whom the page dates 1176 to 1212. In 1551 Ivan the Terrible, going round the holy places before his march on Kazan, was shown in the Theophany monastery the very staff with which the idol had been broken; he took the staff with him on the campaign and left the cross in the house, and on his return from the conquest ordered a new stone church of the Theophany built there with four side chapels, and sent books and icons to it. The day page prints a troparion and a kontakion for him, and the kontakion tells the story of the staff.
