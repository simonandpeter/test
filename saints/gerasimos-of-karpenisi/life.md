# Gerasimos the New of Karpenisi

He came from the Megalo Chorio of Karpenisi, saint.gr says, and his name was George. At eleven he went to Constantinople and stayed with a grocer there. One day he was carrying a copper tray of yoghurt in clay pots on his head; he slipped, the tray fell and every pot broke, and while he stood crying in the street over the misfortune an Ottoman lady of rank took him in and consoled him — and then, with one attention and another, tricked him and contrived to make him a Muslim.

Many years later George understood his sin and left for his own country. Afterwards he went to the Holy Mountain, was tonsured a monk and was named Gerasimos. But he longed for martyrdom, and so he went back to the reigning City and renounced Islam; then, before his former master, he preached his faith in Christ with courage. Flatteries and tortures left him unmoved, and on 3 July 1812 they beheaded him at Baba Houmai in Constantinople. He was twenty-five years old.

His body was buried at first on the island of Proti, the page says; later it was taken to the monastery of Prousos, and from there in 1971 to the great church of his own village, which the people of Megalo Chorio built from wherever they had scattered. The skull and the arms are at Prousos in Eurytania, three vertebrae at the kyriakon of the skete of Koutloumousiou on Athos, and the rest of the relics in his church at Megalo Chorio.
