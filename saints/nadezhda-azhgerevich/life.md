# Nadezhda (Azhgerevich)

She was born in 1877 in the village of Golovenshchitsy in the Sverzhen volost of Minsk province, the daughter of the peasant Grigory Azhgerevich. She was a deeply believing woman, the page says, and had a blessing to take monastic vows, but did not manage to be tonsured. She had no house of her own and lived with the nuns of closed monasteries in Moscow and the Moscow region. Arrested on 29 September 1937 with [Demetrius (Dobroserdov)](/saints/demetrius-dobroserdov), archbishop of Mozhaysk, and seven others, she admitted no guilt.

The life quotes more of her answers than of anyone else's but the archbishop's. Asked what she did and what she lived on, she said: I am without fixed occupation, I wander and I beg. Asked to confirm that she collected money among the faithful to help those exiled for counter-revolutionary activity, she said she did not deny that she gave material help to the arrested out of what she managed to collect. Asked whether she had spread rumours of a famine in the USSR, she said she had not said it — that without her everyone could see the hungry who had come from Ukraine sitting by the churches and asking for food.

Asked whether she had spread provocative rumours of a persecution of religion and the clergy, she answered: yes, I said that the Soviet power arrests innocent people, does outrageous things and shoots innocent people. Asked to name the admirers who gave her a bed for the night, she said she got her lodging from her admirers, but that she did not know their surnames or their addresses.

The NKVD troika sentenced the company to be shot on 17 October 1937, and on 21 October she was shot at the Butovo range outside Moscow and buried in a common unmarked grave. She was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
