# Pompeius, companion of Terentius

Pompeius was one of the forty in Africa who would not obey the edict of Decius, and one of the four whom the governor Fortunatianus set apart from the rest. The life spells his name two ways, «Pompia» where the four are first shut up and «Pompie» where the angel calls them; the calendar's own line for the day has «Pompie». With [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa), [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius) and [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius) he was thrown into the innermost prison and kept under close guard for a second hearing.

At that hearing they told the governor plainly that they were Christians, that their hope was in Christ, that they would not bow to demons nor serve his gods, and that he might lay on them whatever torment he liked. He sent them back in heavy irons, hands and feet bound, iron nails scattered under them and nobody allowed to bring them food; and at midnight a light filled the prison, the Angel of the Lord stood before them and named them one by one, their chains broke at his touch, and a table full of good things was set for them.

When serpents, asps and vipers were loosed into the cell the guards saw from the roof that the creatures would not come near, being held off by the angel; in the morning they turned on the enchanters who had brought them and killed them. Fortunatianus then condemned the four to the sword. They went to the place of execution singing that the Lord had saved them from those who afflicted them, were beheaded there, and were buried about two stadia outside the city.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and enumerates it. saint.gr's line for 10 Απριλίου is «Άγιοι Αφρικανός, Θεόδωρος, Μάξιμος, Πομπήιος, Τερέντιος και άλλοι τεσσαράκοντα Μάρτυρες», and its page dates the martyrdom and names the authorities: «μαρτύρησαν το έτος 250 μ.Χ., επί αυτοκράτορα Δεκίου (249-251 μ.Χ.) και ηγεμόνος Φουρτουνιανού, στην Καρθαγένη της Αφρικής» — in the year 250, under the emperor Decius and the governor Fortunianus, at Carthage in Africa. The torments it tells are one set of torments for them all: they were beaten until their inward parts could be seen, their hips were pierced with red-hot nails and vinegar and salt were thrown on the wounds, they prayed and pulled down the wooden images, the altars and the temples of the idolaters, and at the last they were beheaded.

The page then prints a name and a gloss for each of the rest, and the names are largely those of the Greek past: Alexander, Anaxarchus, Anaximenes, Aristides, Demaratus, Democles, Demosthenes, Dionysius, Epaminondas, Eteocles, Zeno, Elias, Heracles, Isaiah, Hephaestion, Themistocles, Theophrastus, Theseus, Thomas, Isocrates, Luke, Macarius, Miltiades, Mnesarchus, Xenophon, Homer, Parmenion, Pelopidas, Pericles, Pindar, Polybius, Polynicus, Prometheus, Sophocles, Socrates, Timothy, Titus, Philopoemen, Phocion and Chronius. That list holds forty names, while the prose above it says «μαζί με άλλους τριάντα εννέα Χριστιανούς», another thirty-nine, and the day's line says forty others; the page reconciles none of the three counts. Δημᾶς, whom the Romanian calendar keeps in this company, is not in saint.gr's list at all.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same day and names him last of the four its heading enumerates, [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa), [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius), [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius) and Pompeius, with another thirty-six it does not name; and its account makes those four the men who were kept alive when the company was divided. Under Decius the governor of Africa proclaimed that all must sacrifice to the idols and threatened the obstinate with fearful torture; many fell away, but these forty stood unshaken and were given over to torment. The thirty-six were beheaded, after scourging, scraping and the salting of their open wounds; these four were thrown into prison in heavy irons at the neck, the hands and the feet.

There an angel of God came to them, touched the irons so that they fell off, and then set them an abundant table and fed them. They were brought out and tortured and shut up again; and the sorcerers the governor set to gather snakes and scorpions shut them into the same room to no purpose, for the creatures would not touch the servants of God but huddled in one corner and lay there three days, and on the third day, when the prison was opened, rushed at the sorcerers and bit them. At the last the sentence of death was given on the four, and they went to the beheading singing psalms and praising God, who had counted them worthy of a martyr's death, in the year 250 — the year this folder already holds.
