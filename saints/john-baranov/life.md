# John (Baranov)

**A village schoolmaster for sixteen years before he was a priest, and an anonymous correspondent of a Soviet newspaper afterwards.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him second of the sixteen it closes with 1937, among those it calls «сщмчч. ... пресвитеров», and carries no life for him; the life here is what azbyka.ru prints for the civil 5 декабря out of pstgu.ru. Иван Самсонович Баранов was born in 1874 on what is now Belarusian ground, in the village of Рудня-Прибытковская in the province of Гомель. From 1895 to 1911 he was a schoolmaster, and in 1911 was ordained priest. He served the parish of the village of Головичи, not far from where he was born, from 1914 to 1933, and was then arrested and sentenced to five years of exile in Kazakhstan, which he lived out in the village of Тарханка.

**What he did in exile is what he was shot for.** From Тарханка he wrote letters to the newspaper «Спутник агитатора» — the Agitator’s Companion — answering its anti-religious articles with apologetic ones, and signing them with invented names. For this he was arrested a second time, on 5 November 1937; the charge was «систематическая антиколхозная пропаганда», systematic anti-collective-farm propaganda. The indictment put it that he had carried on systematic counter-revolutionary propaganda aimed at breaking up the collective farms and had circulated counter-revolutionary letters in the press under fictitious surnames, against the building of the farms and the mechanisation of agriculture.

**He admitted the handwriting and denied the charge.** Questioned, he said: «Я подтверждаю, что открытка написана действительно мной, но против коллективизации я не выступал. В советские газеты писал письма, по содержанию защищающие всякую поповщину, отвергая материализм» — the postcard was indeed his, but he had not spoken against collectivisation; he had written to the Soviet papers in defence of what they called priestcraft and against materialism, and had taken no part in counter-revolutionary activity.

**One of those letters is in the file, and it is the longest thing the article prints of him.** He wrote that development in the world is such that everything higher contains the properties of the lower; that man completes the evolution but has a spirit set against the organism, with faculties for eternal life in the kingdom of the Spirit — the thought of a Creator, religious and moral feeling, perfecting, answerability for deeds, freedom of self-determination; that there are two laws in the spirit itself, the first of truth, the good of all, and the second of falsehood, private good at others’ cost, and that whoever is governed by the second is left with a carnal mind only. Christianity, he wrote, began with a common life, and socialism is nearer to Christianity than what it replaced; but socialism’s error is that it does not tell true religious men from hypocrites or true pastors from mere performers of rites, and by constraining pure religion weakens itself first of all and makes very many innocent supporters of religion suffer, whose merit is above all that they are carriers of culture. Religion, the letter ends, requires obedience to the powers that be, and no truly religious man will resist them.

**He was sentenced fifteen days after his arrest and shot a fortnight after that.** On 20 November 1937 he was condemned to the highest measure of punishment, and the article closes «Приговор приведен в исполнение 4 декабря 1937 года» — the sentence was carried out on 4 December 1937, which was that year the Julian 21 ноября, the day before the one he is commemorated on.
