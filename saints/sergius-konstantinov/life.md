# Sergius (Konstantinov)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him alone on 14 ноября ст. ст., in a line of one — «Сщмч. Сергия пресвитера (1941).» — and carries no life behind it; the name link, name page 13853, supplies the surname and the grade. azbyka.ru prints the long life, signed by the abbot Damascene Orlovsky out of the sixth book of the lives of the martyrs and confessors of the Russian Church of the twentieth century.

He was born on 15 August 1877 at Rogachevo in the Dmitrov district of the province of Moscow, the son of an archpriest. He was first taught in the church school where his father was a master, then at the Vifania seminary, and on finishing it in 1900 became a teacher in the factory school at Yakhroma. He married in 1906 the daughter of a deacon who later became the last rector of a church that was closed in 1923; she had taken her teaching certificate the same year, and they had ten children, of whom three died as infants. He was ordained priest in 1906 to the Resurrection church at Voskresenskoye, and in 1911 moved to the Trinity church at Yakhroma, where he was made rector and raised archpriest, and where, until the persecution began, he was the head of the school and taught the Law of God. Everyone who knew him remembered him as strict and fair.

The first thing done to him was that his house was taken, which for so large a family was a hard trial. He rented a room in the next village, then borrowed money and bought a house at Leonovo; to pay the debt he took work as a book-keeper at a brickworks without leaving his church, and was dismissed as soon as the management learned that he served and would not stop. They lived poorly, and his wife sewed their daughters' dresses out of the kerchiefs parishioners sometimes gave him, for which the boys in the street mocked the girls.

During the dekulakisation officials came often to take the surplus, and the surplus was sometimes five kilograms of grain, which was the whole of the family's food for the time being. Almost nothing was left them; camp beds were set out at night and there were not enough of those, so they lay two to a bed. Sometimes he was called to night interrogations, and his wife sent a daughter with him to sit on the wooden bench in the district NKVD office and wait until they let her father go, while the mother prayed at home. The church was closed in 1930, and before it was closed the bells were taken down; the priest wept, looking at it.

In 1930 [Thaddeus](/saints/thaddeus-of-tver), archbishop of Tver, made him rector of the Ascension church in the town of Kimry, and he served there until his arrest. The authorities took him on 13 November 1937, questioned him the next day and sent him to the prison at Kashin. Twice he answered that he had carried on no anti-Soviet agitation and had expressed no counter-revolutionary views; and since he would not admit it, duty witnesses were called who signed what the case required. The NKVD troika gave him ten years on 2 December 1937.

He was held at Kashin, then in the Smutikha labour colony, then in the prisons of Bezhetsk and Zubtsov, then in a colony by the Lykoshino station. His letters home survive, and they are mostly about whether a parcel has come and whether his wife is well and how she might travel to see him, and about being poorer than at any time in the whole of his imprisonment, with no sugar and no shop to buy any. In May 1940 he petitioned the regional prosecutor to reopen the case and set him face to face with the men who had borne false witness; the prosecutor answered that he had been convicted correctly.

The case was nevertheless reinvestigated in August and September 1940 on his second complaint. One of the two false witnesses repeated his evidence; the other could not be re-examined, having been arrested himself six weeks after giving it. Three further witnesses, called to confirm that the man was a priest, said that they knew the rector of the Ascension church very well and knew nothing of any anti-Soviet activity of his. The conclusion was that the new evidence had given nothing of substance beyond confirming that he belonged to the clergy, and the sentence was left standing. He was sent to the camp at the Nilov hermitage on Lake Seliger, and on 6 September 1941 to a colony on the edge of the town of Kovrov, where he died of forced labour and hunger on 26 November 1941. He was buried the same day in grave number 226 of the camp cemetery by the woods near the village of Sergeitsevo.
