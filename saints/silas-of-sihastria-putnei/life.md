# Silas of Sihăstria Putnei

Silas was born in 1697 in the district of Botoșani, of Orthodox parents, Ion and Ioana. Very young, he entered the skete of Orășeni as a novice, and from there he came in 1714, at seventeen, to Sihăstria Putnei, the place of stillness to which the monks of Putna withdrew who wanted more prayer and more struggle. There the elder Teodosie received him and tonsured him; and after Teodosie's death the new spiritual father of the skete, the elder Dosoftei, had him ordained deacon and priest and shortly afterwards tonsured into the great schema.

As Dosoftei's disciple and helper, the hieroschemamonk Silas carried for more than thirty years all the care of the community's needs, labouring at the same time at the church services and at the rule of his cell. When Dosoftei died in the autumn of 1753, Silas was made abbot by the metropolitan of Moldavia, Jacob Putneanul. As spiritual father he renewed the inner life of Sihăstria Putnei, and as a good steward, with the metropolitan's blessing, he built a new stone church dedicated to the Annunciation and furnished it with everything needful, and a new refectory and cells besides.

He became a known confessor and guide of souls, valued by simple believers and by the princes of Moldavia Constantin Cehan Racoviță and Grigorie Calimachi, and by high officials, abbots and bishops, whom he led wisely on the way of salvation. Under him the monks of the skete copied out manuscripts containing the holy services and important writings of the Fathers. The best known of his disciples is Dosoftei Herescu, bishop of Rădăuți.

His last years were spent in great want and trial, after the seizure of Bukovina by the Habsburg Empire in 1774 and the restrictions the new rule imposed; the skete lacked what was needed to feed the monks and had to borrow money and food. Knowing his end was near, Silas appointed [Nathan](/saints/nathan-of-sihastria-putnei) to guide Sihăstria Putnei, then asked forgiveness of everyone; and on 23 April 1783, having struggled nearly seventy years in that place, he gave up his soul in peace.

The skete fell empty soon after the three hieroschemamonks — Silas, [Paisius](/saints/paisius-of-sihastria-putnei) and Nathan — had died, and stayed so for more than two hundred years. At the beginning of Lent in 1990 a monk of Putna living on the site of the old skete saw a heavenly light above the porch of the ruined church; and on 24 April 1990, when the rebuilding began, their three graves were found in that porch, the bones yellow as wax and giving off a sweet scent. Many healings followed at the reliquary, and the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church numbered the three among the saints at its session of 6–7 June 2016, with 16 May as their day.
