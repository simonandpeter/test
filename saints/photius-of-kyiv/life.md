# Photius of Kyiv

Photius was a Greek from Monemvasia in the Peloponnese. He entered a monastery as a boy and was tonsured by the elder Acacius, a great ascetic and afterwards metropolitan of Monemvasia. In 1408, when he was at Constantinople with the patriarch on his metropolitan’s business, the Russian see stood empty after the death of [Cyprian](/saints/cyprian-of-kyiv) in 1406, and the patriarch Matthew chose Photius for his learning and holy life. He was consecrated metropolitan on 1 September 1408 and came to Rus’ a year later. He spent half a year at Kyiv, from September 1409 to February 1410, setting in order the southern dioceses that lay in the Lithuanian principality; seeing that the metropolitan’s throne could not stay in a land falling more and more under Catholic Poland, he followed the earlier metropolitans who had moved to Vladimir and then to Moscow, and came to Moscow at Pascha 1410.

For twenty-two years he led the Russian Church through wars, feuds among the princes and Tatar raids — in the spring of 1410, when he had come from Moscow to Vladimir, the khan Edigu sent a force under the prince Talych to take the metropolitan himself prisoner — and raised the spiritual standing, the means and the beauty of the churches of the Moscow see, enough to send large help to the impoverished patriarchate of Constantinople.

The separate Lithuanian metropolia, set up at Prince Vytautas’s insistence, was abolished in 1420, and that year he visited the returned dioceses and greeted them with a long letter of teaching. He wrote against the strigolniki heresy of Pskov, which ceased in 1427, and left an order for the election and consecration of bishops (1423), a teaching on the dignity of the priesthood and its duties, and a spiritual testament telling his life; the all-Russian chronicle compilation of about 1423 was made under his direction.

On 20 April 1430 an angel told him of his coming death, and he died at the time foretold, on the feast of the Placing of the Robe of the Theotokos, 2 July 1431. His relics were found in 1471, and two of his sakkoi are kept in the Armoury of the Moscow Kremlin.
