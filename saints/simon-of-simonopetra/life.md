# Simon of Simonopetra

Fleeing the vanities of this world, Simon turned towards the Holy Mountain, to work there at the salvation of his soul beside a spiritual father. He chose an elder tried in ascetic labours, harsh and exacting, to whom he submitted in body and soul as to God himself; and his exemplary obedience, his humility and his love for his spiritual father — who did not spare to rebuke him with a word and even with the staff — raised him quickly to a great height of virtue.

He became a wonder in the eyes of many Athonite monks, and in the end even his own elder no longer looked on him as a disciple but as a brother in arms in the spiritual war. But it was not fitting that one who had chosen to follow the humility and the passion of Jesus should receive such marks of honour, and so by persistence he obtained the blessing of living in solitude. After much searching he chose for his dwelling a small damp cave in the western part of Athos, more than three hundred metres above the sea; and there he persevered day and night, subject to the unceasing attacks of the demons, armed only with faith, with hope and with the calling of the almighty Name of the Lord.

One night, a few days before the feast of the Nativity, he saw a star come down suddenly from heaven and stand above the rock in front of his cave. Fearing the cunning of the enemy, who often changes himself into an angel of light, the hermit did not trust the vision; but it was repeated several nights running, until the eve of the Nativity, when, after the star had come down over the rock like the star of Bethlehem, a voice was heard from heaven: «Do not doubt this, faithful servant of my Son. Receive this sign, and do not go from here to look for a still more solitary place, as you meant to; for I wish you to found a monastery here for the salvation of many souls».

Set at rest by the voice of the Mother of God, Simon was then caught up and carried at once to Bethlehem, before the child Christ surrounded by angels and shepherds; and when he came to himself he set about building the «New Bethlehem» without delay. A little after that vision three young brothers of a rich family of Macedonia, or perhaps Thessaly, hearing how the virtues of Saint Simon were praised, came to him, and like new magi they laid their fortune at his feet and asked to be received as disciples.

Masons were then called, who, when the saint showed them the place chosen for the work, were afraid to build in such a place, ringed with rocks and hard to reach. A few lines of the page here were not read; what follows them is that a brother was seen appearing unharmed, and that once the building was finished the New Bethlehem began to fill with monks.

Then one day Muslim pirates put in at the shore. Simon went down and met them with gifts, hoping to soften them so that they should not plunder the monastery; but they were not content with the gifts and rushed at him savagely, and all at once they were struck blind, and the arm of one who had threatened the saint with his sword was made stiff. Healed by the prayer of the man of God, they all repented, received holy Baptism and became monks.

Having shown for a long time, by many wonders and prophecies and above all by his enlightened teaching, the grace he had received from God, Saint Simon fell asleep in the peace of Christ before his disciples, whom he had gathered to speak to them one last time of the importance of keeping in the fear of God the traditions entrusted to them: faith, love for one another, and full obedience to their abbot and spiritual father.

Afterwards the saint's grave began to pour out, as water from a spring, a fragrant myrrh that worked wonders — and this is where the saint's name, the Myrrh-streaming, comes from. The frequent devastations the monastery suffered have wiped away every trace of his grave and of his relics; but the saint has never ceased to be present unseen, often showing himself the defender of the obedient and the hard rebuker of the irreverent and the slack. At his yearly commemoration a divine light is sometimes seen by some, coming out of his cave or overshadowing his icon in the church like a canopy.

A century after the saint's repose the daughter of the despot of the principality of Macedonia, whose capital was at Serres, was delivered of an unclean spirit by the saint's intercession; and in thanksgiving the despot turned the saint's small monastery into a great foundation, endowed with many properties. Later the monastery had its troubles: three times it was destroyed by fire, in 1581, in 1625 and in 1890, and in the eighteenth century it fell so low that it was closed and handed over to the Turkish authorities. In 1763 a great restorer of the hesychast tradition settled there with his sixty-nine monks, but, at once beset by Turkish creditors, he decided to leave Athos for Moldavia, where his community grew greatly.

The New Bethlehem came back to the common life only in 1801, and since 1973 monks who came there from Meteora have given it a new life; today a community of nearly sixty monks of various nationalities travels towards the kingdom of God under Saint Simon's protection. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Simon Izvorâtorul de mir, ctitorul Mănăstirii Simonos Petras», gives no year for his birth or his death, and its page for him prints no hymn; the life it carries is taken from Alexander Golitzin, Mărturia vie a Sfântului Munte (E.I.B.M.B.O.R., Bucharest, 2006, pp. 156-159).
