# Tikhon (Arkhangelsky)

He was born on 30 May 1875 at Bolshe-Popovo in the Voronezh province, the son of a priest named John Arkhangelsky. His parents died early and he and his sister were brought up by a cousin, Zinaida, and her husband Peter, who in time sent him to the seminary. On leaving it he married [Chionia](/saints/chionia-arkhangelskaya), the daughter of another priest, and was ordained soon after the wedding to the church at Troekurovo in the Voronezh diocese, near Lebedyan. Eighteen children were born to them between 1901 and 1923, and nine lived — six daughters and three sons.

Troekurovo stood on the bank of the river Krasivaya Mecha near a women's monastery since destroyed. The priest was given land, and the big family lived off what they could work out of it; the plot was a poor one, full of stones, and clearing it took a great deal of labour, which all the older children shared. The calendar says plainly what that was worth later: it accustomed them to every kind of work and helped them bear what came.

He was a conscientious and ardent priest who prayed much and served often, welcoming and quick to feel another's grief, and always able to comfort somebody who came to him in trouble. He was also firm: nobody could speak coarsely or foully in his presence without being stopped and told of it. With all that he was a man of few words and reserved. For irreproachable and zealous service he was raised to archpriest.

In 1928 the authorities closed the church at Troekurovo and decided to register him as a kulak, so as to dekulakise him and take everything. Many in the village soviet respected him, and one of its own officials came to the house and told him what was planned. It was Chionia who settled it: rather than wait to be thrown out of the house, better to pack what was needed and go to Lebedyan for a while. He agreed; the same official who had warned them drove them to the town in a small peasant sledge. Bishop Uar of Lipetsk sent him to a parish three kilometres from Lebedyan, which was closed within a year, then to Ilyinskoye, which was closed too — those were the years of the campaign to close the churches across the whole country — and then to another village.

At his last interrogation he was read the witnesses' statements: that he had carried on counter-revolutionary agitation using religion as a superstition, and had expressed terrorist intentions against the leaders of the party and the Soviet power. «Все эти обвинения я отрицаю, а также отрицаю и показания свидетелей, как вымышленные» — I deny all these charges, and I deny the witnesses' statements as invented. Asked to describe his counter-revolutionary connections, he answered that he had none and never had. The questioning ended there.

The NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot on 4 October 1937, which is the day the Church now keeps him on. Those condemned were killed beyond the edge of the town of Lipetsk. Before the shooting an NKVD man asked him: will you not renounce? «Нет, не отрекусь!» — no, I will not renounce. He was shot on 17 October 1937 and buried in a common grave which is now unknown.
