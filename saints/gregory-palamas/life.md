# Gregory Palamas

Gregory was born at Constantinople in 1296. His father rose to be a prominent dignitary at the court of Andronicus II Palaeologus but died early, and the emperor himself took a hand in raising and schooling the fatherless boy. He was able and diligent and mastered the whole course of what then counted as higher education, and Andronicus hoped he would give himself to the work of the state.

Instead, barely twenty, he withdrew in 1316 to Mount Athos and entered Vatopedi as a novice under the elder Nikodemos, was tonsured there and began the ascetic life; a year later the Evangelist John appeared to him in a vision and promised his protection. His mother and sisters also became monastics.

After Nikodemos died he spent eight years under the elder Nikephoros, and after his death moved to the Lavra of Saint Athanasius, serving in the refectory and then as a church singer. Three years later he settled in the small skete of Glossia, seeking a greater perfection, and there its head began to teach him the method of unceasing prayer. He was afterwards archbishop of Thessalonica.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 14 noiembrie, and keeps his family with him on the same day. The life doxologia prints there gives him as born at Constantinople in 1296, the eldest of the seven children of [Constantius](/saints/constantius-father-of-gregory-palamas) and [Kalloni](/saints/kalloni-mother-of-gregory-palamas), courtiers of the emperor Andronicus II who had fled Asia Minor before the Turkish invasions; his teacher, it says, thought he was hearing Aristotle himself. Around 1316 he took his mother, two brothers — [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-brother-of-gregory-palamas) and [Macarius](/saints/macarius-brother-of-gregory-palamas) — and two sisters — [Epicharis](/saints/epicharis-sister-of-gregory-palamas) and [Theodota](/saints/theodota-sister-of-gregory-palamas) — with him into the monastic life, and went on foot to Athos.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 14 November, and calls him archbishop of Thessalonica and wonderworker. saint.gr says it does not know the time or place of his birth, but reports from S. Eustratiades that he was born in 1296 at Constantinople, of Constantine the Senator and the most pious Kalloni, and that in the first half of the fourteenth century he was at the imperial court and withdrew to the Holy Mountain. In 1335 his two treatises on the procession of the Holy Spirit brought him up against Barlaam the Calabrian, who taught that man cannot know God and called the hesychasts Messalians.

Palamas settled at Thessalonica and began the struggle for those who keep holy silence, distinguishing the unparticipable divine essence from the participable divine energy; four councils confirmed his reading, the last at Constantinople in 1351. The patriarch Isidore elected him bishop of Thessalonica, and after a time on Lemnos he took up his duties; he died in 1360 and was honoured as a saint at once.

The calendar of the Сретенский monastery, which this folder already cites for the feast and the year, prints three hymns under his name on that day: a troparion of the hierarch Gregory in tone eight, a second troparion headed for Gregory Palamas, archbishop of Thessalonica, in the same tone, and a kontakion of the hierarch Gregory, also in tone eight. All three are now in the folder, with the English typed here. The two troparia are one prayer twice over in different words — lamp or teacher of Orthodoxy, champion of theologians, the boast of Thessalonica or of Salonica, preacher of grace — and the calendar prints both without saying which belongs to the day.

The Ohrid Prologue begins him at court. His [father](/saints/constantius-father-of-gregory-palamas) was a notable official at the court of the emperor Andronikos II Palaiologos; and he, gifted, having finished the worldly sciences, would not go into court service but withdrew to the Holy Mountain and was tonsured. He struggled at Vatopedi and at the Lavra, carried on the fight with the heretic the book names and in the end defeated him, and was consecrated metropolitan of Thessalonica in 1347.

He was glorified as an ascetic, as a theologian, as a hierarch and as a wonderworker, it says, and lists those who appeared to him by turns: the Most Holy Theotokos, [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), [Demetrius](/saints/demetrius-the-myrrhstreamer), Antony the Great, Chrysostom, and the angels of God.

He governed the church of Thessalonica thirteen years, of which he spent one year in captivity among the Saracens in Asia, and reposed in peace in 1360; his relics rest at Thessalonica, where there is also a beautiful church of his name. The years agree with what this folder already holds — 1347 and thirteen years reach the c. 1360 it records — so nothing is widened and nothing closed. The title the entry gives him is archbishop in the heading and metropolitan in the body, and the folder's `types` keep the first of those.
