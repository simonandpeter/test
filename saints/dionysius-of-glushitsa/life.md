# Dionysius of Glushitsa

saint.gr says Dionysius, whose name in the world was Demetrios, was born probably in 1362 in the outskirts of the city of Vologda in Russia, and loved the monastic life from a small age. For that reason he left his father's house and entered, in 1386 or 1387, the monastery of Spasokamensky, whose abbot was Dionysius the Greek, afterwards bishop of Rostov. The young Demetrios begged the abbot with tears in his eyes to make him a monk; and the abbot, seeing his godly zeal, tonsured him, gave him the name Dionysius, and put him under the guidance of one of the brethren.

There he lived nine years in obedience, fasting and unceasing prayer, and never slackened his discipline to the end of his life. His humility, his love and his industry made him much loved among the monks, who reckoned him a man of great spiritual weight. He had the gift of the iconographer, and worked besides as carpenter and smith for the needs of the house; he let no minute pass without occupation and ate only the least food, and that when his strength was quite spent. In 1396 he was ordained deacon and presbyter by Gregory, archbishop of Rostov.

With the monk Pachomios and the abbot's blessing he went off to the forgotten cenobitic monastery of Saint Luke in search of quiet; but Pachomios could not bear the hard discipline, and Dionysius had to leave the place with him. He went east, to lake Kubenskoye, fifteen kilometres from the bank of the river Glushitsa, settled in a remote place ringed with trees, raised there the cross he had carried from Saint Luke's, built a cell and began the hard life of a hermit alone.

When a starets and some other brethren came wishing to stay with him, about the year 1400, a small brotherhood formed, and with it the thought of building a monastery. The prince of the region sent workmen, and in 1402 Dionysius went to Rostov for bishop Gregory's leave; the bishop advised him to found a cenobitic house, so that the monks should have no private property but live in common after the pattern of the Apostles. The wooden church, dedicated to the Lady Theotokos, was finished in 1403, with some fifteen fathers keeping the strict cenobitic rules of the Holy Mountain, and a new church, also of the Mother of God, was built in 1412 as the brotherhood grew.

The love of the desert stayed rooted in him, and he withdrew four kilometres down the Glushitsa to the place later called Sosnovets, where he lived unknown to the monks in prayer and strict fasting. Pressed hard by the others he came back, but resolved to build small hermitages at Sosnovets for the monks who wanted the desert; and in 1419 he went again to Rostov for the bishop's blessing on the new house, and had of him an icon of the Mother of God with the Child and vessels for the church. The church at Sosnovets, dedicated to Saint John the Forerunner, was built in 1420, with the help of prince George, three of whose letters about his gifts survive.

He taught the other monks, saying: my children, do not be afraid of the labours the desert has, and do not leave off the discipline; through many trials we shall come to the Kingdom of Heaven. Free yourselves by fasting from everything earthy and corruptible. Our prayer must well up out of a clean heart, and we must be humble. As for almsgiving, I remind you of the Lord's words, blessed are the merciful; let us be merciful towards all, and the Lord will show mercy to us also, for only he who loves his brother loves God.

Seven years before his repose he dug his own grave and visited it every day, cultivating in his heart the remembrance of death; and he fell asleep in peace in 1437, at the age of seventy-five.
