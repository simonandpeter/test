# Joseph the Merciful

Joseph Naniescu was born in Bessarabia on 15 July 1818, in the village of Răzălăi in the district of Soroca, the son of the priest Anania Mihalache and of his wife Teodosia, and was given the name Ioan at his baptism. His father died while he was small and his mother became a nun; at ten he was taken by an uncle, the hierodeacon Teofilact, into the brotherhood of the monastery of Frumoasa.

From Frumoasa the two went on to the monastery of Saint Spyridon in Iaşi, where there was a well-known hospital, and afterwards to Saint Samuel's at Focşani, where there was another. doxologia.ro says it was in those houses that the boy learned obedience, humility, and the service of God and of the suffering. At Buzău he was tonsured and received from Bishop Chesarie the name Joseph, after the first bishop of Argeş, and the same bishop ordained him deacon.

He studied at the seminary of Buzău and then at the Academy of Saint Sava in Bucharest, learning Greek from the teacher Ioanid and Romanian psaltic chant from the hieromonk Macarie, and he composed chants of his own, for his voice was pleasant. He was made auxiliary bishop of the Metropolis of Wallachia with the title of Myra, then bishop of Argeş in 1873, and two years after that Metropolitan of Moldavia.

He finished the metropolitan cathedral at Iaşi in 1886, the building Veniamin Costachi had begun in 1826 and whose beginning Joseph had happened as a child to see. He moved Veniamin's seminary from Socola into the palace of the prince who had exiled that hierarch, and renewed the churches of Saint Nicholas Domnesc and of the Three Hierarchs. In 1889 he carried the relics of the Venerable Parascheva from the Three Hierarchs to the new cathedral in a new silver reliquary, having seen the old one burn while the relics stayed unharmed.

The two virtues the page sets above the rest are the holiness of his life and his almsgiving. He gave away whatever came to him, even the cassock off his back, and borrowed from his confessor and from others so as to go on giving, so that at each month's end he was in debt rather than leave the poor unpaid; he never gave less than the price of a loaf. While he had the strength he went down himself among the hundred poor at his door, blessing them and putting the money into their hands, and when his strength failed he let it down to them from the balcony.

He shepherded the Metropolis of Moldavia twenty-seven years and departed in peace on 26 January 1902. The people called him Joseph the Holy and the Merciful, and the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church, at its session of 5–6 October 2017, numbered him among the saints and fixed his commemoration on 26 January, the day he passed to eternal things.
