# Menander, companion of Patrick of Prusa

Patrick, bishop of Prusa in Bithynia, did not take up the work of the Gospel alone. saint.gr's account of 19 Μαΐου names three ready fellow-workers who went with him — Acacius, Menander and Polyaenus — and says that with them Patrick brought many idolaters to the Christian faith. That was denounced to the governor Julian the Consular, and the four of them were arrested at once.

The governor tried to argue Patrick out of the faith rather than torture him out of it, setting philosophical questions to prove that Christ is not God. Patrick had the rhetorical skill and the theological grounding for that argument, and overturned the governor's points one after another. Julian, seeing himself broken on the ground he had himself chosen, gave the order, and Patrick was beheaded together with his three fellow-workers.

Of Menander himself saint.gr prints no separate life. What it gives him besides the shared narrative is a couplet of the synaxarion — «Πολύαινος Mένανδρος εκτετμημένοι, Πολλών επαίνων αξιούσθων αξίως», Polyaenus and Menander, cut down, let them be held worthy, as they deserve, of many praises — and the apolytikion that names him with Patrick and the others. The page records no country, no year and no burial place for him.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 19 May, in an entry headed for [Patrick](/saints/patrick-of-prusa), bishop of Brusa, with his three presbyters [Acacius](/saints/acacius-companion-of-patrick), Menander and [Polyaenus](/saints/polyaenus-companion-of-patrick) — whose names stand in the heading and nowhere else in the entry, which speaks of them only as the other three of his presbyters and as these guileless followers of Christ. They suffered for the faith of Christ, it says, in the time of Julian the Apostate, at Brusa in Asia.

When the imperial deputy, whom this book calls Junius, had Patrick thrown into the boiling spring and saw him come out unhurt, he was enraged with shame and ordered that the bishop and his three presbyters have their heads struck off with an axe; and they read a prayer and laid their heads under the executioner’s axe. It is this book, and not the Romanian or the Greek, that calls the three of them presbyters outright. The heading names him second of the three, and in the instrumental, as it names all three.

The Russian calendar names him second of the three presbyters taken with their bishop at Prusa in Bithynia. They were arrested for preaching Christ openly and reproving the errors of the pagans, and brought before the governor Юлий, who was going to the hot springs for a cure and had the four led behind him in iron chains. After bathing and sacrificing he offered them the same and threatened torture; the bishop answered that he worshipped the one true God who had made the warm springs for the good of all men, and was thrown into the spring and came out unharmed. In helpless rage the governor had the bishop and all three presbyters beheaded. The calendar gives him nothing of his own — no birthplace, no age, no year — and sets the company in the first century.
