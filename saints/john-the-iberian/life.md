# John the Iberian

John was the founder of the holy Athonite monastery of the Iberians. He was of the Iberian people, John Barasbatse in the world, from the town of Artanuji. Born of a renowned family, he held the title of kouropalates — the keeper of the palace, from the Latin cura palatii — and was a counsellor to David, the governor of Iberia. After his marriage he left the world and lived apart in various places, ending on Athos in the year 965.

There he became the close friend and cell-disciple of Saint Athanasius of the Great Lavra. Athanasius valued him so highly that in his testament he left him guardian of the Lavra. With Athanasius's blessing he built the holy monastery of the Iberians, to which his kinsman the commander John Tornikios brought many gifts; and the emperor Basil the Second, the conqueror of the Bulgarians, showed it unceasing favour, that it might be for many centuries a place of the worship of God for the Iberian and the Greek nobility.

His memory is kept together with that of his son, the venerable [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-the-iberian), and of [George](/saints/george-the-iberian), whom the page calls his nepot — a word that carries both grandson and nephew — on 13 May. Icons of him are kept in his monastery. doxologia.ro's page gives no year for his death.
