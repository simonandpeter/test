# Thaddeus of Stepantsminda

Thaddeus of Stepantsminda was one of the twelve disciples whom [John of Zedazeni](/saints/john-of-zedazeni) chose out of his Syrian community and brought with him into Georgia, in obedience to a vision in which the Mother of God told him to take twelve worthy men to strengthen the faith in her newly founded Church there. doxologia.ro prints no separate life for him. Its life of their teacher opens by naming all thirteen together — «Sfântul Cuvios Ioan Zedazneli, dimpreună cu 12 ucenici din Georgia» — and calls them Syrian ascetics and the founding fathers of Georgian monastic life.

When the fathers were divided he was sent into Kartli, westward, and the life calls him there «stâlpul credinței» — the pillar of the faith. That epithet and the place he was sent to are the whole of what this page says about him by himself.

Their teacher sent them out to preach as the apostles did, to baptise in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and to lead those in darkness to worship Christ; and he divided among them relics of the saints he had brought, so that they might be worthier on the road and might follow the example of those saints. The life says that they suffered for faith and truth as the apostles and the martyrs did, wandering in deserts and in mountains and in caves and in the clefts of the earth; that most of them held themselves to a complete lack of necessities and would not even use clothes, going out to preach in skins through the heat of the day and the cold of the night; that the faithful came to them for teaching and the sick for healing; and that where the idolatrous altars were destroyed churches were raised in their place.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and tells how it was chosen. saint.gr prints one line and one page for [John](/saints/john-of-zedazeni) and his disciples, and gives each of the thirteen a head line of his own, his reading «Οσιος Θαδδαιος Μαθητης Του Οσιου Ιωαννη». John came out of Syria, it says, and lived about the end of the fifth century; and when the crowds that came to him had broken his stillness and he had hidden deep in the desert, he saw the Mother of God in a dream, who commanded him to choose twelve disciples and go with them into Iberia to establish the Christian faith of its people. All his subordinates wished to go, so he wrote every name on a separate paper and laid them on the holy table; and after a night of prayer, in the sight of them all, an angel of the Lord took twelve papers from the altar and put them into his hands.

Thaddeus is named among the twelve the papers chose. An angel warned Eulogius, bishop of the Georgian capital, of their coming, and the bishop went out with his clergy and his people to receive them; and there followed a wonder like the one at Pentecost, for John spoke the Georgian tongue. The thirteen preached through all Georgia and venerated the blessed places through which Saint Nina had passed, whom this calendar keeps on 14 January. The page gives no year for any of them, no hymn, and no word of where each of the twelve settled afterwards.
