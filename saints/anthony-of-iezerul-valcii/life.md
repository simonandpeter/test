# Anthony of Iezerul Vâlcii

Anthony was a Romanian by birth, and doxologia's life places him in the days of the pious voievod Matei Basarab and of the martyr voievod Saint [Constantine Brâncoveanu](/saints/constantine-brancoveanu). He grew up in the true faith, going to church from childhood, where he found the delight of his soul; and as he grew older he was made a priest, to the glory of God and the spiritual joy of his parents.

Leaving the pleasures of this world he embraced the monastic life in the very beautiful wilderness of the Skete of Iezeru, in the land of Vâlcea. From the first he showed himself diligent in every labour and in the whole hardness of monastic life, so much so that the others of the community thought the blessed Anthony must be living in a spiritual body.

Wishing to add to his labours, he went with his abbot's blessing to the bishop of the place, Hilarion, to ask leave to go to Mount Athos. The bishop, knowing him for a monk of proved virtue who could be useful to many here, pressed him to stay in the country. So Anthony returned to the Skete of Iezeru, and finding the church falling into ruin he was filled with zeal and renewed the holy place with God's help, with the bishop Hilarion's help and out of his own earnings.

After many labours he was kindled with longing for the hermit's life, and with his abbot's blessing he left the skete, searched the wilderness more closely, and found a small cave in a rock. There he dug out a little church with his own hands, and in it prayed without ceasing day and night. The hater of mankind brought him many temptations and troubles, and he overcame them all by the grace of God, by prayer and by unceasing work; and who can tell his vigils that lasted the whole night, his fasts and the bowing of his knees?

Doxologia's life describes him: small of stature and bent with age, his beard thick, short and fairly white, cheerful in his glance, his face comely, a little quick by nature and easily forgiving. His clothing was plain and he carried only what he needed. For the restraining of the body he wore a girdle of iron chains about him; he took food only at the ninth hour, and then only dry bread and water, and those by measure. Wine and strong drink he never tasted. He did not sleep in a bed, but leaned against some stones for rest, and tears were never absent from his eyes at prayer.

By the grace of God he knew that his passing from this world was near, and forty days before it he called his disciple Nicholas and told him in secret: *My end has drawn near. When my soul has gone out, take care to lay my body in the grave which I have dug here in the rock.* After forty days he fell ill, and speaking words of teaching for the soul in peace, he fell asleep in the Lord.
