# Sophia of Kleisoura

Sophia laboured at the monastery of Kleisoura in Greece. She was born in 1883 in the region of Trebizond, in Asia Minor; her parents were called Amanatios and Maria Saoulidis. In 1907 she married Iordanis Hortokoridis, and in 1914 her husband vanished without trace, leaving her alone with a newborn child. Soon afterwards the infant died too, and she began her ascetic life in Pontus, on a mountain far from the place she was born.

Living in prayer and fasting, she was visited there by Saint George, who asked her to warn the people of a nearby village of a coming persecution; and by that warning the villagers were saved. She reached Greece in 1919, as into an exile. As she came into the harbour the Mother of God appeared to her and said: «Vino în casa mea!» — come into my house. Asked where her house was, she answered: «Eu locuiesc în Kleisoura» — I live in Kleisoura.

She went and entered the monastery of the Nativity of the Mother of God at Kleisoura, in the region of Kastoria, and laboured there for years in fasting and prayer. She always went with her head covered in a black kerchief. From the day she left Pontus she never washed, and it was never noticed, her body being sound. She fasted continually, taking a little oil on feasts and Sundays.

People began to love her and to come to her for counsel; often she would call them by name and speak to them of their own troubles, for God had blessed her with spiritual gifts. She was a pattern of simplicity, and every word she spoke was clothed in humility and love. The page gives no year for her death; it says only that she is commemorated on 6 May, and its life of her prints no hymn.
