# Sophia of Kleisoura

Sophia laboured at the monastery of Kleisoura in Greece. She was born in 1883 in the region of Trebizond, in Asia Minor; her parents were called Amanatios and Maria Saoulidis. In 1907 she married Iordanis Hortokoridis, and in 1914 her husband vanished without trace, leaving her alone with a newborn child. Soon afterwards the infant died too, and she began her ascetic life in Pontus, on a mountain far from the place she was born.

Living in prayer and fasting, she was visited there by Saint George, who asked her to warn the people of a nearby village of a coming persecution; and by that warning the villagers were saved. She reached Greece in 1919, as into an exile. As she came into the harbour the Mother of God appeared to her and said: «Vino în casa mea!» — come into my house. Asked where her house was, she answered: «Eu locuiesc în Kleisoura» — I live in Kleisoura.

She went and entered the monastery of the Nativity of the Mother of God at Kleisoura, in the region of Kastoria, and laboured there for years in fasting and prayer. She always went with her head covered in a black kerchief. From the day she left Pontus she never washed, and it was never noticed, her body being sound. She fasted continually, taking a little oil on feasts and Sundays.

People began to love her and to come to her for counsel; often she would call them by name and speak to them of their own troubles, for God had blessed her with spiritual gifts. She was a pattern of simplicity, and every word she spoke was clothed in humility and love. The page gives no year for her death; it says only that she is commemorated on 6 May, and its life of her prints no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, gives the year of her death that the Romanian page withheld, and fills in the life. She was Sophia Hotokouridou, born Amanatiou Saoulidou, born in 1883 in the village of Sari-popa in the district of Ardasa of Tripolis, in the Pontus; she married Iordanis Hotokouridis in 1907, had a child in 1910, lost it two years later to a horrible death, and in 1914 lost her husband, whom the Turks took into the labour battalions and who most likely died there. The young widow fled to the mountains and lived there in strict fasting, and there Saint George appeared to her and warned her of a coming raid of the Chetes, so that she was able to tell her fellow villagers, who hid and escaped.

In the exchange of populations the ship carrying her village to Greece was in danger of going down, and she saw the waves full of angels and the Mother of God among them, and asked her that she herself should drown and her people be saved; and the Mother of God saved them all. In 1927, at the Theotokos's own urging, she came to the monastery of the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos at Kleisoura of Kastoria, where she lived ascetically for about half a century. There she found a virtuous hieromonk, Father Gregory, come from the Holy Mountain, who trained her in the monastic life; and she lived as a laywoman in the black of her widowhood, sitting on the hearth and smearing her face with ash so that its beauty should not show.

Most of those years she passed alone with God, the monastery being left without monks. She bore winters of fifteen degrees below and the damp of the place, and when they told her to light a fire she shouted a long drawn-out no. She went barefoot in rags, gave away the new clothes she was brought, slept on straw laid over sharpened stones, ate mushrooms and moss and wild greens and mouldy pickled tomato, and kept the fasts of both the old calendar and the new so as to scandalise nobody; when people complained of her excesses she said, I am disciplining this poor flesh. She was as gentle with others as she was harsh with herself, dowered poor girls out of the money she was given, and kept a she-bear that came out of the forest to take food from her hands.

She fell gravely ill in 1967 and would have no doctor, saying that the Mother of God would come and take away the pain; and the Mother of God came with the archangel Gabriel and with Saint George, and the archangel said, we shall cut you now. She fell asleep in the Lord on 6 May 1974. On 7 July 1981 her relics were translated the first time and gave out a fragrance, and on 27 May 1998 they were translated a second time and carried into the monastery by Seraphim, Metropolitan of Kastoria.
