# John of Konitsa

John was born at Konitsa in Epirus to Muslim parents. His father was a dervish and held the rank of sheikh, and at twenty John too entered the order of dervishes. After some years at Ioannina he went to Vrachori, now Agrinio, in Aetolia, and lived in a building called the Muselim Serai. Then, suddenly, he began to live as a Christian: he threw off the dervish’s dress, dressed as a Christian, and went to Ithaca, where he was baptized with the name John.

Back in Aetolia he married in the village of Machalas and worked as a field-guard. His father sent men to win him back, and he drove them away. He was then arrested by the muselim of Vrachori, before whom he boldly confessed his Christian name and his love for Christ; he was tortured without mercy, and at last beheaded on 23 September 1814. Christians took up his body and buried it on a farm at Vrachori; the faithful later moved the relics to a crypt of the monastery of Proussos, where they were found on 4 January 1974. The Greek calendar keeps him with two apolytikia and a kontakion; the kontakion is printed without a tone.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps him on 23 September old style: from Konitsa in Albania, born a Muslim of Muslim parents, he was later baptised, having seen the wonderful power of the Christian faith in various places and happenings; for this he was accused and brought before a Turkish court, tortured for the faith of Christ by the Turks in Aetolia and beheaded after his torments in 1814, crying out before his death, “Remember me, O Lord, in thy kingdom.”
