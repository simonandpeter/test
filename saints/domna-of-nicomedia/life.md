# Domna of Nicomedia

Domna was one of the martyrs of Nicomedia whom the Russian calendar keeps on 3 September (Julian) with their bishop [Anthimus](/saints/anthimus-of-nicomedia): the deacon Theophilus, Dorotheus, Mardonius, Migdonius, Peter, Indes, Gorgonius, Zeno, the virgin Domna and Euthymius, who suffered in the persecution of Diocletian and Maximian, after the fire in the imperial palace at Nicomedia for which the pagans blamed the Christians and took an unheard-of vengeance — in Nicomedia alone up to twenty thousand worshippers were burned in a church on the day of the Nativity. The Sretensky calendar’s life of the twenty thousand names them among the company; it was read only as far as the palace fire.

The Greek calendar keeps her on 28 December and gives her the life the Russian line did not. saint.gr makes Domna a priestess of the idols under Maximian at Nicomedia, and specifically in the temple of the Twelve Gods. The letters of the Apostle Paul opened her spiritual eyes, and she was baptised a Christian together with her servant [Indes](/saints/indes-of-nicomedia) by Kyrillos, bishop of Nicomedia. From then on she lived a deliberate Christian life, giving away to the poor what she had of her own property and what she received from the palace besides.

The chief servant of the palace found this out, and when he was about to punish her she feigned madness and was sent to the bishop to be cured. Afterwards, so as not to be arrested, she dressed as a man and went about burying the bodies of the martyrs. When Maximian came back to Nicomedia he asked for Domna, and learning that she had become a Christian he ordered her arrest; and because she was not found, he ordered the general killing of the Christians, among whom she too was recognised, and so they beheaded her. That killing is the one in which [the twenty thousand](/saints/twenty-thousand-martyrs-of-nicomedia) died.

The Охридски пролог keeps her on 28. децембар ст. ст., „Света мученица Домна.“ She was a virgin, it says, and a priestess of the foul idols at the court of the emperor Maximian. Having read the Acts of the holy Apostles, got from somewhere, she believed in Christ and was baptised together with the eunuch [Indes](/saints/indes-of-nicomedia) by the bishop Кирил in Nicomedia. Saint Кирил sent her on to a women’s monastery, where the abbess was the blessed Агатија; and when the emperor began to search for her, Агатија dressed her in man’s clothing and sent her to a men’s monastery.

It was in that time that [twenty thousand Christians](/saints/twenty-thousand-martyrs-of-nicomedia) were burnt in the church by the emperor Maximian. Immediately afterwards, by the emperor’s order, свети Индис, Горгоније and Петар were thrown into the sea with stones about their necks; војвода Зинон, who publicly reproved the emperor for his idolatry, was beheaded; свети Теофил, deacon of the bishop Антим, was killed with stones and arrows; and игуманија Агатија, монахиња Теофила and the great men Доротеј, Мардоније, Мигдоније and Јевтимије were all put to death for Christ. Several of those names are folders of this corpus, kept on this same day by another calendar; they are left in the book’s own Cyrillic here because this heading names Domna alone and the Serbian church’s keeping of them on this day is not something this page attests.

One night Domna was walking along the seashore, and saw fishermen casting their net into the sea; she was grieving at that time for свети Индис above all. Being called by the fishermen to help them she helped, and by God’s providence they drew up in the net three human bodies as well. Domna recognised in them свети Индис, Горгоније and Петар, took their bodies and buried them honourably. When the emperor learned that a certain youth was tending and censing the graves of the Christian martyrs, he ordered that he be beheaded; and holy Domna was seized and beheaded, and crowned with the crown of glory in the heavenly kingdom with the rest of the martyrs. The book gives no year of her own; the 302 it prints belongs to the burning of the twenty thousand.
