# Fevronia of Murom

[Peter](/saints/peter-of-murom) was the second son of Yuri Vladimirovich, prince of Murom, and came to the throne of Murom in 1203. Some years before, he had fallen ill with leprosy, which no one could cure; in a dream it was revealed to him that he could be healed by the devout maiden Fevronia, a beekeeper’s daughter, a peasant of the village of Laskovaya in the Ryazan land. He sent his men to the village, and when he saw her he so loved her for her piety, wisdom and goodness that he vowed to marry her when he was healed; she healed him and married him, and the holy couple carried their love for each other through every trial.

The proud boyars would not have a peasant woman as princess — the Sretensky calendar’s life, read as far as that, tells the rest of the well-known story: exile together, return, the monastic tonsure of both as David and Euphrosyne, and their death on the same day. The Russian calendar keeps on 31 August (Julian) the translation of their relics in 1992; their principal feast is 25 June.

The Romanian calendar keeps her with her husband on 25 June, and prints for her the same life it prints for him, word for word. It adds two dates: that Peter became prince of the land of Murom in 1203, and that the pair were glorified by a council of the Russian Church in 1552. She is named there as the beekeeper's daughter, famous for healing the sick with herbs, very beautiful and humble, who nursed the young prince back to health and whom he then married although she was a girl of no rank; who comforted and gave him heart in the boat when the boyars had driven them out; and who returned with him to a city that had repented. The two were tonsured as David and Euphrosyne at the approach of death, prayed to die on one day, asked to be buried in one coffin with only a partition between them, and died together on 25 June 1228; they are held to be the protectors of the newly married, having suffered for the holiness and the inviolability of marriage.
