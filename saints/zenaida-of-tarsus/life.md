# Zenaida of Tarsus

Zenaida and Philonilla were sisters, from Tarsus in Cilicia, and kinswomen of the Apostle Paul. They left their homeland and renounced their property, and went about as though they practised the physician’s craft, while in truth they were doing the work of apostles. They came to the city of Demetrias, and entering a cave they made their life there.

In that place Zenaida, healing sicknesses and infirmities, departed to the Lord. Her sister [Philonilla](/saints/philonilla-of-tarsus) gave herself to long fasting and to vigils with prayer and worked many wonders, and having served nature with that service she went out of this temporary life.

That is the whole of what doxologia.ro prints. Its calendar for 11 octombrie names her «Sfânta Muceniță Zenaida», and the life on her page is these three sentences, the same text that stands under her sister’s name on the same day. It gives no year, no emperor and no account of a martyrdom, and nothing further is added here. The page prints no troparion for her.

The Greek calendar keeps the two sisters on the same day, 11 October, and says more of what they did. saint.gr gives their origin as Tarsus of Cilicia, and sets aside one piece of the tradition: some synaxaria say they were kinswomen of the Apostle Paul, but their names have nothing Hebrew about them and the claim therefore has no basis. What is certain, it says, is that they were women marked out for their warm faith, very well educated, and skilled in the art of medicine, which they practised in an entirely charitable and brotherly way: they healed free of charge, and they ran to the sick rather than the other way about, and their healing worked always with success, strengthened by divine grace.

Their medical work, the page goes on, helped them wonderfully to work for the faith as well; beside every sick person and his family they became teachers of the Gospel, comforting and softening hearts wounded by the misfortunes of life with the words and the ways of Christian love and hope, and they had the happiness of enlightening a good many unbelievers into the Christian life. At length they came to the city of Demetrias and stayed in a cave, where Zenaida healed of every illness those who ran to her for help, while Philonilla kept long fasts and worked many miraculous cures and other wonders. Both of them, the page says, fell asleep in peace in that cave, and its distich says the same: «Εἰρηνικῶς ὕπνωσαν εἰρήνης φίλαι, / Ἡ Φιλονίλλα Ζηναΐς τε αἱ δύο».

The Russian calendar keeps the two of them on its own 11 October, «Мцц. Зинаиды и Филониллы (I)», and its life is four sentences that add two things to this folder. The first is kinship: the martyrs Zenaida and [Philonilla](/saints/philonilla-of-tarsus) were kinswomen of the holy apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle), and were of Tarsus — which is the city the folder already carried in its name and is also Paul's own.

The second is a trade. They left their house and settled in a cave near the town of Demetrias and lived there in unceasing prayer and labour; and they were masters of the art of medicine, and willingly treated everyone who came to them for help, healing their souls as well by turning them to the True God. For that holy zeal for the Lord the two preachers received the martyr's crown: the idol-worshippers stoned them to death.
