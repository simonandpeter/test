# Elizabeth of Adrianople

doxologia.ro gives Elizabeth no life of her own. The page under her name prints the same four sentences that stand under each of the four women's names, and they are the synaxarion notice of the bishop who converted them.

Alexander, it says, was a bishop, and teaching and converting and baptising many he was seized by the governor; tormented much and pressed to sacrifice to the idols, he would not submit. Heraclius the soldier, seeing his endurance, believed in Christ; he too was tormented first at length, and then they cut off his head.

Alexander went on working wonders and was healed of his wounds, and by the help and grace of God he drew to the faith of Christ four women — Theodota, Glyceria, Anna and Elizabeth — who stood before the governor and rebuked the deceit of the idols, and were beheaded. Then Alexander himself was ended by the sword.

That is everything doxologia prints. It names no place and no year; the calendar's line for Glyceria alone adds Adrianople. The other three women are [Theodota](/saints/theodota-of-adrianople), [Glyceria](/saints/glyceria-of-adrianople), [Anna](/saints/anna-of-adrianople). There is no troparion on any of the four pages.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day, 22 October, and saint.gr tells the story a little differently. Alexander was a bishop who preached the Gospel with great zeal to crowds of idolaters, it says, and drew a multitude of them to the faith; the ruler of the place had him seized and tortured to make him sacrifice to the idols, and he was not persuaded. A soldier named [Heraclius](/saints/heraclius-companion-of-alexander), seeing that endurance, believed in Christ, and was tortured and beheaded. [Alexander](/saints/alexander-22-october) was then healed by the grace of God of the wounds of his torture and worked a wonder besides, and by it he drew four women to the faith of Christ — Elizabeth herself and [Theodote](/saints/theodota-of-adrianople), [Glyceria](/saints/glyceria-of-adrianople) and [Anna](/saints/anna-of-adrianople). They confessed their faith before the ruler and were led for it under the executioner's sword, and after all of them Alexander himself was beheaded. The Greek page names no city for any of them.

The Russian calendar keeps the company on the same day and tells it in the same order. days.pravoslavie.ru's 22 октября ст. ст. reads «Мчч. Александра еп., Ираклия воина и жен Анны , Елисаветы , Феодотии и Гликерии (II-III).», and its life puts their death at Adrianople, in a century it calls the time when Christianity was spreading among the pagans.

The bishop, it says, baptised many pagans fearlessly in spite of the persecution raised against the Christians; the governor of the province ordered his soldiers to torture him into denying Christ, and he bore the dreadful torments patiently. The soldier Heraclius, struck by what he saw, believed in the Christ for whom the bishop was suffering, and after him Anna, Elizabeth, Theodota and Glyceria declared themselves Christians. The page's heading puts the six in the third century while the day's line reads «II-III», so the folder's date now carries both readings.
