# Theophanes the Confessor (9 September)

Theophanes was born in 283, under the emperors Carus and Carinus, to
fanatical pagan parents; but their son heard the preaching of Christians
and was drawn to the life of truth and love. One day, still young and
unbaptised, he met in freezing weather a child so poor that it was in
danger of dying of cold; the sight tore his heart, and without losing time
he dressed the poor child in his own coat. When he came home his parents
were alarmed, and asked what had become of his garment; he answered that
he had given it to Christ. The fanatical pagan parents soon understood that
their son was drawn to Christianity and forced him to leave the house; then
Theophanes was baptised and went through various cities preaching, and
afterwards withdrew to Mount Diabenos to an old ascetic, from whose
experience and wisdom he learned much. When the elder died he stayed alone
on the mountain in prayer and unceasing study and striving for higher moral
and spiritual perfection; then he came down again into the world and took
up his preaching once more, wiser now, more spiritual and more edifying. In
that time he was arrested, threatened and tortured, but stayed unmoved in
his confession, and he died at seventy-five in his hermitage. Appearing
like a very great star of God, the verse says, you dissolve the night of
error, Theophanes.
