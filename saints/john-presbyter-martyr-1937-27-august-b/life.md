# John (Lebedev)

John was born on 9 October 1869 in the village of Vorontsovo in the
Yegoryevsk district of Ryazan province, the son of the psalmist Theodore
Lebedev. After the Ryazan seminary in 1892 he was made catechist of the
Pazhino parish school; in 1896 he married Nadezhda, daughter of the priest
Martin Sampsonovich Lipyagov, former rector of the church of the Nativity
of Christ in the village of Popovichi, later Pronyukhlovo, in the Zaraysk
district, and soon Bishop Polyeuctus of Mikhailov ordained him priest to
that church. The church had stood in the village since the sixteenth
century; by the beginning of the nineteenth the building was much
decayed, and by tradition the peasants miraculously found a cross, which
was taken as a command to renew the church — in 1839 a large stone church
of the Nativity of Christ with a chapel of St Nicholas was consecrated,
the wonderworking cross was kept on the holy table, and the peasants
took up the pious custom of molebens to the life-giving Cross. At
Pronyukhlovo he taught the Law of God at the parish school and at the
Sekirino and then Mendyukino zemstvo schools; in 1910 the Zaraysk clergy
elected him to the board of the Zaraysk church school, and for long and
zealous service he was made archpriest. With the Bolsheviks came the
persecution of the Church, and foreseeing the heavy trials ahead for
believers he often told his spiritual children: only do not deny him. The
godless power laid a heavy tax on him; the community had no money, and to
keep the church from being closed he went round the village himself
collecting the faithful’s offerings for the tax — for which, on 31
December 1929, the assistant procurator of the Kolomna district proposed a
criminal case “for levying collections on citizens”; the witnesses called
confirmed the collection, and false witnesses were found to accuse him of
anti-Soviet agitation, one of them the priest Seleznev, who counted among
the reactionary-monarchist clergy “my colleague Lebedev, arrested in my
case, a man of strong farm, close to Bishop Boris of Ryazan, who fought
long and stubbornly against the Soviet power, of whom Lebedev was a
follower”. Arrested with other priests of the Zaraysk district and put in
the prison at Kolomna, he said at his interrogation on 4 January 1930
that with the dean, the archpriest Peter Uspensky, he was on friendly
terms as a neighbour and visited him now and then; that he had sent him
no reports on his parish since the revolution; and that the circulars of
the late Bishop Boris (Sokolov) he had not received from Uspensky but
only read at his house, and in his view there was nothing anti-Soviet in
them. On 3 February 1930 the OGPU troika sentenced him to three years’
exile in the Northern Region. Back from exile he served again at
Pronyukhlovo, and from 30 November 1933 also at the church of the
Nativity of the Mother of God at Radushino, where his friend Peter
Uspensky had served before him, so that he cared for two parishes. On the
eve of Christmas 1936 he asked the chairman of the village soviet for
leave to serve molebens in the collective farmers’ houses at the feast, as
the faithful had asked; the chairman refused, citing cases of epidemic
in the district, and in August 1937 told the NKVD that the priest had
answered: Soviet laws are made to darken the people’s minds; the law
speaks of freedom of religion, and in fact there is none, and under every
kind of pretext the Orthodox are denied the right to their rites; the
people demand that I go round the Orthodox houses with molebens, and I
must warn you that you are setting yourself against the people — I shall
tell them all how you oppress us; I have gone with molebens and I shall go
on going; against the people you are powerless, the people follow us and
will follow us, you hold on only by violence, and that will surely end;
you will pay for your outrages — and that going round the houses he had
said, as a pastor I do my duty to you honestly whatever the opposition and
the penalties of the power; we are sure this antichrist power will end,
the people’s patience will end too, and they will pay for everything and
for us martyrs. At the height of the persecution he was arrested and on
20 August 1937 put in a prison of the Kolomna district: the inquiry knows
that visiting citizens you carried on counter-revolutionary agitation —
visiting citizens I said among the collective farmers that work on the
farm is hard, since they work without a break; the inquiry knows that you
called the people to organise and rise against the Soviet power — no,
that I deny, I did not say it. Moved to a Moscow prison, he was
questioned again on 29 August: when had he come back from exile? — in
1933, having served the full term; did he admit spreading anti-Soviet
agitation among the peasants? — no; why had he gone to the chairman in
December 1936? — for leave to go round the peasants’ houses with molebens
at Christmas; what had he said when refused? — he refused me, and I
answered him nothing. On 8 September 1937 the NKVD troika sentenced him to
be shot, and on 9 September 1937 — 27 August of the old style, the day the
Russian calendar keeps him — he was shot at the Butovo range outside
Moscow and buried in an unknown common grave. After his arrest the church
was closed, and when that became known the people took home what icons
they could; the parishioner Pelagia Kostyukhina kept carefully at home the
church’s icon of the Mother of God “Seeker of the Lost” and the
wonderworking altar cross, and in the 1960s gave them to the Annunciation
church at Zaraysk. The life was compiled by the priest Maxim Maximov; the
Russian calendar keeps him also in the synaxis of the saints of Moscow
and of the new martyrs of Butovo.
