# Crescens, companion of Quadratus of Corinth

Crescens was one of the six friends whom saint.gr keeps together on 10 Μαρτίου and names in its line, «Άγιοι Κοδράτος, Ανεκτός, Παύλος, Διονύσιος, Κυπριανός και Κρήσκης». The page puts their martyrdom at Corinth in the persecution of the emperors Decius or Valerian, when the governor of Greece was Jason, and leaves the choice between the two reigns open. It gives no year.

Their guide was [Quadratus](/saints/quadratus-of-corinth), whom the page has born in the mountains above Corinth while his mother hid from the persecution, orphaned as a babe and fed by the clouds that gathered over him; when he was grown he taught the Christian faith to those who had taken refuge with him — Crescens among them, with [Cyprian](/saints/cyprian-companion-of-quadratus) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-quadratus) and the rest.

The page says that they were arrested because they were Christians, that the governor Jason tried by various means to entice them and to persuade them to renounce their faith and sacrifice to the idols, and that they confessed Christ with spiritual manhood, were tortured, and were at last beheaded; and so, it says, the holy martyrs entered into the joy of the Kingdom of God. The page's own line writes his name Κρήσκης and its body once Κρήσκος, and the corpus's other two of the name — Crescens of Myra and the apostle Crescens — are other men on other days.

Crescens has a distich of his own with Cyprian: «Ὁρῶν καταθνῄσκοντα Κρήσκεντα ξίφει, / Σπεύδει σὺν αὐτῷ Κυπριανὸς τεθνάναι» — seeing Crescens dying by the sword, Cyprian makes haste to die with him. The page prints an apolytikion that names all six of them and a kontakion that calls them a lamp of six lights at Corinth, and it gives the six of them one commemoration.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day with his companions, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović names the five of them in one clause. When a new persecution arose under Decius, it says, [Quadratus](/saints/quadratus-of-corinth) was brought to trial and thrown into prison, and five of his companions joined themselves to him there and confessed the name of Christ; and it then prints the five names.

All of them were dragged through the streets and beaten with staves and with stones by the heathen, and by their children above all, until they were dragged to the place of execution. There the martyrs prayed to God and were beheaded with the sword. In that place a spring of water burst out of the earth, which to this day carries Quadratus's name and is a reminder of the heroic death for Christ of the six of them. They suffered honourably for the truth, the entry ends, in the year 250 at Corinth, in the time of the emperor Decius and of his governor Jason.
