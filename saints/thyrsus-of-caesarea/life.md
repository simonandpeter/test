# Thyrsus of Caesarea

Thyrsus, Leucius and Callinicus suffered for Christ under the emperor Decius (249–251) at Caesarea in Bithynia. [Leucius](/saints/leucius-of-caesarea), who had reproached the governor Cumbricius for his unjust persecution of the Christians, was beheaded after torture; Thyrsus, condemned to the cruellest tortures and torments, came through them unharmed and, by God’s will, died in peace; the pagan priest Callinicus, seeing the courage and the miracles of Thyrsus, believed in Christ, confessed the true faith boldly and was beheaded for it. The Russian calendar keeps Thyrsus, Leucius and [Coronatus](/saints/cornutus-of-iconium) “and their company” on 17 August (Julian).

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 17 August as «Sfântul Mucenic Tirs». Doxologia.ro's page for him carries no life and no troparion: the calendar names him and gives nothing more.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 14 decembrie, where doxologia.ro prints at length the life the Russian entry gives in three lines. In it Thyrsus is not yet baptised when he comes before the governor Cumbricius: his limbs are pulled from their joints, his brows and eyes pierced and his jaws and teeth broken, and when molten lead is poured on his bare back it runs off him like a river and harms the unbelievers standing round, while he gets up off the iron bed sound in body. In prison he prays to be counted worthy of Baptism, and that night his bonds fall away and the doors open of themselves; he goes to the bishop of Caesarea, who was in hiding, is baptised, and returns to the prison.

A Persian count named Silvanus takes over the case, and at his invitation Thyrsus prays in the temple of Apollo and the idol falls to dust; a boiling cauldron cracks and spills without touching him; and Silvanus and Cumbricius both die miserably within four days, as he had told them they would. A third governor, Babdus, has him sacked and thrown into the sea thirty stadia from shore, and the sack tears and men shining with light walk out on the water and carry him to land; beasts starved for thirty days fawn on him and are sent back to the desert at his word. Sentenced at last to be laid in a wooden coffin and sawn through, he was not cut, for the saw lost its edge and the wood its weight; the coffin opened of itself, he heard a voice from above calling him, gave thanks, signed himself with the Cross and gave up his soul in peace. On the same sentence the idol-priest [Callinicus](/saints/callinicus-of-caesarea), converted by the wonders done through him, was beheaded.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them on the same menologion day, with their company, and adds two things and no narrative. The first is a doubt of its own: perhaps, saint.gr says, they are confused with those of 14 December, and the name Callinicus of the third martyr became Coronatus. Nothing here is merged on the strength of that; it is set down as the page's own hesitation. The second is a place: of these three, it says, it is recorded that «τελείται αυτών η σύναξις πλησίον των Ελενιανών», their synaxis is held near the Helenianai, in Constantinople. He is named on the page beside [Leucius](/saints/leucius-of-caesarea), [Coronatus](/saints/cornutus-of-iconium).

The distich the page prints is for the three together: «Οἱ τρεῖς ἀθληταὶ νῦν παρίστανται πόθῳ, / Τριάδι θείᾳ, ἧς ἐνήθλησαν χάριν» — the three contenders now stand with longing before the divine Trinity, for whose sake they contended. saint.gr gives no year, no emperor and no hymn.
