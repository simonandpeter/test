# Alexander of Cartagena

doxologia.ro's calendar names him for 25 februarie, and the life it links under his name is five lines long, which is all it has of him. He lived, it says, in the time of the emperor Maximian and of the governor Tiberian, of the city of Cartagena. Being pressed to sacrifice to the idols he refused to do it, and reviled the governor with many hard words.

For that he was tormented at length, and then taken to Carthage and tormented again. Coming at last into Thrace, he was beheaded, and so received the crown of martyrdom. The page gives no year, names no companions, and prints no hymn; and its one sentence is loose enough that Cartagena may be the governor's city as easily as the martyr's.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and settles the Thracian end of the story, which doxologia leaves loose. saint.gr's line calls him «ο εν Δριζιπάρω της Θράκης», the one at Drizipara in Thrace, and its page says that he lived at the beginning of the fourth century and came from Cartagena; that in the persecution Diocletian set going he was seized and tortured at Marcianopolis, an ancient city of Thrace; and that he was beheaded afterwards at Drizipara, also a city of Thrace. So the two calendars agree that he was born in one place and died in another, and differ on the emperor: doxologia names Maximian and the governor Tiberian, saint.gr the persecution of Diocletian. Its couplet has the disc of the sun look on at Alexander cut down for Christ, the sun of glory, and it prints no hymn.

The Ohrid Prologue, read for the Julian 13 May, keeps an Alexander whom this folder is read as holding — on the name, on the governor Tiberian, on the emperor Maximian and on a beheading in Thrace, the river it names being the one that runs past the Drizipara the Greek line names him for. The identification is a reading, and three things in the entry do not agree with what this folder already says. It calls him a Slav, where saint.gr brings him from Cartagena. It leads him through Macedonia towards Constantinople, where saint.gr tortures him at Marcianopolis. And it keeps him on the thirteenth of May, where the Romanian and the Greek keep him on the twenty-fifth of February. Nothing is chosen; both accounts stand.

What the book says is this. He was a Slav, an eighteen-year-old soldier in the army of the emperor Maximian. He refused, against the emperor's order, to pay honour to the Roman idols, and for that was handed over to the captain Tiberian, either to talk him out of Christ or else to torture and kill him. All the counsels being in vain, Tiberian took him along with him and led him through Macedonia towards Constantinople, where he had himself to go on duty. In every place the young Alexander was cruelly tortured; and in every place Christians came out before him, begging his blessing and heartening him in his struggle. His mother Pimenia followed after him.

In the course of that journey an angel of God appeared to Alexander more than once, easing his torments and giving him courage. At one place, called Carassura, the martyr worked a wonder by prayer: when thirst distressed him and the soldiers escorting him, he brought out a spring of cold water in a dry place. On the bank of the river Ergine, Tiberian ordered the executioner to behead him and throw the body into the water. But when the executioner swung at the martyr's head he saw bright angels of God all round him, and was afraid, and his arm went slack. Alexander asked him why his arm had failed, and he said he saw bright young men about him; so Alexander, longing for death and for union with the Lord, prayed God that the angels withdraw, that the executioner should not be afraid.

And so the executioner finished his work, the book says, in the year 298 — a year not taken into this folder's dates, both because the identification above is a reading and because it falls outside the early fourth century the Greek page gives him. Pimenia took up her son's body and buried it honourably. There were many healings at the martyr's grave; and after his death he appeared to his mother and told her of her own near passing into the other world.
