# Dometie the Merciful of Râmeț

Dometie was born Stelian Manolache in the village of Mărculești, in the commune of Bălănești in Buzău county, on 15 October 1924, the fourth of the twelve children of Ioan and [Filofteia](/saints/filotimia-of-ramet). The whole family leaned towards the spiritual life: his elder brother Gheorghe became a church singer, his younger brother Mihai a priest at Sibiu, his sister Eugenia a nun at Râmeț under the name Eudoxia, and after their father's death his mother entered the same monastery and was given the name Filotimia.

He finished the seven classes of the school at Târlele Filiului in 1937, went on to the theological seminary at Buzău, which he left at the head of his year, and read theology from 1945 to 1949, taking his licence with the mark Exceptional under Father Dumitru Stăniloae, on Peter Movilă's Orthodox Confession. On 6 July 1949 — the same day and month, doxologia notes, on which he would die — he entered the monastery of Prislop.

He was ordained priest that August at Hodoș-Bodrog and tonsured a monk at Prislop on 14 September, alongside his fellow student the future Metropolitan Antonie Plămădeală. For ten years he was abbot and missionary priest in villages and monasteries of Transylvania — Prislop and Afteea-Hațeg, Piatra Fântânelor, Dragomirești and Ciolanu — and in 1959 he settled at Râmeț as confessor, serving the shepherd villages of the Apuseni mountains.

There he restored the monastery church, built workshops, a refectory and a guest house, brought in electricity and drinking water, founded the monastery museum with his sister in 1969, and raised a school for the children of the village, all of it beside an unresting pastoral round. He died in the summer of 1975, after twenty-six years of monastic life and shortly after finishing his doctorate at Bucharest.

Doxologia says the people of those parts called him the apostle of the moți, and quotes Father Stăniloae on him: a hero of the faith, a soul of great holiness and purity.
