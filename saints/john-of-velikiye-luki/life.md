# John

John — Ivan Efimovich Troyansky in the world — was born on 10 November 1862
in the village of Novo-Klinskoye in Voronezh province, a priest’s son. He
finished the Tambov seminary and was ordained on 10 September 1884. After
his wife’s death he was consecrated bishop in 1922 by Renovationist bishops;
in November 1924 he broke with the Renovationists, who suspended him, and
early in 1925 he made his repentance and, as one consecrated by bishops of
the old ordination, was received in his rank and made bishop of Akmolinsk
and Petropavlovsk, vicar of Omsk. On 9 March 1932 he was appointed to
Rybinsk, but the authorities had not forgiven his leaving the
Renovationists, and soon after he was arrested and sentenced to three years’
exile. Coming back in 1935 he was made bishop of Velikiye Luki and Toropets
and settled at Toropets in the Tver region, where, having got with great
difficulty the registration and the permission to serve in the town’s
churches, he served as often as he could and preached at every service. The
ill-disposed reported to the NKVD that he preached the certain existence of
God, the great weight of holiness and the saints in our lives, and the
wonder-working icons — the miracles worked by the prayers of the saints and
before a wonder-working image were facts, he said — and worse, that he lured
children into church with treats, handing out sweets and gingerbread in the
middle of the Ascension church. He was arrested on 23 July 1937 and put in
the Toropets prison. Asked whether he performed rites at home, he said yes,
he had once allowed a panikhida for a dead man in his house, and he held the
evening and morning services in his flat regularly; asked his view of
Renovationism, that by the canons it was a heresy, an old one, fighting for
novelties in the life of the clergy, and that the Orthodox church of course
fought it; asked his influence on it in the Velikiye Luki diocese, that he
had shown none visibly, but that four churches had lately come over from
the Renovationists to the Tikhonite side; asked about the sweets, yes, he
admitted it — out of pity for the children: as every Orthodox gives a beggar
a kopeck, so children, he thought, should be shown attention and care; and
yes, he admitted his guilt in having preached a religious sermon outside the
walls of a church. Witnesses ready to lie against him were called: that in
the Ascension church in July he had said the apostle Paul suffered for the
Christian faith and feared neither torture nor death, while we betray the
faith for fear of repression; that he fought Renovationism systematically,
so that thirteen priests had gone over to the Tikhonites; that at a
parishioner’s house in May he had criticised the fashions of the USSR and
said they would lead the young to degeneracy. The chairman of the Toropets
town soviet certified for the NKVD that the bishop had taken an active part
in petitioning for the reopening of the town’s closed churches. On
31 August the NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on
4 September 1937 — 22 August of the old style, the day the Russian calendar
keeps him, with Alexius, archbishop of Omsk, the presbyters Alexander,
Michael and Theodore and the monk-martyrs Hilarion, John and Hierotheus,
all of that year. The Jubilee Council of Bishops of August 2000 numbered him
among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia.
