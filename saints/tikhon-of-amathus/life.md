# Tikhon of Amathus

Tikhon came of the city of Amathus in the island of Cyprus, of believing and Christ-loving parents, from whom he learned from his youth to know Christ God and the holy Scriptures. He was numbered in the clergy of the church and appointed to read the divine words to the people; then, for the purity of his life, he was ordained deacon by Saint Memnon, bishop of that same city. When Memnon departed to the Lord, Tikhon was raised by election to the episcopal throne and consecrated bishop by the great Epiphanius, archbishop of Cyprus.

There were still at that time many unbelieving people in Cyprus who held to the error of idols, and Tikhon laboured hard to turn them from it, bringing to the Church those who were perishing in the Greek paganism, and making sheep of Christ out of goats. He broke all the idols that stood about Amathus, pulled down their dwellings and destroyed their temples. He lived many years, governing well the Church entrusted to him, and departed to the Lord, and is called a wonderworker, for he did many wonders both in his life and after his death.

His whole life and the history of his miracles could not be recovered, doxologia says, through the age of the thing and the wars; but the page keeps two. The first belongs to his youth, when his father, a baker, set him to sell bread, and he gave it to the poor for nothing. When his father reproached him, he said that he had lent the bread to God, who has written that whoever gives to him receives a hundredfold; and taking his father to the granary, they found the empty storeroom full of clean wheat, and his father wondered and worshipped and never again kept him from giving bread away.

The second is the vine. Labourers in a vineyard were cutting away the dry shoots and throwing them out, and Tikhon gathered them and planted them in his own vineyard, asking God for four things: that the dry wood should take root and grow, that the vineyard should be plentiful, that the grapes should be sweet and wholesome, and that they should ripen before all other vineyards. The next day he found the cuttings green and sprouting; they grew that year beyond nature and gave a great crop, ripe while the other vineyards were still green, and sweet and good for the health of men. That vineyard was worked for many years after his death, and every year, before the usual season of ripening, on the sixteenth day of June, his memorial, ripe grapes from it were brought to his church, and the bloodless sacrifice was offered at the Liturgy with the wine pressed from them. His service adds that he foresaw the time of his end and told it to others.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 16 June, and saint.gr gives no year but sets him in the reigns of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius. It says he came of a devout family, and that his virtuous parents brought him up in the instruction and admonition of the Lord, so that he was early marked out for the quality of his character, for good sense and discernment, and for his study of the divine Scriptures. While he was still young the bishop [Mnemonius](/saints/mnemonius-of-amathus), whom the same day's listing honours, valued his rare virtues and ordained him deacon; and his virtue and his great catechetical work soon made him Mnemonius's successor in the see.

As bishop, the page says, he was marked for his gifts of administration, his charitable work and his spreading of the word of God, which bore rich fruit and turned many idolaters; he pulled down many pagan temples and raised Christian ones in their place. He was honoured with the gift of wonderworking, performing many miracles, healings and benefactions, and by his warm prayer he even brought the dry and barren land of the district back to life to help the poor and struggling farmers who had come to a dead end. He lived by prayer, ascesis and fasting and above all humility, contended for Orthodoxy and the truth to his last breath, and reposed in peace and was buried amid general emotion. A portion of his relic, the page adds, is at the monastery of Kykkos in Cyprus.
