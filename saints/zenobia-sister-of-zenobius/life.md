# Zenobia, sister of Zenobius

Zenobia was born in the city of Aegae in Cilicia, sister of the bishop and martyr [Zenobius](/saints/zenobius-of-aegae), of right-believing parents who raised them both in good teaching and the fear of God. Their parents died while they were young and left them much property, and the two, young in years but old in understanding, saw the vanity of this world and agreed to leave everything and follow Christ. Zenobia gave her share of the inheritance to her brother to be divided among the poor.

She lived willingly in poverty and stillness, keeping her virginity undefiled for the heavenly Bridegroom; and the life says that all her life she had the hand of God for her helper, which defended her from every oppression of the body and of the world and from the warfare of the demons that troubled her. Her brother meanwhile gave away both portions, became poor himself, received the gift of healing, and for his good works was chosen bishop of that city.

When the great persecution came on under the emperor Diocletian, the governor Lysias came into Cilicia to torture all who confessed Christ, and had Zenobius seized, stripped, hung on a beam and beaten without mercy, saying that they would see whether his Christ would come to help him. Zenobia heard that her brother was suffering for Christ, rose at once and ran to the place where he was being tortured; and seeing him hung there and wounded she took fire with zeal and came forward to the torturer.

She was a Christian, she said, and confessed the same one God and Lord Jesus Christ as her brother; let him order her tortured as he had ordered her beloved brother, for she wished to drink the same cup of sufferings and to be crowned with the same crown. Lysias marvelled at the courage and the boldness of it and tried to warn her off: she should not wish to destroy herself or come to such dishonour, for she would be stripped and filled with shame, and when they began on her body heavy pains would take hold of her; let her sacrifice and be delivered from all these evils.

She answered that the nakedness of the soul brings a greater shame than the body's, and that everlasting torments are more painful than temporal ones; she took no thought for bodily nakedness, since she had put on Christ, and she did not fear the hands of torturers, since she was crucified with Christ. "Do what you will, torturer; you will not turn me from Christ my Lord." He had her stripped at once and beaten like her brother.

Then they made a bed red-hot in the fire, stretched the two of them on it and set fire underneath, telling them to let Christ come and help them; and they answered that their Christ was with them though he could not see him, bedewing them with the dew of his grace from heaven, so that they took no thought of the torments. After that they were thrown into a boiling cauldron and remained unharmed, standing as in warm water and singing the psalm of David: Thou hast saved us from them that afflict us, and hast put to shame them that hate us.

At the last the torturer ordered them taken outside the city and beheaded. They went to death with unspeakable joy, and at the place they prayed together, thanking God who had counted them worthy to fight the good fight, to finish the course and to keep the faith, and asking to be made sharers of his glory and numbered with those who had pleased his holy name. As the prayer ended a voice was heard out of heaven promising them crowns and calling them into everlasting rest, and they were beheaded. Their bodies lay unburied until the priest Hermogenes took them away secretly by night and laid them in a tomb.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day as her brother, 30 October, and tells her part of the story plainly. The two of them lived and suffered in the time of Diocletian, saint.gr says; they came from Aegae in Cilicia, of a rich and devout family, and her brother was a physician who took no fee. The works of charity they did together raised the anger of the idolaters, and particularly of the governor Lysias, who ordered the brother arrested; and while he was in prison Zenobia came before the authorities of her own accord, with the wish to be martyred along with him. Both were beheaded after torments in the year 285. The epigram the page sets over them says that the sword holds out against the brother and that Zenobia, woman though she is, is the stout-minded one. A fragment of her relics is at the monastery of Kato Xenia at Almyros in Magnesia.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps her with her brother on the Julian 30 October and gives the two of them a life. They took the holy Christian faith from their parents as children and lived godly and chaste lives, and in their grown years gave away the property they had inherited to those who had none. Her brother was made bishop of the Christian community in Cilicia, and when Diocletian's persecution began he was the first seized and brought before the governor Lysias, who offered him life for worship and death for refusal.

What the Russian life adds about her is that she was not arrested with him. When the hierarch had been nailed to a cross and the torture had begun, his sister, seeing her brother's sufferings, wished to share them with him; she confessed her faith in Christ before the persecutors without fear, and for that she too was given over to the torments. Left alive by the power of the Lord after a red-hot bed and a boiling cauldron, the two were beheaded together, and a presbyter named Hermogenes buried them in secret. The calendar prints their martyrdom as one event of the year 285.
