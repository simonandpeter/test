# John the Baptist and Forerunner

The Greek and Romanian calendars keep 29 August for the Beheading of the Forerunner, and the synaxarion’s entry tells that day.

“It is not lawful for you to have your brother’s wife.” The Forerunner’s words to Herod Antipas and to Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip who was still living, were knife-thrusts to their corrupt consciences. Herod, unable to bear the rebuke, imprisoned him; at his birthday feast he swore to give Herodias’s daughter whatever she asked, because her dancing pleased him, and the bloodthirsty Herodias told her to ask for John’s head on a platter — which was done. So, the synaxarion says, the glorious Forerunner of the Saviour remains through the ages the model for all who would serve the truth and fight corruption regardless of danger and cost.

The Russian and Serbian calendars keep the Beheading the same day, 29 August (Julian), a strict fast — and the Russian calendar, after the liturgy, a moleben for those who suffer from drunkenness, with the troparion that says the memory of the righteous is kept with praises, but for you the Lord’s own witness suffices, Forerunner. The Prologue tells it: Herod Antipas, son of the old Herod who killed the infants of Bethlehem, lord of Galilee in the days of John’s preaching, was married to the daughter of the Arab prince Aretas, but, evil shoot of an evil root, drove out his lawful wife and took unlawfully Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, who was still alive; John rose against this lawlessness and rebuked him mightily, and Herod threw him into prison. At a feast in his palace at Sebaste in Galilee Salome, daughter of Herodias and Philip, danced before the guests, and the drunken Herod, carried away, promised the dancer whatever she asked, even half the kingdom; prompted by her mother she asked the head of John the Baptist, and Herod had John beheaded in the prison and his head brought on a dish. John’s disciples took their teacher’s body by night and buried it honourably; Herodias pierced John’s tongue with a needle and buried the head in an unclean place — what became of it afterwards stands under 24 February. God’s punishment soon overtook the band of evildoers: Aretas, to wash the honour of his daughter, fell on Herod with an army and routed him, and the defeated Herod was condemned by the Roman Caesar Caligula to exile, first in Gaul and then in Spain, where he and Herodias lived in misery and humiliation until the earth opened and swallowed them, and Salome died an evil death in the river Sicoris. John’s death happened before the Passover, and the feast of 29 August was fixed because on that day was consecrated the church which the emperor Constantine and the empress Helen raised over his tomb at Sebaste, where the relics of his disciples Elisha and Obadiah were also laid.
