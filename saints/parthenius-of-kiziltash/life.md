# Parthenius

Parthenius was born in 1815 at Elisavetgrad. He was tonsured on 23 December
1845 and ordained hieromonk on 8 April 1846; from July 1848 he was dean of
the Korsun monastery at Kherson and later steward of the Kherson bishop’s
house, and in 1852, at the Tenginsk fortification of the Black Sea coastal
guard, he showed an engineer’s talent, proposing the easiest way of raising
sunken cargo — a special commission approved the invention, such machines
were ordered for the ports, and the rear-admiral of the Black Sea fleet
thanked him for it. In the Crimean War, when enemy ships bombarded
Novorossiysk from 28 February to 4 March 1855, he confessed and communicated
the wounded and buried the dead without leaving them, under mortal danger;
for his courage and his inventions the Synod gave him the pectoral cross on
20 March 1857, and on 7 April he was made igumen, with a bronze cross for
the war and the medal and cross of those who served in the army of the
Caucasus.

On 20 August 1858 he was made superior of the Kiziltash coenobium in the
Crimea, where in the eighth century Stephen, the confessor-archbishop of
Sourozh, had had his summer residence, and which had fallen into utter
neglect. With his energy and his zeal for God’s glory he set about
rebuilding the ancient house from nothing — absolute ruin and poverty, no
help or even sympathy from outside, brethren who could barely read — and
did what no one in the monastery’s history had done before or after, so
that the whole district came to value, respect and love him and seek his
advice. Brave and cheerful, in constant prayer, he mastered the wretched
conditions rather than being crushed by them: where a mediocre or
weak-willed man perishes, the igumen conquered and built. An ascetic who
led by his own example, he lifted everyone round him to his own level and
would not accept baseness of any kind; disinterested and fatherly, he was
loved by all, and the smallest event in his people’s lives found an echo in
his heart. All things to all men, in the apostle’s phrase, he worked from
dawn to dark with prayer on his lips and his handful of monks after him;
possessing nothing, he forbade the brethren to beg, and there was nothing
to hire workmen with. Before him Kiziltash was a cave with a healing spring
and two or three wattle huts; by his labour roads were laid, gardens and
vineyards planted, and out of the little cave in the rock grew a whole
skete with two guesthouses and a stone church, and the pilgrims poured in.

The sorrows began in 1863 with the first, and fatal, clashes with the local
Tatars, who felled the monastery’s timber and grazed their herds on its
land without shame. He could reach no understanding with them at any level,
and being straight and whole in character he spoke and acted from a
Christian conscience alone, without the cover of what everyone calls common
sense; unable to break the mutual protection and the bureaucratic formalism
on the spot, he appealed to the governor of Tauride, without result, and the
raids and thefts went on, sure of impunity, until one day they beat him
savagely for catching them at it. He did not lose hope of bringing them to
reason; but not wanting a witness and accuser of their crimes, four Tatars,
one of them a student preparing to be a mullah, resolved to kill him, and
lay in ambush by the road for several days. On 4 September, the eve of the
leave-taking of the Dormition, riding back from Sudak to be in time for the
service, he was killed by three shots; to hide the crime they burned him,
breaking his bones with clubs, from five in the evening until two in the
morning, and cut the throat of his horse and buried it in the forest. It
would never have come out but for witnesses who happened on the scene —
Muslims, whom the killers did not dare touch — and who, troubled by
conscience and not wanting to be suspected, told of it; the ashes and
remains were found where they said, gathered with reverence, and buried
with the blessing of Bishop Alexis of Tauride by a gathering of the clergy
near the monastery on 2 December 1866. The Council of Bishops of August
2000 numbered him among the saints; the Russian calendar keeps him on
22 August / 4 September.
