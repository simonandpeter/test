# Auxibius of Soloi

Auxibius — or Euxiphius, saint.gr allows both — was the first bishop of Soloi and the enlightener of the province of Morphou, and his homeland was the great city of Rome. His parents were rich in worldly goods and idolaters in religion, and he had a brother named Themistagoras. He was handsome in face, gentle in spirit and temperate in thought, the page says, and when he came of age his parents wished to marry him to a wife; but being young in years and an old man in judgement he answered the love of the flesh with a divine love, for he had heard of Christ and had a great longing to become a Christian.

Seeing what his parents intended, he left Rome for the parts of the East. He sailed by Rhodes and across the sea of Pamphylia and came to Cyprus, to the village of Limnitis by the sea, four stades from the city of Soloi. It was the time when the Apostle [Barnabas](/saints/barnabas-the-apostle) had come to his own country of Cyprus with his nephew [Mark](/saints/mark-the-evangelist) on his second journey, after parting from [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle); going about the whole island they came to Salamis and found [Heraclides](/saints/heraclides-of-tamasos), the archbishop of the island. Barnabas finished the good course of the faith and received the crown of martyrdom at Constantia; but the Jews were looking for the Evangelist Mark too, and having chased him as far as Ledra — the Nicosia of today — he hid three days in a cave with the apostles Timon and Rhodon, crossed the mountains of the snowy range, the Troodos, and came down to Limnitis, where they met Auxibius, who told them his longing to be a Christian.

Mark, seeing him a man full of faith and learned, catechised him, baptised him in the spring of that place and ordained him bishop of Soloi; and he taught him how to preach the Gospel in the city. Since the city is full of the darkness of the idols, he said, it will not receive the light of Christ at once: do not show at first that you are a Christian, but feign their religion, talking with them as with infants fed on milk, and when they are grown let them share the solid food of the faith too. Mark sailed for Alexandria, and Auxibius went off to Soloi.

He chose to live outside the city, in the district of Zeus, and was lodged in the house of the priest of the idols, feigning that priest's religion. Time enough passed, and by his prayer and his discernment the priest of the idols was pricked in the heart and was the first to be enlightened in the truth of Christ; and so he went on with others of the city, until the archbishop of the island Heraclides came round, installing bishops in the cities by a written order of Paul the Apostle of the nations — Epaphras at Paphos, Tychicus at Neapolis, which is Limassol.

After that Auxibius began at once to build the church, and when it was finished he threw himself on the ground and cried to Christ with tears to strengthen his servant and give him boldness to preach without fear, to put the fear of God into the heart of this people and enlighten them by his grace. Then he went out to a public place of the city and began to teach; and the grace of healing the sick was given him, and he drove out the unclean spirits, so that those who had sick brought them to him and he healed them by the power of the name of Christ. His holy fame went out into the country round Soloi, they carried the sick of the villages into the city, and having healed their diseases he baptised those who believed in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

The page tells two things of the end. A good man of the village Solopotamion, himself also named Auxibius, came and threw himself at the hierarch's feet asking for the seal of Christ, was baptised and enlightened and stayed with his bishop for ever; and later, while the younger man slept in the open in a tree's shade, the bishop passed and saw a crowd of ants that had made a halo about his head, and understood that the crown of ants foretold the worth of the priesthood and that the disciple was to sit on his teacher's throne. Then his brother Themistagoras came from Rome with his wife, the blessed Timo, and he baptised the two of them and ordained them both deacons of the Church. Almost all the people of Soloi believed in the God of Auxibius, and seeing the first church too small for so great a flock he raised, God working with him, a great and wonderful church that became famous in all Cyprus.

Having done all things well and honoured the high priesthood fifty whole years, the great Auxibius came to the end of his life. He called to him the Auxibius he had baptised, his faithful disciple, and gave over to him the bishoprick of the reasonable sheep, saying: God has chosen you a priest; you shall shepherd the flock of Christ. On the third day the word went through all the city that Auxibius our father was about to leave this human life, and they all gathered at the bishop's house with weeping and great lamentation; and having kissed each one of them he gave up his spirit in peace to his Lord and God Jesus Christ. Its couplet says that speech will not overlook Auxibius, who was shown, against his name, to be a man whose life ran out.
