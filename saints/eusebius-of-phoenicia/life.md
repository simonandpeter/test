# Eusebius of Phoenicia

Eusebius came of his own accord before the ruler of Phoenicia and rebuked him sharply for persecuting the Christians. The enraged ruler had him flayed and his wounds rubbed with hair-cloths steeped in salt, but Eusebius rejoiced as though it were not he who suffered, and the ruler, at a loss, had him beheaded. That is the whole of the Greek notice; its verse sets the impious who killed him with the sword against the man who lived piously as Christ’s friend.

The Russian calendar keeps a martyr Eusebius on 21 September old style on one line with [Priscus](/saints/priscus-the-martyr), in the fourth century, and prints no life for either; its line gives only the two names in the genitive, so no Russian form is written here. This folder takes that Eusebius to be the Greek page’s on the day, the name and the rank alone, which is a thin call: neither page says more that could confirm or refuse it.
