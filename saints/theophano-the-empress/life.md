# Theophano the Empress

doxologia.ro says that the blessed Theophano was born and brought up at Constantinople, of an imperial line, kin to three emperors and the wife of an emperor. Her father was called Constantine and held the rank of illustris, and her mother was called Anna. They lived together honourably but were childless, and grieved over it always; and they prayed to the Mother of God, going continually to her honoured church in Forachia and pouring out before her the fire of their hearts: loose our barrenness, Lady of the world, by your mercy, and let us receive the birth of children from the Creator through your intercession.

What they asked in faith they were given. A daughter was born to them and they called her Theophano, and from her sixth year she was taken to her letters and guided in every virtue; and the signs of the great virtues and of the holiness that were to come were seen in her from childhood. Her parents rejoiced in her good ways, and began to look out for a young man of good family and good habits to marry her to according to the law, for she was now of age and flowered with more gifts than others of her years.

In those days the emperor [Basil the Macedonian](/saints/basil-the-macedonian) had a search made for a maiden well adorned with virtues to marry his son Leo, the Most Wise; and finding Theophano fairer than the others he married her to his son, who was by then emperor himself, and the wedding was kept with much joy and gladness. After some time the crafty enemy sowed tares between the father and the son: Basil rose in great anger against Leo and shut him, with his wife Theophano, in a dark place under heavy guard. The life lays this to the hidden malice of Theodore Santabarenus, bishop of Euchaita, who was a sorcerer, and whom the emperor Leo did not love.

It began this way. Basil's firstborn son Constantine died, and the emperor grieved and wept for him without comfort; and the sorcerer, seeing him in such distress and meaning to console him, showed him by his sorceries Constantine after his death, coming towards him on horseback. The emperor took his son in his arms and suddenly saw nothing, the sorcery having gone out. Then he was frightened, believing that what he had seen was true, and he honoured Santabarenus greatly, keeping him as a close friend and listening to him; but the young emperor Leo, being wise and God-fearing, was disgusted with the sorcerer, and hating him as an enemy of God took no notice of him.

So the sorcerer, turning over how to get the better of Leo for that contempt, came to him as a well-wisher and a friend, and advised him: you are a young emperor and always go out hunting with your father, so you should carry a little sword about you, against the beasts, or to give your father at need, or to wound an enemy if some of the many who hate him should fall on him unawares. The young emperor listened to those deceiving words, not seeing through them, and began to carry a small sword whenever he went hunting or anywhere else with his father.

Then Santabarenus said to the old emperor in secret: your son Leo means to kill you unawares and reign alone, and this shall be your sign of his evil purpose — when you go out hunting and he is with you, he carries a small sword ready hidden in his boot, to find a good moment and strike you down. Try it: go out hunting, and in the field order his boot to be searched, and see whether it is not as you hear from me. Basil took his young son hunting, had the boot searched in the field, found a small sword sharpened on both sides, and was at once inflamed against him.

He would not hear Leo's word that he had the sword not for his father's death but against dangers and for the keeping of his own life; and being angry he shut his son in a dark house within the palace, with the blessed Theophano his wife, and set a strong guard. So the sorcerer had his way, and worse would have followed, for the emperor wanted his son's eyes put out, and the patriarch and all his suite opposed it. The innocent emperor Leo and the blessed Theophano stayed in that dark prison more than three years, having done no wrong, occupied with nothing but fasting and prayer, mourning in their prison and setting God, who sees all things, as their witness.

The rest of what the page prints is of her life as empress, and she reckoned the imperial glory as nothing and overlooked the vanity of this life. Day and night she had psalms and hymns and prayers on her lips, and spent her whole life in what pleased God and in almsgiving. She never took pains over imperial adornment: outwardly an empress dressed in costly ornaments, underneath she wore rough garments of hair with which she wore her body down. Her way of life was a hermit's, for she fed only on coarse bread and dried greens, and the tables of many dishes were altogether hateful to her.

The wealth that came into her hand she divided among the poor, the destitute, the orphaned and the widowed, and the costly clothes and ornaments she gave into their hands; and the poor dwellings in which those who lived the monastic life were housed she renewed with means and with everything needed. Her servants and her slaves she kept as brothers and sisters, and called nobody by a low name but honoured the name of each in whatever office or service he stood. Out of her mouth there never came a curse, nor a lying word, nor reviling, nor slander, nor anything bad; she wept with those who wept and rejoiced with those who rejoiced.

Her bed was spread with fine linen and adorned with the shining of gold, but a poor mat was laid on the floor, or she bowed down to sleep on sharp bones; and every night, like David, she wetted that bedding with her tears, and having very little sleep would rise at once to the praise of God. So hard a life soon brought a great bodily illness on her, but her soul did not weaken in its prayers to the Lord, and she did not stop learning the laws of God, reading the holy books or hearing them read. Her whole diligence was to help the wronged, to defend widows and care for the poor, to comfort the grieved and wipe the tears of those who wept; and so she became a mother to all who had no refuge and no help.

Living in the world she renounced all worldly things and took the good yoke of Christ, loving him, and took up the Cross and carried it on her shoulders and pleased God perfectly. Feeling her own going out of the body, she had them all come to her to take leave; and giving them all the last kiss she passed from the earthly kingdom to the heavenly, and stood before the King of Glory adorned with many virtues, and her honoured body was buried with great honour.

Her husband, the emperor Leo the Most Wise, seeing his wife's life and her great holiness, and honouring her not as a wife but as his lady and his intercessor with God, thought to build a church in her name before her time. When the saint learnt of it she not only would not have it but forbade him altogether; so the church begun in her name became the church of All Saints, and by the counsel of the whole Church that emperor appointed the feast of all the saints to be kept on the first Sunday after the descent of the Holy Spirit — for, said the emperor, if Theophano is a saint, then let her be celebrated together with all the saints. doxologia.ro gives no year for her birth or her death, and this folder gives none.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, and saint.gr tells the same marriage with its years in it. She was the daughter of Constantine Martinakios the illustris and of Anna, born at Constantinople and brought up with care; and at a suitable age the emperor [Basil the Macedonian](/saints/basil-the-macedonian) gave her as wife to his son Leo the Wise, whose reign the page dates 886 to 912, with whom she lived twelve years in wifely devotion and was recognised at once by her contemporaries as a saint and a wonderworker for the many works of love she did.

For all the grandeur and the wealth about her she kept the humility and modesty that had marked her before. She preferred to be plainly dressed and to be beside the people who needed her, and so she dressed plainly in order not to be recognised and went round the houses of the poor and the hard-pressed with two trusted servants of hers, and gave them her help. Such was her faith that she was counted worthy to work wonders: when the medical art gave a sick man up because it could not cure him, she brought his health back by the strength of her soul; and for all the bitterness she met with in her life she praised the Lord with an unquenchable flame.

When she died, the page says, her husband built a most beautiful church near the church of the Holy Apostles and laid her honoured relic there. The patriarch Gennadios Scholarios moved it into the church of the Holy Apostles, and from there it was later carried to the Patriarchate, where it is preserved to this day: incorrupt in the patriarchal church of Saint George at the Phanar in Constantinople, with part of her head at the monastery of Dionysiou on the Holy Mountain.
