# Gerontius the Athonite

The monastery of Vouleutiria stood by the sea, where the cell of Saint Eleutherius of the skete of Saint Anna is today. Standing where it did, it was often attacked by pirates, and for that reason Gerontius went up with his brotherhood onto the slope of the mountain, over the ground the skete of Saint Anna covers now. At first they put up huts, and then, as the brothers grew in number, they built larger cells.

Wanting more quiet, Gerontius went higher still into the mountain with one of his disciples and built there, as a place of ascesis, the cell of Saint Panteleimon; and it was there that the wonder of the springing of his holy water happened. He was taught by the most holy Mother of God the measure of the divine love of her Son, and by the help of that love he came to be a man humble, quiet and free of all love for material things, having one care only: to keep God always in his mind and to be a good example to his disciples.

He fell asleep in peace at a great age. He is remembered on 26 July, the day after the feast of the falling asleep of Saint Anna, the mother of the most holy Theotokos, which is the patronal feast of the skete he founded. The page gives no year, either of his birth or of his death.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 26 July, and its page gives the life the Romanian line did not. saint.gr says the venerable Gerontius was abbot of the old Monastery of the Bouleuteria on the Holy Mountain. Because that monastery lay on the shore and suffered many raids of the barbarians it was abandoned by its monks, who went off to other hard-to-reach parts of Athos; and so Gerontius too, with one disciple of his, settled in the upper part of the Skete of Saint Anna, where he built a hermitage with a church in the name of Saint Panteleimon. He is therefore the first man to have made the Skete of Saint Anna. Nearby, by a revelation of the Mother of God, he found water where the place had been altogether waterless, and it still springs today and the monks use it as holy water.

The venerable Gerontius lived a life beyond wonder, the page says, and fell asleep at a great age; it gives no year for either end of it. He was bound in spiritual friendship with the venerable [Maximus of Kapsokalyvia](/saints/maximus-of-kapsokalyvia), as appears in that saint's life, and the skete he founded has brought forward about fifteen known saints and many fathers of great virtue. A chanted service for him was composed by the monk Gerasimos Mikragiannanitis.
