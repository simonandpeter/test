# Speusippus

Speusippus was one of three brothers born at a single birth in the city doxologia.ro's life calls Lingonia, in Gaul, and raised in the house of their grandmother [Neonilla](/saints/neonilla) after their father died in unbelief. The three had learned the liberal arts and the wisdom of the world, and kept their fathers' custom of serving idols, until Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna, hearing that the persecution was heaviest in Gaul, sent there the presbyters Antiochus and Benignus and the deacon Thyrsus. Neonilla's brother Faustus, a senator of the city the life calls Edua, sent Benignus on to his sister's house.

The day the priest came the brothers were away at a village called Pasmasia, sacrificing to an idol named Nemesis, and brought the remains of the offering home to their grandmother; she threw it to the dogs and began, in front of Benignus, to teach them. When she and the priest had finished, each brother told a vision of the night before. Speusippus had seen himself taken to his grandmother's breast and fed with milk, and heard her say that so strengthened he would stand against his adversaries and win a glorious victory; [Eleusippus](/saints/eleusippus) and [Meleusippus](/saints/meleusippus) told the like, and Benignus baptised all three.

They sent their servants to break the idol of Nemesis and broke twelve more in their own house, and the city rose. Before the judges Quadratus, Palmatus and Hermogenes, Speusippus answered that to die for Christ is a welcome thing, since only so does one pass the sooner to eternal life, where there is no sorrow but unceasing joy. The three were bound hand and foot, hung from a beam and stretched until their joints parted, and then thrown into a great fire out of which they walked unharmed. They knelt, prayed together, and gave up their souls; they were buried two stadia from the city at a village called Urvat, where a church was afterwards built over them.

The Greek calendar keeps the three brothers and their grandmother on the same day and tells a wholly different story from the Gallic one. saint.gr's Πεύσιππος, Ελάσιππος and Μέσιππος are triplet brothers out of Cappadocia in Asia Minor, trained to break in small and unruly horses; the religion of their fathers was idolatry, but their grandmother [Neonilla](/saints/neonilla) was a Christian and brought all three grandsons to the truth of Christ. Their change of faith did not stay hidden long: they were seized, challenged to deny Christ, confessed him bravely, and the idolaters in their rage threw them into a fire, and so the three received the martyr's crown. The names, the kinship and the day are what doxologia has; the country, the trade and the manner of death are not. The Greek couplet plays on the same thing the names do — that though their names make them horsemen, the three run to the flame on foot.
