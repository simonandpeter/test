# Michael Mavroeidis

saint.gr says the new martyr Michael came from Adrianople and belonged among its notable and well-off inhabitants. He was denounced to the judge of Adrianople by fanatical Turks, who said he had despised the name of their god. The judge, who knew the saint's honesty, released him; but the slanderers threatened that they would report him to the Sultan as one negligent of their faith. The judge grew afraid and gave orders to imprison him, and at the same time made what had happened known to the Sultan and waited for his orders.

The order was plain: either he was to deny the faith of his fathers or be burned alive. For all the promises and all the threats the martyr stayed unshaken in his faith in Christ, and for that reason he was beheaded, in the year 1490. The page gives him no relics, no couplet and no hymn.

Then it sets out a disagreement of its own about who he is and when he is kept, and the disagreement is recorded here because it is the source's. Nikodemos the Hagiorite, it says, calls this martyr Mavroudis and sets his memory on 10 March, while the Paris codex calls him Mavroeidis and notes his memory on 17 February. Sophronios Eustratiades held that the new martyr Michael of Adrianople and the new martyr Michael Mavroudis who came from Granitsa in Eurytania and is kept on 21 March are not two men but one and the same person; and, the page ends, newer research holds that they are not the same person but two separate saints. What is recorded here is the day the site lists him on and the name its line prints; neither of the other two days was read.
