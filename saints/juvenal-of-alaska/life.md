# Juvenal of Alaska

Juvenal of Alaska is held to be the first martyr of America. He was born at Ekaterinburg in Russia in the year 1761 under the name Jacob Govoruchkin, though other sources, the life says, put his birth at Nerchinsk in Siberia. After his wife died in 1791 he entered a monastery in Saint Petersburg and was tonsured a monk with the name Juvenal, after the fifth-century Patriarch of Jerusalem.

Three years later he left for Alaska as a missionary, one of the eight monks of the monastery of Valaam who were to preach the word of God to the native people there. The group reached Kodiak Island on 24 September 1794, and Juvenal with the others began their work of preaching, bringing more than twelve thousand of the natives of Alaska to the Orthodox faith.

In the summer of 1796 he left Kodiak Island for the Alaskan mainland. His martyrdom is remembered in the oral tradition of the native people: one of the Eskimos did not understand his gestures as he made the sign of the cross, and a shaman, troubled by it, ordered that the body of the hieromonk Juvenal be pierced with spears and arrows. So he became the first martyr of America.

It is said that the shaman who took his pectoral cross was lifted into the air when he tried to work sorcery with it; having tried three times with the same result, he understood that he had to do with a power greater than his own, and ordered the tribe to attack no one who carried that sign. At the place of the martyrdom a pillar of light was seen for a long time afterwards rising towards heaven. He was numbered among the saints by the Orthodox Church in Alaska in 1977. The page gives no year for his death.
