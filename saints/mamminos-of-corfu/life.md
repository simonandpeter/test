# Mamminos of Corfu

The Greek calendar keeps seven martyrs together on 29 Απριλίου — Saturninus, Iakischolos, Faustianus, Januarius, Marsalios, Euphrasius and Mamminos — and saint.gr's whole notice of them is two sentences. These holy martyrs, it says, were robbers in their former life. While they were shut up in prison at Corfu they came to believe in Christ through the holy apostles [Jason](/saints/jason-of-tarsus) and [Sosipater](/saints/sosipater-of-iconium); and afterwards they were martyred inside boiling pitch.

That is the whole of it: no year, no country, no emperor and no judge, and nothing that tells one of the seven from another beyond his name. The apostles' own page for the same day tells the same thing from the other side, that in the prison they turned seven robbers to Christ, who were later martyred for their faith, and the gaoler Antonius with them. The couplet over the seven turns on the thief of the Gospel: «Λῃσταί, μαθόντες τὴν Ἐδὲμ λῃστοῦ λάχος, / Πίσσῃ λαχεῖν ἔσπευσαν αὐτὴν ἐμφλόγῳ» — robbers, having learned that Eden had fallen to a robber's lot, made haste to obtain it themselves by flaming pitch.

saint.gr heads him «Άγιος Μάμμινος ο Μάρτυρας», and the other six of the company are [Saturninus](/saints/saturninus-of-corfu), [Iakischolos](/saints/iakischolos-of-corfu), [Faustianus](/saints/faustianus-of-corfu), [Januarius](/saints/januarius-of-corfu), [Marsalios](/saints/marsalios-of-corfu) and [Euphrasius](/saints/euphrasius-of-corfu).

*After saint.gr's calendar for 29 Απριλίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/04/29/index.aspx) and [the entry for the seven](https://www.saint.gr/370/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
