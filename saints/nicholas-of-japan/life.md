# Nicholas of Japan

doxologia.ro gives him two short paragraphs and no more, and this is what they say. In the nineteenth century the Russian Orthodox Church sent him to Hakodate, in Hokkaido, as priest of the chapel of the Russian consulate, and two monks, Anatolie and Iacob Tihai, went with him on the mission. When they reached Japan the government forbade conversion to Christianity; even so the three were able to kindle in people a love for God, so that men and women began to be baptised from all over the country.

He was consecrated the first bishop of the Orthodox Church in Japan, and he was in the country through the Russo-Japanese war. He founded the cathedral at Tokyo, dedicated to the Resurrection of the Lord, which bears the name Nikorai-Do in honour of Nicholas, the enlightener of Japan. The page gives no year for his birth, none for his repose, and no account of his end.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, calls him «ο Ισαπόστολος», equal to the apostles, and gives the two years the Romanian page did not: born Ioann Dimitrievich Kasatkin on 1 August 1836 in the village of Beryozovsky in Belsk near Smolensk, to a priest Demetrius and his wife Xenia; reposed in peace in 1912. Between them it sets the whole mission — the seminary at Melinski and the academy of St Petersburg, tonsured and ordained presbyter in 1860, sent at twenty-six in 1861 as chaplain to the Russian legation at Hakodate; the cold welcome from Russians and Japanese alike, and Archbishop Innocent of America rebuking his flagging zeal on 9 September 1861 and telling him to learn the language. It adds the night a samurai burst into his cell with a drawn sword, meaning to kill him for corrupting the natives, and agreed to hear first what the monk was reading: that man, Takuma Sawabe, was baptised and became Father Paul, the first Orthodox Japanese priest. In 1880 Nicholas was elected and consecrated bishop, and used to say that an Orthodox missionary is bound to keep the doors of his house open to whoever wants to speak with him in person.
