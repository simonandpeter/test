# Nicholas of Japan

doxologia.ro gives him two short paragraphs and no more, and this is what they say. In the nineteenth century the Russian Orthodox Church sent him to Hakodate, in Hokkaido, as priest of the chapel of the Russian consulate, and two monks, Anatolie and Iacob Tihai, went with him on the mission. When they reached Japan the government forbade conversion to Christianity; even so the three were able to kindle in people a love for God, so that men and women began to be baptised from all over the country.

He was consecrated the first bishop of the Orthodox Church in Japan, and he was in the country through the Russo-Japanese war. He founded the cathedral at Tokyo, dedicated to the Resurrection of the Lord, which bears the name Nikorai-Do in honour of Nicholas, the enlightener of Japan. The page gives no year for his birth, none for his repose, and no account of his end.
