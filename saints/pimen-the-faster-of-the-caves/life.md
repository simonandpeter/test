# Pimen the Faster of the Kyiv Caves

The venerable Kuksha and Pimen the Faster died after 1114. Simon, bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal, wrote of Kuksha in his letter to Polycarp, archimandrite of the Caves: how can I worthily glorify the holy men who were in the holy monastery of the Caves, where pagans were baptised and became monks and Jews received the holy faith? — but I cannot keep silent about the blessed hieromartyr and monk of this monastery Kuksha, of whom all know that he drove out demons, baptised the Vyatichi, brought down rain, dried up a lake, worked many other wonders and after many torments was killed with his disciple Nikon. Pimen the Faster foresaw his death: standing in the midst of the great church of the Caves he said aloud, our brother Kuksha was killed at dawn — and having said it, the Prologue adds, himself expired, in 1113.

The Vyatichi among whom Kuksha preached and died lived along the Oka, in the lands of Oryol and Kaluga, pagans whose foul customs the chronicler Nestor deplored, living so “even to the present day”, strangers to the law of God; Kuksha preached among them in the days of Theoctistus, bishop of Chernigov (1113–1123), and was buried, like Pimen the Faster, in the Near Caves, whose synaxis of venerable fathers the Russian calendar keeps on 28 September. The Russian calendar keeps the three on 27 August (Julian), with a troparion and kontakion; the Serbian calendar keeps Kuksha and Pimen the Faster from the Prologue.

The Greek calendar keeps him too, and keeps him on 7 August rather than on the 27th of the Slavic books. Of the man himself saint.gr has nothing to add and says so: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Οσίου Ποίμην παρά μόνο ότι ήταν Ρώσος μοναχός που πέθανε τον 14ο αιώνα μ.Χ.» — we have no details of the venerable Pimen's life, except that he was a Russian monk who died in the fourteenth century. The same day's listing holds a second Russian Pimen, the one in the cave, the much-suffering, whom the page dates to 1110 and keeps as an entry of his own; the two are not one man, and the Greek calendar prints them side by side.
