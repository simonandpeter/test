# Marana

saint.gr's calendar for 28 Φεβρουαρίου names two women ascetics in one line — «Οσίες Μαράνα και Κύρα» — and says that their life was written by Theodoret of Cyrrhus in his Philotheos Historia. They came, the page says, from Beroea in Syria, which it calls by its present name of Aleppo, and lived at the beginning of the fifth century; their birth was distinguished and noble and their education matched it, and their devotion was turned to the spiritual life and the life of stillness.

So they left the world and built themselves a wall of stones outside the city and gave themselves there to the spiritual struggle. The door of their enclosure they closed with clay, so that nobody should come in, and left only a small window, to speak with those outside and to take their food through. They trained themselves in silence, and wore irons on their hands, their feet, their neck and their waist, to mortify the body and overcome the temptations. Their devout longing took them to the Holy Places and to the church of St Thekla in Isauria, from which they came back to their hermitage strengthened in spirit and went on with their life in humility and good works; and so, having lived, they fell asleep in peace and gave their souls to Christ the Bridegroom. The couplet over the pair says that Marana and Kyra are counted worthy, by the withering of the flesh, to have the mastery of heaven. Because the line names each of them, each is kept here: Marana with [Kyra](/saints/kyra-of-beroea).

The Russian calendar's reading of the pair is longer than the Greek one and differs from it in the country. It puts them at Beria in Asia Minor in the fourth century, born of noble and wealthy parents, and says that on coming of age they left the house and went out of the city; they fenced off a small corner of ground, blocked the entrance of their shelter with stones and clay, left only a small opening through which food was passed in to them, and lived there under the open sky. They wore heavy iron chains on their bodies and bore hunger patiently, eating once in forty days for three years together.

Their former servants came wanting to share the life, and the sisters housed them apart nearby and directed them. The bishop of Cyrus knew the two well, and out of respect for his rank he alone was let into their dwelling; he urged them not to burden themselves with the chains, which were so heavy that the weaker of the sisters was always bent under them and could not straighten. So they laboured for forty years, breaking their solitude twice only — once for Jerusalem and the Lord's tomb and once for the tomb of the first martyr in Isauria — and taking no food on either road until they had bowed before the holy places. The calendar closes with «около 450 года», the year this folder already holds.
