# Theodore of Kythera

Theodore was born at Korone in the Peloponnese between 870 and 890, the child of a mother who had been barren before him, and who called him Theodore — the gift of God — when God gave him. He was taught the sacred letters and handed over by his parents to the bishop of Korone, who made him a reader; and when his parents died a priest of Nauplion who had been their friend took him into his care. He came in time to the age for it, married, and had two children.

The bishop of Argos, seeing his virtues, ordained him deacon. He went afterwards to Rome and venerated the places of the martyrs, and on his return he stayed a long while at Monemvasia in a cell of the church of the Mother of God of the Diaconia. Then, longing for the higher goods of the spirit and against his family's pleading that he stay with them, he came about the year 921 to Kythera, which the raids of the Saracens of Crete had left «ἔρημος καί ἀοίκητος», deserted and unpeopled.

He took up his solitude in the old Christian church of Saints Sergius and Bacchus, lived very austerely, came to great heights of virtue, and died in peace on 12 May 922. Sailors putting in at the island a little afterwards found his body untouched, and three years later men of Monemvasia buried it; they rebuilt the old church of Sergius and Bacchus and dedicated it to him, and a monastery grew there in time. The chronicle of the Kytheran monk Cheilas, written about 1460, is the monastery's most precious source, and his skull is kept there still.
