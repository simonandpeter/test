# Joseph of Astrakhan

The holy hieromartyr Joseph, first Metropolitan of Astrakhan, was born at Astrakhan in the year 1579, saint.gr says. He was tonsured a monk and became abbot of the monastery of the Holy Trinity at Astrakhan at the age of fifty-two. In 1656 he was elected Metropolitan of Astrakhan.

On the eleventh of May in the year 1672, during a rising of the inhabitants of the city, Saint Joseph was perfected as a martyr. The page does not say by what means. His martyrdom was written down in detail by two eyewitnesses, priests of the cathedral of Astrakhan, Father Cyril and Father Peter.

The priests took the honourable relic of the hieromartyr, clothed it in the vestments of a bishop and laid it in a tomb prepared for it. The next day, after the serving of the Panychis, the relic was carried to the chapel and remained unburied for nine days. The relics of the holy hierarch were then placed within a tomb, and they worked wonders for those who came to them with faith. Saint Joseph was numbered among the saints by the Synod of the Russian Orthodox Church in April 1918, and his relics, the page says, are in the cathedral of the Ascension at Astrakhan in Russia.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 11 мая ст. ст. and gives him two lives, a long one and a short one. He was born at Astrakhan in 1579 to parents named Климент and Варвара, and the longer life says that sorrows and sufferings went with him from his youth to his martyr’s end. He was seven years old when the troubles raised in the city by the partisans of the first pretender broke out: Cossacks burst into his parents’ house and one of them struck the boy on the head with a stone, and he suffered from it for the rest of his life.

Seeking the salvation of his soul he entered the local Trinity monastery, and laboured there in prayer, fasting and work, first as a simple monk and afterwards as abbot and archimandrite; the shorter life puts the archimandrite’s rank in his fifty-second year and has him at Moscow in 1656. The longer says he was then consecrated archbishop of Astrakhan by patriarch Никон. He was a most worthy archpastor, and his virtuous life and wise discernment were known well beyond the Astrakhan country. The tsar Алексей Михайлович had a particular good will towards him and called him to Moscow for a church council in 1663 and 1664.

He also drew to himself the eastern patriarchs of Alexandria and Antioch, Паисий and Макарий, whom he received in his own house when they passed through Astrakhan on their way to Moscow at the tsar’s summons. He seemed to them then «аки светлейшая звезда», as a most bright star, showing every service of kindness and hospitality — comfort in sorrows, help in disasters, and instruction by word and deed where the way was hard. They took him with them to the council at Moscow and asked the tsar to raise him to the rank of metropolitan for his great labours and virtues, that the other archpastors might look at him and imitate him; the tsar agreed willingly, and he was made metropolitan in 1667, the patriarchs giving him in token of their respect a costly sakkos woven of twisted silk with gold.

On 11 мая 1672, during the revolt of Стенька Разин, he received a martyr’s end at Astrakhan. The event was written down in detail by eyewitnesses, the priests of the Astrakhan cathedral Кирилл and Пётр. When the Cossacks had taken the city and surrounded the cathedral they demanded that the hierarch come out to them; he ordered the great bell rung so that all the clergy should gather, vested himself, and came out with them. They accused him of dealings with the Moscow government and with the Cossacks who had broken with Разин, demanded that he unvest, and after long torture threw him from the slope of the cathedral roof. The priests took up the hieromartyr’s body, clothed it in a hierarch’s vestments and laid it in a coffin made ready; the next day, after a panikhida, it was carried into the chapel of saints Афанасий and Кирилл in the cathedral and lay nine days unburied. Then the relics were laid in a tomb and were soon glorified by wonders. His canonisation was made at the Local Council of the Russian Orthodox Church in April 1918.
