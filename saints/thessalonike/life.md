# Thessalonike

These saints were martyred at Amphipolis of Macedonia, which lay near Kavala, saint.gr says. Thessalonike was the daughter of a priest of the idols named Kleon, a man of some wealth; and when he learned that his daughter had become a Christian he begged her warmly to deny Christ, and could not move her. Then she was stripped and scourged by four men with whips of raw hide, and afterwards they broke her ribs; and having taken her property from her they sent her into exile, where she died confessing Christ.

The page gives her no year, no emperor and no governor, no place of exile and no burial, and prints no hymn. Her own couplet plays the sentence of exile against where she came to rest: «Φυγὴν κατακριθεῖσα πατρῴου πέδου, / Πέδον χλόης ᾤκησε Θεσσαλονίκη» — condemned to banishment from her father's ground, Thessalonike came to dwell in the ground of green pasture.

The two kept with her, [Auctus](/saints/auctus) and [Taurion](/saints/taurion), were denounced for accusing the man who had tortured and killed her.

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps the three of them together on the same day the Greek calendar does. Its line for 7 ноября ст. ст. is «Мчч. Авкта , Тавриона и Фессалоникии (739).», and the name link behind «Фессалоникии» gives the calendar's own nominative, «Фессалоникия Амфипольская (Македонская), мц.» — of Amphipolis and of Macedonia, which is where the life buries all three.

The life behind the three name links is one paragraph and is the same for all three. Thessalonike was the daughter of a pagan priest. When her impious father learned that his daughter had become a Christian he beat her without pity and drove her out of the house, leaving her no means of living at all. Auctus and Taurion tried to stand up for the Christian woman and to bring the hardened father to his senses; the priest denounced them and they were seized. Having confessed their faith in Christ before their torturers and borne cruel torments, they were beheaded. Soon after their martyrdom Thessalonike died too, and her body was buried with honour in the city of Amphipolis in Macedonia together with the martyrs Auctus and Taurion.

**The line prints a year nothing else gives these three.** It closes «(739)», and that figure is now the only date this folder carries; the life gives no year, names no emperor and no persecutor but the father, and saint.gr's line «Άγιοι Αύκτος, Ταυρίων και Θεσσαλονίκη» gave them no year either. It is recorded because the calendar prints it, and the date's note says that it stands alone.

The day page prints a troparion of tone 4 under the names of the other two of the three, «Тропарь мучеников Авкта и Тавриона», which names Taurion and Auctus and not her; it is in their folders and not in this one, which carries the day's common of martyrs only.

The Ohrid Prologue gives her a father and a city, and neither was in what this folder held. She was the daughter of an idol-priest, a rich and haughty man, and for her faith in Christ he drove her out of his house and out of the city. Two honourable citizens, [Auctus](/saints/auctus) and [Taurion](/saints/taurion), reproached him for his inhuman conduct towards his own daughter, and for that he denounced them as Christians: they were bitterly tortured and put to the sword for Christ, and after them she was tortured and killed.

They suffered, the book says, in the Macedonian city of Amphipolis, over against present-day Kavala. That is the city the Russian calendar's own name forms for the three already carry as an adjective and which no page this folder cites had named in words, and the entry gives no year at all, so the 739 the folder holds from the Russian line stands alone and untouched.
