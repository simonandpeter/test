# Antoninus (20 April)

Antoninus is one of nine martyrs saint.gr prints together on 20 April, and the page makes the cause of all nine the same: «αίτια του ενδόξου μαρτυρίου τους, ο μεγαλομάρτυς και τροπαιοφόρος Γεώργιος» — the cause of their glorious martyrdom was the great martyr and trophy-bearer [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer). They all suffered, it says, in the years of the king Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305.

Antoninus was one of four who were the king's own bodyguards — Christopher, Theonas, Caesarius and Antoninus. When these saw that George had raised a dead Greek by his prayer, they threw off the insignia of their rank and, before the king and the crowd, confessed Christ true God. They were imprisoned at once.

After some days they were brought before Diocletian, and there they were hung up and torn, and burned with lighted torches; and at the last they were thrown into the furnace of fire, and so received the glorious crown of martyrdom. It was the year 303. The couplet the page sets over them says that, filled with the soot of the furnace, those about Christopher came to Christ.

The page gives Antoninus no country, no city, no rank beyond that of the guards, and no relic, and it prints no hymn. Its whole account is the shared one, and the nine stand in it as they stand in the calendar's line: [Victor](/saints/victor-20-april), [Acindynus](/saints/acindynus-20-april), [Zoticus](/saints/zoticus-20-april), [Zeno](/saints/zeno-20-april), [Severian](/saints/severian-20-april), [Christopher](/saints/christopher-20-april), [Theonas](/saints/theonas-20-april) and [Caesarius](/saints/caesarius-20-april).

The Ohrid Prologue keeps three of this company on the Julian 19 April — the day after it printed the other five — where the Greek calendar keeps all nine together on 20 April. These three were young officers in the service of the emperor Diocletian, it says. When [George the Great Martyr](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) was being tortured they watched his torments and the wonders that happened on that occasion; and having seen all of it they came out before the emperor, threw down their weapons, took off their soldiers' belts and bravely confessed the name of the Lord Jesus. For that they were put to great torments and at last cast into the fire, where their bodies burned up while their souls went to the Lord into everlasting joy. They suffered honourably at Nicomedia in the year 303.

Its heading names «Антонин» among three — Христофор, Теона and Антонин — and the entry of the previous day names Victor, Zoticus, Zeno, Acindynus and Severian, which is eight of the nine the Greek page counts in this company; the ninth is named on neither day. Its 303 agrees exactly with the year this folder already held, so nothing was added and nothing displaced, and the place of death — Nicomedia — is new with this entry and is recorded in words only, this folder claiming no coordinate.

The Russian church calendar keeps these three on the Julian 19 April, the Prologue’s day and one before the Greek, and prints them in a line of their own: «Мчч. Феоны , Христофора и Антонина (303)». Its reading agrees with the Greek page that the cause of the martyrdom was [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) and adds four things to it. It says what their office was: they were копьеносцы, spear-bearers, of the emperor Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305. It has them present at the great martyr’s sufferings and witnesses of the wonders worked there by the power of God, and of his faith and his unshaken courage. It has them throw off their golden soldiers’ belts and declare themselves Christians before the emperor himself, and be thrown into prison on the spot.

The next day, it says, the emperor set about persuading the former soldiers to deny Christ, and they confessed their faith firmly and glorified the Saviour as the true God; so he ordered them beaten with iron rods and their bodies scraped with hooks, and they bore all the torments manfully and stayed unbending, and then Diocletian ordered them burned. Their martyrdom was accomplished in the year 303, which is the year this folder already held off the Greek page, so nothing in `dates` was changed. Where the Greek page has the furnace at the end of a longer list, the Russian has the fire as the single sentence it closes on.

Two things about the shape of the company are worth setting down. The Russian keeps the five of the other half — «Мчч. Виктора , Зотика , Зинона , Акиндина и Севериана» — on the day before, so this calendar divides the nine of the Greek page between two days and does not hold them as one company at all. And it never prints a line for [Caesarius](/saints/caesarius-20-april) on either day, though the life page it links for the five is headed with his name among them.
