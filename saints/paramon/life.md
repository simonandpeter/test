# Paramon

In the reign of the ill-believing emperor Decius there was in the East a governor named Aquilinus, a great persecutor of Christians. He had once gathered three hundred and seventy Christians into prison, and, meaning to go to his vineyard at Valsatin, gave orders that the martyrs be taken along with him, because the road passed the temple of Poseidon and he wanted to force them to sacrifice there to the idols.

When they reached the temple he pressed the saints to sacrifice to his unclean god, and could not bend them to his impure will either by flattery or by threats. A man of honour named Paramon, a Christian, happened to pass that way; and seeing so great a crowd of holy martyrs made ready for suffering, he stood before the idol's temple and cried out with a loud voice: "How many righteous men are slaughtered without cause by this unclean governor, because they will not worship his idols, that have no soul and no speech."

Having said it in the hearing of them all, he went on his way. The governor, hearing his words, flared into anger and ordered his servants to overtake him at once and kill him; and Paramon, knowing nothing of the order, was caught on the road by the pagan servants. First they drew out of his mouth the tongue that had rebuked and reproached the governor and pierced it with sharpened reeds; then they drove other reeds into all his limbs, and after that shot him through with spears.

So Paramon gave his honoured soul into the hands of God. At the same hour the three hundred and seventy martyrs spoken of before, tormented beside the temple of Poseidon, were put to the sword for the confession of Christ. The page names none of them, so none of them is entered here.
