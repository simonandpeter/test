# Antim Ivireanul

A treasury of gifts, the Romanian synaxarion calls him - God's envoy to the Romanian land from far Iviria, which is Georgia. Baptized Andrei, he fell young into Turkish captivity, where his sharpness of mind showed itself in the languages he took up with ease - Greek, Turkish, Old Slavonic, Arabic, and later Romanian - and after his release he lived near the Patriarchate at Constantinople, learning wood-carving, painting and embroidery. Around 1690 the devout voivode, the martyr Constantine Brâncoveanu, brought him to Bucharest; he learned the printer's craft at the princely press, moved to Snagov and founded a new press there, and from the presses he directed came sixty-three books, thirty-eight worked by his own hand, in Romanian, Greek, Arabic and Georgian.

For his virtues he was made abbot of Snagov, then bishop of Râmnic, and soon after Metropolitan of Wallachia. He shepherded his clergy and people gently and unsparingly, raised new churches and renewed the old; his own foundation, the Antim monastery in Bucharest with its church of All Saints, he adorned with carvings and endowed with a press, and he founded schools for the children of the poor. To the Church he left the great literary and theological treasure of his Didahii, his sermons - a sweet speaker of divine wisdom in the Romanian tongue.

All of it counted for nothing with his enemies: unjustly he was stripped of his rank and struck from the episcopal roll, condemned to exile, and given into the keeping of Turkish soldiers, who tormented him to death - beheading him and throwing his body into the waters of the Tungia, a tributary of the Marița south of the Danube. So the Metropolitan of Wallachia finished by a martyr's death, and his memory, the life says, remains written for ever in the book of the Church and of the Romanian people.
