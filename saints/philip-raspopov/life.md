# Philip (Raspopov)

**A psalm-reader, a soldier in China, a missionary on the Amur, and then a hole in the river ice.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar gives him a sentence to itself — «Сщмч. Филиппа (1919).» — and carries no life for him; the life here is what azbyka.ru prints for the civil 6 декабря. Филипп Кузьмич Распопов was born on 3 October 1875 in the village of Матвеевка in the Бугуруслан district of the province of Самара, a peasant’s son. After the parish school he was a novice in the Никольский monastery at Самара, but understanding that he had no bent for the monastic road he asked the bishop to send him to parish obedience instead, and was sent to the village of Туликовка in the Бузулук district as psalm-reader and teacher of the parish school.

**He served his military years in China and came back to the Far East for good.** From 1900 to 1903 he was on military service on Chinese ground. In December 1903 he was received into the diocese of Благовещенск and appointed reader and singer of the Дуйская field church, with a part in the services of the Khabarovsk cathedral and the direction of its choir; in the summer of 1904 he was moved to the Бурукан missionary station, bound to direct the cathedral choir of singers and to help in the building of a catechists’ school. In 1904 he married, and was then received into the clerical estate: deacon on 11 June 1906, serving at Khabarovsk, and priest on 8 January 1912 to the church of the village of Вятское. In November 1914 he was moved to the Holy Trinity parish of the village of Доле-Троицкое, keeping the care of his former parish as well.

**He taught all his life and he preached to the peoples of the Amur.** At Николаевск-на-Амуре he was teacher of the Law of God in the Пьянковское public school; serving at Вятское he taught the Law of God to the local children and preached the Gospel to the native peoples of the Amur country; in 1914 he was appointed teacher of the Law of God of the Троицкое school. During the first war he took part in collecting offerings for Russian prisoners of war, and had an archpastoral thanks expressed to him for it.

**On 23 November, old style, 1919, the partisans came to his house.** azbyka.ru gives the day in both styles — «23 ноября / 6 декабря 1919 года партизаны ворвались в дом Распоповых» — and what followed: in a bitter frost they dragged him out into the street almost without clothes and hauled him to the Amur, where on the river ice they abused him a long while, insulting him and beating him, and then let him down into a hole in the ice under the water, where he met a martyr’s end. By the witness of those who saw it he bore himself with dignity and did not renounce the faith. The honouring of him began in the Amur village of Троицкое as soon as services were restored there in the middle nineteen-nineties, and in Матвеевка where he was born they keep the days of his death and his birth in prayer.
