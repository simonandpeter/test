# Theophilus of Ephesus

The Greek synaxarion gives Theophilus in the words of S. Eustratiades: wholly unknown to the printed synaxaria, his memory and his service, a work of Theophanes the Branded, are found in the Paris codex 1619, folio 62. From the service, Eustratiades judged, he had first been a monk and a fugitive in the desert, and was then raised for his holiness and virtue to the metropolis of Ephesus, which he governed in a way pleasing to God, refuting in his teaching those who in their madness dishonoured the icon of Christ; so he plainly flourished under the iconoclasts, and perhaps lived in the time of Theophanes, who composed his service.

Delehaye’s Synaxarion keeps the memory on this day as that of the venerable Theophilus, Archbishop of Ephesus, the Confessor, and the Patmos codex 266 has it too. The Synodikon of the Sunday of Orthodoxy names an archbishop Theophilus without his see, beside an archbishop Nicholas, perhaps of Thessalonica, and Eustratiades guessed these were the two, though Nicholas is said to have died in peace. None of it gives a year.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 25 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2516/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026.*
