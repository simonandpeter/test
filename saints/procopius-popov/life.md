# Procopius (Popov)

The village of Sholga in the Vologda province stands close to the water of the broad Yug, and a little back from the bank its people had built a large and well-proportioned church of the Holy Trinity. The parish ran to more than a hundred villages and held the deanery, with three priests, two deacons and three readers serving it. Procopius Popov was its rector, and his life on the Russian church calendar's own site begins with that parish rather than with his birth, which it does not record.

Long before the revolution he showed the church's trustee a handful of the tsar's banknotes and told him that the time was coming when this money would be pasted on walls and wanted by nobody. The trustee, a pious man, heard it as a call to revolution and barely kept himself from rebuking the priest aloud. Then the imperial government fell, the provisional government was carried off after it, and the thousand years behind them began to tilt and be cut about.

1918 brought the punitive detachments, which went through the country killing clergy and any layman the people listened to. On 13 October, the eve of the Protection of the Mother of God, they came to Sholga and arrested him. A pit was dug in the middle of a field, and they chose to shoot him by daylight without keeping anyone away: sure of their strength, they wanted it seen. His flock watched and gave him up without a sound.

He stood before the grave, prayed, took leave of his parishioners, bowed to the ground before them and said, «Простите меня, грешного» — forgive me, a sinner. They wept. He took off his outer cassock and handed it to his sons, who had been standing beside him the whole time, turned to face east, prayed again and said, «Я готов» — I am ready. The first shot felled him and the second killed him.

He was buried where he fell. His sons asked leave to move the body to a cemetery and were refused, and the family did not stop asking until permission came to bury him at the cemetery of the village of Koskovo. He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
