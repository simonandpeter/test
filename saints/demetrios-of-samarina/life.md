# Demetrios of Samarina

saint.gr calls the monk Demetrios a pillar and a prop of the enslaved Greeks. He was born at Samarina of the Pindus, the page says, at the end of the eighteenth century, and became a monk in the monastery of his own village, where by prayer and fasting he purified his body and his soul. After Ali Pasha put down, in 1808, the rising that father Euthymios Vlachavas had stirred up, Demetrios came out of his monastery and went round the villages preaching the word of God and teaching patience in afflictions.

After a slander Ali Pasha arrested him and shut him in prison, and then ordered him savagely tortured. The executioners pierced his arms with reed points and afterwards drove them under the nails of his hands and feet; then they clamped his head in a vice; then they hung him upside down and burned him from below with fire. A certain Turk, seeing Demetrios's bravery, believed in Christ and was afterwards martyred himself.

Then Ali Pasha had Demetrios built into a wall, leaving only his head outside so as to prolong the martyrdom, and the martyr endured in that state for ten days. At last he gave up his spirit to God, in the year 1808. His martyrdom was written by the French consul at Ioannina, E. Pouqueville, and copied afterwards by the monk Gerasimos Mikragiannanitis.

The page is careful about the record, and its carefulness is set down here with the rest. It notes that Doukakis and Saint Nikodemos in their synaxaristai, and S. Eustratiades in his hagiology, do not mention this new martyr's memory at all; that in the local hagiology of the metropolis of Grevena a calendar of the Church of Greece of 1963 puts his memory on 18 August; and that the Mega Euchologion, and Otto Meinardus, put it on 17 August — which is the day saint.gr keeps him on, and the day this folder records. It prints no hymn to him.
