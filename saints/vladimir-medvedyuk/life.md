# Vladimir (Medvedyuk)

**He is the one of the fifteen of 1937 whose life the source of record itself prints.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him third of the fifteen and hangs behind his name page a life titled for him and for the novice Татиана (Фомичева) together. He was born on 15 July 1888 in Poland, in the town of Луков in the province of Siedlce, into the pious family of a railway worker, Фаддей Медведюк. Dying, his father said to him: «Дитя мое, как хотелось бы мне видеть тебя священником или даже псаломщиком, но только служителем Церкви», and the son answered that this was his own wish too.

Out of the ecclesiastical school in 1910 he was psalm-reader at the cathedral of Radom in Poland. The First World War made him, like thousands of others, a refugee; coming to Moscow he met Варвара Дмитриевна Иванюкович, of a deeply believing family from Belorussia and a refugee like himself, and in 1915 they were married. In 1916 he was ordained deacon to the church of the martyr Irene on Vozdvizhenka in Moscow, served there until 1919, was ordained priest to the church of the Savvinskoye podvorye on Tverskaya, and in 1921 was appointed rector of the church of the hierarch Mitrophan of Voronezh in Petrovsky Park.

From his first days there he set about building up the life of the parish, and in the ocean of passions and sufferings that was then Soviet Russia the parish became for the faithful an island of love. The believing young came to him at once and he tried to plant in them a love of the Orthodox services; choirs came often from other churches, which drew many worshippers and lovers of church singing, so that there were times when the church could not hold all who wished to be there.

**In the height of the Renovationist schism he kept the keys of his church in his own house.** Seeing that they could not seize it without the priest’s consent, the Renovationists brought him before their bishop Антонин (Грановский), who demanded the keys and shouted at him. «Отдай ключи!» — «Не отдам, владыка, не отдам!» — «Убью! Как собаку убью!» — «Убейте. Перед престолом Божиим мы с вами вместе предстанем»: kill me, then; we shall stand together before the throne of God. «Ишь, какой!» said the bishop, and pressed it no further, and the church was not taken.

**The life does not hide the one thing in it that is not courage.** He was given the kamilavka in 1923. In 1925 the authorities arrested him, laid invented charges and threatened him with a camp, and said he could be freed only by agreeing to work with the OGPU; he agreed and was freed. They gave him tasks, mostly touching the patriarchal locum tenens the metropolitan Пётр, and he carried them out; but the further it went the more he was at odds with his conscience, and neither his zeal in the church nor his pastoral conscientiousness could quiet the pain of it. In the end he broke off the connection and confessed the sin of betrayal to his confessor. Summoned to the Lubyanka on 9 December 1929, he told the investigator he refused to go on; for three days they tried to turn him, and he refused firmly, saying that he had told a priest everything in confession anyway. The warrant was issued on 11 December, and on 3 February 1930 the OGPU Collegium sentenced him to three years in a camp, which he served on the building of the White Sea–Baltic canal.

His family were meanwhile turned out of the church house and left without a roof, which was what he had feared most; in prison he prayed hard to the venerable [Sergius](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh) and to his parents the schemamonk [Cyril](/saints/cyril-of-radonezh) and the schemanun [Maria](/saints/maria-of-radonezh) that by their prayers the family might find shelter; and they found it at Sergiyev Posad. At the Elevation of the Cross in 1930 his son Николай came to the Elias church there; the rector, learning that five of them were left and that nobody would take a family with five children, called a parishioner and told her that she of all people, having a great grief herself, should understand and take them in. «Благословите», she answered. So they came to the family of a deacon who had been arrested and shot some months before, and that house was their shelter for many years. After his release in 1932 he lived there with them and travelled to Moscow to serve; in 1933 the Mitrophan church was closed and he was given a place in the Trinity church of the village of Язвище in the Volokolamsk district; in 1935 he was raised archpriest.

At Язвище the parish could give him only a small two-windowed watchman’s hut for the whole family, and a neighbour from across the way came and said: «Отец Владимир, предлагаю вам свой дом. Живите сколько хотите, мне ни копейки от вас не надо» — live in my house as long as you like and I want not a kopeck from you. The family lived in that benefactor’s house ten years. Many who had come back from exile and were forbidden to live in Moscow were then in the Volokolamsk district, among them the protodeacon Николай Цветков, honoured by the faithful for his ascetic life and his clairvoyance, to whom he often went with difficult questions. Once the protodeacon asked him to serve at night in his house with the windows curtained; somebody knocked at the window during the service and he began to take off his vestments, and was told «не малодушествуйте, стойте как стояли» — do not lose heart, stand as you stood. It was a chance traveller asking the way to the station.

The last time he went to the protodeacon was in the spring of 1937, to greet him on his name-day. The old man did not come out, but said from behind the door only «Христос воскресе!»; and when he sent word by the righteous man’s novice who it was that had come, the answer came back «скажи ему: воистину воскресе!» He was much distressed, understanding that the clairvoyant elder had said it as a sign that they would not meet again in this life. The mass arrests began in the summer. He went to Moscow in November and came back saying he was sure he would soon be arrested, and that what he feared was not exile or death but the marches, «когда гонят заключенных по нескольку десятков километров в день, и падающих, обессилевших конвоиры добивают прикладами».

A memorandum reached the Volokolamsk NKVD on 11 November 1937 about a meeting in the village at which almost no young people had been present, and asserting that up to twenty people came to the priest every day; the warrant for his arrest was issued on 24 November. On the evening of the 25th he was standing at his window reading the priest’s rule before serving a requiem liturgy, with two monastery novices in the house who after the closing of their house lived by the Trinity church as psalm-reader and altar servant; he saw the chairman of the village council and a policeman pass the window, said «кажется, сейчас за мной придут», blessed them all, and told his daughter «вряд ли, деточка, мы теперь увидимся». The two novices were arrested with him.

**The family saw him once more, through a barred window.** His wife was told that the prisoners were being taken to Moscow and that the train would pass the nearest station at three in the afternoon, in the first carriage, the one with bars. She went with the children and the things she meant to hand him; the train stopped, the guard surrounded the carriage and let nobody near, and as they stared at the little barred windows a hand appeared in one of them and blessed them with a priest’s blessing. The train stood three minutes, which seemed to them one instant. Questioned on 26 November, he denied every charge: «контрреволюционной и антисоветской агитацией я никогда не занимался». On 29 November the troika sentenced him to be shot and the two novices to ten years in a corrective labour camp. He was shot on 3 December 1937 and buried in a common unmarked grave at the Butovo range near Moscow, and was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000 for veneration by the whole church.
