# Gennadius of Corfu

Gennadius was born in 1805 in the village of Kalarites in Epirus, one of the seven children of Athanasios and Elisabeta, and baptised George. In 1820 he moved with his mother and his sisters to the island of Corfu; an Ionian passport of 1850 that allowed him to travel describes him as a monk with black hair and black eyes. In that same year the heir Elias gave him, for twenty Venetian talents, the church of the Mother of God of Kassopitra at Kanoni, whose building dates from 1530, and it was the old man's dream to found a women's monastery there.

In 1856 the hieromonk Gennadius came into full possession of the church and its lands. He built the two-storeyed common building, laid out the grounds, restored the church, bought a silver icon and a vigil lamp and a gilt reliquary to hold relics of the holy martyrs, and established a monastery of three nuns — the abbess Eupraxia Zampira and the sisters Athanasia and Syncletica. He left the monastery afterwards to Maria Mavrogiannis, a friend of the abbess Eupraxia.

He fell asleep in peace on 2 January 1859, at the age of fifty-four. Doxologia.ro calls him a holy hieromonk, a man full of virtue and of prayer who loved God and men; he ate and drank very little and slept on the ground when he sought his little rest, and he was a wonderworker who healed the possessed and helped many women to children. His cell and his grave can still be visited, and the page says his commemoration is kept on 2 January.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same second of January, and saint.gr tells him almost entirely through the house he served: the little monastery of the Most Holy Theotokos of Kassopitra at Figareto Kanoniou on Corfu, built in 1530. The page gives his secular name as George, son of Athanasius Vasilis, and says he came from the village of Kalarrytes in Epirus; in 1850 the church passed to him, a hieromonk, on a perpetual ground-rent of twenty Venetian talers a year, and the God-bearing elder Gennadius conceived from it the vision of founding a women's monastery there.
