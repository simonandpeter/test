# Mariamne, sister of the Apostle Philip

After the Ascension of our Saviour Jesus Christ, the Apostle Philip was at Hierapolis with [Bartholomew](/saints/bartholomew-the-apostle) and with Mariamne, his sister, preaching the word of Christ there, and he was hung up. While he was dying he prayed to God, and the proconsul and the people under his authority were swallowed into the ground. The rest, in fear, begged Bartholomew and Mariamne — who were hanging there themselves — that they too should not be swallowed.

Then Bartholomew and Mariamne prayed to Philip, and he destroyed them no further, but brought out again those who had been swallowed; only the proconsul and Echidna his wife they left below. So Bartholomew and Mariamne were let down. Bartholomew went from there to India, where he was crucified and made his end; and Mariamne went to Lycaonia, preached the word of Christ there, brought many to baptism, and died in peace.

That is the whole of what doxologia prints for her. It gives no year and no century, and its page for her carries no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and calls her equal to the apostles. She came, saint.gr says, from Bethsaida of Galilee; a Christian herself, she had the same divine zeal as her brother, and burning with longing for the wider spreading of the Gospel and the salvation of more souls she followed [Philip](/saints/philip-the-apostle) on many of his journeys, helping him in his work of enlightenment and sharing his dangers. After her brother's death she took the Apostle Philip's body, together with the apostle [Bartholomew](/saints/bartholomew-the-apostle), and buried it. Bartholomew then set the affairs of the church of Hierapolis in order, installed there as bishop a certain Stachys, and went on towards Lycaonia in her company, preaching the word of God; later he went to the Indies, where he continued his work and had a martyr's end by crucifixion. Mariamne, after Lycaonia, returned to Palestine, to the parts of the Jordan, and there fell asleep in peace. Of her relics the page records a heel at the monastery of Dionysiou on the Holy Mountain and a fragment at Kykkos in Cyprus. Its couplet says that Mariamne the virgin, having let the earth go, beholds the Christ who is of Mary the Virgin.
