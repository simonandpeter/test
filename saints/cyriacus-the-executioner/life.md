# Cyriacus the Executioner

saint.gr's calendar for 16 July names two men on one line, «Άγιοι Αντίοχος και Κυριάκος ο δήμιος» — Antiochus, and Cyriacus the executioner — and Cyriacus comes into the story only at its end. The page tells of [Antiochus the Physician](/saints/antiochus-the-physician), brother of [Plato](/saints/plato-of-ancyra), who came from Sebastia in Cappadocia and went from town to town and village to village healing the sick, not in body only but in soul, until the governor Hadrian arrested him and put him through one torment after another.

Hung on a frame and his sides torn, left in the fire, thrown into a cauldron of boiling oil and then set before wild beasts, Antiochus came out of all of it unharmed by the grace of God; he broke the idols by his prayer, and received the crown of martyrdom by beheading. Cyriacus was the executioner who beheaded him, and he saw milk run from the neck instead of blood. At that he too confessed Christ with courage, and was beheaded on the spot.

That is all the page gives him. There is no birthplace, no year, no age, no word of his life before that morning, nothing of his burial and no hymn. The byname it hangs on him, «ο δήμιος», is his trade, and apart from his name and his death it is the only thing the Greek calendar records about him.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 16 Ιουλίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/07/16/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2100/saint.aspx), which he shares with Antiochus; read 1 October 2026.*
