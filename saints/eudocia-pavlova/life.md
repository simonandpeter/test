# Eudocia (Pavlova)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names her on 7 апреля ст. ст. in three words, «Прмц. Евдокии (1939).», and carries no life for her; the name link behind the line, page 14244, gives the surname and the office the line withholds, «Евдокия (Павлова), послушница, прмц.», and lists beside this day only the moveable synaxis of the new martyrs on 26 января. azbyka.ru’s calendar for the same civil day has the life, signed игумен Дамаскин (Орловский) out of «Жития новомучеников и исповедников Российских ХХ века. Апрель», Тверь 2006, and adds two moveable synaxes of its own, of the saints of the Kuzbass and of the saints of Moscow — synaxes, and not written into the feast.

She was Евдокия Павловна Павлова, born on 1 August 1876 in Moscow. When she was eleven her parents gave her, together with her sister «Пелагией», two years younger, to be brought up in the Moscow Страстной monastery; afterwards the sisters were received into the monastery as novices and struggled in it until it was closed. After the house was broken up some of the nuns and novices rented a half-basement room on Тихвинская street, put it in order and settled there, earning their living by handwork — the sewing of quilts.

Her sister lived with her, and three other novices: [Vera (Morozova)](/saints/vera-morozova), «Мария Носова» and «София Селиверстова». All of them kept their rules of prayer as they had in the monastery, and sometimes invited priests to serve molebens for them. **That household is the whole of the identification this folder makes**, and it is a reading: azbyka’s own footnote against the first of those names says «Преподобномученица Вера (Морозова); память празднуется 13/26 февраля», which is the day and the name the corpus’s folder for her was read on off this same Russian calendar’s 13 февраля ст. ст. The footnote against the third names a София (Селиверстова) kept on 15/28 февраля, and no folder holds her.

She was arrested on 25 October 1937, shut in the Butyrka prison and interrogated the same day. The investigator asked whether any of her relations had been arrested by the soviet power and to whom among prisoners she had given help; she answered that she had sent a parcel to a nephew in a camp, and to a priest in bonds. Told that the investigation had material that she had carried on anti-soviet conversations among those about her and asked for truthful evidence, she answered: «Я никогда антисоветских разговоров не вела.» On that the interrogations ended and witnesses began to be called.

**The first of them said nothing against her, and his evidence was written down as something else.** A neighbour in the house testified that he had heard no anti-soviet conversations from the nuns at all — only once, passing by, that he had heard them saying among themselves that there was now nowhere for them, old women, to pray; and that he had seen a priest come to them several times. While he spoke the investigator was writing in the record, which the witness supposed to be his own words; then he was asked to sign, and he signed without reading it. What the investigator had written was that the novices had carried on anti-soviet and counter-revolutionary activity.

The second witness was another neighbour, a woman who worked as a warder in that same Butyrka prison, and she testified that the novices living in their house carried on counter-revolutionary agitation and that socially alien people — priests, sacristans and other servants of the cult — often visited their flat with the appropriate conversations on political subjects. On 16 November 1937 the investigator who had the case of the nuns and novices drew the indictment: that after the liquidation of the Страстной monastery a group of nuns had settled in one flat in which they organised illegal prayers, and that being hostile to the soviet power they had carried on systematic counter-revolutionary agitation among those about them.

On 19 November 1937 a troika of the NKVD sentenced her to ten years in a corrective labour camp. On 27 December 1937 she arrived with a transport at the Mariinsky distribution point of Siblag. **She died on 20 April 1939 in an NKVD corrective labour camp in the Novosibirsk region and was buried in an unmarked grave**, and 20 April 1939 is the civil date of the Julian 7 April, so this calendar keeps her on the day she died.

The page has one thing more, and it is the kind of thing a file keeps and a calendar cannot. In 1940 one of the arrested novices demanded that the case be reviewed, and the witnesses were questioned again. **In place of the Butyrka warder her sister came**, who lived in the same house, and said that her sister could not come herself, having been sentenced for slander to two years’ deprivation of liberty; and she said of the former nuns who were her neighbours: «Плохого я о них ничего не знаю, антисоветских разговоров никогда от них не слышала. Они были очень трудолюбивые…» — I know nothing bad of them, I never heard anti-soviet conversations from them, they were very hard-working.

**The sentence was not overturned**, on the ground that the arrested had been a long time in a monastery and therefore belonged to the socially dangerous elements. She was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
