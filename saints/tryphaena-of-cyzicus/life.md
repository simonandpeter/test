# Tryphaena of Cyzicus

Tryphaena was of the city of Cyzicus, which lies on the Hellespont, the daughter of a senator named Anastasius and of Socratia, who was a Christian. She confessed Christ, the Romanian life says, not dragged to judgement by others but presenting herself, reproaching and mocking the horrors by which the mad and the ignorant supposed they were honouring their idols.

At the same time she taught everyone to leave off vanities, and prayed God to turn them from unbelief. For this she was given over to torments by Caesarius the governor. At the last they threw her to wild beasts, and the beasts did not touch her; but a bull came and struck her with its horns and tore her open.

Tradition says, the page adds, that where her blood was spilt a spring of clear water broke out, and that women who lost their milk after childbirth drank of it and had their milk again at once, and not women only but female animals without milk. The page gives no year and no emperor, and nothing more is added here.
