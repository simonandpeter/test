# Chariton of Syanzhema

All saint.gr says of Chariton it says in the course of telling [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-of-syanzhema)'s life. Euthymius, born in the town the page writes Μπολογκντά and a monk of Spaso-Kamensky by the lake of Kubenskoye, went out to be a hermit on the river Kubenka; and his longing for a greater stillness brought him to the country of the river Syanzhema, where he met Chariton. That meeting is the first the page says of him.

The two of them together founded the monastery of the Ascension of the Saviour at Bologkda, as the page spells it, built the only church there was in that country, and grew herbs for the very little food they took. When Euthymius fell asleep, about the year 1465, Chariton succeeded him in the guidance of the monastery, and he himself fell asleep in the year 1509. Both were buried in the church of the monastery, which later became a parish church.

That is the whole of it. The page does not say where Chariton came from, what his name in the world was, who tonsured him, or what he did in the forty-four years he outlived his companion. It lists no relic and prints neither couplet nor hymn, and under his name it prints a gloss that belongs to an ancient Cypriot historian of the same name and not to him.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 28 September rather than the Greek's 11 April, with the same year — «Прп. Харитона Сянжемского (1509)» — and its index of names adds the epithet the Greek page does not print: «Харитон Сянжемский, Простой, игумен», Chariton of Syanzhema the Simple, abbot. It also keeps Chariton the Confessor on the same day, a different man of the same name five centuries earlier, which is why the Russian line has to carry the place.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 11 Απριλίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/04/11/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1876/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026; and the Russian church calendar's [28 September](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260928.html), read 4 October 2026.*
