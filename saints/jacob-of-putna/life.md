# Jacob of Putna

Jacob was born on 20 January 1719 to a settled and believing family in the north of Moldavia, and almost the whole household went in time into the monastic state: his parents were tonsured as Adrian and Mariana, his brother became the monk Ioil, one sister the nun Pelaghia, and the husband of another sister became the hieromonk Misail of the Doljești skete. At twelve he entered the monastery of Putna, where, doxologia says, he found a true monastic school, with spiritual fathers who handed on the knowledge of life in Christ from one generation to the next.

At Putna he was the disciple of Metropolitan Antonie, whom he called his elder and his spiritual father, and so entered a line of descent that ran from Metropolitan Sava Balaci through Bishop Calistru of Rădăuți and Antonie to himself. The commemoration book of the monastery, drawn up at Jacob's own order in 1756, records the chain beside Sava's name. From the monastery he was made abbot of Putna, then bishop of Rădăuți, and then metropolitan of Moldavia.

The page sets his labours under the head of what they were for: the good of the family, the raising and schooling of children, and the help of the poor and of those in need. He founded several churches and monasteries; he printed the first primer in Moldavia, the Bucvar, and four years later a corrected edition of it, which was reprinted at Vienna in 1771 for the schools of Bukovina; he saw to the founding of the first rural school in Moldavia and of the Saint Spiridon hospital at Iași. In 1755, at his urging, the prince Matei Ghica confirmed the rule of the gravediggers' guild of Botoșani, which obliged them to bury strangers too, who until then had been left unburied for weeks.

For Putna he is the second great founder, and the father of the monks who struggled there and in its dependent houses — the sketes of Sihăstria Putnei and Sihăstria Ursoaia and the old monastery — in the eighteenth century. In 1773 he made a new iconostasis for the church, gilded, and took as fellow founders of it the bishops Antonie of Roman and Dositei of Rădăuți with four hieromonks. Around him gathered fathers who formed others in their turn.

The deep desire of his heart he showed to everyone and fulfilled five days before he passed into eternity: he went and was tonsured into the great schema by his disciple and confessor of decades, the venerable Natan, in the church of the Sihăstria Putnei skete, and took the name Eftimie after Euthymius the Great, whom he had honoured all his life. Two years earlier, in 1776, he had written in his testament that he begged with tears the forgiveness of all the brethren and Christians for whatever he had done wrong, and called them all with love to the last kiss.

With grief at the seizure of Bukovina, but with the joy of having renewed his monastery, he died on 15 May 1778. The fathers buried him in the porch of the monastery church as its new founder, beside the earthly remains of his own parents, who had been tonsured in their old age; and when his relics were found he was seen to have been laid in the grave in the vesture of a schemamonk, with a small plain cross. The crypt was opened on 15 June 2016, the relics were placed in a reliquary on 25 September 2016, and the canonisation of Jacob together with his disciples the venerable Sila, Paisie and Natan was proclaimed at Putna on 14 May 2017; his own commemoration is on 15 May, the day of his death, and theirs on the day after.
