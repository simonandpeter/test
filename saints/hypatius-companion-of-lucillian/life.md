# Hypatius, companion of Lucillian

Hypatius is the first of the four young men named in doxologia's line for the day, and one of the four its life names together — Claudius, Hypatius, Paul and Dionysius — thrown into the prison at Nicomedia for their faith in Christ, in the days of Aurelian, emperor of Rome. There the old man [Lucillian](/saints/lucillian) found them: a former servant of the idols, baptised in his old age, who had brought many pagans to Christ and had been denounced and brought before Silvanus, then count in Nicomedia, who broke his jaws, had him beaten with rods and hung head downwards, and then cast him into that same prison.

Lucillian rejoiced over the four, and spoke with them of Christ and strengthened them for the martyr's contest: not to fear passing torments, for the everlasting reward in heaven; not to be afraid of death, for the life that is to come; and not to spare the flower of their youth for Christ, who prepares for them in His kingdom a happiness that does not grow old. So they all prayed together day and night and comforted one another in the hope of Christ.

After some days they were tormented again and thrown together into a heated furnace, and God poured out His mercy on them as He did of old on the Hebrew children in the furnace of Babylon: the fire turned to coolness and the flame to dew, and a great rain came down and quenched the furnace, and they came out unhurt. The pagans set the miracle down to Christian sorcery, and the unjust judge condemned them to death and sent them to Byzantium for it. There the four young men were cut down with the sword, and Lucillian was hung upon a cross and pierced with nails through his whole body.

The calendar's line for the day names each of the four, so each has a folder here; but the life gives them no separate words, and what it says of Hypatius it says of all of them together. It names Aurelian and Silvanus and prints no year, so none is recorded here. The virgin Paula, who served them in prison and followed them to their death, gathered the bodies of the four young men after they were beheaded and buried them reverently.
