# Euthymius of Suzdal

saint.gr says Euthymius the Wonderworker was born in 1316 at Nizhny Novgorod, a small town set where the Volga and the Oka meet, in the principality of Suzdal. They were the years of the Tatar yoke and of the wars among the Russian princes. From childhood he showed a thirst for letters and a love of the monastic life; under the guidance of his own village priest he began to learn to read and write, so as to be able to read the divine Scriptures and the works of the holy Fathers. In time the resolve ripened in him to give his life wholly to God, and he sought out a spiritual guide who could lead him along the way of perfection. That guidance was taken up by St Dionysius, a monk of the caves monastery of Nizhny Novgorod and later archbishop of Suzdal.

In 1351 the last prince of Suzdal, Boris, decided to found a monastery in the country of his birth, and wanted the blessing of Dionysius, abbot of the monastery of the Ascension; having it, he asked for a monk to be sent to oversee the building and the ordering of the house. Dionysius chose from among his disciples not only the man he would send to Suzdal but others for other places, so that the Church and the people might be served and monasticism spread. He called the brotherhood together, picked twelve of those strongest in faith and most zealous, and sent them across all the north-eastern parts of Russia. Euthymius, then thirty-six, undertook to go to Suzdal, to prince Boris.

The church was finished in 1352, and so splendid that it drew everyone's admiration; the prince paid for its painting himself. But the church was only the beginning of what had been given Euthymius to do: there were still the cells for the monks, the refectory, the outbuildings, and the walls that would set the monastery apart from the rest of the world. Until then he had been a simple monk; now that he was to be the spiritual guide of the house he was ordained by the bishop deacon first and then presbyter, and was later placed as its archimandrite. Prince Boris gave generously towards the building, gold and silver for the gilding of the domes and other materials besides, and Euthymius tended the holy work with labour, asceticism, tears and unceasing prayer.

As strict as he was with himself, the page says, so merciful was he to others. His monastery stood on the edge of a great city where many roads crossed, and it was open to everyone; the abbot never refused help to anyone who asked. The stranger found shelter with him, the poor man alms, the hungry food. His almsgiving and generosity seemed excessive to some, so that he was driven to give in secret, lest he stir complaints in the brotherhood and lead it into temptation. He bought out the debts of those who had no means to pay their creditors, and often forgave debts owed to the monastery itself. He exposed unjust and corrupt judges, protecting from abuse everyone who had been wrongly condemned, and he pleaded that even true criminals be treated with clemency and compassion. Every sinner who sought salvation found in him a guide to repentance, and by his prayer he healed the sick and drove out demons.

When he felt that his end was near he called all the monks, blessed each one of them separately and entrusted them all to the hands of God; he kissed them as a father and asked forgiveness of them all, then communicated of the immaculate Mysteries and gave up his soul to God. He fell asleep in peace in 1404, at the age of eighty-eight, and the monks buried him under the walls of the church of the Transfiguration, in the grave that he had built with his own hands while the church was going up. After his repose he went on protecting the monastery, as the many wonders that happened near his tomb bear witness; and on 4 July 1507, when the church was being rebuilt, his relics were found incorrupt. Most of them now lie in the church of St Constantine at Suzdal.
