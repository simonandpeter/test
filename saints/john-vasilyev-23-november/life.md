# John (Vasilyev) (23 November)

**A shop assistant who became a church warden, and was exiled for saying the priest was innocent.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar gives him a sentence to itself — «Св. Иоанна исп (1932).» — and carries no life for him; the life here is what azbyka.ru prints for the civil 6 декабря out of fond.ru, under a slug of its own, `sv-ioann-vasilev-ispovednik`. Иван Сергеевич Васильев was born in 1869 in the village of Мартьяново in the Серпухов district of the province of Moscow, son of a locksmith. From 1884 he worked in the victualling shop of a factory, becoming in time its assistant, and after he was let go he opened a trade of his own in 1922. From 1919 he was church warden of the Никольский church in the village of Бутурлино in the same district, whose rector was the priest Константин Уаров.

**The case grew out of a funeral the Komsomol tried to take over.** In 1927 the Komsomol of Бутурлино wanted a radio set on the church belfry and the believers would not have it. In July 1929 an eighteen-year-old Komsomol member of the village died; his parents bought the coffin and all that was needed and asked the priest to sing the burial service at home the next morning. The evening before, the head of the Komsomol cell told the mother that the Komsomol wished to bury their comrade themselves, having collected money for a wreath and a brass band and bought red cloth to line the coffin, and insisted there be no priest; the mother would not agree, «так как я уже купила гроб за свои деньги», because she had already bought the coffin with her own money. The priest came at eleven, and before the service was over the dead boy’s brother and other Komsomol members took up the coffin and carried it to the red corner. The priest walked a while behind it with the censer until he was told «Довольно тебе кадить, ты, может, с нами и в красный уголок пойдешь?» — enough of your censing; perhaps you will come to the red corner with us too — and went home without a word.

**The crowd took the coffin back.** The Komsomol lined it with red cloth, set a guard of honour, had themselves photographed with it, and carried it to the cemetery with music, holding one anti-religious meeting by the red corner and another on the square opposite the church, where the speakers said a civil funeral was better than a church one. Out of the five hundred or so who had gathered came shouts of «Отдайте покойника священнику, что вы так хороните, как скотину» — give the dead man to the priest, why do you bury him like a beast. At the grave the mother and sisters would not let the coffin down; there was shouting and uproar, the crowd pushed the Komsomol away from the grave, took the coffin and carried it into the church, and the priest and his clergy finished the burial service there. On 26 July 1929 the communist paper of the district printed a piece headed «Силой унесли гроб в церковь», signed «Безбожник», laying it all to the priest; the district administrative department investigated and concluded that the facts in the article were wrongly stated, that no hooliganism had been shown on the priest’s part, and that the case had happened through the fault of the Komsomol organisation, which had not agreed the matter with the relatives.

**The charge against him was saying so.** On 30 August 1929 the OGPU opened a criminal case against the priest, and the warden was charged «в распространении ложных слухов о невиновности попа», with spreading false rumours of the priest’s innocence. False witnesses reported that he had spoken at the church of a report in the same paper that thirty-nine churches were to be turned to the cultural needs of another district, and had said it would be better to build new schools and keep the old buildings, and that some three hundred parishioners then present shouted to him that the Bolsheviks did not know what they were doing and that he spoke rightly. He was arrested on 3 September and questioned the same day, answering that he had not been at the funeral and so had seen and knew nothing, and that he did not know why the priest had sung the service in the church. On 16 September the investigation closed, charging him with having tried to create a public opinion in the priest’s defence and agitated among religiously minded workers and peasants for an organised struggle against the Soviet power’s policy towards the Church — while recording in the same document that «в деле не имеется достаточных свидетельских показаний», that the case held no sufficient testimony of their part in the crime imputed to them, and asking all the same, on the ground of their social danger, for administrative banishment of three years each.

**He outlived his sentence by three months and died in the place of exile.** On 4 November 1929 the troika of the OGPU gave the priest three years in a camp and the warden three years of banishment to Kazakhstan. He was sent to a hamlet in the Каунрадский district by way of Караганда, taking half a year on the road in a convoy with common criminals. His term expired on 3 September 1932 and he was not released. The hard conditions told on an already elderly man and he fell badly ill with rheumatism of the legs, so that for the sharpness of the pain he could not walk; the dug-out he lived in was three and a half metres square and three men lived in it; the place of exile was more than four hundred versts from the railway, so that even when the term was over it was no easy thing for a sick man to get away. His son petitioned for early release, pointing out that if the papers were held up even a month the impassability of the desert would keep his father there another seven or eight months. On 4 December 1932 the family had a telegram and then a letter from a priest serving his own sentence beside him, saying their father was mortally ill, that his hands and feet were paralysed and that nothing could be done. **Иван Сергеевич Васильев died on 6 December 1932 and was buried in an unmarked grave** — the civil day of this Julian 23 November.
