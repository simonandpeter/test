# James, companion of John the Bishop

saint.gr's calendar for 1 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιοι Ιωάννης ο Επίσκοπος και Ιάκωβος ο Πρεσβύτερος» — a bishop and a presbyter, each with a heading of his own on the page and neither with a see or a city. These hieromartyrs lived in the years of Shapur, king of the Persians, the notice says, which it dates 332; and they taught the word of the true faith to the devout and managed to draw many to it.

They were arrested by Shapur, and after being put first to hard torments they were beheaded at the end, and so received the unfading crown of martyrdom. That is the whole of it: no birthplace, no church that James served, nothing of where they were buried and no relics.

The page sets one epigram over the pair, and it makes them both priests of Christ: along with James, the sacrificer of Christ, [John](/saints/john-companion-of-james-the-presbyter) the sacrificer of Christ bore the sword. No hymn is printed. The page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου», so the site vouches for the entry as it stands.

The Russian church calendar keeps the same pair on the same day, and its line is the Greek pairing with a later year: «Сщмчч. Иоанна епископа и Иакова пресвитера, в Персии пострадавших (ок. 345)» — the hieromartyrs John the bishop and James the presbyter, who suffered in Persia, about 345. Its index of names gives him two by-names the Greek page withholds: «Иаков Ревнитель, Персидский, пресвитер, сщмч.», James the Zealot, the Persian, a presbyter.

Nothing else on that page is his. The one life the calendar hangs behind his name is of a venerable martyr of the same forename who was tonsured at Dochiariou on the Holy Mountain, renewed a deserted skete, was given visions of paradise and of hell, and was tortured to death with two disciples in 1520 — a different man on the same day of the year, a thousand years later, whom this corpus already keeps on this day from the Greek calendar as three folders of his own. So this row records the day, the name, the grade and the year, and takes no story: a life page that is about somebody else is not this saint's life.

The year is now two readings and not one. The Greek page set the pair in the reign of Shapur and printed 332; this line prints about 345. The folder's interval holds both and chooses neither.
