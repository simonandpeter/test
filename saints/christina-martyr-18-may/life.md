# Christina (18 May)

Christina was a virgin of great beauty who lived, doxologia's short notice says, in the third century, in the time of the emperor Decius. At sixteen, when the time had come for her to be married, she was seized by the pagans together with Dionysius and imprisoned. The two soldiers set to guard them were inflamed at the sight of her beauty and desired her; and when they proposed the sin to her she refused, and an angel appeared, and instead of forcing her they listened to her and came to know the love of the Lord. For that the three men were stoned to death, and Christina was beheaded.

The longer life the same day links tells the end differently placed. There the people drag the martyrs out of Lampsacus, a city of the Hellespont, to stone them, and the virgin Christina, hearing of it, runs to the place crying and weeping and falls upon the bodies of those being killed, saying, Let me die with you on earth, that I may live with you in heaven. Word came to the governor that the maiden who had been given over to dishonour had been delivered out of the hands of the debauchers by a shining young man, and had escaped and fallen on the bodies of the Christians; and he ordered her pulled aside and killed with the sword.

The two accounts agree on the manner of her death and on the reign, and differ on the city: her own page puts her arrest at Athens, the longer life puts the killing at Lampsacus. Both readings are left standing here, and no place is recorded in this folder. The calendar's line for the day joins seven holy virgins to her, «cele 7 sfinte fecioare împreună cu ea», and neither of these two pages names them; the seven are named in the life of Theodotus of Ancyra printed for the same day, which says they suffered on the eighteenth of May.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same civil day, on one page with the seven men of her company, and she is the only woman among them. saint.gr says all eight were martyred in the years of the emperor Decius, who reigned from 249 to 251, and that [Paul](/saints/paul-companion-of-andrew) and [Andrew](/saints/andrew-companion-of-paul), Mesopotamians and soldiers of Decius, became soldiers of Christ when they came to Athens, and that together with [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-of-lampsacus) and Christina all of them were stoned to death. Her own couplet reads: you stood before Christ made wholly radiant, by your own blood, virgin Christina. It gives her no city, no parents and no age, and the company's apolytikion calls her «Χριστίναν τὴν θείαν».
