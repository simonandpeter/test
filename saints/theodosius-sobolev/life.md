# Theodosius (Sobolev)

Theodosius, in the world Theodore Sobolev, was born on 11 September 1842, the son of Dormidont Sobolev, priest of the church of Peter and Paul at Zharovo in the Velsk district of Vologda province and himself a priest’s son, and of Olga Alekseevna, a deacon’s daughter. He finished the Vologda seminary in 1864, married, and that year was ordained priest at the Dormition church in Totma; from 1869 he taught Latin at the Totma church school, and a Synod inspector commended his teaching in 1875; later he was also priest of the Totma prison church. When his wife died he gave himself wholly to the service of God: he joined the brotherhood of the Spaso-Sumorin monastery at Totma on 3 November 1879, became its treasurer a year later, and took the habit there on 6 March 1881 with the name Theodosius.

From 1885 he was made superior in turn of the Spaso-Prilutsky monastery at Vologda and the Dionysius-Glushitsky monastery, with the rank of hegumen, but the office weighed on him, and at his own request he went back to Sumorin in December 1889, where he became confessor of the brethren and from 1892 taught the faith at the monastery’s parish school, seeing in learning a way of saving people. From 1896 he was moved again — superior of the Arsenius-Komelsky monastery at Gryazovets, confessor of the Ustyug church school and two monasteries, superior of the Nikolo-Prilutsky monastery at Ustyug — and in September 1907 became superior of the Vvedensky monastery at Solvychegodsk, and archimandrite that November. He retired for age and weak health on 13 September 1916 but went on helping the brethren, and in August 1918 he went to the Koryazhma monastery to talk over the changes in the country with its superior, [Paul](/saints/paul-moiseev).

After October 1917 a commandant sent by the new authorities lived in the Koryazhma monastery. On 23 September 1918 an illiterate villager told the Solvychegodsk Cheka that suspicious people were hiding in the monastery, and on 30 September the military commissar and members of the commission came there, demanded that its administration hand over every hiding place and the money, and recorded the refusal as resistance to the Soviet power. The commandant wrote all the answers on one sheet: [Paul](/saints/paul-moiseev) said no to every question and that nothing more was in the monastery; [Nicodemus](/saints/nicodemus-shchapkov) admitted nothing; [Seraphim](/saints/seraphim-kulakov) said he had been in charge of nothing; and Theodosius said he took no part in the monastery’s affairs and lived apart. The commission counted Seraphim, who had no part in the monastery’s business, and the retired Theodosius among its administration, and ordered the four shot as ringleaders of a hostile band. No money was found, and without waiting for the sentence to be confirmed they were shot at two in the afternoon of 30 September 1918, on a deserted island in the Vychegda on the road from the monastery: Theodosius in his seventy-seventh year, Paul in his seventieth, Nicodemus at sixty-five and Seraphim at forty-five.

The Russian calendar keeps the four on 17 September old style.
