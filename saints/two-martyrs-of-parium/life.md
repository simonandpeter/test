# The Two Martyrs of Parium

The Greek calendar keeps on 23 Ιανουαρίου two martyrs whose names it does not give, calling them only «οἱ ἐν τῷ Παρίῳ», those at Parium. These two saints, the page says, bore witness after being thrown into a pit, in the city of Parium; and it then tells what Parium was: a city by the sea with a harbour, between Cyzicus and Lampsacus in Asia Minor, which, according to Meletios, was built by the inhabitants of the island of Paros, and was named Parium after them.

The distich over them reads «Ἔνδον βόθρου χωροῦσι Μάρτυρες δύω, Θείου πόθου βάλλοντος ἔξω τὸν φόβον» — two martyrs go down inside the pit, divine longing casting fear out. Because the source enumerates nobody, this is one entry and not two: there is no name to give a folder to. No year, no emperor, no judge and no hymn is printed.
