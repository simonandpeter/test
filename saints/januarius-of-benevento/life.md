# Januarius of Benevento

When Diocletian and Maximian, the pagan emperors, were enraged against the Christians, Januarius the bishop was seized and brought before Timothy, the governor of Campania. Coaxed with promises and frightened with threats toward the worship of the idols, he stood unmoved in the Christian faith like a pillar, and the governor ordered him thrown into a heated furnace to be burned. He was unharmed, as the three youths once were in the furnace of Babylon: an angel of God came down to him and turned the power of the fire into coolness, and the saint glorified God, dancing and singing in the midst of it. When he came out, all saw that the fire had not so much as touched his clothes, and they marvelled.

The governor took it that the Christians' bishop had been kept whole by the power of sorcery, and in greater fury had him stretched until the joints parted from their sockets, and beaten so long in that stretching that his whole body was broken, the bare bones showed and the ground was full of blood. Two clerics came to that dreadful sight, Faustus the deacon and Desiderius the reader, and stood in the crowd; and the pagans knew them for Christians, because they rejoiced in spirit at his brave endurance and at the same time wept for the cruelty of his torments, for the very sinews of the saint were seen to be cut through.

They seized them both, bound them together with Januarius, and took them after the governor to the city of Puteoli, where they were thrown into prison. Shut up there for Christ already were Proculus and Sosius, deacons of Puteoli, and two simple men, Eutychius and Acutius, condemned to be eaten by the beasts. The next day they were all brought out to the spectacle together with the bishop and given to the beasts.

But God, who is glorified in his saints, stopped the mouths of the beasts at that spectacle, as once in the pit into which Daniel was thrown: not one of them touched a martyr, but all of them changed their natural cruelty for the gentleness of sheep, and came and fell at the bishop's feet. The governor counted this divine power also for Christian sorcery, and while his mind was labouring at how to destroy the bishop and those with him he was struck suddenly blind in the body — being long blind in the soul — and felt along the walls for a guide. Januarius, who bore no malice, prayed for that enemy of his, and Timothy saw again with the body, but not with the soul.

The people who had watched all these wonders believed in Christ, about five thousand of them. The governor, unthankful and hardened, who had been given his sight back by the martyr's prayers, did not acknowledge the truth but blasphemed the more; and, maddened with anger at the turning of so great a crowd, he ordered the holy martyrs put to the sword. So Januarius the hieromartyr, bishop of God, with those who suffered together with him — Faustus, Proculus and Sosius the deacons, Desiderius the reader, Eutychius and Acutius — received the martyr's crown, beheaded outside the city of Puteoli.

The cities round about gathered the bodies one by one, each striving to have its own intercessor before God; the Neapolitans took the body of Januarius and carried it with honour into their city and laid it in their church. At the time they took it from Puteoli, his blood, which had clotted there, was gathered from the ground and put in a vessel of glass and kept; and whenever it was set beside the martyr's head, that clotted blood melted at once and boiled as though freshly shed.

Many kinds of miracle were done at Naples by his prayers, and one above the rest was glorified there: when the mountain called Vesuvius sent up out of the bosom of the earth a great flame of fire, so that not the near cities only but the far ones were filled with fear and trembling, the people ran to the tomb of Januarius and cried to him with tears for help; and at his prayers the flame was hidden again in the mountain and the fear ceased, and neither the far cities nor the near took any harm.

The page tells one miracle more. A widow named Maximilla, whose newborn son had died, was weeping without comfort when she looked toward the church and saw above its door a little board bearing the likeness of the bishop Januarius. Remembering how the prophet Elisha raised the son of the Shunammite woman, she took the icon and laid it upon her dead child, eye to eye and mouth to mouth and limb to limb, and prayed with sighs and hot tears, asking him to still her grief and raise her only son; and the child rose up living and whole, and all who had gathered for the burial gave thanks to God. No year is given anywhere in the notice.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr's account names both his companions and a different see. Januarius was bishop at Naples in Italy, it says, in the years of Diocletian, when the prefect of the region was Timothy; and with a very good band of Christian fellow workers — the deacons [Proculus](/saints/proculus-companion-of-januarius), whom the page also calls Proklos, [Sossus](/saints/sossus-companion-of-januarius) and [Faustus](/saints/faustus-companion-of-januarius), the reader [Desiderius](/saints/desiderius-companion-of-januarius), and [Acutius](/saints/acutius-companion-of-januarius) and [Eutychius](/saints/eutychius-companion-of-januarius) — he fought the holy struggle of virtue and brought many idolaters to Christ.

When the great persecution came they were all arrested and endured fearful tortures. Januarius they threw into the fire to be burned, but by divine grace he stayed unharmed; so they led him at once to another place and cut his sinews, and thus he received the crown of martyrdom. On the road to his death, the page says, the many Christians of Naples tried to take him out of the soldiers' hands; but he refused, and said to them: «Αφήστε, παιδιά μου, να τελειώσω τον καλό αγώνα του μαρτυρίου, και σας υπόσχομαι ότι θα είμαι πάντοτε προστάτης της πόλης σας» — let me finish the good struggle of martyrdom, my children, and I promise you that I shall always be the protector of your city. So it was, and Naples proclaimed him its patron.

The synaxarion adds a wonder. A widow named Maximina had the misfortune to lose her only child; and once, while she was lamenting, she came to herself for a moment and, looking up, saw a cloth hanging above the church gate on which the image of Januarius was painted. She remembered what the prophet Elisha had done when he raised the son of the Shunammite, and, moved by divine illumination, did the same: she laid her son out and then set the likeness of the saint over him, fitting the eyes of the saint's image to her child's eyes, and the ears, and the mouth, and so with the rest of the members. And doing this she prayed fervently to him: «Δούλε του Θεού, ελέησέ με και ανάστησε τον υιό μου, γιατί είναι το μόνο μου παιδί, δεν έχω άλλο» — servant of God, have mercy on me and raise up my son, for he is my only child, I have no other. And the saint heard her, and raised her son.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the bishop alone — „Свешт. муч. Јануарије.“ — and then opens its body with „И други с њим.“, *and others with him*, before naming all seven. Januarius was bishop in Campania in Italy, it says, and in the persecution of Maximian he was brought before the court and racked with various torments, which he bore without malice and with patience. When they cast him into the fire, the fire was cooled by an invisible dew, and the martyr stood unharmed in the midst of it singing praise to God; then they scraped his body with iron combs until the bones showed white, and still he bore it.

His deacon [Faustus](/saints/faustus-companion-of-januarius) and his reader [Desiderius](/saints/desiderius-companion-of-januarius) watched their spiritual father's torments and wept for him, and for that they too were bound, brought with the bishop to the city of Puteoli and thrown into prison. In the same prison, for Christ's sake, were the deacons of Puteoli, [Proculus](/saints/proculus-companion-of-januarius) and [Sossus](/saints/sossus-companion-of-januarius), and two plain Christian men, [Eutychius](/saints/eutychius-companion-of-januarius) and [Acutius](/saints/acutius-companion-of-januarius). The next day all seven were thrown before the beasts, and the beasts did not so much as touch them; then all of them were cut down with the sword.

The Christians of the city of Naples carried his body secretly to their own city and laid it honourably in a church, and to this day, the Prologue says, countless wonders have appeared at his tomb. Among many it remembers one: a wretched widow whose only son had died took the icon of Saint Januarius out of the church and laid it on the dead boy, weeping and praying to the saint — and her son came alive. He suffered honourably in 305, which is the first year this folder has carried.
