# Innocent of Odessa

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 25 mai reads «Sfântul Ierarh Inochentie al Odessei» — the holy hierarch Innocent of Odessa — and his own page on the site carries that line as heading and title, the feast date, and a tab of icons, but no Viață and no Tropar. Nothing on the page says when he lived, what see he held besides the city the name gives him, or how he died.

What the calendar's words say is held here and nothing more: that the Romanian church keeps a hierarch called Innocent of Odessa on 25 May. The rank word «Ierarh» is the whole of what is said about his office. The corpus keeps another Innocent, the hierarch of Moscow, who is a different man on a different day and has not been identified with this one. No year is claimed here, and no other calendar has been checked.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives him the life the Romanian line does not. saint.gr calls him bishop of Kherson and says he was John Alexeyevich Borisov in the world, born on 15 December 1800 in the village of Yelets in the province of Orel, of a priestly family; his parents were Alexis and Aquilina. He finished the seminary of Orel in 1819 and the theological academy of Kiev in 1823, gave his days and nights to the scriptures and the Fathers and to the writing of sermons, and out of love for the monastic life was tonsured with the name Innocent. He was called to teach at the academy of Saint Petersburg, was made archimandrite in 1826, and on 21 November 1836, the feast of the Entry of the Mother of God, was consecrated bishop of Chigirin in the region of Kiev; he was moved to Vologda in 1841, to Kharkov from 1842 to 1848, and in 1857, on the death of the bishop of Kherson and all Taurida, was set over that province.

The page ends with the Crimean War, and it is the part of his life it tells most warmly. He struggled to save churches and monasteries from destruction and to put heart into the people; he stood by the soldiers defending the city with a fighting spirit and with faith; and the greatness of his soul and of his pastoral work, saint.gr says, was shown when he went himself to visit and care for the wounded, the suffering and those sick with typhus, and was to them all an earthly angel and a comforter. He fell asleep in peace in 1857.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 25 мая ст. ст. and gives him the fullest life it has printed for anybody in this block. He was Иван Алексеевич Борисов in the world, born on 15 December 1800 at Yelets in the province of Orel, in the family of Алексий Борисов, priest of the Dormition church. His parents were simple people of a good life; his father had his education at home and rose from the lower degrees of the clergy to the priesthood, and tried in frequent sermons to carry the words of the fathers and teachers of the Church to his parishioners. His mother Акилина was unlettered but intelligent and devout, and the cross and prayer were the ground of her whole life; she kept a household medicine chest of herbs and flowers, of incense, of blessed breads, of oil from wonderworking icons and of the Mother of God’s prosphora, and with these she treated herself and her children. In his father’s house he learned the Slavonic alphabet, the Book of Hours and the Psalter, and learned to write.

He finished the seminary course in 1819 with excellent success and entered the Kyiv Theological Academy, where he gave himself to study with such ardour that he sometimes spent whole nights over a book, and busied himself above all, obeying an inward call, with the making and working up of sermons. In 1823, at twenty-three, he finished the full academic course as first master and was appointed to the St Petersburg Theological Seminary as inspector and professor of church history; within three months he held also the rectorship of the «Санкт-Петербургского Александро-Невского Духовного училища». There he was tonsured a monk with the name Иннокентий and ordained hieromonk. In December 1824 he was made a bachelor of theological sciences at the St Petersburg Theological Academy, a few months later its inspector and extraordinary professor, and in March 1826 archimandrite.

His lectures he gave by heart. He followed the state and the advances of the natural sciences closely, and in his view that knowledge did not contradict theology but served it best. He had the art of binding the professors together in good fellowship and brought peace wherever he went, and in the nine years of his rectorship the academic family thought one thought with him and lived one life with him; with the students he was always kind and generous, and most attentive when some grief came on them, a serious illness above all, when helping the unfortunate became his whole care and he gave not only his means but sometimes his own comfort. His particular glory, the calendar says, is an extraordinary gift for preaching: he was afterwards called «Русским Златоустым», the Russian Chrysostom, and he worked above all on the hearts of his hearers, carrying them by the clearness and simplicity of his words, by fine and witty comparisons, by the art of finding new and interesting sides to his matter and the skill of bringing his teaching as close as could be to the various occasions and circumstances of his hearers. So he made a new Russian school of preaching, far from all outward effect and dry learning. Besides the sermons he left many scientific works and translations — «Жизнь св. Киприана», «Жизнь св. Апостола Павла», «Памятник веры», «История Соборов Вселенских», a translation of the «Кормчая Книга», and much else.

What he wanted most in his governing of the diocese of Kherson and Taurida was to restore the ancient Christian monuments of the Crimea that the Tatars had destroyed, and to found there his own «Русский Афон», a Russian Athos. Wishing to preserve the ruins of ancient Chersonesus, made famous by the baptism of the great prince Владимир, he asked the viceroy of the Caucasus for the ruins and set about building there, in the middle of a wilderness and near the remains of the old cathedral, a small church in the name of the princess Ольга with a small lodging for monks. Then he renewed the ancient church cut by the hands of St Климент in the Inkerman rock, consecrated it in memory of the two hieromartyrs Климент and Мартин, who suffered at Chersonesus where they had been sent to the quarries, and made a small skete in the same rock. Travelling in the Crimea he would leave his companions at the foot of the mountains and go up to the top himself, to pray on the places where the old ascetics had laboured.

In the last years of his episcopate he took the liveliest pastoral part in the disasters of the Crimean war and had the most beneficial influence on the soldiers. His greatness of spirit showed in his visiting of the suffering soldiers in the hospitals, where infectious typhus was raging and all the heavy grief and suffering that war breeds could be seen; in the battles he went along the ranks encouraging the heroes, and there too the manful father and pastor was an angel comforting the afflicted. At the coronation of the emperor Alexander II he was made a member of the Holy Synod. Hard labours and anxieties broke his health down: he fell ill while at Sevastopol during a battle of the Russian forces with the enemy, and on the way back to Odessa died at Kherson on 25 мая 1857, on the bright feast of the Life-giving Trinity. He was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church in 1997.
