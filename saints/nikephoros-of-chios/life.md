# Nikephoros of Chios

Nikephoros was born about 1750 at Kardamyla of Chios of devout parents, and his first name was George. Saved as a boy from the sickness of the plague, he was dedicated by his parents to the Panagia Neamonitissa, to serve her; and afterwards he entered the Nea Moni and was tonsured a monk and took the name Nikephoros. The brotherhood of the Nea Moni sent him to the schools of Chios, where his teachers were Gabriel Astrakaris, Neophytos Kausokalyvites and the learned preacher Iakovos Mavros, and at the monastery he stayed always near the venerable elder Anthimos the Hagiopateritis.

After his studies he came back to the monastery, was ordained deacon and then presbyter, and was appointed a teacher in the school of Chios under the headship of [Athanasius of Paros](/saints/athanasius-of-paros), until 1802, when he took up the abbacy of the Nea Moni. Because of the disorder in the house and the various reactions of the fathers he withdrew from it and took refuge at Resta, at the little monastery of Saint George, where the teacher Joseph of Agrapha was also living as a monk. Under the influence of [Macarius of Corinth](/saints/macarius-notaras), then in the last years of his life and always in close touch with him, and of Athanasius of Paros, who retired to Resta in 1812, and together with the monks Joseph and Nilos Kalognomos, the hieromonk Nikephoros settled in the monastery of Resta.

From Resta, the page says, passed the new-martyrs Demetrius the Peloponnesian, Mark the New and Angelis of Argos, and took strength there for their faith. Twenty years of hard ascetic struggle at Resta went with untiring labour: he planted a great many trees, which still give their shade and their beauty to the pilgrims, and he handed out saplings freely as a blessing and urged the villagers to plant. He wrote many books for the profit of souls — the service of the holy fathers Niketas, John and Joseph, and the history of the Nea Moni — and in 1819 he published at Venice the Neon Leimonarion with the lives of the new-martyrs, a collective work whose first material had been gathered by Macarius archbishop of Corinth and the great teacher Athanasius of Paros. He composed salutations and hymns and idiomela and set services.

At last he fell ill at Chora, in his sister's house, and died, having lived a holy life, in the summer of 1821. His body was carried back and buried at Resta, and his relics were found in 1845. His own life and service were written in 1907, when the saint was first formally celebrated.
