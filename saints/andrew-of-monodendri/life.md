# Andrew of Monodendri

saint.gr begins by saying that this Andrew is unknown to the synaxarists, and that what is known of his life, together with his service, was written by Andreas Idromenos Hypargios, who died in 1847. According to that material he came from the village of Monodendri in Epirus and lived in the days of Michael II Komnenos, despot of Epirus, who ruled from 1234 to 1271. Others, the page adds without choosing between them, say he was born at Sivista in Eurytania, on the mountain Kalana, a village now sunk under the artificial lake of Kremasta, and went to Monodendri when he was grown.

Having left behind the pleasures of money, property and marriage, the page says, he made his home in the desert. For his place of stillness he seems to have chosen the country around the monasteries of Saint Paraskevi and of the Prophet Elijah at Monodendri, and later he took refuge in a cave on Kalana. There, by ascesis and prayer, he sanctified his life, and he died in peace.

At his falling asleep, according to his biographer, the Lord gave a wonderful sign to glorify him: heavenly light and lit lamps came down from the sky upon the place where his honoured relic lay. As soon as she heard of it, Saint Theodora, queen of Arta, whom the page sends to 11 Μαρτίου, gathered her council, went out to the place where the holy relic was, and buried it outside the cave with honour and reverence; and she gave orders that a church be built in his name.
