# Nahum

Nahum was from Elkosh, on the far side of the Jordan over against Begabar, of the tribe of Simeon. After the prophet [Jonah](/saints/jonah-the-prophet) he prophesied to the Ninevites that their city would be destroyed with water and with fire, and so it came about. For the Ninevites, who had repented for a little while at Jonah's preaching, seeing that his prophecy had not been fulfilled upon them, went back to their first evil deeds and stirred God's long patience to anger again.

Their destruction came in this way. Beside Nineveh was a great lake of water that went round the city; and a great earthquake happened, and the city sank into that lake, while the part of it that was left standing on the hill was burnt by fire that came out of the wilderness. So Nahum's prophecy was fulfilled, and God punished a sinful people by his righteous judgment: those he had spared in the days of the prophet Jonah for their repentance, those same he destroyed at the last when they had turned back.

He prophesied other things also that were to come, and fell asleep in peace forty-five years from his birth, and was buried with honour in his own land.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr sets him in a tribe, a village and a century. He is one of the twelve so-called minor prophets; he lived in the fifth century before Christ, flourishing about 460, and he was of the tribe of Simeon. His homeland was Elkesem, and from it he was called Nahum the Elkesite.

The book of his prophecy, the page says, is of three short chapters and concerns the fate of the city of Nineveh: in the first he hymns God, in the second he foretells the ruin of Nineveh with her chariots, her horsemen and her treasures, and in the third he characterises her as a city of blood, of lying, of great injustice and of fornication. It quotes his first chapter on the two sorts of men — «Χρηστὸς Κύριος τοῖς ὑπομένουσιν αὐτὸν ἐν ἡμέρᾳ θλίψεως» — and says that the prophet Nahum died in peace and was buried in the place of his fathers. A piece of his relic is at the monastery of Simonopetra on the Holy Mountain.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 1 December and sets him two hundred years after [Jonah](/saints/jonah-the-prophet), at whose preaching the Ninevites had repented for a season. God spared them then and did not destroy them; but in time they forgot that mercy and were corrupted again, and it was to those people that Nahum foretold ruin. Where there was no repentance there was no sparing either, and by earthquake, by flood and by fire the whole city perished, so utterly that afterwards not even the place of it could be made out.

This book puts his life seven hundred years before Christ, where the Greek calendar places his flourishing about 460; the folder carries both readings and chooses neither. It says that he lived forty-five years and fell asleep in the Lord, leaving behind him the small book of his true prophecies.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the Julian 1 December and gives a short notice with one fact the other books do not. It makes him one of the twelve lesser prophets, of the village of Elkosh in Galilee, and puts his life in the seventh century before Christ. His book, it says, holds the foretelling of the ruin of Nineveh for her lawlessness, of the breaking of the kingdom of Israel, and of the blasphemy of the Assyrian king Sennacherib against the Lord.

The circumstances of the prophet’s life, this calendar says plainly, are unknown. He died in the forty-fifth year of his life and was buried in his own native soil. The seventh century it gives agrees with the Ohrid Prologue’s seven hundred years before Christ and not with the Greek flourishing about 460, so the interval the folder already carries stands unchanged.
