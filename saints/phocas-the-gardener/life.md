# Phocas the Gardener

Phocas came from Sinope, and all he owned was a garden, which he worked with great care. It brought in very little, but he lived so frugally that he always had something to give the poor. He read the holy books gladly, and told those who found him at it that the soul too is a garden, which has to be tended if it is not to put out thorns and thistles; and he wanted every soul to be such a garden, so that as he sold his vegetables and fruit he spoke words of spiritual profit, and won over unbelievers as well as helping Christians.

In a persecution it became known that Phocas the gardener was a Christian, and soldiers were sent to take him. His house stood by the gate of the town that led to the harbour, and he often had guests, travellers and the poor, so he did not know the soldiers for what they were and looked after them as strangers. After some days he asked their errand, and, bound by his hospitality, they told him they were looking for Phocas the Christian to behead him, and would be much obliged if he would point him out. He went out, dug his own grave, and the next day told them who he was. They were ashamed, having been so kindly received; he told them not to hesitate, since the guilt for his death lay with those who had sent them and not with them, and so persuaded them to behead him. Its verse has the martyr Phocas give thanks to the Word as he dies for his sake by the sword.

The Christians built a great church on the place of his beheading and laid his relics there. The Greek calendar tells how by his protection grain was found when Sinope was threatened by famine, and how he appeared to sailors in danger, waking a helmsman in the night to warn him of a storm and working the ropes and sails in heavy seas; so sailors took to keeping a share for him at table, which one of them bought each day, and gave the money to the poor when the ship came in. It says nothing of when he lived. A fragment of his relics is at the monastery of Proussos in Evrytania.
