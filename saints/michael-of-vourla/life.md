# Michael of Vourla

saint.gr says Michael was born at Vourla in Asia Minor and came young to Smyrna, where he worked as a coppersmith. He was made a Muslim by a trick on the first Saturday of the fast, when he was eighteen, by a Turkish coffee-seller, and afterwards went to work as a servant in the man's shop. But on the night of the Resurrection, hearing others of his own age singing «Χριστός Ανέστη» in their joy, he felt the weight of his apostasy, left the coffee-house, and sang the hymns of the Resurrection along with them.

He was arrested, and the next day brought before the judge, to whom he said: when a man has been cheated, and has given gold and taken lead, is it not lawful for him to give back the lead and take his gold again? The Muslim religion forced on him by a trick was the lead, and his fathers' faith the gold. The judges admired the wit of his answer, the page says, but tried with flatteries and threats to make him change his faith once more. He would not be persuaded, and they put him in prison.

Two days later he came before the judge again and was asked whether he had changed his mind. Michael said no, and was condemned to death. He was beheaded in 1772 and his body was thrown into the sea; but some pious Christian dyers gathered it up and buried it with honours in the church of Saint Photini at Smyrna. The page's couplet is of the dyers' trade: «Bαφείς Mιχαήλ τω λύθρω σων αιμάτων, Λευκός δέδειξαι ως χιών αθληφόρε» — dyed in the gore of your own blood, Michael, you are shown white as snow, bearer of the prize.
