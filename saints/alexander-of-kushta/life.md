# Alexander of Kushta

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on 9 июня, and the reading behind its line is titled for three names at once, Alexander abbot of Kushta and of Vologda. He was born about 1371 at Vologda and was Alexis in the world. He was tonsured in the Spaso-Kamenny monastery by the abbot Dionysius the Athonite, who had brought the rule of Athos into that house; and there he went through every kind of obedience and of strict fasting and was counted worthy of the rank of hieromonk. He was continually at work and at prayer, and the brethren looked on him as an angel of God, which was a weight on him.

So he left the monastery secretly by night and came to the river Syanzhema, where there was thick forest and a lake, and set himself a cell and lived in prayer and extreme abstinence. But little by little people began to come to him, and he went away from that place to the shore of lake Kubenskoye, to the mouth of the little river Kushta. [Euthymius of Syanzhema](/saints/euthymius-of-syanzhema) was living there at the time, and Alexander proposed that they exchange cells; Euthymius agreed, and at their parting left him his own cross for a blessing. The quiet hermitage pleased Alexander greatly, and coming down to the lake he dipped the cross in the water and prayed the Lord to gather there men zealous for the way of the cross.

After a time an elder came to him and lived with him five years, and when a third brother came Alexander resolved to build a church in honour of the Dormition of the Mother of God, and went to Rostov to the archbishop Dionysius, who had been his own abbot, and was blessed to build it. Once, while the prince of Zaozerye Dmitry Vasilyevich was away and the Tatars fell on his patrimony, five of them rode into the Kushta house; Alexander met them calmly and signed them with the cross, and they fell as dead and lay senseless some hours until he woke them out of the stupor by the name of the Life-giving Trinity.

After the prince's death his widow, the princess Maria, who held Alexander in great honour, gave the house a village for her husband's commemoration. Coming once into the church she found him reading the Psalter with his breast bare and insects tearing at it, and he was grieved at the visit and said, "It is not for thee, princess, to oversee our poverty." She asked his pardon humbly and he blessed her, but said, "Feed thy poor at home." She went back and fell ill, and came to ask prayers for her recovery; but he had seen her end and said, "Let her prepare for that other life." Twenty days later the princess died.

Wheat had been gathered on the monastery threshing floor, and a peasant thought to steal a sack of it and could not lift it at all. The saint came on him and said, "In vain, my son, dost thou lift what is beyond thy strength." The man, found out, threw himself at his feet and asked pardon; and Alexander told him to add more wheat to the sack, taught him not to take what was another's, and blessed him to take it and go with God — and the forgiven peasant carried the blessed load away easily, thanking the great-hearted elder.

Feeling his end near he said to those with him, "I am growing weak — but you, endure in this place, keep humility and love for one another." On a Sunday he served the divine liturgy and communicated of the holy mysteries, then prayed on his knees with tears for himself and for his house, and in the sixty-eighth year of his age gave up his spirit peacefully to the Lord on 9 June 1439.

By his own charge his body was laid on the southern side of the sanctuary, and a year later a little rowan tree grew up over his grave. On a feast of the Dormition a peasant boy broke a branch off the tree and his arm suddenly began to ache; his parents brought him with prayer to the grave and he was healed, and from then on people took the berries of that tree for healing. His disciples built a warm church over his grave in honour of the hierarch Nicholas and consecrated it on the day of his memory, and many sick people who were brought into that church saw Alexander together with the hierarch, praying or censing the church, and received healings at his tomb.

The day page prints four hymns over him, two troparia and two kontakia, which are copied here with their English typed; the second of them has him living with the wild beasts and the first and both kontakia name the wilderness he settled.
