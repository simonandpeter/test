# Ennatha of Gaza

Doxologia prints one short life for the three martyrs of this day, and it begins with her: Saint Ennatha was from the region of Gaza, and [Valentina](/saints/valentina-of-caesarea) from Caesarea. When the governor Firmilian was sitting in judgement, the brave virgin Ennatha was brought before him. She was asked whether she would deny Christ; she confessed that Christ is God, and for that she was cruelly tortured.

Then the honourable Valentina, who was a virgin also, could not bear to watch the inhumanity and cruelty of what was being done, and was filled with boldness. When she was ordered to sacrifice to the idols — for there was an altar standing there beside the judgement seat — she struck it with her feet and threw it down together with the fire that was on it. At that the tyrant was enraged and tortured her cruelly as well; and when he left off torturing them he sentenced them both to be burned in the fire.

After them, the same page says, [Paul](/saints/paul-with-valentina-and-ennatha) was brought to suffer. The page gives no year, names no emperor, and carries no hymn for her. This Ennatha is not the Ennatha of Aza whom the Greek synaxarion keeps in September with four other Persian virgins; that one was killed by her own money-loving priest under Shapur, and this one was burned at Caesarea under the governor Firmilian.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, with [Valentina](/saints/valentina-of-caesarea) and [Paul](/saints/paul-with-valentina-and-ennatha), on one page, and saint.gr gives the sequence plainly: Ennatha was from Gaza and Valentina from Caesarea in Palestine, and when the Christians were being persecuted and tortured in Palestine, Ennatha was denounced to the governor Firmilian. The brave girl declared at once that she believed in Christ and advised the governor to study the Christian religion himself and find his own salvation; and because she held to her confession they bound her to a piece of wood and tortured her in various ways. The page's distich for the two women makes the flame a betrothal: «Εἰς ἀρραβῶνα παρθένοις κόραις δύο, Ὁ νυμφίος δίδωσι θάρσος πρὸς φλόγα» — the bridegroom gives the two virgin girls courage before the flame as their betrothal gift.
