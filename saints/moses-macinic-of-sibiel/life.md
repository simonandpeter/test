# Moses Măcinic of Sibiel

About the middle of the eighteenth century many defenders of the right faith rose from among the Orthodox Romanian priests and people of Transylvania against the attempts of the Habsburg authorities to draw them away from the "ancestral law". Among them were the priests Moses Măcinic of Sibiel and [John of Galeș](/saints/john-of-gales), villages that then belonged to the seat of Săliște.

Moses was ordained at Bucharest by Metropolitan Neofit of Wallachia about the year 1746. Rising against the union, he was seized and imprisoned at Sibiu, where he suffered seventeen months; he was let out on condition that he perform no priestly office and live and work as a simple peasant.

In 1752 he was sent with the layman [Oprea](/saints/oprea-of-saliste) Nicolae of Săliște to Vienna, to lay before the empress Maria Theresa the complaint of the faithful of southern Transylvania — Făgăraș, Sibiu, Sebeș and Orăștie — asking for rights for the Orthodox Church. The empress received them, and instead of an answer to their complaints they were thrown into the terrible prison of Kufstein in the mountains of the Tyrol.

In the years that followed the clergy and people of Transylvania asked the Habsburg authorities more than once to free the two captives. On 24 July 1784 Stana, Oprea's wife, begged the emperor Joseph II to release her husband after thirty-two years of captivity, and the prison authorities reported that nothing more was known of him. Both men, then, ended their days in Kufstein, giving their lives for the Orthodox faith and gaining the crowns of martyrdom.
