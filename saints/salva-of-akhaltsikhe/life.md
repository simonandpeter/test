# Salva of Akhaltsikhe

Salva came of a family of princes, and doxologia's account says he was endowed with many virtues but distinguished above all by his great courage. For that the holy queen Tamar the Great raised him to the rank of general and made him governor of the region of Akhaltsikhe, with his seat in the city of that name, setting his brother John, himself famous for his deeds, under him as deputy. Salva commanded the Georgian armies in the war against the sultan Rukn al-Din, with his brother John and prince Zachariah Mkhargrdzeli as his officers, and by his skill led them in 1203 to a triumphant victory at Basiani, after which Georgia had a long and untroubled peace.

After Tamar fell asleep in 1212 her son George came to the throne and died very young, at twenty-nine, in 1223; his sons being minors, the rule passed to his sister Rusudan. Under Rusudan, who had neither her mother's administrative skill nor her other gifts, flattering courtiers unworthy of their ranks were put into the public, political and military offices, and the country began to decline; only in the south-west, in the region of Akhaltsikhe, did the brothers Salva and John govern wisely.

In those years the shah of Persia, Jalal ad-Din, driven out of his own kingdom by the Mongol hordes of Genghis Khan, moved towards Armenia with his family, his treasure, a great multitude and a large army. After laying waste the south-eastern regions of Armenia he attacked the rich cities of Dvin and Ani, held by the princes Mkhargrdzeli; and only when those cities had been put to fire and sword did Rusudan resolve, in 1225, to send troops against him under the atabek. The armies met in the region of Garnisi, near the Armenian border, and Salva and his brother led the regiments that were to strike first, and at the word they went at the enemy like lions.

The atabek did not support them. Doxologia says the reason is a puzzle to historians, who set it down variously to cowardice, to envy of Salva's courage, or to a wish to revenge himself on Rusudan, with whom he had a personal quarrel. Left unaided at the hardest hour of the battle, the brothers gave way before the enemy's numbers. John fell dead, and Salva's wounded horse threw him to the ground; he stood on foot without a helmet and with a broken sword, and the Persians fell on him and took him captive.

Brought before Jalal ad-Din and pressed with promises and with threats, he answered that he confessed only the faith in the one consubstantial Trinity and nothing else; that earthly goods did not attract him and torments did not frighten him; that of glory he desired only the glory of heaven, and of treasure only the eternal blessedness of the kingdom; and that the shah had power over his body but none over his soul. Soon he could no longer whisper a word, for his bones were breaking under the blows, and he was half dead when Jalal ad-Din ordered him thrown into a wretched cell. Shut up there, in the year 1227, he gave his hallowed soul into the hands of the Lord, having kept the faith bravely to the end.
