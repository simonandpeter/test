# John (Khrenov)

He was born on 3 September 1888 in Moscow, the son of Semyon Khrenov, an employee of the Taganka prison. The family was very religious, and from early childhood he went to pray at the Novospassky monastery near the house where they lived; in time he sang in the choir with the monks and read at some of the services. He finished a pedagogical institute in 1907 but did not become a teacher: he felt no calling to it and found that he had great difficulty explaining the lessons to children. He took a post as a bookkeeper in the Taganka prison, and then the hieroschemamonk Aristoklius, whom he knew well, helped him to a bookkeeper's place in the Moscow Merchants' Bank.

In 1911 he married a girl named Eudocia, from a devout family and a parishioner of the Novospassky monastery, where they had met. After the banks were nationalised in 1917 he went on keeping books, in the city's state institutions. When the Novospassky monastery was closed he began, like the rest of its parishioners, to go to the nearby church of the Forty Martyrs, and there he was offered the diaconate. It was a serious choice, and he went to the church of St Nicholas on Maroseyka to ask the advice and the blessing of the archpriest Alexis Mechev. Father Alexis blessed it, and in 1921 bishop Hilarion (Troitsky) of Vereya ordained him deacon to the church of the Forty Martyrs.

There was still a Sunday school at the church then and he was put in charge of it. He served there about a year; quarrels began in the church and he left it, and then served for a time in the Trinity church at Kotelniki, keeping his books all the while. His household was his wife and his daughter, and the page says it was a close one: their free time on weekdays went to spiritual reading, and the lives of the saints above all, read day by day; on Sundays and feasts, and whenever else they could, they went to church.

Early in the nineteen-thirties he was called to the OGPU and told that he was a good worker, that they had checked him, and that they were offering him work as an agent reporting on everyone he worked with. He answered that he could not do it, because he bore the rank of a deacon. Take the rank off yourself, they said. God gave it, he answered, and God will take it off me. They did not press him further, and one of them said that they could do with more such steadfast people.

In 1937 somebody reported that a great many clergy came to his flat, and they came to search it and arrest him. In the search they found the certificate of his ordination to the diaconate — one more charge, one of the searchers said with satisfaction. Under interrogation he confirmed that he still bore the rank of deacon and did not wish to take it off himself, and on the counter-revolutionary activity he was charged with he admitted no guilt. He was arrested with [Demetrius (Dobroserdov)](/saints/demetrius-dobroserdov), archbishop of Mozhaysk, and seven others; the NKVD troika sentenced them on 17 October 1937, and on 21 October he was shot at the Butovo range outside Moscow and buried in a common unmarked grave. He was numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia in August 2000.
