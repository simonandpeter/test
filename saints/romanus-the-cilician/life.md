# Romanus the Cilician

The venerable Romanus came from Cilicia, and saint.gr names the town: Rhosos, an ancient city of Syria on the Issic gulf, the gulf of Alexandretta, which lies near the Cilician Gates. His own spiritual struggle, though, was made in the country around Antioch. There, at the foot of a mountain, he built himself a small cell which served him at once as a study and as a hermitage. He was temperate by temper and plain in his manners, the page says, full of humility and gentleness.

The fame of his holiness and of his good sense brought crowds to him, who showed him their esteem and their reverence; but he never for a moment felt the wind of pride. He did not look at how far he had come on, only at how slow he was in coming on; and he would often repeat the word of the Gospel, that whoever thinks he is standing firm in spiritual things had better take care that he does not fall.

God endowed him also with the gift of healing diseases. But he, to keep his feet on the ground, would say again the word of the Gospel, that working miracles is nothing at all: the great thing is to labour at virtue and at justice. After a life truly holy in that way, the venerable Romanus died peacefully. No year is printed for him, and no hymn.

The Russian calendar keeps him on a day of its own, 27 ноября, and the dossier is the same man's. days.pravoslavie.ru has him born in the city of Рос — the Rhosos the Greek page names — and ascetising in the country round Antioch, which is where the Greek page puts his whole struggle; and like it, it gives him the gift of healing. What it adds is a second gift, foresight; the particular mercy he was known for, that by his prayers the Lord gave many barren women the joy of motherhood; and the shape of his asceticism, which the Greek page only calls plain. He was a strict faster, he wore heavy chains under a hair shirt, and he lived many years shut in without lighting a fire. Having reached deep old age he departed to the Lord in peace.

Two calendars and two days, then, for one man: the Greek on 9 February under «Όσιος Ρωμανός ο Κίλικας ο θαυματουργός», this one on 27 November under «Прп. Романа чудотворца», whose name page is headed «Роман Антиохийский (Сирийский), отшельник» and gives that one day. Rhosos and Antioch are in both, the wonderworking is in both, and nothing read puts a second Romanus in either place. The Roman numeral this calendar closes him with, V, is the only dating either calendar supplies.
