# Nicetas of Chalcedon

Nicetas loved Christ from his youth. He renounced the world and all that is in it and, having pleased God by a life of virtue, was raised to the episcopal throne of Chalcedon. As archbishop, doxologia's life says, he stood like a lamp on its stand, giving light to the world and adorning the Church of Christ. He was very merciful to the poor: he fed the hungry, clothed the naked, and took in strangers and gave them rest.

He was the father of orphans, the defender of widows and the deliverer of the wronged. When the heresy of the icon-fighters came in the reign of Leo the Armenian, Nicetas showed himself a confessor of Christ. He contended hard against the wrong belief, rebuking its impious dogmas and casting them off, and teaching and counselling men in right belief to venerate the icon of Christ, of the most pure Mother of God and of all the saints.

For this he suffered much from the ill-believing emperor and from those of one mind with him, and endured banishment, slander and torment. After many years and many labours of confession he passed over in peace to God. The life says that God glorified him in heaven before His angels and on earth before men, since at his honoured relics miracles were worked that gave healing of every sickness. It names the reign but prints no year, so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and says where its knowledge of him comes from. saint.gr says he is unknown to the synaxaria and to the Menaia, and that his name is found in four Lavra codices — Δ 39, Θ 87, Δ 36 and Ω 147 — with a service to him. From that service, the page says, we learn that he was bishop of Chalcedon between the years 726 and 775 and lived in the time of the iconoclasm; that he was shown a confessor for his struggles on behalf of the holy icons, together with two other confessors who are unknown and were his kinsmen, Nicetas and Ignatius; and that he may even have resigned the episcopal throne of Chalcedon and withdrawn to some monastery of Palestine to give himself wholly to ascetic labours. He reposed in peace. The page closes with the resort of Ai-Nikitas in the north-west of Lefkada, which owes him its name: an icon of a saint dressed as a bishop and inscribed «Άγιος Νικήτας» was found there among the rocks, and the metropolitan of the island, who knew of no bishop Nicetas but only a martyr, wrote to the monasteries of the Holy Mountain, where the fuller lists of the saints' names are kept, and learned from them that it was this archbishop of Chalcedon.
