# Nicodemus of Vatopedi

saint.gr says the venerable Nicodemus, Nikephoros in the world, lived the ascetic life in a cell near the monastery of Vatopedi at the beginning of the fourteenth century. He came from the hill of Auxentius, which lay near Chalcedon in Asia Minor and had been a famous monastic centre since the fifth century; the raids of the Turks brought him to the Holy Mountain, and he settled beside Vatopedi. There [Gregory Palamas](/saints/gregory-palamas) found him at his ascesis, and because of the greatness of his virtue wished to put himself under him.

All of the page's information, it says in so many words, comes from Philotheos Kokkinos, who wrote Gregory's life. Philotheos has Gregory come to the lavra of Vatopedi and attach himself to «Νικοδήμω… τω γενναίω, ανδρί θαυμαστώ κατά τε πράξιν και θεωρίαν» — Nicodemus the valiant, a man wonderful in action and in contemplation, as all who dwelt on Athos but a few knew — who had first practised every way of virtue to the utmost on the hill of Auxentius, and had afterwards come to Vatopedi and carried the same contests of virtue through for a long time; and there, says Philotheos, Gregory came to him where he was living in quiet, was tonsured by him, and gave himself over to complete obedience.

Beside this God-bearing elder, the page says, Gregory kept perfect obedience and subjection, continual fasting and vigil, unceasing prayer, a warm love for the Mother of God, and high spiritual states, into which the contemplative and discerning Nicodemus led him and in which he kept him. It was in this period that, after an appearance of John the Theologian, Gregory was shown to repeat in his prayer the words «Φώτισόν μου το σκότος» — enlighten my darkness.

Every biographer of Gregory Palamas, the page adds, speaks of his elder Nicodemus and calls him a valiant and wonderful monk in action and in contemplation, known throughout the whole Holy Mountain for his virtue, and says that he lived as a hesychast and was a great elder. Philotheos gives his end: Gregory was in his third year since his renunciation when Nicodemus, his teacher and father, full of days both divine and human, departed gloriously and truly blessedly to the Lord; so Gregory remained with him three years, which the page dates 1319 to 1322. His skull is kept in the monastery of Vatopedi, and a service for him was composed by the hymnographer Charalampis Bousias.
