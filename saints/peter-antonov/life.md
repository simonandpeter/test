# Peter (Antonov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him twenty-second of twenty-four and gives no life; the life here is the one azbyka.ru prints for the civil 2 декабря, out of pstgu.ru. Pyotr Ivanovich Antonov was born in 1872 in the stanitsa of Kamenskaya in the Donetsk region. Before the revolution he was an officer in the tsar’s army, and after it he chose the service of the Church in the office of psalmist. In 1923 he was charged with religious propaganda and sentenced to a year of deprivation of liberty.

He was arrested again in 1930, and for anti-Soviet propaganda was sentenced to ten years of deprivation of liberty and five of loss of rights. After five years of it, in 1935, he tried to escape, and a fresh sentence followed: ten years of camps over again. He was taken under escort to Karlag in Kazakhstan. On 21 November 1937 he was arrested once more and a new case opened, which charged him with spreading provocative rumours and predictions of his own, from a religious point of view, of the imminent fall of the Soviet power. The indictment records that he did not admit the charge but did not himself deny taking part in the gatherings at which he spoke of his «видениях».

On 17 December 1937 the troika of the NKVD sentenced him to the highest measure of punishment, and the sentence was carried out on 7 March 1938. That is more than three months after the day the Sretensky calendar keeps him on and after the year its line gives the whole company, and the folder’s `dates` carry both readings rather than choosing between them.
