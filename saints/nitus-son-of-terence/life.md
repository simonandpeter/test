# Nitus, son of Terence

Nitus was one of the seven sons of the martyrs [Terence](/saints/terence-husband-of-neonilla) and [Neonilla](/saints/neonilla-wife-of-terence), whom doxologia.ro's life for 28 October names one by one: Sarvil, Fota, Teodul, Vila, Ierax, Nitul and Eunichie. His brothers were [Sarbelus](/saints/sarbelus-son-of-terence), [Photus](/saints/photus-son-of-terence), [Theodulus](/saints/theodulus-son-of-terence), [Vilas](/saints/vilas-son-of-terence), [Hierax](/saints/hierax-son-of-terence) and [Eunicius](/saints/eunicius-son-of-terence). Their father was a right-believing Christian and married Neonilla, who held the same faith, in lawful marriage, and the two brought up all seven sons in the right faith. The life tells the family's passion as one story and says nothing of the sons apart from it.

All of them were taken by the pagans, parents and children alike, and brought before a lawless court, where they confessed Christ and reviled the idols. For this they were tortured beyond measure and then hung up; their wounds were sprinkled with vinegar and burned with fire, and while they were being tortured the saints prayed quietly for one another and comforted one another.

God did not overlook their prayers, but sent his holy angels, who loosed them from their bonds and healed their wounds. The pagans, seeing them loosed and whole, were terrified, and gave them to the beasts to be eaten; but they suffered no harm, because at God's command the beasts changed their cruelty for the gentleness of sheep. Then they were thrown into a cauldron of boiling pitch, and at once the fire went out and the pitch grew cold.

Seeing that the tortures would not touch them, the pagans cut off their heads with the sword, and so they came to their end. Nitus was beheaded with his father, his mother and his six brothers, and the Romanian calendar keeps them all on 28 October.

The Greek calendar keeps the whole household on the same day, 28 October, and saint.gr's notice is about the family rather than about any one of them. It calls them a true «κατ᾿ οἶκον ἐκκλησία», a church in the house, citing 1 Corinthians 16.19: a Christian family with one soul, one heart and one mind. When the persecution began, word came to the parents that they were in danger of arrest, and they asked themselves whether to go far away to protect their children or to stay and await martyrdom bravely. The five sons and the two daughters gave the decisive answer: why flee, when the persecution had spread everywhere, and when their going would sow panic among the Christians there? The Church, they said, is not strengthened by fugitives but by martyrs. So all of them stayed, confessed Christ, and died together by beheading.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps the household on the same day and tells it in one line — «Мчч. Терентия и Неониллы и чад их» and then the seven children by name — with a separate name link behind each of the nine. This one reads «Нит Сирийский, мч.», which adds to the bare forename the province neither the Romanian nor the Greek page names: Syria. That line also carries the year the other two calendars left out, «ок. 249-250», and it is the household's year and not this one person's. The day page prints two hymns for them, a common of martyrs under their name and a kontakion of their own that calls on Terence the most wise and his consort to heal the sicknesses of our souls.
