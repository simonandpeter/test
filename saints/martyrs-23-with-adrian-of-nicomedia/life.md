# The Twenty-three Martyrs with Adrian of Nicomedia

These were the twenty-three Christians hiding in a cave near Nicomedia who, on a denunciation, were taken under Maximian (305–311), beaten with ox-sinews and rods, tortured and pressed to worship the idols, and brought to the court to have their names and answers written down — where [Adrian](/saints/adrian-of-nicomedia), the head of the court, watching their courage, asked what reward they expected from their God for their torments, and on their answer had his own name written with theirs. They were imprisoned with him, served in prison by [Natalia](/saints/natalia-of-nicomedia), and when the emperor ordered an anvil brought in and their legs and arms broken with a hammer they died with Adrian in the heaviest torments; Natalia carried their relics to Constantinople and buried them there with honour. The Russian calendar counts them with Adrian and Natalia on 26 August (Julian), and the Prologue tells their story with the couple’s.
