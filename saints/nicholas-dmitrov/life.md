# Nicholas (Dmitrov)

He was born on 14 May 1878 in the village of Kuntsevo in the Moscow province, the son of a priest, Lavr Dmitrov, a Bulgarian by descent. After the Moscow theological seminary he worked as a schoolmaster in Kuntsevo, and in 1909 he was ordained in the church of Christ the Saviour in Moscow to the church of the village of Zavidovo in the Tver diocese. His parishioners came to love him for his kindness, for his readiness to answer them and for the exactness with which he kept his pastoral duties.

When under the Bolsheviks he no longer had a horse, he would set out on foot in any weather to walk twenty-five kilometres to a far village to give communion to a sick man. He was not a rich man and had a large family, and still he never refused anyone help: where he could not help with money he helped with the field work or with mending a house. When beggars came he always took them in and sat them at his table, and his children learned the same love for the poor, running out to them with whatever they could find in the house.

In the persecution after the revolution he had to sell his house and buy a hovel, to keep his family on the difference and to pay the authorities, who each year demanded larger taxes of him. They did not dare arrest him, so great was the respect and love of the parish for him, but for several years they tried to talk him into leaving the church and giving up his priesthood, promising to lift the taxes in exchange; and to all of it he answered always the same words: «I shall never leave the church and never take off the priesthood».

In 1930 they arrested the second priest of Zavidovo, the archpriest [Gregory Raevsky](/saints/gregory-raevsky), and demanded false witness from him against his brother priest; he refused, saying only that the man had done nothing against the Soviet power. He understood then that he too would be arrested, and was ready for it. In 1932 he was ordered to saw and deliver a hundred and fifty cubic metres of firewood in three months, his children being gone by then and no one left to help him; he appealed, and the local authorities refused but halved the quota. In 1933, when he could no longer pay the taxes, he was arrested and sentenced to a year in a labour camp.

He came back to serve in the church, under threats of a new arrest, and on 8 February 1938 he was taken again, at night, while he was gravely ill. Ill as he was, his spirit was up: he told his wife not to lose heart, not to fall away from the church or the faith, and never to give in on any terms. He was charged with counter-revolutionary activity and did not admit his guilt. On 6 March an NKVD troika sentenced him to be shot, and on 23 февраля — 8 March by the new style — in 1938 he was shot in the prison at Tver and buried in a common grave in one of the city's cemeteries; the exact place is not known.

In 1956 his widow petitioned for his rehabilitation. The authorities called in the peasants of Zavidovo for questioning, and all of them testified to their faces that the priest had great authority among them and was loved, and had carried on no agitation against the state; on that evidence, from believers and unbelievers alike, the authorities declared him innocent that same year. He was canonised as a local saint of the Tver diocese on 6 September 1999, old style, and numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia for veneration throughout the church at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
