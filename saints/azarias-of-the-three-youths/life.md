# Azarias, one of the Three Youths

doxologia.ro prints one long life for the three youths and [Daniel](/saints/daniel-the-prophet) together, and Azarias is named in it second of the three. He was of the sons of Judah and of royal family, one of the children Nebuchadnezzar had brought to the palace out of the captivity to be taught letters and the Chaldean tongue and fed three years from the king's own table; and the chief of the eunuchs renamed him Abednego, as he called [Ananias](/saints/ananias-of-the-three-youths) Shadrach, [Misael](/saints/misael-of-the-three-youths) Meshach, and Daniel Belteshazzar.

With the other three he refused the king's meat and wine so as not to be defiled, asking for the seeds of the earth and water instead; and after ten days their faces were fairer than those of the children fed from the king's table. God gave the four sharpness and wisdom, and the king found them ten times wiser than all the enchanters and sorcerers in his kingdom.

In the eighteenth year the king set up an image of gold sixty cubits high in the plain of Dura and summoned all the lords of his dominions to its unveiling, and a furnace was built there for whoever should disobey. All the peoples fell down and worshipped when the music sounded, but Chaldean men accused Ananias, Azarias and Misael of not worshipping the king's gods or his image; and the three answered that their God was in the heavens and able to bring them out of the burning furnace, and that they would not serve his gods nor worship the golden image. The king had the furnace heated seven times over and strong men bind them and throw them in.

The fire that came out of the furnace burnt their accusers, while the three walked bound in the middle of the flame praising God; and though the servants kept feeding the fire with brimstone, pitch, tow and vine-shoots until the flame rose some forty cubits and burnt the Chaldeans standing round, the angel of the Lord came down — the life says, to those who were with Azarias in the furnace — and blew the flame away from them like a breath of dew, so that the fire did not touch them at all.

Then they praised and blessed and glorified God, saying: blessed are you, O Lord, God of our fathers, most praised and most exalted for ever; and the rest of the songs written in the book of Daniel. Nebuchadnezzar, hearing them sing, asked his lords whether he had not thrown three bound men into the fire, and said that he saw four men loosed and unhurt, and that the form of the fourth was like the Son of God; and he called them out by their Chaldean names from the mouth of the furnace.

They came out, and all who were gathered saw that the fire had not overcome their bodies, that the hair of their heads was not burnt and that there was no smell of fire on them; and the king bowed to God before them, blessed the God who had sent his angel, forbade any blasphemy against him on pain of destruction, and magnified the three youths before all and set them over all the Jews in his kingdom.

They came with Daniel to a great old age. The life says, citing Cyril of Alexandria and others, that a king named Attic afterwards questioned them about their faith and, reproached for his ungodliness, had their heads struck off; and that Azarias spread out his garment and received the head of Ananias, and that his own head was received by Misael, and Misael's by Daniel. The heads are said to have joined themselves to their bodies again, and the angel of the Lord to have carried the bodies to Mount Gebal and laid them under a stone; and four hundred years later, at the Resurrection of Christ, these also rose and appeared to many and fell asleep again. doxologia.ro gives no year for him, and this folder gives none.
