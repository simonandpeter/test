# Attalus of Niculițel

The Syriac martyrology and that of Blessed Jerome, of the third and fourth centuries, remember — beside twenty-five other martyrs — four who came from the east of Europe, and name them Zoticus, Attalus, Camasis and Philippus. They suffered for Christ under the reign of Diocletian and Maximian. It is believed that they were soldiers in the Roman army, and that for their faith in Christ they were condemned to die by the sword.

Neither the date of their death nor the place of their martyrdom is known exactly. Some hold that they were banished to the mouths of the Danube, in the north of what is today Dobrogea, where many Christians of the Roman Empire who would not sacrifice to the idols were sent into exile. Tradition says that these holy soldier-martyrs were tried by the Roman authorities in the city of Noviodunum, in Dobrogea as well.

In the fourth and fifth centuries a stone basilica was raised at Niculițel, in what is now the county of Tulcea, dedicated to Saint Athanasius the Great and renewed in the thirteenth century; the relics of these martyrs were laid in it. In September 1971, by God's will, heavy rains made the Niculițel brook flood and brought to light a crypt beneath the old fourth-century foundation of the church, and in it, in one common coffin, the relics of the four.

On the inner wall of the crypt is written, in Greek, «Martyres tou Hristou»; on the wall opposite stand their names, «Zotikos, Attalos, Kamasis și Filippos». This martyrs' crypt, the calendar says, is unique in Romania and in south-eastern Europe. The relics were laid in four wooden reliquaries and set out for veneration in the church of the nearby Monastery of Cocoș, where they are kept to this day, their feast being 4 June.

Of Attalus himself the page says nothing apart from his name, which stands second among the four on the calendar's line and second among the four on the crypt wall. His companions are [Zoticus](/saints/zoticus-of-niculitel), [Camasis](/saints/camasis-of-niculitel) and [Philippus](/saints/philippus-of-niculitel).

The Greek calendar keeps the four on the same day, 4 June, and saint.gr is franker than the Romanian source about how little is known of when they died. Some researchers, it says, hold that the holy martyrs Attalus, Zoticus, Camasis and Philippus suffered in the time of the emperor Diocletian, who reigned from 284 to 305; others say they contended between the years 319 and 324, when Licinius was emperor; but the likeliest thing is that these holy martyrs were perfected north of the Danube, where the Goths then lived, to whom [Sava](/saints/sava-of-buzau) of Buzău spread the Gospel, and that the persecution which king Athanaric let loose against the Christians in the years 370 to 372 may have brought about their martyr's death.

The holy relics of the saints, the page adds, were discovered in the crypt of a basilica in the region of Niculițel, in the province of Tulcea in Romania, in 1971; and today they are at the monastery of Cocoș in Tulcea, which it states again, separately, of each of the four. No year is recorded in this folder on the strength of the three datings, because they do not agree; what the page gives with confidence is the place the relics were found and the house that keeps them.
