# Proclus of Constantinople

Proclus was a disciple of [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom), and his service in the patriarch's household was to remind him of those who came to see him and to bring them in. A man of rank in Constantinople had been slandered to the emperor Arcadius, driven from the palace and stripped of his office, and he asked through a go-between that the patriarch let him come by night, since he feared to be seen by day and slandered the worse for it. John told Proclus to remind him of the man at night and bring him.

The man came, and Proclus went to the door of the patriarch's room and looked through a crack. He saw John sitting and writing, and a stranger bent a little behind him with his mouth at the patriarch's right ear, speaking. In face the stranger was like the prophet Elisha, bald, with a great plaited beard. Proclus wanted to hear what was said and could not; he went back and told the man to wait, since another who had come before him was with the patriarch and he could bring nobody in until that one came out.

He was frightened, because nobody reached the patriarch except by him, and there was no other door. Twice more he looked, and twice more saw the same; and when the bell went for Matins he sent the man away, telling him that in the hours of night prayer the patriarch's whole mind was on prayer and he spoke with God alone until daylight. The next night went the same way, and the man went home weeping. So Proclus vowed not to eat or drink or sleep or leave that door until he had brought the man in and learned who was entering.

On the third night he told the man he would go in and announce him — and saw the stranger again at the patriarch's ear, and said only that the one who spoke with the patriarch must be sent by God, since he came in unseen and nobody had passed those doors. In the morning John himself remembered the man and asked why he had not been brought. Proclus said he had not dared, because the patriarch was speaking with another. *With whom did I speak? Nobody has been with me these nights.*

Then Proclus described the face and the stooping and the whispering, and looking up at the icon of the Apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) which John kept on the wall of his cell, he said: this is the very one I saw speaking with you, father, for he is greatly like him. So they knew that the Apostle had appeared — one of them that he was secretly taught by Paul as he wrote, the other that he had been counted worthy to see a citizen of heaven. John gave the wronged man his help, and Proclus grew in virtue under his teacher's guidance.

After Chrysostom's exile and repose, Proclus was chosen bishop of Cyzicus by Sisinnius, patriarch of the city of Constantine; but when he went to his see the clergy there would not receive him, reckoning him a heretic, so he returned to Constantinople and spent a year. Then the patriarch died, and while his relics were still in the church Proclus was chosen patriarch of the city, and was consecrated on Great and Holy Thursday of the Lord's saving Passion.

Having taken the throne he shepherded the flock of Christ well, and he counselled the emperor Theodosius, son of Arcadius, to bring the relics of John Chrysostom from Comana to Constantinople. In his patriarchate there was a great earthquake that went on six months, so that great stone buildings, churches and palaces fell, villages and small towns were swallowed, islands perished, springs and rivers dried up and water broke out in dry places; it was everywhere, but worst in Bithynia, the Hellespont and Phrygia.

When the strongest and fairest buildings of Constantinople were falling, the emperor Theodosius with his sister [Pulcheria](/saints/pulcheria-the-empress), with Proclus and with all the people went out of the city and walked the fields, praying with tears that God would be merciful to his people. Then a small child was caught up out of the middle of the crowd and carried into the height, all watching until they could see him no longer.

After an hour he came down the same way, and testified before the emperor, the patriarch and all the people that he had heard angels in heaven singing: *Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us.* The people took up that thrice-holy song and the earthquake ceased. The child gave up his soul at once into God's hands and was buried with honour in the church of Saint Irene, and from that time the thrice-holy hymn began to be sung throughout the Christian world. Proclus sat on the patriarchal throne twenty years and five months, and departed to the Lord in peace.
