# Proclus of Constantinople

Proclus was a disciple of [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom), and his service in the patriarch's household was to remind him of those who came to see him and to bring them in. A man of rank in Constantinople had been slandered to the emperor Arcadius, driven from the palace and stripped of his office, and he asked through a go-between that the patriarch let him come by night, since he feared to be seen by day and slandered the worse for it. John told Proclus to remind him of the man at night and bring him.

The man came, and Proclus went to the door of the patriarch's room and looked through a crack. He saw John sitting and writing, and a stranger bent a little behind him with his mouth at the patriarch's right ear, speaking. In face the stranger was like the prophet Elisha, bald, with a great plaited beard. Proclus wanted to hear what was said and could not; he went back and told the man to wait, since another who had come before him was with the patriarch and he could bring nobody in until that one came out.

He was frightened, because nobody reached the patriarch except by him, and there was no other door. Twice more he looked, and twice more saw the same; and when the bell went for Matins he sent the man away, telling him that in the hours of night prayer the patriarch's whole mind was on prayer and he spoke with God alone until daylight. The next night went the same way, and the man went home weeping. So Proclus vowed not to eat or drink or sleep or leave that door until he had brought the man in and learned who was entering.

On the third night he told the man he would go in and announce him — and saw the stranger again at the patriarch's ear, and said only that the one who spoke with the patriarch must be sent by God, since he came in unseen and nobody had passed those doors. In the morning John himself remembered the man and asked why he had not been brought. Proclus said he had not dared, because the patriarch was speaking with another. *With whom did I speak? Nobody has been with me these nights.*

Then Proclus described the face and the stooping and the whispering, and looking up at the icon of the Apostle [Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) which John kept on the wall of his cell, he said: this is the very one I saw speaking with you, father, for he is greatly like him. So they knew that the Apostle had appeared — one of them that he was secretly taught by Paul as he wrote, the other that he had been counted worthy to see a citizen of heaven. John gave the wronged man his help, and Proclus grew in virtue under his teacher's guidance.

After Chrysostom's exile and repose, Proclus was chosen bishop of Cyzicus by Sisinnius, patriarch of the city of Constantine; but when he went to his see the clergy there would not receive him, reckoning him a heretic, so he returned to Constantinople and spent a year. Then the patriarch died, and while his relics were still in the church Proclus was chosen patriarch of the city, and was consecrated on Great and Holy Thursday of the Lord's saving Passion.

Having taken the throne he shepherded the flock of Christ well, and he counselled the emperor Theodosius, son of Arcadius, to bring the relics of John Chrysostom from Comana to Constantinople. In his patriarchate there was a great earthquake that went on six months, so that great stone buildings, churches and palaces fell, villages and small towns were swallowed, islands perished, springs and rivers dried up and water broke out in dry places; it was everywhere, but worst in Bithynia, the Hellespont and Phrygia.

When the strongest and fairest buildings of Constantinople were falling, the emperor Theodosius with his sister [Pulcheria](/saints/pulcheria-the-empress), with Proclus and with all the people went out of the city and walked the fields, praying with tears that God would be merciful to his people. Then a small child was caught up out of the middle of the crowd and carried into the height, all watching until they could see him no longer.

After an hour he came down the same way, and testified before the emperor, the patriarch and all the people that he had heard angels in heaven singing: *Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us.* The people took up that thrice-holy song and the earthquake ceased. The child gave up his soul at once into God's hands and was buried with honour in the church of Saint Irene, and from that time the thrice-holy hymn began to be sung throughout the Christian world. Proclus sat on the patriarchal throne twenty years and five months, and departed to the Lord in peace.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 20 November, and sets out his career. He was a worthy disciple of [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom), saint.gr says; the patriarch [Atticus](/saints/atticus-of-constantinople) made him a deacon and then a presbyter, and because he stood out for his learning, his virtue and his gift for teaching he was loved by all his unforgettable master's admirers and put forward by them for the patriarchal throne — though the opposition of a corrupt establishment wrecked the candidacy. In 425 the patriarch Sisinnius ordained him bishop of Cyzicus, but because of the disorders of that province one Dalmatius took the see, and Proclus, without being distressed by it, went on preaching the divine word at Constantinople. When the patriarch Maximian died in 434, under the emperor Theodosius II, Proclus was raised to the throne in triumph, before Maximian was even buried. His first care was to bring Chrysostom's relics from Comana to Constantinople; and it was under him that the Trisagion was established in the churches. He was patriarch twelve years and three months and fell asleep in 447. A fragment of his relics, the page adds, is at the monastery of Kykkos in Cyprus.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 20 November on a line of his own, «Свт. Прокла, архиеп. Константинопольского (446-447).», and its life page gives the career in full. From his youth he gave all his time to prayer and the study of Scripture; he was the disciple of [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom), who ordained him first deacon and then presbyter, and he was a witness of the appearing of [the apostle Paul](/saints/paul-the-apostle) to his teacher. After Chrysostom’s exile and death he was ordained bishop of the city of Cyzicus by the patriarch [Sisinnius](/saints/sisinnius-of-constantinople) — whose years the page gives as «(426 - 427)».

But the flock at Cyzicus, under the influence of the Nestorian heretics, drove their hierarch out; so he returned to the capital and preached in the churches of Constantinople until, at Sisinnius’s death, he was raised archbishop, and he governed the Church twelve years, «(434 - 447)». By his efforts the relics of his teacher were brought from Comana to Constantinople under the emperor Theodosius II.

**The calendar makes him the patriarch under whom the Trisagion got its last words.** During his patriarchate a destructive earthquake fell on the empire and went on for months: in Bithynia, the Hellespont and Phrygia cities collapsed, rivers vanished from the face of the earth, and ruinous floods rose in places that had been waterless. The people of Constantinople, with their patriarch and their emperor at their head, went out of the city and held services of supplication.

At one of them a boy was lifted out of the crowd into the air by an invisible power and carried up to a height where the human eye could no longer see him. Then he was set down on the earth whole and unhurt, and told that up there he had heard and seen the angels glorifying God and singing «Святый Боже, Святый Крепкий, Святый Бессмертный». The whole people sang the thrice-holy prayer, adding to it «помилуй нас», and the earthquake stopped; and the page says the Orthodox Church sings that prayer at the services to this day.

The flock loved him for his ascetic life, for his care of the destitute and for his preaching. Many of his writings have come down, the best known being two words in praise of the Mother of God directed against the Nestorians and four words on the Nativity of Christ setting out the Orthodox teaching of the Incarnation of the Son of God; and the page adds that his work in setting good order in all church affairs won universal respect. Surrounded by love and honour, he departed to the Lord in advanced age, and the page closes «(+ 446 - 447)» — which is the disjunction the line prints too, and the reason this folder’s year is no longer flat.

The Охридски пролог makes him a disciple of Saint [John Chrysostom](/saints/john-chrysostom), consecrated bishop of Cyzicus in the year 426 and elected patriarch of Constantinople in 435, who governed the Church of God as a wise hierarch. Two notable events, it says, happened in his time. The first was the translation of the relics of John Chrysostom from Comana to Constantinople, by the wish of the emperor and of the patriarch; and the emperor reigning then was [Theodosius the Younger](/saints/theodosius-the-younger), with his sister [Pulcheria](/saints/pulcheria-the-empress).

The second was a great earthquake at Constantinople and in the country about it, in which many of the finest and largest buildings fell down. The patriarch then went out in a litany with the emperor and a multitude of clergy, nobles and people; and while they prayed to God in the litany it happened that a child was taken up wonderfully high into the air, so high that at last it could not be followed with the eyes. Then it came back and was set down gently on the ground. Asked where it had been, the child answered that it had been carried to heaven among the angels, and that it had heard the angels singing: Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us.

Hearing that song, all the people in the litany began to sing it, and the earthquake ceased at once; and from then on that wonderful hymn became established in the Church. The child itself reposed soon afterwards and was buried in the church of Saint Irene. Saint Proclus served as a hierarch for twenty years and ended peacefully in the Lord in the year 446 — the earlier of the two years this folder already holds from two calendars, and the interval is left open rather than closed on a third book's choice.
