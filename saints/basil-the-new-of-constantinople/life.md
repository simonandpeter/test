# Basil the New of Constantinople

The Ohrid Prologue's entry for the Julian 26 March is the whole of what this folder records. Basil lived at first in a forest, without roof or hearth. When they caught him and asked who he was, he answered that he was one of the living upon the earth. They suspected him of being some kind of spy, and tormented him much for it. At the last, free, he lived many years at Constantinople, where he saw into all the secrets of men, foretold what was to come, and worked great wonders.

His disciple was the eldress Theodora; and when she died she appeared to another of his disciples and described to him the twenty toll-houses through which every soul must pass. Saint Basil reposed in peace on 25 March 944 and passed over into the glorious dwellings of heaven, and after his death he was seen in great glory in the heavens by a citizen of Constantinople. The entry heads him on 26 March and dates his repose to the day before, and this folder records both as they stand.

One caution belongs here, and it is the mirror of one already written on another folder. The Greek calendar keeps a venerable Basil the New on this same menologion day who is a different man — an ascetic of Mount Latros in Caria, identified there only by that mountain and by his brother — and nothing from that page was taken into this folder, or from this entry into that one. The Prologue prints no hymn for him, or for any saint of the day.

The Russian calendar's reading is much the fuller of the two and fills in the frame the Prologue only sketches. It names the court official who had him seized — the patrician Samon, who put him to the question — and says that when he would answer nothing but that he was a sojourner and a stranger upon the earth, Samon had him hung head downward with his hands and feet bound behind him, and that he came down after three days of it alive and unhurt. Samon called the wonder sorcery and threw him to a starving lion, which would not touch him and lay down quietly at his feet; and when he was ordered drowned in the sea, two dolphins carried him ashore at Eudomon outside Constantinople.

He went into the city, healed a man lying at the Golden Gate in a fever by the name of the Saviour, and at that man's asking stayed and lodged with him; and people came in numbers for his counsel and for healing by his prayers. Among them was the disciple who afterwards wrote the long life of him, and whom he rebuked in a dream for keeping a costly belt he had found, showing him a broken pot and telling him that a man who steals even so worthless a thing is punished fourfold and must give back what he has found. It was to that same disciple, at his repeated asking, that the eldress who had served the saint appeared in sleep and told how her soul had passed the toll-houses, and how the saint's prayers had carried her through them; the calendar keeps her on 30 декабря. The Church calls him the New, it says, to tell him from the ascetics of the same name who lived before him.
