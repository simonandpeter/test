# Claudius (18 December)

saint.gr's calendar for 18 December keeps ten people together and names every one of them: Nikostratos and his wife Zoe, Tranquillinus and his wife Marcia, Claudius, Tiburtius, Castor, Castulus, Marcus and Marcellinus. They all suffered, the page says, in the persecution Diocletian set in motion against the Christians, whose years it gives as 284 to 304, and they suffered together with Saint [Sebastian](/saints/sebastian-of-rome), whom the same day's calendar keeps.

Claudius and [Castor](/saints/castor-18-december) they drowned in the sea; the page's epigram for him says that though the deep held them, heaven bears them.

That is the whole of what the page gives for this company: the ten names, the one reign, and for each of them the manner of the death. It names no city and no judge, gives no year of birth for any of them and no year inside the reign, lists no relic and prints no hymn of its own; the day's apolytikion stands on Sebastian's page and names Sebastian. The others of the ten here are [Nicostratus](/saints/nicostratus-18-december), [Tranquillinus](/saints/tranquillinus), [Marcia](/saints/marcia-wife-of-tranquillinus), [Tiburtius](/saints/tiburtius-18-december), [Castor](/saints/castor-18-december), [Castulus](/saints/castulus-18-december), [Marcus](/saints/marcus-son-of-tranquillinus), [Marcellinus](/saints/marcellinus-son-of-tranquillinus), and [Zoe](/saints/zoe-18-december), whom this corpus already kept from the Romanian calendar.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day, inside the heading „Свети мученик Севастијан и други с њим.“ — Sebastian and the others with him — and names him in the body. He was a commander, it says, and his two sons were sick; [Sebastian](/saints/sebastian-of-rome) healed them both, and brought Claudius and his house to baptism.

That is all it says of him, and it is worth saying what it does not. Of the others in this company the book names a death apiece — the river for Zoe, the sword for Tiburtius, the club for Nicostratus, the water for Tranquillinus, the spears for the two brothers. Of Claudius it names none. This calendar keeps him on the day with the rest, and the book that puts him there leaves his end unwritten.
