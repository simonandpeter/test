# Macarius of Kyiv

Macarius, saint.gr says, was abbot of the monastery of the Holy Trinity at Vilna. In 1495, after the death of Jonah, metropolitan of Kyiv, whose years the page gives as 1488 to 1494, Macarius was elected metropolitan of Kyiv in his place. He was martyred in the year 1497, in the village of Strigolovo on the river Vzhizha, when the Tatars came into Russia.

His incorrupt relics, the page says twice, are kept in the cathedral church of Saint Vladimir in Kyiv. That is the whole of the notice: no year of birth, no account of the martyrdom beyond the Tatar raid, no place of origin, and no hymn.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day and gives him two lives, which together name the house he came from, the bishops who consecrated him, the day of his death and where his relics lie. He had been archimandrite of the monastery of the Holy Trinity at Vilna. In 1495, after the death of the metropolitan of Kyiv Иона Глезна, he was chosen and consecrated in his place by a council of hierarchs — Вассиан of Vladimir, Лука of Polotsk, Вассиан of Turov and Иона of Lutsk — and letters of blessing were sent from Constantinople by the patriarch Нифонт confirming the election.

In 1497, obedient to his pastoral duty, he set out to visit the see of Kyiv, which had suffered worst from the Tatar incursions. On 1 May 1497 Tatars who had broken into the Russian land killed him in the village of Стриголово on the river Вжище while he was celebrating the services, and many of his flock were killed or carried off with him. His body was brought to Kyiv and laid in the cathedral church of the Holy Wisdom, where it was glorified by wonders in 1625 and 1634: candles were more than once lit in a marvellous manner before the iconostasis of the church where he lay, and light and fire went out from his tomb. The second life says his relics now rest in the cathedral of St Vladimir in Kyiv.
