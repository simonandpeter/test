# Parascheva of Iași

Doxologia’s page says that Parascheva lived on earth in the first half of the eleventh century, and that the first teaching about her in Romanian is in the «Cartea românească de învățătură» of [Varlaam](/saints/varlaam-of-moldavia), metropolitan of Moldavia, printed at Iași in 1643. She was born at Epivata on the shore of the Sea of Marmara, near Constantinople. Her parents were of good family and devout, and raised her in the fear of God and in fasting, prayer and almsgiving; a brother of hers became a monk under the name Eftimie and was chosen bishop of Madyta.

When she was ten, the page says, she was in a church of the Mother of God and heard the Gospel read at the Liturgy: whoever wishes to come after Me, let him deny himself, take up his cross and follow Me. She gave away her clothes to the poor then, and again afterwards, without minding her parents’ reproaches. Having inherited a great estate with her brother, she gave her portion to the poor and withdrew, in Varlaam’s words, into the deep of the wilderness.

She stopped first at Constantinople, to hear the teaching of monks and nuns of high spiritual life, and at their counsel left the capital for the country of Pontus. Five years she stayed at the monastery of the Mother of God at Heraclea, and from there went to the Holy Land, wishing to spend the rest of her life where the Saviour and the apostles had lived. Having seen Jerusalem, she settled in a convent in the desert of the Jordan, and rose there in fasting and in prayer.

Varlaam’s Cazania says that she now had no care «nici de veșminte și de așternuturi, nici de mâncări și de mese, nici de casă sau slujnice», but only for the purity of her soul and for the judgement to come. When she was about twenty-five an angel told her in a dream to go back to the place of her birth. She came to Epivata telling nobody who she was or where she came from, and added, the Cazania says, labour to labour and pain to pain, watering the ground with her tears and praying the Lord to send the gentle angel to take her soul in peace. So she gave up her soul into the rest of the heavenly Bridegroom.

She was buried as a stranger, and nobody knew who she was. A sailor died aboard ship and his body was thrown into the sea; the waves brought it ashore, and a hermit asked some Christians to bury it in Christian order. Digging the grave they found the body of Parascheva incorrupt and full of fragrance, and even so they laid the sailor’s foul body beside it. The next night one of the diggers, named Gheorghe, saw in a dream an empress seated on a shining throne among a host of angels, and an angel took him by the hand and asked why they had not regarded the body of Saint Parascheva, and whether they did not know that God had loved her beauty and willed to glorify her on earth.

The empress of the dream, who was Parascheva herself, told him to take her body up at once and lay it in a place of honour. A believing woman named Eftimia had the same vision on the same night, and the next day both told it to everyone. The faithful understood the sign, took the body out of the grave and carried it with great joy, «cu lumini și cu tămâie», into the church of the Holy Apostles at Epivata, where healings followed at once upon the prayers made beside the relics; and a church was built on the ground where her parents had lived and where she was born.

The oldest life of her, the page says, was written by the deacon Vasilisc at the request of the ecumenical patriarch Nicholas IV Mouzalon in the middle of the twelfth century; another by Euthymius, patriarch of Târnovo, in the second half of the fourteenth; and Matthew of Myra, who lived at the monastery of Dealu near Târgoviște, made a new life and the service of the saint in Greek about 1605. The relics stayed at Epivata some two hundred years, then were moved to Târnovo, where they remained a hundred and sixty years, then to Belgrade, and in 1521, when the Turks took that city, to Constantinople, the patriarch Jeremiah I having asked them of the sultan.

A hundred and twenty years later came the last removal, to Romanian ground. In 1641, after Vasile Lupu, prince of Moldavia, had paid all the debts of the Patriarchate of Constantinople, the patriarch Parthenius I and his Synod resolved to give him the relics in gratitude, «pentru sfințirea și binecuvântarea acelui loc al Bogdaniei», as their synodal letter says. The reliquary was carried by ship over the Black Sea, accompanied by three Greek metropolitans, Ioannikios of Heraclea, Parthenios of Adrianople and Theophanes of Palaiopatra, and came by Galați to Iași.

The relics survived a fire in the chapel of the Three Hierarchs at Iași, which the page calls a wonder worked by the power of God; Metropolitan Iosif Naniescu examined the chapel, and the prefect of the county, Leon Negruzzi, and the city’s procurator recorded what had happened in official minutes. Taken out of the heap of embers, the relics were sheltered for a time in the sanctuary of that chapel and soon moved into the new metropolitan cathedral of Iași, consecrated on 23 April 1887, where they are to this day.

In its session of 28 February 1950 the Holy Synod of the Romanian Orthodox Church resolved to canonise saints of the Romanian people and to generalise the cult of saints whose relics are kept in the country. For Parascheva this was done in the metropolitan cathedral of Iași on 14 October 1955, in the presence of many Romanian hierarchs and of representatives of the Russian and Bulgarian Churches. She is reckoned the protectress of the city of Iași and of the whole of Moldavia. The Tropar tab of her page is an audio recording with no printed text, so no hymn is recorded here.
