# Conon the Gardener

This Conon lived in the days of the emperor Decius, and was by descent from Nazareth in Galilee. From there he came to the city of Mandon in Pamphylia, and at a place called Carmila he made himself a garden, and sowing green things in it got his food from that. He was good in his ways, without malice and innocent of heart, and though he was unlettered, being a Christian he lived in a manner pleasing to God.

Publius the governor, hearing of him, sent soldiers to take him. They bowed to him with fine words, in mockery, and he answered their bow gladly, with words of greeting and of love. When they told him that the governor Publius was calling for him, he said: what need has he of me, seeing that I am a Christian? Let him call to himself those who are of one mind with him.

Conon was bound and brought to the governor, who pressed him to sacrifice to the idols. He sighed from the depths of his heart over the blindness and the wandering of the unbelievers, rebuked the governor to his face, and stood unshaken in his confession of Christ. So they drove iron nails into his feet and made him run before the governor's carriage, until, weakening, he fell upon his knees; and there, praying to God, he gave up his spirit into God's hands.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives him his words. saint.gr says the holy martyr Conon came of Nazareth in Galilee and lived in the years of the reign of Decius; that having come into the country of Pamphylia he picked out a small place and turned it into a garden, planting cabbages, and from them supplied himself and the poor strangers with food. He was so simple in character, the page says, that when the very men who had come to seize him and take him to the archon for being a Christian kissed him in mockery, he returned the kiss; and when they told him the governor was calling for him he answered: «Γιατί; Ποια ανάγκη έχει εκείνος από μένα και περισσότερο που είμαι Χριστιανός;» — why? what need has he of me, and all the more since I am a Christian? The soldiers bound him and brought him before the governor, who urged him to sacrifice to the idols, and the saint said with a spiritual courage: would God that you too, governor, could renounce the idols and come to Christ. Then the governor gave the order to torture him: they bored through his ankles, drove iron nails into him, and made him run in front of a chariot of horses. So, the page ends, the holy Conon was martyred and entered into the joy of his Lord; and it prints a distich over him which turns on those nails, that he goes to a Lord who bears the marks of nails, himself bearing the marks of nails in his feet.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him five sentences and one detail of the death the other calendars do not put this way. He was of Nazareth by birth, it says, mild and without malice, and pleasing to God in everything. In the persecution of Decius he was tortured for Christ, but stood firm in the faith and rebuked the heathen judges sharply for their folly.

With nails in his feet, tied to the prince's chariot, this good and guileless saint was dragged until he gave out of himself and fell. Then he prayed to God for the last time and gave Him his spirit, in the year 251. That year falls inside the reign this folder's interval already holds and is narrower than it, so the interval was left as it stood.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, and its life is four sentences that add a birthplace and a manner of death. He was born at Nazareth in Galilee and lived in the city of Мандон, where he kept a market garden; the calendar calls him a God-fearing man, kind, simple of heart and without malice. He suffered for the faith under the emperor Декий, whose years it gives as 249 to 251, which is the interval this folder already carried; brought to trial, he confessed his faith unshakeably and firmly.

**The death is this calendar's own**: «Мучители вбили ему в ноги гвозди и гнали перед колесницей, пока страдалец в изнеможении не упал, предав с молитвой дух свой Господу» — they drove nails into his feet and drove him before a chariot until he fell from exhaustion and gave up his spirit with a prayer. It gives no family, no relic, no hymn and no year beyond the reign, and the day page prints no hymn under his name.
