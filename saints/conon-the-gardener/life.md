# Conon the Gardener

This Conon lived in the days of the emperor Decius, and was by descent from Nazareth in Galilee. From there he came to the city of Mandon in Pamphylia, and at a place called Carmila he made himself a garden, and sowing green things in it got his food from that. He was good in his ways, without malice and innocent of heart, and though he was unlettered, being a Christian he lived in a manner pleasing to God.

Publius the governor, hearing of him, sent soldiers to take him. They bowed to him with fine words, in mockery, and he answered their bow gladly, with words of greeting and of love. When they told him that the governor Publius was calling for him, he said: what need has he of me, seeing that I am a Christian? Let him call to himself those who are of one mind with him.

Conon was bound and brought to the governor, who pressed him to sacrifice to the idols. He sighed from the depths of his heart over the blindness and the wandering of the unbelievers, rebuked the governor to his face, and stood unshaken in his confession of Christ. So they drove iron nails into his feet and made him run before the governor's carriage, until, weakening, he fell upon his knees; and there, praying to God, he gave up his spirit into God's hands.
