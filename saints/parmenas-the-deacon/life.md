# Parmenas the Deacon

In the days of the holy apostles, after the Lord's Ascension and the receiving of the Holy Spirit, while they had not yet scattered through the whole world to preach Christ and the churches of the faithful in Jerusalem had grown many, seven men full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom were chosen for the service of the diaconate: Stephen, Philip, [Prochorus](/saints/prochorus-the-deacon), [Nicanor](/saints/nicanor-the-deacon), [Timon](/saints/timon-the-deacon), Parmenas and Nicolas of Antioch. The apostles prayed, laid their hands on them and made them deacons.

Of these, the memory of the first martyr and archdeacon Stephen is kept on 27 December and that of Philip on 11 October; the rest — except Nicolas, who is not written in the number of the saints — the holy Church has been accustomed to keep on this day, although they ended by suffering at different times, preaching Christ apostolically in various places.

Parmenas, having fulfilled the service entrusted to him by the apostles in a preaching full of faith, died before the eyes of the apostles, and was buried by them and mourned with humility. There are some, however, the page adds, who say of him that he had a martyr's end and was crowned with the crown of martyrdom; and the two readings are left here side by side, as the page leaves them.
