# Nicholas (Pokrovsky) of Simbirsk

**The calendar gives him a rank, a forename and a year, and nothing else.** The Сретенский monastery’s line for 5 марта ст. ст. reads «Сщмч. Николая пресвитера (1919).», and behind it there is a name page with a title and no life. The life here is the long one azbyka.ru prints, compiled by протоиерей Алексий Скала out of «Святые, в земле Симбирской просиявшие» (Ульяновск, 2009), and it is the life of one diocese from end to end. Николай Сергеевич Покровский was born in 1864 into a priest’s family, wanting the same calling; he went to the Симбирская Духовная семинария and finished it in 1889 in the first class.

A year followed as psalm-reader at the church of the village Шерауты in the Буинский уезд, and a marriage to Елена, a priest’s daughter. In 1891 the bishop of Симбирск and Сызрань ordained him deacon and, a short while after, priest. His first parish of his own was the village Никулино in the Карсунский уезд, where he taught the Law of God in the local zemstvo school beside the duties of a rector. So four years passed.

In 1895 the consistory moved him to a convent at the village Кашпир in the Сызранский уезд. The monastic order of service — the daily offices, the unhurried reading and singing — would have wearied another priest, and suited him: he liked to serve, the quiet of the place pleased him, and the sisters of the small country house took to him as a guide. He became warden and teacher of its parish school of letters, and the life says why that mattered to him — he and his wife were given no children, and it came out as a father’s care for his pupils. He stayed thirteen years, and was given the набедренник in 1898 and the скуфья with a medal in 1906.

The drought of 1907 brought a failed harvest and famine to the Volga country, and it fell hardest on the poorest of the peasantry. He organised a collection of grain, asking the landowners and those peasants who had stores to give for their neighbours who had none, and the worst of what was expected was avoided. His service record carries the thanks it earned: «За содействие делу оказания трудовой помощи населению, пострадавшему от неурожая, объявлена Высочайшая Ея Императорскаго Величества Государыни Марии Феодоровны 12-го августа 1908 года благодарность».

In 1908 he was moved to the village Горюшки in the Сенгилеевский уезд, and served there to the end. His wife’s father, an aged archpriest gone out of office, came to live with them in 1916; the sudden death of her brother, a priest, left a small girl orphaned, and she was taken into the house and brought up as their own. Then came the autumn of 1917.

**One act undid him, and the parish chronicle he kept himself says what it was.** The villagers, having heard of churches robbed elsewhere, gathered on the church square one evening and called him out: they wanted to draw up a general resolution that their own church should not be touched, and to do it inside the church. «Не мог устоять священник Покровский Николай Сергеевич против подобного всеобщего желания впустить их в церковь» — he could not stand out against so general a wish, and let them in. «И вот единственно этот его поступок послужил жизненной мукой для него.»

Denunciations went to the town that the priest was holding meetings in the church to turn the village against the revolution. There were repeated visits to his house, interrogations, searches; nothing could be charged, and he passed for a counter-revolutionary all the same. His one horse was taken with its harness and carriage, his property was inventoried against a signed undertaking that none of it should go missing, and he was put under house arrest. «И так весь год священник Покровский был в тревоге». By then the chronicle was being kept by his wife’s father and not by him, and the life guesses why: things had grown too tight about him for the writing.

At the end of February 1919 alarm went through most of the province. Armed men were going into houses, searching, taking grain; those who stood against them were declared counter-revolutionaries, arrested, sent to the prisons, and some of them paid with their lives. The rising began with men beaten to death, a service sung in the church, and a crowd setting off towards the town with a protest; they were turned back off the road, and punitive detachments were sent into every village the rising had started from. One came to Горюшки, and the village communists, who had hated the priest for years, laid the whole of it on him.

He was arrested late in the evening of the 4th, secretly, and shut in the lock-up at the volost; in the night he was condemned to be shot, and «к утру его не стало». The dean of the district reported it to the archbishop: «в ночь с 4/17 на 5 марта с.г, по приговору Волостного Революционного Комитета, расстрелян священник села Горюшки Сенгилеевского уезда Николай Покровский. 6 марта усопший похоронен на приходском кладбище». They allowed him to be buried with the honour due to him, and would not allow it inside the church enclosure. Most of what his widow had was taken from her.

The sentence survives. Dated 18 March 1919 in the new style, it condemns four men of two villages: two for blows struck at officials of the Soviet power, a third for urging the risen on towards Сызрань, and fourth «Священника с. Горюшки Николая Покровского, 55 лет. За подстрекательство и контрреволюционную напутственную проповедь перед восставшими» — for incitement and a counter-revolutionary parting sermon before the risen. The age agrees with the year of birth the life gives. «Приговор этот привести в исполнение немедленно.»
