# Antonius (9 November)

Antonius came of Syria, saint.gr says, and his trade was stonecutting; and when he came to know Christ he gave the trade up, because it had to do with the making of idols, and withdrew into the desert. There he found a devout anchorite named Timothy and lived with him three years.

Then, with that elder's blessing, he went down to his own village, and in a moment of holy indignation smashed the altars of the idols. The idolaters beat him hard for it, and he went to Apamea in Syria, where he asked the bishop — Hosios, which the page notes is his name — and received leave to build a church in the name of the Holy Trinity. When he had begun the building his fellow-villagers, the idolaters, learned of it; they came by night and killed him with clubs, in the most pitiless way.

The page gives him no year and no emperor, and prints no hymn. His distich turns the wood of the clubs against the wood they worshipped: «Ἀντώνιον κτείνουσι τὸν θεῖον ξύλοις, / Οἱ τὸ ξύλον τιμῶντες ὡς Θεὸν πλάνοι» — they kill the divine Antonius with staves, the deceived men who honour the stock as God.

The Russian calendar is the first source read for this folder to say who he was. He was a Syrian and a stonecutter, living in the fifth century, and with the blessing of the bishop of Apamea in Syria he began to build a church in the name of the Holy Trinity. The pagans of the town, learning of it, broke into his house by night and cut him down with swords. That is the whole of the life, and the folder's own `office` stays empty: a mason's trade is not one.
