# Marinus (17 March)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 17 martie reads «Sfântul Mucenic Marin», and the page it links to carries neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab: there is the heading, the day, and nothing further. What the line says is that he was a martyr and that the Romanian church keeps him on this day, and that is the whole of what has been read here.

No year, no country, no account of his suffering and no hymn has been taken from anywhere else, and none has been supplied from memory. Martyrs of this name are kept on other days and in other calendars, and nothing on this page identifies him with any of them. If a life for him is printed elsewhere, it has not been read.

The Greek calendar keeps a Marinus on this same day, and where doxologia printed a line saint.gr prints a short account. It says he lived in the years of the persecutions of the Church; that he was a Christian from his birth and was brought up with careful piety and a most fervent zeal for the faith; and that when he was arrested as a Christian he confessed his faith with courage and rebuked the idolaters for their senseless sacrifices when they reviled the Christian religion. Then they tortured him savagely: they beat him with whips, broke his mouth and his teeth, and at the last he received the crown of martyric perfection by beheading.

The page still gives him no year, no country and no emperor, names no relics and prints no hymn. The couplet over him says that Marinus, beheaded, cuts off the head of error, and with his head remains wholly with Christ. Nothing here identifies him with any of the other martyrs of the name kept on other days.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and is the first of the three calendars to say anything of him. He was a soldier. He not only refused to offer sacrifice to the idols; he scattered the sacrifices others had offered and trampled them underfoot. For that he was tortured and beheaded, in the third century — the only date any page read for this folder gives him.

A senator named Asterius, dressed in costly white, watched the passion of Saint Marinus, and was so stirred by faith in Christ, who gives such courage to his followers, that he took the martyr's body on his own back, carried it away and buried it honourably. The heathen, seeing it, killed him too as a Christian. The Prologue names no city, no judge and no year within the century.

The Russian church keeps him on the same menologion day and tells the same act in two sentences, with the city added in his name rather than in the story. The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery says that, kindled with a burning love for Christ the Saviour, he broke down the altar at one of the festivals in an idol temple, trampled the sacrifices underfoot, and confessed himself a Christian before them all; and that after cruel torments he was beheaded. The line closes with the year 260, which the Prologue did not give, and the name link sets him at Caesarea in Palestine. The page prints no hymn for him.
