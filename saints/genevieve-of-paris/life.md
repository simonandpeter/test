# Genevieve of Paris

Genevieve was born of wealthy parents in Gaul, in the village of Nanterre near Paris, about the year 422. Her father was called Severus and her mother Gerontia, and after the custom of the time she often pastured her father's sheep on Mount Valerien. When the child was about seven, Germanus of Auxerre noticed her as he passed through Nanterre.

At the service that night the bishop laid his hand on her head and told her parents that she would become great before God and would lead many to salvation. When Genevieve said that she wished to give her life to Christ, he gave her a medallion with a cross on it to wear at her neck, and charged her never to wear bracelets or necklaces of pearl or gold or silver, and to avoid other ornament at her throat or on her fingers.

At fifteen she entered the monastic life at Paris. By many vigils, by fasting and by prayer she received from the Lord the gift of foresight and of working wonders, and became quickly known to the people of Paris for her holiness. By her prayers, the life says, she saved Paris from the attack of the Huns, and she helped in the building of churches.

She passed to the things that are eternal on 3 January, most probably in the year 512, at the age of eighty-nine. Her honoured relics were laid first in the church of Dionysius, the first bishop of Paris, and afterwards moved to the church of Saint Stephen at Paris, near the house where she had kept her ascetic life.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same third of January, and names her the patron of Paris. saint.gr says she was born by tradition about 419 at Nanterre, near Paris, and felt the monastic calling in her heart from an early age; her spiritual friendship with Germanus of Auxerre helped that longing grow. After her parents died she was tonsured a nun by the bishop of the city of Paris and reached great heights of ascetic practice and perfection, and she fell asleep in peace at ninety-three. The church of the Holy Apostles in Paris, where her relic was laid, took her name.
