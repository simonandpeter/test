# Michael (Belyustin)

Michael was born on 1 March 1881 in the city of Tver, the son of an official of the Tver consistory. He finished the Tver seminary and served at Kotovo in the Molokovo district; in 1931 he moved to the Sonkovo district and served the church of the Saburovo churchyard. When the persecution was declared the OGPU began gathering material on the clergy, and its man for the district called in those who might give evidence against him — but few would.

On 6 April 1933 a collective-farm foreman was questioned and said that the priest was anti-Soviet but cautious, and recalled that, called in the autumn of 1932 to give his mother communion, Belyustin had said: «Вот смотри, до чего довели ваши колхозы, у мужика последний хлеб и картошку отбирают». A widow who had had him sing her husband's funeral said only that he had said something about a change of government, and something to a dispossessed grave-digger about his property being given back, and that she could not remember the words. He was arrested the same day and held at Bezhetsk, and told the investigator that it was the custom of the village to ask the priest to a funeral meal and that he had talked politics with nobody. The troika gave him three years in the Sarov camps.

Released in 1935, he went with the blessing of [Thaddeus](/saints/thaddeus-of-tver), archbishop of Tver, to Sonkovo, where the church of the Priluki churchyard had no priest; and in November 1936, in the teeth of the state campaign to close churches, he managed to re-register the Orthodox community of Priluki and the seven villages around it. He was arrested on 12 November 1937 and held at Bezhetsk, where duty witnesses signed whatever the investigator wanted. On 20 November he denied every charge — the agitation against the collective farms, the rumours of war and of the fall of Soviet power, the praise of the enemies of the people: «Все факты предъявленного мне обвинения в контрреволюционной деятельности отрицаю». The troika sentenced him on 25 November and he was shot on 27 November 1937. He was canonised for the whole church in August 2000.

The day's line names twenty-seven new martyrs of 1937 together — twenty-three presbyters, a deacon, a hieromonk, a lay martyr and a woman — and the calendar carries a life page for ten of them, of whom he is one. The men of that line were tried separately, in different districts and different prisons, and no one of these lives names another of them, so no relation is recorded between them here.
