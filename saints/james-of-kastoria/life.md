# James of Kastoria

saint.gr's calendar for 1 Νοεμβρίου keeps «Άγιοι Ιάκωβος ο νέος Οσιομάρτυρας από την Καστοριά και οι δύο μαθητές του Ιάκωβος ο Διάκονος και Διονύσιος ο Μοναχός». James was born in a village of Kastoria, Korisos, of Christian parents, Martinos and Paraskevi. He became a shepherd of sheep and got a fair fortune, so that his own brother envied him and denounced him to the judge, pretending that he had found treasure. To escape that envy James left for Constantinople, where he worked as a dealer in sheep and became rich again.

One day, however, he went to the Patriarch, confessed, and shared out his property among the poor; then he went to the Holy Mountain, where he was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Docheiariou. After that he went to the monastery of the Forerunner, which was then a skete of Iviron, where he kept silence in obedience to an elder named Ignatios.

When he had laboured enough in the virtues he left there too, and came to the inner parts of the Holy Mountain, where he settled with six disciples and was known as a teacher of virtue in the monastic life. Later he left the Mountain with his disciples and went to the Castle of Petra, and from there to Meteora, where he taught the monks; and then to the monastery of the Forerunner at Deverkista, near Nafpaktos, where he lived with prayer and the word of God.

There he was slandered by the Turks as stirring up the Christians against the authorities. He was arrested and taken, with two of his disciples, to the Bey of Trikala, who shut him in prison for forty days; and from that prison he was taken in irons, with [James the Deacon](/saints/james-the-deacon-of-kastoria) and [Dionysius the Monk](/saints/dionysius-the-monk-of-kastoria), to Didymoteicho in Thrace, where Sultan Selim was. There they were frightfully tortured, and then sent on to Adrianople, where the Sultan also came, and pressed them to change their faith; and the saints answered with one voice: «μὴ γένοιτο ποτὲ νὰ ἀρνηθῶμεν τὸν Κύριον ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστόν, κἂν μύρια βάσανα μᾶς παιδεύσετε» — God forbid that we should ever deny our Lord Jesus Christ, though you punish us with ten thousand torments.

Then, by the Sultan's order, the torturers scraped their flesh with iron claws, beat the old man James about the jaws and stripped the skin from his chest in strips, and threw salt and vinegar into the wounds; and the two disciples they whipped cruelly with whips of ox sinew. But because all three were immovable in their faith, they were hanged on 1 November 1520. The relics of the venerable martyr and of those who suffered with him are at the monastery of Saint Anastasia near Thessalonica, and a life and an office of this new martyr were written by the rhetor Theophanes of Thessalonica, who was his contemporary.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him with his two disciples under one heading on the Julian 1 November, and it gives the one thing the Greek notice does not: why he gave everything away. He was born in the eparchy of Kastoria. Working with sheep he grew rich, and so drew the envy of his own brother, who slandered him to the Turks as having found treasure in the ground; James fled to Constantinople, and there grew very rich again.

Once he was a guest of a Turkish bey. The Turks were eating meat while James kept the fast, and the bey said to him that great was their Christian faith — and told how his own wife had gone out of her mind, and how, after all the doctors and all the treatments, he had taken her to the Patriarch to have a prayer read over her. The moment the Patriarch opened the book to read, a light out of heaven shone through the church; and when the prayer was finished his wife was well. Hearing a Turk praise the Christian faith, James gave away the whole of his property and went to the Holy Mountain.

Two things this book says differ from the Greek notice and neither reading is chosen here. It has him tonsured at the monastery of Iviron, where saint.gr has him tonsured at Docheiariou and only afterwards at the skete of the Forerunner that then belonged to Iviron. And it puts the monastery of Saint Anastasia, where his wonderworking relics and those of his disciples rest, at Galatista near Thessalonica, where the Greek page puts it at Vasilika. He struggled on the Holy Mountain and suffered for the faith at the hands of the Turks at Adrianople on 1 November 1520, which is the day and the year this folder already held.
