# Seraphim (Shakhmut)

Seraphim — Roman Romanovich-Shakhmut in the world — was born in 1901 in the
village of Podlesye in the Lyakhovichi district, into a large family of poor
peasants. From childhood Roman was drawn to the church: while the other
children played the usual games he would make little crosses of sticks, put
on an apron for an epitrachelion and begin to “serve”. For all the family’s
want he finished the two-class school at Lyakhovichi, and in 1922 entered
the Zhirovichi monastery of the Dormition, where he was tonsured Seraphim in
1923; for his good voice he was given the choir, and became a fine precentor
and keeper of the typikon, and in 1926 he was ordained hierodeacon and soon
hieromonk. In August 1941, with the blessing of Metropolitan Panteleimon
(Rozhnovsky), he left Zhirovichi for Minsk to rebuild parish life where the
Bolsheviks had destroyed it: he went through a great many villages gathering
the faithful’s petitions to the metropolitan for the opening of parishes,
baptised many, and collected material on the persecution of the Orthodox
church in Belarus. After that journey he served at the church of the Holy
Spirit in Minsk and had the pastoral care of the city’s hospitals and
orphanages. In 1944, for his work in opening churches, he was arrested at
Grodno and held under investigation for exactly ten months; charged with
belonging to “German counter-revolutionary organs”, he was sentenced to
five years in a concentration camp, and died there soon after — in 1946, it
is thought — of a ruptured heart, after the terrible torments he had been
through in the cells of the NKVD. The Russian calendar keeps him on
24 August (Julian) among the new martyrs of Belarus, the twenty-three
hieromartyrs of Minsk, whose lives the Sretensky calendar prints together.
