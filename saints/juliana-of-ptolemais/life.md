# Juliana of Ptolemais

Juliana and her brother [Paul](/saints/paul-of-ptolemais) were put to death under the emperor Aurelian (270–275) in the Phoenician city of Ptolemais. Paul was seized for making the sign of the cross as the emperor entered the city, and confessed Christ at his trial; Juliana, seeing her brother tortured, accused the emperor before everyone of injustice and cruelty, and was given over to the same torments. They were beaten, torn with iron hooks and burned on red-hot grills, and their endurance could not be broken; three of the soldiers who tortured them believed in Christ and died with them.

doxologia.ro keeps her with her brother on 4 martie and prints a longer life of the two. It says that Aurelian had given his order against the Christians throughout the world, and that he came through Assyria into Phoenicia and entered Ptolemais himself to force them to sacrifice; that Paul, seeing him come in, signed his face with the cross and said to Juliana, «Îndrăznește, sora mea, și nu te teme» — take courage, sister, and do not be afraid, for a great trial is coming upon the Christians. After they were put to the sword their bodies were left unburied outside the city with soldiers watching from a distance, and for seven days and nights the dogs would not touch them, the wolves that came sat beside them as guards, and the carrion birds flew over without settling; when the guard was withdrawn by night the Christians came and buried them with honour.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and gives the executioners names. saint.gr makes [Paul](/saints/paul-of-ptolemais) and Juliana brother and sister, living in the years of the emperor Aurelian; their parents brought them up in the whole life of the Christian faith, fit not only to believe and to be adorned by an upright life but to carry their cross on their shoulder and to be ready for every sacrifice of self-denial in Christ. When their father and mother left this life the two kept to the road God had marked for them: Paul was eminent among the young men, and Juliana the same among the young girls, so that brother and sister became two of the brightest ornaments of the Christian Church in Ptolemais. That drew the hatred of the idolaters, who denounced the pair to the emperor Aurelian when he once passed through their city.

Aurelian could not shake their faith, and ordered them hard tortured; and while the executioners [Stratonicus](/saints/stratonicus-the-executioner), [Codratus](/saints/codratus-the-executioner) and [Acacius](/saints/acacius-the-executioner) were torturing them they marvelled at the courage of their souls, confessed Christ and were beheaded on the spot. At the last the new executioners burnt the flesh of the two with lighted torches, and seeing that the brother and sister stayed unmoved in their faith, beheaded them. The page adds that their memory is kept again on the seventeenth of August, which is the day this folder's Russian row already holds.

The Serbian church keeps her on this day with her brother. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the pair four sentences: brother and sister of Ptolemais in Phoenicia, frightfully tortured for Christ by the emperor Aurelian and beheaded at the last. At their torments, it says, many wonders were shown, and many of the heathen who saw them turned to the faith, so that some of those too were beheaded in the year 273 and received their crowns. It names none of them, and the three executioners the other calendars name on this day are not in it.
