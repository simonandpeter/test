# Macarius

Macarius (Gnevushev), bishop of Orel and Sevsk, was born in 1858; he had a
higher theological education and served as a priest, and became known as a
spiritual and national figure while diocesan missionary of Kyiv, so that
when he was widowed he was offered the episcopate — consecrated bishop of
Vyazma in January 1917 — and settled in the ancient monastery of the Holy
Spirit at Vyazma. From May of that year, at the demand of the
revolutionaries, he was retired as “not answering to the spirit of the
times”. In the summer of 1918 a Bolshevik detachment came to the monastery
and arrested him; when his cell-attendant came to him in his cell the next
day he saw on the bishop’s face and body the marks of beating and abuse —
he had been shorn, his beard cut off, and put into a soldier’s clothes. On
4 September 1918 — 17 September of the new style — he was taken with a
group of fourteen to a deserted place outside Smolensk and lined up with
his back to a freshly dug grave. They were killed one by one, the soldier
coming right up and putting the rifle to the forehead; the bishop was last,
praying with his prayer-rope in his hands and blessing each one: go in
peace. When his turn came the Red Army man’s hand shook, and seeing the
fear in the executioner’s eyes the bishop said: my son, let not your heart
be troubled — do the will of him who sent you. Soon afterwards that soldier,
a simple peasant, was in a hospital for the insane: every night he saw in
his sleep the murdered bishop blessing him. I understand it this way, he
said — we killed a holy man; how else could he have known that my heart had
gone cold? But he knew, and blessed me out of pity, and now out of pity he
comes to me and blesses me, as if to say he is not angry. But I know there
is no forgiveness for my sin; God’s light is no longer sweet to me, I am
not worthy to live and do not want to. The Jubilee Council of Bishops of
August 2000 numbered him among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia for
veneration by the whole church; the Russian calendar keeps him on 22 August
(Julian) with the presbyters John and Alexius, killed in the same year.
