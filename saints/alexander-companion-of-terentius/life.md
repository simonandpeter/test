# Alexander, companion of Terentius

saint.gr keeps a company of martyrs at Carthage in Africa on 10 April and names every one of them. Its whole account of the martyrdom is two sentences long: «Οι Άγιοι Μάρτυρες Αφρικανός, Θεόδωρος, Μάξιμος, Πομπήιος και Τερέντιος μαρτύρησαν το έτος 250 μ.Χ., επί αυτοκράτορα Δεκίου (249-251 μ.Χ.) και ηγεμόνος Φουρτουνιανού, στην Καρθαγένη της Αφρικής μαζί με άλλους τριάντα εννέα Χριστιανούς» — [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius), [Theodore](/saints/theodore-companion-of-terentius), [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius), [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius) and [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa) were martyred in the year 250, under the emperor Decius and the governor Fortunianus, at Carthage in Africa, together with another thirty-nine Christians. The page then prints the names of those others, and forty names stand in the list; the day's own line says forty others as well. Both readings are the page's and it reconciles neither.

The martyrdom it tells is one martyrdom for the whole company. They were seized because they were Christians and were tried with many torments: first they were beaten beyond all measure, until their inward parts could be seen; then their hips were pierced with red-hot nails, and vinegar and salt were thrown upon the wounds. But when they had prayed, they destroyed the wooden images, the altars and the temples of the idolaters. At the last, after the dreadful torments, they were all beheaded. The page's couplet for the rest of them says: let us see the contestants they cut down, and how many — a fourfold ten, and others besides.

The page gives each of the company a heading of his own with a gloss on his name, and the names are largely those of the Greek past. It says nothing else of any one of them: no city any of them came from, no trade, no age, no order in the Church, and nothing at all that tells one from another beyond the name. Its three hymns address them all together. Because the page enumerates the company, the corpus keeps one entry for each man, and each entry says only what the page says of him.

The page's heading for him is «Αγιος Αλέξανδρος», and it glosses the name — from ἀλέξω, to ward off, with ἀνήρ, a man: the champion of his country and the conqueror of its enemies, and symbolically the victor, the triumphant, the bringer of civilisation. Under the gloss it names who else has borne it: dozens of emperors, kings, princes, popes, patriarchs, bishops, generals and politicians, the greatest of them Alexander the Great, king of Macedon, son of Philip II and Olympias. Of the martyr it says nothing beyond that.

**The Russian calendar keeps the company on the same menologion day and names him in its own line**, where the Greek page this folder was written from named him only in a list: «Мчч. Терентия , Помпия , Африкана , Максима , Зинона , Александра , Феодора и иных 33-х (ок. 249-251).» — seven men named and thirty-three others, which makes forty. The name link behind his place in that line, name page 104, gives the calendar’s own nominative with the African epithet, «Александр Африканский, мч.», and lists one day for him and only one, 10 апреля. **The bare forename was refused**: `.tmp/foldchk.py` finds it on twelve other folders of this corpus as a `ru` or `sr` form, and the epithet is what the calendar itself prints.

The life page behind that line names him a second time and gives the company a story, where saint.gr gave it two sentences. The martyrdom falls under the emperor Decius, whose reign this page dates «(249 - 251)»: he published a decree ordering every subject to sacrifice to the pagan idols, and when it reached «правитель Африки Фортунатиан» — the governor of Africa, who is the Φουρτουνιανός of the Greek entry under this calendar’s spelling — he called the people into the square, showed them the fearful instruments of torture, and declared that all without exception must sacrifice.

Many agreed, in fear of the torments; forty Christians at the head of whom stood Terentius declared their faithfulness to the Saviour courageously. Fortunatianus marvelled at their boldness and asked how they, being reasonable men, could confess as God one whom the Jews had crucified as a malefactor; and [Terentius](/saints/terentius-of-africa) answered that they believed in the Saviour who had suffered the death of the Cross willingly and had risen on the third day. **It is at this point that the page divides the forty, and it puts him on the other side of the division from Terentius.**

Seeing that Terentius was heartening the rest by his example, the governor had him shut in prison with his three closest friends — [Africanus](/saints/africanus-companion-of-terentius), [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-terentius) and [Pompeius](/saints/pompeius-companion-of-terentius) — and set about forcing the remainder, «в том числе Зинона, Александра и Феодора», among them Zeno, Alexander and Theodore, to deny Christ. Neither persuasion nor dreadful torment shook them: they were burned with red-hot iron, their wounds were doused with vinegar and rubbed with salt, and they were scraped with iron claws, and the Lord strengthened them.

Brought into the idol temple and offered the sacrifice once more, these soldiers of Christ called on the God who had once rained fire on Sodom for its lawlessness to overthrow this temple also for the sake of His truth; the idols fell with a crash and broke in pieces, and then the whole temple came down. The enraged governor ordered their execution, and the martyrs bowed their heads under the headsman’s sword, praising God. **So where saint.gr tells one martyrdom for all of them, this page beheads thirty-six first** and keeps the four back for chains, hunger, an angel who loosed them in the night, and serpents that turned on the enchanters who had brought them.

And the number. saint.gr’s line gives five names and «άλλους τριάντα εννέα», another thirty-nine, which is forty-four, while the list of names on that same page runs to forty — the disagreement this folder already records and which neither page reconciles. **This calendar reads forty, three times over**: «сорок христиан во главе со святым Терентием» in the life, seven named and thirty-three others on this day’s line, and four named and thirty-six others on the Julian 28 October, where it keeps the same forty men a second time and where the four folders named above already carry their own `russian` rows. Nothing is chosen here; the forty is now what three lines of a second calendar agree on.
