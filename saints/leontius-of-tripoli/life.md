# Leontius of Tripoli

In the days of Vespasian there was at Rome a man of the order of senators named Hadrian, harsh by habit, a pitiless tormentor and an inventor of every cruelty. Hearing that the Christians turned in disgust from the multitude of the gods and from the idol sacrifices, blasphemed the gods honoured by Romans and Greeks, said that Christ alone is the true God, and were bringing many over to their faith, he asked the emperor for power against them, and was sent as governor into Phoenicia to persecute everyone who believed in Christ. Approaching Phoenicia he learned that in the city of Tripoli there was a commander of soldiers named Leontius who reviled the old gods and turned men away from them.

Hadrian sent ahead the tribune [Hypatius](/saints/hypatius-the-tribune) with soldiers to find him, and [Theodulus](/saints/theodulus-of-tripoli), the tribune's friend, went with them. On the road the tribune fell ill and saw in a vision a young man who told him to call on the God of Leontius; when all the soldiers had stood together and cried three times with him, "God of Leontius, help me," he rose up whole. [Theodulus](/saints/theodulus-of-tripoli), who had questioned him closely about the vision, felt his heart take fire towards that God he did not yet know.

Climbing to the hill on which Tripoli was built they were met by Leontius himself, who greeted them and asked whom they sought; they answered that the emperor had heard of a worthy, wise and valiant man of that name and that the governor Hadrian was coming behind them to send him to Rome with honour. Leontius took them into his house and set a table for them, and when they pressed him again to point the man out, he said: "I am the Leontius you are looking for. I am the soldier of Jesus Christ. I am the man Hadrian sent you to seize." They fell at his feet.

As he prayed over them a shining cloud came down upon the tribune and his friend, overshadowed them and let fall rain on them, and so baptised them; and Leontius called over them, while the rain fell, the name of the Holy Trinity. He clothed the newly baptised in white and had lighted candles carried before them. The rest of the soldiers came up, saw the two in white with candles burning, and were angry; the city fell into an uproar, some defending the Christians and others demanding they be burned, and all waiting for the governor.

Hadrian came two days later, heard the accusation, and had them brought out of prison. To Leontius he said, "Are you Leontius?", and was answered, "I am" — a soldier of Christ, a son of the true light. The tribune and his friend, asked why they had thrown off the customs of their fathers and the army's pay, answered that they had found better pay with the heavenly Emperor, and that their reward was in heaven, and told the governor that his own days were already shortened. They were put to death before Leontius; and when Hadrian threw their punishment in his face, Leontius answered that what he called punishment was life and peace and joy, and that they were now rejoicing with the ranks of the angels.

Leontius was beaten and questioned again and again and would not yield; he finished his contest on the eighteenth day of June, under Vespasian's rule at Rome, and was laid near the shore at Tripoli. His passion was written by a servant of Christ named Notarius, who saw all of it with his own eyes, on tablets of lead, and laid it in the grave beside the relics for the generations to come.

Doxologia's calendar for 18 iunie gives Leontius a line of his own, «Sfântul Mucenic Leontie»; the shared life is his passion, and he is the commander of soldiers at Tripoli around whom the rest of it turns.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them on the same day, 18 June, and saint.gr sets their martyrdom in the years of the emperor Vespasian. Leontius, it says, came from Greece — from Aegina — and was marked out for his imposing and haughty bearing and his bodily strength; he enlisted young, showed great bravery and manliness in every battle, and for those qualities was quickly promoted to high military rank, yet for all his swift advance and high position he bore himself with much modesty and honesty. While he was in Africa he was taught the Christian faith, to which his noble and honest soul responded warmly.

He began to preach the word of God to those under him and to the soldiers, and whatever money he earned he spent on the relief of the poor, the widows and the orphans; and this Christ-imitating work soon bore good fruit, for two of his fellow officers, [Hypatius](/saints/hypatius-the-tribune) — whom the page also calls «Υπάτος» — and [Theodulus](/saints/theodulus-of-tripoli), believed in Christ and began to live in Christian purity and ascesis. When the African governor Hadrian learned of it he tried by various promises to make them deny their faith, and soon understood that they stood unshaken, as brave soldiers of Jesus Christ. So he ordered Hypatius and Theodulus put to death by beheading, and had Leontius scourged until he died; and with their honourable blood they sealed their faith and their love for their Saviour and won the unfading crowns of martyrdom.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 18 June, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep him on, and it tells the passion above a third time. What it alone gives is the year: he suffered honourably, it says, in the year 73 — inside Vespasian’s reign, which is as near as either of the other books came, and the first year this folder has held. It makes him a Roman commander in the Phoenician city of Tripoli in the time of that emperor, of Hellas by origin, and sets its own description of him in quotation marks: great of body in his stature, strong, robust, and brave in battles.

Three things in it are new. When the governor learned that [Hypatius](/saints/hypatius-the-tribune) and [Theodulus](/saints/theodulus-of-tripoli) had become Christians he ordered them beaten without mercy and then their heads struck off with an axe; and so, this book says, these two spiritual children of Leontius came to their end. Then, in the middle of the worst of his own torments, when his whole body had been covered with wounds and he was praying hard that God would not leave him, an angel of the Lord appeared to Leontius and encouraged him and comforted him. And at the last they threw him down on the ground and beat him until he gave up his spirit to God.
