# Demetrius of Prilutsk

The venerable Demetrius of Prilutsk was born in the region of Pereyaslavl-Zalessky in Russia, in the fourteenth century, of a rich and pious family. He was tonsured a monk young and founded a monastery dedicated to Saint Nicholas. In 1354 he met [Sergius of Radonezh](/saints/sergius-of-radonezh), who had come to Pereyaslavl to visit the metropolitan Athanasius, and from then on the two saints were bound by friendship. His fame spread so far that the grand prince Demetrius Ivanovich asked him to become the spiritual father of his children.

But he wanted monastic stillness. Under Sergius's influence he withdrew to a remote place and settled to the north with his disciple Pachomius. In the forests of the Vologda country, near the river Velika, he built a church of the Resurrection of the Lord and was preparing to lay the foundations of a monastery; but the local people, afraid that the fields of the district would become monastic property, objected and asked the two monks to leave, and they, not wanting to burden anyone with their presence, withdrew in bitterness.

Demetrius then established the first coenobitic monastery of the Russian north. In 1372, with the valuable help of the people of the district, the wooden cathedral church of the Saviour was finished, and his person began to draw disciples and monks even from the country of his birth. In the new monastic community he combined prayer with strict ascesis and fasting, and with charity: he saw to the feeding of the hungry, took in the homeless, helped the poor, comforted the grieving and counselled all who came seeking the Lord.

The gifts the faithful made to the monastery he received with discernment and care, and managed in a way that gave no offence, watching particularly that no worldly mind should be cultivated in the hearts of the monks. God endowed him with the gift of discernment. Having lived out the rest of his life in a way pleasing to God, Demetrius fell asleep in deep old age in the year 1392.
