# Theodote of Nicaea

Theodote lived in the years of the emperor Alexander Severus (222), when Simplicius had been sent as governor to Cappadocia. She was from the Euxine Pontus, a rich Christian, but incomparably richer in piety and works of charity; she was denounced to Simplicius for winning pagans to Christianity by her personal efforts and her money, and at once they seized her and tortured her horribly, but miraculously her wounds closed and the prison door opened of itself, and so she was saved from boiling water too and other deadly tortures. At last they beheaded her, and so she gloriously received the crown of martyrdom — the God-given grace, the verse says, makes Theodote eager for the sharpened sword. The Greek calendar keeps her on 17 September, and the entry adds that she is probably the same saint as the one kept with Socrates on 21 October.

The Russian calendar keeps her as Theodotia on 17 September old style, about 230, and its life calls her a native of Cappadocia who suffered at Nicaea under Alexander Severus (222–235). Denounced to Simplicius, the new governor of Cappadocia, she was hung up and torn with iron hooks as though she felt nothing, and after eight days in chains in prison showed only faint marks of it.

When she was thrown into a furnace the flame burst out and burned those standing by, and the pagan priests who came to scatter her ashes were burned too, while the survivors saw her standing unharmed in the flame between two young men in white. Simplicius, coming back from Byzantium, had her brought to him at Ancyra and laid on red-hot iron, then sent her to Nicaea, where the idols fell and broke at her prayer; the saw his men put to her did her no harm, and at last she was beheaded, and Sophronius, bishop of Nicaea, buried her.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, says she bore cruel torments for Christ for eight years from a hegemon Simplicius, who at last went out of his mind, and that she was beheaded about 230 under Alexander Severus.

That 21 October entry is the second Greek day on this record. There saint.gr
keeps her with [Socrates the presbyter](/saints/socrates-companion-of-theodote)
and tells one story for the two: a woman of a noble family, bold in the faith,
who spoke of the Gospel wherever she found herself and by her teaching prepared
pagan women for the knowledge of the faith and for holy baptism, and who was his
precious helper in that work; the two of them denounced and arrested, pressed
with threats and tortures to sacrifice to the idols, rejecting it with
indignation and sealing their confession with their blood under the sword. Her
couplet there calls her a pure sacrifice given to God, as her name says.

This corpus held the two days as two folders. The author ruled on 3 October 2026
that they are one woman, and this record is the two; the synaxarion's own
"probably" is left standing, because the source has not gone further than it.
The one thing that tells for it beyond the word is the geography: the 21 October
entry martyrs her at Ancyra and the 17 September life has Simplicius bring her
to Ancyra, lay her on red-hot iron there, and only then send her to Nicaea.
