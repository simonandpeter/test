# Herodion

Herodion, called also Rodion, was of Tarsus in Cilicia by birth and is numbered among the seventy apostles. He was a kinsman of the apostle Paul, as the letter to the Romans says, and bishop in Neopatras, where he suffered much at the hands of the Jews — but not before he had turned many of the Greeks to Christ by his holy teaching.

Hating him for it, they fell on him with the servants of the idols and tormented him hard: some beat him, some broke his mouth with stones, others struck him over the head with pieces of wood, and afterwards he was struck with a knife and left as dead, bathed in blood. Thinking him dead, they left him and went away; but Herodion rose up and went on helping the apostles, and being kept alive by the grace of God he preached the word of the Lord afterwards at Rome together with the apostle Peter. It is of him that Paul wrote: greet Herodion, my kinsman.

By the witness of Metaphrastes, he was beheaded at Rome together with the apostle Olympas while the two were helping the apostle Peter, on the same day on which Peter himself was crucified with a multitude of the faithful. His commemoration is kept on 4 January with the seventy apostles, on 28 March, on 8 April and on 10 November; the Romanian calendar prints him on 8 aprilie, with the apostles [Agabus](/saints/agabus-the-apostle), [Rufus](/saints/rufus-apostle-of-thebes), [Phlegon](/saints/phlegon-of-marathon), [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle) and [Hermes](/saints/hermes-apostle-of-dalmatia).

The page adds a caution of its own. The Hermeneia of Dionysius of Fourna, in the Bucharest edition of 2000, names two apostles of this name, Herodion and Rodion, and says that one is to be painted as an old man and the other as a young one, holding a rolled scroll, the sign of apostleship; the page does not settle whether they are two men or one, and neither does this entry.
