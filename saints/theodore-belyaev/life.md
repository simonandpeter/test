# Theodore (Belyaev)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him fourth of fifteen on 21 октября ст. ст., in a line of eleven priests, two deacons and two monks of 1937. He was born on 12 November 1867 in the village of Vezgum in the Belozersk volost of Novgorod province, now the Vologda region, the son of a priest, Eugene Belyaev. He finished the Olonets seminary in 1889, was ordained priest, and served in the Trinity church of the village of Uloma in the Cherepovets uyezd. He was arrested there by the Cherepovets Cheka in 1919 and soon released.

In March 1922 the parish learned that a commission was coming to take the church's valuables away. The women of the parish resolved to defend it themselves, and on Sunday 26 March held a meeting of their own, chairing it with an educated and deeply believing widow, Olga Vasilyevna Levina; they agreed to ring the bells if the commission came and to resist if the vessels were taken by force, and the people of nearly twenty villages set their refusal down in writing. They came to Fr Theodore afterwards and told him, and he approved of what they had done, saying the decision was the believers' own and had to be measured by how dear the church was to them.

On 28 March the rumour ran that the commission had arrived. A tocsin was rung, riders went round the villages, and by three in the afternoon a crowd of two thousand, mostly women, stood before the volost executive committee. Fr Theodore had left at seven that morning for firewood, then gone to bury an infant in one village and baptise a child in another, and came back past the crowd late in the day. A messenger fetched him and he was ordered to go out and quiet the people; he told them that no seizure had begun, said what he had to say, turned and went home. He was arrested the next day, and told the investigator that handing over church property was the business of the believers and not of the servants of the altar.

On 17 May 1922 the provincial revolutionary tribunal at Cherepovets tried eight people. Four members of the parish council were given two years, suspended on the ground of their low cultural level; two others were given a year and a half and three years; Fyodor Yevgenyevich Belyaev, fifty-four, priest of the Uloma church and chairman of its council, was given four years in a house of correction, and Olga Levina five, a widow with a son of ten and a daughter of eight. Released, he came back to the same village, and in 1931 was arrested again for failing to meet an arbitrarily fixed grain quota and exiled for five years.

He returned in 1933 to the Makaryev church at Makarovo in the Vesyegonsk district of the Tver region. Of the fifteen years of his ministry from 1918 to 1933 he had spent eight in prison and exile. He had nothing left, neither house nor property, and lived in the church watchman's hut with the watchman; his four daughters and three sons were grown and scattered, and his wife Larisa Mikhailovna lived at Vesyegonsk. When the taxes left nothing for the church's upkeep or its firewood he went round the parish collecting money himself.

On 17 March 1937 the village soviet closed the church. He wrote a complaint to the central executive committee and gathered the believers' signatures under it himself, and while the church stood shut he went from house to house serving. A month later the soviet announced that typhus had broken out in the neighbouring villages and forbade him to visit; it was a plain lie and he went on. When he came to pay his taxes the chairman would set about persuading him that there is no God and that religion is a deception, and he always answered the same way: he would never leave the faith and would die a priest in his service.

He was arrested on 8 October 1937 and imprisoned at Vesyegonsk; he was seventy. They took the last of what he had, nineteen books of spiritual reading and a portrait. Witnesses were questioned on 12 and 13 October — the collective farm's brigade leader and its members, chosen from the unbelieving, though his standing among the people was such that none of them would say much more than that he had gathered signatures to have the church reopened and had spoken of the grain levies. He was interrogated on 15 October, admitted saying that the churches would all soon be closed and that there had been more of everything under the tsar, and denied the rest. The indictment went out the same day; the troika condemned him on 1 November and he was shot on 3 November 1937. He was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
