# Daniel

doxologia.ro prints one long life for Daniel and the three youths together, and this is read out of it. Daniel came of a royal line, of the tribe of Judah, and while he was still a small child he was taken captive by Nebuchadnezzar and carried from Jerusalem to Babylon together with Joachim, king of Judah; and there, the page says, his youth was made wonderful and glorious by the grace of God. The first thing it tells of him is that with good understanding he convicted the unjust and lawless judges — two elders whom the Jews in captivity had chosen to judge the people's cases — and so delivered the innocent Susanna from death.

Nebuchadnezzar told Ashpenaz, the chief of his eunuchs, to bring to the palace sons of Israel of the royal and the noble seed: young men in whom there was no blemish, fair of face, wise, learned, deep in thought and strong, to be in the king's house before him and to be taught letters and the Chaldean tongue. He appointed them food every day from his own table and from his own wine, and they were to be fed so for three years and then brought before him. Among them, from the sons of Judah, were Daniel and three other children of the royal family: [Ananias](/saints/ananias-of-the-three-youths), [Azarias](/saints/azarias-of-the-three-youths) and [Misael](/saints/misael-of-the-three-youths).

The chief of the eunuchs gave them names: Daniel he called Belteshazzar; Ananias, Shadrach; Misael, Meshach; and Azarias, Abednego. But Daniel set it in his heart, with his three friends, not to be defiled from the king's table and his wine, and asked the eunuch not to defile them; and God put Daniel into the mercy and compassion of the chief of the eunuchs, who said to him: I am afraid of my king, who commanded that your food should be good and your drink likewise, lest he see your faces gloomier than the faces of the children of your own age, and you condemn my head to death.

Then Daniel said to Amelsad, who was set over the eunuchs and over the four of them: try your children for ten days, and give us only the seeds of the earth to eat and water to drink; and then let our faces be shown before you, and the faces of the children who eat from the king's table, and do with your servants as you will. He listened to them and tried them for ten days, and their faces were seen much fairer than the faces of the children who ate from the king's table; so he set aside the dishes and the wine and gave them only seeds and water. God gave those four young men sharpness and wisdom, and to Daniel he gave the gift of interpreting dreams; and when the chief of the eunuchs brought them before Nebuchadnezzar the king spoke with them, found out their wisdom, their speech and their knowledge, and found them ten times wiser than all the enchanters and sorcerers in his whole kingdom.

In the eighteenth year Nebuchadnezzar set up a golden image sixty cubits high in the plain of Dura, and a furnace was built in that field for whoever should disobey the royal order; and the three friends, who would not worship it, were bound and thrown into it and walked unharmed in the flame. That is told at length in their own folders. Daniel interpreted the king's dreams, and stood before Belshazzar after him, and the life follows the Babylonian and Persian kings through to Darius and Cyrus.

Under Darius, who set a hundred and twenty lords over his kingdom and officers above them, Daniel was the chief, because the Holy Spirit was in him, and the king set him over the whole kingdom. The officers and lords looked for a fault in him and found no fault, no stumbling and no sin, because he was faithful; so they said: we shall find no fault against Daniel except in the laws of his God. They came to the king and had him decree that whoever should pray to any god or man but himself for thirty days should be thrown into the pit of the lions, and that the order of the Persians and the Medes should not be changed.

Darius did not see through their crafty counsel and had the order written. Daniel, knowing it was set, went into his house, whose upper windows were open towards Jerusalem, and three times a day bent his knees and prayed and confessed God as he had done before; and those men watched and found him praying, and brought it to the king. The king was much grieved for him and set himself until evening to deliver him, but they told him that no order and no decree the king made could be changed for the Medes and the Persians.

So Daniel was brought and thrown into the pit of the lions, and the king said to him: your God, whom you serve continually, may he deliver you. They brought a stone and laid it on the mouth of the pit, and the king sealed it with his own ring and with the rings of his lords, so that his accusers should do no worse; and he went to his house and lay down without eating, for neither food nor sleep would come to him. But God shut the mouths of the lions and they did not trouble Daniel. In the morning the king came in haste and cried with a loud voice, and Daniel answered that God had sent his angel and shut the lions' mouths, and that his righteousness had been found before God and no fault in him before the king.

The king rejoiced greatly, had him taken out with no harm found upon him, and threw the men who had accused him into the pit with their wives and their sons, and the lions tore them before they reached the bottom; and he wrote to all the peoples of his kingdom that they should fear the God of Daniel, who is the living God, whose kingdom shall not be destroyed. From that time the king held Daniel in greater honour than anyone, and showed him to be a better friend than all, and his three friends with him. The life adds, out of Josephus the Jewish historian, that Daniel, having great power in that land as the first lord, built at Ecbatana a most wonderful tower which still looked new in the historian's own day, where the kings of the Medes and the Persians were buried, and whose keeping was entrusted to one of the Jewish priests.

There were seven lions in the pit, and two bodies and two sheep were given them every day; but then they were given nothing, because they were to eat Daniel, and God stopped their mouths as before, and he was among them as among gentle lambs. At that time the prophet Habakkuk was in Judaea, and had made a pottage and broken bread into a bowl and was going out to the field to give it to the reapers; and the angel of the Lord told him to carry the dinner he had made to Daniel in Babylon, in the pit of the lions. Habakkuk answered that he had never seen Babylon and did not know where the lions' pit was; so the angel took him by the crown of the head and, holding him by the hair, set him in Babylon above the pit. He called out, Daniel, Daniel, take the dinner God has sent you; and Daniel answered, you have remembered me, O God, and have not forsaken those who love you, and rose and ate; and the angel carried Habakkuk back to his own place. On the seventh day the king came to mourn for Daniel, looked into the pit and saw him sitting there, and cried out that great is the Lord, the God of Daniel, and there is none other but him, and brought him out and threw the guilty in.

Daniel came with his friends to a great old age. The life says, citing Cyril of Alexandria and others, that after the death of Nebuchadnezzar and of the other kings who had held Daniel and his three friends in great honour, another king was chosen named Attic, who questioned the saints about their faith and, being reproached by them for his ungodliness, gave order that their heads be struck off. When Ananias was beheaded Azarias spread out his garment and received the head; Misael received the head of Azarias, and Daniel received the head of Misael; and last of all they cut off Daniel's head. It is said that after the beheading the heads joined themselves to their bodies again, and that the angel of the Lord took the bodies and carried them to Mount Gebal and laid them there under a stone.

Four hundred years later, at the Resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, these also rose and appeared to many, and fell asleep again. Their memory was appointed by the holy Fathers to be kept seven days before the Nativity of Christ the Saviour, the page says, because they were of the tribe of Judah, from which our Saviour draws his descent according to the flesh, so that they are shown to be his kin. doxologia.ro gives no year of birth and no year of death for him, and this folder gives none.

The Greek calendar keeps the four of them on the same day, and saint.gr sets Daniel among the four major prophets and dates him to the end of the seventh and the beginning of the sixth century before Christ. He belonged to the tribe of Judah, it says, was of royal descent and was born at Upper Bethara; still an infant, he was led away captive to Babylon with his parents. By Nebuchadnezzar's provision Daniel — whom the emperor renamed Belteshazzar — studied at the imperial court together with the three young Hebrews [Ananias](/saints/ananias-of-the-three-youths), [Azarias](/saints/azarias-of-the-three-youths) and [Misael](/saints/misael-of-the-three-youths); and because they did excellently at their studies, the king gave them great place in the state when they came of age. Daniel, it adds, had the gift of interpreting dreams, and later prophesied the coming of the Son of Man.

When Nebuchadnezzar made a golden image of his own and required all his officials and the people to worship it, Daniel was away on a mission, and it was the three youths who did not worship it. The same thing happened to Daniel afterwards, when Darius threw him into the pit of the lions because he had said his prayer while the king had given orders that for thirty days nobody should say a private prayer. Seeing the wonder, Darius kept Daniel at his court, where he remained and where he died in deep old age — most probably, saint.gr says, at Susa.
