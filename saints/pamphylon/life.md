# Pamphylon

The holy martyrs [Solochon](/saints/solochon), [Pamphamer](/saints/pamphamer) and Pamphylon — whose name saint.gr also gives as Pamphalon or Pamphalion — came from Egypt and contended in the years of the emperor Maximian, who reigned from 286 to 305. They were serving in the army, and while they were at Chalcedon, the emperor having ordered that the Christian soldiers be sought out, the tribune Campanus commanded them all to sacrifice to the idols. All complied except these three, who refused and confessed their faith in the Trinity; they were arrested at once and put to frightful tortures.

Pamphylon and Pamphamer found a martyr's death under those tortures, and Solochon, pierced from the ear to the brain, died afterwards in the house of a devout widow. The couplet saint.gr prints for the two of them reads: take courage though you are struck, you two martyrs, for God will make of your wounds a soothing balm and crowns. Of Pamphylon himself nothing else is printed — no age, no town within Egypt, no relics, no hymn — and nothing else is set down here.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 17 May inside the entry it heads for [Solochon](/saints/solochon), and it writes his name Pamphalon — the second of the two forms this life already records saint.gr as giving. When the imperial order came that all the soldiers should sacrifice to the idols, and Solochon declared himself a Christian, his two comrades [Pamphamer](/saints/pamphamer) and Pamphalon did the same; and the commander had them beaten and tortured with frightful tortures, under which the two of them expired. The Prologue calls the officer a commander where the Greek page calls him a tribune, and it names no place. It gives the year 298 for Solochon, who outlived them both, and no year for these two, so none is set down here.
