# Benedict of Konstamonitou

saint.gr says he was born in the village of Eziova — or Ezouva, or Ezouva — which is the present-day Daphni in the eparchy of Visaltia of Serres, or else in the village of Amourmei, or Amourbeki, the present-day Kastanochori; and it says the confusion about his exact birthplace is a reasonable one, because a dependency of the monastery of Konstamonitou on the Holy Mountain stood in the country between those two villages. Its sentence about when he was born — shortly before the Revolution of 1821 — cannot be read together with the rest of its own account, and is left here as the page has it.

As a boy he went with his father to the Holy Mountain and, naturally enough, to the monastery of Konstamonitou. There his father became a novice and was afterwards tonsured a monk, while Benedict, still young, was sent to Polygyros to be taught his letters. When he had finished his studies and had come to a suitable age he returned to the monastery to be tonsured, and his tonsure was made in Great Lent. In the monastery he served for years in every office, and when he had come to a great age he was ordained presbyter.

With other fathers he was sent, for the monastery's needs, to its dependency outside Kalamaria of Thessalonica. The Revolution of 1821, which set off matching risings in Macedonia and terrible reprisals from the Turkish authorities, forced the monks to abandon the dependency. Benedict was seized by the men of Rubut Pasha and taken in irons to Thessalonica with a number of monks from the dependencies round about, and on 12 June 1821, after frightful tortures, he was beheaded along with many monks and with the headmen of the villages about Thessalonica.

The night after the martyrdom, the page says on the authority of the Life, a shining cross was seen above the martyrs' unburied bodies. The soldiers guarding them were astonished at the sight and confessed it to the Christians; and on the strength of the wonder the authorities gave leave for the holy relics to be buried. He is one of four monks of Konstamonitou whom saint.gr keeps on this day, with [Synesius](/saints/synesius-of-triglia), [Timothy](/saints/timothy-of-konstamonitou) and [Paul](/saints/paul-of-ioannina).
