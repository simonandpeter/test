# Martha, mother of Symeon of the Wonderful Mountain

Martha was born in Antioch of right-believing parents. They pressed her to marry, and she would not consent, wishing to live in virginity; but a divine vision in the church of the Forerunner commanded her to obey her parents and take a husband. By God's gift she conceived a son, Symeon, whose commemoration the page places on 24 May — the stylite of the Wonderful Mountain, and not the elder Symeon whose mother is kept on 1 September. The gift was announced to her by the appearing of Saint John the Forerunner, as is written at length in Symeon's own life.

She did not live many years with her husband, but was left a widow, and brought up her child with all attention, keeping him as the holy Forerunner had commanded, since he was to be a vessel of the Holy Spirit. She prayed to God always for her son, that He would receive him from her into His service, as He once received Samuel from the prophetess Anna.

Once, wondering what the child would be when he was grown, she saw a vision in her sleep: it seemed to her that she was winged and flying upwards, holding the child in her hands and offering him up as a gift to the Lord, saying: O son, such a raising up of yours I have wished to see; let my Maker now release me in peace, for I have found grace with Him and have been counted worthy to give Him the fruit of my womb. That vision, and the revelations she had been given before it, she hid in her heart and gave thanks to God.

She went constantly to the church of God and never left off the church's order of prayer; so she had made her dwelling near the church, where she was first of all at the singing and last of all to leave. After her death many wonders were done at her tomb: the blind were given light, demons were driven out of the possessed and quick healing was given from every infirmity. She appeared to a sick man who had let the lamp at her tomb go out, and told him that when they light the lamp at her grave they do it for their own salvation, since it moves her to intercede for them before the Lord; and touching him with a portion of the life-giving Body of Christ which she held in her right hand, she said, By this be alive and well, and vanished, and the man rose up healed.

Doxologia's life gives no year for her birth or her death, so none is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 4 July, and saint.gr adds that her memory is borne also on 1 September. Its account says she was adorned with many Christian virtues and bore [Symeon](/saints/symeon-of-the-wonderful-mountain) after a promise from God himself; that she was charitable to a great degree and helped her neighbour without limit; and that when she reposed in peace she was buried at Daphne near Antioch. Later, it says, her son is said to have moved her holy relic to the pillar where he practised his asceticism, and there, by the prayers of her holy son, her grave worked wonders.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the Julian 4 July, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep her on, and it gives the year neither of them had: she reposed in peace, it says, in the year 551. Its account of the marriage is the folder’s two scenes made one, with the betrothal added. Being wholly given in soul to the faith she did not think of marriage; and when her parents betrothed her to a young man she determined to leave her father’s house and withdraw from the world — but the Forerunner appeared to her and counselled her to do her parents’ will and enter the marriage, which she did. Of that marriage was born the wonderful saint Symeon, the ascetic of the Wonderful Mountain.

She had a settled habit of rising at midnight to pray. With great mercy she helped the poor, visited the orphaned and served the sick. A year before her death she saw a multitude of angels with candles in their hands and learned from them the time of her own death; and knowing it, she gave herself to prayer and good works with still greater zeal. This book has her buried beside her son’s pillar from the first, where the Greek page has her buried at Daphne near Antioch and the son said to have moved her relic to the pillar later; both are written and neither is chosen.

After her death she appeared more than once, for the instruction of people and for the healing of the sick, and this book makes the appearance over the lamp the one it calls significant. The superior of her son’s monastery had set a lamp at her grave with a resolve that it should never be put out; after a time people grew slack and it went out, and the superior fell ill. She appeared to him and said: why do you not light the lamp at my grave? Know that I have no need of the light of your candles, having been counted worthy of the eternal heavenly light with God; but you have need of it, for when you kindle a light at my grave you move me to pray to the Lord for you.

From which, the entry ends, it is clear that the aim of our honouring the saints is to move them, as worthier than we are, to pray to God for us and for our salvation. The folder’s other books tell the same wonder of a sick man rather than of the superior of the monastery.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps her second on 4 июля ст. ст. — «Прп. Марфы , матери прп. Симеона Дивногорца (551).» — the same menologion day, the same year and the same son that the three other calendars of this folder give her, and its name link adds two epithets they do not print, «Марфа Антиохийская, Едесская», of Antioch and of Edessa. The day page prints three hymns of hers: a troparion in which her pure life and her love for the Most Pure Mother of God gained her the glorious gift of childbearing, a second troparion which is the common of a venerable woman, and a kontakion that has her stand before the Lord in prayer. A fourth heading in her name carries a text addressed to a venerable Andrew, and that one is not hers.
