# Martha, mother of Symeon of the Wonderful Mountain

Martha was born in Antioch of right-believing parents. They pressed her to marry, and she would not consent, wishing to live in virginity; but a divine vision in the church of the Forerunner commanded her to obey her parents and take a husband. By God's gift she conceived a son, Symeon, whose commemoration the page places on 24 May — the stylite of the Wonderful Mountain, and not the elder Symeon whose mother is kept on 1 September. The gift was announced to her by the appearing of Saint John the Forerunner, as is written at length in Symeon's own life.

She did not live many years with her husband, but was left a widow, and brought up her child with all attention, keeping him as the holy Forerunner had commanded, since he was to be a vessel of the Holy Spirit. She prayed to God always for her son, that He would receive him from her into His service, as He once received Samuel from the prophetess Anna.

Once, wondering what the child would be when he was grown, she saw a vision in her sleep: it seemed to her that she was winged and flying upwards, holding the child in her hands and offering him up as a gift to the Lord, saying: O son, such a raising up of yours I have wished to see; let my Maker now release me in peace, for I have found grace with Him and have been counted worthy to give Him the fruit of my womb. That vision, and the revelations she had been given before it, she hid in her heart and gave thanks to God.

She went constantly to the church of God and never left off the church's order of prayer; so she had made her dwelling near the church, where she was first of all at the singing and last of all to leave. After her death many wonders were done at her tomb: the blind were given light, demons were driven out of the possessed and quick healing was given from every infirmity. She appeared to a sick man who had let the lamp at her tomb go out, and told him that when they light the lamp at her grave they do it for their own salvation, since it moves her to intercede for them before the Lord; and touching him with a portion of the life-giving Body of Christ which she held in her right hand, she said, By this be alive and well, and vanished, and the man rose up healed.

Doxologia's life gives no year for her birth or her death, so none is recorded here.
