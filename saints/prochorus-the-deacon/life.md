# Prochorus the Deacon

In the days of the holy apostles, after the Lord's Ascension and the receiving of the Holy Spirit, while they had not yet scattered through the whole world to preach Christ and the churches of the faithful in Jerusalem had grown many, seven men full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom were chosen for the service of the diaconate: Stephen, Philip, Prochorus, [Nicanor](/saints/nicanor-the-deacon), [Timon](/saints/timon-the-deacon), [Parmenas](/saints/parmenas-the-deacon) and Nicolas of Antioch. The apostles prayed, laid their hands on them and made them deacons.

Of these, the memory of the first martyr and archdeacon Stephen is kept on 27 December and that of Philip on 11 October; the rest — except Nicolas, who is not written in the number of the saints — the holy Church has been accustomed to keep on this day, although they ended by suffering at different times, preaching Christ apostolically in various places.

Prochorus followed first the chief apostle Peter, with the other disciples, before the Dormition of the most holy Theotokos, and was set by him as bishop of the city of Nicomedia in the land of Bithynia, as the blessed Symeon Metaphrastes attests in the life of the apostle Peter for 29 June. After the Dormition of the Mother of God, Prochorus was for a long time the fellow-traveller and the sharer of the labours of [John the Theologian](/saints/john-the-theologian), going through many countries with him and suffering pains and torments from the unbelieving for the good news of Christ. He was exiled with him to the island of Patmos, and what John told him out of the revelation of God he wrote down from his mouth. Afterwards he laboured again in the city of Nicomedia, of which he had been the first bishop, turning the people who had gone astray to Christ; and going to Antioch he ended as a martyr, killed by the unbelieving for the preaching of Christ.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 28 July, and keeps the four deacons together. saint.gr sets over their notice the Lord's word that whoever would be great among you shall be your servant, and says that Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon and Parmenas — apostles of the Seventy — were among the seven chosen deacons of the first Christian Church of Jerusalem, in Acts 6:5. Their work was to serve and to see to the feeding of the poor members of the Church, especially the orphans and the widows, but they served in the spreading of the divine word as well. Prochorus, the page says, afterwards followed the evangelist [John](/saints/john-the-theologian) into Asia Minor, where he became bishop of Nicomedia and was shown a perfect deacon of the episcopal office. Each of the four, it ends, was shown to be a faithful minister in the Lord. The Greek page adds that portions of his relics are at the monasteries of the Great Lavra and of Saint Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain, and above the notice it sets a distich for each man; his own runs: «Mίμημα ὁ Πρόχορος ἐπιστηθίου, Ὤφθη ἀληθῶς. Eὖγε τῆς προθυμίας!» — Prochorus was shown truly an imitator of the disciple who leaned on the breast of the Lord; well done for his eagerness.

The Охридски пролог for the Julian 28 July keeps the four deacons together and says in words why: these first four of the Seven have no separate days of celebration, but are all commemorated on this one day, 28 July. Of Prochorus it says that he was ordained bishop of Nicomedia by the apostle Петар; that he was for a time in the service of Јован the Evangelist, and wrote down on the island of Patmos the revelations he heard from his own lips; that he then returned to Nicomedia and laboured greatly at the turning of the people to the faith; and that he ended as a martyr at Antioch, killed by unbelievers. Of the other three of the Seven the entry says that Стефан the protomartyr is kept on 27 December and Филип on 11 October, and that Николај did not enter the ranks of the saints because of his heresy.
