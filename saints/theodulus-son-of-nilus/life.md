# Theodulus, son of Nilus of Sinai

Theodulus was the son of the wise [Nilus](/saints/nilus-the-hermit-of-sinai), whom the calendar keeps on 12 November: a man who became prefect of Constantinople and then left the glory of the world and became a monk on Mount Sinai, taking his son with him. The two venerable men lived, saint.gr says, before the middle of the fifth century, and the page prints a couplet that makes the son's whole distinction consist in the father: that Theodulus was shown great in virtues, imitating above all his father's manner of life.

The page's account of the years between is longer than what is set down here, and this life does not attempt it; what it closes with is that Nilus's writings hold precious counsels on the way Christian anchorites ought to live if they are to reach their holy purpose. Both of them died in peace, and in the reign of the emperor Justinian their holy relics were brought to Constantinople and laid in the church of the Holy Apostles.

The page prints no hymn for Theodulus.
