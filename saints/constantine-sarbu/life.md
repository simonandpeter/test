# Constantine Sârbu

Constantine Sârbu was born on 10 January 1905 in the commune of Cavadinești in the former county of Covurlui, into a plain family of farmers. Orphaned young, he was brought up by his grandmother, a poor woman but uncommonly devout, and at her urging he went in 1919 to the theological seminary at Galați and from 1925 to the Faculty of Orthodox Theology in Bucharest. Finishing in 1929 and finding no parish in the archdiocese of Bucharest, he took work as a chanter at the Lucaci church in the capital, where he stayed almost five years.

In 1934 he moved to Huși at the invitation of Bishop Nifon Criveanu, who ordained him deacon on 15 August and priest the next day, and made him director of the school of chanters there and a teacher of sectology, catechism and vocal music. From 1 November 1938 he asked to be transferred to the newly founded parish of Parcul Călărași in the Vergului quarter of Bucharest — a poor working district with neither church nor parish house — and there he built the church of the Holy Emperors Constantine and Helen. Left a widower, he took up a life of ascetic struggle and unceasing prayer.

For the courage of his preaching he was arrested by the Securitate on 10 January 1954, the charge being anti-communist activity, and on 9 October 1954 he was sentenced to eight years and three months for plotting against the social order. He was carried through the prisons of Jilava, Dej and Gherla and the labour camps of Poarta Albă and Salcia, and on 4 December 1961 was sent to forced residence in the village of Viișoara on the Bărăgan plain for two years more.

Father Gheorghe Calciu, who knew him there, remembered: "I stayed with Father Constantine Sârbu at Viișoara a few days, until a house was assigned to me, and in that time the wounds of my soul began to heal. Strangely, he preached no sermons, he did not urge me to pray, he showed himself in no way a bigot, but my heart kept festival beside him. He moved slowly, hieratically, with his small quiet voice, like a holy father of the patristic centuries."

Only on 25 February 1964, with the decree freeing political prisoners, was he allowed to go where he wished, and the freedom was more apparent than real, for the Securitate went on following and harassing him. Back in Bucharest, after many obstacles, on 8 April 1964 he was permitted to serve in the Sapienței church, which had been shut for forty years. His last years were marked by the ulcer the prison regime had given him and by that same surveillance. On 18 October 1975 he was operated on by a doctor at the Elias hospital and, suspiciously, took septicaemia from the operation; on 23 October 1975, after confession and communion, he passed to eternity on his hospital bed.
