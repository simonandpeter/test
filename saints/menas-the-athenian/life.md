# Menas the Athenian

doxologia.ro prints one life for the three martyrs of 10 decembrie, and this is it. Diocletian and Maximian had laid down the sceptre, and the persecution went on in the East under Maximinus, a hot partisan of his fathers' many gods. Word came to him that Alexandria was in an uproar, the Christians there having grown very many, and since he had to stay at Byzantium he sent one of his officers to quiet the city, put down the Christian name and bring back to the old worship those who had left it. The man he sent was Menas, an Athenian by birth, a good rhetor and skilled in Greek learning — and secretly a Christian, who had hidden his faith until the call should come.

He reached Alexandria and quieted the trouble without labour, making peace between Christians and pagans and ordering that each should keep his own faith unhindered; then, having set the city's affairs in order and written to the emperor, he decided to bring his own faith into the open. If I give myself to the torments another time, he said in himself, I alone am crowned; now I can bring others to the crown with me. So he praised the name of Jesus Christ openly and taught the faith, and healed the lame, the blind, the dumb, the deaf and the possessed in the middle of the city by calling on the Name and making the sign of the Cross, and many believed. Those who loved the old feasts sent secretly to the emperor to say what he was doing.

The emperor, in anger, sent the eparch of the city, [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-alexandria) — also an Athenian, brought up in the worship of the idols but of a kind and merciful disposition — to set right what had been overturned and to bring the officer back to the gods. He sat in judgement instead, and Menas answered him out of the Scriptures, comparing the Christian writings with the Greek learning; and the people listened. When the crowd grew bold the judge had him bound and tortured, and had his tongue cut out, and then his eyes put out, and threw him back into the prison, saying he would give his body to the birds in the morning.

At the third hour of the night a light like lightning shone in the prison, and Christ stood by him, healed his tongue, gave him back his eyes, made him whole, and told him that the judge who warred against him would be humbled before him by morning and would afterwards be his companion in the struggle, because it was not right that so many good works should perish for want of knowledge.

So it came about. The judge, seeing him whole in the middle of the theatre, was struck with fear and then with belief, and would have fallen at his feet; thirteen bishops gathered from the country round and out of the desert and baptised him before the people with three immersions, and a great multitude was baptised with him. Within a few days [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-alexandria) was chosen bishop of Alexandria, gave all he had to the poor, pulled down the pagan temples and built churches in their place, and brought a countless number of Greeks to Christ. A hardened Greek named Rusticus carried the news to the emperor, who came himself to Alexandria in a fury.

He tormented them both, and both were preserved; and when they appeared alive and whole before him in the theatre the whole people cried out with one voice that the God of the Christians is truly great. Then [Eugraphus](/saints/eugraphus), a man of Greek learning who had been one of the notaries under Menas when he was judge of the city, was filled with zeal at the sight, signed himself with the Cross, stepped out into the middle and told the emperor to his face that he too was a Christian, that dying for Christ was life, and that they laughed at him as at an old fox. The emperor snatched a sword from one of the men standing by him and cut him down with his own hand, hacking him to pieces; and he gave thanks as he was cut that he went to God before the others and by many wounds rather than one.

Then, since many were touching the other two with their hands and finding their bodies whole and their wounds healed, the emperor gave up and ordered their heads struck off. They prayed at the place of execution for peace to the churches and that no one who asked their help should go away empty, gave one another the kiss of peace and stretched out their necks. Menas, while still alive, had asked that his body be carried to Byzantium.

The emperor had an iron coffin made, put the bodies in it and threw it into the sea so that Christians should not honour them; but the iron did not sink, and went to Byzantium ahead of him. The bishop of Constantinople, warned in a vision, came down to the shore by night with his clergy and saw a pillar of light and two shining men drawing the coffin to land, and buried the relics by the city wall, to be a guard to those who sail and physicians to the sick. Maximinus went blind on his road, said he had been beaten by unseen hands, and died a few days after.

Of the three, Menas is the officer the emperor sent and the first to confess: an Athenian, a rhetor and a judge, who healed in the open street, bore the cutting out of his tongue and the putting out of his eyes, was made whole by Christ in the prison, and was beheaded at Alexandria, his body carried at his own asking to Byzantium. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mucenic Mina» and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 December, and tells how the three came to be one entry. Menas was an Athenian, saint.gr says, and an idolater by family; but when he had been educated and had read enough he found that the many gods were rather a lie and a delusion, and in the study of the philosophers he could find nothing true either. Then he went on to the study of Christian writings, and after them of the Gospel, where he found what filled his soul — the light and the truth — and so Menas became a Christian.

Later the emperor Maximinus, whose reign the page dates 311 to 313 and whom it also allows may have been Maximian, 286 to 305, not knowing that he was a Christian, made him eparch of Alexandria. But when that emperor ordered persecutions in the city, Menas not only did not carry out the order but helped the Christian ranks to grow.

Then Maximinus sent a new eparch, the Athenian scholar [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-alexandria), who kept to the letter of the law, tortured Menas hard and shut him in prison to die there of his wounds. After a time, when Hermogenes sent to find out whether and when Menas had died, they found that he had not died at all and that his wounds were healed; and asked publicly how this had happened, Menas answered that he was healed at the hour when, lying on the ground, he was chanting: though I walk in the midst of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me, Lord. The answer made Christians of Hermogenes and of [Eugraphus](/saints/eugraphus), who was Menas's secretary; and afterwards all of them together were beheaded.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the three of them on the same menologion day under one heading, and where the Romanian and Greek lines gave three names it gives the whole story. He and [Hermogenes](/saints/hermogenes-of-alexandria) were Athenians by birth, and both lived at Constantinople in great honour with the emperor and with the people. He was known for his great learning and his eloquence, and outwardly he passed for a pagan while in his heart he was a convinced Christian; Hermogenes was eparch of Constantinople and held to paganism outwardly and inwardly, though he was merciful and did many good works.

When a quarrel broke out between Christians and pagans at Alexandria, the emperor Maximinus sent him to quiet the tumult and to root Christianity out of that city. He went and made the peace — and then declared himself a Christian, and turned many pagans to the true faith by his eloquence and by many wonders. Hearing of it, the emperor sent Hermogenes to punish him and put Christianity down. Hermogenes brought him to trial, cut off his feet and his tongue, put out his eyes and threw him into prison; and in the prison the Lord Jesus himself appeared, and healed and comforted his sufferer.

Seeing him wonderfully healed, Hermogenes was baptised and began to preach the mighty faith of Christ, and was made bishop in Alexandria. Then Maximinus in a fury came to Alexandria himself and put the two of them to great torments, which they bore bravely, helped by the grace of God. Seeing the courage of these soldiers of Christ and the wonders of God upon them, [Eugraphus](/saints/eugraphus), his secretary and friend, came forward before the court and shouted in the emperor's face: I too am a Christian. The emperor raged, drew his sword and cut him down; and then he ordered the executioners to behead the other two with the sword. Their holy relics were thrown into the sea and came by a wonder to Constantinople, where the bishop, warned of it in a dream, met them in state and buried them honourably.
