# Theoctistus (Smelnitsky)

Theoctistus Smelnitsky was born in January 1874 — on the 17th in azbyka.ru’s short life, the 4th in its full one — in the village of Tarasovichi in the Oster district of Chernigov province, the son of Elisha Smelnitsky, psalm-reader of the Resurrection church. He finished the Chernigov seminary in 1898, married Claudia Yagodovskaya, with whom he lived all his life, and had a son, Valentin, and a daughter, Nonna. Ordained priest in 1898 by Bishop Antony (Sokolov) of Chernigov, he served at Verkhlichi, from 1900 at Gorsk, where he started a church choir, and from 1905 at Gogolev, teaching in the village schools wherever he went. In the troubles of 1905–1907 he and his family kept watch day and night against the pogroms, guarding Jewish houses and hiding whole families, and for his stand the bishop, at the governor’s demand, would at first give him no parish; in 1906 he was sent to the unfinished church at Voropayev, where an inquiry cleared him. From 1913 he was at the Chernigov seminary, its teacher in the short life and its bursar in the full one, serving in the seminary church. In 1917 the family moved to Kyiv, where for about three years he was rector at Darnytsia and again shielded Jews from looters and went round the prisons freeing whom he could; in 1919 the Kyiv Cheka held him for three months and released him on the petition of many people, some of whom he had himself freed.

Late in 1919 his first parishioners at Voropayev called him back; he kept his family by joinery and carried bread to the children of hungry Kyiv. Arrested in August 1921 on denunciations for agitation against the Soviet power, he refused food in the Oster prison and admitted nothing, and letters from the workers of Darnytsia, from officials and from Jews who owed him their lives won his release on 13 October 1921. In 1922, during the seizure of church valuables, a silver chalice found in the altar brought him a year’s conditional sentence. In the 1930s he was rector of the Trinity church at Losinovka until the authorities closed it in 1936; arrested that March and given a conditional sentence, he was arrested again on 27 May 1936, accused of telling prisoners that the Soviet power was a power of bandits, and on 17 July sentenced to five years in the camps. Found too frail for work, he reached Karlag in Kazakhstan on 10 September 1936. On 19 September 1937 he was charged with anti-Soviet agitation in the camp, on 28 September an NKVD troika at Karaganda condemned him to death, and he was shot at the Tartaul section of Karlag at half past eleven at night on 3 October 1937 and buried in an unknown grave.

He was rehabilitated in 1990 and glorified among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia in August 2000. azbyka.ru notes that the investigation file gives him a birth in Tomsk in 1863 and studies at Tomsk and the Kyiv academy that do not fit his record, and that the details of several people may have been confused. The Russian calendar keeps him on 20 September old style, on one line with the priest [Alexander (Tetyuev)](/saints/alexander-tetyuev).
