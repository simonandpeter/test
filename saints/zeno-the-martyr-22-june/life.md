# Zeno (22 June)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 22 iunie reads «Sfântul Mucenic Zenon», and the page behind it carries the day, the epistle appointed for it and the gospel read with it, and nothing more — no country, no century, no manner of death, no year and no hymn. The corpus already keeps six other men of this name, and nothing on this page ties him to any of them.

The same day's calendar prints «Sfântul Mucenic Zina», whose page is equally bare and whose icon there is titled «Sfinții Mucenici Zinon și Zina; Sfântul Ierarh Eusebie» — the two martyrs together with the bishop of Samosata. That the calendar keeps them on one day is all that is set down here.

The readings appointed are 2 Timothy 2:1–10 and John 15:17–27 with 16:1–2.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 22 June, and saint.gr gives the life doxologia.ro did not. It says the two of them lived at the beginning of the fourth century; that Zeno was a rich Christian, from Philadelphia in Arabia, rich not only in money but in piety, and that [Zenas](/saints/zenas-the-martyr-22-june) was his servant, and that what bound them was more a brotherly love, as it binds all the members of the Church. Burning with the longing to give himself wholly to the spreading of the Gospel, Zeno shared out all his possessions among the poor and gave their freedom to all his slaves; but Zenas refused to leave his former master and begged to stay with him, and Zeno agreed to keep him, as a friend and a brother in Christ. In the year 303, at Philadelphia in Arabia, the eparch Maximus declared a persecution of the Christians, and Zeno presented himself before him and set about showing him the futility of his campaign against the Christian truth. The eparch, for all other answer, had him beaten and imprisoned; and when Zenas heard of it he ran to the eparch and begged him to set his master free. The eparch imprisoned Zenas too, and a few days later had them both beheaded.
