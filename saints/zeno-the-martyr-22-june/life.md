# Zeno (22 June)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 22 iunie reads «Sfântul Mucenic Zenon», and the page behind it carries the day, the epistle appointed for it and the gospel read with it, and nothing more — no country, no century, no manner of death, no year and no hymn. The corpus already keeps six other men of this name, and nothing on this page ties him to any of them.

The same day's calendar prints «Sfântul Mucenic Zina», whose page is equally bare and whose icon there is titled «Sfinții Mucenici Zinon și Zina; Sfântul Ierarh Eusebie» — the two martyrs together with the bishop of Samosata. That the calendar keeps them on one day is all that is set down here.

The readings appointed are 2 Timothy 2:1–10 and John 15:17–27 with 16:1–2.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 22 June, and saint.gr gives the life doxologia.ro did not. It says the two of them lived at the beginning of the fourth century; that Zeno was a rich Christian, from Philadelphia in Arabia, rich not only in money but in piety, and that [Zenas](/saints/zenas-the-martyr-22-june) was his servant, and that what bound them was more a brotherly love, as it binds all the members of the Church. Burning with the longing to give himself wholly to the spreading of the Gospel, Zeno shared out all his possessions among the poor and gave their freedom to all his slaves; but Zenas refused to leave his former master and begged to stay with him, and Zeno agreed to keep him, as a friend and a brother in Christ. In the year 303, at Philadelphia in Arabia, the eparch Maximus declared a persecution of the Christians, and Zeno presented himself before him and set about showing him the futility of his campaign against the Christian truth. The eparch, for all other answer, had him beaten and imprisoned; and when Zenas heard of it he ran to the eparch and begged him to set his master free. The eparch imprisoned Zenas too, and a few days later had them both beheaded.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the two of them together on the Julian 22 June, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep them on, and gives the fuller of the two accounts the corpus now has. Zeno, it says, was a Roman officer in the city of Philadelphia in Arabia, and [Zenas](/saints/zenas-the-martyr-22-june) was his servant — where the Greek page has him a rich Christian who shared out his goods among the poor and freed all his slaves, and Zenas the one who begged to stay. An officer and a householder who frees his household are two different accounts, and both are written here.

When the persecution of the Christians arose in the time of the emperor Maximian, this book says, Zeno made bold to come before the duke Maximus, confessed his faith in the one living God, and counselled Maximus himself to leave the dead idols and receive the one true faith. The duke grew angry and threw him into prison; and when his faithful servant visited his master there, he too was seized and held. Then both were tortured for Christ and at the last thrown into a fire, into which the pagans poured oil — where the Greek page has the eparch behead them both a few days after. Their souls were crowned in the kingdom of Christ, and their bodily remains were buried in the church of Saint George in a place called Kyparision. This book gives no year of any kind.

The Russian calendar's own account adds the tortures, which the books already quoted here pass over. The persecutor ordered the confessors racked, it says: they were hung on a pillar and their bodies scraped with iron hooks, the wounds rubbed with vinegar and salt, their sides and breasts burned with fire; they were thrown into a pit of fire and had boiling oil poured over them. They bore it all patiently and by God's power remained alive, and at the last they were beheaded with the sword. That page dates the end to 304, which is the year the day line gives as well and the only one it states.
