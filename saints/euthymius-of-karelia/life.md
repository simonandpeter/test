# Euthymius of Karelia

saint.gr says the venerable Euthymius the Wonderworker, of Arkhangelsk as the old manuscript calendars call him, was born in the second half of the fourteenth century. About the year 1400 he went north to lead the life of an anchorite, but later, around 1410, surrounded by disciples who wanted to follow the same calling, he founded a monastery dedicated to Saint Nicholas — on the shores of the Black Sea, the page says, in Karelia in Finland.

At first nothing favoured the growth of the monastic life in that region, where the people lived by primitive customs. But Euthymius knew how to be steady and to trust in God's help; and so he stayed in the place and worked for the strengthening of the monastic life and the spreading of the faith. He fell asleep in peace in the year 1435, and was buried in the monastery in Karelia, where his disciples Stephen the Ascetic, Isaiah and Nikanor were buried too.

The recognition of his relics took place in 1647. In the calendar of the Brotherhood of Saint Herman of Alaska, the page adds, he is commemorated as the Enlightener of Karelia together with the saints Antony and Eutyches — where saint.gr's own entry for [Antony](/saints/antony-of-korel) and [Felix](/saints/felix-of-korel) of Korel, kept on this same day, gives the second of the two another name.
