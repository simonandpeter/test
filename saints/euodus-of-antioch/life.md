# Euodus

Euodus belongs to the chorus of the seventy apostles and was the first
bishop of great Antioch in Syria after the apostle Peter, a loud-voiced
herald of the Gospel who shone in all the virtues. His successor Ignatius
the God-bearer, the disciple of John the Theologian, remembers him in his
letter to the Antiochenes: remember your blessed father Euodus, who was
appointed you by the apostles as your first shepherd; be his true sons and
not adulterers, that the father be not ashamed of you. He held the see
twenty-seven years and wrote several works, of which only the title of one
is known, the Lamp, which the church historian Nicephorus Callistus
mentions; in one of them he wrote of the most holy Virgin that she was
brought into the temple at three, stayed there eleven years, was given at
twelve to Joseph to guard, lived four months in his house when the angel
brought her the glad tidings, and at the end of her fifteenth year bore the
light of the world. The rest perished in the calamitous times when the
Church of God was persecuted. The Roman martyrologies say that he shed his
blood for Christ, and some place his death at Antioch under Vespasian, when
a Jew named Antiochus, having abandoned his faith and sacrificed to idols,
raised a great disturbance in the city and many Jews were killed for
refusing the idols; very many then received the Christian faith and had the
blessed Euodus for their bishop, who as their chief and shepherd was soon
put to death. The Russian church calendar gives his martyrdom under Nero, in
the year 66. Running the straight road of the word, the Greek verse says, he
taught all to run it. The Greek calendar keeps him on 7 September with
[Onesiphorus](/saints/onesiphorus-of-colophon), and the Russian calendar the
same day of the old style, 20 September, and in the synaxis of the seventy
apostles.
