# Golinduhia of Persia

In the land of Persia, in the reign of Khosrow the Elder, there was a young and beautiful woman of a famous family and great nobility, called by the Persian name Golinduhia, married to a notable man, the chief of the magi. Three years into that marriage she was made wise by divine enlightenment and began to see the error of the Persian religion, and she searched for which was the right faith; and hearing of the undefiled Christian faith she wondered whether that was the true one, and wished to be taught and brought to the knowledge of the truth.

After many days of such thinking she had a vision in the night. A shining angel of God stood before her and took her to a dark and burning place, full of great fear and terror, where a multitude of people were being tormented; and when she asked what the place was and who they were, he told her it was the punishment of sinners and unbelievers, and that her own ancestors, who had worshipped the Persian idols, were among them. She grieved and sighed for them. Then he took her to another place, Paradise, the dwelling of the righteous, and through a small door showed her a great light within, where many men and women were rejoicing beyond telling.

The angel went in by that door and she wanted to follow him, and he would not let her: You cannot come in here, he said, for you are not a Christian; no one enters who has not the holy baptism of our Lord Jesus Christ. She woke in fear and wonder, and in time she was baptised, and was given the name Maria.

After her baptism she went back to her own house, but being betrothed to Christ she would not live as a wife with her unclean husband. She kept the fast and the prayer her spiritual father had taught her, standing whole nights without sleep and keeping silence by day, and would not let her husband near her; and he, not understanding the change, tried flattery, entreaty, threats, blows and force, and got nothing, for she was held by the unseen power of God. He even worked spells and called the devils to help him, and they did not dare come near her, seeing her lit with the grace of Christ.

He went then to the emperor Khosrow and complained that his wife was a Christian, and the emperor sent counsellor after counsellor, and honourable men and noble women, to talk her round, and all of them came back with nothing. At last he sent to say that if she left the Christian faith and returned to the Persian he would take her to wife and make her his empress. Tell me one thing, she said to the messengers: if the emperor takes me to wife, will he not die? Will he live for ever? If he is deathless, I will listen to him. No man can be deathless, they answered, and the emperor is a man, and will die. Then I do not want a mortal husband and a short-lived one, she said, for I am married to the deathless Emperor, Christ my God, who abides for ever, and for Him I am ready to suffer and to die.

She spent eighteen years in prison. In those years Khosrow the Elder died and his son Hormizd followed him, and among the Greeks reigned Justin the Younger, then Tiberius the second of that name, and after them Maurice. An envoy came into Persia in that time, an honourable man named Aristobulus, who, hearing that the martyr Golinduhia — Maria — was in prison, asked leave to see her, kissed the chains she wore for Christ, took a piece of them for a blessing, and while he stayed in the country came to her often and taught her the psalms of David.

After he had gone Hormizd handed her over to the magi to be tortured. They brought her out every day and beat her without mercy, and the next day found her whole; once her breast was split in two with the beating, and the day after she was brought out sound in body, so that the Persians who watched wondered and glorified the power of Christ, and many of them inclined to the Christian faith. They burnt her head with coals, sewed her into a sealed sack and threw her into a deep pit to die there, and the strong hand of God kept her alive.

They threw her at last to a great and terrible dragon which they kept and fed in a deep cave; but He who shut the lions' mouths for Daniel sent His angel and shut the dragon's, so that it did not touch her, and it lay at her feet like a gentle lamb. She was four months in that pit with it, its daily food thrown down to it while she lived without food or drink. When at length she was hungry the angel of the Lord appeared, touched her mouth with the sign of the cross and told her that from then on she would neither hunger nor thirst except when she herself chose to eat a little, to show that she was still in the body; and he brought her up out of the pit.

She was carried afterwards by the angel through other cities and countries. In Hierapolis of Syria she visited the bishop Stephen, who later wrote her life. Once, uncertain whether to communicate with a company she had come among, she saw an angel holding two cups, one full of darkness and one full of light — the dark cup the heretical faith and the cup of light that of the catholic Church — and she grieved at the heretical teaching and left quickly.

When her blessed end drew near she fell a little ill while she was in the church of the holy martyr Sergius, which stands between Nisibis and the city called Dara. She prayed long for the salvation of the whole world, gave thanks to God for the great mercy He had shown her, and gave up her holy soul with joy into the hands of the Lord she had loved and for whom she had suffered so much; and so she was numbered with the women martyrs in His heavenly kingdom. The page gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 13 July, and tells the story the Romanian line only named. saint.gr says she was from Persia and lived in the years of Khosrow king of the Persians and of Maurice of the Romans, whom it brackets 582 to 602. An idolatress at first, she was turned to Christianity after a vision she saw, and when she was baptised took the name Mary. Her husband denounced her to Khosrow, who exiled her to a fortress called the fortress of Forgetfulness, where she stayed shut up for eighteen whole years; and afterwards, because she refused to change her faith, she suffered many and various tortures. When she was freed from them she visited Jerusalem, where she lived as a nun in a monastery, and at the suggestion of the patriarch of Jerusalem she set out for Constantinople to pray for the emperors. She died on the road, between Nisibis and Dara, inside the oratory of Saint Sergius, while she was at prayer. The page adds that the Patmos codex 266 says her martyrdom was written up by Eustratios, presbyter of the Great Church, who also wrote the life of Saint Eutychios.
