# Maria Ivanovna of Diveyevo, Fool for Christ

Maria was born in the village of Goletkovo in the province of Tambov. At thirteen she lost both parents, Zacharias and Pelagia, and went to live with her elder brother’s family, who did not want her: she neglected herself, never combed her hair, dressed in rags and behaved strangely from very young. So she left, and wandered between Sarov, Diveyevo and Ardatov, half-naked and hungry, in broken shoes winter and summer, passing the nights in the forest in prayer and almost always caked with mud.

She often came to the Diveyevo convent, where the nuns who pitied her gave her clean clothes, which within days she had given away to the poor; others drove her off, and she never complained of anyone. At some point she was received into the convent and tonsured, and went on feigning folly to hide her virtues, above all the gift of foresight. People came for counsel: to a small boy she said, look, the priest Alexis has come — and he became a hieromonk of that name; to a lady from Murom who smoked like a chimney, even before the Liturgy, she had the expensive cigarette case thrown in the fire, and the lady wrote later that she no longer thought of tobacco; of a young man she said that he had taken up with a gypsy woman, and it was the brand on the cigarettes he had just begun to buy. After 1917 she began to use foul language, to point at the new troubles of the Church; asked how a nun could speak so, she said: under Tsar Nicholas it was easy — try it under the Soviets. She died in peace in 1927.

The Russian calendar keeps her on 26 August (Julian) as the blessed Maria of Diveyevo, who died in 1931, and prints no life under the line.
