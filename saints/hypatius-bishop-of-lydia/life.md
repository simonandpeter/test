# Hypatius

Hypatius and [Andrew](/saints/andrew-the-presbyter-confessor) were
schoolfellows and models of brotherhood even as boys, raised together in a
monastery in the Christian faith and marked out for diligence and zeal;
Hypatius took the monastic habit, and Andrew became a cleric and taught the
people the faith with zeal. Both lived under the iconoclast emperor Leo the
Isaurian (717–741), and Hypatius was in time made bishop — of Ephesus, the
Russian church calendar says; in Lydia, where he came from, says the Greek
synaxarion — and carried his office with a full sense of what it asked. When
Leo began to persecute those who honoured the holy icons, throwing them out
of the churches, trampling them and burning them, the two stood up for their
veneration and urged their flock to keep faith with Orthodoxy. The emperor,
hoping to win them over, summoned them and held a disputation on the icons,
in which the two defended the Orthodox veneration point by point; they were
thrown into prison and kept there a long time in the hope that it would
break them, and stayed unbending. Then he ordered them tortured: they were
beaten, the skin torn from their heads with the hair, their beards smeared
with pitch and set alight, and holy icons burned on their heads, and they
bore it all patiently and lived; the emperor had them dragged through the
city bound, for the people to mock, and only then killed. Their bodies were
thrown to the dogs, and the faithful buried them with reverence. The Greek
calendar keeps the two together on 20 September, and the Russian calendar on
21 September of the old style, 4 October.
