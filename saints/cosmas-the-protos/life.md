# Cosmas the Protos of Athos

Cosmas was Protos of the Holy Community of Athos at the time when the supporters of union with the Roman Catholics, following the church policy of the emperor Michael VIII Palaiologos and of the patriarch John Bekkos, pressed the monks of the Holy Mountain to support that union. Like Saint [Euthymius and the twelve monks of Vatopedi](/saints/euthymius-of-vatopedi), and many others, especially of the monasteries of Iviron and Zographou, who suffered persecution and a martyr's death for their opposition to the union, Cosmas was put to death by hanging.

His martyrdom was confirmed by the Holy Community's exhumation of his remains in the year 1981, in the place where tradition held the martyrdom to have happened and where an unsleeping lamp was kept burning. His memory is kept on 5 December. The page gives no year for his birth or for his death, and none is written here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 5 December, together with the venerable martyrs who suffered with him, and its page puts a year to his death and the Protaton at the centre of it. saint.gr gives 1280, and says that the emperor Michael VIII Palaiologos himself, by one tradition, led the company of soldiers that took the cell-lavra of Karyes where the primacy of the Mountain is seated; that the Protos and those with him withstood him manfully and reproached him as the others had done; and that the king, moved to anger, ordered them all to become the work of the sword, burning their church and plundering the monks' dwellings. Those who died, it says, were monks of the monasteries of Vatopedi, Iviron, Zographou and Xenophontos, and it names none of them. Cosmas the Protos was hanged and the rest beheaded.

The page quotes the deacon Arkadios of Vatopedi, who says that a patriarchal exarchy decided a Protos should be sent from the monasteries of Phalakron or of Plakas, and that Cosmas was chosen from the monastery of Plakeus, which stood between Vatopedi and Esphigmenou where the place is now called Zevgarospita. His hanging, the page adds, was confirmed by the uncovering of his relics by the Holy Community in 1981, on the spot tradition named, where a lamp had always been kept burning.
