# Athanasius, founder of Vatopedi

doxologia.ro prints one notice for the three founders of Vatopedi, taken from Moses the Athonite's *The Saints of the Holy Mountain*, and this is read out of it. By holy tradition, it says, the founders of the monastery of Vatopedi are held to be the noble officials of Adrianople, Athanasius, Nicholas and Antony, who in the middle of the tenth century, while the Great Lavra was being built, came to its founder [Athanasius the Athonite](/saints/athanasius-the-athonite), who also drew up the typikon of the new monastery; and listening to the great man's urging they refounded and rebuilt a monastery that pirates had laid waste. The three are presented as rich men, as brothers, as monks and as founders.

They had come to the Holy Mountain meaning to build a monastery, drawn by the fame of Athanasius as many others were, and proposed to stay with him, offering the nine thousand gold pieces they had brought for the purpose. He answered them: this monastery is dedicated to its founder, the emperor Nicephorus; but if you want to build a monastery, see, the monastery of Vatopedi is a ruin — renew it, and you will have your reward from God.

This Athanasius is not the founder of the Lavra but one of the three brothers, and he is the second of them in the monastery's documents. [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-founder-of-vatopedi) signs as abbot of Vatopedi in 985 and again in 998, 1001 and 1012; his successor Athanasius, whom the notice describes as very active, signs the documents between 1020 and 1048; and later [Antony](/saints/antony-founder-of-vatopedi) signs. Their fame drew many monks to them, and the monastery very soon gained wealth and glory.

The notice reckons, from the documents, that Nicholas was the first superior and the first to be buried in the tomb in the naos, after 1012, and that Athanasius, who signs for the last time in 1048, was buried second, in the upper part of the same tomb, and Antony later. Of that tomb a Russian monk, Basil Barsky, tells in 1744 that although it is never opened the lamp before it burns without ceasing. In 1992 it was opened, and the bones of the three were found, and with them a lead tablet in which Athanasius orders that nobody else be buried in his tomb after the old custom.

Above the tomb, in an apse, the three are shown beside the founding emperors in monastic habit and with shining crowns, in a fresco painted in 1760; the tomb stands on the right, northern side of the naos. The last remaking of the tomb into a reliquary, and the painting of the three just above the wall which in 1312 bore the Mother of God of Blachernae, shows, the notice says, that the three founders were honoured as venerable very early. Their sung service was composed by X. M. Bousias, and their memory is kept on 17 December.
