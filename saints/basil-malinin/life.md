# Basil (Malinin)

The Malinins had been priests in the Kalyazin district of the Tver province since the eighteenth century, and the life the calendar links opens with how they came by the name: a schoolmaster looked at a boy whose face was red from a long winter road, decided the family name Sorokin did not suit him - a magpie is white, and you are raspberry - and wrote him down Malinin. Basil was born on 26 March 1898 in the village of Vasyanskoye, finished the Tver seminary in 1918, and chose ordination in 1921, when the choice already meant what it would go on meaning. The whole property of the priest and his family was a house, two goat kids and seven hens.

In 1932 he was accused of stealing collective-farm straw and sentenced to ten years; he served two at hard labour and was released early, returning to his church at Konakovo, where services resumed to his parishioners' joy and he was soon made a dean - a duty he later asked to be relieved of, as the deanery's churches were emptied of their priests one by one. When the authorities demanded a five-hundred-rouble tax in March 1937 on pain of arrest and closure, the parish resolved in the church there and then to collect it, and the services went on. He preached at every service and walked the far villages of the parish to commune the sick and bury the dead. At his interrogations he refused through every session to confirm the charges - let the witnesses prove it, he said. On 20 September the troika sentenced him, and he was shot two days later, on 23 September 1937. He was glorified among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia in 2000.
