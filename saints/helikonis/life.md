# Helikonis

Doxologia.ro's calendar names her and gives no life. Its line for 28 mai reads «Sfânta Muceniță Eliconida», the holy martyr Helikonis, and the page the calendar links for her carries no Viață tab and no Tropar tab. It prints her name, the date, and the readings appointed for the day — the epistle from 2 Corinthians 6 and the gospel of the Canaanite woman from Matthew 15 — and nothing else about her.

So the whole of what this source states is the rank and the name: a woman who died a martyr, kept on 28 May. Her country, her century and the manner of her death are not recorded here, because the page prints none of them, and no year and no place is entered for her.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and sets her in a reign. saint.gr says she lived in the years of the emperor Gordian III, in the middle of the third century, which it dates 238 to 244; that she was a Thessalonican but moved to Corinth, where she was denounced as a Christian to the ruler Perinios. He tried in vain to draw her from the faith, and she stood firm and cried out that she was a Christian, so that in his rage he abused her foully and shaved her head. Smiling, she answered him that the pagans take the body and its beauty for the man himself and rest their humble and passing happiness on it, but that for Christians the earthly body is like a garment which will one day wear out and be thrown away — so that whatever he did to her perishable body would not trouble her, since by the grace of God he would not manage to harm the body of her soul, where Christians rest their eternal happiness. Perinios then had her put into molten lead and pitch, and the divine grace kept her unharmed; the same happened later when the proconsul Justin threw her into the fire and to the wild beasts. At the last she was beheaded, and so put on the unfading crown of martyrdom for Christ. The couplet on the page plays on her name: Helikonis, her head cut off with the sword, has not Helicon but the delight of Eden.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps her on the same day, agrees with the Greek page that she was born at Thessalonica and went over to Corinth, and differs from it on nearly everything else. She was brought up in Christian piety, it says; and in the time of Gordian and Philip she came to Corinth, where she loudly rebuked all those who offered sacrifice to idols — where the Greek page has her denounced as a Christian to the ruler Perinios instead. The second emperor, Philip, is this book’s alone, and no year has been taken from it.

When Perinios advised her to offer sacrifice to the idol Asclepius, the martyr of Christ said to him: hear me, I am a handmaid of Christ, and who Asclepius is I do not know; do as you please. For that she was brought to trial and tormented with dreadful torments. She was thrown into fire, and much blood poured out of her body and quenched the fire, and she remained alive. She was thrown before lions, and the lions did not touch her but fawned about her. Let into the temple to sacrifice, she smashed the idols, and so angered her tormentor the more.

While she lay all wounded in the prison, the Lord Himself appeared to her with the archangels Michael and Gabriel, healed her wounds, and comforted and strengthened her. Then she was led out to the place of execution to be beheaded with the sword. Before the beheading she raised her hands on high and prayed God to receive her and join her to His sheep in the heavenly fold; and when she had finished the prayer a voice was heard from heaven: come, set out, both the crown and the throne are ready for thee. At the last she was beheaded with the sword. The Greek page has molten lead and pitch, and a second judge named Justin, and neither is here.

The Russian church calendar keeps her on 28 мая ст. ст. and gives her a long life. She lived in the third century in the city of Thessalonica, and during a persecution of the followers of Christ she came to the city of Corinth and began to urge the pagans to leave the service of senseless idols and worship the one true God, the Maker of the universe. For that preaching she was seized and brought before the governor Периний, who tried in vain, with kindness and with threats, to bend her to sacrifice. She was given over to torments and bore them manfully; and when they threw her into a burning furnace she came out of it unhurt, for an angel of the Lord had cooled the heat.

Taking her for a sorceress, the governor thought out new torments: the skin was flayed from her head, and her breast and head were seared with fire. Stopping the torture, the judge began again to persuade her to sacrifice to the idols, promising her honours and the rank of a priestess; and suddenly she agreed. The priests and the people, exulting, led her to the temple with the sound of trumpets and kettledrums and, at her own asking, left her there alone. And she, filled with a giant’s strength, threw down and broke all the idols. The priests, having waited a long while, came into the temple, saw the destruction, fell into a mad rage and dragged her out with shouts of «Предать волшебницу смерти!» — put the sorceress to death.

They beat her and then threw her into prison, where she spent five days; and in the prison Christ the Saviour appeared to her with the holy archangels Michael and Gabriel and healed her of her wounds. At the last she was given to the beasts, and three hungry lions were let out on her, and the beasts came up to her submissively and licked her feet; and while the crowd of pagans shouted for the sorceress’s death the lions sprang off the arena and rushed at the people, who fled in terror. Not knowing what else to do, the governor ordered her beheaded. She went to the execution with joy and heard a Voice calling her to the mansions of heaven, and her body was buried with reverence by the Christians. Her end came in the year 244.
