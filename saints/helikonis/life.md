# Helikonis

Doxologia.ro's calendar names her and gives no life. Its line for 28 mai reads «Sfânta Muceniță Eliconida», the holy martyr Helikonis, and the page the calendar links for her carries no Viață tab and no Tropar tab. It prints her name, the date, and the readings appointed for the day — the epistle from 2 Corinthians 6 and the gospel of the Canaanite woman from Matthew 15 — and nothing else about her.

So the whole of what this source states is the rank and the name: a woman who died a martyr, kept on 28 May. Her country, her century and the manner of her death are not recorded here, because the page prints none of them, and no year and no place is entered for her.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and sets her in a reign. saint.gr says she lived in the years of the emperor Gordian III, in the middle of the third century, which it dates 238 to 244; that she was a Thessalonican but moved to Corinth, where she was denounced as a Christian to the ruler Perinios. He tried in vain to draw her from the faith, and she stood firm and cried out that she was a Christian, so that in his rage he abused her foully and shaved her head. Smiling, she answered him that the pagans take the body and its beauty for the man himself and rest their humble and passing happiness on it, but that for Christians the earthly body is like a garment which will one day wear out and be thrown away — so that whatever he did to her perishable body would not trouble her, since by the grace of God he would not manage to harm the body of her soul, where Christians rest their eternal happiness. Perinios then had her put into molten lead and pitch, and the divine grace kept her unharmed; the same happened later when the proconsul Justin threw her into the fire and to the wild beasts. At the last she was beheaded, and so put on the unfading crown of martyrdom for Christ. The couplet on the page plays on her name: Helikonis, her head cut off with the sword, has not Helicon but the delight of Eden.
