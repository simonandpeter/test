# Zoe of Bethlehem

The calendar's life of the hermit [Martinian of Caesarea](/saints/martinian-of-caesarea) names her at its end. A woman of ill fame in Caesarea in Palestine wagered with dissolute men that she would seduce the ascetic whose virtue the whole city talked of. She came to his cell at a night hour in the guise of a traveller asking shelter, and he let her in because the weather was foul; then she changed into costly clothes and set about tempting him.

He went out of the cell instead, lit a fire and stood barefoot on the burning coals, saying to himself that if this passing fire was hard to bear, the everlasting fire the devil had prepared would be harder still. The sight broke her. She repented and asked him to set her on the way of salvation, and at his direction she went to Bethlehem, to the monastery of Saint Paula, where she lived twelve years in strict asceticism until her blessed repose. The page gives her name only once it has told all this: «Имя женщины было Зоя».

The day's line keeps her with Photina under one rank word and one century — «Прпп. Зои и Фотинии (Светланы) (V).» — and the calendar prints no hymn under her name. Her epithet in the name link behind the line is «Вифлеемская», of Bethlehem, for the monastery where she spent those twelve years.

The Serbian church keeps her on the same day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović puts her and Photina in one entry under one heading and makes her the woman of [Martinian](/saints/martinian-of-caesarea)'s own story. Zoe was at first a loose woman and his tempter, it says, and when she saw the ascetic jump into the fire to kill every lust in himself she repented bitterly. She went to a monastery at Bethlehem and there struggled heroically as an ascetic and a recluse; and having repented of all her sins she received from God the gift of working wonders.
