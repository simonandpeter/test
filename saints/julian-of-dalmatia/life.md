# Julian of Dalmatia

The Охридски пролог for the Julian 28 July is the whole of what this folder records, and it is four-fifths of a page. In the time of the emperor Antoninus, Julian crossed from Dalmatia into Italian Campania. He was a young man and handsome, and his soul was given wholly to the Lord. On the road he met imperial soldiers who were going out to arrest Christians, and greeted them: „Мир вам, браћо!“ — peace to you, brothers. From that greeting and from the meekness of the young man's face the soldiers concluded that he was a Christian.

To their question he answered: „Хришћанин сам, родом из Далмације“ — I am a Christian, by birth from Dalmatia. And he confessed to them openly besides that he was travelling with the aim of turning the worshippers of idols to the one living God. The soldiers beat him without pity and at last threw him into a pit, where he passed seven days without any human food; but an angel of God appeared to him and gave him food of heaven.

Brought out to trial, Julian stood firm in the faith as a diamond, and at the sight of his manliness and his unshakeableness thirty men turned to Christ the Lord. Condemned to death, he knelt and raised a prayer to God, thanking him for the struggle of martyrdom and asking him to have mercy on all who should honour his memory. He was beheaded with an axe, and gave up his spirit to God.

The book gives him no year. It names the emperor Antoninus and nothing else that would date him, so this folder records no date at all; and because the heading gives his name bare, with no by-name and no place, it stores no Serbian form of it either. The Prologue prints no hymn for him, or for any saint of the day.

*After the Охридски пролог of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović, as the Православни подсетник serves it for 28. јул ст. ст. — [the day](https://www.pravoslavno.rs/index.php?q=citanja&datum=2026-08-10&prolog=1); read 8 October 2026.*
