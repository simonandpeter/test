# Theoctista of Lesbos

In the Aegean Sea is an island called Paros, and on it stood a very beautiful church of stone built in the name of the Mother of God, but deserted, for the whole island had been emptied of people — the life does not say why — and was inhabited by wild beasts and not by men. Hunters who lived on a mountain by the sea called Elvea sailed there together to hunt, for there were many stags and wild roe on it. One of them, a God-fearing man who cared for his own salvation, went off alone on the tracks of the beasts, found the church, and prayed in it as best he knew, being a simple and unlettered man.

As he prayed he saw a little hollow in the ground with water in it, and in the water the seeds of the plant called *iliotropion*, which grew plentifully on that island; and he thought to himself that some servant of God must be living there and feeding on those seeds. On his second visit, as he stood praying in the middle of the church, something to the right of the holy table moved in the wind behind a thick spider's web; and as he went to break the web and see, he heard a voice: stand, man, and come no nearer, for I am ashamed, being a woman and naked.

What he then saw was not a living body but as one dead: bones covered with skin, the hair white, the face black, the eyes sunk deep, and the whole appearance that of one long in the grave, scarcely able to breathe or to speak above a whisper. He fell to the ground and asked her blessing; she turned to the east, raised her hands and prayed, and then told him everything.

Her country, she said, was Lesbos, and she was born in the city of Methymna; her name was Theoctista, and in her life she was a nun, for she had been orphaned of her parents as a child and given by her kin to a convent of virgins and clothed in the monastic habit. When she was eighteen, at the feast of the Lord's Resurrection, she went with a blessing to a village not far off to see a sister of hers who lived there with her husband, and stayed with her; and at midnight the Arabs, whose leader was Nizir, notorious for wickedness, fell on the place and carried off all the villages, and her with the rest.

At daylight they were put aboard the ships and sailed all day and reached this island, and there the captives were brought out and priced for sale. Seeing a meadow nearby she made for it and ran; her captors ran after her as hunters after a beast, but God covered her and they could not find her. She ran far into the island and did not stop for fear until her feet were torn by sharp wood, thorns and jagged stones, and she fell as dead upon the ground, and the ground was wet with her blood, and she passed that whole night in great pain — thanking God, she said, that He had saved her from her enemies and kept her pure, since she would rather die a virgin in that wilderness than live among an unclean people and lose the virginity she had betrothed to Christ.

The next day, seeing that the heathen had gone from the island, she was so filled with joy at escaping them that she forgot the pain of her body. Thirty-six years, she said, she had now lived on that island, feeding on the seeds of the *iliotropion* that grew there in plenty, but more on the word of God, for she still held in her memory all the psalms, hymns and readings she had learned in her convent, and with those she comforted and fed her soul; her garment had worn away in a short time, and she had stayed naked, with only the grace of God for a covering.

She asked the hunter one thing: that when he came hunting to the island the following year — for she knew he would come, since God willed it — he should bring her in a clean vessel a portion of the most pure and life-giving Mysteries of Christ, because since she began to live in that wilderness she had not been counted worthy to communicate; and she told him to go in peace to his companions and say nothing to anyone. He kept the secret, waited out the year with joy, and came back with the Mysteries in a small clean box from the priest, and a garment he had given her the year before.

He did not find her at once; only when he had slipped away from his companions and come alone to the church did she appear in the same place and in that garment. He fell to the ground before her, and she came quickly and said with tears that he must not do it, since he carried the divine Gifts: do not slight the Mysteries of Christ, and do not honour my foolishness, for I am unworthy; and she took him by the garment and raised him up. She fell first before the Gifts and wet the ground with her tears, then rose, received them, and said with humility: now let Thy servant depart, Master, for my eyes have seen Thy salvation, and I have taken the forgiveness of sins into my hands; now I shall go where Thy goodness commands. Then she prayed a long while and sent the hunter after his companions with her blessing.

She died, and the hunter, who had taken her hand, was kept with the others on the shore all that night; and when he told them the whole story they were grieved and angry with him for not telling them while they were still on the island, so that they too might have had the blessing of one pleasing to God. They turned the ship back to Paros, went up to the church, came with fear to the place where he had buried her honourable body, and found the place but not the body: they could see plainly the marks where the head and the feet had lain, and they all wondered greatly and could not understand where she had been hidden. Some of them said she had risen, others that she would not rise before the general resurrection but had been carried by angelic hands and buried elsewhere, as Saint Catherine had been long before. They scattered over the island to look for her, alive or dead, being simple men who wished to search out the hidden things of God; and finding her nowhere they came back to the church, kissed the place where her body had lain with compunction, prayed, and went home, and told everyone what had happened.
