# Ammoun the Deacon

The [forty virgins](/saints/forty-virgins-of-adrianople) and the deacon Ammoun who had enlightened them with the light of Christ’s faith died as martyrs under Licinius at the beginning of the fourth century, at Adrianople in Thrace. The Greek synaxarion tells how the governor Babdos, about 305, arrested them as Christians and urged them to worship the idols, and how Celsina, one of them and the first lady of the city, after her brave confession gathered them all in her house with their teacher Ammoun to be strengthened for martyrdom; Ammoun took the paper with their names and read them out one by one. After many tortures they were sent to Heraclea, to another tormentor, before whom they confessed Christ as firmly and refused to sacrifice — the Romanian calendar adds that at Heraclea, keeping vigil at the relics of the martyr Glyceria, they were strengthened by her appearing, that the beasts let loose on them fell asleep, and that they foretold Constantine’s victory. By the tormentor’s order Ammoun and eight of the virgins were beheaded, ten burned, six died with red-hot iron put into their mouths, six were killed with the knife, and the rest by the sword.
