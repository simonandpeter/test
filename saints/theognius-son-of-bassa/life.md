# Theognius, son of Bassa

[Bassa](/saints/bassa-of-edessa) lived at Edessa in Macedonia, married to a pagan priest; brought up from childhood in the Christian faith, she handed it on to her sons Theognius, Agapius and Pistus. Under Maximian Galerius (305–311) her husband denounced his wife and children to the governor; all of them, despite the threats, refused to sacrifice to the idols, and the children were tortured before their mother’s eyes — the eldest, Theognius, hung up and raked with iron claws; the boy Agapius flayed from head to breast without a sound; then the youngest, Pistus — while the mother never stopped heartening them to endure for Christ.

The boys were beheaded. The Prologue adds that Bassa, full of joy at seeing her sons finish their contest, went the more willingly from torment to torment; thrown into the sea, she was carried by angels to an island in the Sea of Marmara, where she was beheaded — doubly worthy of the kingdom, as martyr and as mother of martyrs.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 21 August among the sons of [Bassa](/saints/bassa-of-edessa), whom its line names without naming them and whom her life names: Theognius, [Agapius](/saints/agapius-son-of-bassa) and [Pistus](/saints/pistus-son-of-bassa). It says their mother, a Christian taught the faith by her forebears, raised them in it in the city of Edessa in the reign of Maximian, though their father Valerius was a servant of the idols; that she was denounced by him and imprisoned with them; and that the sons were tortured before her eyes, Theognius first, who was hung up and then raked. All three bore the torments manfully for Christ and were beheaded, and their mother rejoiced that she had sent her beloved sons ahead of her to Christ.

The Greek calendar keeps the family on the same day, 21 August, and names the husband. saint.gr says Bassa lived in the years of Diocletian and dwelt at Edessa — most probably the Macedonian one, the page adds — and that she had married an idol-priest, Valerius, by whom she had three sons, Theognius, Agapius and Pistus. To Bassa, a soul that loved truth and virtue, the chance was given to be taught and to come to the Christian faith, and she brought her three sons with her, who loved her with their whole heart.

When Valerius learned of it, about the year 290, he tried by various devices to bring them back to idolatry, and in vain, for Bassa was on the contrary labouring to enlighten her idolatrous husband. Enraged at that, Valerius denounced all four to the proconsul Vicarius, who at once ordered their arrest. The firstborn, Theognius, died as soon as he confessed Jesus, his breast and his ribs having been torn open. The others were thrown into prison.

Because their resolve was not bent, they killed Agapius, flaying the skin from his head to his breast and then burning the flayed body; the torment was dreadful, the page says, but the young contender cried out «ουδέν ούτως ηδύ, ως το πάσχειν υπέρ Χριστού» — nothing is so sweet as to suffer for Christ. The third son, Pistus, they beheaded. The mother they let go; but afterwards the eparch of Cyzicus arrested her, and having broken her hands and feet, beheaded her.
