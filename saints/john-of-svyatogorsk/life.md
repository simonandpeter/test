# John of Svyatogorsk, Recluse

Ivan Kryukov was born in Kursk on 20 September 1795 to poor townspeople. From the age of seven he longed to save his soul — a playmate’s tale of a grandfather who lived in a monastery in reclusion settled it — but his parents, who saw idleness in the wish, refused him schooling and bound him for seven years to a tile-maker, a harsh master who beat his apprentices, once nearly to death, over a tile worth a kopeck. He left home for good on 30 June 1833, aged thirty-eight, and entered the Glinsk hermitage that August; tonsured in the mantle on 22 June 1840 with the name Ioannikios, he was one of the twelve Glinsk monks who on 20 April 1844 came to refound the ancient monastery of Svyatogorsk on the Donets, closed since 1788, and was ordained hierodeacon and hieromonk there in August 1849.

Clearing the chalk caves of the cliff, he came to love one of the chalk cells and shut himself in it in 1850; on 29 August 1852, the Beheading of the Forerunner, he took the great schema with the name John. For seventeen years he lived in the cave in strict fasting and prayer, enduring cold, damp and the mockery of brethren; from the mid-1850s he received communion weekly, blessed the crowds who filled the church after the service, and opened the door of his cell to talk with visitors. From early 1867 his strength failed — his legs, swollen from constant standing in the damp, were covered with sores — and a week before the end, in obedience to the abbot, he was carried to the monastery’s hospital farm of the Akhtyrka Mother of God, where he died in peace on 11 August 1867. His life was written by a contemporary, Andrei Kovalevsky; the Sretensky calendar prints none, and this follows azbyka.ru.
