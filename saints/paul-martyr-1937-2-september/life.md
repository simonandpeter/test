# Paul (Yelkin)

Paul Nikolaevich Yelkin was born on 23 June 1876 in the village of Shoshka; the family was large, and the boy was taken to be raised by his grandfather, who lived at Ust-Sysolsk. He finished the parish school, learned the trades of smith and locksmith, lived for a time in Petersburg and on his return worked at a factory — and from then his political life began: he met the news of the revolution and the overthrow of the tsar’s regime with enthusiasm, became one of the active founders of the Soviet power in the Komi country, and from 1918 a member of the Communist party, but by the end of the twenties had withdrawn from affairs. He lived with his family at Kochpon, near the church, and in the thirties, when life washed many remarkable people up in Syktyvkar, the church was the refuge where bishops and clergy found shelter and prayed — the calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. Under the influence of his acquaintance with the exiled clergy and Bishop [Herman (Ryashentsev)](/saints/herman-of-vyazniki) he came to deep faith; arrested in 1937 in the case of the Sacred Brotherhood and charged with counter-revolutionary activity, he was sentenced to be shot and executed on 15 September 1937. He was numbered among the saints in 2002, and the Russian calendar keeps him on 2 September (Julian) with Herman and Stephen.
