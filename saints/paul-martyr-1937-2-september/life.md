# Paul (Yelkin)

Paul Nikolaevich Yelkin was born on 23 June 1876 in the village of
Shoshka; the family was large, and the boy was taken to be raised by his
grandfather, who lived at Ust-Sysolsk. He finished the parish school,
learned the trades of smith and locksmith, lived for a time in Petersburg
and on his return worked at a factory — and from then his political life
began: he met the news of the revolution and the overthrow of the tsar’s
regime with enthusiasm, became one of the active founders of the Soviet
power in the Komi country, and from 1918 a member of the Communist party,
but by the end of the twenties had withdrawn from affairs. He lived with
his family at Kochpon, near the church, and in the thirties, when life
washed many remarkable people up in Syktyvkar, the church was the refuge
where bishops and clergy found shelter and prayed; acquaintance with them
turned the communist’s life upside down, and his wife, for a time the
church’s warden, played no small part, as did his personal acquaintance
and friendship with Bishop [Herman (Ryashentsev)](/saints/herman-of-vyazniki),
with whom he was on close terms. He was arrested, like the bishop, on 24
February 1937, in the same case of the “Sacred Company”, suffering while
held in the Syktyvkar prison from a heart defect and emphysema; the troika
of the Komi NKVD sentenced him under article 58-10 to the highest penalty,
and the sentence was carried out on 15 September 1937 — 2 September of the
old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him, with Herman and
Stephen. By a decision of the Holy Synod of 12 March 2002 he was numbered
among the saints; the Russian calendar keeps him also in the synaxis of the
saints of Komi.
