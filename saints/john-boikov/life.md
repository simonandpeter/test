# John (Boikov)

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery names him on 6 апреля ст. ст. in four words, «Сщмч. Иоанна пресвитера (1934).», and the name link behind the line, page 9598, gives the surname and the office the line withholds, «Иоанн (Бойков), иерей, сщмч.» The same line of the same day carries a second priest of the same surname — his own younger brother, [James (Boikov)](/saints/james-boikov) — and the calendar separates them by the forename and by the year, 1934 against 1943. Behind each name link is a life, and this one is long.

He was born in 1891 in the town of Bezhetsk, the elder of the two. In 1915 he finished the Tver seminary and took a post as psalmist at the cathedral in the town of Ostashkov. His father, a priest, died in 1917, and his relations persuaded him to come home to Bezhetsk, where he arrived in 1918 and found work as a schoolmaster. But teaching in the new school, hostile to Orthodoxy, was not to the mind of a religious young man. He married, gave up the school, and in 1921 was ordained priest to the church of the village Залужанье in the Молоковский district of the Tver region, where he served until 1929.

Collectivisation began in those years, and there as almost everywhere it began with the closing of the church. After it was closed his wife «Евфросиния Михайловна» began to beg him not to look for another parish and to stop serving, and some of the peasants asked him the same, seeing that trouble was coming and the priest might be arrested any day. «Отец Иван, ведь уже и в Москве всё разрушают, храмы взрывают, оставь служение.» — «Нет, я не изменник Богу. Море переплывешь — на берегу утонешь.» No, I am no traitor to God; you will swim the sea and drown on the shore. But to keep the authorities from persecuting his family for his priesthood he let his wife divorce him.

In 1929 he got a place at the churchyard of Белое in the same district, and on 1 November 1930 moved to the church of the village Бошарово, where nuns from many closed monasteries were living and the services were kept in the monastic order. From time to time he came to Залужанье to see his family and the parishioners, who loved him sincerely; he had to come at night, so as not to bring trouble on those near him. Even so it often happened that the neighbours, whom the GPU had charged with watching the priest’s house, reported his coming to the OGPU’s man, and he sent a policeman — who came, asked whether the priest was there, and went away again at once, not wishing to arrest him.

Having been home and seen them all, the priest would walk back at night to the village where he served. The OGPU decided in the end to arrest him, and the peasants of his parish with him; formally they were charged with being opponents of the setting up of collective farms, speaking everywhere against them, and having formed a group on that ground which he was said to lead. The neighbours reported once more that the priest had come to his wife and children, and this time the GPU arrested him itself. He did not acknowledge himself guilty.

What he said under interrogation the page prints whole, and it is the plainest thing in the life: that he had stopped serving at Залужанье in October 1929 because the church was closed, having served there eight years; that he had kept no particular friendship with anyone, being hard of hearing and sickly; that he knew a man of a neighbouring hamlet as a painter, because the man had painted an icon for him personally and two for the church and gilded the cross on the bell tower, and that when there were molebens in that hamlet and the weather was bad he had had to stop at his house, there being a shelter there convenient for a horse — and that he had not gone there as a guest, had given him no help and no advice, and had not seen him for nearly a year.

He added that since moving to Бошарово on 1 November 1930 he had seen no parishioner of the Залужанье parish and had had no connection with any of them, in writing or in life; that he had come to Залужанье on 11 March, having resigned at Бошарово altogether, and brought his children two bundles of potatoes, and had meant to go the next day to Bezhetsk by Красный Холм to ask the bishop for a place in some other parish, but had not managed to go, being detained on 12 March at the lodging of his divorced wife. To the present case, he said, he could add nothing more. The peasants were arrested too, and not one of them acknowledged himself guilty.

A month later, on 20 April 1931, a troika of the OGPU sentenced him to five years in the corrective labour camps, and four peasants with him to various terms of imprisonment and exile. The page then follows his wife rather than him, through the house taken from her, the two daughters of eight and four left with nothing, the loss of civil rights as a priest’s wife although she was divorced, the begging in far-off villages that she hid from her children, the journey to Иваново-Вознесенск on a stranger’s word, the old woman who opened her door saying she had dreamt that night of a priest knocking at her window, the heaviest and dirtiest work at the mill, the rights restored and lost again each time a school found out whose wife she was, and the legs that gave way in 1937.

Of his own end it says only this. He wrote to all his relations as soon as he reached the camp and went on writing the whole time he was there. The last letter is dated 28 July 1934: that he was alive and well, thank God; that he had been sent on from Суянка on 25 July in a transport to Вишера, and that at five that morning they were sending him on from Вишера and he did not know where; that they were not to worry about him, and he would write when he knew where he would be. **Though he comforted his family, his health was broken, the tuberculosis was telling, and with heavy work it proved unbearable; soon after that last letter he died.** He was numbered among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
