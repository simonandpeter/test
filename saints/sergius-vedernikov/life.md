# Sergius (Vedernikov)

Sergius, Sergei Nikolaevich Vedernikov, was born in 1875 in the village of Pogoreloye in Tver province to a poor peasant family, and worked his way out of need: from 1907 to 1916 he kept a draper’s shop in the village, then a tea-house, and farmed with the most up-to-date implements of the day, and until his arrest in 1928 he chaired the church council. In the campaign to close churches that year he was sentenced to six months of labour camp, and in 1930, after all his property was confiscated, he was exiled to the Urals. He had no wife and no children and could give himself wholly to the Church, and when he came home the believers chose him churchwarden, on 28 February 1937.

That election brought his arrest: on 28 July 1937 he was put in the Zubtsov prison, and the next day was questioned about anti-Soviet talk in the church lodge, that there was no bread and all the grain had gone to Spain, and said he did not remember it and had not said it. The investigation found him guilty of being churchwarden. On 27 September the NKVD troika sentenced him to death, and he was shot on 1 October 1937. He was glorified with the new martyrs and confessors of Russia in August 2000.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 18 September old style, and he is also remembered on 26 January with the new martyrs.
