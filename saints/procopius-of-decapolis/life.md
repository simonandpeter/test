# Procopius of Decapolis

The country of the Decapolis, so called from the number of its ten cities, lies beside the sea of Galilee, and is named in the Gospel of Mark, where Jesus came to the sea of Galilee that lies within the borders of the Decapolis. Procopius the Confessor came from those parts. Living first in the monastic life, trying the whole of the hermit's discipline in earnest and adorned with perfect purity, he was famous among the venerable fathers.

Then the heresy of the war against the holy icons appeared, whose inventor was Leo the Isaurian, the lawless emperor of the Greeks, who called the holy icons and those who honoured and venerated them idolaters, and anathematised many believing emperors and bishops and peoples called by the name of Christ who had lived before him and kept the veneration of the icons in right belief — himself cursed by all.

Against those heretical camps, which blasphemed the incarnation of the Word of God and dishonoured the likeness of Christ pictured on an icon in His humanity, this great and unmoved pillar and strong defender of the right faith stood up like a man. He put them all to shame, rebuked their mad reasoning, and beat them with unanswerable words breathed by God, tearing their skilful weavings like a spider's web; and so he stirred the emperor to anger, who had a beast's name and a beast's nature, and roared like a lion coming out of the desert, seeking someone to swallow.

By the emperor's order he was taken and beaten, then bitterly torn with iron tools over the whole of his body and thrown into a dark and filthy prison; and all the ill usage done to him in his chains he bore with greatness of soul, having in all his sufferings [Basil the Confessor](/saints/basil-the-confessor) for friend and fellow sufferer, whom he had had before in his hermit life as a beloved companion and housemate. With him, after many wounds taken for the holy icons, he endured a long imprisonment, until the tyrant came to his ruinous end.

When that evil emperor was deprived of this life and of the life to come, dying in body and in soul, Procopius and Basil and other venerable fathers were freed from their chains and their prison. They lived out the rest of their days in the accustomed hermit's labours, guiding everyone towards virtue and bringing them to salvation; and in deep old age he went to our Lord Jesus Christ, to see Him now not in an icon but face to face, and to receive the longed-for wages of his labours, as a hermit and as one who suffered for Christ and struggled for His holy icon as far as blood. The page gives no year, and its troparion tab carries a Greek recording and no text.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and puts a reign to him. saint.gr says Procopius the Decapolite lived in the years of the iconoclast emperor Leo the Isaurian and was marked out for his spiritual courage as a champion of Orthodoxy; that although he took up the monastic life young, he did not stay in the seclusion of his cell but contended stoutly against the iconoclasts, and for that endured many tortures, floggings, prisons and exiles; and that he was marked out too in the Church's struggle against the Monophysite heretics. How he died the page will not settle: «Ο Άγιος Προκόπιος φαίνεται ότι λίγο μετά την αποφυλάκισή του κοιμήθηκε, ενώ κατ' άλλους υπέμεινε μαρτυρικό θάνατο» — it seems he fell asleep soon after his release from prison, while according to others he endured a martyr's death. Its couplets make nothing of all the cities of the earth beside the city of the mind where the Decapolite is now enrolled, and put his end on the twenty-seventh.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović explains his by-name at once: he was from the Ten Cities about the sea of Galilee. In his youth he gave himself to the ascetic life and went through all its appointed labours, by which the heart is cleansed and the spirit raised to God; and when the persecution over the icons came from the wicked emperor Leo the Isaurian he stood up in their defence, arguing that the veneration of icons is not idolatry, because Christians know that in bowing before icons they are not bowing to dead matter but to the living saints who are painted on them.

For that he was savagely tortured, arrested, beaten and raked with iron. When the wicked emperor was killed in the body, the Prologue says — having perished in the soul before that — the icons were given back to the churches and he returned to his own monastery, where he spent the rest of his days in peace; and in old age he passed over to the kingdom of God, where he looks with joy on the living angels and saints whose likenesses on icons he had honoured on earth. It puts his peaceful end in the ninth century, where the folder's `floruit` holds the reign he suffered under.
