# Sebbi

Sebbi — Sebba — the son of Seward, began to reign over the East Saxons in
664, the year of the great pestilence, the tenth king from Erkenwin who
founded the kingdom in 527 and the sixth from Sebert, its first Christian
king, who founded St Paul’s and Thorney abbey about 604; his people held
Essex, Middlesex and most of Hertfordshire. By his wise and pious government
he was the father of his people and a pattern of every virtue, and on the
throne he sanctified his soul by the heroic exercises of austere penance,
profuse alms and constant prayer. After thirty years of a happy and glorious
reign he gave up his crown to his two sons Sigeard and Senfrid, which he had
long wished to do so as to be freer to prepare for his last hour; his queen
took the veil about the same time, and he received the monastic habit from
Waldhere, Erkenwald’s successor in the see of London, whom he charged to
give all his personal estate to the poor. Death seemed always present to his
mind, and his grievous fear of that tremendous passage turned at length into
a longing and joyful hope. After two years of great fervour in his
retirement he died at London in holy joy, about 697, forewarned by God of
his last hour three days before; Bede says his death was attended by many
miracles and heavenly favours. He was buried in St Paul’s, where his tomb
stood by the north wall until the great fire of 1666, with a Latin epitaph
that Weever’s Funeral Monuments preserves: here lies Sebba, king of the East
Saxons, converted to the faith by St Erkenwald, bishop of London, in 677, a
man very devout to God and fervent in religion, constant prayer and pious
alms, who preferred a monastic life to the riches of a kingdom and took the
habit from Waldhere. His name is in the Roman Martyrology; Butler’s sources
are Bede, book III chapter 30 and book IV chapter 11, and Alford’s Annals.
The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 29 August as an English saint with no
details of his life, and Butler on the same day; the year this corpus
records for his death, 694 or 695, comes from another reckoning of the same
reign, and Butler’s “about 697” is left beside it rather than over it.

*After Alban Butler’s Lives of the Saints (1866), volume VIII, August — [St. Sebbi, or Sebba, King and Confessor](https://www.bartleby.com/lit-hub/lives-of-the-saints/volume-viii-august/st-sebbi-or-sebba-king-and-confessor), read 6 September 2026; the Greek synaxarion’s line (saint.gr, 29 August) was all this entry had until then.*
