# Bassa of Edessa

Bassa lived at Edessa in Macedonia, married to a pagan priest; brought up from childhood in the Christian faith, she handed it on to her sons [Theognius](/saints/theognius-son-of-bassa), [Agapius](/saints/agapius-son-of-bassa) and [Pistus](/saints/pistus-son-of-bassa). Under Maximian Galerius (305–311) her husband denounced his wife and children to the governor; all of them, despite the threats, refused to sacrifice to the idols, and the children were tortured before their mother’s eyes — the eldest, Theognius, hung up and raked with iron claws; the boy Agapius flayed from head to breast without a sound; then the youngest, Pistus — while the mother never stopped heartening them to endure for Christ.

The boys were beheaded. The Prologue adds that Bassa, full of joy at seeing her sons finish their contest, went the more willingly from torment to torment; thrown into the sea, she was carried by angels to an island in the Sea of Marmara, where she was beheaded — doubly worthy of the kingdom, as martyr and as mother of martyrs.

The Romanian calendar keeps her on 21 August with her sons, and doxologia.ro's life names all three. It says Bassa lived in the reign of Maximian in the city of Edessa; that she was joined to a certain servant of the idols named Valerius and bore him three sons, [Theognius](/saints/theognius-son-of-bassa), [Agapius](/saints/agapius-son-of-bassa) and [Pistus](/saints/pistus-son-of-bassa), and raised them in the Christian faith, being herself a Christian taught the holy faith in Christ by her forebears. Denounced by her husband, she confessed before the judge that she was a Christian and was thrown into prison with her sons; then her sons were tortured before her eyes — Theognius hung up and raked, Agapius flayed from the head to the breast and saying nothing, the third tormented in every way — while their mother, watching, strengthened them and urged them on, and all three were beheaded. She was returned to prison, where, being starved, she took food from the hand of an angel. Cast into the deep some thirty stadia from shore, she was seen by those watching to be carried into a ship by three shining men and set on a seat, and eight days later appeared to the soldiers on an island in the Hellespont. Philip, the governor in Macedonia, wrote to the consular of Cyzicus to seize her; when she would not sacrifice he had her hands bound behind her, her limbs broken with a cruel beating, and at last her head cut off.
