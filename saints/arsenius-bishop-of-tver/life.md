# Arsenius of Tver

saint.gr gives this Arsenius three sentences. He lived in Russia at the end of the fourteenth and the beginning of the fifteenth century; early on he entered the monastic polity of the Lavra of Kyiv, and he was elected bishop of the city of Tver; and he fell asleep in peace, in a holy manner. The head of the page dates his repose to 1409, and a portion of his relic, it says, is kept at the Lavra of St Alexander Nevsky in St Petersburg.

That is all the notice holds. It gives no year for his birth, no name for his parents or his elder, nothing of his years in the see, no foundation, no distich and no hymn.

Two other entries on the same Greek day have particulars that run alongside his almost exactly: [Arethas the Recluse](/saints/arethas-the-recluse), of Tver and of the Lavra of Kyiv, elected bishop and then withdrawn into reclusion, whose repose the site also puts in 1409, and a bare «Όσιος Αρσένιος εκ Ρωσίας», born at Tver, an ascetic of the Lavra of Kyiv and of the monastery of the Dormition of Zheltikovo. Arethas bears another name and is kept here as another man. The second Arsenius bears this one's own name and was not drafted at all, because nothing on either page tells the two apart.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar is the first of the four this folder has been read against to give him more than a line, and it gives him two lives and the day’s troparion and kontakion, both of which this folder now carries. Church tradition, the longer of the two says, has him born at Tver; the year of his birth and his parents’ names are unknown. Losing them early and left heir to a rich estate, he turned his house into a shelter for strangers and the poor, and drew the reproach of his kindred for it; despairing of the quarrel, he asked the Lord to show him a way, heard a voice in sleep calling him to Kyiv, and left Tver secretly for the Caves monastery, where he was tonsured.

There the metropolitan Cyprian, then living in Kyiv, took note of him, made him his own, ordained him hierodeacon and, on taking the see of Moscow, brought him there as his archdeacon with the writing-office and the inner business of the metropolitanate in his charge. In Tver meanwhile the prince and the bishop Euthymius had fallen out beyond mending; the bishop was deprived of his see and sent to the Chudov monastery in Moscow, where he soon died, and the metropolitan named his own archdeacon in his place. He would not have it, and the chronicler’s words are that in 1390 they «едва умолиша» him — barely prevailed on him — to be bishop at Tver.

Among the bishops at his consecration was the bishop of Perm. Here the two lives of one host part: the shorter dates the consecration 15 August 1390, the longer 15 August 1391, and neither is chosen. In his see he worked to end the princes’ quarrels, and in 1403 reconciled the prince of Tver with his brother the prince of Kashin; when the old prince felt his end near he came to be tonsured, and was given the name Matthew. He built and consecrated cathedrals at Staritsa and Mikulino, renewed the stone cathedral of the Transfiguration at Tver and laid its new bell tower.

Twenty versts from Tver, on the river Tma, he founded in 1397 the Savvin monastery of the Meeting, and the longer life says its first monks were the two it calls «преподобные Савва и Варсонофий (память 2 марта)», tonsured on Athos. The day page itself prints neither of them, so neither gains a row from it and no relation is recorded here; the corpus holds a folder for each, and the rows that reading would owe are left for a hand that can read the name-form folds they stand in. In 1394 he founded the Zheltikov monastery four versts from Tver, where the stone church of the Dormition was consecrated by him on 30 August 1405.

For that house he had a copy made in 1406 of the Caves Paterikon, the oldest surviving redaction of the first collection of Russian saints’ lives and known to scholarship as the Arsenian; two manuscript Ladders copied at his order, of 1402 and 1404, also survive. He died in Great Lent of 1409. The clergy had come in to Tver as usual for the diocesan council on the Sunday after the first week of the fast; on the Tuesday he gave the pastors an instruction, blessed them and dismissed the council, and he died on 2 March, the day the calendar keeps him on, and was buried at Zheltikov in the tomb he had made with his own hands.

His relics were found incorrupt at Zheltikov in 1483 and a feast was appointed for him in Tver that same year; the general glorification followed at the Moscow council of 1547. The silver shrine bore seven of his wonders, and the longer life numbers them: a drowned fisherman raised; a son given to a man who had none; a priest healed of his legs; a girl healed; a man saved from hanging; the horse of a Polish rider who tore the cover off the shrine in 1606 rearing and falling and killing them both; and a man and his wife healed of madness. The icon-painter’s manual describes him old and grey, the beard like Sergius’s, in the schema, the omophorion crossed before him, the right hand blessing and the Gospel in the left.

Both life pages head themselves «Дни памяти: 2 марта , 6 июля», two days. The day recorded here is the one whose page lists him and which this row cites; the July day is the finding of the relics of 1483, and it is a note and not a second feast, because the schema keeps one feast to a church and because a relic-finding is not this man’s repose. The longer life also says that the synaxis of the saints of Tver is now kept on the first Sunday after 29 June, which is moveable and no day this corpus can hold.
