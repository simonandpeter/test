# Longinus the Ascetic

saint.gr's calendar for 17 November keeps «Όσιος Λογγίνος», and makes him one of the learned and wise ascetics of the desert. Some of his wise sayings, the page says, are included in the Euergetinon, where Longinus questions Abba Lucius about various matters. He died in peace. Everything else the page has it takes out of Nikodemos the Hagiorite's synaxarion, and it is all sayings.

This venerable man left us the saying: as a dead man feels nothing and judges nobody, so the humble-minded man cannot judge a man, even if he sees him worshipping idols. He himself asked Abba Lucius, I wish to go into exile; and Lucius answered, if you do not hold your tongue you are no exile wherever you go — so hold your tongue here, and you are an exile. He asked a second time, I wish to fast; and Lucius answered, Isaiah the prophet said, if you bend your neck like a hoop, not even so shall it be called an acceptable fast; rather hold back your evil thoughts. He asked a third time, I wish to flee from men; and Lucius answered, unless you first set virtue right among men, you cannot set it right alone either.

Asked once which virtue is greater than all, he answered that as pride is greater than all the vices and passions, so humility is greater than all the virtues. In the manuscript Paradise of the Fathers, the page adds, he says these soul-saving words: fasting humbles the body, vigil cleanses the mind, stillness brings mourning; mourning baptises a man and makes him sinless. He had great compunction in his prayer and his psalmody, and when a disciple asked whether weeping in prayer is the monk's spiritual rule he answered, yes, child, this is the rule God asks — for God did not make man to weep but to rejoice and be glad, and to glorify him purely and sinlessly as the angels do; it was after man fell into sin that weeping became necessary, since where there is no sin there is no need of tears either.
