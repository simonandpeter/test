# Gabriel the Iberian

Gabriel, the Iberian monk of Svyatogorsk, was marked out by the harshness of his life and by his simplicity. In summer he withdrew to the peaks of the mountains round about, to untrodden and wild places, into the cave that is kept to this day above the monastery of Iviron; in winter he withdrew to the shore or to the neighbourhood of the monastery. His clothes were of hair, and his only drink was water.

The Mother of God appeared to him and told him to go down to the shore and take her icon out of the sea. Obeying her voice, he walked on the waves as on dry land and brought into the monastery the wonderworking icon called Portaitissa, the Doorkeeper — so called because it was set at the gate of the monastery as porter and watchman, and as the praise of the whole of Mount Athos, its support and its hope. The same Mother of the Lord promised him: I give you a sign; as long as you see my icon in the monastery, the grace and the mercy of my Son will not fail you.

He ended his life in peace in 1028, and his memory is kept together with the venerable Iberian founders on 13 May. In the cave of his struggles a little church was raised in his honour in 1985, and a word of praise to him was composed by an Iberian monk at the beginning of the nineteenth century.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day and dates the finding of the icon. saint.gr has him an Iberian of the ninth century who practised asceticism inside a cave above the boundary between the monasteries of Xeropotamou and Saint Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain. On the Tuesday of Renewal Week in the year 829, at the prompting of a divine voice, he went down to the shore, where he found and drew out of the sea the icon of the Mother of God called «της Πορταϊτίσσης», of the Gate — an icon which a devout Christian of Nicaea had entrusted to the water to save it from the fury of the iconoclasts. It stands today, the page says, in the church of the Dormition of the Mother of God near the monastery and to the east of it, and a portion of Gabriel's own relic is kept at New Thebaid on the Mountain.

The Ohrid Prologue, read for the Julian 13 May, keeps the four of them under one heading as the founders of the famous Iberian — that is, the Georgian — monastery on the Holy Mountain, and gives him one clause of its five sentences. [John](/saints/john-the-iberian) struggled first in the lavra of Athanasius and afterwards founded his own monastery, Iver, and fell asleep in 998; [Euthymius](/saints/euthymius-the-iberian) and [George](/saints/george-the-iberian) translated the Holy Scriptures into the Georgian tongue, and fell asleep in 1028 and 1066. And Gabriel, it says, was counted worthy to receive the wonderworking icon of the Mother of God which came to the monastery over the sea. It gives him no year of his own, where it dates the other three.
