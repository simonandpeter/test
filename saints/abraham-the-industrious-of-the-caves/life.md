# Abraham the Industrious of the Kyiv Caves

When Abraham lived is not securely known — the written record of the Caves is
thin — and the life the Orthodox Church in America prints sets the guesses out
honestly. Archbishop Sergius (Spassky) put him in the twelfth or thirteenth
century; the Orthodox Encyclopaedia puts him in the second half of the
thirteenth or the beginning of the fourteenth; the likeliest reading is that
he lived through the Mongol invasions. His title is old. The Teraturgima of
the hieromonk Athanasius Kalnofoysky, in the seventeenth century, already
calls him “the venerable elder Abraham, the lover of labour”, and Archbishop
Philaret (Gumilevsky) explains it: towards the end of his life he laboured in
a cave, and after his prayers he set to preparing whatever the brotherhood of
the Caves needed. An old icon of the wonderworkers of the Near Caves, and a
manuscript list of the saints, call him an igumen; if that is right, it was he
who buried Prince Skirgaila — John in baptism — killed by his own servants at
Vyshhorod in 1396, beside the tomb of St Theodosius.

His relics rest in the Near Caves of St Anthony. He has long been kept on
21 August, the same day as Abraham of Smolensk, and again on 28 September
with all the venerable fathers whose relics lie in the Near Caves, and on the
second Sunday of Great Lent with the whole synaxis of the Caves. The Russian
calendar keeps the 21 August line — of the twelfth or thirteenth century,
relics in the Near Caves — and prints no life under it.

*After the life printed by the Orthodox Church in America for 21 August —
[the day’s lives](https://www.oca.org/saints/all-lives/2026/08/21); the
Russian calendar’s line is the Sretensky calendar (days.pravoslavie.ru),
21 August (Julian) — [the page](https://days.pravoslavie.ru/Days/20260821.html),
which prints no life; read 5 September 2026.*
