# Hesychius the Senator

Hesychius lived in the days of the emperor doxologia's page calls Maximilian, and was the first servant of the imperial palace and a member of the senate besides. When the emperor commanded that every Christian in the army who would not renounce Christ should be stripped of his uniform and live as a common man, Hesychius was stripped of the costly garments he wore, dressed instead in a poor sleeveless coat of wool, and sent away to live among the women.

The emperor then called him and asked whether he was not ashamed to have been brought down from the honour of a magistrate to so dishonourable a way of living, seeing that he, as emperor, could not set Christians in the dignities and the power Hesychius had held before. The saint answered that the honour of this age passes away, and that only the honour a man has before the Lord Christ is everlasting and without end.

At that the emperor ordered a millstone to be tied about his neck and the saint to be thrown into the river that is called the Orontes; and there Hesychius found his blessed end. doxologia gives no year for any of it, and names no city.
