# Paisius of Galich

saint.gr says he lived in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries and came to the city of Galich in Russia from the south about the year 1385. He was tonsured a monk at the monastery of Saint Nicholas of Galich and began the hard spiritual struggle at once. His life records that he was once in a cell on the river Uchma together with the hermit [Cassian](/saints/cassian-the-greek), called the Greek, and with the monks Adrian and Gerasimus.

While they were singing the Akathist hymn a most radiant light appeared over the whole monastery, and the monks heard a voice calling them to come out of the cell. They came out in terror, and an angel of the Lord showed them a vision: the Mother of God seated on a throne and holding in her arms Jesus Christ as an infant. The monks fell to the ground, but the angel told them to rise and gave them the Mother of God's command to build on that spot a church in honour of the Protecting Mother of God. The church was built in 1482, and Adrian took part in the making of the stone church; and in 1489 he helped the venerable Paisius in the building of the monastery of Saint Nicholas, on the little river Grekov on the right bank of the Volga, which was a dependency of the monastery of the Protection of the Most Holy Mother of God.

The venerable one strove for the unity of the Russian land and of the rulers of the region and set himself against the feudal wars, and for that reason he visited Moscow as well. After a life pleasing to God and unceasing prayer he foresaw his end, and so began to press his spiritual struggles harder and to prepare himself inwardly to meet his Lord and God. He fell asleep in peace in 1460, or in 1463, and was buried on the south side of the Uspensky cathedral.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 23 мая ст. ст. and gives him a long life, most of which is the history of an icon. He came to Galich, by the traditions it reports, «с далекого юга», from the far south, about 1385, and took up the ascetic life in the Nicholas house near the city — a monastery founded by the devout boyar Иоанн Овин and living under the rule of separate cells. There he went through the monastic obediences of woodcutter, baker and gatekeeper, and for his diligence in them was ordained first hierodeacon, then hieromonk, and later became abbot.

Long before he came, in 1378, the boyar Иоанн Овин had thought to put up a new church of St Nicholas in the monastery in place of the old one; and going out from his house on the road to the house he met two beautiful young men, who gave him an icon of the Mother of God, saying that it was sent by the parents of the boyar’s wife with a request that the altar of the church being built be named for the Dormition. At the sight of them the boyar was seized with indescribable terror; and having carried the precious image into the monastery and given it to the abbot, he suddenly remembered that he had not asked the young men into his house to eat and rest, and sent servants to look for them. Only when the search came to nothing did he understand that they had been angels of heaven, carrying the will of the Mother of God herself. Her will was done exactly, and from that time the monastery was called the Dormition, and the wonderworking image, after the boyar who received it, the Ovinovskaya.

Some years later, about 1387, when he was already abbot, the church where the icon stood caught fire; and while they looked for the sacristan, who had gone off somewhere with the keys, the flame took the whole church. Then the hieromonk Иаков, his own disciple, broke the door in with a beam, went in, took the precious icon and came out with it, and neither the icon nor the hieromonk who saved it was harmed. In 1434 the grand prince of Moscow Василий Васильевич, in his war with his uncle, besieged Galich and could not take it, and on his way back to Moscow carried the Ovinovskaya image out of the Dormition monastery, meaning to deprive Galich of the help of the heavenly Protectress; but that same night the icon was carried back by a wonder to its old place.

He was the spiritual father of Дмитрий Ярцов, the house’s patron, and when Дмитрий fell mortally ill he called him and, in the prince’s presence, set out his last will, leaving all his estates to the Dormition monastery on condition that the abbot bring in the rule of common life, which was done. The calendar says that history has kept little record of whose side he took in the civil war played out before his eyes; remembering that all authority is from God he did not meddle in the struggle of the lay rulers, but could not stand aside from the horrors of a quarrel between brothers, and the traditions say that he tried more than once to reconcile the warring princes and hold them back from shedding blood, and that each time the rulers stayed deaf to him.

Between June 1454 and the beginning of 1460 he came to Moscow with the copy of the Ovinovskaya image, and was met with the ringing of bells and a procession of the cross led by the grand prince, by his son and co-ruler Иван, and by Иона, metropolitan of Moscow and of all Russia, himself a man of the Galich country. The grand prince asked him for his blessing, and out of humility he would not give it in the metropolitan’s presence, saying that the lesser is blessed by the greater and not the other way; and seeing such meekness the grand prince cried out, «Воистину Божий раб еси ты, о преподобне, и верный слуга Божия Матере» — truly thou art a servant of God, O venerable one, and a faithful servant of the Mother of God. Only after the metropolitan had blessed him and ordained him archimandrite did he bless the prince in turn. A few days later he asked leave to go back to his house, troubled for the brethren who had been so long without their father, and the grand prince let him go with an escort and sent the governor of Galich a charter bidding him provide for the house and guard it as the apple of his eye; the copy of the wonderworking image he refused outright to keep in Moscow, fearing to break the will of the Mother of God and lose it a second time.
