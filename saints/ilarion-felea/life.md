# Ilarion Felea, Priest and Martyr

Ilarion Felea was a preacher of the Gospel greatly valued by people and intelligentsia alike in the middle of the last century, professor at the Theological Academy of Arad, who wrote many theological works, the most important Towards Tabor, which Father Justin Pârvu called the best work of Romanian Orthodoxy so far — a true liturgical genius of Orthodox theology. He was born on 21 March 1903 in the commune of Valea Bradului in Hunedoara county, where his father was priest; he went to primary school in his native commune (1910–1914), to the Avram Iancu lyceum at Brad (1914–1920) and the Moise Nicoară lyceum at Arad (1920–1922), where he took his baccalaureate, and from 1922 to 1926 studied theology at the Andreian Academy in Sibiu, taking his diploma for the priesthood on 22 June 1926; in 1926–1927 he was a supply teacher at Brad. On 29 July 1927 he was ordained priest in the archdiocese of Sibiu and from 6 August served as parish priest at Valea Bradului until 30 August 1930, when he passed to the diocese of Arad, winning by competition the parish of Arad-Șega; from 1927 to 1929 he also followed and finished the Faculty of Letters and Philosophy at Cluj. In the Arad-Șega parish, lately separated from Arad-Centre, the young and enthusiastic priest inherited a new church raised in red brick, a modest parish house and a cultural house, once the confessional school, which needed renovating and extending, and a population mostly of Orthodox Romanians, most of them craftsmen and workers at the nearby Astra and UTA factories; with the toughness of a great fighter and sacrificer for Church and nation, with his missionary zeal, a devout server at the altar and a shepherd by vocation who lays down his life for his sheep — the Romanian life runs on past the point read here. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 18 September with a troparion and kontakion — a torch of right belief, the kontakion says, sprung from the soil of Crișana.
