# Jason of Tarsus

Jason was a man of Tarsus, and the life says he was the first out of Tarsus to come over to the right faith. He and [Sosipater](/saints/sosipater-of-iconium), an apostle from Achaia, both became disciples of the apostle Paul after receiving the faith of Christ; Paul calls the two of them his kinsmen in the letter to the Romans. Jason was chosen bishop of his own city of Tarsus, and Sosipater took the governance of the Church of Iconium, and both shepherded their churches well and increased them.

Afterwards they went into the parts of the West, and coming to the island of Corfu they built a very beautiful church in the name of Stephen the First Martyr, where they served God and drew many to the faith. Being denounced to Kerkylinos, the ruler of that island, they were shut in a prison where seven robbers sat — Saturninus, Jakishchol, Faustinian, Januarius, Marsalius, Euphrasius and Mammius — and with their God-inspired speech the two apostles brought them to the faith and made sheep of wolves.

The ruler had a great cauldron filled with pitch, brimstone and wax and set boiling, and the seven thrown in bound; so they ended as martyrs. The gaoler too believed; they cut off first his hand and then both his feet, and because he never stopped calling on the name of Christ they cut off his head. Jason and Sosipater the governor handed over to a noble named Karpianus, who bound them and sent them back to prison.

Seeing that suffering, Kerkyra the ruler's daughter confessed herself a Christian and gave her costly ornaments away to the poor. Her father shut her in a prison of her own and in his anger gave her to an Ethiopian for her ruin; a wild bear seized the man at the prison door, and she drove the beast off with the name of Christ, healed his wounds through the window and taught him Christ, so that he cried out that great is the God of the Christians and was tortured to death for it. Wood was piled round her prison and lit, and she was found alive and unburnt; then she was hanged on a tree, choked with heavy smoke and shot with arrows until she gave up her spirit.

After her death the ruler began to hunt the believers, who fled and hid on a nearby island. He swam after them to punish them, and in the middle of the sea was drowned by the anger of God, as Pharaoh of old; and the faithful people, delivered from his hands, brought songs of thanksgiving to God. Jason and Sosipater came out of prison and taught the people the word of God unhindered.

Then another governor came, seized them, and had them thrown into an iron bath of boiling pitch, brimstone and wax; by the grace of Christ they were unburnt in it, while many of the unbelievers standing round were burnt by that fire. Seeing it, many believed, and the king of the island believed too, and hanging a stone about his neck lamented, «Dumnezeul lui Iason și al lui Sosipatru, miluiește-mă pe mine» — God of Jason and Sosipater, have mercy on me. They taught him and baptised him and gave him the name Sebastian.

A few days later the king's son fell ill and died, and the apostles prayed to God and raised him from the dead. They worked many other wonders, built beautiful churches together with the king, brought all to a good and holy end and increased the flock of Christ; and at last, in old age, their life being finished, they went up to Christ whom they had desired. The page gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps the two apostles on 29 Απριλίου, a day later than the Romanian, and saint.gr spends most of its notice on where Jason came from and what Paul meant by calling him his kinsman. Jason it gives as of Tarsus, or of Thessalonica by an old manuscript that Nikodemos the Hagiorite notes, and Sosipater as of Achaia. It rehearses the two places in the New Testament where Jason's name stands — the Acts, where the Jews who could not find Paul in Jason's house dragged Jason and the other brethren before the politarchs, and the Epistle to the Romans, where he is among those sending greetings.

The «συγγενεῖς» of that epistle, the page argues, most probably means that Paul and Jason were of one trade and not kin by blood; and it cites Chrysostom, Theophylact, Theodoret of Cyrus and Oecumenius for the two Jasons being one man. Jason seems to have kept a small weaving workshop, in which Paul found work — which would mean he was settled at Thessalonica, perhaps permanently; and the one certain thing, the page says, is that Jason lived at Thessalonica and became a disciple of the apostle Paul. Dorotheus of Tyre's report that he was one of the Seventy disciples it sets aside.

At Corfu the two were prisoners of the ruler Kerkyllinos and were handed over to the eparch Capreanus, and in the prison they turned seven robbers to Christ, who were martyred later, and the gaoler Antonius with them. The torments they bore moved the ruler's daughter Kerkyra, who embraced the faith. They were thrown into an iron cauldron of pitch and resin: Jason came out unharmed and Sosipater died in it. At that trial Kerkyllinos himself repented, was catechized, baptized, and renamed Sebastianos; and Jason, the Acts of the Saints say, lived to great old age serving the Church of Corfu and building churches, one of which the people of Corfu reckon the oldest in their town.

On the relics the Greek page says two different things and neither is resolved here. Its list of holy relics puts Jason's skull at the Metropolis of Thebes and Sosipater's at the Metropolis of Thebes and Levadeia, with fragments of both at the Metropolis of Kerkyra and at the monastery of Dionysiou on the Holy Mountain, of Jason also at Kykkos in Cyprus and of Sosipater also at the metropolitan church of Veroia; but its last line says that the honourable heads of both are kept with reverence at the holy monastery of Hosios Loukas in Boeotia. The couplets say of Jason that he received the end of a corruptible life and found another that has no end, and of Sosipater that the Father shows the glory of His countenance to him who has died.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, where the Greek keeps the whole company on 29 April, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells the Corfu mission as one continuous story. Jason and [Sosipater](/saints/sosipater-of-iconium) were two of the Seventy, and the apostle Paul names them at Romans 16:21 and calls them his kinsmen; Jason was of Tarsus, as Paul was, and Sosipater of Achaia. The apostles set the first over Tarsus as its bishop and the second over Iconium. Travelling and preaching, the two came to the island of Corfu, where they managed to build a church „у име св. Стефана првомученика“ — in the name of [Stephen](/saints/stephen-the-first-martyr) the first martyr — and to win some of the heathen for the Church.

The king of that island threw them into prison, where seven robbers were already shut up. These seven the apostles brought over to the faith of Christ and „од вукова направише јагањце“, made lambs out of wolves; and when the king heard of it he ordered the seven put to death in boiling pitch, and so they received the martyr's crown. While he was afterwards putting the apostles to the question, his own daughter [Kerkyra](/saints/kerkyra) watched the torments of God's men from a window, learned why they were being tortured, declared herself a Christian and gave away all her ornaments to the poor.

Her father shut her in a prison of her own, and when that did not turn her from Christ he had the prison set on fire. The prison burned and the maiden stayed alive; many of the people were baptized at the wonder, and the king in his fury had her tied to a tree and killed with arrows. Those who had believed fled to a nearby island, and the king, setting out by ship to take them, went down in the sea — „и тако нечестиви погибе као негда фараон“, and so the impious man perished as Pharaoh once did.

**Here this book and the Greek one part over a single man.** This one drowns the persecuting king and has a *new* king receive the faith, be baptized and be given the name Sebastian; saint.gr has the king himself, Kerkyllinos, repent at the end, be catechized, baptized and renamed Sebastianos. One page kills him and the other converts him, and nothing is chosen here between them. Jason and Sosipater, the entry ends, preached the Gospel freely and strengthened the Church of God on Corfu into deep old age, and there ended their earthly life and passed over into the courts of the Lord. **It gives no year**, so this folder stands undated as the other two calendars left it.
