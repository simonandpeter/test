# Arsenius of Corfu

Arsenius was born in 876 at Jerusalem, in the reign of Basil the Macedonian; his father was of Jerusalem and his mother of Bethany. When the child was three they took him to a monastery nearby, as they had promised God before his birth, and there he was marked out for a virtuous life and a love of learning; at twelve he took the monastic vows, and afterwards his parents themselves left everything worldly, were tonsured, and joined the brotherhood in which their son was living.

After his first studies at Bethany he went on to Seleucia in Syria, then an important centre of learning in the East, where the bishop soon recognised his gifts and ordained him deacon and priest. Wanting to see his birthplace again and to venerate the holy places, he set out and fell into the hands of pirates, who took him captive — and then, bowing before his piety, the gentleness of his character and the sweetness of his words, let him go. After his parents died he settled at Constantinople and lived near Patriarch Tryphon.

In 933, under Patriarch Theophylact, he was chosen archbishop of the island of Corfu, which he shepherded for twenty years, from 933 to 953; the church histories note that in his time, because of the weight of his person, the see of Corfu was raised from an archbishopric to a metropolis. He became the protector of widows, the physician of the sick, the father of orphans and the comfort of all in trouble, and God gave him the gift of miracles. About 941, hearing that Slav pirates who were plundering the opposite coast meant to attack Corfu, he went himself to them at the risk of his life; they arrested and threatened him, and the Corfiots put out in their ships, sank many of the pirate vessels and freed their bishop.

He did as much again at the end of his life. In the middle of the tenth century a greedy and deceitful governor ruled Corfu, and, pressing the rich Corfiots for large sums, denounced them at the imperial court as stirring up rebellion, which put their lives in danger. Old as he was, Arsenius went to Constantinople rather than let his flock be wronged by false accusation, and the case ended well; at seventy-seven he took ship to return to them, fell ill near Skyros from the labour of the long winter journey, and reposed near Corinth on 19 January 953. He was buried at Corinth with due honours, and a little afterwards the Corfiots moved his remains to a new tomb in the cathedral of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul in the Old Fortress of Corfu; miracles soon showed his sanctity, and he was quickly honoured as a saint and patron of the island.
