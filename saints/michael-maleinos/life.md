# Michael Maleinos

Michael came from a district of Cappadocia called Charsianon, born of a good and famous family, very believing and very rich. His father's father, Eustathius, held the rank of patrician and was notable among the generals; his mother's father, Adralestus, was a patrician before he was made commander of the whole East for his courage and good sense; and his grandmother was a kinswoman of the emperor Romanus. His parents, Eudokimos and Anastasia, were related to the emperor Leo, who was then in Constantinople.

His birth was by promise, doxologia says, so that he was called a son of prayer. His mother was many years barren, and both parents grieved that their youth had gone by with no child to inherit what they had; they went often to churches and prayed and were not heard, and at last they went to a church of the Mother of God at a place called Kouna, whose icon the people there held in great reverence, and prayed for an heir. The all-immaculate one heard them, and appeared to the priest of the church, Methodius, holding an omophorion and with three veils in her right hand.

When he left his father's house for the monastic life the servants brought word to Eudokimos, who cried out like a man gone mad and pierced with spears, saying that he had destroyed his own light and lost the hope of his old age; but after a while the grief passed into spiritual joy. Eudokimos died suddenly not long after, and Michael's mother divided all the property among her children and herself became a nun, living to God's pleasure.

He settled on Mount Kyminas, and the wilderness that had been empty was filled: he brought God a people for an inheritance day by day, the page says, making worthy men out of unworthy and bringing them dry through the troubled sea of this life to the land of promise, by his profitable talk and his letters. It tells among other things of a disciple of his named Constantine, a singer, weak in body and weaker in soul, who kept three gold pieces carefully by him for his own need while the elder counselled him not to set his hope on them.

It was Michael who tonsured Athanasius, the builder of the Lavra of Athos. Athanasius saw him once in Constantinople, was won by his virtues, went with him to Kyminas and stayed many years under his obedience; and when he knew that the holy elder was about to die he fled, so as not to be made abbot in his place. He took Michael's koukoulion out of reverence and wore it ever after at the great feasts, to his own last day, which he knew beforehand; and putting on his mantle and that holy koukoulion he wore them until he gave up his soul. The Lavra, the page adds, was built at the cost of the emperor Nikephoros, who was this Michael's nephew.

Michael loved the feasts, and above all when it was a feast of Christ the Master or of the Mother of God he would keep vigil the whole night, rejoicing in the singing; on other days nobody saw him merry. For that, the page says, the all-praised Lady came herself to that church and gave him her blessing — as was shown by a wonder: a lover of Christ in Constantinople, keeping a feast devoutly and standing in the stall at the vigil of the Most Holy Mother of God, saw her in a vision saying to him that she must go to Mount Kyminas to keep the feast with the monk Michael, her faithful servant, who receives her with much longing and reverence. He went to Kyminas, spoke with the venerable man, saw that the Queen rightly set the monk Michael before all, and bore witness to the wonder. So living the life of the angels, and bearing an angel's name, he departed to the eternal kingdom. The page gives no year for his birth or his death.
