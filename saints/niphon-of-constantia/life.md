# Niphon of Constantia

In the days of the emperor Constantine the Great there was in Constantinople a nobleman of the imperial palace named Savatie, skilled in war, whom the emperor raised to the rank of general of the metropolis. Coming to that place he was received with much honour and ceremony by all, and above all by the first nobleman of the city, Agapit, who came often to the general and brought with him his son Nifon, a boy of about eight. Asked one day whether the boy knew letters, Agapit answered that he did not, the city being without teachers; and the general said that there were able teachers in Constantinople, and that if he wished he might send the boy to his own house there to learn the holy Scriptures.

Received with love by Savatie's wife, the boy was given to an able and God-fearing teacher named Petre, a priest who lived in the general's house. He set to his lessons with great eagerness, spending the greater part of the night over them, and in a little time learned much, being quick of mind and full of reverence for the things of God. He was good in his ways, gentle and humble; he ran to the church services and listened to the contests of the martyrs as they were read, wondering at the zeal and greatness of soul the saints had shown, and wherever he found the lives of the venerable and of the martyrs he read them with much love and attention.

But when he had grown and increased in good works he remembered his parents and his country and wished to leave Constantinople. The general's wife, who had no sons and had seen his life and his good works, sought to keep him there and make him her adopted son and the heir of their wealth; and when she could not, she fell into great sorrow. Seeing her downcast, the steward of her household learned the cause and undertook to make the boy forget his parents and his country.

The steward took him to the gatherings of dissolute and pampered young men, and Nifon, being young and without malice, gave himself to food and drink and feasting for some comfort in the sadness he had for his parents. Darkened in mind by drink, he who had been silent, gentle and humble became a great talker, a slanderer, a reviler and a mocker, a singer of empty songs; and forgetting his parents, his kinsfolk and his country altogether, and leaving his learning, he gave himself wholly to pleasure and drunkenness.

A devout and good Christian named Vasile, seeing him in that wretched state, told him often that he was alive only in the body while his soul had died, and that only his shadow now walked before men; but Nifon paid no attention. At other times he began to sigh and to weep for his sins, yet drawn by his bad habits he sank in unclean things as in a sea, despairing of his salvation and saying that he had perished already and could not turn to repentance, and that he might as well take his sweetness in these earthly pleasures. The devil had so hardened his heart that he could never pray, always having a stone upon it.

He had a friend named Nicodim, who came and looked at his face in wonder and was frightened, and kept silent; and when Nifon asked why he looked at him as at a stranger, he said that he had never seen him so, for his face was frightful as a blackamoor's. The young man was afraid and ashamed, covered his face with his hand and went away grieving, saying to himself that if he was black in soul and in body in this world, how would he stand at the judgement of God, and whether he could repent at all, having done so much that was displeasing to him.

He came home troubled, and contrary thoughts tossed him: the good ones told him to pray to God at night, and the bad ones that if he prayed he would be possessed and be a laughing-stock to everyone. Barely strengthening himself a little, he said that when he walked in debauchery no evil met him, and now that he wished to pray to God should he suffer harm? — and he cursed the unclean and cunning devil who brought him such thoughts. When night came he rose from his bed and began to weep and beat his breast with his hands, saying that he had lived in good faith and good ways in his earlier years and now had died, cruelly wounded with sins.

As he stood at prayer looking towards the east, a dark mist came near him and threw him into sickness and fear beyond measure, and he lay on his bed sighing and weeping for his sins. In the morning he came into the church, lifted his eyes to the icon of the most pure Virgin Mary, the Mother of God, and asked her mercy, calling her the defender of Christians and the hope of those who repent; and at that word the image of the Mother of God looked at him with a glad and gentle face. Seeing the wonder he marvelled and began to rejoice in his heart, and having prayed much and kissed the icon with love he went out saying that God has given us his most pure Mother for our defence, and we overlook even this helper.

After this the devil appeared to him in a dream in the shape of a boy who had been his companion in lawlessness, standing against him very angry and very sad; and asked why he was so sad, he answered that for three days, since Nifon had been at his friend Nicodim's and had turned about, he had been grieving, for he could not bear to be overlooked. Waking at once, Nifon understood that the devil grieves over a man's repentance, and rising in haste he ran to the church and fixed his mind and his eyes on the icon of the most holy Mother of God, and prayed until he saw her smiling again and felt a divine sweetness in his heart.

One day, going to the church to pray, he saw a man doing a lawless thing on the road and condemned him in his mind; and coming into the church and lifting his eyes to the icon as he used to, he saw the most holy Mother of God with a frightening and gloomy face, turning away from him. He was greatly afraid, and looking down he said that he had had one joy only, her image, and now that too turned its face away, and he did not know for what fault; then, gathering his thoughts, he barely remembered that he had condemned the man who sinned, and understood that it was for this. doxologia.ro's life is long, and the middle of it, on his years of ascetic struggle and on his visions, was not read.

In time a man appeared to him with sheep and a fold, and waking he understood that the man shown him was the holy apostle Paul, the sheep the people and the fold the church; and he feared greatly that he would be chosen bishop of Constantinople — Alexander being bishop then, after Mitrofan. He said in himself that he had asked God never to be a ruler over men, and now authority was to be given him; so he would do as the prophet Jonah did and flee. Taking his disciple he fled from his cell and went aboard a ship, and a gentle wind brought him quickly to Alexandria, where Alexander the third was then bishop after Peter, he who suffered for Christ in the days of Diocletian.

That same day men came from the island of Cyprus, from the city called Constantia, and asked the archbishop of Alexandria to give them a worthy bishop, their own pastor Cristofor, a holy and honoured man, having died; and he told them to wait a little until God should show such a man. That night the archbishop saw the holy apostle Paul, who asked him whom he would set as bishop of the city of Constantia. He answered that he did not know, but whomever God should will; and he was told that God would show him the worthy one, and that he should come in the morning with all the clergy into the church, watch those who entered, and entrust the pastorate to the one he saw like the apostle in everything.

In the morning the patriarch was in the church watching those who came in. Nifon, knowing nothing of this, came into the church, having said to his disciple on the way that he felt some sorrow and some joy in his heart, for something was about to happen to them. The archbishop saw him and said to his archdeacon, [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria), that this man was like the holy apostle Paul in the face; and the archdeacon answered that it was true, and that he believed he would be worthy to shepherd the Church of Christ, for he saw the angel of God walking and speaking with him, and besides that a crown of precious stone on his head.

The archbishop called the blessed Nifon, kissed him and sat down; and the archdeacon said to him, smiling, that what he had fled from he had now come to unwilling. Learning what was to happen to him, Nifon sighed and said that it was hard for him, being unworthy and sinful, that such authority should be given him; and the archbishop answered that he wished he were as unworthy himself. With that he rose, and beginning the divine Liturgy he passed him through the degrees of ordination in their whole order, making him deacon and, not many days after, priest, and then raised him to the rank of the episcopate, all the people rejoicing at such a pastor shown them by God.

After three days in Alexandria, speaking with the saints who were there, the archbishop let him go to his see, sending his archdeacon and other holy men with him for honour; and coming to the city of Constantia in Cyprus on the fourth of September he was seated, and the messengers went back to Alexandria praising God. The hierarch of God began with much diligence to shepherd the sheep of Christ, caring for their salvation. He was like a true father to orphans and widows, a benefactor to the weak and a physician without payment, and besides this he worked wonders in many ways, more than it was possible to write, for which he was greatly honoured and loved by all.

In his last sickness he asked that a mat be laid for him on the ground, and lay on it, being very ill. When day came [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-alexandria) came to him and, sitting beside him, asked whether a man gets any good from sickness; and he answered that as gold is cleansed of rust by being burned in the fire, so a sick man is cleansed of his sins. He was silent a little, and then wept; afterwards he smiled, and his face grew bright, and he said, «Welcome, holy angels», and after a little, «Rejoice, holy martyrs», and his face grew brighter still, and then, «Thanks be to you, blessed prophets».

Then Athanasius's eyes were opened, and he saw the saint kissed by all the ranks of the saints, one by one. Smiling, Nifon said, «Rejoice, most venerable and hallowed, and all you saints», and fell silent; and after a little he cried out to her who is full of grace, his most beautiful light, his helper and his strength. Then he was silent, and his face shone like the sun, so that all who were present were afraid, and a sweet fragrance was felt. So he gave his honoured and hallowed soul into the hands of God, on the twenty-third day of the month of December; and there was much weeping and lamentation through the whole city, and they buried him with honour in the great church of the Holy Apostles.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and in place of a life it prints where the life is to be found. Niphon, saint.gr says, is unknown to the synaxaristes and the Menaia; his life stands in the ancient codices and in later ones, in the Lavriotic Β81, 123 and Λ. 66 and in the Vatopedi 618, under the title «Life and conduct of our venerable father Niphon, who practised asceticism at Constantinople and became bishop of Constantia by Alexandria» — which is the Egyptian Constantia, and not one of the others of that name. The Lavriotic Codex Β 81 calls him instead bishop of Almyroupolis, and says that he died on 23 December. A service for him is in the Cryptoferrata Codex Δ. δ. II, and a free rendering of the Byzantine manuscript was published by the monastery of the Paraclete at Oropos in 1993 as «Ένας ασκητής Επίσκοπος», an ascetic bishop.
