# Quartus

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 10 noiembrie reads «Sfântul Apostol Cvart», and the page it points to has no Viață tab at all: it carries the name, the day, and a hymn — a common of the apostles, headed «Troparul Sfinților Apostoli Olimp, Rodion, Sosipatru, Erast, Terțiu, Cvart» for the six of them together and naming none of them in its text.

So the calendar calls him an apostle and sets him in that company of six kept on this day, and says nothing further: no see, no country, no century, no year, no manner of death. His is a name the corpus has not held before, and the name sweep found nothing in it to confuse him with.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 November, in one entry with four others of the Seventy. saint.gr says the five were of the Lord's seventy apostles, whose synaxis it puts on 4 January, that all of them are named in the sixteenth chapter of Romans, and that most were bishops who kept well the command of 1 Peter 5 to shepherd the flock of God willingly, not for shameful gain, and to become patterns for it. Of Quartus it says that as bishop of Berytus he struggled with courage and brought many idolaters into his bishopric as Christians. His distich makes a priest of him twice over: «Ἄρτον Θεοῦ τὸν ζῶντα Κούαρτος θύων, / Καὶ τὴν ψυχὴν ἔθυσε λοιπὸν τῷ Λόγῳ» — Quartus, sacrificing the living bread of God, sacrificed his soul also to the Word.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the same day, in one line of six of the Seventy, and closes that line «(I)» — the first century, the first date this folder has carried. Its life page quotes the greeting of Romans 16 that names him as a brother, «Приветствует вас Ераст, городской казнохранитель, и брат Кварт» (Рим. 16, 23), and makes him a disciple of the apostle who wrote it.

Of his course it says that he bore much suffering for his piety, turned a great many pagans to Christ, and reposed in peace in the rank of bishop of the city of Berytus. The calendar's own title for him spells that see twice over, «епископ Беритский (Бейрутский)», and it is on that reading that this folder is now a bishop as well as an apostle. The name page adds the synaxis of the Seventy on 4 января and nothing else.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps six of the Seventy under one heading on the Julian 10 November and gives each of them a see or a death in a single clause. His is the fullest of the six and it is new to this folder, which had no see recorded at all: he was bishop in Beirut, and he suffered much but also brought many to the faith of Christ. The see is left in this paragraph rather than written into the folder's `office`, which is a field the site has to be able to say in four languages. It closes on all six together — they fought the spiritual war, were victors, and were crowned with the crowns of glory.

The entry gives no year for any of them, so the first century this folder holds stands as it was. It says of the last three names in the heading that the book keeps them on other days of its own as well, which it prints: 8 April, 28 April and 30 October.
