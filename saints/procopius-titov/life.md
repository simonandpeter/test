# Procopius (Titov)

Procopius was born Peter Semyonovich Titov on 25 December 1877, a priest's son of a town in the Tomsk province — Кузьминск in the life the Sretensky calendar prints, Кузнецк in the longer life azbyka.ru carries, and the two pages do not agree about its name. He finished the Kazan theological academy in 1901 with a candidate's degree in theology, was tonsured a monk the same year with the name Procopius, ordained hieromonk, and set to teach in the church schools of Tomsk, then of Irkutsk from 1906.

From 1909 he was assistant head of the school of pastorship at Zhitomir in the rank of archimandrite, and on 30 August 1914 was consecrated bishop of Yelisavetgrad, a vicar of the Kherson diocese. He sat in the council of 1917–1918, which in the autumn of 1917 made him superior of the great Lavra at Petrograd. When Red Army men tried to seize it in January 1918 he was declared under arrest and let go again on the demand of the faithful, and a few days later was made its deputy head, which he was until April 1918. Then he left for Ukraine.

From 1919 he was bishop of Nikolaev, a vicar of the Odessa diocese, and from 1921 bishop of Odessa and Kherson; in the early 1920s he was tried for resisting the seizure of the churches' valuables and for his dealings with the volunteer command. Freed by February 1925, he was made archbishop of Kherson and Nikolaev that June and sat on the temporary synod set up that year. From November 1925 he governed nothing, being under arrest; in May 1926 he was given three years, which he served in the Solovki camp until November 1928, and was then held in exile at Tobolsk.

In September 1928, while he was still a prisoner, he was removed from the Kherson see, which displeased many of the diocese's clergy who did not accept the declaration of 1927. He himself issued a message condemning that declaration but did not break communion with its author. In the second half of the 1930s he was in exile at Tashkent and at Turtkul in Uzbekistan, and in that exile the priest John Skadovsky was with him.

The indictment the longer life quotes says that the two of them, having come to Turtkul, «организовали нелегальную молельню, в которой занимались совершением религиозных обрядов и сколачиванием религиозной контрреволюционной группы». On 28 October 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced both to be shot, and on 23 November 1937 the archbishop and the priest were shot together and buried in a common unmarked grave. 10 ноября ст. ст. is that civil day, so for once the calendar keeps a man on the day he died.

**The two hosts, and the calendar's own two pages, give him three different sees.** The day's line names him archbishop of Odessa, the name page archbishop of Kherson, the life page archbishop of Kherson and Nikolaev, and azbyka's life, at the sentence that kills him, archbishop of Odessa and Kherson. Three of the four are sees he did hold in succession, so the `office` here is left at the bare rank and the readings are recorded in this paragraph. **The day's line and azbyka's line also disagree about which of the day's two priests named John belongs with him**: the line puts John Skadovsky in the company of the archbishop of Kaluga, and azbyka puts him here. The calendar's own life page is titled for the two of them together, its name page for Skadovsky serves this same life, and the troparion azbyka prints has him suffer «со пресви́тером Иоа́нном» — so this folder follows the reading three pages make and says so. He was glorified in 1996 by the synod of the Ukrainian Orthodox Church and in August 2000 by the jubilee council of bishops.
