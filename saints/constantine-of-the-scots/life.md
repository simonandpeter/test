# Constantine of the Scots

The holy Constantine lived in the sixth century, saint.gr says. It is said that he was a British king, who after the death of his wife gave up the throne in favour of his son and became a monk in the monastery of Saint David, the patron of Wales. He worked together with Saint Columba, whom the page notes is honoured on the ninth of June, for the spreading of the Gospel among the Picts of Scotland; he founded a monastery there, and he was martyred by the idolaters in the year 576.

That is all the page holds, and it holds it carefully: «Λέγεται ότι», it is said that, is how the account of his kingship opens. The day's own line calls him king of the Scots and the body calls him a Briton, and the page does not reconcile the two. It gives no year for his birth, no name for his wife or his son, no name for the monastery he founded, no place for it, no manner of martyrdom and no burial place, and it prints neither apolytikion nor kontakion for him.
