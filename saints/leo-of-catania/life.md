# Leo of Catania

Leo was born in the metropolis of Ravenna, of parents of good family, though he was of far better family in his soul, for his surpassing virtue and his praiseworthy manner of life. Even before he took the office of a bishop he was doing works of virtue, caring for the affairs of the church and its government as a faithful and wise steward, serving all his fellow servants and dealing out their measures from the master's grain. When Sabinus, who had been bishop before him, died, the whole multitude of the metropolis of Catania gathered, and by God's will they all chose Leo.

He had courage of soul as well as the name of it, and fought manfully against the wolves of the mind, labouring with vigils, with prayers and with other good works to keep his sheep unharmed by heretics and by devils, rebuking every day and overturning the false glory of empty speech and showing up the fables of the pagans; and to the faithful he was very merciful and compassionate, giving many alms as one who followed Christ. He was the feeder of the poor and the readiest support of the hard-pressed, and received and helped every needy and ruined man with an open hand.

He set himself to wipe out paganism altogether, for there were still some who worshipped idols in their ignorance, and he taught them not with words only but with deeds and with wonders, that they might know the weakness of the vain gods and the surpassing power of the true God. One day he went with many people to a place where the Greeks had an idol from the time of Decius, which the mad thought a god; and Leo prayed there with tears to Christ the Master to overthrow it, and at once it fell to the ground and was broken. A most wonderful cross was found in that place at that hour, and those present were astonished. He built there a church of the holy Forty Martyrs, and another, beautiful and famous, in the name of the holy virgin martyr Lucy, who confessed at Catania; and his own relics are in that second church, and a stream flows from them always, fragrant, delivering from every evil, cleansing the passions and driving out demons.

Of the many wonders he did the life tells one at length, to show what boldness he had before Christ. There was in the island of Sicily a sorcerer named Heliodorus, who did signs and wonders by the help of the devil and outdid Jannes and Jambres and Simon, for every diabolical working was in him. He was the son of a Christian woman named Barbara and passed for a Christian himself, but from childhood he was proud and haughty and wanted to be prefect of the city so as to do his evil will unashamed. Wanting it and not getting it, he found a Jew famous in sorceries, made a friend of him, and was given a written charm and told to go at midnight to the tombs of the nobles and climb a pillar; a man dreadful to look at would come, and he was not to come down until the man had promised to do all his will.

So he went, and threw the paper into the air, and saw the devil riding on a circle. What do you want of me? said the devil. I want you to do all I desire, said he. And the devil answered: if you agree to deny Christ, I will do whatever you ask of me quickly. The wretch denied Christ, ranged himself with Satan, and was given the strongest and craftiest of the demons in malice, one called Gaspar, to stand beside him and obey him always. After that he troubled everyone with illusions and charms, not only in the metropolis of Catania but through the other parts and cities of Sicily.

One day in the market he made a river seem to run through the middle of it, so that the women passing lifted their clothes as high as they could to keep dry and the men laughed. He turned stones and wood into gold, which turned back into stone and wood in the buyer's hand; and he charmed the daughters of famous and honoured men into a satanic infatuation, so that they let themselves down from windows. The citizens denounced him to the prefect, who sent men for him and could not find him; so the prefect Lucius wrote to Constantine, the grandson of Heraclius, then ruler of the Greeks, setting out all the sorcerer's wickedness, how many Christians he had made believe in idols, and how he terrified the whole city with the threat of burning it with the fire of Etna by his spells.

The emperor sent a worthy man of his suite named Heraclides, whom he had made proconsul, to bring Heliodorus back in chains. When Heraclides reached the island the sorcerer was waiting on the shore and greeted him: I am the man you are looking for; see, I come willingly, at the emperor's command, so do not bind me like a condemned man, for had I not wished to come I would have fled and you would not have found me. And he said he would take them to Byzantium in one day, which frightened them, since it had taken them thirty days to reach Sicily. He led them into a bath and told them to wash but neither to cross themselves nor to name Christ; and as soon as they had washed they found themselves in the imperial bath at Constantinople, and their own clothes waiting outside. The emperor, hearing it all, would not speak a word with him but ordered his head struck off at once — whereupon Heliodorus asked for water, made as if to drink, went into the cup and vanished.

Back in Sicily he went on as before, until one day at the horse races he met Chrysis, a nephew of blessed Leo, and offered to make him a horse better than anyone's; the horse came at the word, by the devil's illusion, and vanished when the prefect sent for it. Leo told the prefect the cause of it, and the prefect shut Heliodorus in prison as a sorcerer and condemned him to death; but on the way to the block he promised the executioners three pounds of gold, made them a stone that seemed gold, and was let go, and when they came to share the gold it was a stone as before.

The merciful shepherd counselled him often, for Christ's goodness, to leave off his evil works and not be tormented in the eternal fire with the devils; but he took the saint's counsels for idle talk and added lawlessness to lawlessness, until he dared to come into the holy church to mock the divine Mysteries. On a great feast, while Leo was serving the Liturgy, he came in dancing out of all order and saying blasphemies for a laugh, and boasted that he would make the saint and everyone else dance; and he could not, for the divine power stopped the devil's working.

Sickened by it, Leo knelt and prayed God fervently to help him shame the man's arts. Then, having communicated of the Holy Mysteries, he ran out of the holy altar before he had taken off his vestments, bound Heliodorus fast by the neck with his own omophorion, and said: The Lord God, who drove your father the devil out of heaven, rebuke you, that you may work your sorceries no longer to the deceiving and ruin of many. So saying he dragged him to the place of the condemned, which was called Achillion, and told the crowd to gather much wood and make a great fire; and when he had first made him confess all his evil deeds, he pulled him in and went into the fire with him, and stood there until Heliodorus was burnt through and became ashes.

The great hierarch and wonderworker came out of the flame unharmed, to the terror of those who watched: the fire had not dared touch his holy vestments, nor burn them, nor so much as one hair of his honoured head. The report of it went everywhere, and the emperor at Constantinople wrote asking him to come and be seen and give his blessing; and he went, so as not to seem disobedient to an imperial command, and was honoured by the emperor himself, and did many wonders in the imperial city — above all, putting live coals and incense into his garment and censing with them, to the glory of God. When he wished to return to his throne they all escorted him with great honour. Doxologia prints no year for him anywhere in the life.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and tells the same story with more of it. saint.gr says Leo was born at Ravenna in Italy of devout and noble parents, was ordained presbyter there after his studies, and was later elected bishop of Catania in Sicily for the purity and spirituality of his life. He became the protector of orphans and widows, taught and admonished his flock, and built the church of the holy martyr Lucy; and he was marked out, the page adds, for his contests against the heretics, whom he defeated and put to shame not with words only but in writing.

God endowed him with the gift of wonderworking. The magician Heliodoros, who had led many astray with his sorceries and boasted that he could do anything and had no fear of fire, was burnt up by fire after Leo's prayer; the faithful who saw it were astounded, and word of it reached the imperial city at once, so that the emperors Leo IV and Constantine VI summoned him to Constantinople and did him much honour. He fell asleep in peace in 785, and his body was buried in the church of St Lucy. The page closes with a caution of its own: «Ο Άγιος Νικόδημος αναφέρει ότι ο Όσιος Λέων έζησε στην εποχή του Λέοντος του Σοφού (886 - 912 μ.Χ.)» — St Nikodemos puts him a century later, in the days of Leo the Wise, so no year is recorded in this folder.
