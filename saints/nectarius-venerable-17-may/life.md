# Nectarius (17 May)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 17 mai reads «Sfântul Cuvios Nectarie» — the holy venerable Nectarius — with no epithet, no monastery, no country and no century, and his own page on the site carries only that line, the date, and the day's scripture readings. There is no Viață tab and no Tropar tab; the page is a heading and nothing more.

The display name here carries the day, because the calendar gives nothing else to tell him by, and the corpus already keeps men of that name. He is not to be taken for Nektarios of Aegina, who is a twentieth-century Greek hierarch kept in November; the only thing said of this Nectarius is the rank word «Cuvios», that he was a monastic. No other calendar was checked for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same civil day, and it is the Greek that gives this bare Romanian line a life. saint.gr has Nektarios and [Theophanes](/saints/theophanes-venerable-17-may) as brothers by the same parents and hieromonks, born and bred of the noble house of the Apsarades at Ioannina late in the fifteenth century — a family the page traces back to a fishmonger's trade and forward into the politics of Epirus. Their own autobiography, it says, begins at the moment they gave themselves to God and makes no mention of their noble descent, their upbringing, their education or their property; their parents and their three sisters took the monastic habit and lived in a cell near the village of the island, and the third sister's name was probably Magdalene. The two had a schooling remarkable for their day at the famous Philanthropinon monastery under the abbot Makarios Philanthropinos, and neither riches nor glory nor learning could overcome their love of God.

Very young, they took the holy habit about 1495 from a venerable elder named Savvas, and stayed with him ten whole years in the hermitage of the Forerunner on the island of Ioannina until his falling asleep on 9 April 1505. Then they went to the Holy Mountain, where they were received by the former Ecumenical Patriarch Niphon II, who was living as a monk at Dionysiou, and asked and received of him a rule and canon of the monastic state; he told them to return to the cell of their repentance. Finding it occupied by certain lay lords who claimed founders' rights over it, they withdrew into the inner parts of the island and settled by a half-ruined hermitage of Saint Panteleimon built in a cave above the lake, where a hermit named Antony had been shut up eighteen years and been given the gift of discernment. With the written leave of the metropolitan of Ioannina and the patriarch Pachomios I they cut away a great piece of the rock, filled the hollow with earth and stones, and at their own expense raised the church of the Forerunner in 1506 or 1507, with cells and the other necessary buildings; and afterwards they built the hermitage of Saint Nicholas at Lepeno for their parents and sisters. The Greek calendar names them founders of the Varlaam monastery at Meteora, and its head line dates Nektarios's death to 1550; his right palm is kept incorrupt at Varlaam.
