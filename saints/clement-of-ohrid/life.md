# Clement of Ohrid

Clement of Ohrid was the disciple of Saints Cyril and Methodius, and is counted one of the seven apostles of Bulgaria. He was a scholar and a writer, fluent in Slavonic, in Greek and in Latin, and is reckoned an enlightener of the Slavs. He carried on the work of translation that Cyril and Methodius had begun, and he was also the author of works of his own — the first volumes of Slavonic spiritual literature.

He was the first Bulgarian hierarch to serve, to preach and to write in the Slavonic tongue. He defended the faith in Christ before the pagans, baptising and proclaiming the gospel through the whole province of Moesia, the old name of the region between the Balkan mountains of Bulgaria and the Danube. He founded many schools for children and for grown men in south-western Macedonia, and the number of his students was enormous; of those chosen and accepted for ordination there were about three thousand five hundred men.

At Ohrid he founded the monastery of Saint Panteleimon, and there he was buried, in the year 916, in a coffin he had made with his own hands. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 25 November, the day of his falling asleep, and on 27 July as well.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 22 November, where the Romanian keeps him on 27 July, and tells his life in one short paragraph. saint.gr says he came from Bulgaria and lived in the years of the emperor Michael, 842 to 867, the son of Theophilos the iconoclast. From a child he had the monastic inclination, and so took refuge in a monastery, where he practised the spiritual life and studied the Holy Scripture methodically; and for his evangelical virtues he was ordained bishop of Bulgaria by the holy [Methodius](/saints/methodius-of-moravia), and shepherded his flock apostolically to the end of his life. He died in peace, and his holy relic was laid in the monastery of Saint Panteleimon which he had himself built at Ohrid in Bulgaria. His head, the page adds, is at the monastery of the Honourable Forerunner at Veroia.
