# Crispus

Crispus was an apostle, saint.gr says, and it is said that he was the founder of the Church of the island of Aegina; and he was, besides, the ruler of the synagogue at Corinth. His memory is mentioned by certain hagiologies on the eighth of December as well, together with others of the Seventy, such as Sosthenes and the rest.

According to the divine Chrysostom, however, Crispus is the apostle Sosthenes. The page quotes him from the thirty-ninth homily on the Acts: «Oίμαι δε τούτον και Σωσθένη λέγεσθαι. Oς τοσούτον ανήρ πιστός ην, ώστε και τύπτεσθαι, και παρείναι αεί τω Παύλω» — I think that this man is also called Sosthenes, who was so faithful a man as to be beaten, and to be always with Paul.

That identification is the page's report of Chrysostom's opinion and not a thing this folder settles. It gives Crispus no century beyond the apostolic age, no manner of death, no relic and no hymn, and prints no couplet for him; and the corpus's own Sosthenes is a different man on a different day. What the Greek calendar attests is the day, the apostolate, and the two traditions about Aegina and about the name.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 4 Δεκεμβρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/12/04/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3141/saint.aspx); read 2 October 2026.*
