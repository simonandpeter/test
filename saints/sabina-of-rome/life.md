# Sabina of Rome

Sabina was a Roman noblewoman of the second century, the widow of the senator Valentinus and the daughter of Herod Metallarius. She owed her conversion to her servant Serapia, a devout Christian from Antioch, who shared the teaching of the faith with her — salvation, the moral life, devotion to Christ — and drew the widow gradually to it despite her standing in the empire; this was around the beginning of the second century, probably in the reign of Hadrian (about 117–138), and Sabina became known for her piety, her courage and her devotion to the Gospel, a support to the Christians of Rome.

She was beheaded under Hadrian, on 29 August in about the year 126, a month after her servant Serapia had suffered the same death for Christ.
