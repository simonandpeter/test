# Eusebius of Syria

Where he was from and of what parents he was born, his history does not show. This only is known of him: that by his labours as a hermit he gained heaven for his country. He went first to a monastery and there became a monk. Then he climbed to the top of a mountain, and there made himself a small enclosure of stones, and inside it struggled with neither roof nor shade over him. He had one garment of skin, and his food was soaked chickpeas and beans; now and then he ate dried figs.

He held to that from the beginning to the end, so that when he had come to great old age and nearly all his teeth had fallen out he changed neither his food nor his dwelling, but bore bravely the blowing of the contrary winds. His face was wrinkled and the flesh of his body dried up, so that not even his belt would stay at his waist but fell to the ground, the flesh beneath it being wasted away, and the bones with it, on both sides.

Because many came to him and disturbed his quiet, he went off to a hermitage that lay near, and making himself an enclosure in the angle of its wall he struggled there at his usual labour. It is said of him besides that through all seven weeks of the Great Fast he ate only fifteen dried figs, though he was tormented by an illness past telling. With labours like these he lived more than ninety years, and then departed to the Lord. Doxologia gives him no year and prints no troparion.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and puts him in the fifth century. Out of love towards God he took the road of the monastic life, saint.gr says: he went first to a monastery and was tonsured there, and afterwards withdrew to the ridge of a mountain near the city of Asicha, where he fenced round a roofless patch of ground with stones and passed his life in fasting and bodily hardship, so that his flesh fell away and only the bones were left. The fame of his holiness brought many to him asking his prayer and his blessing; and to escape the noise and keep the stillness of his spiritual struggle he left that place and moved to a monastery nearby, where he went on living in prayer and severe ascesis. So, having lived in a manner pleasing to God, he fell asleep in peace at the age of ninety.

The Serbian church keeps him on the same day. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives him six short sentences and nothing else: a Syrian hermit, who struggled first under the direction of holy men and later withdrew to be alone in the desert. He fed on plant food only, the Prologue says, and did not so much as taste fruit, and he spent his whole time out in the open at prayer, bearing every kind of foul weather. It has him live to the age of ninety-five and fall asleep peacefully in the Lord in 440 — a year and an age the Greek page this folder was read from does not give, and whose age it contradicts, so neither has been written into the dates here.
