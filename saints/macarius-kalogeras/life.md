# Macarius Kalogeras

Macarius Kalogeras, whom saint.gr calls the teacher of the nation, was born on Patmos in 1688, of a well-off family. He went afterwards to Constantinople for his studies and came into contact with important families and clergy of the reigning city, and when his long years of study were over he served as deacon beside the Metropolitan of Nicomedia.

Because he cared greatly for the teaching and the bringing up of the Greeks then under subjection, he returned to Patmos in 1713 and founded the Patmian School. The lessons there were given free to every pupil, and at first Macarius taught alone; after some years the best of the pupils were chosen as teachers of the school. They came from Patmos and from other parts of the Ottoman empire, and when their number rose to a hundred new buildings were judged necessary and were raised with the help of well-off donors from Constantinople. The Patmias grew into one of the most important schools of the Greek world.

Macarius, whose constitution was weak, died on Patmos in 1737, and his vigorous work preserved his name. His priesthood never rose above the rank of deacon, because, as he said himself, it pleased him better to serve than to be served. The page prints no hymn for him.
