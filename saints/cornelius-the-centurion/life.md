# Cornelius the Centurion

Of Cornelius the Acts of the Apostles tell (10:1–13): a God-fearing Roman centurion, to whom an angel of the Lord pointed out that he should meet the apostle Peter; converted to Christianity — the first of the pagans to believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, the Romanian Lives say, who after his baptism left all pagan things and followed Peter — he taught the teaching of Christ in Phoenicia, Cyprus, Antioch and Ephesus, and was chosen bishop of Skepsis in Mysia.

His missionary zeal bore rich fruit and many pagans believed the Gospel; some denounced him to the governor Demetrius, who seized him and led him into a pagan temple before a crowd to sacrifice to the idols, and Cornelius not only stayed immovable in his faith but by his confession brought the governor’s wife and son to believe the teaching of the Gospel — he told Demetrius his family would come to no harm, and the governor understood a little later, when a great earthquake levelled the temple and his wife and son came out of the ruins unhurt; the [Demetrius, Evanthia and Demetrianus](/saints/demetrius-of-skepsis) kept on 11 September are that family. You lead Cornelius out of the faithless life, the verse says, my Christ, first-fruit of the faithful from the nations. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 13 September, the Romanian with a troparion.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, calls him a Roman and a Roman officer at Caesarea in Palestine, baptized by the apostle Peter after a revelation from heaven (Acts 10:1), and the first of the Gentiles to enter the Church, when some still thought it was only for the Jews and those who took their circumcision. Once baptized he left everything and followed Peter, who later made him a bishop and sent him to the pagan city of Scepsis.

There he bore many insults and torments for Christ, but by God’s power brought down the temple of Apollo and baptized the city’s prince, [Demetrius](/saints/demetrius-of-skepsis), and 277 pagans. Knowing from God the day of his death, he called the Christians together, counselled them, prayed for them and died in peace in honoured old age. In time his grave was forgotten and neglected, until he appeared to Silvanus, bishop of Troas, showed him the grave and told him to build a church over it, which the bishop did with the help of a rich citizen, Eugenius; many wonders came from his relics.
