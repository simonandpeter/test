# The Seven Maccabee Brothers

After the martyrdom of their teacher [Eleazar](/saints/eleazar-the-scribe), seven brothers of an honoured family were taken with [their mother](/saints/solomone-mother-of-the-maccabees) and, because they were well born, sent to the king at Antioch to be examined. Antiochus surnamed Epiphanes had ordered the Jews driven from the laws of their fathers, made to worship idols and to eat of what was sacrificed to them, and above all made to eat swine's flesh, which their law forbids; and the temple of the Lord had been turned into a shrine of Olympian Zeus, with the idol set in the holy place.

The seven had been disciples of Eleazar, the teacher of Jerusalem, and remembering his teaching well they stood with greatness of soul in their faith and would not obey the king's command or break their law. For that they were beaten long with whips and with ox sinews. What followed the Romanian life takes, as it says, from the second book of the Maccabees. The first of them said to the king: What do you wish to ask and to learn from us? We are ready to die rather than break the laws of our fathers.

The king in anger had pans and cauldrons heated, and ordered that the tongue of the one who had spoken for them be cut out, his skin flayed from his body and the ends of his limbs cut off, while his brothers and his mother looked on; and when all his limbs were cut away he was given to the fire and fried in the pan, though he still breathed. As the steam rose from the pan the rest with their mother encouraged one another to die manfully, saying that the Lord God sees and is truly comforted in His servants, as Moses testified in his song before the people.

The second was brought out and mocked, the skin drawn off his head with the hair, and asked whether he would eat swine's flesh before every limb of his body was tortured; and answering in the speech of his fathers he said, No. He too was tortured as the first had been, and at his last breath said: You, most wretched man, destroy us out of this present life, but the King of the world will raise us up, who are killed for His laws, to the resurrection of everlasting life. So it went with the third and with those after him.

The youngest told the king that his brothers had gained the life that is eternal, and that the king by the judgement of God would take the just punishment of his pride; that he gave up his own soul and body for the laws of his fathers, as his brothers had done, calling on God to be swiftly merciful to Israel; and that the king by his torturing and his beating would himself confess that God is One, and that with him and his brothers the wrath of the Almighty, justly brought upon their whole people, would cease. At that the king burned against him more bitterly than against the others; and he too was killed, and went pure out of this life, hoping in the Lord.

Their mother, seeing it, was filled with unspeakable gladness that she had sent all seven of her sons blameless before God; and standing over their bodies she stretched out her hands and prayed with hot tears and gave up her soul into the hands of God. Afterwards, the life says, God had mercy on the Hebrew people for the blood of His servants and raised up Judas Maccabeus of the priestly stock, who withstood Antiochus, drove out his generals, and cleansed the temple of Jerusalem of the idols' defilements; and Antiochus, struck inwardly with an incurable wound, so that worms bred in him and an unbearable stench came from his body, confessed at last the God of Israel whom he had blasphemed, but found no place of repentance.

The life will not give the brothers' names, and says why. They are not set down in the Scripture of the book of the Maccabees, and among other tellers they do not agree: the Prologue and the Synaxarion of the months call them Avim, Antonin, Gurie, Eleazar, Evseon, Alim and Marcel, while Josephus the Jew, who lived in the days of Vespasian and wrote their passion at length, calls them Maccabeus, Aver, Mahir, Iuda, Ahas, Aret and Iacov. The life holds Josephus the more trustworthy, because Antonin and Marcel are Greek names and not Hebrew ones; but it leaves the matter with God, who has written them in the book of life. So this entry keeps the seven together and names none of them.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day, 1 August, and gives the story a date and a text. saint.gr opens with the word of the fourth book of Maccabees, «Αὐτοκράτωρ ἐστὶ τῶν παθῶν ὁ εὐσεβὴς λογισμός» — pious reasoning is sovereign and master over the passions — and says that the seven Maccabee brothers proved it with surpassing courage by their stand before Antiochus king of Syria, about the year 5327 from the creation of the world, or 173 before Christ, when he promised them glories, honours and earthly enjoyments if they would trample the Mosaic law and eat of the forbidden foods he offered them.

Their teacher went first. He was ninety years old, the page says, and he carried out to the last degree the law he taught them, with the result that Antiochus threw him into the fire. Inspired by the sacrifice of their old teacher, the seven brothers kept the same brave stand when the king called them before him. At first Antiochus tried to flatter them with various praises of their youth, telling them that if they ate of the meats offered to idols they would enjoy great honours and would of course be saved from death. Then the seven brothers answered him: «χαλεπώτερον γὰρ αὐτοῦ τοῦ θανάτου νομίζομεν εἶναι σοῦ τὸν ἐπὶ τὴ παρανομῶ σωτηρία ἠμῶν ἔλεον» — more harmful even than death itself, we think, is your pity for our unlawful rescue.

Enraged at that, Antiochus killed them all, one by one, with wheels and fire and javelins; and when their mother Solomone saw it she threw herself into the fire of her own accord, and so all of them together took the crown of martyrdom. The page then names the seven, which the Romanian calendar does not: Abeim (or Abibus), Antonius (or Antoninus), Gurias, Eleazar, Eusebonas, Acheim and Marcellus (or Samonas, or Eulalus, or Marcus). It adds that a portion of the relic of Saint Eleazar is at the monastery of Panteleimon on the Holy Mountain, that the relic of Saint Solomone lies incorrupt in the patriarchal church of Saint George at the Phanar in Constantinople, and that a portion of it is at Xeropotamou.

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović keeps the ten of them under one heading on the Julian 1 August and tells the whole persecution. They suffered, it says, for the purity of the faith of Israel, under the king Antiochus, whom some called Epiphanes, the enlightened, and others Epimanes, the mad. God let a great calamity fall on the holy city because of the great sins in Jerusalem, and above all because of the snatching after the high-priestly power and the crimes committed in the course of it; and some faithless high priests and other elders of Jerusalem helped the king in his design.

Returning to Antioch the king took with him the seven sons called Maccabees and their mother [Solomone](/saints/solomone-mother-of-the-maccabees). The seven brothers were named, this book says, Abim, Antonin, Eleazar, Gurias, Eusebon, Alim and Marcellus. Before their mother’s eyes the wicked king tortured one son after another, flaying the skin from each one’s face and then throwing them into the fire. They all bore the torments and the death bravely and did not renounce their faith.

The book gives the year, which the Romanian and Greek pages did not: all of them suffered honourably for the faith in the one living God about the year 180 before Christ. One of the seven bears the same name as the old priest of the heading and is not that man, and the youngest of them was three years old.
