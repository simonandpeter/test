# Benjamin (Voskresensky)

Benjamin, in the world Basil Konstantinovich Voskresensky, was born on 15 January 1871 in the village of Pereslavtsevo in the Uglich district of Yaroslavl province, the son of a priest, Constantine, who had a great gift for music, as had his sons: all five were regents of their school choirs. He studied at the Rostov church school, at the Yaroslavl seminary from 1886 to 1892 and at the Moscow Theological Academy, which he finished in 1896, and in 1898 he was made assistant inspector of the Kutaisi seminary, where he taught Russian literature and history.

On 4 June 1921 a congress of the clergy and laity of the Tutaev district chose him as candidate for its see; that year he was tonsured with the name Benjamin and consecrated bishop of Tutaev (Romanov-Borisoglebsk), vicar of the Yaroslavl diocese and one of the closest helpers of Metropolitan Agathangel. azbyka.ru’s summary says he opposed the Renovationist schism, was arrested for it in 1922 and sentenced to seven years, and was released early.

On the evening of 12 June 1927, after his journeys and sermons in the district, among them at Poshekhonye-Volodarsk, he was arrested and imprisoned at Yaroslavl, and on 23 September 1927 the OGPU sentenced him to three years’ exile in Kazakhstan. He was sent to Dzhambeity in the Uralsk region and later moved to Karatyube, and from exile he kept up a correspondence with the clergy and faithful of his vicariate; the summary says he criticised the government’s anti-religious policy and the Declaration of Metropolitan Sergius.

On 18 February 1930 an OGPU investigator ordered him charged for spreading anti-Soviet letters, and on 17 March he was summoned with other exiles from Karatyube to Uralsk and imprisoned. The investigation lasted about a year; he was gravely ill throughout and lay in the prison hospital, with emphysema, myocarditis and a paralysis of his right side after a stroke, so that he could hardly write or speak, but he was not released. On 10 September 1931 a troika of the OGPU in Kazakhstan sentenced him to ten years in a camp, a sentence that could not be carried out: he died in the prison hospital and was buried in an unknown grave.

The Sretensky calendar keeps him on 22 September old style as bishop of Romanov and dates his death 1930; azbyka.ru’s day prints 1932 and its summary 1931. Its long life, which quotes many of his letters at length, was read for his story and not in full, and it prints no hymn for him.
