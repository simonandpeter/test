# Nicanor of Zavorda

Nicanor was born in 1491 at Thessalonica, to well-off parents, John and Maria, who had waited long for a child; after many years of prayer, fasting and good works a son was given them, and at his baptism he was called Nicholas. They lived in the quarter of Agios Menas and set him from the first to be brought up by the Gospel, giving him to a devout teacher. From boyhood he wanted his whole life to be the Lord's, and spent it in prayer, fasting, vigils and study.

When he was twenty his father died, and his mother not long after. He divided the whole of the inheritance among the needy, the orphans and the poor, and became a monk with the name Nicanor. Word of his manner of life reached the Metropolitan of Thessalonica, who ordained him deacon and then priest to serve beside him. One night, as he kept vigil at prayer, a voice told him: Go to Mount Kallistratos, to carry on the higher struggle.

At only twenty-seven he set out with [Dionysius of Olympus](/saints/dionysius-of-olympus) for that mountain, also called Vermion, in the country of Grevena. By God's ordering the two were parted on the road and Nicanor alone arrived. There he dug himself a hermit's cave in the rock face above the waters of the Aliakmon, and from time to time went down to the villages round about to strengthen the Christians in a hard time.

Praying one night he heard the voice again, telling him to go to the summit and find an icon of the Saviour hidden there since the days of the iconoclasts, and to build a monastery on that place. He found the icon, and built there a church and a monastery dedicated to the Transfiguration of the Lord, known today as the monastery of Zavorda. He died on 7 August 1549, at fifty-eight, and his relics are kept in the chapel of Saint John the Baptist within his monastery.
