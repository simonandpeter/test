# Joseph the Hesychast

Joseph the Hesychast was one of the most remarkable Athonite ascetics of the twentieth century; through the disciples who carried his teaching and his way of life into ever larger communities he can fairly be called the chief author of the present restoration of the prayer of the heart on the Holy Mountain.

He was born in 1898 on the island of Paros and baptised Francis. Fatherless from childhood, he went as a youth to work in the port of Piraeus and then served in the army; after his service he met in Athens an Athonite monk from a cell at Karyes and asked to be taken with him when he returned. He gave all he had to the poor and to his household and came to Athos at twenty-three, looking at once for a spiritual father to teach him the prayer of the heart — and found none.

After a short stay at Katounakia with the elder Daniel he went to Vigla, near the cave of Athanasius the Athonite, searching the caves and places where the old ascetics had lived; at last he met Father Arsenios, who became his companion in the struggle, and together they found the elder Ephrem the Barrel-maker and settled in a poor and ruinous hut at the skete of St Basil. They kept to the hut only in the coldest months; for nearly ten years the rest of the year was spent in the rock caves of Athos, on a little dry bread and prayer.

In January 1938 Joseph and Arsenios left the skete of Little St Anne for a remote and hidden place in the mountains, where they built a chapel of John the Baptist and three small cells; in nearly thirteen years there seven ascetics gathered round him. He never allowed himself rest, despite a frail health that brought him slowly to a state of half-paralysis; because his younger disciples could not bear the harshness of the caves he moved the community in June 1951 to the more accessible New Skete, and there, on 28 August 1959, he gave his soul to God. God had given him many spiritual gifts — the vision of light, wonderful visions, foresight and the reading of thoughts — and after his falling asleep the Holy Spirit bore witness in him, his remains giving off a sweet fragrance.

The Greek calendar keeps him too, on 16 August, and its page adds the Athonite stages of his life and a second account of his repose. saint.gr says he was born in 1898 — or, according to others, on 12 February 1897 — at the village of Leukes on Paros, that his worldly name was Frangiskos, his father George and his mother Maria, and that before the Holy Mountain he traded in Piraeus and then in Athens. His first stop on Athos was Katounakia, where the elder Daniel helped him greatly, though he did not stay, loving the stricter hesychastic life; at a festival of the Transfiguration on the peak he met the elder Arsenios, who from then on never left him.

They submitted to the elder Ephraim of the hut of the Annunciation at Katounakia and went with him to the Skete of Saint Basil; after Ephraim's repose their great struggles began, with watchfulness and the enclosing of the mind in the heart for their chief work. In 1938 the two moved to the sheer caves of Little Saint Anne, one of which held a church of the Forerunner, and stayed until 1947; in 1951 he moved to the New Skete, to the hut of the Annunciation of the Theotokos. To him, the page says, is owed the spiritual rebirth and manning of six monasteries of the Holy Mountain and of many women's communities in Greece, and from that cave the elder Ephraim went out to found houses in Canada and America. His teaching is in the sixty-five letters published by the monastery of Philotheou, and his manner of life showed the whole teaching of Saint Gregory Palamas put into practice.

saint.gr puts his repose on 15 August 1959, the feast of the Dormition, where the life above has 28 August — the same day under the two styles, thirteen days apart — and quotes the elder Ephraim's account of it: that his love for the Mother of God was beyond all description, that he had long been asking her to take him and that she informed him a month beforehand; that on that morning he sat in his chair in the courtyard waiting for the hour and asked why God delayed; and that at the last he said, it is all finished, I am going, I am departing, bless me, and leaned his head to the right and was gone. Nothing of the date above is altered here; the Greek reading is set beside it.
