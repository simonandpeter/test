# Joseph the Hesychast

Joseph the Hesychast was one of the most remarkable Athonite ascetics of the twentieth century; through the disciples who carried his teaching and his way of life into ever larger communities he can fairly be called the chief author of the present restoration of the prayer of the heart on the Holy Mountain.

He was born in 1898 on the island of Paros and baptised Francis. Fatherless from childhood, he went as a youth to work in the port of Piraeus and then served in the army; after his service he met in Athens an Athonite monk from a cell at Karyes and asked to be taken with him when he returned. He gave all he had to the poor and to his household and came to Athos at twenty-three, looking at once for a spiritual father to teach him the prayer of the heart — and found none. After a short stay at Katounakia with the elder Daniel he went to Vigla, near the cave of Athanasius the Athonite, searching the caves and places where the old ascetics had lived; at last he met Father Arsenios, who became his companion in the struggle, and together they found the elder Ephrem the Barrel-maker and settled in a poor and ruinous hut at the skete of St Basil. They kept to the hut only in the coldest months; for nearly ten years the rest of the year was spent in the rock caves of Athos, on a little dry bread and prayer.

In January 1938 Joseph and Arsenios left the skete of Little St Anne for a remote and hidden place in the mountains, where they built a chapel of John the Baptist and three small cells; in nearly thirteen years there seven ascetics gathered round him. He never allowed himself rest, despite a frail health that brought him slowly to a state of half-paralysis; because his younger disciples could not bear the harshness of the caves he moved the community in June 1951 to the more accessible New Skete, and there, on 28 August 1959, he gave his soul to God. God had given him many spiritual gifts — the vision of light, wonderful visions, foresight and the reading of thoughts — and after his falling asleep the Holy Spirit bore witness in him, his remains giving off a sweet fragrance.
