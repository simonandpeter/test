# Cyril the New of Paros

saint.gr says Cyril the New was born at Marpissa on Paros in 1748 and bore the surname Papadopoulos. While still young he went to the Holy Mountain and became a pupil of the Athonias school at Vatopedi. His teacher there was his own countryman [Athanasius the Parian](/saints/athanasius-of-paros), from whom, the page quotes, «αντέγραψε και εφάρμοσε ισόβια τον πύρινο ζήλο και την ασταμάτητη, ακαταπόνητη δράση για τη δόξα του Παντοκράτορα» — he copied, and applied for the rest of his life, that fiery zeal and that ceaseless, untiring activity for the glory of the Almighty.

The page quotes the school's rector, Archimandrite Nikephoros Mikragiannanites, to the effect that in the Parian's time the Athonias knew splendid days and that his pupils advanced in virtue — among them the new martyr [Athanasius Koulakiotis](/saints/athanasius-koulakiotis) and the founder of the monastery of the Great Tree on Paros, the venerable Cyril Papadopoulos. He stayed on the Holy Mountain as a monk for a good while, learning the sacred letters and the outward wisdom too, and attached himself to the renewing movement of the reverend Kollyvades and to its chief representatives, among whom were the brothers Hierotheos and Philotheos Georgiou.

He was, the page says, the founder of the monastery of the Great Tree on Paros and of its community of nuns, the renovator of the holy monastery of Saint George of Langada, and the man who mediated the revival of the monastery of Longovarda and of the monastery of the Phaneromeni on Naxos.

He was enriched, it says, with the gift of foresight and of wonderworking: he made the sign of the cross over a snake and it was killed; he laid his cassock on the sea and travelled on it; and he struck the rock at the waterless monastery of Saint George, and holy water came out that runs to this day. He fell asleep in the Lord on 11 July 1833 at the monastery of Saint George, of which he had been renovator and abbot, and was buried there; his venerable skull survives in that monastery. The page names its source for all of this: Monk Moses of the Holy Mountain, *Βατοπαιδινό Συναξάρι*, published by the Great Monastery of Vatopedi, Holy Mountain, 2007.
