# Peter of Damascus

Doxologia's life of Peter of Damascus opens with the uncertainty about when he lived. Some say this saint was of the eighth century, others of the twelfth, and the page explains the confusion by there having been two Damascenes — that is, saints who lived in Damascus, a city in what is now Syria — both of them named Peter. The one spoken of here, it says, was a great ascetic, and he was exceedingly merciful.

Because of that mercy, the page goes on, the Damascene did not own so much as one book; rather he would borrow books in order to read them, and he read with zeal, gathering wisdom as the bee gathers honey. For a time he was bishop in Damascus, but when he stood against the Islamic faith and against the Manichaean heresy, the Arabs cut out his tongue and cast him into exile in the innermost part of Arabia. Even so God gave him the gift of speech, so that in his exile too he preached the holy Gospel and turned many to faith in Christ.

Peter of Damascus was a monk, the page adds, most probably a hesychast, whom [Nicodemus of the Holy Mountain](/saints/nicodemus-the-hagiorite), going by the epithet «Damaschin», identifies with Peter, bishop in Damascus in the second half of the eighth century. He is the author of a writing in two books, a synthesis of Christian spirituality down to his own time, which may be counted the first method, or the first itinerary of the spiritual life, to appear in the Christian East; over against the older authors he brings out the organic bond between the virtues and puts the accent on the practical character of the spiritual life.

He died a confessor and a martyr, the life ends, and passed over to the heavenly dwellings of the Kingdom of Christ. The page prints no year of its own and no hymn, and the two centuries it offers are left standing here as it leaves them.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day. saint.gr has him a priest in full awareness of his obligations to Christ and to the people, who lit up and equipped the faithful by his own blameless and most instructive example and by regular and thoroughly practical preaching; and it names him the author of the Νηπτική βίβλος, the book on watchfulness contained in the Philokalia. His love for Christ, the page says, he showed not only in peaceful times but in hard ones: about the year 775 he crowned his life, giving his head readily to death by the sword rather than go back on his confession of Christ. The distich over him turns on that one stroke: «Ὁ διελέγξας τοὺς παραπλῆγας Πέτρος, Θνῄσκει μονοπλὴξ τῷ διὰ ξίφους τέλει» — Peter, who confuted the deranged, dies at one blow by the sword's end.
