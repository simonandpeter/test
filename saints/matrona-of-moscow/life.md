# Matrona of Moscow

Matrona was born in 1881 into a family of poor peasants in the village of Sebino, in the government of Tula. Her parents, Dimitrie and Natalia, were devout and honest people who lived very modestly with their four children; she was the youngest, born when they were no longer young. Before the birth her mother thought of giving her to an orphanage, but a white bird with a human face and closed eyes appeared to her in a dream, settled on her right hand, and the God-fearing woman took the dream for a reproof and gave up the thought. The child was born without sight, and her mother loved her beyond measure.

God meant to show his chosen vessel from the first: at her baptism, as she was dipped in the water, a column of fragrant incense was seen rising out of the font, and the pious priest Vasile told all who were there of the child's holiness; there was also a raised mark in the shape of a cross on her breast. Because she was blind from birth the children mocked her and the older girls beat her with nettles, so that from childhood she kept away from people, stopped going out of the house and gave herself to prayer and to thinking on the things of God.

She lived near the village church and went to all the services, standing unmoving, and in time learned all the chants. Her inward sight made her love the icons, so that in her room the whole wall was covered with them from top to bottom, with lamps burning before them without ceasing, and she passed long hours at prayer there, holding the whole world in her child's heart. At six or seven the rich gifts God had poured into her appeared: she could see people's thoughts and sins plainly.

Through the care of the village's landowner she was able to make pilgrimages to Kyiv, Moscow and Saint Petersburg. When she came into the cathedral of Saint Andrew, where [John of Kronstadt](/saints/john-of-kronstadt) was serving, the holy father ordered the people to stand aside and make room for the young Matrona, then only fourteen, and called her "the eighth pillar of Russia".

Hard and troubled times soon came upon Russia: the revolution, the dividing of the land, the famine. In 1925, at forty, she came to live in Moscow, staying short spells in Christians' houses, where great crowds came to her for comfort and for release from griefs and troubles. She would quiet them, stroke their heads, sign them with the cross and say words of encouragement; sometimes she joked and sometimes she was severe, and sometimes one word healed a soul of its trouble. Not long before her repose she told those near her that they should all come to her and tell her their troubles as if she were alive, for she would see them and hear them and help them.

She passed to the dwellings above on 2 May 1952. On 8 March 1998 her relics were found incorrupt and fragrant, pouring out wonders without ceasing, and were laid with great reverence in the monastery of the Protection of the Mother of God in Moscow; a year later, on 2 May 1999, she was numbered among the saints, and her veneration has since spread through the world.
