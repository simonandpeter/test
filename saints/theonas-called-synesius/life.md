# Theonas, called Synesius

Theonas was a magician. He came and stood before Diocletian at Nicomedia and said that he had come at the imperial command to undo the Christians' spells the faster, and that no injustice would be found in him before the emperor: let the Christian work some power before me, he said, and I will scatter all his workings. He asked for a wild ox to be brought, whispered certain words into its ears, and at once the ox split in two, and each half stood apart from the other.

He then told the bishop [Theopemptus](/saints/theopemptus-of-nicomedia) that he would work two arts of sorcery upon him, and that if they did him no harm he too would believe in his God. When they did not, he said he would work one more, a wonderful one; and when that failed as well he fell at the bishop's feet and said: I am a Christian, and I worship him who was crucified. They were sent together to prison, and there the bishop taught him the faith and named him Synesius, which is to say full of understanding, because he had understood with a pure heart and known Christ as God; and he baptised him there.

The emperor had Theonas brought before him afterwards, and when neither promises nor threats could bend him to the idol sacrifices, ordered him thrown into a deep pit, covered over with earth, and horses brought to trample him in it. So, stopped up with earth and much trodden by the horses' feet, Theonas passed to the Lord. He and the bishop, the page says, finished their martyr's contest under the emperor Diocletian at Nicomedia.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same fifth of January, under one notice with Theopemptus. saint.gr says he was the pagan magician who prepared the deadly poisons the bishop was made to drink, and that it was the bravery, the unshaken faith and the moral brightness in the martyr's face that persuaded him: while [Theopemptus](/saints/theopemptus-of-nicomedia)'s head, washed in blood, was still lying on the ground, Theonas confessed Christ himself. The idolaters, maddened by a confession they had not looked for, buried him alive in the earth; and so, the page says, Theonas found a glorious death, and his soul went up with the martyr's to the God who sets the contest and gives the crown. The page's own verse leaves him in a pit at the very bottom, crying out the words of the psalm.
