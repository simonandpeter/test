# Eugene (Vasilyev)

days.pravoslavie.ru's calendar closes its 11 ноября ст. ст. with one line of 1937, «Сщмч. Евгения пресвитера (1937).» — the forename, the grade and the year, and nothing else. The name link behind it gives the calendar's own nominative with the surname the line leaves out, «Евгений (Васильев), иерей, сщмч.», and that is where the name form in this folder is read from. **That name page carries a title, two days and no text**, and no `/Life/` link of any kind, so the life below is azbyka.ru's, read in the in-app browser.

He was born on 20 December 1892 in Moscow, the son of a priest. Having had his first theological schooling he entered the Moscow Theological Academy, but the godless seizure of power left him no time to finish it, and he went on studying privately, sitting his examinations with the academy's own professors. He was ordained priest and after some time appointed rector of the church of the Dormition in the village of Kosino in the Ukhtomsky district of the Moscow region, and assistant to the dean.

**What the file against him was built out of was a lake.** In 1936 reports began reaching the secret section of the NKVD that the number of pilgrims to the Dormition church and to the Святое озеро, the Holy Lake beside it, was growing. Having had a moleben sung in the church, the pilgrims went down to the lake, took water from it, and some of them bathed; there were frequent cases of healing, known both to the faithful and to the priest. Supposing that all this was happening because of the rector's activity, the authorities decided to arrest him.

He was arrested on 31 March 1936 and shut in the Butyrka prison in Moscow. Told that the investigation had evidence that he had spread false rumours of supposed miraculous healings at the Holy Lake for counter-revolutionary ends, he denied nothing of the facts and nothing of the faith in them: «Я не отрицаю, что ко мне в церковь в село Косино приезжали верующие из различных мест, служили в церкви молебны, после этого из церкви уходили на Святое озеро за водой, купались в нем с целью получить исцеление от болезней», and in the summer there were days when ten or fifteen came.

The witnesses questioned said the same thing from the other side: that they had met at the services many believers from different districts of the Moscow region who had come specially to this priest to have a moleben sung and to draw water from the Holy Lake, water which has a healing power and helps against every sickness, and who went home telling that they had been healed of illnesses they had suffered from for years. One of them reported a conversation at length, and what it reports the priest saying is the fullest thing on the page.

Asked why he told believers to drink dirty lake water, he had answered that the lake had been greatly honoured before, that people came to it from every quarter and there were many cases of wonderful healing, that he knew several people himself who had been healed after bathing in it, and that the authorities now persecuted it so hard that everything had to be done in secret; before the revolution several thousand pilgrims had come, many of them sick, and many went away whole. Here, he said, is your proof of how firmly the people still believe in God — many of them have been round all the hospitals and have come here.

The same witness put a second conversation on the record, from February 1936: that the communists and the young people had resolved to close the church and fill it with grain, «но я сумел организовать верующих, и мы снова отстояли наш храм» — but I managed to organise the faithful and we defended our church again. It was not for long, he added, but perhaps God would have mercy on them.

That was enough. On 2 July 1936 the Special Conference of the NKVD of the USSR sentenced him to three years in a corrective labour camp. He was sent first to the Mariinsk camp in the Novosibirsk region and then to Khabarovsk, **and he died there in the camp on 24 November 1937** and was buried in an unmarked grave. He was not shot: the page says «скончался», and the sentence he was serving was three years and not death. 11 ноября ст. ст. is that civil day, so the calendar keeps him on the day he died.

**He is not `eugene-martyr-1937`**, the other Eugene of that year this corpus keeps. That one is name page «Евгений Дмитрев», a psalmist born in 1913 and kept on the Julian 18 August; this one is «Евгений (Васильев)», a priest born in 1892 and kept on 11 ноября. Forename, year of birth, surname, grade and day all differ, and nothing read links them.
