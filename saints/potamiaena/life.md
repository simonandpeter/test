# Potamiaena of Alexandria

saint.gr says she lived at the end of the third century in Alexandria, in the years of the emperor Maximian. She was a slave, but a Christian — with a soul free through Jesus Christ, as the page puts it. Another man bought her from her former master, an idolater like him, and because Potamiaena was very beautiful in body he reckoned it his right to have the enjoyment of her.

Her stubborn resistance astonished and enraged him, for it was the first time he had been pushed off by a slave, and he demanded her reason. She did not hide it, and the page gives her answer: that she was a Christian and set her honour far above her life; that her only property was her faith and her purity, and she would deny neither; that he might kill her and she would not protest, but that he must not touch her, or she would kill herself — for their holy religion forbids suicide in every circumstance and permits it only when death is the last means left of protecting honour against brutal violence.

Her idolatrous master handed her over to the eparch at once. He gave the order and they put her to death in a cauldron of boiling pitch, and so she gave up her pure and clean soul to God. The couplet the page prints says that no praise will be enough for Potamiaena, even if it imitate the flowing of the river her name is made from.
