# Barbarus (6 May)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 6 mai reads «Sfântul Mucenic Varvar» — Barbarus the martyr — a man's name and a man's rank, not the great martyr Barbara whom the same calendar keeps in December; and his page opens no Viață tab, carrying only the day and the readings appointed for a martyr.

No century, no country, no manner of death and no hymn is printed there, and none is set down here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and adds the one thing the Romanian line did not have: how he died. «Ο Άγιος Βάρβαρος μαρτύρησε δια ξίφους», saint.gr says — the holy Barbarus was martyred by the sword. That is the whole of its prose. Over it stands a couplet which turns on his name: you are Barbarus to us in name, O noble one, but he who cut you down was the barbarian, and barbarian in his heart. Beyond the name, the rank, the sword and the day the page gives nothing — no country, no century, no emperor, no place, no relics and no hymn.

The Serbian church keeps him on this same menologion day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the man behind the bare Romanian and Greek lines: a soldier in the time of Julian the Apostate and a secret Christian. When the emperor's commander Bacchus led the Roman army against the Franks, Barbarus was in that army. On the field a champion came out from the Frankish side, like the Goliath of old, and taunted the Romans to send a man out against him; and the commander advised Barbarus to go. He prayed in his heart to the living Lord, went out, and overcame the giant, and at that the Frankish army was thrown into confusion and fled.

The commander then made a great feast and ordered sacrifices offered to the idols, and at the sacrifice he learned that Barbarus was holding himself apart. Asked about it, Barbarus declared that he was a Christian. The commander reported it to the emperor and the emperor ordered him put to the heaviest torments, which he bore with rare courage and presence of mind. Many wonders were shown at his torturing, and many soldiers who saw them took the faith of Christ — the commander Bacchus himself among them, with Callimachus and Dionysius. All three of them were beheaded for the name of Christ, and Barbarus after them, **in the year 362**; and their souls, the Prologue ends, passed over into the kingdom of Christ the immortal King.

**That year is the first date this folder has carried.** Neither of the other two calendars prints a century, a country or an emperor for him: doxologia names him and gives no life at all, and the Greek page adds only that he was martyred by the sword — which this account agrees with, since he was beheaded. Nothing narrower and nothing wider was taken; 362 is what the book prints.

**The three beheaded before him take no folder here, and the refusal is deliberate.** The heading names Barbarus alone and the three come in the body, and this corpus holds no folder for any of them: the two Bacchi it keeps are the companion of Sergius and a monk of Saint Sabbas's lavra, there is no folder of Callimachus at all, and none of its thirty-three Dionysii is a soldier of this company. The body of an entry does not mint folders, so their names stand in the account above with no rows behind them, as the source gives them, and the question goes to the author instead.
