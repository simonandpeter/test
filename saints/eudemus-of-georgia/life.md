# Eudemus of Georgia

Eudemus was Catholicos-Patriarch of the Georgian Orthodox Church in the first half of the seventeenth century, under King Rostom Khan (1632–1658), the Romanian synaxarion says. Rostom had become a Muslim and came to the throne with the help of Shah Abbas I of Persia; he tried to come to terms with the Georgians but served above all the Persian ruler who had put him there, and under him his people declined fast in body and soul, the passions became habits, sins like those of Sodom and Gomorrah multiplied, and even the priests had ceased to please the Lord. Eudemus stood against the debasement of his flock and faced the Persian power without fear. Several times he urged on and supported the strongest Georgian commanders in revolt against Persia, and following his example some of the Georgian princes rose against Rostom and kept their people from Muslim influence. He opposed the Muslim custom that the king’s heirs be brought up at the Shah’s court, and never missed a chance to tell the king that he was the father of the Muslims but the stepfather of the Christians. He was also the spiritual father of Rostom’s wife, the faithful Queen Mariam, daughter of Manuchar Dadiani, prince of Samegrelo, and their work together, the life says, kept the fire of grace in the Georgian people from going out: churches were built, spiritual books were written, and little by little the nation’s conscience woke again.

When he began to preach for the return of King Teimuraz, whom Shah Abbas had driven out, Rostom, feeling threatened by the great hierarch’s hold on the people, imprisoned him in 1642. He tried to buy his submission, but neither false kindness nor threats could break him, and from prison Eudemus went on rebuking the king and calling everyone to rise against the tyrant. At last Rostom had him strangled and his body thrown from the fortress of Nariqala in Tbilisi towards the Turkish baths, and that same night some Christians found it and buried it with honour in the Anchiskhati church in Tbilisi.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 4 October. Its life is taken from the Great Synaxarion for October published by Doxologia, and gives no year for his death, which fell between his imprisonment in 1642 and the end of Rostom’s reign in 1658.
