# Liberius

Liberius succeeded Pope Julius on the episcopal throne of Rome. Like him he was an advocate of Athanasius the Great in the struggle against Arianism and for the Orthodox creed, and he protected Paul I, archbishop of Constantinople, whom the emperor Constantius persecuted for his Orthodoxy.

When he came to Constantinople and differed on church matters with the emperor, the patron of Arianism, Constantius exiled him — to Thessalonica, the synaxarion says in one place, to Thrace in 354 in the note it quotes from Eustratiades, who counts him among the Orthodox popes of Rome who fought Arianism. He returned to his throne in 358 at the demand of the Christians of Rome, and died in peace in 365, or by another count 366.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 27 August (Julian): he came to the throne in 352 after the death of Pope Julius, a zealous champion of Orthodoxy against the Arians and defender of Athanasius of Alexandria; the emperor Constantius, leaning to the Arians, could not persuade him to condemn Athanasius, and so Orthodoxy, and for this stubbornness exiled him to Beroea in Thrace, but soon brought him back at the Romans’ unceasing pleas. Before his return he was summoned to the third, semi-Arian council of Sirmium, where he was forced to sign its decree; afterwards he repented deeply of it and laboured much at Rome to confirm Orthodoxy, and died in peace in 366.
