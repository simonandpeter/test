# Nicodemus of Tismana

Our venerable father Nicodemus the hallowed was of Macedo-Romanian stock, born of believing parents at Prilep in the south of Serbia in the year 1320, and kin to the family of the despot Lazăr and to Nicolae Alexandru Basarab, prince of Wallachia. After learning his letters in his own country he was called by Christ to the labour of the monastic life in the monastery of Hilandar on Mount Athos, where he received the angelic habit, and became in time abbot of that lavra and even proto-epistate in the government of the Holy Mountain.

Coming to Athos, the mountain of the Mother of God, he struggled first in the community and then alone in a cave near Hilandar, enduring hard temptations from the devil; but overshadowed by the grace of Christ he was cleansed of the passions in a little while, learned the divine work of holy prayer, and was counted worthy of the gift of foresight and of the working of wonders, so that he became renowned throughout the Mountain.

As abbot of Hilandar he gathered into his community as many as a hundred Athonite monks — Greeks, Serbs, Macedonians, Romanians and Bulgarians — teaching them all the fear of God and feeding them with the teachings of holy Scripture; for he was an able master of the prayer of Jesus, a deep theologian and a spiritual father to many, so that not a few hermits, monks of coenobia and abbots came to him for counsel and a word of profit.

Because of the honour he enjoyed everywhere, at the request of the prince [Lazar](/saints/lazar-of-serbia) he went to Constantinople with his disciples Isaia and Partenie to mediate the reconciliation of the Serbian Orthodox Church with the Ecumenical Patriarchate; and the patriarch and the emperor, seeing the humility and the wisdom of the venerable one and won over by the holiness of his life, lifted at once the anathema laid upon the Serbian Church. That was how he understood the fulfilling of the Gospel and the helping of his fellow men to salvation.

After a divine revelation Nicodemus came from Mount Athos with several disciples to the south of the Danube, near Vidin, where he founded two small monastic settlements, Vratna and Mănăstirița. In 1364 he crossed into Wallachia and settled in the valley of the river Vodița, where there was a small hermitage founded by Vlach monks; and there, with the help of the princes Vlaicu Vodă (1364-1377) and Radu (1377-1384) and of the hermits of the place, he built cells and a stone church, which he consecrated in 1369.

The monastery of Vodița was then endowed with gifts and confirmed by a princely charter which laid down that «after the death of kyr Nicodim neither the prince nor the bishop nor anyone else shall be free to set an abbot in that place; but as kyr Nicodim shall say and shall order, so shall the monks who are there keep it, and they themselves shall set their abbot». In the valley of the Tismana stream, where several hermits had been labouring since the beginning of the fourteenth century round a small wooden church, he raised as well, at the place called «Cascade», the princely monastery of Tismana, with the help of the princes of Wallachia Radu I and Dan I, between 1377 and 1378, and formed there a renowned community of dozens of monks.

Having received from God the gift of working wonders and power over unclean spirits, he did many and numberless wonders while he lived, driving devils out of men and healing every sickness and every infirmity. It is said that he went into fire and came out unharmed, so that the fire touched neither his clothes nor the hair of his head. His name was known beyond the borders of Wallachia for the holiness of his life and for the gift of healing every kind of illness; the monastery's tradition says that some of the sick were healed as soon as they reached Tismana, others by the venerable one's prayer and blessing, or merely by touching his cassock, and that among those he healed was the daughter of King Sigismund, who suffered from epilepsy.

In his old age he entrusted the care of the two monasteries, Vodița and Tismana, to his disciple the hieromonk Agaton, and withdrew to a harsher labour in the cave above the monastery, which is kept to this day. There he laboured the whole week in fasting, in vigil all night and in unceasing prayer, and only on Sundays and feasts came down from the cave into the monastery of Tismana and celebrated the holy Liturgy; then he healed the sick who came to him, ate at table with the fathers, counselled and comforted everyone with useful words, and went up again to the cave.

At the end of the fourteenth century he founded, with a few disciples, the monastery of Vișina in the valley of the Jiu, in the place of an older hermitage; and in 1400 the monastery of Prislop, called also Silvașul de Sus, in the land of Hunedoara, helped by the prince Mircea cel Bătrân. In that monastery he laboured some years, and there he wrote with his own hand a Slavonic Gospel book (1404-1405), which is kept to this day. Coming to a deep old age in holiness, he passed from this temporary life to the heavenly and imperishable one on the twenty-sixth day of December in the year of salvation 1406, wept for by all his disciples.

His holy relics were buried with much weeping in the church of the monastery of Tismana, in the grave prepared beforehand, where his commemoration is kept every year; and after God had glorified them with a divine fragrance and with a gift of flowing myrrh and of wonders, they were taken up and laid in a shrine, set with honour in the church he had built. Long afterwards a prince of Wallachia wished to take the relics from Tismana to the city of Bucharest; but it was not the saint's will that they should be estranged from his own dwelling, and he worked a wonder, so that the prince left off the thing that displeased him.

After that the saint appeared in a vision to one of the monks, ordering him to tell the abbot to hide the relics and to take only one finger from the hand, for the devotion of the inhabitants; and he appeared in the same way to the abbot, ordering him to do as he had told the brother. Being assured of it, the abbot took a finger from the saint's hand and myrrh from his relics, and they were put in a pewter vessel together with the great lead cross the saint wore at his neck; and they are in the holy monastery of Tismana to this day as honoured and precious spiritual ornaments. But nobody is allowed to take even a little of the holy myrrh, only to kiss the vessel and be filled with the spiritual fragrance.

The relics being hidden in this way, the secret was long known only to the abbot and to one of the brethren; later, through many troubles and captivities, they remained wholly hidden and unknown to anyone, as they are to this day. Perhaps that was the saint's will; yet the holy treasures named above are there for the comfort of the fathers of the monastery and of the other Christian inhabitants, and by them many and numberless wonders are still worked, unclean spirits being driven out of men at the calling of the saint's name, and healings of many kinds given to those who run with faith to his help.
