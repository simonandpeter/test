# Claudius, husband of Hilaria

Claudius was a tribune at Rome, and the prefect handed [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) over to him to be forced to sacrifice. His soldiers bound the prisoner with raw ox-sinews until they cut to the bone, and the bonds untied themselves faster than a word could be spoken; the shackles and the stocks fell to dust, the filth they poured over him turned to a good smell, the wet hide they wrapped him in did not dry in a day of fierce sun, the iron chains shattered, and the dark cell filled with light as though many candles were burning.

When the soldiers reported it, Claudius went to the prison, saw the light, had Chrysanthus brought to him and asked what power of sorcery he had — he had tamed many magicians, he said, and never found such strength. He asked him only to leave off the bold Christian teaching that was raising such tumult among the people. Chrysanthus answered that if there were a spark of wisdom in him he would see that it was not sorcery but the power of God, and that his gods neither see nor hear, and have no heart in them but dust and earth and lead.

Claudius had him tied to a stake and beaten with staves, and the heavy staves went soft as rushes in the beaters' hands. Then he stopped the beating, untied him, put his own cloak on him, and turned to his soldiers: he had examined every deceit of magic, he said, and this was God's own power, and what was left but to fall at the feet of this man of God and ask pardon for what they had done to him? He fell down with all his soldiers, and said that they had come to know that his God was the true God.

Chrysanthus told them to come to God not with their feet but with their hearts, and taught them for some time; and Claudius believed and was baptised, with his wife [Hilaria](/saints/hilaria-wife-of-claudius), his two sons Jason and Maurus, his whole household, and his soldiers with their families. They stayed under his teaching day and night, and all of them wished to suffer for Christ.

When the emperor Numerian heard that a tribune had been baptised with his house and his men, he ordered Claudius drowned in the sea with a stone tied about his neck, and his two sons and all his soldiers put to the sword. Where the martyrs were beheaded there was an old tomb, which the Christians cleaned out, and they took the bodies by night and laid them in it; and there Hilaria used to come and pray, until she was taken at that same place and died in prayer before they could torture her.

The Greek calendar keeps the household on the same day and tells the end more fully than doxologia does. saint.gr says Claudius held the office of tribune in the years of Numerian (283–284), and that the eparch Celarinus handed [Chrysanthus](/saints/chrysanthus) and [Daria](/saints/daria) over to him to be punished; when he saw the holy pair come unharmed out of the tortures he believed in Christ and was baptised together with his wife [Hilaria](/saints/hilaria-wife-of-claudius) and his sons [Jason](/saints/jason-son-of-claudius) and [Maurus](/saints/maurus-son-of-claudius), and the soldiers of his guard did the same. Then a great block of stone was bound to his body and he was thrown into the sea, and so he found a glorious martyr's end; his sons were beheaded together with their soldiers. The couplet over him turns on it: Claudius, having fled the sea of error, is cast into the sea by men in error.
