# Cyril

Cyril, bishop of Gortyna, lived under Diocletian and his co-emperor Maximian. As a Christian he stood trial before the governor Agrippinus and after questioning was thrown into prison; in the night he heard a voice bidding him go to Rome, and in the morning the prison doors stood open and the idols lay overthrown and broken. On the road to Rome he had a vision: the venerable Philoxenus appeared and foretold him two crowns, a bishop’s and a martyr’s.

At Rome he gave the Church great help by his preaching, and when persecution broke out set off for Jerusalem to keep up the spirits of the Christians there; on the way a vision bade him not to pass Crete by, and arriving there he was chosen bishop of Gortyna, being then sixty. He held the see until he was ninety-five, and in the new persecution was tried, condemned and beheaded at the place called Raxa at the beginning of the fourth century. The Russian calendar keeps him on 6 September and 9 July (Julian), with a troparion — led out of prison by a divine voice, it begins — and a kontakion.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 9 iulie and prints a life of its own there, which tells the story differently. It puts him under Decius and the governor Lucius; it says he was set as bishop of Gortyna in Crete when he was grown and governed that see fifty years, and that he was taken at eighty-four. Told to have pity on his old age and worship the idols, he answered that he was not old, for his Lord says that a man's youth shall be renewed like the eagle's. He was once drawn out of a fire by the governor's own order, and when he went on strengthening the faithful he was condemned a second time and beheaded on the ninth day of July, Decius ruling and Lucius governing in Gortyna.

The Greek calendar keeps him not on 9 June or 9 July but on 14 June, and saint.gr's account puts an arithmetic to his life that the folder's other sources do not: it brackets him «(206 - 299)», says that after passing his life in holiness and asceticism he was ordained bishop of Gortyna in Crete in the sixty-eighth year of his life, and that after governing his flock twenty-five years he was brought before the governor Agrianos in the year 299, in the days of the emperors Diocletian and Maximian. Because he proclaimed Christ boldly he was thrown into the fire; but although his bonds burned and all the wood of the fire turned to ash, the saint remained entirely unharmed, and so he was set free. Later, however, because he was converting many idolaters to the faith of Christ, he was condemned to death. They put a bridle in his mouth and loaded him onto a cart, because he was ninety-three years old and could not walk; and when they reached a place called Raxos the oxen stopped of their own accord, and there the worthy hierarch and contestant of the Lord received the crown of martyrdom by beheading. The page then notes that according to Dositheos of Jerusalem, at page 979 of the Dodekabiblos, there is another Cyril, hieromartyr of Gortyna, who was martyred by the Saracens in 780 because he would not deny Christ, and from whose wounds myrrh ran instead of blood.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 9 July, the same menologion day the Romanian calendar keeps him on, where the Russian calendar keeps him on 6 September and the Greek on 14 June. Its entry is four sentences and it puts him in another century altogether. He was tortured for Christ as an old man of eighty-four, it says, in the time of the reign of Decius — where the Russian page has him beheaded at the beginning of the fourth century, aged ninety-five. Decius reigned fifty years before that century began. The two books cannot be reconciled and neither is chosen: the folder’s recorded interval is left exactly as it stood, no reign is read as a year, and the two ages are ages and not dates.

Cast into fire, this book says, he was saved by the providence of God. Then the judge passed this sentence: Cyril, brought out of the fire, the just court cannot suffer among the living; therefore I order that he be killed with the sword. With joy the holy old man laid his head under the sword and was beheaded, that he might live for ever in the kingdom of Christ.
