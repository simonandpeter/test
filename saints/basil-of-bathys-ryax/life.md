# Basil of Bathys Ryax

He came from Cappadocia, saint.gr says, and lived in the years of the emperor Constantine Porphyrogennetos, son of Leo the Philosopher and of Zoe, whose reign the page dates 901 to 959. He built the monastery of Bathys Ryax at Triglia, a small town in Bithynia, and the page marks him out not only for piety but for learning and for a zeal to serve the faith and the Church.

So in the house he founded he was also a teacher of the devout young men who came to it, and of those, the page says, many went on to distinguish themselves in teaching the people and in the excellent government of other monasteries. The distich set over him turns on his name: «Ώφθης αληθώς Bασίλειος πράγματι, / Ως βασιλεύσας των παθών των αλόγων» — you were seen to be truly a Basil in fact, as one who reigned over the irrational passions. Nothing else of him is on the page, and his own monastery's later abbots the Greek calendar keeps on their own days.
