# Irene, wife of Leo IV

The Romanian calendar keeps on 7 august the empress it calls Irina, Împărăteasa icoanelor — Irene, Empress of the Icons. She was the wife of the Byzantine emperor Leo IV the Khazar, whom her page names as one of the fiercest persecutors of the veneration of the holy icons. At his death she became regent in the name of her son, the future emperor Constantine VI, and one of the first decisions of the new empress was to bring the icons back into the churches, into the houses and into the life of Christians.

Her great merit, the page says, was the convoking of the Seventh Ecumenical Council, which met at Nicaea in Asia Minor in 787, where three hundred and fifty Orthodox bishops from the whole Christian world gathered. In that official setting, with the empress's guarantee, the war on the icons was condemned as heresy and the veneration of the holy icons restored: the council kept unchanged all the traditions of the Church, written and unwritten, and among them the painting of icons, in accord with the preaching of the Gospel, for the strengthening of faith in the true and not imagined incarnation of God the Word.

On the ground of that history, the page concludes, the Church appointed her honour as a saint and entered her in its synaxarion. In a world governed by men she had the courage to put the icons back in the churches, and across the centuries she has kept the name the Holy Irene, Empress of the Icons. Her page prints no year of her birth or of her death, and none is set down here.
