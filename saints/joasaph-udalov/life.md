# Joasaph (Udalov)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar names him second of twenty-four and gives no life; the life here is the one azbyka.ru prints for the civil 2 декабря, out of pstgu.ru. Ivan Ivanovich Udalov was born at Ufa on 5 April 1886 into a merchant family. He finished the Ufa ecclesiastical school in 1900, the Ufa seminary in 1906 and the Kazan ecclesiastical academy in 1910 with the degree of candidate of theology. His spiritual father at the seminary was the schema-archimandrite Gabriel (Zyryanov), and the archbishop Anthony (Khrapovitsky), who tonsured him, weighed with him greatly.

He was tonsured in 1910 and ordained hierodeacon and then hieromonk, taught at the Zhitomir school of pastorship, and from October 1911 was assistant inspector of the Kazan academy. From 1912 he acted as superior of the Kazan Transfiguration missionary monastery with the rank of abbot and chaired the council of the missionary courses; from 1915 he was an archimandrite. Through the civil war he sat on the diocesan council under the bishop Anatolius (Grisyuk) and was his closest helper. On 12 July 1920 he was made bishop of Mamadysh, and after the arrest of the metropolitan Cyril (Smirnov) he administered the Kazan diocese for a time.

From 1922 he was bishop of Chistopol, and in that year came out against the Renovationist movement and had for a while to serve in secret. In 1923 he received back into the Patriarchal church Renovationists who repented and reconsecrated the churches that had been theirs, but he could not get the Orthodox diocesan administration registered, and in November 1923 the authorities forbade him to serve and held him briefly. Called to Moscow in May 1924 he was arrested again and put in the Butyrka prison, released in August on an undertaking not to leave, and lived at the Danilov monastery.

In November 1925 he was arrested with other bishops who held to the patriarchal locum tenens Peter (Polyansky), charged with harbouring a group of monarchist bishops and laymen, and denied the charge. Three years of exile followed: Turukhansk from 1926, then Kozmodemyansk. He took the 1927 declaration of the metropolitan Sergius badly. Arrested in 1931 in the case of the so-called True Orthodox Church, he answered that two years under watch at Kozmodemyansk had not once left him suspected of anything, and that the charge named no single fact about him. Three years of imprisonment followed, served in a camp near Kemerovo, wheeling barrows of coal in the pits; arrested again in the camp in 1933, he had two years added.

He was released in 1936 and lived at Kazan, serving molebens and panikhidas in the cemetery church and not recognising the authority of the metropolitan Sergius. He lived very poorly, and the food and clothing the faithful brought him he sent on to priests in prison and in exile. Asked by an archpriest what he thought of the Soviet power he said: «Чтобы судить о советской власти, нужно побывать в концлагерях». He was arrested in November 1937, charged with organising a counter-revolutionary church underground, denied it again, was sentenced on 29 November and shot on 2 December. The Holy Synod added his name to the synaxis of the new martyrs on 23 June 2008.
