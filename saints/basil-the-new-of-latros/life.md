# Basil the New of Latros

saint.gr's calendar for 26 Μαρτίου names the venerable Basil the New, and the page behind the line is three sentences long. He was the brother of the venerable [Paul of Latros](/saints/paul-of-latros), whom it calls «ὁ ἐν τῷ Λάτρῳ» and whose memory it puts on 15 December; he practised ascesis on Mount Latros, the mountain in Caria where his brother's monastery stood; and he fell asleep in peace in the year 944, or else in 952, the page giving both years and choosing neither.

That is the whole of the notice. The page records no birthplace, no parents beyond the brother, no teacher, no office, no wonder, no relic and no hymn, and it does not say which of the two brothers came to the mountain first. What it prints around the three sentences is a note about the name — «ΒΑΣΙΛΕΙΟΣ (από το βασιλεύς) = ο βασιλικός», the kingly, the founder of his people's happiness — and a list of the emperors and patriarchs who bore it, ending with Basil II the Bulgar-slayer. None of that is about him.

One caution belongs on the folder. The Greek calendar keeps another and far better known Basil the New, the ascetic of Constantinople in whose life the vision of Theodora is told; this page's Basil is identified only by Mount Latros and by his brother Paul, and nothing here is taken from that other man's life.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 26 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/26/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3864/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
