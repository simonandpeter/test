# Alexandra of Diveevo

Alexandra was born in Russia at the beginning of the eighteenth century, her name in the world Agatha Semionovna Melgunova. She lived as a landowner of some wealth, with estates in the districts of Yaroslavl, Vladimir and Ryazan. Widowed young, she resolved to enter the Florovsky convent in Kiev, and was tonsured there about the year 1760 under the name Alexandra, living an ascetic life. She was not to stay long in the house of her tonsure: the Mother of God appeared to her in a vision and told her that she was chosen to found a new monastery.

She went on many pilgrimages, and under that guidance found the place appointed for it, the village of Diveevo in the province of Nizhny Novgorod. Not far off the men's monastery of Sarov was then flourishing, with many hermits living under a severe rule, and it was under their direction that Mother Alexandra gathered a small community of disciples beside the church built in honour of the Kazan icon of the Mother of God. Hospitality to pilgrims, the work of the hands, voluntary poverty and the unceasing prayer of Jesus were what she gave that community.

She gave away her whole fortune, half to the building and repair of churches and half to orphans, widows and the poor, and kept herself by her own labour, cleaning the cattle sheds and washing clothes and turning from no work however heavy. Her humility and the good she did in secret told on the peasants of Diveevo, and people of every sort, simple and highly placed, came to her for counsel and blessing. Two weeks before her death she was tonsured into the great schema, and in that same time she foretold the building of a great monastery there and the troubles that would attend it. She died on 13 June 1789.

[Seraphim of Sarov](/saints/seraphim-of-sarov) spoke of her with deep reverence while she was still alive — her clothes much patched, her shawl old, her eyes always wet — and said that to that day he kissed the prints of her feet. After her death he testified that she stood beside the throne of God. Doxologia's page records healings at her relics at Diveevo and adds that a portion of them is kept in the shrine of the great church of Frumoasa monastery in Iași.
