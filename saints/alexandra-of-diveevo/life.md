# Alexandra of Diveevo

Alexandra was born in Russia at the beginning of the eighteenth century, her name in the world Agatha Semionovna Melgunova. She lived as a landowner of some wealth, with estates in the districts of Yaroslavl, Vladimir and Ryazan. Widowed young, she resolved to enter the Florovsky convent in Kiev, and was tonsured there about the year 1760 under the name Alexandra, living an ascetic life. She was not to stay long in the house of her tonsure: the Mother of God appeared to her in a vision and told her that she was chosen to found a new monastery.

She went on many pilgrimages, and under that guidance found the place appointed for it, the village of Diveevo in the province of Nizhny Novgorod. Not far off the men's monastery of Sarov was then flourishing, with many hermits living under a severe rule, and it was under their direction that Mother Alexandra gathered a small community of disciples beside the church built in honour of the Kazan icon of the Mother of God. Hospitality to pilgrims, the work of the hands, voluntary poverty and the unceasing prayer of Jesus were what she gave that community.

She gave away her whole fortune, half to the building and repair of churches and half to orphans, widows and the poor, and kept herself by her own labour, cleaning the cattle sheds and washing clothes and turning from no work however heavy. Her humility and the good she did in secret told on the peasants of Diveevo, and people of every sort, simple and highly placed, came to her for counsel and blessing. Two weeks before her death she was tonsured into the great schema, and in that same time she foretold the building of a great monastery there and the troubles that would attend it. She died on 13 June 1789.

[Seraphim of Sarov](/saints/seraphim-of-sarov) spoke of her with deep reverence while she was still alive — her clothes much patched, her shawl old, her eyes always wet — and said that to that day he kissed the prints of her feet. After her death he testified that she stood beside the throne of God. Doxologia's page records healings at her relics at Diveevo and adds that a portion of them is kept in the shrine of the great church of Frumoasa monastery in Iași.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 13 June, and saint.gr's account begins with her name in the world. Agatha Semyonovna Melgunova was very rich and held many estates at Yaroslavl, at Vladimir and at Ryazan; she was widowed young, and in 1760 became a nun at the Florovsky monastery of Kiev with the name Alexandra. It was not written that she should stay there long, for she saw the Mother of God in a vision, who told her to build a new monastery. Under that guidance, and after dozens of pilgrimages, she came to the place the Mother of God had chosen: it was at Diveevo, not far from Sarov, where at that time there was a men's monastery famous for its great spiritual stature. Under the guidance of the hermits of Sarov she began her life of stillness in a cell beside a church dedicated to the icon of the Mother of God of Kazan. She gave away her whole fortune for churches to be built and renovated and for widows, orphans and the poor to be helped, while she herself lived by the sweat of her own face, cleaning stables and washing clothes; and the people of Sarov remembered for many years her humility and the help she gave. From the very beginning people of every social rank came to her for counsel and for her blessing. She fell asleep on 13 June 1789, having taken the great angelic habit two weeks before, and having said that a great monastery would be built there — and having foretold also the troubles that would take place there. [Seraphim of Sarov](/saints/seraphim-of-sarov), whom the page sends the reader to find on 2 Ιανουαρίου, held her in deep esteem while she still lived, and after her death said that she stood near the throne of God, and told a sister to teach the rest to pray to her thus: «Μητέρα και Κυρία μου προσευχήσου για μένα στον Κύριο να με συγχωρήσει, όπως Εκείνος συγχώρεσε εσένα και να με θυμηθείς και εμένα μπροστά στο θρόνο του Θεού» — my mother and my lady, pray to the Lord for me that he forgive me as he forgave you, and remember me too before the throne of God. The monastery, the page ends, holds many written testimonies of the wonders she worked, and her relics are kept there.

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery keeps her on 13 июня ст. ст., the same menologion day, in a single line — «Прп. Александры Дивеевской (1789).» — with the same year of her falling asleep. The name link behind that line gives the calendar's own fuller form, «Александра (Мельгунова) Дивеевская, прп.», which adds her family name. That calendar prints no life page for her and no hymn under her name.
