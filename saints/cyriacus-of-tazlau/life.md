# Cyriacus of Tazlău

The best-known hermit who laboured near Tazlău monastery, one of the great
saints of the Romanian people who has stayed in the people’s memory, is
Cyriacus — Chiriac — who toiled in the cave on Mount Măgura; by a chosen
life given wholly to God in ascesis and unceasing prayer he showed himself a
chosen vessel of the Holy Spirit, among the great ascetics of
seventeenth-century Moldavia. He was born at the beginning of that century
into a believing family of the village of Mesteacăn; taken as a small child
by his parents to the services at Tazlău he felt his soul winged with divine
longing, and with their blessing entered the community in early youth,
learned the monastic life and took the angelic habit, a chosen worker of
humility in obedience, unceasing prayer and long fasting. After a little he
was ordained deacon and priest against his will and became a skilled guide
of souls; longing for a harsher struggle he withdrew to a cave on Măgura
Tazlăului, the mountain near the monastery, where he laboured in all-night
vigils, bearing and conquering the cruel cold and the devils’ temptations,
and, gaining the gifts of pure prayer, of healing sickness and of driving
out demons, was sought by crowds of monks and faithful. In that time of
intense Calvinist propaganda from Transylvania he was a true defender and
confessor of the right faith, keeping the flame of Orthodoxy alight in all
the Neamț country, a follower of the fathers and a zealot for their example.
He went round the monasteries of Moldavia gathering counsel like a bee and
learning from each father the work pleasing to God, unceasing prayer; he
ate once a day after sunset and passed the night in vigil, dozing two or
three hours in a chair, and calling without rest on the name of the sweetest
Jesus until every passion of the flesh was quenched he was counted worthy
of the sight of the divine light and blessed with the gift of discerning
thoughts, so that monks and lay people sought him and marvelled at his
gentleness and wisdom, and he set many on the right road and worked wonders
in his lifetime. Love and spiritual zeal, the hermit’s labour, humility,
perfect obedience, silence, long fasting and unceasing prayer — those, the
life says, were the chosen deeds of his life.

He went to the Lord about 1660 and was buried in the porch of the monastery
church. The people held him a saint in his lifetime, and so did Dositheus,
metropolitan of Moldavia, who knew him and afterwards kissed his relics —
we have caught in our days fathers high in struggle and bowed in deep
humility, he wrote, Father Chiriac of Bisericani and Chiriac of Tazlău —
and the Paterikon of the Moldo-Romanian saints of 1888 says he fought hard
against the unseen enemies and wholly conquered them, and gives his feast
as 31 December. When his body was taken up after his death, by the old
custom, it was found incorrupt, and out of devotion was divided, part
remaining in his grave, where monks and faithful from everywhere still
come to venerate him. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 9 September with a
troparion: kindled with the love of Christ, you lived as a hermit in the
cave of Măgura Tazlăului, glorifying God and conquering the devils.
