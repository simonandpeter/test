# Cyriacus of Tazlău

The best-known hermit who laboured near Tazlău monastery, one of the great saints of the Romanian people who has stayed in the people’s memory, is Cyriacus — Chiriac — who toiled in the cave on Mount Măgura; by a chosen life given wholly to God in ascesis and unceasing prayer he showed himself a chosen vessel of the Holy Spirit, among the great ascetics of seventeenth-century Moldavia. He was born at the beginning of the seventeenth century into a believing family of the village of Mesteacăn; taken as a small child by his parents to the services at Tazlău he felt his soul winged with divine longing, and with their blessing entered the community in early youth, learned the monastic life and received the angelic habit, showing much love and spiritual zeal, a chosen worker of humility in obedience, unceasing prayer and long fasting; after a little he was ordained deacon and priest against his will and became a skilled guide of souls. Longing for a harsher struggle he withdrew to a cave on a mountain near the monastery, Măgura Tazlăului, where he laboured in all-night vigils, bearing and conquering the cruel cold and the devils’ temptations, and gaining the gift of pure prayer, of healing sickness and driving out demons, was much sought by crowds of monks and faithful; in that time of intense Calvinist propaganda from Transylvania he was a true defender and confessor of the right faith, keeping the flame of Orthodoxy alight in all the Neamț country — the Romanian life runs on past the point read here. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 9 September with a troparion: kindled with the love of Christ, you lived as a hermit in the cave of Măgura Tazlăului, glorifying God and conquering the devils.
