# Ignatius the Sinaite

Ignatius came from the town of Rethymno in Crete, saint.gr says, and practised his asceticism on Mount Sinai. Keeping the terms of the monastic state — virginity, obedience and the owning of nothing — he reached heights of spiritual perfection and became the spiritual guide of many ascetics.

He was counted worthy, the page says, to find the incorrupt and myrrh-streaming relic of his own disciple Nikandros; and having embraced and kissed it he gave thanks to God, who had told him in this way that his spiritual child's struggle had been vindicated. Ignatius himself fell asleep in peace. The page gives no year for his birth and none for his death.

saint.gr prints for him an apolytikion and a kontakion, both copied here, and beside them two megalynaria, an oikos and a kathisma, which this corpus has no fields for. The kathisma calls him a hieromonk, which is the only place the page states his rank; the rest of it greets him as the sweet-smelling violet of Crete and the beacon of the unwedded of Sinai.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 29 Ιανουαρίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/01/29/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/3557/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
