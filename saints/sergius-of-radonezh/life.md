# Sergius of Radonezh

The long life the Romanian synaxarion keeps for the abbot of Radonezh begins before his birth: at the Liturgy in Rostov, the unborn child cried out three times from his mother's womb - at the Gospel, at the Cherubic hymn, at "the Holy things for the holy" - and all understood that a great luminary and servant of the Holy Trinity would be shown to the world. Born to the devout Cyril and Mary and named Bartholomew, the infant would take no milk on Wednesdays and Fridays; the boy could not learn his letters until an angelic monk, met praying in a grove, blessed him and told him he would surpass his teachers.

Tonsured Sergius, he settled in the forest and gathered a brotherhood around the church of the Holy Trinity, and the life gives its measure of him in one vision: a multitude of beautiful birds in and around the monastery, singing with unspeakable sweetness, and a voice - "as you have seen these birds, so will the flock of your disciples multiply." When Mamai the Tatar emperor came against the Russian land, the Grand Prince Dimitri came to him in his grief, and Sergius armed him with prayer and prophesied: go out against the barbarians putting away all doubt, and with God's help you will conquer and return whole to your throne.

Having lived seventy-eight years, he saw his departure to God seven months beforehand, called the brothers and entrusted the abbacy to his disciple Nicon, young in years and grey in mind. His body was laid in the monastery of his labours, and when his relics were later uncovered, the life says, they were found whole and untouched by corruption, with an unspeakable fragrance and healings flowing as from a spring - as they flow, it adds, to this day. The Romanian calendar keeps him twice, on 5 July and this day.
