# Sergius of Radonezh

The long life the Romanian synaxarion keeps for the abbot of Radonezh begins before his birth: at the Liturgy in Rostov, the unborn child cried out three times from his mother's womb - at the Gospel, at the Cherubic hymn, at "the Holy things for the holy" - and all understood that a great luminary and servant of the Holy Trinity would be shown to the world. Born to the devout Cyril and Mary and named Bartholomew, the infant would take no milk on Wednesdays and Fridays; the boy could not learn his letters until an angelic monk, met praying in a grove, blessed him and told him he would surpass his teachers.

Tonsured Sergius, he settled in the forest and gathered a brotherhood around the church of the Holy Trinity, and the life gives its measure of him in one vision: a multitude of beautiful birds in and around the monastery, singing with unspeakable sweetness, and a voice - "as you have seen these birds, so will the flock of your disciples multiply." When Mamai the Tatar emperor came against the Russian land, the Grand Prince Dimitri came to him in his grief, and Sergius armed him with prayer and prophesied: go out against the barbarians putting away all doubt, and with God's help you will conquer and return whole to your throne.

Having lived seventy-eight years, he saw his departure to God seven months beforehand, called the brothers and entrusted the abbacy to his disciple Nicon, young in years and grey in mind. His body was laid in the monastery of his labours, and when his relics were later uncovered, the life says, they were found whole and untouched by corruption, with an unspeakable fragrance and healings flowing as from a spring - as they flow, it adds, to this day. The Romanian calendar keeps him twice, on 5 July and this day.

The Greek synaxarion keeps him on 25 September as the wonderworker and abbot of Radonezh, and says it has no details of his life; its heading prints his death as 1329, where the Russian calendar has 1392. His body, it says, lies incorrupt in the Lavra of the Holy Trinity near Moscow.

The Russian calendar, read for the first time on this day, keeps the same 25 September and calls it his «Преставление» — his falling asleep — with the year the Romanian gives, 1392, and not the Greek page's 1329. It names him «игумена Радонежского, всея России чудотворца», abbot of Radonezh and wonderworker of all Russia, and prints for the day both a troparion and a kontakion of his own and a common troparion to him and Nicon together, the disciple he had handed the abbacy to.

The Serbian church's Ohrid Prologue keeps him second of three on this day and calls him «велики подвижник и светилник руске цркве», the great ascetic and lamp of the Russian church. It is the third calendar to give him a different year: born 1313 at Rostov of pious parents [Cyril](/saints/cyril-of-radonezh) and [Maria](/saints/maria-of-radonezh) — which is how it reaches this corpus, and it is the only one of the four to name them here — tonsured after their death under the baptismal name Bartholomew, and reposing in 1391. The Russian says 1392 and the Greek page's heading 1329.

Its account is short and all of it is gifts. The quiet and gentle servant of God knew only labour and prayer; for the purity of his heart he was granted the gift of wonders, and raised the dead in the name of Christ; the Mother of God appeared to him more than once; princes and rulers came to him for counsel. He blessed prince Dimitri Donskoy and foretold his victory in the war to free Russia from the Tatars, and he saw into men's hearts and into events at a distance. His house filled with monks in his own lifetime and served for centuries after as one of the chief centres of the spiritual life, and the Prologue ends where the Russian life does: after his death he appeared more than once, to various people.
