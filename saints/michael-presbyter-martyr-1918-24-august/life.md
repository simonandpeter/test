# Michael (Voskresensky), with 28 martyrs

Michael Grigorievich Voskresensky was born in 1883 in the village of Tyoply Stan, the son of the district police officer Gregory Dmitrievich Voskresensky; his grandfather Father Demetrius was rector of the church at Poretskoye and taught the Law of God in the school, where his future father-in-law Ivan Danilovich studied — to whom Father Demetrius once appeared after his death, which he took as a special sign, so that when years later the grandson Michael asked for his daughter Maria he agreed without hesitation. At Bortsurmany he served from 1910, loved by his parishioners for kindness, piety and blameless fulfilment of his pastoral duties; then came 1917 and the open persecution of the Church, and in half a year of cruel rule the Bolsheviks set the people against them and risings broke out everywhere — in the summer of 1918 — the calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. Arrested by a punitive detachment during the suppression of a peasant rising in August 1918 and charged with supporting the anti-Bolshevik forces, after cruel tortures he was shot with the reader Eulampius Nikolaev and twenty-seven peasants, meeting death with prayer on his lips. The Russian calendar keeps him with his twenty-eight on 24 August (Julian).
