# Vatas the Persian

Vatas came of Persia and was born of Christian parents in the fourth century, saint.gr says, and that is all the page will fix in time. At the age of thirty he left his parents, his children and his wife and went off to a monastery and became a monk, longing for the life of the martyrs. So when the persecution of the Christians broke out in Persia, and the rest of his brethren fled and abandoned the house, he was the one who stayed; and he was taken by the Persians and handed over to Iasdich, the governor of Nisibis and brother of Barzabanas.

Iasdich ordered him to worship the sun. He refused, and confessed the Name of the Lord with a spiritual manhood, and for that he was given a dreadful torture: they bound his hands and stretched them so far that his shoulders were pulled out of joint, and then they tied him and dragged him along the ground, and cut him about with a knife, and at the last struck off his honourable head.

That is the whole of the notice. The page gives no year for the martyrdom and no relic, and it prints no hymn of his. Its couplet turns on his name: «Καὶ τῷ Βατᾷ, τμηθέντι τὴν κάραν ξίφει, / Βατὰ πρεπόντως οὐρανοῦ τὰ χωρία» — to Vatas, whose head was cut off with the sword, the regions of heaven are fittingly passable.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on the same day and its whole notice is two sentences, which name the city his Greek dossier does not. He was a monk, lived in the fourth century, came of Persia and practised ascesis there in one of the monasteries; and in the persecution of the Christians raised by the Persian king he was killed in the city of Низивия — Nisibis — for confessing the faith of Christ.
