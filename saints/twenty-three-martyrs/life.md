# The Twenty-three Martyrs

These are the twenty-three Christians taken in a cave near Nicomedia under
Maximian, after the emperor had promised a reward to anyone who informed on
Christians. They were tortured, pressed to worship the idols, and brought
before the praetor to have their names and their answers written down — and
it was watching them, the courage they suffered with and the firmness of
their confession, that turned the head of the praetorium,
[Adrian](/saints/adrian-of-nicomedia). What reward do you expect from your
God for this, he asked them; such a reward, they said, as we cannot describe
and your mind cannot grasp; and he told the scribes to write his name down
with theirs. They were kept in prison together, and it was on their pledge
that Adrian was let out to tell [Natalia](/saints/natalia-of-nicomedia) the
day of the execution. Their hands and legs were broken on an anvil, Adrian’s
first at Natalia’s own asking; the fire meant for their bodies was put out
by a storm. The synaxarion gives their names: Anatolius, Anthimus,
Antiochus, Gentilius, Eleutherius, Hermogenes, Euethius, Euretus, Eutychius,
Theagones, Theodore, Thyrsus, John, Carteras, Claudius, Cyriacus, Marinus,
Mardonius, Menodius, Plato, Synetus, Troadius and Pharetrius — in the fourth
century. The Greek calendar keeps them with Adrian on 26 August.
