# Joseph the Sanctified of Samakos

The Greek calendar keeps 29 August for the deposition of his relic; his life stands under 22 January.

Joseph was born and bred in Crete, in a village called Keramon — today’s Azokeramo in Sitia — in 1440. When he was of age his devout parents gave him to a teacher, a venerable spiritual father who lived at the small monastery of St John the Theologian called Dermatanos, near the sea at Chandax, which is Heraklion; there he learned divine contemplation and fine writing. When his parents died he shared his large inheritance among the poor and gave himself the more to spiritual struggle; God made him worthy of the priesthood and of a pilgrimage to the Holy Places, after which he returned to his little monastery and lived a holy life of love and almsgiving.

He died past seventy on 22 January 1511 and was buried at his monastery; at the translation of his relics his sanctity was shown, the body whole and fragrant, and the constant healing of the sick, the blind and the possessed spread his fame as a wonderworker. When the Ottomans took Chandax in 1669 the devout cleric Antonios Armakis carried the relic to Zakynthos and on 29 August 1669 deposited it at the monastery of St John of Mantineios at Xerovounia, where it stayed until 1915; it now rests in the parish church of the Pantokrator at Gaitani on Zakynthos.
