# Barlaam of the Kyiv Caves

saint.gr's calendar for 19 Νοεμβρίου names «Όσιος Βαρλαάμ ηγούμενος του Σπηλαίου» — Barlaam, abbot of the Caves — heads his page «(; - 1065)», giving the year of his death and no year of birth, and then says outright that it has nothing more: «Δεν έχουμε λεπτομέρειες για τον βίο του Ρώσου Αγίου», we have no details of the life of the Russian saint. That one word, Russian, is the whole of what it says about where he came from.

The only other thing it prints is his relics: «Το Λείψανο του Οσίου βρίσκεται αδιάφθορο στη Λαύρα των Σπηλαίων του Κιέβου» — the relic of the venerable one lies incorrupt in the Lavra of the Caves at Kyiv, which is the house the office in his title belongs to. The page gives him no birth year, no manner of death, no companions and no hymn.

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps him on the Julian 19 November, «Прп. Варлаама, игумена Печерского (1065).», and prints two lives behind one index. He lived in the eleventh century at Kyiv and was the son of a notable boyar. From his young years he often visited [Anthony of the Kyiv Caves](/saints/anthony-of-the-caves) and listened to his instruction with love, and was so drawn to the monastic life that he resolved to become a monk himself; Anthony received the young man, firm in that resolve, and bade [Nikon of the Kyiv Caves](/saints/nikon-of-the-kyiv-caves) perform the tonsure over him. His father tried to bring him home by force, and the firmness of spirit the young man showed made the father give way.

When the number of monks in the caves began to grow, Anthony set him as abbot and himself moved to another cave to live in solitude again; so that he was the first abbot of the Kyiv Caves monastery. In 1058, having asked Anthony’s blessing, he built a wooden church over the cave in honour of the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos, and afterwards he became abbot of a newly founded monastery in honour of the great-martyr Demetrius — which the first of the two pages makes the wish of the great prince Izyaslav, Demetrius at his baptism.

Twice he made the pilgrimage to the holy places of Jerusalem and Constantinople, both by his own drawing and for the good of the Church, since Christianity in Rus was then still a new thing and much of the ancient order of the Eastern Church had to be brought in. Returning from the second journey he died at the Svyatogorsky monastery of Vladimir in Volhynia in 1065, and was buried, as he had willed, in the Caves monastery, in the Near Caves.
