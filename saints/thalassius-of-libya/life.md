# Thalassius of Libya

saint.gr's Thalassius came from Libya in Africa. He became a presbyter and then an abbot, and he was an ascetical writer — his works stand in the Philokalia — and a contemporary of [Maximus the Confessor](/saints/maximus-the-confessor). He wrote four hundred chapters on love and on self-restraint and on the ordering of life according to the mind, and he fell asleep in peace in 648.

The page's own dating will not hold together: it puts him in the years of the emperor Constantine IV Pogonatus, whose reign it gives as 668 to 685, and then closes his life in 648, twenty years before that reign began. The year of the death is what the page's head line repeats, and it is the one recorded here; the reign is set down as the page has it and not reconciled.

Over his memory the synaxarion sets a couplet on his name and his grave: «O Θαλάσσιος και κατοικήσας τάφον, Bλύζει θαλάσσας χαρίτων εκ του τάφου» — Thalassius, who has taken a tomb for his dwelling, makes seas of graces well up out of the tomb. The page prints no hymn to him; what it prints instead is a prayer of his own in modern Greek, asking Christ the Lord of all to free us from the ruinous passions and from the thoughts that come of them.
