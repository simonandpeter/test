# Sabel of Persia

Manuel, Sabel and Ismael were brothers of one mother. Their country was Persia; their parents were not of one faith, for their father held to the Persian paganism and their mother to the good faith of the Christians. As she had fed her sons with her own milk, so she gave them the Christian faith from their youth and raised them in the fear of God; and the blessed priest Eunoicus made them Christians in the bath of holy baptism and taught them the divine books. Grown to manhood, they were numbered in the army and served Alamundar, king of Persia, with the body, while with the spirit they served the heavenly King.

Their king sent them to Julian the Apostate, the Greek emperor, to confirm the peace. Julian first received them with honour and showed them much favour, making himself a loving friend to them; then, learning that they were Christians, he was filled with anger, and against the custom and the law of all peoples he tortured for their faith in Christ these men who had been sent to him as envoys, and destroyed them. It began at a sacrificial feast, where the brothers stood apart and wept and refused the summons to the imperial table.

The next day he sat in judgement and argued that there could be no peace between the two empires without the union of friendship, and no friendship while they would not keep the festival with him. They answered that he should not hope to change their constancy in the faith of Christ, and that he might do more to them if he wished, for they were ready to bear everything. He had an iron nail driven into the head of each of them and sharpened reeds thrust under the nails of their hands and feet, and at last condemned them to death by the sword, ordering their bodies burned afterwards.

They were led out to the place of execution called Constantine's, and there, before their blood was shed, they made their last prayer to God. When the servants would have carried out the rest of the order and burned the bodies, the earth shook at God's command and the ground where they stood opened and received them and covered them, so that no tormentor's hands should touch them again nor the fire turn them to ashes; the servants fled in fear, and many who saw it believed in Christ. The page ends with the war that followed: the Persians broke the Greek ranks, and Julian himself was struck down by an unseen hand.

Doxologia's calendar for 17 iunie names the three together on one line, «Sfinții Mucenici Manuil, Savel și Ismail». Sabel is kept here in his own folder beside his brothers [Manuel](/saints/manuel-of-persia) and [Ismael](/saints/ismael-of-persia), because the line names each of them.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 17 June, and saint.gr heads his page «(; - 362)». All three, it says, were brothers from Persia — he and [Manuel](/saints/manuel-of-persia) and [Ismael](/saints/ismael-of-persia); their father was a fire-worshipper and their mother a Christian who handed on the Christian upbringing to them, while their Christian schooling came from a devout priest named Eunicus — and they had come to Constantinople on some embassy. During their stay there they happened to see at Chalcedon the emperor Julian sacrificing to the idols in the midst of a procession.

The sight grieved them, and they said what they thought of a state whose ruler made himself guilty of such impiety. Informers who overheard them denounced them to Julian, who ordered the three Persians at once to deny Christ; and they answered that they had long ago denied themselves, and that for the Jesus to whom they had given over their souls they were ready to pour out their blood. Then their torture began: they pierced their ankles with a sharp instrument and burned their armpits with lighted torches, and afterwards beheaded them. Divine justice, the page adds, was not slow — when Julian campaigned against the Persians soon after, he fell dead in some battle in the flower of his age, paying for his sins.
