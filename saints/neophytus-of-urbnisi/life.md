# Neophytus of Urbnisi

Neophytus lived in the seventh century and came of the Persians, who worshipped before fire. In his time, at the order of the Saracen emir Mumni — Mu'min — the commander of his army, Ahmad, fell on Georgia with a countless host. Having taken the middle part of Shida, in Kartli, Ahmad sent two of his commanders, Omar and Burul, to take the capital Mtskheta as well. At the meeting of the rivers Mtkvari and Aragvi, in the rocky country of Sarkineti, the invaders found caves and planned to search them once Mtskheta was theirs; but they could not cross the Mtkvari, the courage of the Georgians holding them back.

The Saracen commanders brought prisoners before them and asked what was in those caves, and so learned that the monastery of Shio-Mgvime was there, where chosen men of God lived who had renounced every earthly good. Astonished at the answer, Omar and Burul sent word of it to Ahmad, and he ordered Omar to go to the monastery and ask the monks to pray for him and commemorate him at the tomb of their elder, Saint Shio, as though this were something he often did: "Pray for me, servants of God, and take these gifts of aloes and incense as an offering for your elder."

Coming near the caves, Omar sent a messenger ahead to say that he came in peace and brought gifts. At the gates of the monastery he saw an army of bodiless powers coming down from heaven, and among them an old man shining with light. The gentle and humble bearing of the monks astonished him, and he understood quickly that those unusual soldiers were the angels of God and that the old man was Shio of Mgvime, the first abbot of the community. He told the monks his vision and promised to come back, to be baptised and to stay there as a monk.

Not long afterwards Omar left everything of the world and came to the Mgvime monastery to keep his promise; he was baptised, and at his tonsure received the name Neophytus — newly planted, from the Greek word the Apostle Paul uses in 1 Timothy 3:6 of a bishop newly baptised. Two of the former commander's servants were baptised as well, and were called from then on Christodoulos, the servant of God, and Christophoros, the bearer of Christ.

Growing in the labours of a monk, Neophytus was filled with the grace of God and with wisdom, so that he astonished all who came to know him, and after a time he was ordained bishop of Urbnisi. As a hierarch, the life says, "he strengthened the weak, healed the sick, raised the fallen, freed those held by the devil, guided the lost and sought out the straying, caring for them and stopping them from turning aside again."

The devil could not bear the apostolic work of Christ's good shepherd, and tempted the fire-worshippers to kill him: they burst into his cell, bound him and began to mock him. Knowing beforehand that he wished to become like the martyr Stephen, they planned to kill him with stones. But Neophytus said to them gently: "Death is sweet to me, you unbelievers — truly it is sweet. I wish to part the bond between my mortal nature and my immortal one. With my blood I shall strengthen the holy Church, founded on the precious Blood of the Son and Word of God, whom I preach. Let the will of God, who orders all things, be fulfilled in me also, for he called me into his light out of the depths of paganism." And while the furious pagans threw stones at him he said with his last breath: "Lord Jesus Christ, receive my spirit."

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery tells the same story in fewer words and dates it a hundred years earlier, and the difference is not resolved here. Its life makes Omar a Persian commander in the army of «султан Ахмет» invading Georgia, and has him see, as he rode up to the Shio-Mgvime house with his advance party, a host of angels above the monastery with an elder among them — [Shio](/saints/shio-of-mgvime) himself. Struck by the beauty of the place he left it untouched and carried away the wish to become a monk of it, came back, was baptised and was tonsured Neophytus, which the page glosses as the newly converted. It then adds what the Romanian life does not: he became the superior of the house, and the fame of his angelic life went through the whole of Georgia, so that the Catholicos Samuel IV, whom the page dates 582 to 591, called him out of the monastery and raised him to the see of Urbnisi.

The end is the same end — the pagans and fire-worshippers whose false teaching he was rooting out broke into the bishop's house, found him at prayer, dragged him out and stoned him as the first martyr of the Church was stoned — and the Russian page sets it in the year 587, where the Romanian puts his whole life in the seventh century. Afterwards, it says, his body was carried back to Shio-Mgvime and laid under the altar of the cathedral church. The folder's interval now holds both readings. Neither calendar gives a hymn under his name, and the Russian marks the commemoration as the Georgian church's, «(Груз.)», which its closing sentence repeats.
