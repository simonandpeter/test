# Palladius of Scotland

saint.gr says Palladius was of Celtic descent and served as a deacon at Rome, and that pope Celestine I, valuing his abilities, took him as his archdeacon. The heretic Agricola, one of the chief Pelagians, had at that time thrown the Church of the British Isles into confusion with his soul-destroying preaching; and it was Palladius who persuaded the pope to send there, in 429, the holy bishops Germanus of Auxerre and Lupus of Troyes, who managed by systematic and persistent mission to make the heresy disappear.

In 431, two years after Loegaire came to the throne as high king of Ireland — which was then called Scotia — the pope sent Palladius to that island as its first bishop, to preach the Gospel and to found the local Church. He landed in Leinster with twelve companions to help him in the work, but could not stay long: at the end of the same year he was driven out by the chief Dathi. He had time, though, to baptise a good number of Scots, that is Irish, and with his fellow workers to build three wooden churches, and on leaving he left behind four of his twelve companions — Augustine, Benedict, Silvester and Solinus — together with his manuscripts and portions of holy relics.

The evangelising of the country, the page says, was carried out later and in more favourable conditions by the apostle of Ireland proper, Saint Patrick. Palladius turned to the northern provinces of Britain, where many Scottish, that is Irish, settlers lived alongside the half-wild tribe of the Picts, and in that region, known to this day as Scotland, he preached the truth of the Gospel for nineteen whole years. His success was such that, although Christianity had reached that country as early as the second century, local tradition reckons Saint Palladius the apostle and first bishop of Scotland. He reposed in peace in the year 450.
