# John (Pankratovich)

John (Ivan Fyodorovich) Pankratovich was born in 1870 in the small town of Kletsk in the Slutsk district of Minsk province; the dates of his ordination as deacon and priest are not known. In 1913 he became rector of the church of the Protection in the village of Chizhevichi, now near Soligorsk, and at Easter 1918 was given the skufia for zealous service. He was the church’s last rector: the Soviet authorities closed it in 1934 and burned its nineteenth-century iconostasis in the square before it. He went on secretly blessing houses, baptising the newborn and burying the dead, and urged his parishioners to stand firm against the authorities and sign for the services to be restored; during the all-Union census of 1937 he told them not to hide their faith but to enter themselves as Orthodox.

On 23 August 1937 he was arrested, charged with going round the villages calling on the peasants to sign for the reopening of the closed church and telling many parishioners to register as believers. At his interrogations he was pressed to renounce his priesthood and refused outright, and he did not admit to any crime. On 28 August 1937 a special troika of the NKVD of Belarus sentenced him to be shot, and on 6 October 1937 he was executed at Slutsk and buried in an unknown grave. The Synod of the Belarusian Orthodox Church canonised him as a locally venerated saint on 28 October 1999, and the Council of Bishops of the Russian Church numbered him among the new martyrs for veneration by the whole Church in August 2000. The Russian calendar keeps him on 23 September old style, and azbyka.ru prints no hymn for him.
