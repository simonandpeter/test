# Audelas, companion of Simeon of Persia

saint.gr says there was a Christian community in Persia whose people were of blameless life and great self-denial, ready to be sacrificed for the name and the glory of Christ, and that the heart of it was their bishop [Simeon](/saints/simeon-of-persia), who was head of the churches of Ctesiphon and of Salik in Seleucia. The Persians looked on the Christians as a thorn in the eye, and slandered them to king Shapur II on the pretext that they had revolutionary designs. Simeon assured the king that the Christian religion makes law-abiding citizens and not foolish rebels, but the king was so prejudiced against him that he ordered him imprisoned.

In the prison Simeon met [Gothazat](/saints/gothazat-companion-of-simeon), a Christian deserter who had changed his faith to save his head, and brought his heart back to Christ. When Shapur learnt of it he beheaded Simeon and Gothazat in his anger, and with them, the page says, another 1150 Christians of the church there, together with the presbyter Audelas. So they all gave themselves as an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweet-smelling savour. At the last moment [Phousik](/saints/phousik-companion-of-simeon), the king's kouropalates and a secret Christian, was added to the martyrs' camp.

That is the whole of what the page prints of Audelas, whom it names only in the list and in his own couplet: «Ἄρκτου τὸ δεινὸν Αὐδελλᾶς ἔδυ στόμα, Βδέλλης ἀπλήστου τοῦ Σατᾶν φυγὼν στόμα» — Audelas went down into the terrible mouth of a bear, having fled the mouth of Satan, the insatiable leech. His couplet is the only place the page says how he died, and it gives him no year and no country of his own.

**The Russian calendar tells this company's story at far greater length than the Greek page these folders were written from, and it is a different story in its shape.** The Greek has the bishop meet an apostate in the prison and bring him back; the Russian has the apostate meet him on the road to the prison and be shamed into repentance in the street. Shapur II, whose reign it dates 310 to 381, issued a decree in 344 laying a heavy tax on the Christians, and when some refused to pay it — which was presented as a revolt — the persecution began. The bishop was brought to judgement in iron fetters with two presbyters, and would not bow to the king: he had bowed to his rank before, he said, but now, brought there to deny his God, it was not fitting.

Pressed to worship the sun and threatened with the wiping out of Christianity in the country, he was taken to prison; and on the road the king's own former tutor, a eunuch who had worshipped the sun out of fear, stood up and bowed to him, and was turned away from and reproached. The eunuch repented, put off his costly clothes for coarse ones and wept at the palace doors, and when the king asked what grieved him he said openly that he would worship the sun no more. He was condemned, and asked only that heralds should proclaim through the city that he died as a Christian and not for any crime against the king; and the king granted it.

Called before the king a second time the bishop refused again, and the king had every Christian then in the prison beheaded before his eyes; they laid their own heads under the sword as he blessed them. So the first of the two presbyters died. When the turn came to the second he began to tremble, and one of the nobles, a secret Christian, cried out to him not to fear but to close his eyes and he would see at once the divine Light of our Lord Jesus Christ — and with that cry gave himself away. His tongue was torn out and his skin flayed from him, and his daughter was tortured to death with him. Last of all the bishop laid his head on the block with a prayer, on 13 April 344, and the killing went on through the whole of Pascha week to the twenty-third of April.

Of him by himself the Russian calendar adds only that he was the first of the two presbyters to be beheaded in the prison yard, before the one who trembled; and it is the source that gives this folder its year, which the Greek page did not.
