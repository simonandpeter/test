# Tatiana (Fomicheva)

**Hers is the only name on this day with a year clause of its own, and her life explains it.** The Сретенский monastery’s calendar closes the Julian 20 November with a sentence to itself, «Прмц. Татианы (после 1937).», after the company of fourteen it has just closed with «(1937)». She was born in 1897 in the village of Надовражное near the town of Истра in the province of Moscow, daughter of a peasant, Алексей Фомичев. In 1916 she entered a monastery as a novice, and after the revolution was under obedience at the Борисоглебский monastery until the authorities closed it in 1928, when she went back to her parents at Надовражное.

In 1931 the authorities began to pursue the monks and nuns of the closed houses, many of whom kept to the monastic rule in their lives notwithstanding; some settled near their houses, earned their bread by handwork like the desert-dwellers of old, and went to the nearest parish church to pray. So at the beginning of 1931 the OGPU made a case against the nuns of the house of the Elevation of the Cross near a village of the Podolsk district, where before the revolution about a hundred nuns had struggled; twelve sisters had stayed on there after the final suppression of 1926, some working in the rest home that had been put in its buildings, some living in the neighbouring villages and doing handwork, and all going to the Elias church of a nearby village to pray. The choir of that church was made up of nuns and novices of the closed houses, and she sang in it.

**The evidence against them is the most candid thing in the day.** The director of the rest home told the investigator that the bells, the crosses and the church ornaments were still hanging in the former monastery, that it was hard to say why they had not been taken down, and that the twelve nuns were «не что иное как община вокруг бывшего монастыря». The home’s culture worker, who had met them two or three times while surveying the locality, said he had vividly felt «именно гнездо и рассадник, враждебный нам, со своими зловещими старухами, проклинающими нашу установку», that peasant girls under twenty-five whom he had tried to argue out of their faith had listened at first and then waved him off and gone into the church, and that his opinion was «нужно вообще ликвидировать этот очаг вплоть до снесения церкви».

She was arrested with another novice on 18 May 1931 and shut in the Butyrka prison in Moscow, one of seventeen nuns and novices of various houses taken that day. The woman whose house she lodged in testified that she did handwork and sold it to the peasants of the neighbouring villages and was set against the measures of the Soviet power; a false witness said she was a fierce churchwoman carrying on active anti-Soviet activity. On 29 May 1931 the troika of the OGPU sentenced her to five years in a corrective labour camp. Freed in 1934 she settled in the village of Шелудьково in the Volokolamsk district and began helping the archpriest [Vladimir (Medvedyuk)](/saints/vladimir-medvedyuk) in the Trinity church.

She was arrested with him on 25 November 1937, in the house where she and another novice had been helping his wife chop cabbage that evening. Questioned on the 26th, the two novices «категорически отказались подтвердить обвинения, возводимые на них следователями, и не согласились никого оговаривать» — refused flatly to confirm the charges and would not speak against anybody. On 29 November the troika sentenced the archpriest to be shot and the two novices to ten years in a corrective labour camp.

**The life’s last sentence is the whole of what is known of her end.** Of the other novice it says that she came home when her term was over; of her, «послушница Татиана Фомичева приняла смерть в заключении» — she took death in confinement. No year, no place, no date. That is why the calendar gives her «(после 1937)» instead of the company’s year, and why her recorded death has no upper bound.
