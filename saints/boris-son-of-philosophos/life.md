# Boris, son of Philosophos Ornatsky

saint.gr keeps him on 31 Μαΐου with his father and his brother, and the life behind the line is the father's. [Philosophos Ornatsky](/saints/philosophos-ornatsky), it says, finished the theological academy of Saint Petersburg in 1885, married Helen Zaozerkov, was ordained presbyter, and built up an enormous work of charity and mission; through the first world war he stood beside the wounded soldiers and their families.

Of Boris the page says one thing: that he was his son, that he had been appointed head of the twenty-third artillery brigade, and that he fought bravely on the Austro-Hungarian front. His brother [Nicholas](/saints/nicholas-son-of-philosophos) served with senior rank in the ninth battalion of the Russian army.

After the revolution of 1917 the father was arrested, on 9 August 1918, together with his sons, by men of the state security, who took them to the prisons of Kronstadt; and there the three of them were executed by shooting, and so gave the witness of their faith. The page gives Boris no year of birth, no trial and no further word, and prints no hymn for him.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 31 мая ст. ст. with his father and his brother, and gives the three of them one life. He was born in 1887, finished the Konstantinovsky artillery school, was a staff captain of the 23rd artillery brigade and fought heroically, the calendar says, on the Austro-Hungarian front. His brother, born the year before, was a military doctor with the Ninth Russian Army.

Their father was rector of the Kazan cathedral at St Petersburg and had called his flock not to take in the dissolving ideas of Bolshevism. On 9 August 1918 the three were arrested together; the father’s parishioners gathered in a crowd of many thousands and went along the Nevsky Prospect to the Cheka demanding his release, and were promised treacherously that it would be done. That same night the father was moved to the prison of Kronstadt. About 30 October 1918, the calendar says — giving the date as a guess — the three were taken with thirty other imprisoned officers to be shot, and the father read the prayers for the departing aloud over the condemned on the way. Where it happened the calendar does not know: at Kronstadt by one guess, by another near the Gulf of Finland between Ligovo and Oranienbaum, and the bodies, it seems, were thrown into the gulf. The three were numbered among the saints at the jubilee council of August 2000.
