# Heliodorus (Golovanitsky)

Heliodorus — Iliodor, in the Russian form his brethren used; John
Golovanitsky in the world — was born in 1795 in the village of Staroselye
in the Cherkasy district of Kiev province, the son of the priest Theodore
and his wife Eudocia, who raised him in the fear of God. He studied at the
Kiev-Mohyla Academy from 1806 to 1813, was taken that year into the Nizhny
Novgorod militia and served at the chancery of the army that followed
Napoleon’s retreat abroad, and retiring in 1815 entered the Kiev Caves
Lavra as a novice; he soon moved to the Sophroniev hermitage, where he
learned from discerning fathers that a monk needs not bodily labour only
but the inner work in the garden of his heart, and thence, called by
[Philaret (Danilevsky)](/saints/philaret-danilevsky), to Glinsk, to the
bakery and the kliros, where under Philaret’s guidance he laboured at his
perfecting and fed his thirsting soul on Scripture and the fathers. After
two years he was made a rasophore with the name Joel. Before his tonsure he
had to go to Petersburg to explain his early retirement; on the road,
finding a river’s ice-crossing closed, he slept at an inn on the bank and
was woken by a stranger who led him over the river on a causeway that was
not there and vanished; and on a pilgrimage to Ploshchansk afterwards
robbers twice made to attack him and each time soldiers happened by —
soldiers no one else had seen on that road: angels guarded him. On 9 March
1823, the day of the Forty Martyrs, Philaret tonsured him with the name
Joannicius, and in the days he kept in church after it he sat down on the
ambo worn out with watching, and saw the south door of the altar open and
a beautiful youth in a white sticharion with an orarion crossed on his
breast come to him and say, Joannicius, watch! you are a monk, and must
therefore watch without ceasing — and go in again by the north door. As he
grew, the temptations grew: the devil raised slander, the strongest weapon
in his hand, and he was driven from the hermitage; he set off north,
suffered terrible demonic terrors on the way, and turned back resolved to
die under the monastery walls if need be; at the elder brethren’s petition
the abbot let him stay, put him two months in the kitchen under a penance
he had not earned, and, convinced of his innocence, presented him for the
diaconate, to which Bishop Vladimir of Kursk ordained him on 12 November
1824 — as the bishop’s hand lay on his head he felt a sweet warmth pour
through his head into his heart and over his whole body, so that his face
changed and the bishop kept glancing at him through the Liturgy, and at the
communion, standing behind the altar among the deacons, he was in rapture
and saw a wonderful vision with the eyes of his mind.

To keep out of an intrigue a newcomer tried to raise among the brethren in
1826 he moved to the Rykhly monastery, served it three years as treasurer,
and then fell into disgrace, made worse by his own act: coming into the
altar he found a fat landowner sitting on the table of oblation while the
sacristan showed him the sacred things — to tell him what he should would
only mean being stuck with him, to leave him trampling the holy place was
unbearable — so he prayed at the altar, Lord, help; if it please Thee that
I suffer, Thy will be done, slipped his elbow behind the sitter’s back and
tipped him off. In 1828–1829 the superior had him moved to the Domnitsky
monastery under strict watch and forbidden to serve; on business at
Chernigov he begged the bishop to let him go to Athos or Solovki and met
only prejudice — let you go north and you will go to Petersburg and
justify yourself, and I may have unpleasantness; no, I will not let you go
until they put the red cap on you, that is, strip him of his habit and
send him for a soldier; but in 1831 the bishop was changed, and at their
last meeting the old one unexpectedly asked his forgiveness, and the new
one ordained him hieromonk in 1832 and made him superior of Rykhly. He was
made igumen in 1835 and archimandrite in 1840, with the Peter and Paul
monastery at Glukhov, and at his own petition was retired in 1845 to
Glinsk, his heart’s home through all his wanderings, to give himself wholly
to the one thing needful, adding attention to attention. In the autumn of
1850 he went with other pilgrims to Palestine, going constantly round the
places hallowed by the Saviour’s life and passion — the Sepulchre,
Golgotha, the Way of the Cross, Gethsemane, Olivet — and pouring out his
prayer to him who wrought our redemption; on the way back he landed on
Athos meaning to stay for ever, sprinkled its earth on his head — this is
my rest, here will I dwell — and was soon so famed that many begged him to
stay and guide them and a monastery offered him a cell for nothing; he
agreed at first, then declared that it was not God’s will that he stay on
Athos, and against God’s will he would not go, and returned to Russia in
the first days of July 1851, to Glinsk, where God had appointed him to go
on serving God and men. On 24 December 1858, the eve of Christmas, he was
clothed in the great schema with the name Iliodor, and in 1863 withdrew to
a hermit’s cell he had built three kilometres east of the monastery, where
the Far Skete was afterwards founded, and took up higher labours of
abstinence and prayer.

In his humility he thought himself lower than every creature and never in
his life allowed his portrait to be taken; in food he kept the skete rule
strictly, without oil through the fasts and on Mondays, Wednesdays and
Fridays, following the Church’s own allowances on other days not for the
taste but for obedience, and allowed himself no departure from his rules
even in grave illness. With the gifts of discernment and foresight he was
the guide of many Glinsk elders; his teaching agrees in sense and sometimes
almost word for word with that of the elders of Optina, Ambrose, Moses and
Anthony, and it says much that his surviving letters were collected in the
library of the Optina skete; he was not only a most experienced director
but, through grace-filled contemplations of the world above, a witness to
God’s truth. Child, he told a monk who asked how to act in every
circumstance without sinning, if you want your actions never to be at
fault, see that they are always founded on Holy Scripture — the truths of
the Gospel, the teaching of the apostles and the sayings of the ascetic
fathers; in that holy treasury you will find a foundation for everything
you need, for every case, for your whole life. In his last year he lay
almost constantly, melting like the wax of a burning-down candle, giving
his instructions and answers lying on his back in a weakening voice, and
was brought by the brethren into the monastery; on Monday 25 June 1879 he
communicated once more and waited for his departure in peace, the peace of
a conscience reconciled and assured of grace, expecting not death but a
passing to another world; on the Wednesday, asked whether he wished to be
anointed and to communicate, he said with effort, barely audibly, I wish it
very much, and the igumen [Innocent](/saints/innocent-stepanov) anointed
him and gave him the Mysteries for the last time; and he fell asleep in the
Lord at exactly one in the morning of 28 June 1879, in the eighty-fourth
year of his much-suffering life. The Council of Bishops of 2017 entered him
in the calendar of the Russian Church, which keeps him on 9 September of
the old style, 22 September, in the synaxis of the Glinsk fathers.
