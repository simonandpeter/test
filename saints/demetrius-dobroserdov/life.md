# Demetrius (Dobroserdov)

The Russian calendar's 8 October names a company of eight shot on one day, and he is the first of them: «Сщмчч. Димитрия , архиеп. Можайского, и с ним Иоанна диакона, прмчч. Амвросия и Пахомия , прмц. Татианы , мч. Николая , мцц. Марии и Надежды (1937)». He was Ivan Ivanovich Dobroserdov in the world, born on 22 January 1864 in the village of Pakhotny Ugol in Tambov province, in a priest's family. He finished the Tambov seminary in 1885 and was made a teacher in a zemstvo school in the Morshansk district, where he worked until 1889 and was thanked more than once by the zemstvo assembly and the provincial school council for his work in popular education. He married.

On 6 May 1889 he was ordained priest to the church of St Nicholas in the village of Mamontovo, a place of about two thousand people, and made the head and the teacher of religion of its parish school. Soon his wife and his children died. Left alone, he went away from Tambov province and in 1894 entered the Moscow theological academy; he finished it in 1898 a candidate of theology and was appointed teacher of religion at the fourth Moscow gymnasium and priest of its church. The metropolitan of Moscow made him rector of the gymnasium's church of the Annunciation in 1899, and in the same year the pedagogical society at the Imperial Moscow university elected him a full member and the secretary of its section on religious and moral education.

In 1908 the metropolitan of Moscow called him in and told him that there are two ways, the family way and the monastic, that the family way was closed to him, and that he advised him to become a monk. He agreed, was tonsured that December in the Smolensk Zosima hermitage with the name Demetrius, raised to archimandrite, and made synodal sacristan and rector of the church of the Twelve Apostles in the Kremlin. On 18 May 1914, in the Dormition cathedral of the Kremlin, he was consecrated bishop of Mozhaysk and vicar of the Moscow diocese, and given the Savva-Storozhevsky monastery.

From 1921 the life is a record of being moved on. Bishop of Stavropol, where the Renovationists had taken nearly every parish and he had to leave; then, from September 1923, bishop of Kozlov and acting administrator of Tambov, where the Renovationists repented and all the churches but one came back to the Orthodox. The page quotes the local OGPU man on him: a very cunning and careful person, of enormous authority among the faithful, under whom the Tikhonite movement in Kozlov and its district was growing not by the day but by the hour, and who answered every request for a posting by sending the priest to get the parish's own written wish for him first.

In Lent 1925 the summonses to the OGPU became frequent, and sometimes he went straight from an interrogation to a church where people were waiting for the service to begin. He was pressed to leave Kozlov and refused, and then threatened with prison. At last, pleading his heart, he asked leave to go to Egypt for treatment, got a passport and the English visa, and went to Sergiev Posad the next day to say goodbye to Alexander Samarin, once over-procurator of the Synod. A rider stopped him on the way to Samarin's house and he was arrested — Sergiev Posad, then a week on the Lubyanka, then released with orders to go to Kozlov, where he was arrested again.

He was let out at Tambov and went to Moscow, where he served the vigil of the feast of the great martyr Catherine with the patriarchal locum tenens metropolitan Peter; it was Peter's last service, and three days later he was arrested. In July 1926 the OGPU's Tuchkov required him to leave the capital, and before going to Kislovodsk he saw metropolitan Sergius, who divided the Stavropol diocese in two and gave him the new see of Pyatigorsk. He lived in Kislovodsk in a rented room, out beyond the railway station, and served often in the church of St Panteleimon. He was raised to archbishop on 14 April 1932, and on 23 March 1934 made archbishop of Mozhaysk and vicar of the Moscow diocese. In Moscow he lived in the watchman's lodge of the church of the Prophet Elijah on Bolshaya Cherkizovskaya street, and lived there until his arrest.

He was arrested on 29 September 1937 and held in the Butyrka prison. He admitted nothing and would not incriminate anybody. Asked whether he had told those around him that religion and the clergy were persecuted in the USSR, he said he had not said it to anyone. Asked where he had got the counter-revolutionary rumour that metropolitan Peter had been shot, he answered that he knew from metropolitan Sergius that Peter had died in exile in December 1936, and that he had heard nothing of a shooting and spread no such rumour. Arrested with him were [Ambrose (Astakhov)](/saints/ambrose-astakhov), [Pachomius (Turkevich)](/saints/pachomius-turkevich), the deacon [John Khrenov](/saints/john-khrenov), the nun [Tatiana (Besfamilnaya)](/saints/tatiana-besfamilnaya) and the lay people [Nicholas Rein](/saints/nicholas-rein), Vasily Efimov, [Maria Volnukhina](/saints/maria-volnukhina) and [Nadezhda Azhgerevich](/saints/nadezhda-azhgerevich).

The NKVD troika sentenced them to be shot on 17 October 1937, and on 21 October eight of them were shot at the Butovo range outside Moscow and buried in a common unmarked grave. Vasily Efimov, the ninth, was given ten years instead and died soon afterwards in the Lokchimlag in Komi; he is in the life's heading and not on the calendar's day line. The eight were numbered among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia for church-wide veneration at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000.
