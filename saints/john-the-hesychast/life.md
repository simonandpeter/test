# John the Hesychast

John was born at Nikopolis of Armenia in the year 454, saint.gr says, of Enkratios and Euphemia, in the reign of the emperor Marcian. After the death of his parents he built a church of the Most Holy Mother of God and lived there as a monk with ten others; and because of the greatness of his virtue he was ordained bishop of Colonia at twenty-eight.

For nine years he set the church's affairs there in order, and then he went to Jerusalem and took up residence in the monastery of Saint Sabbas. There he hid his true dignity, and [Sabbas](/saints/sabbas-the-sanctified) appointed him a servant in the guest-house and in the kitchen, which he obeyed with all humility. Seeing the greatness of his virtue, Sabbas recommended to Elias, bishop of Jerusalem, that John be ordained deacon and presbyter; and then John, unable to do otherwise, revealed his dignity.

He stayed twelve years in the Lavra and six years at Routha. Afterwards he passed his years ascetically in various deserts for forty-eight whole years, and died in peace at a hundred and four. The page prints no hymn for him, and its couplet is a pun on the name of the stillness he kept: I do not keep still — for I crown even in death, with words, John the Hesychast.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and fills in what the Greek page passed over. He was of Nikopolis in Armenia, of a father Enkratios and a mother Euphemia; in his eighteenth year he became a monk and struggled hard and resolutely, cleansing his heart with many tears, with fasting and with prayer. After ten years he was made bishop of Colonia, and by his own example he drew to a life pleasing to God his brother Pergamius and his uncle Theodore, men of note at the courts of the emperors Zeno and Justinian.

Seeing the malice and the turmoil of this world, which he could not cure, he left the episcopal throne and came disguised as a simple monk to the community of Saint [Sabbas](/saints/sabbas-the-sanctified) near Jerusalem, where for many years he stayed unrecognised, performing conscientiously and excellently every task the abbot set him. Then he was shut up in his cell, and passed year after year in silence and prayer; afterwards he spent nine years in the desert, eating nothing but wild herbs, and returned again to the monastery.

He turned the faithful away from the heresy of Origen and did much towards its being put down and condemned. He saw clearly into the spiritual world and healed people of their sicknesses, and having conquered himself he easily conquered demons. Great in humility, in strength and in the wisdom of God, this servant of Christ reposed in peace in the year 558, in the hundred and fourth year of his life.
