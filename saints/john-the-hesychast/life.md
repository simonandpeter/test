# John the Hesychast

John was born at Nikopolis of Armenia in the year 454, saint.gr says, of Enkratios and Euphemia, in the reign of the emperor Marcian. After the death of his parents he built a church of the Most Holy Mother of God and lived there as a monk with ten others; and because of the greatness of his virtue he was ordained bishop of Colonia at twenty-eight.

For nine years he set the church's affairs there in order, and then he went to Jerusalem and took up residence in the monastery of Saint Sabbas. There he hid his true dignity, and [Sabbas](/saints/sabbas-the-sanctified) appointed him a servant in the guest-house and in the kitchen, which he obeyed with all humility. Seeing the greatness of his virtue, Sabbas recommended to Elias, bishop of Jerusalem, that John be ordained deacon and presbyter; and then John, unable to do otherwise, revealed his dignity.

He stayed twelve years in the Lavra and six years at Routha. Afterwards he passed his years ascetically in various deserts for forty-eight whole years, and died in peace at a hundred and four. The page prints no hymn for him, and its couplet is a pun on the name of the stillness he kept: I do not keep still — for I crown even in death, with words, John the Hesychast.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and fills in what the Greek page passed over. He was of Nikopolis in Armenia, of a father Enkratios and a mother Euphemia; in his eighteenth year he became a monk and struggled hard and resolutely, cleansing his heart with many tears, with fasting and with prayer. After ten years he was made bishop of Colonia, and by his own example he drew to a life pleasing to God his brother Pergamius and his uncle Theodore, men of note at the courts of the emperors Zeno and Justinian.

Seeing the malice and the turmoil of this world, which he could not cure, he left the episcopal throne and came disguised as a simple monk to the community of Saint [Sabbas](/saints/sabbas-the-sanctified) near Jerusalem, where for many years he stayed unrecognised, performing conscientiously and excellently every task the abbot set him. Then he was shut up in his cell, and passed year after year in silence and prayer; afterwards he spent nine years in the desert, eating nothing but wild herbs, and returned again to the monastery.

He turned the faithful away from the heresy of Origen and did much towards its being put down and condemned. He saw clearly into the spiritual world and healed people of their sicknesses, and having conquered himself he easily conquered demons. Great in humility, in strength and in the wisdom of God, this servant of Christ reposed in peace in the year 558, in the hundred and fourth year of his life.

The Russian calendar keeps him on the Julian 3 December and its life is much the fullest this folder has. He was born about 454 at Nicopolis in Armenia, the son of a commander named Enkratios and his wife Euphemia, began early on the Scriptures and loved solitude and prayer with his whole heart. With his share of the estate left at his parents’ death he built a church in the name of the Mother of God, and from eighteen he lived beside it with ten monks in fasting, prayer and abstinence; and at the request of the citizens of Colonia the metropolitan of Sebaste ordained him, at twenty-eight, bishop of the church of Colonia. Taking the government of a church he did not change his strict ascetic way of life, and under his influence his own household lived as Christians — his brother Pergamios, a man close to the emperors Zeno and Anastasius, and his nephew Theodore, close to Justinian.

In the tenth year of his episcopate power in Armenia passed to Pazinik, the husband of his sister Maria, who began to interfere in spiritual and church matters with a high hand, and disorders began in the Church. John went to Constantinople and through archbishop Euthymius asked the emperor Zeno to defend the Armenian Church from these rough encroachments. Worn down by the quarrel of the world, he left his see secretly and sailed for Jerusalem, praying with tears that God would show him where to live and be saved; and a bright star appeared and brought him to the Lavra of [Sabbas](/saints/sabbas-the-sanctified).

Hiding his episcopal dignity, he was received among the brethren as a simple novice, and for more than four years did the heaviest work on the building of the guest house and of a monastery for beginners. Seeing his humility and his love of work, Sabbas judged him worthy of ordination to the priesthood, and the secret had to be told to the patriarch of Jerusalem, Elias, who reigned 494 to 517; by the first hierarch’s blessing he took instead a vow of silence, and soon the Lord revealed the secret to Sabbas as well. Four years he spent in his cell receiving nobody and not even going out to church.

Wanting still more solitude he left the Lavra for the desert, where he spent more than nine years living on herbs. There he came through a devastating raid of Saracens, and survived only because God sent him a defender, a fierce lion, at the sight of which enemies who had more than once meant to kill him ran away in fear. When Sabbas came back from a long absence at Scythopolis he persuaded him to leave the desert and settle in the house again, and after that God revealed to all the Lavra’s inhabitants that John was a bishop.

When John was seventy Sabbas died, and he grieved deeply; Sabbas appeared to him in a vision, comforted him and foretold that he would have much to labour in the struggle against heresy — and indeed he had to leave his cell to strengthen the brethren against the Origenists. He spent sixty-six years in that Lavra, and by unresting monastic labour, unsleeping prayer and humility of mind he gained the grace of the Holy Spirit: many wonders were worked at his prayer, people’s secret thoughts were plain to him, he healed the sick and the possessed, in his own lifetime he saved from certain death those who called on his name, and from a fig seed he threw into dry ground a beautiful tree grew and bore fruit. At a hundred and four he departed to the Lord in peace, which is the age the Greek page gives him too.
