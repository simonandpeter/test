# The Twenty Thousand Martyrs (2 June)

saint.gr's calendar for 2 June keeps twenty thousand martyrs in one line and names none of them. What it says of them is short and is all there is: all these saints believed in Christ through Saint Erasmus, whom the same day keeps, and bore witness by the sword. The page adds one thing more, and it is a place a reader can go to: relics of the twenty thousand martyrs are kept at the holy monastery of the Theotokos at Arta.

The couplet the page sets above that sentence counts them as the synaxarion counts: the heads of the contestants, cut through by the sword — you will find twice ten thousand of the fallen. They are not the twenty thousand whom the Romanian calendar keeps on 28 December, who were burnt inside the church at Nicomedia in Maximian's persecution; those have their own folder in this corpus and their own story. These are the company of one preacher's converts, and the number is the only account the source gives of how many that preaching reached.

The Ohrid Prologue is the second book to keep them on this menologion day and the first to give the scene. They are not an entry of its own: they stand inside the life of the hieromartyr Erasmus, which is the day’s fourth and fullest, and the Greek page had already said as much in one sentence. This book tells it out. Erasmus, born at Antioch and long an ascetic on Mount Lebanon, came as a hierarch to preach the Gospel; at Ohrid he raised by his prayer the dead son of a man of the city, baptised him and many other heathen with him, and pulled down the altars of the idols there.

He was accused to the emperor Maximian, who was then in Illyricum, and brought before a brazen image of Zeus and ordered to sacrifice to it. At his word a dreadful dragon came out of the idol and frightened the people, and at his word it perished; and then, the book says, he preached Christ and baptised twenty thousand souls. The embittered emperor gave his order, and all these twenty thousand were cut down by the sword. So the number this folder is kept under is the number of one preaching’s converts, which is what the Greek page said in a line, and the sword is the manner both books give. No year is recorded here: the one year the entry prints is approximate and it is the preacher’s own repose, long after these died.
