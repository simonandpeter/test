# Nicholas the Grocer of Karpenisi

Nicholas was born at Karpenisi of pious parents, who saw to his piety and his schooling; by a tradition kept by word of mouth to this day he was of the Karanikas family. At fifteen he was in Constantinople, serving in his father’s grocery at Tahtakale. A Turkish barber who was teaching him the Turkish language gave him the Muslim profession of faith to read before witnesses, without Nicholas knowing what it was, and when he was told that he was now a Turk he at once confessed his faith in Christ. He was a Christian, he told the judge, he believed Christ to be true God, he had no need of the honours and offices he was promised, and he would die for Christ’s name rather than become a Turk. For that answer he was tortured in prison in the cruellest way, and stood unshaken; on Monday 23 September 1672 he was beheaded, fifteen years old.

His body was buried at the monastery of the Panagia on Halki, and his head was later taken to the monastery of Xeropotamou on Mount Athos; other relics are at Grigoriou and at Tatarna in Evrytania, his jaw at Proussos, and a fragment in his church at Karpenisi. Much of what is known of his martyrdom, the entry says, came from the secretary of the French embassy in Constantinople at the time, who had been struck by the boy’s courage and faith. The Greek calendar keeps him with two apolytikia; the second calls him the offshoot of Kallidromos and the boast of the schools of Eugenius.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps him on 23 September old style as a young man who suffered for the faith of Christ at the hands of the Turks in Constantinople in 1672, where his father had moved from Thessaly; because his father kept a general store he was called the grocer, in Greek Pantopolis. After much pressure to turn Turk, and torture because he would not, he was beheaded; his relics, it says, rest in the monastery of Xeropotamou on the Holy Mountain. Where the Greek calendar has him born at Karpenisi, the Prologue says only that his father came from Thessaly.
