# Abrahamios of Bulgar

saint.gr says the holy new martyr Abrahamios came of Bulgaria and lived in Russia. He was a Muslim at first; when he heard the preaching of the Gospel he embraced the Orthodox faith, and he was a generous man and merciful to the poor. In the city of Bolgar, on the lower reaches of the river Volga, he began to teach his own countrymen about the true God.

For that they seized him and tried to force him to deny Christ. He stayed firm, the page says, in the confession of the piety he had received; and so in the year 1229 they cut him in pieces and then struck off his honourable head. The Russian Christians who lived in the city buried his holy relic in the Christian cemetery.

The translation of the relics followed on 6 March 1230, at the hands of the grand prince of Vladimir, George, whose own day the page gives as 4 February; he laid them in the church of the Dormition of the Mother of God at the Knyatzinin monastery. That is the whole of the page: no year of birth, no hymn, and not even the distich the site usually prints.

The Russian calendar knows where his people were and what he was before. He was born in Kama Bulgaria — the page explains that the Bulgars lived between the Volga and the Kama, and afterwards lost their nationhood little by little and mingled with the Tatars who had conquered them — and was brought up in the Mohammedan faith his countrymen held. He was a man of large means and a trader, given to the vain goods of this world and to the pleasures of the senses, and at the same time hospitable to wanderers and to the poor; and by the grace of God he was convinced of the falsehood of Islam and the truth of Christianity and became a Christian in earnest.

When he came to trade in the great city of Bolgary his own countrymen urged and then forced him to deny Christ, and to their threats he answered no. They shut him in prison for many days, and then, seeing that he would not bend, cut off first his hands, then his feet, and then his head. The lives date his death to 1 April 1229. The Christians who were at the fair — probably merchants of Murom, one of the two readings says — buried him reverently in the Christian cemetery; noticing the healings worked at his grave they reported it to the grand prince, and a year afterwards carried the body to Vladimir, where the prince with his wife and children, the bishop Митрофан, the clergy and the people met the relics and laid them on 6 March 1230 in the church of the Dormition in the Knyaginin monastery. From that time, the shorter reading says, the keeping of his memory began.

One thing more the longer reading sets down. After his death the city of Bolgary burned almost wholly, and the Russian chroniclers say that God sent those fires in punishment for the innocent blood of Abrahamios.
