# Basil (Kolmykov)

Basil Dmitrievich Kolmykov — Kalmykov — was born in 1866 to a merchant
family. On 2 January 1913 he entered the service of the Omsk diocese as
psalmist at the church of the village of Yuzhno-Podolskoye in the
Tyukalinsk district, and on 20 January was ordained deacon; from 5
February 1914 he held the psalmist’s vacancy at the church of the
settlement of Chernovinskoye in the Zmeinogorsk district, and on 12 March
1914 was moved to the church of Chernoluchinskoye in the Tyukalinsk
district. He and his wife brought up an adopted daughter. In 1916 he was
ordained priest and made rector of the church of the village of
Meshchanskoye in the Dzharkent district of the Semirechye region, in
Turkestan, his parish taking in the settlers’ villages round about —
Karkara, Vladislavskoye, Novo-Kievskoye and Okhotnichye. That year the
Kirghiz rising broke out in Semirechye, with thousands of local people
its victims: in August, having finished with the Issyk-Kul country, the
Kirghiz bands turned east of Przhevalsk and on 12 August fell on the
Russian villages of the Dzharkent district, and the priest, saving his
parishioners from the rebels, showed real heroism. He described it in a
letter to Innocent (Pustynsky), bishop of Turkestan and Tashkent: at four
or five in the morning of 12 August he was woken by cries — father, save
yourself, the Kirghiz are surrounding us, they will kill and cut down
everyone — and dressing and going out saw indeed a mass of armed horsemen,
some ten thousand, coming down the hills around with shouts and whoops;
there was a terrible panic, women and children screaming and crying,
senseless running about, but thanks to the good order of the cavalry
captain Michael Kravchenko all was calmed and the attack easily beaten
off. Measures were taken to carry the grain into the stores to feed the
people, and the carrying was laid on him; at his request Kravchenko
detached thirty mounted men to save the people of his parish from a
massacre — had the detachment been ten or fifteen minutes late all would
have been over. The Kirghiz were burning houses, and the prayer-house had
been looted and set alight; he called people, they rushed to put it out,
and the house was saved from final ruin, and everything that could be
saved he packed into a broken chest and took afterwards to Przhevalsk.
When he came to his own house he saw only smoking embers; the church
books, the documents and the money had burned or been stolen, and he and
his family were left in what they stood in — he in a torn cassock with a
summer riassa, his wife in a ragged dress and a thin coat. So many tears
and laments, he wrote; God grant no one hear the like. After these events
he was moved to a new post at the stanitsa of Podgornenskaya. In 1918 a
detachment of Red Army men entered Podgornenskaya; the commissars
arrested the priest, the churchwarden and thirteen other Orthodox
Christians, led them all out beyond the village to the cemetery and shot
them with a machine-gun, having beaten the martyrs first and put sacks
over some of their heads. On pain of death the Red Army men forbade their
burial, setting a machine-gun on the hill facing the cemetery and letting
no one near the bodies; and all the same the Cossacks gave the sufferers
to the earth, digging a grave and laying in it the priest Basil, the
warden Zosima and the thirteen villagers. For more than eighty years the
local people tended the grave; a small chapel was built there in time,
with an unquenchable lamp, and on memorial days above all their fellow
villagers came to pray and light candles, and though in the years of the
Great Patriotic War the Uighur peasants who had settled the place — the
Russians had left the stanitsa and gone back to Russia — took the chapel
apart for firewood, the memory of the martyrs has lasted to our day, and
their grave, ringed with a plain fence and crowned with a worn wooden
cross, has never ceased to be a place of veneration. Bishop Pimen
(Belolikov) of Verny and Semirechye appealed to the Turkestan clergy for
help for the families of those killed. Basil Kolmykov was rehabilitated
under the law of the Republic of Kazakhstan of 14 April 1993, and the
Council of Bishops of 13–16 August 2000 numbered him among the new martyrs
and confessors of Russia for veneration by the whole church; the Russian
calendar keeps him on 3 September (Julian) and in the synaxes of the
saints of Omsk and of Central Asia.
