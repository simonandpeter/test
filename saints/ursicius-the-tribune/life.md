# Ursicius the Tribune

saint.gr says that Ursicius lived in the years of the king Maximian, which it dates 286 to 305, and that he came of the city of Sibendum in Upper Illyria. He was a military man and had risen as far as the rank of tribune. He was denounced to Maximian by a certain Ualens as being a Christian, and was referred to the eparch Aristeides for examination. But Ursicius confessed his Christian faith with courage, and was tortured hard and in many ways.

Because he persisted in confessing Christ, the page ends, he was beheaded, and so received gloriously the crown of martyrdom. The couplet printed above it asks the question the beheading puts: «Oυρσικίου τμηθείσαν ω Θεού Λόγε, / Πώς την κεφαλήν αστεφάνωτον λίπης;» — Word of God, how should you leave the severed head of Ursicius uncrowned? That is the whole of what the Greek calendar says of him; there is no hymn, and this folder records none.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 14 Αυγούστου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/08/14/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/2268/saint.aspx); read 1 October 2026.*
