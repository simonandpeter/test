# Shalva of Ksani

[Bidzina](/saints/bidzina-of-ksani) (Cholokashvili), Shalva and [Elizbar](/saints/elizbar-of-ksani) of Ksani were Georgian princes who freed Kakheti, eastern Georgia, from the Persians, the Russian calendar says. At the demand of Shah Abbas II all three were handed over to him, with the consent of the Georgian king Vakhtang V (1658–1675), who had accepted Islam and was known as Shah-Navaz. Brought bound before the shah, they answered the usual questioning by saying they were Christians, and neither promises, threats nor torture could move them. The shah sent them on to the former ruler of Kakheti, the sultan of Aldaran, then living at Isfahan, who after cruel torments had Elizbar and Shalva beheaded before Bidzina’s eyes. Bidzina he had dressed in women’s clothes for shame and led through the city on a donkey, and when he still did not waver, he was tortured again, his body cut apart at the joints, and at last beheaded. The life dates this to 18 September 1660, and adds that by other accounts they suffered under Shah Safi, Abbas II’s son, in 1664. Their bodies were thrown into the refuse pits outside the city; at night a light shone over them from heaven, and the Armenians of the place carried the relics off and hid them in their church, and later they were taken to Kartli and buried with honour in the Ikorta monastery near Gori.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, keeps Bidzina, a Georgian prince who suffered for the faith of Christ under Shah Abbas II with his kinsmen Elizbar and Shalva, in 1661.
