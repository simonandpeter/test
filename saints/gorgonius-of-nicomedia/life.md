# Gorgonius of Nicomedia

Gorgonius was one of the martyrs of Nicomedia whom the Russian calendar keeps on 3 September (Julian) with their bishop [Anthimus](/saints/anthimus-of-nicomedia): the deacon Theophilus, Dorotheus, Mardonius, Migdonius, Peter, Indes, Gorgonius, Zeno, the virgin Domna and Euthymius, who suffered in the persecution of Diocletian and Maximian, after the fire in the imperial palace at Nicomedia for which the pagans blamed the Christians and took an unheard-of vengeance — in Nicomedia alone up to twenty thousand worshippers were burned in a church on the day of the Nativity. The Sretensky calendar’s life of the twenty thousand names them among the company; it was read only as far as the palace fire.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 28 December, with the eight who suffered beside him. saint.gr's entry for the nine says that they were senators, that they were left outside the church which was burned at Maximian's order in Nicomedia — where the twenty thousand Christians were burned alive — and that they were arrested there. It then divides their deaths: the first three were martyred by drowning in the sea, the next two by beheading, the other two by fire, Theophilus by stoning, and Migdonius was put to death in a pit. Gorgonius was drowned in the sea, being one of the first three of the nine in that reckoning.

The couplet the page sets over him is this: «Πόντος καλύπτει Γοργόνιον και Πέτρον, / Mνήμη δε τούτων ου καλύπτεται χρόνω.» He is named there with [Indes](/saints/indes-of-nicomedia), [Peter](/saints/peter-of-nicomedia), [Zeno](/saints/zeno-of-nicomedia), [Dorotheus the praepositus](/saints/dorotheus-of-nicomedia), [Mardonius](/saints/mardonius-of-nicomedia), [Glycerius the presbyter](/saints/glycerius-of-nicomedia), [Theophilus the deacon](/saints/theophilus-the-deacon-of-nicomedia) and [Migdonius](/saints/migdonius-of-nicomedia), and the company of [the twenty thousand](/saints/twenty-thousand-martyrs-of-nicomedia) they were taken beside. The page gives no year, no judge beyond Maximian and no further circumstance, and it closes by noting that the memory of some of them is kept again on 3 December.
