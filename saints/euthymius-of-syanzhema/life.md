# Euthymius of Syanzhema

saint.gr says Euthymius lived between the fifteenth and the sixteenth century in Russia. He was born in the town the page writes Μπολογκντά, and from a young age he loved the monastic life; soon he became a monk in the monastery of Spaso-Kamensky, near the lake of Kubenskoye. A little later he left that monastery to live as a hermit on the river Kubenka, two kilometres from the lake.

His longing for a greater stillness brought him to the country of the river Syanzhema, where he met [Chariton](/saints/chariton-of-syanzhema). The two of them together founded the monastery of the Ascension of the Saviour at Bologkda, as the page spells it. They built the only church there was in that country, and grew herbs for the very little food they took.

Euthymius fell asleep in peace about the year 1465, the page says — its own word is «περί», about — and Chariton, who succeeded him in the guidance of the monastery, fell asleep in 1509. Both were buried in the church of the monastery, which later became a parish church. Nothing else is told of him: no name in the world, no parents, no elder, and no relic; and the page prints neither couplet nor hymn.
