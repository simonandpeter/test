# Alexander the Presbyter, Hieromartyr (1937)

Alexander Sokolov was born in 1893 in the village of Ozerovo in the Tikhvin district of Novgorod province, the son of the priest Nicholas Sokolov. He finished the Novgorod seminary in 1915 and became psalmist at Yershovo in the Cherepovets district; ordained deacon in 1916 to Morotskoye and priest a year later to Ozerovo, where he had grown up, he was moved in 1924 to Yershovo, where he had once been psalmist. The persecution of the Church took, among other forms, that of taxes and fines beyond all paying, and in 1924 he had to move with his wife Elizaveta and three small children to the Tver region — the Krasny Kholm district first, then the village of Porechye in the Molokovo district. He gave all his strength and time to his flock: the authorities forbade molebens in the fields and in parishioners’ houses, and he went round every village of the parish as priests had before the revolution; his people loved him for it, and though the authorities stripped priest and parish bare, the church at Porechye was repaired whenever it needed repair, and priest and parishioners were harried for it every time. In 1935 the parishioners painted and whitewashed the church and sold the leftover paint to the school; priest and warden were arrested at once, and he was charged with speculation and sentenced to five years in the camps — it was still possible then, if the charge was not political, to prove one’s innocence, and the regional court acquitted him in full. The parishioners built a new brick wall round the chapel. At the end of April 1937 the chairman of the village soviet summoned him and demanded the next quarter’s income tax in advance and rent for the land under the church; don’t waste your strength, he said, it is not yet due, and left without more words. That summer the NKVD, warned of the coming arrests of all the clergy, gathered its evidence from people willing to say anything — that he doubted the confessions at the show trials, that he blamed the shortage of bread on inflated harvest returns, that he had told a joke about Kalinin and the five-year plans.

He was arrested that July, and shot on 29 August 1937. He was glorified at the Jubilee Council of 2000. The Russian calendar keeps him on 16 August (Julian) with the nun [Anna](/saints/anna-nun-martyr-1937) and the martyr [James](/saints/james-martyr-1937).
