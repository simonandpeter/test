# Severian of Sebaste

Severian was martyred in the years of Licinius. He came from Sebaste and was marked by a warm zeal in the service of the Gospel, using his wealth for the poor and for imprisoned Christians; he had brought many to the Christian faith and heartened a good number to bear martyrdom with patience and victory — when the forty martyrs were seized by Agricola, the governor of Sebaste, and thrown into prison, the Romanian life says, this man of noble family, believing in the Lord Jesus Christ, strengthened them for their contest. All this made the bloodthirsty governor Lysias summon him to Caesarea, where, failing to bend his resolve, he had him scourged with ox-sinews and his flesh torn with iron claws until the blood ran like a torrent; the pains were unbearable and the wounds great and heavy, but his patience was stronger, and he cried to the Lord a warm prayer which, the synaxarion says, every struggling Christian should use in time of trial: Lord Jesus, who work wonders, who hung on the cross and so cast down the proud one, who to this day are magnified by works beyond wonder, come and save me, crush the arm of the wicked sinner and brace my own strength, good one, and grant me to run this course of martyrdom to the end. And the help came: his soul stayed firm and victorious, and his body was left hanging on the wall of Caesarea; its verse says that though he ached under the weight of stones he rejoiced to be hung up, as one tearing his feet from the earth. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep him on 9 September, the Romanian with a troparion.
