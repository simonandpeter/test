# Nicholas (Varzhansky), Martyr (1918)

Nicholas was born on 25 November 1881 in the Ovruch district of Volhynia province, the son of the official George Varzhansky. Like many natives of the western provinces of Russia who had felt on themselves all the refinement of the influence of sects and heterodoxy, he strove with his whole heart towards Orthodoxy, understanding that outside it there is no salvation and that if one chooses the service of the people for one’s life’s work it must be bound up with the preaching of Christ’s word; so he entered the seminary and then the Moscow Academy, which he finished in 1907 as a candidate of theology — the calendar’s life runs on past the point read here. An active missionary and fighter against sectarianism, he organised the Barnabas temperance society for workers and published Orthodox literature; after the revolution he went on preaching openly, for which he was arrested, charged with counter-revolutionary activity and held in the Butyrka prison, and was shot in 1918. He was numbered among the saints in 2000, and the Russian calendar keeps him on 23 August (Julian) with Bishop Ephrem and the archpriest John Vostorgov.
