# Sophronius of Vratsa

Stoiko, as he was baptised, was born in 1739 in the village of Kotel in Bulgaria - the life leans on his own autobiography, and calls it beautiful. Orphaned young and tried by every kind of misfortune, he finished the church school, learned Slavonic and Greek, was married against his will, and in 1762 was ordained priest, harried by his own countrymen and by the Turks alike. In 1775 he went to the Holy Mountain and spent six months at Hilandar and elsewhere in meditation and prayer, then returned home to preach and to teach, translating lives of the saints from Greek into Slavonic.

Widowed, he took the monastic schema at Tarnovo under the name Seraphim, and in 1794 was consecrated Bishop of Vratsa, taking the name Sophronius. His episcopate was as tried as the rest of his life - wars, poverty, epidemics - and in 1803 he was forced from his see and moved to Bucharest, where he spent his last years as abbot of a monastery and where he continued writing and translating for his people. He died in peace on 22 or 23 September 1813 and was buried in Bucharest.

The Bulgarian Church numbered him among the saints on 31 December 1964; his memory is kept on 22 September and on 11 March.

The Greek calendar keeps him on 11 March, the second of the two days the Romanian calendar prints for him, and calls him ο Διδάσκαλος, the Teacher, and bishop of Vratsa in Bulgaria. saint.gr's page for him is two sentences long: it gives his years, 1739 to 1813, and records that the synodal act of his glorification was made by the Church of Bulgaria on 31 December 1964. It prints no hymn and names no relic.

The Russian church keeps him on this same day, and the Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery marks him «(Болг.)», a saint of the Bulgarian church, and gives the year of his death as 1813, which is the year this folder already held. It carries no life page for him, so the Romanian account this folder was written from stands unaltered; what the Russian calendar adds is a troparion and a kontakion, which it prints under his name and which are copied here. The troparion's own reading of him is worth saying: armed with the shield of faith, cutting down the godless Hagarenes with the sword of the word, he shepherded his flock on earth.
