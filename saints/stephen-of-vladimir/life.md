# Stephen of Vladimir

Stephen lived in Russia in the eleventh century and carried on his ascetic struggle in the Lavra of Kyiv. After the falling asleep of [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) he became abbot of the holy monastery of the Caves of Kyiv, and in that place he was very active: he took careful thought for the completion of the monastery's buildings, and at the same time for the spiritual growth of the monks. He appointed that the Divine Liturgy should be celebrated daily in the monastery for the repose of the blessed founders and of the brethren who had fallen asleep, and for the salvation of the living brethren and of all Orthodox Christians.

But the treacherous devil envied Stephen's godly zeal, saint.gr says, and stirred some of the brethren up against their own abbot, making a great disturbance in the brotherhood. Stephen withdrew from the abbacy and was driven out of the monastery without cause; and he bore it all without a murmur, without complaint and without holding it against anyone. God so blessed His faithful servant that Stephen was counted worthy to build a new monastery at Klov, with a stone church dedicated to the Deposition of the Honourable Robe of the Most Holy Mother of God.

His virtue drew many devout souls, who came to him and received the monastic habit from his honourable hands, and his fame spread through the whole Russian land. So when the bishop of Vladimir fell asleep in the year 1091, Stephen was consecrated hierarch and his successor at Vladimir by John, metropolitan of Kyiv. He shepherded his flock in a way pleasing to God, and fell asleep in peace in the year 1094.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović begins where the Greek notice does not: he was a disciple of [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) of the Caves. Stephen was for a time abbot of the Lavra of the Caves, and laboured much over the good ordering of the monastic life and over the adorning of the churches. But the enemy kindled malice against him among the monks, so that they not only deposed him from the abbacy but drove him out of the monastery as well.

And God, who does not leave the righteous long under humiliation from the unrighteous, so ordered his life that he was chosen bishop of Vladimir. As God's hierarch he governed the Church until his old age, and fell asleep in the Lord in peace in 1094. **That year agrees exactly with the Greek notice's**, so nothing in this folder's dates was touched; and this book is the first calendar read for him to call him Преподобни, venerable.

The Russian calendar keeps him on this same day, heads him «(1094)» as the Greek page does, and gives the day and even the hour of his death: the twenty-seventh of April 1094, at the sixth hour of the night. Its two readings tell his whole course. He was born to a devout family; when his parents died he gave away all they left him and took the habit in the monastery of the Caves, where he held the office of chief of the order of the services on the cliros. [Theodosius](/saints/theodosius-of-the-kyiv-caves) made him his favourite disciple and sometimes sent him to instruct the brethren with a word of counsel in his own place; and when the end came near the brethren asked for him by name — he grew up under your hand, they said, he has served you, give him to us — and Theodosius put the governing of the house into his hands.

Under him the great church of the Dormition that Theodosius had begun was in the main finished, and the brothers' cells were moved nearer to the new church, a few being left on the old site for the monks charged with burying the dead and serving the liturgy for them every day. Then in 1078 he was deposed from the abbacy and driven out of the house, which the reading lays to the contriving of the demons; and bearing undeserved wrongs quietly, he founded a new monastery on another hill not far off, on the river Klov, in honour of the laying of the robe of the Mother of God at Blachernae and in thanksgiving for her benefits to the monastery of the Caves. For he had seen the Greek masons come with her icon and had heard from them the vision of the Queen of heaven at Blachernae, and it was for that he raised the Klov church in honour of the Blachernae icon.

In 1091 he was consecrated bishop of Vladimir in Volhynia, and he took part in the carrying of the relics of Theodosius out of the cave into the church of the monastery, which this calendar keeps on the fourteenth of August. He laboured much to bring the people of Volhynia to Christianity, and in his three years as bishop he gave his flock an unbroken example of meekness and mercy. The day page prints six hymns under his full style, two troparia called «Ин тропарь» beside the first, and two kontakia called «Ин кондак» beside the first, where no calendar read for this folder before had given him one.
