# Mark the Evangelist

Mark was a Hebrew of the tribe of Levi, a disciple of the apostle Peter and, as the life puts it, his beloved son in the Holy Spirit — the son Peter greets in his letter, «Marcu, fiul meu», a son not after the flesh but after the spirit, born through the good tidings and the font of baptism. He was numbered among the seventy apostles of Christ, and it was Peter who set him a bishop.

The two travelled together as far as Rome, and there, at the asking of the faithful of that city who had already believed through Paul, Mark wrote down the Gospel so that Christ's life among men should not be left unwritten. He showed it first to Peter alone; Peter read it, vouched that it was true, and commanded that all should read it and believe what stood in it.

Peter then sent him to preach, first at Aquileia and afterwards in Egypt, where he was bishop in Alexandria; and the regions about it, Libya and the Pentapolis, he brought out of the darkness of idolatry into the light of the faith. The life quotes at length what Philo the Jew, who had met Peter at Rome, wrote in praise of the Christians Mark had gathered in Alexandria and throughout Egypt — that they gave away their goods, kept houses of prayer where nothing worldly was done, ate only bread and water, salt and hyssop, and that many of their women grew old in virginity, freely and not by constraint.

He was seized on his feast day in Alexandria, on the twenty-fifth of April, while he was serving the divine liturgy, the same day the Greeks kept the unclean feast of their god Serapis. They tied a rope about his neck and dragged him over sharp stones, crying that they were taking the ox to the place of oxen, and his blood reddened the road. That evening they threw him into prison; at midnight an earthquake came, and an angel told him his name was written in the book of life and his relics would be kept on earth. Afterwards the Lord Himself stood before him as He had stood before His disciples, and said, «Pace ție, Evanghelistul Meu» — peace to thee, My Evangelist.

In the morning they dragged him out again over the same stones, and saying «Into Thy hands, Lord, I give up my spirit», he died. They made a fire to burn his body, and darkness, thunder, an earthquake and a great rain scattered them; the faithful took up the body and laid it with honour in a stone tomb to the east of his place of prayer, having nothing more precious than his relics. The life gives no year for his death, dating it only by the reign of Nero at Rome.

A second Mark of the Seventy, the one set bishop in Byblos, stands two days later in the same Romanian calendar, on 27 April; the Greek synaxarion is careful to say that he is not the Evangelist, and this corpus keeps the two apart.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, with a rule of its own for the years when the day falls before Pascha: saint.gr heads his page «Ειδικός υπολογισμός. Εορτάζει στις 25 Απριλίου εκάστου έτους. Εάν όμως η εορτή πέσει πρίν το Πάσχα, τότε εορτάζει την Τρίτη της Δικαινησίμου» — kept on 25 April every year, but if the feast should fall before Pascha, then on Bright Tuesday. Its notice sets out his kindred and his names. He was the nephew of the apostle Barnabas and his mother was called Mary; his origin was rather from Cyprus, though he settled later at Jerusalem. The evangelist's Jewish name was John, and Mark was his Roman surname, taken by the custom that then prevailed — and it is by that name that he stayed known in the Christian world.

Mark entered the service of the Church very early, accompanying his uncle Barnabas and the apostle Paul on their various journeys, and he also worked a long while beside the apostle Peter. According to the tradition he preached the Gospel in Egypt, in Libya and in Barbary, and had been the first bishop of Alexandria. On the same day the Greek calendar keeps the man who followed him on that throne: [Annianus](/saints/annianus-of-alexandria), the pagan shoemaker of Alexandria whose wounded hand Mark healed with clay made from his spittle, and whom he then baptized. The page gives no year for either of them; its couplet says that the murderers, dragging Mark upon the ground, did not know that they were sending him to the heavens.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells the Egyptian mission at more length than either of the calendars read for this folder before. It was Peter who consecrated him bishop and sent him to preach in Egypt, so that he was both the first preacher of the Gospel and the first bishop in that country; and the whole of it, the book says, lay pressed under a thick darkness of heathenism, idolatry, soothsaying and malice. With God's help he sowed the seed of Christ's teaching through Libya, Ammonica and the Pentapolis, and out of the Pentapolis the Spirit of God led him to Alexandria.

There he founded the Church of God, appointed for it a bishop, priests and deacons, and settled everything well in the devout faith, confirming his preaching with great and many wonders. When the heathen laid charges against him as the destroyer of their idol-worship and the governor of the city began to hunt for him, he fled back to the Pentapolis and went on with the work he had begun there. **Two years later he returned to Alexandria**, to the great joy of all the faithful, whose number had by then much increased.

On that visit the heathen caught him, bound him fast and began to haul him over the cobblestones, shouting „повуцимо вола у обор!“ — let us drag the ox to the pen. Wounded and covered in blood, he was thrown into prison, where an angel of heaven appeared to him first, to hearten and strengthen him, and afterwards the Lord Jesus Himself, who said „Мир Теби, Марко, јеванђелисте мој!“ — peace to thee, Mark, my evangelist. To that Mark answered „Мир и Теби Господе мој Исусе Христе!“ — and peace to Thee also, my Lord Jesus Christ.

The next day the same men dragged him through the streets with the same cry, until, worn out and spent, he said „у руке Твоје, Господе, предајем дух мој!“ — into Thy hands, Lord, I give up my spirit — and so breathed his last and passed in soul to a better world. The Christians buried his holy relics with honour, and through the centuries they have given men healing from every torment and every sickness. **This book gives no year either**, so the folder stands undated as the other two calendars left it.

The Russian calendar is the one of the four that gives a year. Its fullest reading sets the martyrdom and the burial in a stone tomb at «4 апреля 63 года», and says that the Church keeps his memory on the twenty-fifth of April. It tells the Egyptian mission at more length than the others again. The house of his mother adjoined the garden of Gethsemane, and after the Ascension it became the place where the Christians gathered to pray and the lodging of some of the apostles; he went through Cyprus from east to west and was at Paphos; and he wrote the Gospel at Rome about the years 62 and 63.

Back at Alexandria he laid the beginnings of the Christian school out of which, this reading says, the famous fathers and teachers of that church afterwards came, and being zealous for the ordering of the services he drew up the form of the liturgy for the Christians of the city. He lodged in the house of a cobbler whose diseased hand he healed, who was baptized with joy, and after whom many in that quarter of the city were baptized too; and it was that which stirred the hatred of the heathen, so that before they fell upon him he set the cobbler bishop and three of the Christians presbyters. A church was built over his relics in the year 310; and in 820, when the power of the Arabs was established in Egypt and the Church was pressed hard, the relics were carried to Venice and set in the temple of his name.

The same reading accounts for the lion that stands beside him in the old iconography, which took for each of the evangelists a symbol out of the vision of the Apocalypse, and reads the lion as the mark of the might and the royal dignity of Christ. It holds that he wrote his Gospel for Christians out of the heathen, and therefore dwells by preference on the words and deeds of the Saviour in which His divine omnipotence shows most plainly; that much in his telling is explained by how close he stood to the chief of the apostles, every ancient writer bearing witness that this Gospel is the short record of that man’s preaching and stories; and that one of its central themes is the power of God made perfect in human weakness, since the Lord makes possible what among men is impossible.
