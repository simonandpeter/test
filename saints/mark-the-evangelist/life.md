# Mark the Evangelist

Mark was a Hebrew of the tribe of Levi, a disciple of the apostle Peter and, as the life puts it, his beloved son in the Holy Spirit — the son Peter greets in his letter, «Marcu, fiul meu», a son not after the flesh but after the spirit, born through the good tidings and the font of baptism. He was numbered among the seventy apostles of Christ, and it was Peter who set him a bishop.

The two travelled together as far as Rome, and there, at the asking of the faithful of that city who had already believed through Paul, Mark wrote down the Gospel so that Christ's life among men should not be left unwritten. He showed it first to Peter alone; Peter read it, vouched that it was true, and commanded that all should read it and believe what stood in it.

Peter then sent him to preach, first at Aquileia and afterwards in Egypt, where he was bishop in Alexandria; and the regions about it, Libya and the Pentapolis, he brought out of the darkness of idolatry into the light of the faith. The life quotes at length what Philo the Jew, who had met Peter at Rome, wrote in praise of the Christians Mark had gathered in Alexandria and throughout Egypt — that they gave away their goods, kept houses of prayer where nothing worldly was done, ate only bread and water, salt and hyssop, and that many of their women grew old in virginity, freely and not by constraint.

He was seized on his feast day in Alexandria, on the twenty-fifth of April, while he was serving the divine liturgy, the same day the Greeks kept the unclean feast of their god Serapis. They tied a rope about his neck and dragged him over sharp stones, crying that they were taking the ox to the place of oxen, and his blood reddened the road. That evening they threw him into prison; at midnight an earthquake came, and an angel told him his name was written in the book of life and his relics would be kept on earth. Afterwards the Lord Himself stood before him as He had stood before His disciples, and said, «Pace ție, Evanghelistul Meu» — peace to thee, My Evangelist.

In the morning they dragged him out again over the same stones, and saying «Into Thy hands, Lord, I give up my spirit», he died. They made a fire to burn his body, and darkness, thunder, an earthquake and a great rain scattered them; the faithful took up the body and laid it with honour in a stone tomb to the east of his place of prayer, having nothing more precious than his relics. The life gives no year for his death, dating it only by the reign of Nero at Rome.

A second Mark of the Seventy, the one set bishop in Byblos, stands two days later in the same Romanian calendar, on 27 April; the Greek synaxarion is careful to say that he is not the Evangelist, and this corpus keeps the two apart.
