# Nilus of Athos the Myrrh-streaming

The page doxologia.ro prints for him is short, and this is what it says. He was born at Morea; and being already a hieromonk he went with his uncle to the Holy Mountain of Athos, and there struggled to the end of his days as a recluse in a mountainous and desert place that was called «Pietrele Sfinte», the Holy Stones. The page places his departure to the Lord in the seventeenth century. It names neither his uncle nor his parents, and gives no year.

When he had passed to the Lord, myrrh flowed from his holy relics — a stream that ran down from the summit of that mountain and poured into the sea. The page calls the wonder one never seen before, and says it brought the gravely ill to the place from everywhere. Their numbers so disturbed the stillness of one of his disciples that, at that disciple's prayer to the saint, the myrrh ceased at once to flow.

Doxologia ends by setting him among the great ascetics of the world, like the great desert fathers and wonderworkers of the first Christian ages. Its page for him carries no Tropar tab, so the calendar gives him no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 12 November, and its notice is two sentences: Nilus came from Kynouria and practised ascesis at the Great Lavra of the Holy Mountain; he died in peace, and later his holy relic welled up myrrh. Its head line puts his death in 1651, and for more it sends the reader to 7 May, where the finding of his honoured relics is kept. The page also says where parts of him are now: his skull at the Great Lavra on the Holy Mountain, part of his arm at the kyriakon of the skete of Kausokalyvia, and a fragment of his relic at the monastery of the Panagia of Malevi in Kynouria, his own country. The rest of what it prints is the history of the church built in his honour at Hatzikyriakeio in Piraeus between 1928 and 1931, which is a dedication and not part of his life.

The Russian calendar's life gives the Greek village and the uncle the other calendars read here left out. He was born in Greece, in a settlement bearing the name of «святого Петра» in the diocese the page spells Законийская, and was brought up by his uncle, a hieromonk the life names «Макарий». Coming of age he was tonsured a monk, and was ordained hierodeacon and then hieromonk. A wish for greater labours brought uncle and nephew together to Athos, where they lived at a place called «Святые Камни».

When his uncle fell asleep he climbed higher still and settled in a place almost unreachable by any living creature. After his departure to the Lord he was glorified by an abundant flowing of healing myrrh, for which Christians came from the furthest countries of the East. The day page carries a troparion and a kontakion to him — both of them headed «в честь обретения мощей», in honour of the finding of his relics, which is his own 7 мая feast — and they are now in this folder with the English typed here.

The Ohrid Prologue gives him an origin, an uncle and a wonder that was switched off. He was of the Morea by birth, and came to the Holy Mountain as a hieromonk with his uncle; he struggled as a hermit in a desert place called the Holy Rocks. When he reposed, myrrh flowed out of him in such abundance that it ran down from the top of the mountain into the sea, and that wonder-working myrrh drew sick people from every quarter.

Then the sentence that is the whole of the entry's point: his own disciple grew weary of the multitude of visitors and complained in prayer against his spiritual father — and the flowing of the myrrh was cut off at once. He struggled, it says, in the full sense of the ancient saints, and reposed in the seventeenth century. That century is the one the 1651 this folder already holds sits in, so nothing is widened and nothing is closed.
