# Hermogenes of Alexandria

doxologia.ro prints one life for the three martyrs of 10 decembrie, and this is it. Diocletian and Maximian had laid down the sceptre, and the persecution went on in the East under Maximinus, a hot partisan of his fathers' many gods. Word came to him that Alexandria was in an uproar, the Christians there having grown very many, and since he had to stay at Byzantium he sent one of his officers to quiet the city, put down the Christian name and bring back to the old worship those who had left it. The man he sent was [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian), an Athenian by birth, a good rhetor and skilled in Greek learning — and secretly a Christian, who had hidden his faith until the call should come.

He reached Alexandria and quieted the trouble without labour, making peace between Christians and pagans and ordering that each should keep his own faith unhindered; then, having set the city's affairs in order and written to the emperor, he decided to bring his own faith into the open. If I give myself to the torments another time, he said in himself, I alone am crowned; now I can bring others to the crown with me. So he praised the name of Jesus Christ openly and taught the faith, and healed the lame, the blind, the dumb, the deaf and the possessed in the middle of the city by calling on the Name and making the sign of the Cross, and many believed. Those who loved the old feasts sent secretly to the emperor to say what he was doing.

The emperor, in anger, sent the eparch of the city, Hermogenes — also an Athenian, brought up in the worship of the idols but of a kind and merciful disposition — to set right what had been overturned and to bring the officer back to the gods. He sat in judgement instead, and [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian) answered him out of the Scriptures, comparing the Christian writings with the Greek learning; and the people listened. When the crowd grew bold the judge had him bound and tortured, and had his tongue cut out, and then his eyes put out, and threw him back into the prison, saying he would give his body to the birds in the morning.

At the third hour of the night a light like lightning shone in the prison, and Christ stood by him, healed his tongue, gave him back his eyes, made him whole, and told him that the judge who warred against him would be humbled before him by morning and would afterwards be his companion in the struggle, because it was not right that so many good works should perish for want of knowledge.

So it came about. The judge, seeing him whole in the middle of the theatre, was struck with fear and then with belief, and would have fallen at his feet; thirteen bishops gathered from the country round and out of the desert and baptised him before the people with three immersions, and a great multitude was baptised with him. Within a few days Hermogenes was chosen bishop of Alexandria, gave all he had to the poor, pulled down the pagan temples and built churches in their place, and brought a countless number of Greeks to Christ. A hardened Greek named Rusticus carried the news to the emperor, who came himself to Alexandria in a fury.

He tormented them both, and both were preserved; and when they appeared alive and whole before him in the theatre the whole people cried out with one voice that the God of the Christians is truly great. Then [Eugraphus](/saints/eugraphus), a man of Greek learning who had been one of the notaries under [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian) when he was judge of the city, was filled with zeal at the sight, signed himself with the Cross, stepped out into the middle and told the emperor to his face that he too was a Christian, that dying for Christ was life, and that they laughed at him as at an old fox. The emperor snatched a sword from one of the men standing by him and cut him down with his own hand, hacking him to pieces; and he gave thanks as he was cut that he went to God before the others and by many wounds rather than one.

Then, since many were touching the other two with their hands and finding their bodies whole and their wounds healed, the emperor gave up and ordered their heads struck off. They prayed at the place of execution for peace to the churches and that no one who asked their help should go away empty, gave one another the kiss of peace and stretched out their necks. [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian), while still alive, had asked that his body be carried to Byzantium.

The emperor had an iron coffin made, put the bodies in it and threw it into the sea so that Christians should not honour them; but the iron did not sink, and went to Byzantium ahead of him. The bishop of Constantinople, warned in a vision, came down to the shore by night with his clergy and saw a pillar of light and two shining men drawing the coffin to land, and buried the relics by the city wall, to be a guard to those who sail and physicians to the sick. Maximinus went blind on his road, said he had been beaten by unseen hands, and died a few days after.

Of the three, Hermogenes is the eparch who judged and tortured Menas and was converted by what he saw, baptised before the people by thirteen bishops, chosen bishop of Alexandria within a few days, and beheaded with Menas by the emperor he had once served. doxologia.ro's line for the day names him «Sfântul Mucenic Ermoghen» and gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 10 December, with the two he suffered beside, and gives his part in the story. When the emperor had sent [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian) to Alexandria as eparch without knowing he was a Christian, and Menas had refused to carry out the persecution, saint.gr says, the emperor sent a new eparch in his place: the Athenian scholar Hermogenes.

He, keeping to the letter of the law, tortured Menas hard and shut him in prison so that he should die there of his wounds. After a time he sent to find out whether and when Menas had died, and it was found that he had not died at all and that his wounds were healed. Then he asked him publicly how this had come about, and Menas answered that he was healed at the hour when, lying on the ground, he was chanting the psalm: though I walk in the midst of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me, Lord.

That answer, the page says, made a Christian of Hermogenes, and of [Eugraphus](/saints/eugraphus) who was Menas's secretary; and afterwards all of them together were beheaded. His own couplet says that Hermogenes, spitting out impiety, became a martyr of true religion by the sword. The page does not call him a bishop and does not say where he was from beyond Athens.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the three of them on the same menologion day under one heading, and it is the only one of the calendars to say who he was before his baptism. He was an Athenian by birth, like [Menas](/saints/menas-the-athenian), and lived at Constantinople in great honour with the emperor and with the people; he was eparch of Constantinople, and held to paganism outwardly and inwardly, though he was merciful and did many good works.

When Menas, sent to Alexandria to put Christianity down, declared himself a Christian there instead and converted many, the emperor Maximinus sent him to punish the man and finish the work. He brought Menas to trial, cut off his feet and his tongue, put out his eyes and threw him into prison. In that prison the Lord Jesus appeared to Menas and healed him; and seeing him wonderfully whole, Hermogenes was baptised and began to preach the mighty faith of Christ, and was made bishop in Alexandria — the see this folder is named for.

Then Maximinus came to Alexandria himself in a fury and put the two of them to great torments, which they bore bravely by the grace of God; and when [Eugraphus](/saints/eugraphus), Menas's secretary, cried out before the court that he too was a Christian and was cut down on the spot, the emperor ordered the executioners to behead these two with the sword. Their relics were thrown into the sea and came by a wonder to Constantinople, where the bishop, warned in a dream, met them in state and buried them honourably.
