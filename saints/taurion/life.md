# Taurion

Taurion was martyred at Amphipolis of Macedonia, which saint.gr puts near Kavala, together with Auctus and with Thessalonike, the daughter of the idol-priest Kleon, whose passion the page tells first: she would not deny Christ at her father's begging, was scourged by four men with whips of raw hide, had her ribs broken and her property taken, and was exiled, and died in exile confessing Christ.

Taurion and Auctus then accused her brutal torturer and murderer, and were for that denounced to the consular Thorybios. He ordered them stoned and then put to a series of horrible tortures; and when the two came through all of it whole and unharmed in a wonderful way, he ordered them beheaded, and so they took the glorious crowns of martyrdom.

The couplet for the pair makes bulls of them, on his name: «Ἐφεῦρον Αὖκτος, Ταυρίων εὐκτὸν τέλος, / Ξίφει τμηθέντες, οἷα ταῦροι Κυρίου» — Auctus and Taurion found the end they prayed for, cut down by the sword like bulls of the Lord. The page gives no year and prints no hymn. Kept with him are [Auctus](/saints/auctus) and [Thessalonike](/saints/thessalonike).

days.pravoslavie.ru keeps the three of them together on the same day the Greek calendar does. Its line for 7 ноября ст. ст. is «Мчч. Авкта , Тавриона и Фессалоникии (739).», and the name link behind «Тавриона» gives the calendar's own nominative, «Таврион Амфипольский (Македонский), мч.» — of Amphipolis and of Macedonia, which is where the life buries all three.

The life behind the three name links is one paragraph and is the same for all three. Thessalonike was the daughter of a pagan priest. When her impious father learned that his daughter had become a Christian he beat her without pity and drove her out of the house, leaving her no means of living at all. Auctus and Taurion tried to stand up for the Christian woman and to bring the hardened father to his senses; the priest denounced them and they were seized. Having confessed their faith in Christ before their torturers and borne cruel torments, they were beheaded. Soon after their martyrdom Thessalonike died too, and her body was buried with honour in the city of Amphipolis in Macedonia together with the martyrs Auctus and Taurion.

**The line prints a year nothing else gives these three.** It closes «(739)», and that figure is now the only date this folder carries; the life gives no year, names no emperor and no persecutor but the father, and saint.gr's line «Άγιοι Αύκτος, Ταυρίων και Θεσσαλονίκη» gave them no year either. It is recorded because the calendar prints it, and the date's note says that it stands alone.

The day page prints a troparion of tone 4 under the names of two of the three, «Тропарь мучеников Авкта и Тавриона», and it names Taurion and Auctus and not Thessalonike. It is kept in this folder with the day's common of martyrs.
