# David the Great Komnenos

saint.gr's calendar for 1 Νοεμβρίου keeps «Άγιος Δαβίδ ο Μέγας Κομνηνός ο νεομάρτυρας και η συνοδεία του Βασίλειος, Γεώργιος, Μανουήλ οι υιοί του και Αλέξιος ο ανεψιός του». On 15 August 1461, the page begins, Mehmet the Conqueror took the capital of the Great Komnenoi, Trebizond, after a hard siege: the last strong rampart of Orthodoxy and of Greek life in the East stopped giving light, the empire was put out, and the Greeks of Pontos lived through hard and critical hours.

The last emperor, David the Great Komnenos, a hostage in the Conqueror's hands together with his three children and his nephew and heir [Alexios](/saints/alexios-nephew-of-david-komnenos), was moved to Adrianople; the saving of Trebizond was by then impossible. The page is careful about the blame. It says that the savagery of the fall was certainly less because the city capitulated, that it is the custom to lay every catastrophe on the rulers alone, and that whoever has the misfortune to govern in such last hours becomes the ready-made culprit, the coward, the traitor — and that David was that double victim.

For his end the page quotes the anonymous synaxarion of the nation twice. On 26 March 1463, a Saturday, at the third hour, the holy lord and emperor of Trebizond, David the Great Komnenos, was seized at Adrianople and shut up in chains in the Tower. And: on the first of November, a Sunday, at the fourth hour of the night, he was finished by the sword together with his three sons and his nephew, in the year 1463, at Constantinople. Both notes stand in a parchment gospel-book of the monastery of the Theotokos on the island of Halki, now in the sacristy of the Ecumenical Patriarchate.

When David was brought before Mehmet, the page says, he was offered one of two things: to stay alive if he renounced his faith, or that he and his whole family should be put to death. Out of that terrible offer David chose the second, saying to Mehmet with boldness that no torment would bring him to the point of renouncing the faith of his fathers. So he passed into eternity, exchanging the imperial purple for the halo of a martyr; and all those who have dealt with the tragic end of the empire of Trebizond, the page adds, rank him in the choir of the martyrs, expressing the common conscience of the body of the Church. His three sons, [Basil](/saints/basil-son-of-david-komnenos), [George](/saints/george-son-of-david-komnenos) and [Manuel](/saints/manuel-son-of-david-komnenos), are kept with him on this day.
