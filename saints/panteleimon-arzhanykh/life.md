# Panteleimon (Arzhanykh)

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar gives him a line of his own on the Julian 16 November, «Прмч. Пантелеимона (1937).», and prints no life page behind it; his name page carries a title and a list of days and no text, so the life here is the one the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru prints. He was born on 29 June 1872 in the village of Zalipayevka in the Maloarkhangelsk district of the province of Oryol, the son of the peasant Timothy Arzhanykh, and was called Paul at his baptism. The household was poor and his father had to work hard to keep any kind of footing; from twelve years old Paul helped him, herding in the village, working for the landowner and hiring himself out to the peasants. When he could, he went to pray at Optina.

In 1894 he was called up and enrolled a private in the Kamchatka regiment. After a year’s service he was sent on the courses for medical orderlies and on finishing them was made orderly to his company. He was discharged in 1898, took a post as an orderly in the provincial hospital at Zhitomir and studied at the same time in the Volhynian school for orderlies. When he had finished the school he went back to his own village and lived half a year at home, turning over which way to go next — the more so, the page says, as he had reached the age at which serious mistakes can and should be avoided. He had no family. The strict life of a monastery did not frighten him: four years in the army had accustomed him to discipline.

In 1900 he went to Optina and was about seven years a novice there, after which the superior of the monastery, the archimandrite Xenophon, tonsured him to the mantle with the name Panteleimon, after the great-martyr and healer of that name, in view of his earthly trade of healing — foreshadowing, says the page, what he was to become. On 15 March 1909 Veniamin (Muratovsky), bishop of Kaluga and Borovsk, ordained him hierodeacon, and in 1911 he was ordained hieromonk. In the monastery his obedience was that of the orderly in its hospital. In 1914 he was chosen treasurer, and he held that office until the godless closed the house in 1918.

For a time he worked in the stud-farm and the museum the authorities set up on the monastery’s ground, and then served in the churches at Likhvin, Meshchovsk and Odoyev. In 1925 he came to Kozelsk and began to serve in the church of St Nicholas. On 26 June 1926 Ioasaf (Shishkovsky-Drylevsky), bishop of Maloyaroslavets and vicar of the Kaluga diocese, raised him from hieromonk to igumen and appointed him superior of the St George monastery at Meshchovsk; but the Soviet power had closed that monastery, so he went back to Kozelsk and to the church of St Nicholas.

About a hundred monks and nuns from the closed houses of Optina and Shamordino were then living in Kozelsk. They made a monastery without walls, scattered through the town, several of them settled in each flat that served as a cell, gathering in the church for services that differed little from the monastery’s. On the day of the Spirit, 9 June 1930, the Kozelsk fair drew some thousands of people, and there was a collision between peasants and the militia in which a peasant was killed. The authorities took the occasion to arrest some of the monks on a charge of inciting the riot: on 18 August 1930 forty people were arrested, monks and laymen, and he was among them, charged with leading a monastic-monarchist group said to aim at restoring the monarchy.

All of them were sent to the prison at Sukhinichi. When the charge was put to him he answered that he had been the organiser and leader neither of an underground monastery nor of a counter-revolutionary monarchist group in Kozelsk, that he had given nobody directions of that kind and taken no part in any such thing, and that there was and had been no underground monastery there at all: the gathering of monastics in the town he put down to the nearness of Optina and Shamordino after their liquidation, and to the elders who lived there, and not to anyone drawing them in on purpose. On 27 November 1930 the troika of the ОГПУ sentenced him to ten years, afterwards cut to five; he was sent first to the Vishera camp and then into exile at Yelets, and when the term ran out in 1935 he took a place as psalmist in the church of the Entry there.

The persecution of 1937 found him in that church. He was arrested on 16 September 1937, shut in the prison at Lipetsk and questioned the next day; asked whether he admitted the charge, he said he did not. On 30 October the investigator questioned the witnesses on duty. One of them said that on 14 May 1937 he had come into the church of the Entry on his way home from work, that vespers was being sung, and that at the end of it the igumen preached: that the day was a day of grief given to [Peter (Polyansky)](/saints/peter-polyansky), the metropolitan of Krutitsy, tortured to death in exile by the enemies of the Russian Church, who had first urged him to give up God and the Orthodox faith and, when he refused, tormented him until he died; and that he had showed how a true Christian must live and fight, and the faithful should be as firm as he was.

Called again after those depositions and asked again whether he admitted counter-revolutionary activity, he answered again that he did not and that he had carried on none. On 15 November 1937 the troika of the НКВД sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on 29 November 1937 — the civil day on which the Julian 16 November of that year fell, so that the day of his death and his feast are one day — and buried in a common unmarked grave.
