# Nicholas of Iskrovka

Nicholas served as priest of the village of Iskrovka, in the Yelisavetgrad country, whose church, the Russian church calendar at azbyka.ru says, was built on the personal initiative and at the expense of the emperor Nicholas II. Early in the twentieth century the villagers asked the tsar for land for a church, since Pan Victor, who owned it, wanted a high price; the emperor granted the land and, learning that there was neither money nor a plan, himself paid for bricks sent to Ryadovaya, the nearest station, for their carriage and for the builders, and came to the consecration dressed as a peasant. Afterwards, in agreement with Father [John of Kronstadt](/saints/john-of-kronstadt), he proposed a new priest for the church, and so Nicholas, a deacon of St Isaac’s cathedral in St Petersburg, came to Iskrovka.

He was cheerful and fond of a joke, even when rebuking sin, and known for foresight, for healing and for prayer. He told his wife Anna that he would be killed and buried three times and that she would hide under two surnames, and his son Dmitry that he would be a priest with a large family, and the life says all of it came true. He read prayers over the possessed and the sick, at his spring in summer and in the church in winter, and people came to him from as far as Tula. He foretold the persecution and his own death: the priest would be plucked off like a flower, and the church would stand.

On 2 October 1919 he served the Liturgy, sent the people home and stayed in the bell tower; in the afternoon a Red detachment dragged him down by the hair — the calendar’s summary says they threw him from the tower — beat and mocked him, took him to the cemetery and shot him. Women coming in from the fields found the body and covered it with earth and branches, and it was then buried a second time elsewhere; in 1920 many priests came and buried him a third time behind the altar of the church, and witnesses said the body was incorrupt, as if he had been killed that day.

His memory was kept for eighty years at his grave and his spring, where the life records healings. The Holy Synod resolved on 17 July 2001 to glorify him, and on 17 September 2001 his relics were uncovered; they lie in a glass shrine in the church of the Exaltation of the Cross at Iskrovka, in the Kirovohrad region of Ukraine. The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 September old style.
