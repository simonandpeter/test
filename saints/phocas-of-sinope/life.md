# Phocas of Sinope

Phocas was born in Sinope on the Black Sea, the son of Pamphilus and Maria, and was full of the gift of the Holy Spirit from his youth: he drove demons out of the possessed and healed the sick. For the goodness of his life the city chose him bishop, and he shepherded it in word and in deed, turning many from their error and bringing pagans from the idols to the knowledge of the one God.

His end was announced to him in a vision: a dove flew down with a crown of flowers in its beak, set it on his head, and said with a human voice, "Now your cup is full, and it must be drunk." He understood, and did not flee. The life the Romanian synaxarion keeps tells of his imprisonment - lit with heavenly light in his chains, comforted by angels in his griefs - and of torments of many kinds, until he was thrown into a burning bath-house, where he gave up his spirit in prayer. The faithful buried his body with honour, and many miracles are worked at his tomb.

The Greek calendar names his father Pamphilus a shipbuilder, and says that from a child he steeped himself in the Scriptures and was marked above all by a warm and sincere love of God and of his neighbour, after John’s word that he who loves his brother abides in the light. As bishop of Sinope, with the gift of working wonders in the name of the Trinity, he preached so actively through his diocese that it did not escape the zealous pagans: the prefect Africanus had him arrested and thrown into a scalding bath, where he gave up his spirit, in the years of the emperor Trajan (98–117). Its verse calls the bath one of contest, not of cleansing. Part of his head, it says, is at the monastery of Xenophontos on the Holy Mountain, and pieces of his relics at Stavronikita and at Panagia Soumela in Vermio. The Greek calendar keeps him on 22 September with an apolytikion and a kontakion.
