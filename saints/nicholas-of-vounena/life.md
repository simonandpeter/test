# Nicholas of Vounena

He was born in the parts of the East, saint.gr says, of pious and virtuous parents, and when he came of age he was enrolled in the imperial army with the rank of duke. He drilled his soldiers to be brave and fearless fighters, but above all he admonished and taught them to believe in God, to pray, never to wrong anyone, and to ask Christ for strength to fight their enemies. The enemies were not only foreign: risings broke out inside the empire too, and Nicholas was sent by the emperor Leo the Isaurian, the Iconoclast, to put one down and to bring back peace and stability.

In that campaign he saw human blood shed unjustly and souls lost, and fearing that he might lose not his life only but his soul, he withdrew with twelve of his soldiers to Vounena in Thessaly, where a good many ascetics lived; and among them they lived an ascetic life of fasting, vigils and unceasing prayer. When the Avars came down into Thessaly, scattering death and ruin in their barbarous passage, they seized Nicholas and his soldiers and slaughtered the soldiers. Nicholas they tried with promises and flatteries to persuade to deny his faith, and could not. They ran him through with his own spear, and then cut off his head, on the ninth of May in the year 720, and his young blood watered the Thessalian earth abundantly.

The Avars left his body where they had killed him, and after many years a nobleman named Euphemianus found it whole and fragrant; he was healed of the leprosy he suffered from, and having buried the body he built a church there in the venerable martyr's name. From the trees to which the saint was tied and tortured there flows a red liquid which is called «Αίμα», the Blood; used with faith and trust in the venerable martyr it has healing properties, the page says, and works wonders for those who suffer from diseases of the skin and from headache. The twelve soldiers killed with him are not named.
