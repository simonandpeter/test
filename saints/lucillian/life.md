# Lucillian

Lucillian lived in the days of Aurelian, emperor of Rome. He had been a pagan and indeed a servant of the idols, an old man, honourable in face and white-haired, who lived near the city of Nicomedia and served in the temples of the unclean gods. When he was enlightened by holy baptism with the grace of Christ, who wishes no one to perish but all to be saved, he understood the deceit of the devil and the error of the heathen, came to the knowledge of the truth, and believed in the one true God, our Lord Jesus Christ, casting off the idols and spitting on them.

So in old age his youth was renewed like the eagle's, and he clung with his whole mind and soul to the love of Christ. He showed the other pagans the emptiness and ruin of their uncleanness and led them towards salvation, bringing many to Christ by his teaching and being to many an example of turning to God. The Jews living there, doxologia's life says, seeing such a change in him, and seeing that many by his example and teaching left the service of idols and joined the Christians in baptism, were filled with anger and hatred, denounced him and gave him over to the judgement of the pagans.

He was brought for examination before Silvanus, then count in Nicomedia, who pressed the old man hard to deny Christ and go back to the service of the idols, and could not move him at all. In anger Silvanus ordered him tormented in many ways: they broke his jaws, beat him without mercy with rods, and hung him head downwards; and after many cruel torments they threw him into prison.

There Lucillian found four young men thrown in for their faith in Christ: [Claudius](/saints/claudius-companion-of-lucillian), [Hypatius](/saints/hypatius-companion-of-lucillian), [Paul](/saints/paul-companion-of-lucillian) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-lucillian). He rejoiced over them, and spoke with them of Christ and strengthened them for the martyr's contest: not to fear passing torments, for the everlasting reward in heaven; not to be afraid of death, for the life that is to come; and not to spare the flower of their youth for Christ, who prepares for them in His kingdom a happiness that does not grow old. So they prayed together day and night and comforted one another in the hope of Christ.

After some days Lucillian was tormented again with the young men and thrown with them into a heated furnace. God poured out His wonderful mercy on them as He did of old on the Hebrew children in the furnace of Babylon: the fire turned to coolness and the flame to dew, and then a great rain came down and quenched the furnace altogether, and they came out unhurt. The pagans, blinded by unbelief and malice, set so glorious a miracle down not to the power of God but to Christian sorcery; and the unjust judge condemned the martyrs to death and sent them to Byzantium to take their punishment there.

When they came to Byzantium the four young men were cut down with the sword; and Lucillian they hung upon a cross and drove nails through his whole body, and he gave up his soul into the hands of God. That he was crucified by the Jews, the life adds, is shown in the third ode of the canon, where the verse runs: Judas of old sold Christ the Deliverer to the killers of God, and you now have been sold by the lawless Jews.

Numbered with them in the martyr's crown was the virgin [Paula](/saints/paula-companion-of-lucillian), who had gone about the prisons serving those bound for Christ, followed the five to Byzantium, buried the four young men, went back to Nicomedia, and was beheaded at last in the place where Lucillian died on the cross. The life names Aurelian and Silvanus and prints no year, so none is recorded here.
