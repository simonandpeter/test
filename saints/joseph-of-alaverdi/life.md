# Joseph of Alaverdi

Joseph was a disciple and companion of John of Zedazeni, who came into
Georgia in the sixth century with twelve Syrian ascetics to preach the
gospel — the thirteen Syrian fathers of the Georgian church. With his
teacher’s blessing he settled at Alaverdi in the east of the country,
carrying, the tradition says, a cross made from the wood of the true Cross.
The valley was empty then, and he lived there among the wild animals until
the day a nobleman came hunting in it, saw him, knew at once what he was,
kissed him and asked what had brought him to such a place. Joseph woke in
him a love of God and a hunger for the truth; the nobleman vowed a church in
the Alaverdi wilderness, and laid the foundations of the monastery that
stands there.

People began to hear of the elder and came in crowds to see him and hear
him, unbelief was rooted out of the region and the church’s services
established in it, and enough of those drawn by his life, his love and his
miracles stayed that a community grew, of which he was the first abbot. Worn
out by his labours, he felt his death near, gathered his disciples, blessed
and instructed them, named a new abbot, and departed in peace; his disciples
buried him with honour at the church of Alaverdi, and many miracles have
been worked at his grave. The Romanian calendar keeps him on 15 September
and gives the year of his repose as 570; the Russian calendar keeps him the
same day of its own reckoning.

The Ohrid Prologue, for the Serbian calendar, calls him bishop of Alaverdi and one of the twelve Syrian fathers, kept on 7 May, who were sent into the lands of the Caucasus to preach the Gospel. He died in peace in 570, it says, and his wonderworking relics rest in the cathedral church of Alaverdi.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and tells how it was chosen. saint.gr prints one line and one page for [John](/saints/john-of-zedazeni) and his disciples, and gives each of the thirteen a head line of his own, his reading «Οσιος Ιωσηφ Μαθητης Του Οσιου Ιωαννη». John came out of Syria, it says, and lived about the end of the fifth century; and when the crowds that came to him had broken his stillness and he had hidden deep in the desert, he saw the Mother of God in a dream, who commanded him to choose twelve disciples and go with them into Iberia to establish the Christian faith of its people. All his subordinates wished to go, so he wrote every name on a separate paper and laid them on the holy table; and after a night of prayer, in the sight of them all, an angel of the Lord took twelve papers from the altar and put them into his hands.

Joseph is named among the twelve the papers chose. An angel warned Eulogius, bishop of the Georgian capital, of their coming, and the bishop went out with his clergy and his people to receive them; and there followed a wonder like the one at Pentecost, for John spoke the Georgian tongue. The thirteen preached through all Georgia and venerated the blessed places through which Saint Nina had passed, whom this calendar keeps on 14 January. The page gives no year for any of them, no hymn, and no word of where each of the twelve settled afterwards.
