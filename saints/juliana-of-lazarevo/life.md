# Juliana of Lazarevo, of Murom

The Russian calendar keeps 11 August (Julian) for the translation of her relics; her chief memory is 2 January.

Juliana, the daughter of the nobleman Justin Nedyurov, lived devoutly from her youth, fasting strictly and giving much time to prayer; orphaned early and put in the care of relatives who did not understand her and laughed at her, she bore it patiently, nursing the sick and sewing clothes for the poor. Yuri Osoryin, lord of the village of Lazarevo near Murom, married her, and his parents gave the gentle daughter-in-law the running of the house, which did not interrupt her labours: she always found time for prayer, fed orphans and clothed the poor, and in a famine went hungry herself to give the last piece to whoever asked; in the epidemic that followed she gave herself wholly to the sick.

She had six sons and a daughter; after two sons were killed she wished to enter a monastery, but her husband persuaded her to stay and bring up the children, and she grew stricter with herself — more fasting and prayer, no more than two hours’ sleep with a log for a pillow, as her son Kallistratos, who wrote her life, records. Widowed, she gave her share of the inheritance to the poor and lived in extreme want, always cheerful and thankful; she was granted a visit from Nicholas the Wonderworker and instruction from the Mother of God in prayer at church. She was buried beside her husband in the church of St Lazarus, where her daughter, the schema-nun Theodosia, also lies; in 1614 her relics were found, flowing with fragrant myrrh from which many were healed.

The Romanian calendar keeps her on 2 January, the day her Russian life already gave as her chief memory, and prints her simply as «Sfânta Iuliana din Lazarevo». The long life doxologia.ro carries is the one her son wrote, and it names her father Justin Nediurev, her mother Stefanida, her husband George Osorgin of Lazarevo in the district of Murom, and the priest Patapius who married them and who later became archimandrite of the monastery of the Transfiguration at Murom under the name Pimen.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same second of January, as Juliana the Righteous, and dates her 1530 to 1604. saint.gr says she was born at Moscow in 1530 of pious and charitable parents, Justin and Stephanida Nediurev; her father worked as a steward at the court of the tsar Ivan IV Vasilyevich, called the Terrible, and for all that the household lived poorly but in a Christian way. She was orphaned young. Her last act, the page says, was to receive the Mysteries from her confessor, the priest Athanasius, and to call her children and give them her blessing; her last words were, Glory to God for all things — to You, Lord, I commend my spirit.

The Russian calendar's principal day for her is 2 января old style, the day her folder's feast note already names, and on that day the Сретенский monastery's calendar prints a second troparion for her besides the one it gives on 11 августа — «Ин тропарь», in the third tone, which has her take up her cross and follow Christ by almsgiving, fasting and prayer, and be made a rule of life for all who live in the world.
