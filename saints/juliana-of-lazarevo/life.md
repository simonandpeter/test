# Juliana of Lazarevo, of Murom

The Russian calendar keeps 11 August (Julian) for the translation of her relics; her chief memory is 2 January.

Juliana, the daughter of the nobleman Justin Nedyurov, lived devoutly from her youth, fasting strictly and giving much time to prayer; orphaned early and put in the care of relatives who did not understand her and laughed at her, she bore it patiently, nursing the sick and sewing clothes for the poor. Yuri Osoryin, lord of the village of Lazarevo near Murom, married her, and his parents gave the gentle daughter-in-law the running of the house, which did not interrupt her labours: she always found time for prayer, fed orphans and clothed the poor, and in a famine went hungry herself to give the last piece to whoever asked; in the epidemic that followed she gave herself wholly to the sick. She had six sons and a daughter; after two sons were killed she wished to enter a monastery, but her husband persuaded her to stay and bring up the children, and she grew stricter with herself — more fasting and prayer, no more than two hours’ sleep with a log for a pillow, as her son Kallistratos, who wrote her life, records. Widowed, she gave her share of the inheritance to the poor and lived in extreme want, always cheerful and thankful; she was granted a visit from Nicholas the Wonderworker and instruction from the Mother of God in prayer at church. She was buried beside her husband in the church of St Lazarus, where her daughter, the schema-nun Theodosia, also lies; in 1614 her relics were found, flowing with fragrant myrrh from which many were healed.
