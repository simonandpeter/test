# Isidore of Samtavisi

Isidore of Samtavisi was one of the twelve disciples whom [John of Zedazeni](/saints/john-of-zedazeni) chose out of his Syrian community and brought with him into Georgia, in obedience to a vision in which the Mother of God told him to take twelve worthy men to strengthen the faith in her newly founded Church there. doxologia.ro prints no separate life for him. Its life of their teacher opens by naming all thirteen together — «Sfântul Cuvios Ioan Zedazneli, dimpreună cu 12 ucenici din Georgia» — and calls them Syrian ascetics and the founding fathers of Georgian monastic life.

When the fathers were divided he was sent into Kartli, westward, and the life calls him there «vița virtuților» — the vine of the virtues. That epithet and the place he was sent to are the whole of what this page says about him by himself.

Their teacher sent them out to preach as the apostles did, to baptise in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and to lead those in darkness to worship Christ; and he divided among them relics of the saints he had brought, so that they might be worthier on the road and might follow the example of those saints. The life says that they suffered for faith and truth as the apostles and the martyrs did, wandering in deserts and in mountains and in caves and in the clefts of the earth; that most of them held themselves to a complete lack of necessities and would not even use clothes, going out to preach in skins through the heat of the day and the cold of the night; that the faithful came to them for teaching and the sick for healing; and that where the idolatrous altars were destroyed churches were raised in their place.

The Greek calendar keeps the company on the same day and tells how it was chosen. saint.gr prints one line and one page for [John](/saints/john-of-zedazeni) and his disciples, and gives each of the thirteen a head line of his own, his reading «Οσιος Ισιδωρος Μαθητης Του Οσιου Ιωαννη». John came out of Syria, it says, and lived about the end of the fifth century; and when the crowds that came to him had broken his stillness and he had hidden deep in the desert, he saw the Mother of God in a dream, who commanded him to choose twelve disciples and go with them into Iberia to establish the Christian faith of its people. All his subordinates wished to go, so he wrote every name on a separate paper and laid them on the holy table; and after a night of prayer, in the sight of them all, an angel of the Lord took twelve papers from the altar and put them into his hands.

Isidore is named among the twelve the papers chose. An angel warned Eulogius, bishop of the Georgian capital, of their coming, and the bishop went out with his clergy and his people to receive them; and there followed a wonder like the one at Pentecost, for John spoke the Georgian tongue. The thirteen preached through all Georgia and venerated the blessed places through which Saint Nina had passed, whom this calendar keeps on 14 January. The page gives no year for any of them, no hymn, and no word of where each of the twelve settled afterwards.

The Serbian church keeps the company on this same menologion day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović tells the whole mission under one heading that names nobody — „Преподобни оци грузијски.“, the venerable Georgian fathers — and then names every man of it in the body. In the sixth century, it says, and two hundred years after Saint Nina had preached the Gospel in Georgia, the Most Holy Mother of God appeared to [John](/saints/john-of-zedazeni), an ascetic of Antioch, and ordered him to choose twelve of his own disciples and go into Georgia to establish the Orthodox faith. John did so.

Arriving in Georgia, the twelve missionaries were solemnly received by the prince of that land and by its catholicos, and they began their work at once with zeal. The people gathered about them in crowds, and they strengthened them in the faith with great wisdom and with many wonders. With apostolic zeal they all established the faith of Christ in Georgia, founded many monasteries, and left many disciples after them; and so they were counted worthy of glory in heaven and of power on earth.

**The entry's own dating is the sixth century and nothing narrower**, and the two hundred years it counts from Saint Nina's preaching is a reckoning and not a bound. The Prologue names no country of origin for the twelve beyond Antioch, no monastery, no year of any man's death and no emperor, and it prints no troparion and no kontakion.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 7 мая ст. ст. among the thirteen Syrian — Cappadocian — fathers, the founders of Georgian monasticism, and marks the line a commemoration of the Georgian church. Its life of the company makes him one of the twelve whom [John of Zedazeni](/saints/john-of-zedazeni) chose by lot, at the command of the Mother of God, and brought out of Cappadocia into Georgia in the middle of the sixth century; they were blessed on the road by «Симеон Столпник Младший», crossed the Kura at Mtskheta with dry feet, were met by king Парсман and the Catholicos Евлавий, and settled on the mountain of Zedazeni, living in huts on grasses and roots.

When the Mother of God told their teacher in a dream to send his disciples out through Georgia to preach, he was one of the four sent into Kartli. The life calls him «вертоград добродетели», the garden of virtue, and says that after long apostolic labours he built a monastery at Samtavisi — the place his name is joined to — in honour of the Image of the Saviour not made by hands, and that his holy relics rest there. That is the whole of what this day's reading says of him.
