# Trophimus, companion of Paul

Trophimus is remembered in the Acts of the Apostles, and the apostle Paul names him in the second letter to Timothy: «Pe Trofim l-am lăsat bolnav în Milet» — Trophimus I have left sick at Miletus. That, with what follows, is the whole of what doxologia.ro's life has to say of him, for the page is one short notice shared by the three apostles the Romanian calendar keeps together on 15 April.

Trophimus, together with [Pudens](/saints/pudens-of-rome) and [Aristarchus](/saints/aristarchus-of-apamea), followed Paul through all his persecutions; and at the end, when Paul was beheaded by Nero at Rome, these three apostles of the Lord — Aristarchus, Pudens and Trophimus — were beheaded with him.

The page gives no year and no see for Trophimus, and says nothing of his birth or his manner of life; a different Trophimus, the martyr of Antioch who suffered under Probus with Sabbatius and Dorymedon, is kept in this corpus on 19 September and is not this man.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them a day earlier than the Romanian, on 14 April, and says where it reads them out of. All three were of the Seventy, saint.gr says, and devoted fellow workers of the apostle Paul. Of Aristarchus the Acts of the Apostles report that he was a Macedonian from Thessalonica and in all likelihood a Jew; Paul names him in the letter to the Colossians as his fellow prisoner at Rome, and in the letter to Philemon as his fellow worker. Of Pudens he speaks in the second letter to Timothy, from which the page gathers that Pudens was among those who worked for the Gospel at Rome.

Trophimus was brought to the Christian faith by Paul himself when he came to Ephesus, and from then on he followed him to Jerusalem and to Rome, to work and to suffer ill together with his teacher; Paul names him in the second letter to Timothy. In the terrible persecution of Nero, which saint.gr dates 54 to 68, all three of these fellow workers of Paul were counted worthy to bear witness by death, being beheaded. The page's couplets play on each name in turn, and one line of the synaxarion says simply that the disciples departed on the fourteenth.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the three of them on the Romanian calendar's day, 15 April, and is a third witness that agrees with the other two: all three were of the number of the Seventy, and all three were beheaded in the persecution under Nero at the time the apostle Paul was beheaded. Its one new fact about Trophimus is where he came from — he was from Asia, the entry says, citing the Acts of the Apostles at chapter twenty, verse four — and for the rest it repeats what the Romanian notice has, that he followed the apostle on his journey and that the apostle wrote of leaving him sick at Miletus, which it places at the second letter to Timothy, chapter four, verse twenty. It prints no year, no see and no hymn.
