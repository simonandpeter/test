# Leo of Methone

The venerable Leo was born in Calabria in Italy, and saint.gr says he passed his life «μονοχίτων και ανυπόδητος τη νηστεία και αγρυπνία καταψύχων» — in a single tunic and barefoot, cooling himself with fasting and with vigil. On a pilgrimage to the Holy Places, worn down by his own austerity, he died at sea just as Methone came into sight; the town had been since the fourth century an obligatory stop for travellers going to and from Jerusalem.

The sailors buried his body on the shore at Methone. Years afterwards he appeared to Nicholas, bishop of Methone and a distinguished theologian of the twelfth century, who took up the relic, and it began at once to work wonders. His honour lapsed about the year 1600, and after more than four hundred years the metropolitan of Messenia, Chrysostom III, brought his memory back on 12 May, which is the day the Greek calendar now keeps him on.
