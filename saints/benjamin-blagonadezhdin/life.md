# Benjamin (Blagonadezhdin)

Benjamin was born on 24 March 1880 in the village of Oparino in the Alexandrov district of Vladimir province, the son of the psalm-reader Elijah Blagonadezhdin. He finished the Moscow seminary in 1904 and taught at schools in Serpukhov district, at Kolomna and in Dmitrov district, and in 1906 was ordained priest to the church at Chekovo in the Vladimir district, where he served until his arrest; in the years of persecution he was taken to court several times on the pretext of unpaid taxes.

In 1926 he was called as a witness in the case of Bishop Athanasius (Sakharov), accused of anti-Soviet sermons, and said that he had served with the bishop at the patronal feast of St George at Lykovo in 1925 and had heard nothing of persecution in his sermons, though he could not swear the bishop had not spoken of it. The Chekovo church was closed in 1930, but with the believers’ support he won leave that year to serve in it again, and it stayed open until his arrest.

He was arrested on 8 September 1937 and held in the prison at Yuryev-Polsky. The secretary of the village soviet testified that he had told the collective farmers the new constitution gave the clergy back their rights under pressure from the pope of Rome, and had foretold a dry summer in 1937 in which sowing would be useless. Questioned again on 24 September, he said he understood the constitution’s vote for the clergy as a concession, and did not deny having spoken of a drought. On 27 September the NKVD troika sentenced him to death, and he was shot on 1 October 1937 and buried in an unmarked grave.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 18 September old style.
