# Mitros of Tripoli

saint.gr says he came of a devout and virtuous family of the Peloponnese and was made a Muslim while still young, along with other youths of his district, probably after the crushing of the Peloponnesian rising of 1769. His Christian name was Demetrius; when he became a Mohammedan he was called Mustafa. Gifted with intelligence and wisdom and with other gifts besides, active and able, he rose quickly among the notable Turks of the Peloponnese and took the office of eparch, and he acquired much money and many slaves.

None of it satisfied him. He was eaten, the page says, by the longing to return to the faith of his fathers, so that glory and wealth neither touched nor altered his character. He resolved to come back to Christianity, went to a spiritual father in Tripoli, confessed with tears, took his counsel and his blessing, and lived as a Christian in secret.

His Christian life was not long in being noticed by the Turks, who denounced him to the pasha of Tripoli. He was arrested at Mistra and brought bound before the pasha, where he confessed his faith with courage and his burning wish to be martyred for it. In spite of the pasha's counsels and promises and the reminder of what he owed the Turks for the offices they had given him, the martyr stayed unshaken. He was imprisoned at once, and a few days later beheaded, on 28 May 1794, the Sunday of Pentecost, at Tripoli. The Christians took up his relic and buried it in the church of the great-martyr Demetrius in Tripoli, and Makarios of Corinth wrote the account of his martyrdom.
