# Paulinus the Merciful

The Russian calendar keeps him on this day as «Свт. Павлина Милостивого, еп. Ноланского (431)», Paulinus the Merciful, bishop of Nola, and the life behind that line begins him far from Italy: he came of a noble and wealthy family of the city of Bordeaux. His education carried him early into public life — a Roman senator at twenty, then consul, and at last governor of the region of Campania in Italy.

At twenty-five he and his wife were turned to Christ and baptised, and from then he changed his life altogether: he sold his whole estate and gave the money away to those in need, and had to bear the contempt of his friends and his servants for it. Having no children of their own, the two of them took in poor orphans and brought them up in the fear of God. Seeking solitude he went away to the Spanish city of Barcelona.

The report of his ascetic life spread, and in 393 he was pressed to accept ordination as a presbyter. Soon afterwards he left Spain for the city of Nola in Italy, where he was chosen bishop. When the Vandals fell upon Italy and carried many of the inhabitants off captive to Africa, he spent the property of the church on buying the captives back; and once, having nothing left with which to ransom the son of a poor widow, he went into slavery in the young man's place, and in a slave's clothes began to serve the Vandal prince.

The secret came out before long, and he not only received his own freedom but obtained it for all the captives, and came home with them. Love of mankind and compassion for all the poor and needy, the life says, were the mark of his character. He is known also as a builder of churches and a Christian poet, and he died at the age of seventy-eight on 22 June 431, leaving several hymns and letters of moral counsel. His relics are kept at Rome, in the church of the Apostle [Bartholomew](/saints/bartholomew-the-apostle). The calendar prints no hymn for him.
