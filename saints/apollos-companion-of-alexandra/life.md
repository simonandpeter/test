# Apollos, companion of Alexandra

Apollos was one of three followers of [Alexandra the Empress](/saints/alexandra-the-empress), the wife of Diocletian, whom saint.gr calls honourable and virtuous servants, devoted from the heart to their empress, and who knew her goodness. When she had confessed Christ during the torments of [George the Trophy-bearer](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) and had been carried off and imprisoned for it, and had died in that prison two days before George's own death, in the year 303, they thought that her decision and her faith in Christ ought to make them examine without prejudice the faith in the true God, and order their own conduct accordingly.

So they went to a Christian priest, heard him, and came away from his house fervent believers, enlightened by the grace of the Comforter, with the resolve to follow their empress's example. And one day they too confessed their faith. The other two of the three, as the calendar's line names them, are [Isaacius](/saints/isaacius-companion-of-alexandra) and [Codratus](/saints/codratus-companion-of-alexandra).

Diocletian ordered their deaths. Codratus they beheaded; Apollos, with the other of the two, was put to death by hunger and by thirst. That torment, the page says, was the most painful of all, but they faced it bravely, comforted by the hope that they would meet the holy empress in the dwellings of righteousness and blessedness — and their hope, it adds, was satisfied. The Church, honouring their memory, keeps their feast with hers on the same day.

The page gives Apollos nothing of his own: no country, no parentage, no age, no office in the palace beyond the word servant, and no relic. Its couplets take the three in two groups — that two athletes, bearing the noose of famine, escape the demon's noose that kills souls; and that Codratus, cut down, seasons the broths of his own pot with the salt of his blood — and it prints no hymn.

The Russian calendar says what the three were before and parts their deaths. They were pagans serving at the court of the emperor Diocletian (284–305), and were among the onlookers at the sufferings of the great-martyr [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer), whose day the page gives as 23 апреля; his faith, his courage and his wonders woke faith in Christ in them, and they declared themselves Christians before all the people and reproached the emperor for his impiety and his cruelty. They were condemned to death.

The manner of it differed. [Codratus](/saints/codratus-companion-of-alexandra) was cut down with the sword, and Apollos with [Isaacius](/saints/isaacius-companion-of-alexandra) was starved to death. The name page behind his forename gives him the city the line leaves out, Nicomedia, where Diocletian’s court was.
