# Publius of Athens

Publius was bishop of Athens in the second century after Christ, next in that see after the holy Narcissus. He governed his flock, saint.gr says, as a good shepherd, and at the last gave his life also for his rational sheep, finishing it by a martyr's death in the persecution of the emperor Marcus Aurelius. That is the whole of the page's account of him: it names no charge, no judge and no manner of death, and gives no year within the reign.

What it keeps besides is two lines of verse for him — «Στολὴν ἔβαψας αἱμάτων ῥείθροις, μάκαρ, / Καὶ λαμπρὸς ὤφθης νῦν Κυρίῳ ἢ πλέον» — you dyed your robe in streams of blood, blessed one, and are seen now bright before the Lord, and brighter than before. There is no hymn and no relic on the page. The Greek calendar keeps another Publius on this same day, one of three martyrs with Africanus and Terentius, and he is not this bishop.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same menologion day and in three sentences makes him the same man, with one difference and one addition. It calls him successor in the episcopate of the glorious [Dionysius the Areopagite](/saints/dionysius-the-areopagite) at Athens, where saint.gr makes him next in that see after the holy Narcissus; neither page is preferred here. And where saint.gr names no manner of death, the Prologue says that as bishop he was tortured by unbelievers and beheaded, in the second century, and that for a short time of suffering he inherited life eternal.
