# Gavrilia Papaiannis

Gavrilia was born in Constantinople on 2/15 October 1897, to Elias and Victoria Papaianni, the fourth and last child of the family and the most loved: her brother Alexandros was the first, her sisters Vasiliki and Pavelina the second and third. She grew up in the city until the family moved to Thessaloniki in 1923. In 1938 she left for England and stayed there for the whole of the Second World War, training as an orthopaedist and a psychotherapist; for her services during the war and after it she was offered British citizenship, and politely refused.

In 1945 she returned to Greece and worked with the Friends Refugee Mission and the American Farm School in Thessaloniki in the first years after the war, and later opened her own practice in Athens, where she worked until 1954. In March of that year her mother died. After that grief she closed the practice, gave all her money and goods to the poor, and, resolved to live in complete poverty — though she was already nearly sixty — set out for India with the coat she stood in and a Scripture, and began to serve the lepers and the poor in hospitals and ashrams.

After five years in India she lived as a hermit for eleven months in the Himalayas. In 1959 she entered the Monastery of St Lazarus at Bethany in Palestine, and four years later received the small schema from Father [Amphilochios (Makris)](/saints/amphilochius-makris-of-patmos) of Patmos, at the Cave of St Anthony.

In the two decades that followed she alternated the silence of the monastery with a year of missionary service in East Africa and three more years back in India, at Uttar Pradesh, beside Archimandrite Lazarus (Moore). In the same period Father Sophrony of Essex asked her to take the abbacy of the women's monastery there, and she did not accept: one of the few times she turned down the calls to service that drew her, every time, away from the silence and the solitude she loved.

The last thirteen years of her life she spent in her native Greece, first in a small flat in Athens and then in a hermitage she founded on the island of Leros. There, in 1991, she received the great schema from Father Dionysios of the Athonite Skete of Little St Anne, and she passed to the Lord the following year, on 28 March.
