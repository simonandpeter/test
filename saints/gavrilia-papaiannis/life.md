# Gavrilia Papaiannis

Gavrilia was born in Constantinople on 2/15 October 1897, to Elias and Victoria Papaianni, the fourth and last child of the family and the most loved: her brother Alexandros was the first, her sisters Vasiliki and Pavelina the second and third. She grew up in the city until the family moved to Thessaloniki in 1923. In 1938 she left for England and stayed there for the whole of the Second World War, training as an orthopaedist and a psychotherapist; for her services during the war and after it she was offered British citizenship, and politely refused.

In 1945 she returned to Greece and worked with the Friends Refugee Mission and the American Farm School in Thessaloniki in the first years after the war, and later opened her own practice in Athens, where she worked until 1954. In March of that year her mother died. After that grief she closed the practice, gave all her money and goods to the poor, and, resolved to live in complete poverty — though she was already nearly sixty — set out for India with the coat she stood in and a Scripture, and began to serve the lepers and the poor in hospitals and ashrams.

After five years in India she lived as a hermit for eleven months in the Himalayas. In 1959 she entered the Monastery of St Lazarus at Bethany in Palestine, and four years later received the small schema from Father [Amphilochios (Makris)](/saints/amphilochius-makris-of-patmos) of Patmos, at the Cave of St Anthony.

In the two decades that followed she alternated the silence of the monastery with a year of missionary service in East Africa and three more years back in India, at Uttar Pradesh, beside Archimandrite Lazarus (Moore). In the same period Father Sophrony of Essex asked her to take the abbacy of the women's monastery there, and she did not accept: one of the few times she turned down the calls to service that drew her, every time, away from the silence and the solitude she loved.

The last thirteen years of her life she spent in her native Greece, first in a small flat in Athens and then in a hermitage she founded on the island of Leros. There, in 1991, she received the great schema from Father Dionysios of the Athonite Skete of Little St Anne, and she passed to the Lord the following year, on 28 March.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, and saint.gr's account of her adds the years between the two ends. She was the youngest child of a well-off Constantinopolitan family, and it was her elder sister Vasiliki, it is said, who first spoke to her of God, telling her stories from the Gospel and the Old Testament along with the fairy tales she read her. In 1923, in the exchange of populations between Greece and Turkey, the family was expelled and came to Thessalonica; there Aurelia entered the Aristotle University — the second woman admitted to a Greek university — and took a degree in philosophy, having earlier graduated from the agricultural school of Estavayer-le-Lac in Switzerland, for she loved plants and «μέχρι το τέλος της ζωής της μιλούσε μαζί τους», talked with them to the end of her life. She went to Athens in 1932 to work in a psychiatric clinic, and a year later to England, where she took what work she could, studied podology and physiotherapy, and went on caring for the poor; after the Second World War she came back and opened a physiotherapy practice in Athens, again treating the destitute.

Her mother's death in 1954 was the turn. She took a vow of poverty, gave away everything she had, and left for India, where for five years she stood beside the lepers and did the impossible to relieve them, coming to know Mother Teresa, Sivananda and the social activist Baba Amte. After four years of free physiotherapy for the poor she went up into the Himalayas and spent eleven months as a hermit. In 1960 she reached the Community of the Resurrection of the Lord at Bethany and was tonsured; after three years' probation as a nun she received the name Gavrilia. The Ecumenical Patriarch Athenagoras sent her to the community of Taizé in France, briefly, and then to the United States, where she travelled among the Greek communities of seventeen states and accompanied many of the mentally ill to hospitals in Europe. After nearly a decade of journeys, talks and nursing she was sent to East Africa for three years of missionary work, then, after a short assignment in Germany, back to India for three more. In 1979 she was given the use of a flat in Athens and became a «Γερόντισσα», an eldress, counselling those who came to her; near the end she withdrew to a hermitage on Aegina, returned to Athens when Hodgkin's lymphoma appeared, and two years before her death moved to the island of Leros, where she died on 28 March 1992.

Her place in the calendar is recent and the page prints the document. At the sessions of the Holy Synod of the Ecumenical Patriarchate on 3 and 4 October 2023, on the recommendation of the Canonical Committee, the monk Gavrilia Papaiannis, of Constantinople, who was perfected on Leros, was entered in the register of the saints of the Orthodox Church of the East, «διακριθεῖσα διά τάς κατά Χριστόν ἀρετάς τῆς ἐλεημοσύνης, τῆς φιλανθρωπίας καί τῆς ἀπολύτου ἀφοσιώσεως καί ἐμπιστοσύνης εἰς τό θέλημα τοῦ Κυρίου» — distinguished for the Christlike virtues of almsgiving, of philanthropy, and of absolute devotion and trust in the will of the Lord. The couplet the page gives her is on the same thing: «Ἀγάπης, σεμνή, πάντας στεγούσης στέγη θεοσκέπαστος, Γαβριηλία, ὤφθης» — modest Gavrilia, you were seen as a roof sheltered by God, the roof of a love that shelters everyone.
