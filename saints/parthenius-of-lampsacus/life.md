# Parthenius of Lampsacus

Parthenius, whose name is the name of virginity, was born at Melitopolis. His father Christopher was a deacon of God's holy church in that city. He was never taught his letters, but from the first, listening to what was read, he remembered much of the divine Scripture like a chosen scholar. As he grew he sat by a lake nearby, catching fish and selling them and giving alms to the poor; and from his early years, being granted the grace of God, he hid it from men, until in the eighteenth year of his life he began to work wonders, driving the devils out of men by calling on the most holy name of Christ.

When word of him began to go about among the people, Philip, bishop of Melitopolis, heard of him, called him and looked into everything about him, and marvelled at his good works and at the gift of God that was in him. He gave orders that he should be taught his letters, and when he had learned he ordained him priest, against his will, and put the government of the church in his hands. In the order of the priesthood he laboured the more, and receiving a double gift of holiness and of wonder-working he healed every sickness and did many marvellous signs in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Among his wonders the life tells these. A man met him on the road whom a bull had struck in the face with its horn, so that his eye had come out and he was holding it in his trembling hand; the saint took it in his own hand, put it back in its place, washed it with water, and in three days it was perfectly healed. A woman with a grievous and incurable hurt in the hidden parts of her body came to him for healing, and he made the sign of the cross on her forehead and she was healed at once. Another time, as he was going to visit a sick man and passing a nobleman's house, a great dog broke its chain and ran at him and put its forepaws on his shoulders to bite his face; he breathed on it and made the sign of the cross, and it died, and he threw it down off his shoulders.

Achillius, archbishop of Cyzicus, heard of such wonders, called Parthenius to him and made him bishop in the city of Lampsacus. Coming to the diocese entrusted to him he found the whole city holding to the heathen worship of idols and the Christians few, and the good shepherd was very grieved. So he did not cease counselling, rebuking, entreating and showing them the way of truth, and working wonders in the name of Christ and healing their sick, until he had brought them to the knowledge of the true God.

Seeing the city turn wholly from the service of idols, he wished to pull down the idol temples that stood in it and build honourable churches of God in their place; and for that he went to the great emperor Constantine to obtain authority. He was received with honour and love, and the emperor gave him his imperial letter for the destruction of the idol temples and a quantity of gold for the building of the churches, and let him go in peace. Returning to Lampsacus he overturned all the temples from their foundations and built a great and beautiful church in the middle of the city, setting his own hand to the work and helping the builders.

While the church was building, a man came to him who had an unclean spirit in him that had lodged there long, and the man could not drive it out. The saint, knowing what was in him, gave him no answer; and the devil, troubled in the man, said, "I wished to see you, and therefore I bowed to you; why did you not answer me?" — "You have seen me." — "I have seen you and I have known you." — "If you have seen me and known me, then come out of God's creature." The devil begged not to be driven from the dwelling he had held since the man's childhood, and asked where he should go; and the saint said he would give him a man to live in, if only he would come out of this one. When the devil pressed him whether he meant it, Parthenius opened his mouth and said: "Here am I; enter and dwell in me." And the devil, burned as if by fire, cried out, "Woe is me — dwelling in a good vessel, after so long you drive me out; how shall I enter the house of God?" — and went out of the man into desert and untrodden places, and the man was left whole.

When the church was finished, the saint's care was to make the holy table in the sanctuary. Finding in one of the ruined idol temples a good and chosen stone, he had the workmen prepare it; and as they were bringing it on a cart drawn by strong oxen, the devil, angry at that stone being taken from the temple, startled the oxen so that they ran and no one could hold them, and the man leading them, Eutychianus, was thrown to the ground face down under the cart, and the cart with the stone went over him and broke all his bones, and he died. When he heard of it Parthenius said, "This is the devil's malice; but you shall not hinder God's work, O devil." He rose at once and went with the believing people to the place, and seeing the dead body he bowed his knees to the ground and prayed with tears that God would show the enemy's contrivance empty and give His servant Eutychianus a part in this life again. As he prayed the man's spirit returned into his body, and he rose up whole as he had been before and drove the oxen with the cart and the stone to the church doors.

The sick and those tormented by unclean spirits were brought to him from everywhere and all were healed, so that in the days of that great servant of God the physician's art was of no use to men. Among them was Daphne, the daughter of one Dionysius of an imperial family, whom he delivered in three days from a grievous demon; and Agalmatia, the daughter of one Mamalius, a nobleman of the city of Smyrna, who was thrown to the ground by an unclean spirit and foamed and stiffened, and he healed her; and Zoila, a well-known woman who was hard pressed by a spirit of divination. A youth named Nicon, the son of a priest, was brought to him by his parents, who fell at his feet and begged him to deliver their son; but the man of God told them that their son was not worthy of healing, for the spirit that tormented him had been given him as a punishment, since he was as a murderer of his father, and they had often, in the bitterness of their souls, prayed that God would punish him for the insults they bore from him.

Having turned many from the idols to the true God and healed countless sicknesses, near the end of his life he fell ill, and being called by the Lord he went to Him on the seventh day of the month of February, resting in peace in a blessed old age. News of his honoured departure went at once through the cities and countries round about, and the archbishops gathered from everywhere to his burial — of Heraclea, of Cyzicus, of Melitopolis, of Paria — and many other bishops and priests. They gave him a glorious burial, with psalms and praises and spiritual songs, and laid his honoured body near the cathedral church, in the house of prayer he had built himself; and many healings were given from his holy grave, for not in his life only but after his death this physician who took no fee cleansed the lepers, cast the devils out of men and healed every sickness. The page gives no year for his birth and none for his repose.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and adds a good deal the Romanian did not. It gives him a small town of Bithynia for his birthplace and sets him in the years of Constantine the Great, 324 to 337, and it makes his father Christopher deacon of the church of Melitopolis and his first teacher in the Orthodox faith. It says that what made him love fishing was the way the Lord had fished for the apostles, who were fishermen themselves, so that when he cast his nets into the lake of Apollonias and drew them up full he felt he was working in one of the boats of the Apostle Peter or of John. He kept nothing of what the fish fetched but shared it among the poor out of love for them, and when they thanked him he would ask why: he had no such claim on them, they were not strangers to one another but brothers, and what is simpler or more natural than a brother helping his brothers? Philip — or Philetos — bishop of Melitopolis ordained him presbyter for that life, and Achillios, whom the page also calls Ascholios, bishop of Kyzikos, ordained him bishop of Lampsakos. God gave him the gift of wonder-working, the page says, so that he could drive the demons out of men and heal every kind of sickness, and it is for that reason that those who suffer from the accursed disease of cancer resort to him particularly. It names him gentle, patient, hospitable, long-suffering, the angel of concord, the encourager of the penitent, the ready man for his flock, and says he fell asleep in peace. Of his relics it records the skull at the monastery of Esphigmenou on the Holy Mountain, fragments at the monasteries of Mega Spilaion at Kalavryta, of Rovelista at Arta and of Kykkos in Cyprus, and a part of the honourable head at the monastery of Makrymalli in the Metropolis of Chalkis. Its couplet has him leave the dust to Lampsakos as a lamp kindling his own great light.
