# Brigid of Kildare

Brigid was born about the year 450 at Faughart, some two miles from Dundalk in County Louth, in Ireland. According to the tradition her father was a pagan named Dubthach and her mother Brocessa, or Broiseach, one of his slaves. Even in childhood she was known for her compassion towards the poor: she gave them food and clothing, and gave away her father's goods as well.

She was tonsured a monastic by Saint Mael of Ardagh, whom the Romanian calendar keeps on 6 February, and soon afterwards she founded a monastery on land given her by the king of Leinster. The place was called Cill Dara — Kildare, "the church of the oak" — and this, the life says, was the beginning of cenobitic monasticism for women in Ireland.

God gave her the gift of working wonders, and the page tells one at length. One evening she was talking with a blind nun named Dara, and they spoke all night of the joys of the Kingdom of Heaven and lost count of the hours. When the sun rose Brigid was struck by the beauty of earth and sky in the morning light, and grew sad that sister Dara could not see it. She prayed and made the sign of the cross over Dara's eyes, and the blind nun's eyes opened: she saw the sun in the east, and the trees and flowers glittering with dew.

Dara looked for a while, then turned to Brigid and said: "Close my eyes again, dear Mother, for when the world is seen with the eyes of the body, then God is seen less by the soul." Brigid prayed again, and Dara was blind once more.

She fell asleep in the Lord in 523, having received Holy Communion from Saint Ninnidh of Inismacsaint. She was buried at Kildare, but her relics were moved to Downpatrick during the Viking invasions, and she is believed to have been buried in the same grave as Saint Patrick and Saint Columba of Iona. Her veneration spread at once to England, Scotland and Wales, into the German lands, the Scandinavian countries, France, Italy and Portugal; the custom of making crosses of rushes for her and hanging them in the house is still kept in Ireland. The monastery she founded at Kildare stood until 1540, when it was dissolved by King Henry VIII.
