# Theodore of Perga

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The day's line for 21 April reads «Sfântul Mucenic Teodor din Perga», and his own page carries no Viață tab at all — a troparion, the appointed readings and icons, and nothing more. The one thing said about him beyond the name is where he belongs: the troparion's own title calls him «Teodor din Perga Pamfiliei», Theodore of Perga in Pamphylia.

The troparion itself is the common of martyrs and tells nothing particular of him: «Dumnezeul părinţilor noştri Care faci pururea cu noi după blândeţile Tale, nu îndepărta mila Ta de la noi; ci, pentru rugăciunile lor, în pace îndreptează viaţa noastră» — and it asks in the plural, for several, so that even the hymn suggests companions the calendar does not name here.

Of his years, the persecution in which he suffered, the manner of his death and the names of those with him the calendar says nothing, and this folder invents nothing. Several men called Theodore stand in this corpus; none of them is kept on this day.

The Greek calendar keeps him two days earlier, on 19 April, and its page supplies almost everything the Romanian one withheld — including the companions whose prayers the Romanian troparion asks for without naming them. saint.gr sets Theodore in the years of the emperor Antoninus and of a governor called Theodotus. He came from Perge in Pamphylia and was very devout, and out of his zeal for piety he went about destroying idols, carved images and the temples of the idolaters. For that he was seized, and the tortures put on him were dreadful: they stretched him out on a red-hot gridiron, and then tied him behind wild horses, which dragged him a long way over rough ground.

After that they threw him into a furnace of fire, together with two soldiers who had come to believe in Christ, [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-socrates). Then they imprisoned him, and at the last they crucified him and pierced him with arrows; and after three days of anguish upon the cross he gave up his blessed soul. His mother [Philippa](/saints/philippa-mother-of-theodore), after her son's death on the cross, confessed Christ as true God and was martyred by the sword; and [Dioscorus](/saints/dioscorus-19-april), a priest of the idols in those same years, was brought to the Christian faith by Theodore's wonders and was martyred by fire.

The Greek page's couplets take them in turn: that Theodore was shown a sharer of the Passion with Him who suffered and is above suffering; that Philippa is loved as an athlete's mother and as one contending by the sword; and that the spear which once pierced the dead body of the Most High now pierces two living martyrs. No year is given for any of them, only the reign of Antoninus, and the page prints no hymn.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the young martyr alone — „Св. муч. Теодор.“ — and then opens its body with „И други с њим.“, *and others with him*, before naming the rest. Theodore suffered for the faith of Christ at Perga in Pamphylia in the time of the emperor Antoninus. He was young and fair of face; and when the governor of that region chose him with other youths to be sent for service at the imperial court, he refused and declared that he was a Christian. For that he was tortured with various torments and then thrown on a fire — but water welled up out of the earth and quenched it.

The governor put that down to some magic of Theodore's, and the martyr answered him: this is not the work of my power but of Christ my God; and if you wish to know the power of your gods, lay another fire and throw in one of your own soldiers, and you will know their power and the omnipotence of my God. The governor meant to do it; but the soldiers begged him in fear to throw in the priest [Dioscorus](/saints/dioscorus-19-april) instead of them, and Dioscorus begged him to throw in the idol of Zeus and the rest of the idols, for if they are gods they will easily save themselves — which he said because his heart was already turned to Christ, having seen the wonder of the fire.

Learning this, the governor condemned Dioscorus to death and burned him; and he gave Theodore to death too, with the two soldiers [Socrates](/saints/socrates-companion-of-dionysius) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-socrates), and with [Philippa](/saints/philippa-mother-of-theodore), Theodore's mother. Theodore they stretched on a cross, on which he gave up his spirit only on the third day; Socrates and Dionysius were run through with a spear, and Philippa was beheaded with the sword. All of them, the entry ends, were crowned with the crowns of glory in the kingdom of Christ.

What the entry adds to this folder is the whole of the account above: Perga and Pamphylia the Greek page also has, but the conscription of the fair-faced youths, the water out of the earth, the exchange about the gods and the three days on the cross are new to it here. The entry gives no year for this company, only „у време цара Антонина“, in the time of the emperor Antoninus. More than one Roman emperor bore that name, so no interval was read out of the reign and no date was added here; this folder stays undated, as it was.
