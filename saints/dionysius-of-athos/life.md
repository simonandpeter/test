# Dionysius of Athos

Dionysius was from the borders of Kastoria, from a village called Korison. His parents were believing and virtuous tillers of the soil, neither rich nor poor but living in sufficiency. He learned little in the way of letters, but being by nature quick and wise he thought the good things of this life corruptible and passing, and desired the heavenly and everlasting ones, turning them over in his mind every day; and as the thirsty hart runs to the springs of water, so his heart burned with the desire of God.

He had an elder brother named Theodosius, like him in face as in soul and in virtue. At eighteen Theodosius went to Constantinople to meet men of virtue and profit by their words, leaving house, country, parents and his little brother behind; he lodged at the patriarchate among monks admirable in the chanting and in ascetic labour, and there, being quick to learn, he came to know the whole of Scripture and the dogmas, and was loved by all.

Afterwards, wanting a quiet and untroubled place, he left the city for the Holy Mountain of Athos; and having venerated at all the cells and monasteries there, he made his obedience at the honourable monastery of Philotheou, where the men were truly, as the name says, lovers of God. In time he became abbot of Philotheou, and it was to him that Dionysius came.

The life then tells of a light seen night after night on the mountain, and of how the man who saw it went at last to a far-seeing hieromonk full of the Spirit, [Dometius](/saints/dometius-disciple-of-dionysius), who lived at the church of the Most Holy Theotokos, and told him the cause of it. On Dionysius's counsel cells were built and a church of Saint John the Baptist higher up, on the northern side of the mountain, which is called to this day «the Old Forerunner».

When his end was near he called his brother and the emperor and asked them to have a care for the monastery as long as they lived in this world; and having set all in order and prayed the Lord for them, he gave up his soul into His holy hands on the twenty-fifth day of the month of June, having lived in this world seventy-two years. The hierarch buried his holy and honourable relics with fitting honour and reverence, and afterwards those relics worked many wonders for those who prayed to him in faith.

His monks brought word of his passing back to the monastery, and all the brethren wept for the loss of such a father. The life gives his age at death and the day, and prints no year of birth or of death, so none is recorded here; nor does the calendar print a hymn for him.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 25 June, and names him there by his foundation, «κτήτορας της Μονής Τιμίου Προδρόμου Αγίου Όρους». saint.gr makes him born in the village of Korisso of Kastoria of devout farming parents, and says he went young to the Holy Mountain to his brother Theodosius, who was his teacher in the study of the Scriptures. Under the emperor Alexius III Comnenus, whose years the page gives as 1350 to 1390, Theodosius was made metropolitan of Trebizond; and after his own ordination to the presbyterate Dionysius moved his place of struggle to a steep mountain of the little Athos, called Old Forerunner and Panagia, meaning to build a church and a seemly monastery a little below it. In 1375, the page says, Alexius III approved the raising of the monastery under the name of the Honourable Forerunner and Baptist John, and gave fifty sōmia for the work and a thousand Komnenata three years later. Dionysius died at Trebizond, where he had gone to ask the emperor for further help towards painting the monastery's church.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 25 June, the same menologion day the Romanian and Greek calendars keep him on, calls him the founder of the monastery of Saint John on Athos, the Dionysiou, and makes him a native of **Korica in Albania** — a third placing of his birthplace beside the Korison of the Romanian account above and the Korisso of Kastoria of the Greek one. His elder brother Theodosius, it says, withdrew to the Holy Mountain and in time became abbot of Philotheou; when Dionysius was grown he came to his brother at Philotheou and was given the habit by him; and by the Providence of God Theodosius, being once on monastery business in Constantinople, was chosen and consecrated metropolitan of Trebizond.

Then a strange light began to appear to Dionysius every night on the place where he later raised the house of Saint John the Forerunner — where the Romanian account has another man see the light and bring word of it to a far-seeing hieromonk. Taking the light for a sign from heaven that a monastery should be raised there, he went to Trebizond to his brother and to the emperor to ask for help; and the emperor gave him both money and a charter, which is kept in the monastery to this day. In the year 1380, this book says, he founded the house of Saint John the Forerunner — where the Greek page has the emperor approve the raising of it in 1375, and neither year is written into his dates, a foundation being no date of a person.

When sea robbers plundered the house he went to Trebizond again, and there ended his life, in his seventy-second year — the age the Romanian account gives him too, and still an age and not a year, so this folder records none. The Greek page has him go on that last journey to ask the emperor for help towards painting the church instead. The monastery of Dionysiou, the entry ends, prospers and flowers to this day; and in it is the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos called the Praise, which the emperor gave him, and before which, by tradition, the Akathist to the Theotokos was read for the first time.
