# Ebba the Younger

saint.gr says Ebba lived in the ninth century and was abbess of the monastery of Coldingham, the largest monastery in Scotland. That house had been founded by [Ebba the Elder](/saints/ebba-the-elder), whom the page says is honoured on 25 August and calls a sister of the kings of Northumbria, Oswald and Oswiu. The page distinguishes the two by calling this one η Νέα, the younger.

In the year 870, while Danish pirates were raiding the coast by the monastery, the abbess was troubled not for the saving of her own life but for the keeping of her purity and of the other nuns'. As soon as the raiders broke into the monastery's enclosure she gathered the nuns in the abbess's house and, after words that moved them, cut off her nose and her upper lip with a blade. Every one of the sisters did the same.

When the invaders came into the room where the nuns were assembled, a hideous sight met them. It did not stop them from setting fire to the monastery and burning the venerable Ebba alive together with all her nuns. The page gives her no year of birth, names no family and no relics, and prints no hymn and no couplet under her name.
