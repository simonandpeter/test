# Junia, companion of Andronicus

Junia is kept on this day with [Andronicus](/saints/andronicus-the-apostle), and the Romanian calendar's two pages describe their bond in two ways: the line for 17 mai calls her «soția sa», his wife, while the life itself calls her «ajutătoarea sa», his helper and fellow worker. Both readings are left standing here as the source prints them. With him she was renowned in the company of the seventy apostles, and both had believed in Christ before the apostle Paul, whose kinswoman she was — as Paul writes in the epistle to the Romans, saluting Andronicus and Junia, his kinsfolk and fellow prisoners, of note among the apostles, who were in Christ before him.

The life speaks of the two together. Being dead with Andronicus to the world but living for Christ alone, the wonderful Junia drew many with him to the knowledge of God. They pulled down idol temples, built churches, drove unclean spirits out of men and healed the sufferings of the sick, and Andronicus preached, the page says, not in one city or country but everywhere.

They paid the common debt of nature as good payers do, and going from here they received from the Lord the crown of apostleship and of martyrdom, having suffered much from the unbelievers. Their holy relics, with those of many martyrs, were found in the places at Eugenia and confirmed by a revelation, of which the calendar writes on 22 February.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same civil day, on one page with [Andronicus](/saints/andronicus-the-apostle), and grounds the pair where the Romanian line does: saint.gr counts them both among the Seventy Apostles, whose common feast it puts on 4 January, and says they worked together with the Apostle Paul, who greets in his letter to the Romans «Andronicus and Junia, my kinsmen and my fellow prisoners, who are of note among the apostles, who also were in Christ before me». The page's own couplet for her plays on her name and the month — Junia died in the month of May, which is the first to come in before June — and its joint verse, on the seventeenth died Junia and Andronicus, agrees with the day the calendar keeps them.
