# Nikon of Optina

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The line for 25 iunie reads «Sfântul Nicon de la Optina», and his page has no Viață tab at all: what it carries is an akathist to him, prayers, icons, places of pilgrimage, monasteries and churches, articles about him and texts of his own — everything except an account of his life, a century, or a year.

The monastery in the name is the one thing the calendar itself says of him beyond the name: Optina, the hermitage of the Russian elders. No rank is printed on the day's line — not «Cuvios», not «Mucenic» — so none is recorded here, and no century is either.

A folder that would not survive somebody opening its citations is worse than no folder; so what stands here is the name, the day the Romanian church keeps him, and the plain statement that doxologia prints no life for him.

The Russian calendar prints what doxologia does not. days.pravoslavie.ru's line for 25 июня ст. ст. is «Прп. Никона Оптинского, исповедника (1931).», and behind it is a life. He was Николай Митрофанович Беляев in the world, born at Moscow on 26 September 1888 into a large merchant family, and he and his younger brother decided together to go into a monastery without knowing which: they cut a list of the Russian houses into strips, prayed, and drew the one that read «Козельская Введенская Оптина пустынь».

The brothers came to Optina on 24 February 1907 and were received into the skete's brotherhood that December. The elder Варсонофий took to Nicholas particularly, made him his secretary in 1908, relieved him of every obedience but the singing and the reading, and taught him as his own successor. He was clothed in the rassophore in 1910 and in the mantle on 24 May 1915, taking the name Никон after a martyr of that name; he was ordained hierodeacon in 1916 and hieromonk in 1917.

After the revolution the monastery was closed and the persecution began. «Умру, но не уйду» — I shall die, but I shall not leave — he had written in his diary while still a novice, and that was the mind of the brotherhood. He was arrested first in September 1919; the house was finally shut in the summer of 1923, and the abbot, after serving the last liturgy in the Kazan church, gave him its keys and blessed him to serve and to hear confessions. So he became the last of the Optina elders, and when he began to receive people he always gave his counsel in the words of the elders before him.

Driven out of the monastery in June 1924 he settled at Козельск and served in the Dormition church there. He was arrested again in June 1927 and spent three years in the camp at Кемперпункт, and was then sentenced to exile in the Archangel country. A doctor found advanced consumption in him before he was sent and advised him to ask for a different place; he asked a fellow exile instead, was told not to resist the will of God, and submitted.

He was moved to Пинега in August 1930, and being ill wandered a long time for lodging until a woman of Воепола took him in, charging him a high rent and heavy work besides. When at last he could not get up she began to drive him out, and another exiled monk of Optina carried him to his own village and nursed him. He communicated almost daily in his last months and died on 25 June/8 July 1931, having communicated that day and heard the canon for the departing read over him; twelve priests came together for his burial, and he was laid in the churchyard of Валдокурье by the monastic rite.
