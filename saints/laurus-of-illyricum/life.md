# Laurus of Illyricum

Laurus and his brother [Florus](/saints/florus-of-illyricum) were brothers in the flesh and in the spirit. They lived in the second century in Byzantium and then moved to Illyria; by trade they were stonemasons, taught the craft — and a God-pleasing life — by the Christians Proclus and Maximus. Lycaon, the governor of Illyria, sent them to a neighbouring district to work on a pagan temple that was being built; they laboured on the site, gave their earnings to the poor, kept a strict fast and prayed without ceasing.

One day the son of the local priest Mamertinus came carelessly up to the works and a splinter of stone struck and badly injured his eye; when the priest shouted at the brothers and would have beaten them, they told him that if he believed in the God they believed in his son would be healed — and they prayed with tears, signed the boy’s eye with the cross, and it was whole as before. Mamertinus and his son were baptised, and soon after suffered for Christ in the fire.

When the brothers had finished the temple they set a cross on it, called the Christians together and consecrated it in the name of the Lord Jesus with an all-night vigil; the governor of Illyria, learning of it, burned many of those Christians and had Florus and Laurus thrown alive into a well and the well filled with earth. Their relics were later revealed and taken to Constantinople. The Prologue tells it the same way for the Serbian calendar, with a long poem on the two brothers who cut stone and straightened souls with the cross.

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 18 August with his brother, and doxologia.ro prints the same life under both names. [Florus](/saints/florus-of-illyricum) and Laurus were brothers in the flesh and in the spirit, stonecutters taught their craft and their faith by the holy teachers Proclus and Maximus, who were killed for Christ before them. Sent by the governor of Illyricum to build a stone temple to the Greek gods for a ruler of another country, they were at length seized with all who were with them, among them Memertinus and his son; the others were condemned to the fire, and the two brothers, cruelly beaten, were sent to Lykion, governor of Illyricum, who found them unmoved and had them thrown into a deep well and covered with earth. Many years later their relics were found, pouring out healings of every sickness, and were carried with honour to Constantinople.
