# John (Prudentov)

John Prudentov was born in 1868 in the village of Bolshiye Dorki in the
Vyazniki district, of an old priestly line reaching back to the reign of
Tsar Alexis Mikhailovich; his great-grandfather, grandfather, father and he
himself all finished the Vladimir seminary and academy, and the seminary
gave the family its name, from the Latin for prudent. His great-grandfather
Paul Prudentov, a teacher at the seminary and confessor of the Dormition
convent, ran into its burning church in the fire of 1855 to carry out the
relics of St Abraham at great danger to himself while his own house and
everything in it burned, and is a locally venerated saint of Vladimir. John
was a good pastor who gave his whole self to the Church and a heart full of
Christ’s love to people, and to children most of all: at the church school
at Dorki he taught most of the subjects himself, built a teacher’s flat and
a boarding house for pupils from far villages in 1902, and when the school
was closed in 1918 went on teaching children at home and walked kilometres
to Pleshkovo and Kivernikovo to teach them there, until the authorities
forbade it; musical, he taught the children church singing himself and with
his brother Basil made a children’s choir reckoned the best in the district.
He had eight children, whom he loved without measure and brought up
strictly; the old people of the village, children then, call him nothing but
the good father. The house was open to everyone at feasts without
invitation; he set up a Christmas tree for the whole village at home or in
the school, with the children’s songs and verses, gathered the grown-ups
every Sunday for Sunday talks in which he told and explained the week’s
events in Russia, and kept a library, large for a village, for children and
adults. When the landowner Obolduyev opened a profitable drinking house in
the village and the peasants began to go to it, he went through the villages
preaching for the communes to close it, denounced the landowner to the
district and provincial authorities, and, the landowner having a friend at
the governor’s, wrote to St John of Kronstadt for his prayers, and received
a letter of comfort and an order of assistance from the chairman of the
council of ministers, Witte; the letter hung in the icon corner as a holy
thing through all the Soviet years, until the robbery of 1978 took it with
most of the icons. He carried the whole farm on his own shoulders beside his
priesthood and teaching, and in the civil war gave one of his two horses to
the front, insisting when they would not take a village priest’s horse — no,
no, take her, you need her more. He was a friend of the hieromartyrs Paul
Svetozarov and John Rozhdestvensky, who died in 1922 — Svetozarov’s prayer
book, inscribed for prayerful edification, went with him into exile — and of
the priests of the villages round, all of whom were arrested, exiled and
martyred.

In July 1920, in the hot days of the requisitions, two Bolshevik agitators
cycling from Palekh to Shuya stopped for water at Pustosh, argued with the
women coming from the hayfield about the merits of the Soviet power, and one
of them was beaten to death with pitchforks; the tocsin was rung, peasants
ran from the villages round, and his son Alexander, a student, persuaded
them to let the second man go in peace — who rode to Shuya and came back
with a punitive detachment that shot several unarmed people on the road and
reported a counter-revolutionary rising suppressed. Alexander, remembered by
his student cap and pince-nez, hid at Yuzha and then left for Moscow;
horsemen searched the priest’s house that evening in vain. In 1930 the
affair was laid on the father — that he had rung the tocsin, incited the
rising and agitated; arrested on 20 October 1930, he admitted nothing, but
the commission held his influence on the people extremely dangerous, citing
his call in 1921 not to give the church valuables to the Soviet power and
his talk in the church lodge between the liturgies of the peasants’ hard
life and of a power that corrupted the people and above all the young, and
on 25 December 1930 the troika of the Shuya OGPU exiled him three years to
the Northern region; he served it at Kolomino in the Tomsk district of
Siberia, where, they say, he was urged to lay down his orders and go home,
and answered the godless, I will serve my God as long as I am. He came home
in the autumn of 1933 in his seventh decade, his health broken, without
means — the council of the poor had closed the churches in 1929, the house
had been stripped and the outbuildings taken down — and the local
authorities did not leave the former servant of the cult alone, sending two
old invalids with two garden beds and one goat notices of firm tasks for the
state, rye, oats, a tonne of potatoes, hay, wool, meat and money within
three days, with threats of a new trial for non-fulfilment and cynical
answers to every petition, until his arrest in 1937. Charged again with
agitation against the collective farms, he answered the question of guilt, I
do not admit it, and the record has him saying that the collective farms
built by violence had ruined and beggared the farmers, who worked to
exhaustion and gave all their grain to the state and sat hungry, unshod and
unclothed, standing hours in the co-operative for their own bread, and that
the Soviet power persecuted religion, but religion had existed, existed and
would exist, and if the government tried to close the churches and lay
unbearable taxes on us we must all fight to the end of our lives and explain
to people that without religion there is no life for anyone. On 22 September
the troika of the Ivanovo NKVD sentenced him to death, and at eleven at
night on 25 September 1937 he was shot on the waste ground of the village of
Balino by Ivanovo, now the Balino cemetery, with [Theodore
Lebedev](/saints/theodore-lebedev) and the other priests condemned with him.
The last photograph in the secret file, taken a few hours before he went up
his Golgotha, shows a white-haired old man ready to depart and be with
Christ. He is kept on 12 September of the old style, 25 September, and in
the synaxis of the Ivanovo metropolia.
