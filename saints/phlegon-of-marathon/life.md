# Phlegon of Marathon

Phlegon is numbered among the seventy apostles. He was bishop at Marathon in Thrace, and he is one of those the apostle Paul greets in the sixteenth chapter of the letter to the Romans; his commemoration in the Orthodox Church is kept on 8 April. That is the whole of what doxologia prints for him besides the painter's rule: no birthplace, no year, no account of his preaching and no manner of death of his own.

The day keeps him with five other apostles — [Agabus](/saints/agabus-the-apostle), [Herodion](/saints/herodion-the-apostle), [Rufus](/saints/rufus-apostle-of-thebes), [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle) and [Hermes](/saints/hermes-apostle-of-dalmatia) — and the page for Agabus says of all six that they ended on the same day as a result of the torments they were put to.

Dionysius of Fourna, in his Hermeneia, says that the apostle Phlegon is to be painted as a young man without a beard, holding in his hand a rolled scroll, the sign of his apostolic preaching.
