# Phlegon of Marathon

Phlegon is numbered among the seventy apostles. He was bishop at Marathon in Thrace, and he is one of those the apostle Paul greets in the sixteenth chapter of the letter to the Romans; his commemoration in the Orthodox Church is kept on 8 April. That is the whole of what doxologia prints for him besides the painter's rule: no birthplace, no year, no account of his preaching and no manner of death of his own.

The day keeps him with five other apostles — [Agabus](/saints/agabus-the-apostle), [Herodion](/saints/herodion-the-apostle), [Rufus](/saints/rufus-apostle-of-thebes), [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle) and [Hermes](/saints/hermes-apostle-of-dalmatia) — and the page for Agabus says of all six that they ended on the same day as a result of the torments they were put to.

Dionysius of Fourna, in his Hermeneia, says that the apostle Phlegon is to be painted as a young man without a beard, holding in his hand a rolled scroll, the sign of his apostolic preaching.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, in the same company of four, and joins his end to Asyncritus's. saint.gr says that Phlegon, who became bishop of Marathon, and [Asyncritus](/saints/asyncritus-the-apostle), having preached the Gospel in various parts of the world, brought many unbelievers to the true faith; and that in the end they were tortured in various ways by Jews and Greeks, with the result that both died on the same day, and so received from the Lord the heavenly goods laid up for the true labourers of the Gospel. The page's couplet for him plays on the name: Phlegon, having quenched the burning flame of error, sees those whom David called a burning fire.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the six together on this day and gives this one a single line. Saint Phlegon, who is mentioned in the same place of the epistle, was bishop in the Thracian city of Marathon — and that locating of Marathon in Thrace is the Prologue's own: neither of the other two calendars read for this folder at that time says where the see lay, and no coordinate has been written for it here on the strength of one calendar's word — and the Russian calendar, read later and set down below, is a second book that says Thrace. The entry gives him no year and no account of his end of its own. It closes of all six that, like bees of Christ, they carried the honey of the Gospel about into various countries, suffering much for the love of Christ, and that all of them passed over into the eternal kingdom of Christ the beloved.

**The Russian church calendar keeps the six of them together on the Julian 8 April**, which is the day the Romanian and the Serbian keep, and its line is «Апп. от 70 Иродиона, Агава, Асинкрита, Руфа, Флегонта, Ерма и иже с ними (I).» — six genitives under one rank word, a century in a Roman numeral, and a company left open at the end, «и иже с ними», *and those with them*, which is a phrase no other calendar read for this folder prints. The six name links behind the line, pages 1871, 46, 282, 1886, 2313 and 791, all lead to one life of the company; three of them give in their own titles the see the day’s line withholds, and those same three close with «сщмч.», hieromartyr, which is a rank none of these folders carries. The rank is set down here and `types` is left as it was.

That life sets them in the number of the seventy apostles chosen by Christ and sent out to preach, points to the synaxis of the Seventy on 4 января, and then disposes of four of them in a single sentence: «апостол Руф, которого приветствует святой апостол Павел в Послании к Римлянам (Рим. 16, 11), был епископом в греческом городе Фивы; апостол Асинкрит (Рим. 16, 14) епископом в Гиркании (Малая Азия); апостол Флегонт – в городе Марафоне (Фракия), апостол Ерм – в Далматии». It closes that all of them bore cruel sufferings for their fearless service to Christ and were counted worthy of a martyr’s end — which is what the Romanian page says of all six and the Greek page says of two.

**A second book now puts Marathon in Thrace.** The sentence above says that the locating of the see in Thrace is the Ohrid Prologue’s own and that no coordinate was written on one calendar’s word; the Russian life says «апостол Флегонт – в городе Марафоне (Фракия)», with Thrace in the same brackets it uses for Hyrcania’s Asia Minor, so two of the four calendars now agree on it against two that say nothing. **No `locations` row follows**: neither page gives a coordinate, and a Thracian Marathon is a place this desk cannot point to. The Russian gives him no year, no account of his preaching and no death of his own, and the end it gives him is the one it gives all six in a closing sentence.

**The day prints one hymn for the company, and it is the first hymn in Russian this folder has had.** It is a kontakion in the second tone, «Явистеся Христовы ученицы и апостоли всечестнии», and it names all six of them in order before asking them to pray for the forgiveness of the sins of those who sing them. He is named fifth in it, «Асинкрите, Флегонте со Ермом», and the folder held no Russian hymn before.
