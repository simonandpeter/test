# Panegyrius of Malounta

saint.gr says at the outset that no life of Panegyrius survives and that he has no synaxarion, and that what it prints is gathered from his service and from the description of his icon. On that evidence he came, most probably, from the village of Malounta in the district of Nicosia, and kept his ascesis at a hermitage beside that village. He seems, the page says, to have taken up the ascetic life while young, and to have been a hieromonk, most likely made a monk first and then a priest at some nearby monastery.

The icon shows him vested in a phelonion as a priest, blessing with his right hand and holding a cross in his left. It was painted in 1708 and repaired again in 1841, and it carries both dates in writing: on the right, «1708. Έγινεν η παρούσα εικών, Μνήσθητι Άγιε του δούλου σου Χριστοδούλου Ιερέως μετά πάντος του οίκου», and on the left the note of its renewal under the committee of Charalambos the pilgrim. The icon is large, of the size that stands in an iconostasis, and it hung in the old church of the Chrysopantanassa at Malounta before it was moved, with others, to the new church of the village, which is dedicated to her as well.

The time in which he lived is unknown, the page says; but from his service it appears to have been the period of the Arab raids, when parts of Cyprus came at intervals under Arab power, for he taught the Christians the Christian way and turned them from islamisation, calling that religion «την μυθουργίαν των Αγαρηνών», the fable-making of the Hagarenes. Living in ascesis with the glory of the next life always in mind, he was given compunction and kept his life unstained, and God counted him worthy to become a wonderworker: he drove away destroying locusts and healed people sick of various diseases, and he fell asleep in peace. According to a local tradition of Malounta, traces and ruins were still to be seen some years ago south-east of the village, of a hermitage the people then believed was his.
