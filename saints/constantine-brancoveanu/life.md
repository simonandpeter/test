# Constantine Brâncoveanu

Constantine Brâncoveanu was born in 1654 of good family — descended on his father’s side from the voivode Matei Basarab, and on his mother’s the nephew of the voivode Șerban Cantacuzino, in whose reign, in 1688, the whole Scripture was first printed in Romanian. Fatherless from infancy, he was raised by his uncle the stolnic Constantin Cantacuzino, the most learned boyar of his day, who saw to his education; for his skill and wisdom he held high offices while still young. On 29 October 1688, after Șerban’s death, the boyars and dignitaries of the land pressed him to take the throne of Wallachia, and he was anointed by the patriarch of Constantinople, Dionysius IV, and the metropolitan Theodosius.

He began his reign by laying the foundation of his greatest monastery, Hurezi, where he prepared his own resting place, and the churches and monasteries he built or endowed across Wallachia are beyond counting; his generosity reached the Romanians of Moldavia and Transylvania, all the Orthodox patriarchates, the Christians of Syria, the Caucasus and the Arab lands, and the holy places of the East — Jerusalem, Athos, Sinai and the monasteries of Greece and its islands. He cared for the spiritual enlightenment of his people and the printing of books, and sought the alliance of Orthodox Russia, which in 1700 gave him the order of St Andrew.

After nearly twenty-five years of reign, in Holy Week 1714, betrayed by boyars who slandered him as plotting against the Turks, he was seized by soldiers of Sultan Ahmed III and taken with his sons and sons-in-law to the Porte, to the grief of the whole city. If these misfortunes are from God for my sins, he said as he left, his will be done; if they are the fruit of human malice for my ruin, may God forgive my enemies.

At Constantinople he and his eldest son were tortured for four months to make them give up their wealth, which the Turks supposed far greater than what they found — the wheel, an iron band about the head, burning with red-hot irons, the piercing of hands and feet — and at the last he was given the choice of Islam or death. He and his sons held to their faith, and on 15 August 1714, the feast of the Dormition, he was beheaded with his four sons Constantin, Ștefan, Radu and Matei and his counsellor Ianache, and crowned with the holy crown of martyrdom. The Russian calendar keeps him on 16 August (Julian).

The Romanian calendar keeps him on 16 August at the head of the day, and it keeps his household with him: doxologia.ro's line names «Constantin Vodă cu cei patru fii ai săi, Constantin, Ștefan, Radu și Matei, și sfetnicul Ianache», and the same day carries his wife. The life on that page says the Holy Synod of the Romanian Church numbered the prince, his four sons — [Constantin](/saints/constantin-son-of-brancoveanu), [Ștefan](/saints/stefan-brancoveanu), [Radu](/saints/radu-brancoveanu) and [Matei](/saints/matei-brancoveanu) — and his kinsman [Ianache Văcărescu](/saints/ianache-vacarescu) among the saints on 20–21 June 1992, with 16 August for their day; [Maria](/saints/maria-brancoveanu), who brought his relics home in 1720, was canonised on 1 July 2025.

The Greek calendar keeps him **on 21 Μαΐου**, not on the 16 August this folder stands on, and it is the same company: saint.gr's page for that day is headed for Constantine the new martyr «και οι συν αυτώ μαρτυρήσαντες», and those martyred with him. Its account agrees with this one in almost everything and adds a few particulars. It has the prince orphaned of his father at a year old, in a rising of the people in February 1655; brought up by his mother and by his grandparents Păuna and Constantin Cantacuzino in the family house at Bucharest; taught Greek and Latin; left heir of the whole property after the death of his two brothers; married to Marica, niece of Antonie Vodă of Popești; and raised to the highest offices from 1678 by his uncle Șerban Cantacuzino. It gives Mustafa Agha the arrest on 24 March 1714, the Great Tuesday, and a bitter journey of three weeks to Constantinople; and it names the order in which the swords fell — the treasurer Ianache first, then the eldest son, then Ștefan, Radu and Matei. It adds that the heads were set on poles, carried through the streets of the City and left three days at the first gate of the Seraglio before they too were thrown into the sea, and that Christians took them out secretly at the urging of the Ecumenical Patriarchate and buried them on Halki, outside the church of the monastery of the Mother of God that the emperor John V Palaiologos had built. Marica brought the relics secretly back to Romania in 1720, in the reign of Nicholas Mavrocordat, to the church of Saint George the New.
