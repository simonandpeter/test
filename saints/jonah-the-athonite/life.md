# Jonah the Athonite

The venerable martyr Jonah — in the world Ivan Andreevich Sankov — was born on 20 July 1879 into the devout family of a merchant, in the village of Soltsino in the Alpatyevo district of Zaraysk in the province of Ryazan. His father traded in bread in Moscow and the whole family lived there with him. In 1890, when Ivan was eleven, his mother fell gravely ill and on the doctors' advice went back to her own village, where the family had a house and a small holding, and she took the boy with her.

She died, and he returned to his father in Moscow, finished his schooling and began to trade alongside him; but the devout young man's heart inclined neither to trade nor to family life. In his family there was a tradition of dedicating one of the children to the service of God. So in 1897, at eighteen, he came to Athos and entered the monastery of Saint Panteleimon as a novice, and there, he said himself, his spiritual formation was completed. He was clothed with the name Jonah, made a deacon in 1905 and a hieromonk in 1913, and in 1914 was sent to the monastery's dependency in Constantinople, from which all Russians were expelled that July.

He was posted next to the dependency of Saint Panteleimon in Odessa, and as the godless authorities closed one church after another — the dependency in 1923, the Annunciation in 1927, All Saints by the graves in 1930 — he moved from each to the next. In 1930 letters from home told him that the priest of Alpatyevo had died and invited him to serve there, and he served in that church until his arrest. In the mass persecutions at the end of 1937 the NKVD questioned the village and collective-farm chairmen about him, and witnesses testified that when the church was to be closed and the building given over for a school he had told the faithful, who came to the meeting and prevented it.

He was arrested on 24 February 1938 and held in the prison of Kolomna. He refused to sign the warrant, and under questioning he refused the charge of counter-revolutionary propaganda and of stirring up the women against the closing of the church. On 7 June 1938 the troika of the NKVD sentenced him to be shot; he was taken to the Taganka prison in Moscow and the prison photographer took his picture on 13 June. The hieromonk Jonah (Sankov) was executed on 4 July 1938 and was commemorated in a common grave at the Butovo firing range below Moscow.
