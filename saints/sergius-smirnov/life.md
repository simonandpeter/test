# Sergius (Smirnov)

The Russian church calendar of the Сретенский monastery names him second of fifteen on 21 октября ст. ст., in a line of eleven priests, two deacons and two monks of 1937. He was born on 15 June 1893 in the village of Borok in the Poshekhonye uyezd of Yaroslavl province, the son of a priest, John Smirnov. He finished the school at Rybinsk, taught for two years in his own village, and went to the Yaroslavl seminary because his father insisted that his son give his life to God and to God's people.

He married Nadezhda, a devout peasant girl who sang in the choir, and in 1919 was ordained priest to the church at Sannikovo, forty kilometres from Borok. The parish was poor and had no house for its clergy; his wife's father helped them buy one. He served there until 1935 and four children were born to them. The calendar's life calls him a zealous pastor and a great lover of the poor, who shared his last piece of bread.

In 1935 a tax was demanded of him that he had no money to pay. His property was inventoried, the family was driven out of the house, and they moved from one rented room to another, for however well the peasants thought of their priest, fear was stronger and no householder dared keep him long. A parishioner gave him three kilogrammes of rye and he borrowed a mortar to pound it; someone reported that the priest was milling grain, and he was arrested and sentenced to a year in a corrective labour camp for the unpaid tax. While he was away the whole village of Sannikovo was cleared of its people and flooded under the Rybinsk reservoir.

Released after the year, he went to Tver for a parish and was sent to the church of the Exaltation at Teblishi. He lived there alone for eight months until they gave him a small house, and in April 1937 brought his family. He was arrested on 11 October 1937, the third day after St Sergius's day, and everything in the house was taken, down to an old worn cassock. His wife and his daughter Maria came to the prison at Keverichi the next day and saw him at the barred window; he called out to his wife to hold on and not weep, because the children were on her shoulders now, and to his daughter to obey her mother and help her. A guard took the girl by the collar and threw her back from the window.

The head of the local NKVD offered him a teacher's post in the school at Teblishi if he would give up his priesthood. He refused outright: he was a priest, he said, the son of a priest, and his great-great-grandfather had been a priest. A week at Keverichi, then the prison at Bezhetsk and the interrogations, in which he admitted nothing but that he had held the services in church; after a while he would say nothing more at all, not wishing to bear false witness against himself or against anyone near him. He was fifty-four, with a wife, two daughters and a son, the youngest barely seven.

The indictment was drawn up on 20 October and sent to the NKVD troika, which on 1 November sentenced him to be shot. He was shot on 3 November 1937, and was numbered among the saints at the Jubilee Council of Bishops in August 2000. The day page prints troparia and kontakia for other saints it heads and nothing under his name.
