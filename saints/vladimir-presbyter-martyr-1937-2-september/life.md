# Vladimir (Morinsky)

Vladimir Alexandrovich Morinsky was born on 24 June 1883 in St Petersburg;
his father sang in church choirs. Orphaned early, the boy was given to be
brought up in a private church choir under patronage, and in 1903 moved
with the choir to Moscow, where he sang in the Moscow churches; in 1912 he
got a place as singing teacher at Klin in Moscow province and for half a
year was also director of the cathedral choir, then moved to the village
of Spas-Kvashonki in the Kalyazin district of Tver province, teaching
singing in the school and leading the church choir. In 1916 he was called
up and served as a private in the 238th reserve rifle regiment at Oryol;
in 1917 he returned to Moscow and taught singing in the schools, and from
1924 worked in various Soviet offices, sang in a church choir and prepared
for holy orders. He and his wife had no children and took an orphan boy to
raise. On 27 July 1934 he was ordained deacon and in August priest, to the
church of the martyrs Adrian and Natalia at Losinoostrovsk outside Moscow,
today within the city; he fulfilled the pastor’s duties with zeal and was
soon known as a diligent man of prayer and an ascetic — witnesses at the
inquiry into his case later told of healings by his prayers. On 1 April
1935 he was arrested and put in the Butyrka prison, charged with
systematically carrying on anti-Soviet agitation, dealing in the hysterics
of the possessed and spreading provocative rumours of the persecution of
the Orthodox Church in the USSR; he did not accept the charge, and on 8
June the Special Board of the NKVD sentenced him to three years in the
camps, served in the Burma section of the Karaganda camp, where he ran the
fodder store. On 1 September 1937 he was arrested in the camp in the group
case of the hieromartyr Bishop
[Damascene (Tsedrik)](/saints/damascene-of-starodub), charged that, a
prisoner serving his sentence in the Karlag, he continued his
counter-revolutionary activity — Tsedrik, Morinsky and Lilov gathered and
sang prayers in the postal building, he read the evening prayers more than
once with Goryachev, and they urged a prisoner not to renounce his
priesthood; he did not admit guilt of counter-revolutionary activity. By
decision of the troika of the NKVD for the Karaganda region of 10 September
he was shot on 15 September 1937 — 2 September of the old style, the day
the Russian calendar keeps him — on one day with the hieromartyrs Bishop
Damascene, the archpriest
[Euthymius (Goryachev)](/saints/euthymius-presbyter-martyr-1937) and
others. He was glorified by the jubilee Council of Bishops in 2000; the
life is by Igumen Damascene (Orlovsky), and the calendar keeps him also in
the synaxis of the saints of Moscow.
