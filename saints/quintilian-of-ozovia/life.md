# Quintilian of Ozovia

Quintilian is one of three men of the village of Ozovia whom doxologia.ro's calendar keeps together on 28 April, and the life it prints is the passion of the three. It begins with an edict of Diocletian and Maximian, quoted in full, commanding that all under Roman rule be brought to the honour of the gods, and warning that whoever should be found in any place naming the name of Christ would bring the emperors' anger upon himself.

When the peoples had gathered and sacrificed at the bidding of the proconsul Tarquinius, an idolater came the next day and denounced three men who had not obeyed and who, he said, worshipped One God in the heavens. The proconsul's servants found them in Ozovia at prayer, bound them with iron chains, and took them to the city of Dorostolon, in the south of what the page calls today's Dobruja, where Tarquinius and the other proconsul, Gabinius, were together.

The three passed the night in prayer for strength to overcome their adversaries. In the morning they were brought before the judgement seat, and it was [Maximus](/saints/maximus-of-ozovia) who answered first for all three, saying that he and his brothers were Christians by the faith of Christ and that by human custom he was called Maximus. The life gives no separate word or answer of Quintilian's own; throughout, the three speak together.

They were beaten, and asked again whether they would sacrifice; they answered as one that, strengthened by their God, they cared nothing for the torments and would not sacrifice to demons. Put back in prison until after the seventh hour, they were brought out again, and again refused. Gabinius threatened to send them to barbarian places to be beheaded, and they told him to do what he had said.

They were led back to the same place, which the life here spells Ozevia, and beheaded there with Maximus and [Dadas](/saints/dadas-of-ozovia) on the twenty-eighth day of April, in the reign of Diocletian and Maximian, Tarquinius and Gabinius being proconsuls. The page gives no year.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them a fortnight earlier than the Romanian, on 13 April, and adds what they were. saint.gr says these saints lived in the years of the emperor Maximian, whose reign it dates 285 to 305, and of Diocletian, 284 to 305, and under the consuls Gabibius and Tarquinius; that they came from Ozovia, and that they were readers of the Church. When the persecution against the Christians came they were imprisoned; afterwards they were beaten hard and thrown back into prison, and there the idolaters made many and various attempts to draw them into denying Christ.

They stayed faithful to the end, the page says, and were counted worthy of the martyr's crown, being beheaded for their faith. The Christians gathered their holy relics up with reverence and carried them to Constantinople, to the church of the Most Holy Mother of God «ἐν τοὶς Βιγλεντίου», where their synaxis used to be kept. Its couplet asks who these are that lie here without their heads, and answers: Quintillianus, Maximus and Dadas.

The Serbian church keeps the three of them on this day, with the Romanian calendar and not with the Greek, which keeps them a fortnight earlier. The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the whole of them in three sentences: they suffered in the time of Diocletian; they were judged and tortured by the voivode Tarquinius; and after imprisonment and torments they were beheaded with the sword. **That reign is the only time it names**, and because the book prints no bounds for it no year was recorded here on its account, so this folder stands undated as the other two calendars left it.
