# Sabbatius of Antioch

Sabbatius was one of the three martyrs Trophimus, Sabbatius and Dorymedon, who were martyred under the emperor Probus and the governor of Antioch Heliodorus, in 278. When Trophimus and Sabbatius, finding themselves in Antioch, saw the manifold sinful orgies held in honour of Apollo — the soul-destroying festival of Apollo, the Romanian Lives say, at which the citizens as their custom was passed the whole day in sacrifices, drunkenness, dancing and every dissipation, and to which two strangers came from outside — they could not hold back and publicly condemned the sinful madness; quickly seized and brought to court, they boldly declared that they were Christians. The governor Heliodorus had them scourged without mercy, so that their flesh was cut in pieces, and there Sabbatius breathed his last; Trophimus was taken to another, harsher governor, Dionysius Perennius, who flayed him with iron claws and threw him half-dead into prison. There a senator, Dorymedon, visited him, saw his martyrdom and was confirmed in the faith of Christ, and when the governor learned of it he tortured Dorymedon harshly — stripping him, the Romanian Lives say, hanging him up and burning his sides with red-hot iron spikes — and then threw the two as food to the beasts in the arena; but the hungry bear and the bloodthirsty leopard stood at their feet like tame lambs, and so did the maddened lion loosed after them, and when the executioners saw that the beasts would not touch them they beheaded them at once; the verses say Trophimus and Dorymedon, breathing one breath, received one end of life from the sword, and Sabbatius, scraped with sharp irons, reached as a martyr the divine sabbath-rest. The Greek and Romanian calendars keep the three on 19 September with a troparion and kontakion — God praised in Trinity glorified the trinity of martyrs.
