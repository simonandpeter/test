# Martha of Aza

[Thecla](/saints/thecla-of-aza), [Mariamne](/saints/mariamne-of-aza), Martha, [Mary](/saints/mary-of-aza) and [Ennatha](/saints/ennatha-of-aza) were five canonical virgins — nuns — who lived in the years of the Persian king Shapur, about 330, with a priest named Paul near a village called Aza. Paul was rich and loved money, and he served and sang with them and hoarded for himself whatever the virgins gave him. When it became known how rich he was, the Persians demanded, on pain of death, that he and the virgins with him either deny their faith or hand over his treasure. He chose his wealth and took up the religion of the Persians, and the five, who would not follow him and deny Christ, he slaughtered with his own hands; but he did not enjoy his riches, for the next night the Persians strangled him to take them.

The Greek synaxarion tells this on 9 June, and on 26 September, where their memory is repeated, says only that their money-loving spiritual father killed them.

*After the Ορθόδοξος Συναξαριστής (saint.gr), 26 Σεπτεμβρίου — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/2501/saint.aspx), and its entry for 9 June — [the entry](https://www.saint.gr/1834/saint.aspx); read 17 September 2026.*
