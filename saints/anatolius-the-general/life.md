# Anatolius the General

saint.gr's whole account of him is one sentence. Anatolius was a soldier — «Ο Άγιος Ανατόλιος ήταν στρατιωτικός» — who, seeing the martyrdom of George, confessed Christ and at the last was martyred by the sword. The martyrdom meant is the one the calendar keeps on this same day, and the confession is made in the sight of it: seeing what George endured, he declared himself a Christian, and was beheaded for it.

The couplet the page sets over him is the only other thing it gives: Anatolius, having set like the sun by the cutting off of his head, saw the eastern light of the Lord that is known by the mind. Beyond the word στρατιωτικός, soldier, and the epithet στρατηλάτης that the day's line adds, it says nothing of his rank, his legion, his country or his years, and it names no judge and no relic.

The page prints no hymn for him. What it does print is a link back to [George the Trophy-bearer](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer), whose passion is the occasion of his own, and that is where the whole of his story is.

The Russian calendar keeps him with the great-martyr and the empress on one day, and says in two places why. The short notice under his own name is the whole of the reason: «Видя веру и мужество св. Георгия, эти воины уверовали во Христа и открыто заявили об этом. Их тут же обезглавили.» — seeing the faith and the courage of St George, these soldiers believed in Christ and declared it openly, and they were beheaded there and then. The long life of the company puts the moment exactly. When the saint rose up healed from the wheel and the pagans crowding into the temple were made to see that a wonder had really happened, many of them believed in the living God of the Christians; and «Два знатных сановника, святые Анатолий и Протолеон, тайные христиане, тотчас открыто исповедали Христа» — two noble dignitaries, secret Christians, at once confessed Christ openly, and at the emperor’s order were put to the sword on the spot, without any trial.

Two smaller things come off the same pages. The calendar gives him no epithet in the heading of his own name page, which reads simply «Мученик Анатолий»; but the caption of the icon on his life page reads «Мч. Анатолий Никомидийский», setting him at Nicomedia, which is also where his companion’s own name page sets that man. The city is recorded here and was not written into the name this folder stores, because a caption is weaker evidence than a heading. And the Moscow parish whose site the calendar links is a memorial church of the holy martyrs Anatolius and Protoleon together, which is how the two are kept.
