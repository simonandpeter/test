# Amphilochius (Makris) of Patmos

Amphilochius — Athanasius Makris in the world — was born on 13 February 1889
on Patmos. He was tonsured on 27 August 1906 at the monastery of St John the
Theologian and given the name Amphilochius, and on 23 March 1913, at
twenty-four, received the great schema at the kathisma of Apollo from the
ascetic and confessor Macarius Antoniadis of Samos; he was ordained deacon
on 27 January 1919 by Metropolitan Agathangelus of Kos in the church of St
Nicholas on Kos, and priest on Thomas Sunday, 5 April of the same year, by
Metropolitan Constantine Vantzalidis of Samos in St Spyridon’s at Vathy. He
served as priest of the monastery of Patmos from 1920 to 1926 and as head of
the holy Cave of the Apocalypse from 1926 to 1932; on 14 November 1935 he
was elected abbot of the monastery of Patmos, in 1937 founded the convent of
the Annunciation, and in 1939 and 1940 served at the shrine of the Panagia
Diasozousa. He was a friend and spiritual child of St Nectarius of Aegina,
and the spiritual father of many great figures of the Church, Greek and
foreign, and the inspirer of the mission in Africa and elsewhere — Father
Chrysostom Papasarantopoulos and Metropolitan Amphilochius (Tsoukos) of
Ganos were his spiritual children — and Metropolitan Kallistos Ware of
Diokleia says that he came to Orthodoxy through knowing the elder, and
remembers him, when Kallistos was a deacon at the monastery in the sixties,
saying to them: do you know that God gave us one more commandment, which is
not in Scripture? It is the commandment to love the trees.

The archimandrite Paul Nikitaras, who wrote his life, tells of his death:
all his life he was tormented by illness, sensitive to colds, visited by
influenza every year; at the end of March 1970 he caught pneumonia, in the
Great Fast, and would hardly take a little milk; he gave everyone the
counsel each needed, for he had the gift of foresight, and when his
spiritual children tried to keep him a few days alive on drips he begged
them, let me go, my good children, my hour has come — why, elder, will you
not stay with us this Pascha? — and only at the third asking, and as a
confession, told how he knew: blessed Paul, I saw the Panagia and the
Theologian a little while ago and asked them to let me stay with my children
this Pascha too, and they said, it cannot be; the decision is taken; you
will keep Pascha in heaven with us. His last counsels of the 14th, 15th and
16th of April were written down — in the kingdom of heaven peace and joy
reign, my child; have love among yourselves; be children of love and you
will not fear the devil; our citizenship is in heaven, and here we
half-live, so let us not grieve whatever befalls; glory to God for all
things, which we must say whether things go well or ill; where there is
egoism there is no Spirit of God; he who has no love cannot be called a
Christian — and to the doctor of Patmos at noon on the last day, I wish from
my heart that you become a missionary doctor, for the Christian doctor’s
approach to the sick plays a great part. He died on 16 April 1970, a
Thursday, at a quarter past two in the afternoon, in full clarity of his
senses, his face taking on a heavenly look of joy and peace, and was buried
on 17 April in the cemetery of the Annunciation. The same life tells how, in
his cell at the monastery, he heard a Helen of Ikaria calling him to come
and save her, went down to the harbour, found by a wonder a sailing boat
leaving for Ikaria, arrived sea-beaten, asked whether there was a widow
Helen and heard that she had lost her husband days before, went straight to
her street without resting, and met a distraught woman running — Helen,
where are you going? I came for you — who confessed that she had been on her
way to drown herself in the sea; how sister Euphrosyne smelt such a
fragrance at his grave that he stands in her mind as a saint of the fathers’
kind; and how a spiritual daughter who visited him in November 1954 and was
lodged two nights in the convent tower was moved on the third at his
insistence, and that night lightning struck exactly that bed. Among his
sayings: Christ wants to take out your thorns, for he is a gardener, and you
want your peace; love the One and even the beasts will love you; as a man
grows simpler he grows godlike; a pure heart passes easily through the gates
of heaven; hospitality is the greatest of the virtues — in the face of every
stranger, my child, I see Christ himself; it is not our works that will save
us, my sister, but the boundless mercy of God. The Holy and Sacred Synod of
the Ecumenical Patriarchate numbered him among the saints on 29 August 2018;
the Greek Church keeps him on 16 April, and the Romanian calendar keeps the
translation of his relics on 19 September, which is the line this entry
stands under.
