# Porphyrios of Kapsokalyvia

Porphyrios was born on 7 February 1906 in the village of Saint John in Karystia, on Euboea, and was Evangelos Bairaktaris in the world; his parents, Leonidas and Eleni, were devout and God-loving, and his father was the cantor of the village. The family met with great want, and at only eight years old the child had to look for work, first at Chalkida and then at Piraeus; but very early he showed a call to the monastic life.

At twelve or thirteen, having barely finished the second class of primary school, he set out from the world for the Holy Mountain, wishing to imitate Saint [John Kalyvites](/saints/john-the-hut-dweller), whose life he had read as a small boy and for whom he had great reverence. He went to the Athonite desert, and namely to the skete of the Holy Trinity at Kavsokalyvia, belonging to the Great Lavra, where he lived nearly six years in the cell of Saint George, under two strict elders, the hieromonks Panteleimon, who died in 1952, and Ioanichie, brothers in the flesh. "Obedience," he said afterwards, "I gave myself to it with joy, with love. That absolute obedience saved me... Because of it God gave me the gift."

At only fourteen he was tonsured a monk with the name Nikitas, in honour of the elder Panteleimon's own elder, the monk Nikitas, who had lived an ascetic life at the end of the nineteenth century in the cell of Saint Demetrius; and two years later he received the great and angelic schema, in the great church of the skete, as the old Kavsokalyvite custom was. "There were a hundred and twenty-seven hermits in the skete then," he remembered. "Such obediences, such a beautiful paradisal life!" With his elders he carved wood — dishes finely worked, and letter-knives with flowered ornament — a handicraft much cultivated in the skete in that time.

In 1925, at nineteen, a serious illness obliged him, with his elders' blessing, to leave the Holy Mountain and return to Euboea, where he settled at the monastery of Saint Charalampos at Lefka near his own village. In 1926 he met the archbishop of Sinai, Porphyrios III, who recognised his spiritual gifts and ordained him priest in July 1927, giving him the name Porphyrios by which he would become known throughout the Orthodox world. When twelve years of his service, chiefly as a confessor, were done, and the monastery of Saint Charalampos had become a convent, he settled at the monastery of Saint Nicholas at Ano Vateia, also on Euboea.

In 1940, shortly before the storm of the Second World War broke over Greece, he settled in Athens, where on 12 October he was made parish priest and confessor at the chapel of Saint Gerasimos in the polyclinic near Omonia square in the centre of the city; at the same time, to keep his parents and some near relatives whom he cared for, he worked with his hands in a poultry farm, a knitting workshop and an incense workshop in turn. He retired from Saint Gerasimos in 1970 but stayed on three years more until a successor was found, and in 1973 settled at the monastery of Saint Nicholas at Kallisia in Attica.

In 1978 he founded the convent of the Transfiguration of the Saviour at Tourkovounia in Athens, in the modest stone house that had been his lodging since 1948, and in the early 1980s moved to Milesi in Malakasa. On 8 September 1984, at his own request and on the petition of the skete of Kavsokalyvia, the Great Lavra granted him the cell of Saint George at Kavsokalyvia, where he had been tonsured; and for all his broken health and his heavy spiritual timetable he managed to visit Kavsokalyvia many times.

He fell asleep in the Lord in that cell, and the funeral was held the next day, with an all-night vigil in the venerable church of the skete and all the Kavsokalyvite fathers present; everything to do with the burial was done according to the detailed directions he had himself given and his disciples had written down, and for its sake the vigil of the Entry of the Mother of God into the Temple was moved to the eve. On the cross set over the simple earthen grave was written: "Hieromonk Porphyrios. Fallen asleep in the Lord on 19 November 1991." Twenty-two years later, on 27 November 2013, the Holy Synod of the Ecumenical Patriarchate under Patriarch Bartholomew entered his name in the calendar of the Orthodox Church.
