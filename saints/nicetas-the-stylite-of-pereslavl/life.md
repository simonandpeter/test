# Nicetas the Stylite of Pereslavl

saint.gr says the holy venerable-martyr Nicetas lived in the twelfth century in the city of Pereslavl in Russia. He worked as a tax-collector for the prince Dolgoruky and exacted from the taxpayers enormous sums which they were obliged to give for the building of the city and of a church; and so many years passed. But the merciful God, who wills that all should be brought to repentance and be saved, did not abandon Nicetas, and prepared the road of his return.

One day when Nicetas went into the church he heard the words of the Prophet Isaiah: «Λούσασθε καὶ καθαροὶ γίνεσθε, ἀφέλετε τὰς πονηρίας ἀπὸ τῶν ψυχῶν ὑμῶν ἀπέναντι τῶν ὀφθαλμῶν μου, παύσασθε ἀπὸ τῶν πονηριῶν ὑμῶν, μάθετε καλὸν ποιεῖν, ἐκζητήσατε κρίσιν, ρύσασθε ἀδικούμενον, κρίνατε ὀρφανῷ καὶ δικαιώσατε χήραν» — wash and be clean, put away the wickedness from your souls before my eyes, cease from your wickednesses, learn to do good, seek out judgement, deliver the wronged, judge for the orphan and do justice for the widow. His sleeping conscience woke and he was brought to repentance. He ran at once to the monastery of Pereslavl-Zalessky, which was dedicated to Saint Nicetas, fell at the feet of the spiritual father and confessed his sins; and the old confessor, to test whether the man confessing was sincere, set him a penance.

What the page records after his death is the history of the visible signs of his martyrdom and his asceticism. Robbers took the chains he had worn, thinking them precious, and when they had got away and understood that the chains were made of iron they threw them into the river Volga. But God willed to honour those signs too: one night the devout old monk Symeon, who was practising asceticism at the monastery of the Apostles Peter and Paul of Yaroslavl, saw three shafts of light above the river. The fathers of the monastery ran at once to see what was happening, and saw with awe the chains of the venerable Nicetas floating; and they took them up with reverence and carried them to his tomb. When, between the years 1420 and 1425, they opened the venerable one's tomb, they found his holy relics incorrupt.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and tells his beginning, which the Greek page does not. As a young man, it says, he lived an unbridled and vicious life. Going into a church once by chance, he heard the words of the prophet Isaiah — wash yourselves, and you shall be clean of sin — and the words went down deep into his heart and made a whole revolution in his life. Nicetas left his house, and his wife, and his property, and went away to a monastery near Pereyaslavl, where he struggled with hard labours until his death.

He laid chains upon himself and shut himself up in a pillar, and for that he was called the Stylite. God gave him great grace, so that he healed people of various torments; and so he healed the prince of Chernigov, Michael, of a palsy. The corpus keeps one prince Michael of Chernigov, whom this same book commemorates in September, but he died in the middle of the next century and cannot be the man healed here, and the Prologue is left unidentified rather than made to agree.

Certain evildoers saw the chains on him, and by their shining thought that they were made of silver; and for that they killed him one night and took the chains away. That happened on 16 May 1186, the Prologue says, where the Greek page gives the year 1196, and the 16 May is eight days off the 24 May both calendars keep him on. After his death he appeared to a certain elder Symeon and ordered him, when the chains were found, to lay them in the grave beside his body.

The Russian church calendar keeps him on 24 мая ст. ст. and gives him a full life. He was a native of Pereslavl Zalessky and had charge of the collection of the state taxes and dues. In 1152 the prince Юрий Долгорукий moved the town, and the stone church of the All-merciful Saviour with it, to a new place, and because of the cost of building the town and the church the taxes were gathered from the townsmen with a heavy hand; and he, who directed that gathering, plundered them without mercy and collected huge sums for himself, and so it went on for many years.

Once he came into the church and heard the words of the prophet Исаия: wash you, and you shall be clean, put away the evils from your souls, learn to do good, deliver the wronged, judge for the orphan and do right by the widow. The words went to the bottom of his heart like thunder, and he lay awake all night over «Измыйтеся и чисти будете». In the morning he decided to ask his friends in and forget the horrors of the night in merry talk; and when his wife began to cook the dinner for the guests she suddenly saw in the boiling pot now a human head, now a hand, now a foot. She called her husband in terror and he saw the same. His sleeping conscience woke in him, and he understood clearly that in his exactions he had been acting as a murderer. «Увы мне, много я согрешил! Господи, наставь меня на путь Твой!» — woe is me, I have sinned much; Lord, set me upon thy way — and with those words he ran out of the house.

Three versts from Pereslavl stood a monastery of the great-martyr Nicetas, and there he came, and fell weeping at the abbot’s feet: save a perishing soul. The abbot, meaning to test whether his repentance was real, gave him his first obedience: to stand three days at the monastery gate and confess his sins to everyone who passed. He took it with deep humility; and when after three days the abbot remembered him and sent a monk to see what he was doing at the gate, the monk did not find him there, but found him lying in a marsh, covered with mosquitoes and midges and his body all blood. Then the abbot came himself with the brethren and asked, my son, what art thou doing to thyself; and he answered, father, save a perishing soul. The abbot clothed him in a hair shirt, brought him into the monastery himself and tonsured him.

He spent his days and nights in prayer, in the singing of psalms and in the reading of the lives of the holy ascetics; with the superior’s blessing he put heavy irons on himself and dug two deep wells at the places of his labours; and soon he increased his labour, digging a deep round pit and standing there at burning prayer with a cap of stone on his head, after the manner of the stylites of old. Only the blue sky and the stars of the night were to be seen from the bottom of that pillar-well, and a narrow underground passage led under the church wall, by which he went into the church for the services.

So labouring in the house of the great-martyr Nicetas, he ended his own life by a martyr’s death. One night his kinsmen, who used to come to him for his blessing, were taken with his shining irons and crosses, thought them silver, and resolved to have them: in the night of 24 мая 1186 they took the covering off the pillar apart, killed him, took off the crosses and the irons, wrapped them in coarse cloth and fled. Before the morning service the sacristan, coming for his blessing, found the roof taken apart and told the abbot, and the abbot and the brethren hurried to the pillar and saw the saint killed, and a fragrance coming from his body.

The murderers meanwhile stopped on the bank of the Volga to divide the spoil, and saw with astonishment that it was not silver but iron, and threw the irons into the river. That same night Симеон, a devout elder of the Yaroslavl monastery of the apostles Peter and Paul, saw three bright beams of light over the Volga, and told the superior of his house and the elder of the city; and a gathering of priests and a great many townsmen came down to the river and saw three crosses and the irons floating «яко древо в водах волжских». They were carried with reverence to the house of the great-martyr Nicetas and laid on his tomb, and healings followed. About 1420 to 1425 the metropolitan Фотий of Moscow blessed the opening of his relics; the abbot and brethren sang the service and uncovered the birch bark in which the incorrupt body was wrapped, and the grave suddenly closed itself with earth and the relics remained hidden. A side chapel in his name was raised between 1511 and 1522, and in the nineteenth century the archpriest А. Свирелин composed him an akathist.
