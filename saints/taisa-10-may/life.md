# Taisa (10 May)

The Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives her one paragraph, and it is all that is written here. Taisa was a rich girl, a Christian, in Egypt. She resolved never to marry, and gave her property away to the monks of the desert. When all of it was spent she gave herself over to a dissolute life.

The hermits heard of it and begged [John Colobos](/saints/john-colobos) to go to her, and he came down to Alexandria and began to weep in front of her. When she heard that the elder was weeping for her sins she repented on the spot, left her house and everything in it, and went out into the desert after the saint. One night, while she slept and John stood at prayer, he saw angels come down in a great light and take Taisa's soul away. And John learned from it that her repentance, which lasted a moment but was warm, was more pleasing to God than the long outward repentance of many hermits.

The book gives no year for anything in the account, and no place beyond Egypt and Alexandria. She is not the other penitent of this name whom the same book keeps on 8 October and whom this corpus holds in a folder of her own: that one was put to the trade by her own mother, was brought to repentance by a different elder of the Egyptian desert, lived three years shut in a cell on bread and water, and died of an illness about the year 340. Two days, two elders, two lives, in one book.

The Russian church calendar keeps her on 10 мая ст. ст. and gives her two lives where the folder had none. Both put her in Egypt in the fifth century, a young Christian left an orphan by rich parents, who at first lived devoutly and gave herself to almsgiving and the care of the sick; the monks who came in from the desert to sell their baskets in the city used often to lodge in her house, and she was loved and respected by everybody. After some years of it her estate was spent and she came to want, fell in with ill-disposed people, and her life grew more and more disordered.

The monks of the desert skete who had been her guests were grieved when they heard of the change, and asked their abba, Иоанн Колов, to take the trouble of visiting her. He came and sat down beside her, looked steadily into her eyes, bowed his head and began to weep bitterly. When she asked why, he said that he saw satan playing on her face, and how should he not weep — what had she found wanting in Jesus, that she had turned to the things against Him? She trembled at it and cried out, «Отец! Есть ли для меня покаяние?» — Father, is there repentance for me? He said there was. «Тогда веди меня куда знаешь», she answered, and rose and went after him in tears.

The elder wondered only that she had given no orders about her property and her things and had said goodbye to nobody. Night fell as they reached the desert; he made her a pillow of sand and another for himself a little way off, signed hers with the cross and told her to sleep there. In the morning he went to wake her and found her already dead. He was greatly grieved, thinking her soul lost because she had had no time to repent, to communicate or to become a nun — and then heard a voice: one hour of her repentance had been accepted above the long repentance of others who do not deny themselves so in repenting. The second of the two lives tells it otherwise, and as kindly: the elder woke in the night to a light coming down from heaven to the place where she lay, and saw holy angels bearing her soul up; he buried her with prayer, went back to the skete and told the monks, and they all gave thanks to God for His mercy to her, who had repented in a single hour like the wise thief.
