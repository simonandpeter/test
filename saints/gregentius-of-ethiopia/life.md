# Gregentius of Ethiopia

Gregentius came from the city of Milan, and his parents were called Agapius and Theodota. The life says he took the divine seed like good ground and bore fruit a hundredfold, and that when he was grown he showed himself obedient and a doer of the commandments of Christ. He was ordained deacon, gave himself to fasting and a hard manner of life, and from that day worked signs and wonders.

Coming back to Milan he found, in a place withdrawn from the world, a very wise old man, from whom he learned everything that would happen to him afterwards; the Apostle Peter, appearing to him face to face, told him the same things later. In those days, while Justin governed the empire of the Romans, Elesbaan king of the Ethiopians wrote to Proterius, patriarch of Alexandria, asking him to send a perfect man as bishop. The corpus keeps a Proterius of Alexandria under Marcian, a century earlier, and whether the calendar means the same man is not settled here, so he is named without a link.

The patriarch grieved, not knowing where such a man was to be found, until the blessed Mark appeared to him and told him of Gregentius, who had lately come to the city and was lodging with a Christian. He took him with great joy, ordained him bishop, and sent him at once into Ethiopia with letters and everything proper to a hierarch. The king embraced him on the strength of the letters and received him as an apostle and a worker of wonders, and gave him every authority.

He ordained priests and deacons, consecrated the churches the king had newly built in Arabia, and baptised many Jews, for almost the whole people had been Jewish before. He convinced their teachers and elders out of the Scriptures and with proofs, astonished them with his miracles, and brought them all to the true faith; then he gave the people laws. Having shepherded his flock well and worked many wonders, he reposed in peace at a great age. The page gives no year.
