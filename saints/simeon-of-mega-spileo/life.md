# Simeon of Mega Spileo

Simeon and Theodore were brothers in the flesh, born at Thessalonica at the beginning of the fourth century. They came of a devout and educated family, and as young men learned theology, philosophy, rhetoric and poetry. Drawn by the desert labours that were flourishing in their time, they embraced monasticism and lived in turn on Mount Olympus, Mount Ossa and Mount Pelion; later they travelled to the Holy Land and to Mount Sinai, and on their return from Sinai were ordained priests by Maximus, bishop of Jerusalem.

While they were still at Jerusalem the two young monks had the same dream, in which the Mother of God, attended by the apostles Paul, Andrew and [Luke](/saints/luke-the-evangelist), commanded them to go into Achaia to find an icon of her that Luke had carved there. Taking the bishop’s blessing they set out for the north-eastern coast of the Peloponnese, which had been made Christian by those same three preachers of the Gospel: Paul at Corinth, Andrew at Patras and Luke in the outer parts of Achaia.

Coming into Achaia they prayed again for guidance, and that night were told in another dream to go to the river Bouras, two hours away, where they would find a devout shepherdess, [Euphrosyne](/saints/euphrosyne-of-mega-spileo), who would lead them to the icon. The next morning, as they came near the place, they found her waiting for them. She greeted them and called them by name; and when they hesitated, afraid of her apparent sight in the spirit, she called upon the name of the Mother of God and struck with her staff the rock on which she stood, and a spring of clear sweet water sprang out of it. That spring remains to this day and bears her name.

When Euphrosyne had told them how she had found the icon, the monks climbed to the cave and found everything as she had said. They took the icon down reverently, carried it out and went back to clean the cave; and having lit a fire to burn the brushwood and the filth, they waited outside for the flames to die. Suddenly a huge serpent, driven out by the smoke, came crawling from the cave as though it had wings, and as the monks and Euphrosyne drew aside from its path a flash of light out of the icon pinned it to the ground.

Going back in they found a table where, by the monastery’s tradition, the evangelist Luke had served the Liturgy and written his Gospel. Later they widened the cave, raised a little chapel there and set a few cells about it, and the monastery they founded took the name of Mega Spileo, the Great Cave, from the place where the icon of the Mother of God was found.

News of the finding of the icon spread quickly through the whole Peloponnese, and many of the faithful came to venerate it, and God worked many wonders through it. Other monks gathered around the brothers, and the fathers Simeon and [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-mega-spileo) began to travel the whole Peloponnese, preaching and calling the people back to Christianity. They were driven out of that country for a short time under the emperor Julian the Apostate, but returned soon after his death, and both monks made their end at Mega Spileo. Their commemoration is kept every year on 18 October. The page prints no troparion for them.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 18 October, in one line with his brother and with Euphrosyne. saint.gr says of the three wonderworkers plainly that they are unknown to the synaxarists, and that of them Simeon and [Theodore](/saints/theodore-of-mega-spileo) were brothers in the flesh and founders of the monastery of Mega Spileo, born at the beginning of the fourth century, who lived in asceticism and holiness and died in peace; and that all of this is according to the Service of the saints published at Venice in 1706, at Athens in 1840 and in 1911. His skull, the page adds, is kept at the monastery of Mega Spileo of Kalavryta.
