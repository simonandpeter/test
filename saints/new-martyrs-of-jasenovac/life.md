# The New Martyrs of Jasenovac

Jasenovac was a system of death camps set up in August 1941 by the Ustaše,
the Croatian fascists, in the Independent State of Croatia that collaborated
with Nazi Germany, sixty kilometres from Zagreb; the treatment of the
prisoners there horrified even the Nazis. The new martyrs of Jasenovac are
the Orthodox prisoners of Croatia who died a martyr’s death there from 1941
to 1944 — by the various testimonies thousands of Orthodox Serbs were
destroyed in the camp, many of them for their faithfulness to Orthodoxy. The
Bishops’ Council of the Serbian Orthodox Church glorified them as one
synaxis, and their names were entered in the calendar of the Russian
Orthodox Church with a commemoration on 31 August of the old style, 13
September. Of them all the Russian church calendar tells one, the martyr
Vukašin of Klepci. Vukašin — Vuksan, Vukan, Vuk — was an Orthodox Serb of
Herzegovina, of the old family of Mandrap, from the village of Klepci on the
east bank of the Neretva, which had a church of the apostle Luke of the
early sixteenth century, the endowment of the church-building Miloradović
family, and a church of the Transfiguration. He was born probably at the end
of the nineteenth century, grew up in the village, and as a grown man went
to work in Sarajevo, where the Mandraps, an old and prosperous merchant
family, were known as benefactors and defenders of the Serbian church of the
Archangels Michael and Gabriel on the Baščaršija, the oldest building in the
city, and where he served as a chanter and reader with his kinsmen and was
much respected. He stayed in Sarajevo until about June 1941, when the known
Serbs of the city were already leaving it, and hid in the hills of
Herzegovina, and his family lost touch with him; the Ustaše reached those
parts too, his whole family suffered in their violence, and he himself, with
the other surviving Serbs, was driven to the camp for Serbs at Jasenovac.

In January 1943 he was killed by the Ustaša executioner Žile Friganović, and
the killer’s own account, taken down by the doctor Nedo Zec, is what the
Church knows of it. On a night of mass killing, Friganović said, in a wager
among four Ustaše over who would kill the most, he had already far outdone
the others when his eye fell on an elderly peasant standing with an
inexplicable calm and watching him kill; the look stopped him where he
stood. He went up to ask who he was, and the old man said he was Vukašin of
Klepci, that all his family had been killed by the Ustaše and he himself
sent to Jasenovac, and said it with the same calm, which shook the killer
more than the cries of the dying round them; a desire came over him to break
that peace by the cruellest torture. He sat him on a stump and ordered him
to shout, long live Pavelić, on pain of losing an ear; Vukašin was silent,
and he cut it off; a second time, and the other ear; a third, and the nose;
the fourth time, on pain of having his living heart cut out of his breast,
the old man looked as if through him into some infinity and said quietly and
distinctly, do your work, child. The words drove the killer wholly mad: he
put out his eyes, cut out his heart, cut his throat and kicked the body into
the pit — the martyr’s last movement, the account says, was to make the sign
of the cross with three fingers, and for that the executioner cut off his
hand. And then, Friganović said, something in him broke; he could kill no
more, lost the wager, and had no peace afterwards, drinking and still
hearing the voice, do your work, child. The news of the martyrdom reached
the surviving Mandraps in Sarajevo only in 1946, when Dr Zec came to their
house for the family’s slava, and spread from there to Mostar and Čapljina,
though under the new government neither Vukašin nor Jasenovac itself was
spoken of aloud. He is painted among the frescoes of the monastery of the
Archangel Gabriel at Zemun, in the skete of St John at Ostrog, and among the
especially honoured saints of the diocese of Zahumlje and Herzegovina on the
icon made for its seven hundred and eightieth anniversary. Klepci and its
Transfiguration church were destroyed in 1992. The Serbian calendar keeps
the martyrs of Jasenovac on 31 August of the old style, 13 September, and
the Russian calendar the same day.
