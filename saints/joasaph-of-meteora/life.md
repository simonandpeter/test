# Joasaph of Meteora

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The day's line reads «Sfântul Cuvios Ioasaf» and nothing more, and his own page carries no Viață tab: only icons, places of pilgrimage, a section for the Holy Mountain, and the appointed readings.

What says who he is are the titles of the icons on that page, which read «Sfinții Cuvioși Atanasie și Ioasaf de la Meteora» — the venerable Athanasius and Joasaph of Meteora. He is therefore kept here as the companion of [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-meteora), the founder of Meteora, whom the same calendar keeps on this same 20 April; and that identification rests on those captions and on nothing else, which is why it is written out here rather than assumed.

The calendar gives him no years, no country, no office and no troparion, and none is supplied. Of the other men of his name in this corpus — Joasaph of Belgorod, Joasaph son of Abenner, Joasaph of Kubensk, Joasaph of Zographou — none is kept on this day, and nothing on this page identifies him with any of them.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and saint.gr says at the outset what it is working from: no biography of Joasaph of Meteora was ever found, and all the information it has is drawn from the life of [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-of-meteora) and from various official documents. The second founder of the monastery of the Great Meteoron and Athanasius's successor, it says, was «Ἰωάννης Οὔρεσης Παλαιολόγος, ὁ διὰ τοῦ θείου καὶ ἀγγελικοῦ σχήματος ἐπικληθεὶς Ἰωάσαφ μοναχός» — John Uroš Palaiologos, called the monk Joasaph through the divine and angelic habit.

He was the son of Symeon Uroš Palaiologos, the Greek-Serbian king of Epirus and of Great Wallachia, that is Thessaly, seated at Trikala, whose reign the page dates 1359 to 1370; his mother Thomais was the daughter of John II Orsini, despot of Epirus, and sister of the later despot Nikephoros II Orsini. He was born about 1349 or 1350, and through his mother was kin to the imperial house of the Palaiologoi, whose surname he kept. In 1359 or 1360, at barely ten years old, he was proclaimed co-emperor with his father at Kastoria, and about 1370, when his father died, he succeeded to the power.

He did not govern long. Soon he gave up the highest worldly offices, exchanging the royal purple for the monk's hair sack, and refused the royal crown for the love of Christ the thorn-crowned King, handing the administration of Thessaly to the Caesar Alexios Angelos Philanthropenos. So in November 1372, and before June 1373, John Uroš Palaiologos, at about twenty-two, took refuge in the monastery of the Transfiguration of the Meteoron, received the monastic habit and was renamed Joasaph, and struggled there beside Athanasius. Athanasius, a little before his repose, esteeming him and having the rest agreeing, granted him every authority and made him his successor.

After a short while Joasaph left the monastery, for reasons unknown, and went to Thessalonica, about 1379 or 1380; and shortly after Athanasius's repose he came back to the Meteoron and took up the duties of his successor, according to the wish his spiritual father had put into his last instructions to the brethren. Late in December 1384 or early in January 1385 he went to Ioannina on family business: after the murder of Thomas Preljubović, the despot of that city, the subjects of the despotate had proclaimed his widow — Joasaph's sister Maria Angelina — governor of Epirus, and he was invited to come and support her in the government.

He lengthened and heightened the first church of the Transfiguration that Athanasius had raised, and rebuilt it more splendidly. At the end of 1393 or the beginning of 1394 came the Turkish invasion of Thessaly and its seizure by sultan Bayezid I, and for that reason Joasaph, with the hieromonk Serapion and the monks Philotheos and Gerasimos, fled to the Holy Mountain and settled at the monastery of Vatopedi, where, an official document of the Great Meteoron says, on 17 October 1394 he formed brotherhoods and was granted two cells and given a golden cross in exchange. He fell asleep in peace about the year 1423.
