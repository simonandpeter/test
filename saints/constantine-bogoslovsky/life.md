# Constantine (Bogoslovsky)

Constantine Bogoslovsky was born in 1871 in the village of Borisoglebskoye in Vologda province, the son of a priest. He finished the Vologda seminary and the Kazan theological academy, was ordained priest in 1895, and taught all his life: at the diocesan girls’ school in Vologda; at the theological school in Ust-Sysolsk, where he taught Russian and Church Slavonic, penmanship and arithmetic; and at the Vologda seminary, in the chair of the history of the schism. In 1917 he took part in the All-Russian congress of clergy and laity in Moscow, and then in the Local Council of the Russian Church of 1917–1918.

In 1921 he was arrested with Archbishop Alexis (Belkovsky) of Veliky Ustyug and two other members of the diocesan council, charged with organising an “illegal” collection of money for the Church, and sentenced to five years, but he was soon freed under an amnesty and went on serving at Veliky Ustyug. The next year he was arrested again and sentenced to two years in the Arkhangelsk concentration camp; the charge said he had hindered the examination of the icon covers, stirring unrest among the people of Ustyug, and was a supporter of the “Tikhonite” movement.

He was in the Solovki camp from 1923 to 1925 and then in exile at Arkhangelsk. Back from exile, he served in the churches of Vologda and chaired the diocesan council. He was arrested for the last time at Vologda in July 1937, charged among other things with gathering news of closed churches and repressed clergy for Metropolitan Sergius and with prompting a complaint to the central executive committee about the closing of the Bogorodskoye cemetery; he was condemned to death and shot at Vologda on 20 September 1937.

The Russian calendar keeps him on 19 September old style; azbyka.ru keeps him also with the new martyrs of Solovki, the saints of Vologda and the fathers of the Local Council of 1917–1918.
