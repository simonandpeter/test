# Onuphrius the Great

Onuphrius was the son of a king of Persia whom doxologia's life names Narsita, born in the days when Diocletian reigned at Rome. The devil, in the shape of a stranger, persuaded the king that the child in the queen's womb was not his and that fire would settle it; and when the boy was born his father heated a great fire and threw him into it. The child sat unburnt in the flames and lifted his small hands towards heaven as though praying, and an angel came to rebuke the king, telling him to baptise the boy Onuphrius and carry him into the desert.

Father and child went to the Thebaid in Egypt, fed on the road by a white doe that God sent, which knelt before the king and travelled with them. Near the city of Hermopolis they came to the monastery called Eriti, of a hundred brothers, and the boy was left there; the doe stayed by the house and nursed him until he was three. At seven he would take a piece of bread from the refectory to the icon of the Mother of God in the church porch and speak to the painted Christ child as to a living one, offering him his share, and the hand on the icon reached out and took it.

Later he went out into the deep desert. What doxologia prints of it is the account of the monk Paphnutius, who found an old man covered only by the hair grown over his body. He gave his name as Onuphrius and said that for sixty years he had wandered that desert and its mountains and had seen no man until then; that an angel had brought him a little bread and water daily for thirty years; and that afterwards God gave him a date palm of twelve branches, each ripening in its own month, and a spring of living water beside his cave. Paphnutius buried him where he died.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 12 June, alongside [Peter the Athonite](/saints/peter-the-athonite), and saint.gr's account gives no year. It calls him one of the greatest ascetic figures of the Egyptian deserts and says he came from Persia; that from childhood he showed a burning desire to be given wholly to God, and that while still young he entered a coenobitic brotherhood, where for a number of years he trained himself in spiritual and bodily restraint and in obedience, and where his great humility made the brethren love him. When he was riper in years he wished to go deeper into the desert, to know and to imitate the life of the ascetics there, and the brotherhood let its chosen member go with great sorrow. After walking far into the desert he came upon the hut of the hermit Hermias, who by a divine revelation was expecting him; Hermias led him to a hut under an enormous palm, beside which the water of a clear spring ran. There Onuphrius gave himself to a greater asceticism, and his fame spread among all the hermits, who came often to take his counsel and his blessing. At the last, when abba Paphnoutios once visited him, he gave up his final breath, and Paphnoutios buried him under that great palm — reminding everyone, the page ends, of the apostle Paul's word, «γύμναζε σεαυτόν προς ευσέβειαν», train yourself for godliness. The page adds that portions of his relics are kept at the monastery of his name at Keratea in Attica and at the monastery of Leimonos on Lesbos.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the Julian 12 June, the same menologion day the folder’s other two rows carry, and tells the life as a monk who came to him in the desert heard it. He had lived sixty full years there. His hair and beard reached the ground and his body was overgrown with long hair for want of clothing; every hair on him was white as snow, and the whole look of him was dazzling, lofty and terrible. He greeted his visitor by name, and then told him how he had come to that place: his guardian angel had appeared to him and led him there.

For a long time, he said, he ate nothing but earth, which is seldom found in the desert. Afterwards, when he had borne the fierce struggle with the demons and his heart was wholly strengthened in the love of God, an angel of God brought him bread; and by God’s gentle providence a palm grew beside his cell and bore good dates, and a spring of living water opened there. But most of all, he said, he was fed and given sweet drink by the words of God. Asked how he received communion, he answered that an angel of God brought it to him every Saturday.

The next day the elder said that this was the day of his going out of the world. He bent his knees, prayed, and gave up his spirit to God; and his visitor saw a heavenly light lie on the body and heard the singing of the angelic powers. He buried him honourably and went back to his own monastery, to tell others as a living witness. This book ends the entry in 400, which is the only year any of the three calendars gives for him, and the folder now holds it.

The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery keeps him on the same day and opens the day with him — «Прп. Онуфрия Великого (IV).» — giving the century and no year, and heading him there with the other hermit the day keeps. That calendar's hymns stand on its day page and never on a life page, and three of the fifteen it prints for 12 июня are sung to him: a common of venerable fathers for the two of them together, and a troparion and a kontakion of his own, the troparion printed with no number after its glas.
