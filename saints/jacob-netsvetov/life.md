# Jacob Netsvetov

Jacob Netsvetov was the first Orthodox Christian priest born in North America. He was born in 1802 on the island of Atka in Alaska, the son of a Russian father and an Aleut mother, and he was the disciple of [Saint Innocent, Metropolitan of Moscow](/saints/innocent-of-moscow), the enlightener of Siberia and America.

He went to the seminary at Irkutsk in Siberia and then moved back to Alaska; the journey from Irkutsk to Atka took a year. On his return he began his missionary work in the Aleutian Islands, over distances of more than three thousand kilometres. He travelled long, preaching the gospel of Christ in very hard conditions — a difficult climate, sickness, hunger and weariness.

He translated the holy Scriptures and prepared botanical specimens from Alaska for the museums of Saint Petersburg. He was a tireless translator and teacher, working continually at the translation of texts into the languages of Alaska, and he founded bilingual schools. After the death of his wife in 1836 he thought of entering monastic life, but Saint Innocent persuaded him not to take that step; so he was sent on missionary journeys instead, serving the Divine Liturgy in tents and catechising and baptising a countless number of the native people of Alaska.

In 1994 he was canonised by the Holy Synod of the Orthodox Church in America as the enlightener of the people of Alaska. The Romanian calendar gives no year for his death.
