# Nicetas of Nisyros

saint.gr says Nicetas was born in 1716 in the small town of Mandraki on Nisyros, of wealthy parents. His father was an elder of the island, but he fell into disfavour with the Turks and, to escape punishment, went over to Islam with his whole household and moved to Rhodes. There the younger son, Mehmet, learned that he had once been a Christian and had been called Nicetas. Straight away, at fourteen, he went to the New Monastery on Chios and confessed his case to the abbot, who sent him on to Makarios, the former bishop of Thebes, then living in retirement; he anointed him with chrism and gave him useful counsel.

Nicetas stayed in the monastery as a catechumen, and there the longing for martyrdom took hold of him. With the advice of the famous ascetic Anthimos Hagiopateritis, and with the prayer and blessing of the other fathers, he set out for Chios. When he arrived the Turks demanded the head-tax of him, which he could not pay, and an official named Krimlis took him to lead him to the prison at the place called Vounaki. Just then a priest named Daniel passed by and greeted him by the name Mehmet; Krimlis heard it, learned what the case was, and brought him before the Aga.

There Nicetas confessed his faith in Christ with great courage. The Turks tried with promises and flatteries to hold the martyr in their religion, and when they could not, they put him to frightful torments that lasted ten days and nights. Then they dragged him to an edge of the city called Kato Yialo, where the executioners stood him below the dependency of Iviron and began again with flatteries and promises, in the hope of turning him. But the saint answered: «Χριστιανός είμαι, Νικήτας ονομάζομαι και Νικήτας θα πεθάνω» — I am a Christian, I am called Nicetas, and Nicetas I shall die.

Krimlis beheaded him himself on 21 June 1732, at seventeen years of age, on Chios. So that the Christians should take nothing of his holy relic, the Turks smeared it with filth and threw it into the sea. The page adds that his head is now at the monastery of Iviron on Athos, that part of his relics is in the church of his own name on Nisyros, and that in Mandraki, where he was born, a Byzantine church stands honoured in his memory; it names him the patron of Nisyros.
