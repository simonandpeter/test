# Gennadius of Novgorod

Gennadius of Novgorod is one of the twelve hierarchs of Novgorod whom saint.gr's calendar keeps together on 10 Φεβρουαρίου, and the page gives the year of his repose as 1505. The feast, it says, was established after Saint [John](/saints/john-of-novgorod), archbishop of Novgorod, who fell asleep on 7 September 1186, appeared to Saint Euthymius, archbishop of Novgorod, who fell asleep on 11 March 1458, and told him to keep a vigil in the cathedral church of the Holy Wisdom for the blessed hierarchs of that local church who had pleased God by their lives and their labours.

The same feast, the page adds, is kept also on 4 October, the day of the memory of Saint Hierotheus, bishop of Athens, according to what Saint John had said, and on the third Sunday after Pentecost. The twelve are then named in one list, each with the year he fell asleep: Ιωακείμ (κοιμήθηκε 1030 μ.Χ.), Λουκάς (1060), Γερμανός (1096), Αρκάδιος (1162), Γρηγόριος (1193), Μαρτύριος (1199), Αντώνιος (1231), Βασίλειος (1352), Συμεών (1421), Γεννάδιος (1505), Ποιμήν (1571), Αφφώνιος (1652). They are twelve entries enumerated by name, and so twelve folders rather than one heading.

Of Gennadius himself the page records the name, the see and the year, and nothing else: no parentage, no account of his episcopate, no hymn and no relics. Its heading for him is «Αγιος Γεννάδιος (; - 1505)», which leaves the birth open. The page prints no gloss on his name here and no other word about him.

The Ohrid Prologue gives him a day of his own — the Julian 4 December, where the Greek calendar keeps him on 10 February inside the synaxis of the twelve hierarchs — and in four lines it supplies nearly everything that page said it did not have. He was a notable writer, it says, a champion of the truth and a sufferer for the truth of Christ. He gathered the scattered books of Holy Scripture together into one, and he composed a paschal key for five hundred and thirty-two years ahead. He reposed in the Lord in 1505, which is the year the Greek enumeration gave him, and his wonderworking relics are in the Chudov monastery at Moscow.
