# Anectus, companion of Quadratus of Corinth

Anectus was one of the six friends whom saint.gr keeps together on 10 Μαρτίου and names in its line, «Άγιοι Κοδράτος, Ανεκτός, Παύλος, Διονύσιος, Κυπριανός και Κρήσκης». The page puts their martyrdom at Corinth in the persecution of the emperors Decius or Valerian, when the governor of Greece was Jason, and leaves the choice between the two reigns open. It gives no year.

Their guide was [Quadratus](/saints/quadratus-of-corinth), whom the page has born in the mountains above Corinth while his mother hid from the persecution, orphaned as a babe and fed by the clouds that gathered over him; when he was grown he taught the Christian faith to those who had taken refuge with him — Anectus among them, with [Cyprian](/saints/cyprian-companion-of-quadratus) and [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-companion-of-quadratus) and the rest.

The page says that they were arrested because they were Christians, that the governor Jason tried by various means to entice them and to persuade them to renounce their faith and sacrifice to the idols, and that they confessed Christ with spiritual manhood, were tortured, and were at last beheaded; and so, it says, the holy martyrs entered into the joy of the Kingdom of God. Of Anectus by himself it says nothing further; the corpus's other Anectus, on the Romanian 27 June, is another man, killed in another persecution and another way.

For Anectus, with Paul and Dionysius, the page keeps a distich of its own: «Γνωστοῖς Ἄνεκτον σὺν δυσὶ κτείνει ξίφος, / Οἷς οὐκ ἀνεκτὸν μὴ θανεῖν Θεοῦ χάριν» — the sword kills Anectus with two of his acquaintance, to whom it was not bearable not to die for God's sake, which is a play on his name, ἀνεκτόν, bearable. The page prints an apolytikion that names all six of them and a kontakion that calls them a lamp of six lights at Corinth, and it gives the six of them one commemoration.

*After saint.gr's calendar for 10 Μαρτίου — [the day](https://www.saint.gr/03/10/index.aspx) and [the life](https://www.saint.gr/1257/saint.aspx); read 30 September 2026.*
