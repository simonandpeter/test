# Damascene (13 November)

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. The entry for 13 noiembrie reads «Sfântul Cuvios Damaschin» — Damascene, with the rank the Romanian calendar gives to those who lived the monastic life — and the page behind that line carries no Viață tab and no Tropar tab, only the day's Epistle and Gospel. A name, a rank and a day is the whole of what this source has.

No century is given, no place, no monastery, no parentage and no manner of death; nothing here says which of the many Damascenes he is, and nothing should be read into the bare name. This folder records what the Romanian calendar keeps on 13 noiembrie and stops there.

The Greek calendar keeps a Damascene on this same day, 13 November, and unlike the Romanian line it tells his story. saint.gr calls him the new venerable-martyr of Constantinople and dates his death 1681. He came from Galata in Constantinople and was called Diamantis; his parents, pious Christians, were Kyriakos and Kyriaki. Orphaned young, and to escape the anger of the Turks over some fault of his, he was forced to embrace Islam; but later he repented and went to the Holy Mountain.

There, at the monastery of the Megiste Lavra, he became a monk with the name Damascene and trained in virtue for twelve years. Then the longing for martyrdom took him, and he came to the City together with the patriarch of Constantinople Dionysios, who was leaving the Lavra. There he preached Christ publicly and urged the Muslims to deny Muhammad. The Turks took him for a madman and at first paid him no attention; but his preaching became so provoking to them that they arrested him, tortured him hard, and beheaded him before the door of the Patriarchate on 13 November 1681. His relic was buried in the church of the Mother of God on the island of Chalki, and Makarios, abbot of the monastery of Mavros Molos, bought and kept the doors of the workshop opposite the entrance of the Patriarchate, which had been painted with the martyr's blood.

The Ohrid Prologue gives him a name before his name and the whole arc of a repentance. He was born in Galata in Constantinople and was first called Diamandi. In his youth he lived immorally, so far that he even turned Turk. Then a bitter repentance came on him, and he went to the Holy Mountain, where he struggled strictly as a monk for twelve years in the great Lavra there.

But longing for martyrdom, for the cleansing of his sin, he went to Constantinople and began going about the mosques, crossing himself and shouting to the Turks that their faith was a lie and that Jesus Christ is God and Lord. He was beheaded before the gate of the Phanar on 13 November 1681 — the day this folder is kept on in every calendar that keeps him, and the year it already records. His relics rest, it says, on Halki, in the monastery of the Holy Trinity.
