# John (Borozdin)

Ivan Borozdin was born in 1878 at Mstera, the old icon-painting settlement of the Vladimir province, into the workshop family of Pyotr Kuzmich Borozdin - seven sons and five daughters all worked in it, and their icons were prized even by the Old Believers for the fineness of the writing. The boy could grind colours, ground boards and make tracings by six; at eleven he entered the Mstera school of icon-painting, and finished it a master at sixteen. The family belonged to the edinoverie, and in 1899 the Mstera brotherhood sent him to the Moscow church school at the All Saints edinoverie monastery, where he stayed on as reader; he married, was ordained deacon, and served there until the monastery was closed in 1921.

Patriarch Tikhon himself sent him in 1923 to the church of Cosmas and Damian in the Goncharnaya quarter, and there, ordained priest and made rector, he fought for the church as his predecessor had - petitions to the authorities, signatures of the faithful - until it was closed and destroyed in 1930. He fell ill with the grief of it. Appointed archpriest of the edinoverie church of St Nicholas at the Rogozhskoye cemetery, he lived in a cold little room in the bell-tower among the old icons to guard the church from robbers, warmed by an iron stove; one December night in 1931 the reader Fedor and his wife Varvara were each woken by a dream of John of Kronstadt - "go, help your priest" - and ran to the bell-tower in time.

He was arrested in 1937, charged with "spreading false rumours of a supposed persecution of religion in the USSR", sentenced by the troika on 26 September and shot the next day, 27 September 1937, and buried in a common unmarked grave at the Butovo range near Moscow. He was glorified among the New Martyrs and Confessors of Russia in 2000.
