# Agapius of Caesarea in Palestine

Agapius was from the city of Gaza, and he suffered at Caesarea in Palestine under the governor Urban, in the second year of the persecution Diocletian ordered through every land and city. Doxologia's life for the day calls him a man honoured among the Christians and renowned for his endurance under torment: he had confessed the name of Christ boldly many times before, and had suffered much for it.

He is not the earlier martyr of the same name who was given to the beasts in that same city, whom the corpus keeps as [Agapius of Gaza](/saints/agapius-of-gaza); the life sets the two apart in so many words. This Agapius was taken a second time, together with his servant Dionysius the Egyptian, and was shut up beside six young men who had given themselves up a few days earlier, so that there were eight of them.

They were tried many times and tormented in many ways, and because they would not deny Christ and bore all of it, they were condemned to the sword; all of them laid down their heads on this day. Of the eight, doxologia's calendar prints three as its own lines for 15 martie: Agapius, [Timolaus](/saints/timolaus) and [Plesius](/saints/plesius).

The Ohrid Prologue keeps the company on the same menologion day and tells it differently again. Its heading is „Свети мученик Агапије и седморица с њим“ — Agapius and seven with him — and it says they all suffered at Caesarea in Palestine under the prince Urban, in the time of the emperor Diocletian. All seven except Agapius, it says, were quite young men, and were not yet Christians, nor had they ever been baptised with water; but their baptism was by blood.

One day the seven youths watched Christians being tortured — some on the fire, some on the gallows, some before the beasts — and seeing with what patience they bore it all they took fire with zeal for Christ, bound their own hands behind them, and came so bound before Urban, saying: we too are Christians. His flatteries and his threats were wasted on them. Agapius himself, a man of standing in that city who had already suffered much for Christ, joined himself to the youths and kindled in them the faith and the love of the Lord still further. All were beheaded with the sword in 303, which falls inside the reign this folder already records and is not written over it, and passed over to the courts of the King of Heaven.

The Russian church keeps the eight on the same menologion day and tells the story with details the other two calendars do not have. The Православный церковный календарь of the Сретенский monastery puts their suffering at Caesarea in Palestine under Diocletian, whose reign it dates 284 to 305, and closes the line with the year 303. During one of the pagan festivals, it says, Christians who refused to sacrifice to the idols were being publicly tortured and put to death; and young Christians standing in that crowd resolved to confess the faith openly and to suffer for Christ.

As a sign that the act was their own they bound their own hands and came before the governor, who, seeing how young they were, tried to talk them out of it and could not. He then threw them into the prison where two Christians were already held — one who had borne torments for the faith and his servant — and all of them were given over to dreadful torments and beheaded. The page is also the only one of the three calendars to put a grade inside the company: the Romylus of its line it makes a subdeacon of the church of Diospolis.

This folder keeps the man the line heads, and in this telling he is one of the two who were already in the prison when the youths were brought in: a man who had borne torments for the faith of Christ before them. The calendar sets Gaza beside Caesarea in his name, which is where the Greek page puts his birth.
