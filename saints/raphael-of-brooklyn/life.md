# Raphael of Brooklyn

Raphael was born on 8 November 1860 at Beirut, of Syrian parents who had fled from Damascus. He studied at the patriarchal school in Damascus, at the school of Orthodox theology on Halki, the island of the Ecumenical Patriarchate, and at the theological academy of Kyiv in imperial Russia. doxologia's notice opens by calling him «Părintele nostru între sfinți Rafail de Brooklyn» — our father among the saints, Raphael of Brooklyn.

As the Syro-Arab community in the United States grew, he went there in 1895 to serve it, and was given charge of the whole Syrian Orthodox Mission, with its seat at New York, where he organised the parish that was later to become the cathedral of Saint Nicholas in Brooklyn. From that post he founded new parishes for the emigrant communities and saw to the translation of the Great Euchologion into Arabic.

In 1904 he was elected bishop of Brooklyn and consecrated by Tikhon, archbishop of Moscow, and Innocent, bishop of Alaska, becoming in this way the first Orthodox bishop consecrated in North America. As auxiliary bishop of the Russian Orthodox Church in America he built the cathedral of Saint Nicholas, used today by the primate of the Antiochian archdiocese of North America, founded thirty more parishes, blessed the ground of what would be Saint Tikhon's monastery at South Canaan in Pennsylvania, and in 1905 started the paper *The Word*.

After twenty years of service in North America he went to the Lord on 27 February 1915, at the residence in Brooklyn across the street from the cathedral on Pacific Street. He was numbered among the saints by the Holy Synod of the Orthodox Church in America in March 2000, and the service of glorification was held that May at Saint Tikhon's monastery, with bishops of the Antiochian archdiocese, of the Greek archdiocese and of the Church of Poland taking part. The Orthodox Church in America keeps his memory on 27 February.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives the schooling and the dates. saint.gr says Raphael was born in Syria in 1860 of devout parents, Michael Hawaweeny and Mariam, the daughter of a priest of Damascus, and was baptised on the feast of Theophany in 1861 and named Raphael. He studied at the theological school of Halki and was ordained deacon on 8 December 1885; then he attended classes at the theological academy of Kiev, with the blessing of Sylvester, patriarch of Antioch and director of the academy, and a month later received the office of archimandrite from Ioannikios, metropolitan of Moscow. As a presbyter he took up the duties of exarch of the Patriarchate of Antioch in Russia.

His missionary zeal took him to America. He reached New York on 2 November 1895 and served as assistant to bishop Nicholas, took on substantial missionary work, and gave himself to the writing of theological books and to the raising of new churches. In 1903 the Holy Synod of the Russian Church elected him bishop of Brooklyn and entrusted him with the missionary work in North America. He fell asleep in peace in 1915, and his body, saint.gr says, is at the monastery of St Tikhon at South Canaan in Pennsylvania.
