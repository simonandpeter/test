# Pelagia of Antioch

The life doxologia.ro prints is told by a deacon named James. The archbishop of Antioch had called eight bishops of the neighbouring cities to him, among them James's own bishop Nonnus, who had been taken to the episcopate out of the monastery called Tabennisi. While the bishops sat by the doors of the church of the martyr Julian to hear Nonnus teach, the most notorious woman in Antioch passed by, dressed in costly clothes and gold and pearls, bare-headed, with a crowd of young men and maidens about her. The other bishops shut their eyes and turned their faces away as from a great sin; Nonnus looked at her steadily, and afterwards wept in his cell.

By God's ordering she came into the church, who had never entered a church nor remembered her sins, heard Nonnus preach, and wrote to him. He answered that she and her purpose were already known to God, and that she was not to put him to the test. She came before the assembled bishops, threw herself on the ground weeping, kissed Nonnus's feet and would not be raised until he promised her baptism; he raised her at last and told her to learn first what she lacked for it. He taught her the faith, baptised her, chrismated her and gave her communion, and the deaconess Romana took her as her spiritual daughter.

On the third day she sent a servant to list everything in her chests and bring all her jewels, and gave the whole of it into Nonnus's hands, saying that this was the wealth with which Satan had made her rich and that it was time she sought the riches of Christ. Nonnus gave it to the steward of the church before them all, swearing him to spend none of it on the bishop's house, nor on the church, nor on his own house, but to give it to widows, orphans and the poor.

On the eighth day, when the newly baptised put off their white garments, she rose very early, dressed in a hair shirt with an old cloak of Nonnus's over it, and left Antioch secretly. It was revealed to Nonnus that she had gone to Jerusalem. Three years later James went there, and by his bishop's instruction looked for a monk named Pelagius, who was taken for a eunuch and lived shut up on the Mount of Olives in a cell with no door, only a window. She knew him; he did not know her, for the beauty was withered like a flower and her eyes had sunk with her abstinence. When he came again and she did not answer, men opened the cell and found her dead. The patriarch of Jerusalem came with a great crowd to anoint the body, and so it was found that the eunuch had been a woman, and she was buried in the cell where she had laboured.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 8 October, and tells the same conversion more shortly. saint.gr says that Pelagia lived at Antioch and belonged to the class of loose women — that she was a harlot, and that her life was sunk in the frenzy of sinful pleasures, her conscience so hardened by licence that no thought of repentance could enter her soul. Against that it sets the Lord's word that the tax-collectors and the harlots go before you into the kingdom of God.

Then, it says, Pelagia happened by chance at some gathering of Christians to hear a warm sermon on purity from the bishop Nonnus, whose own feast it gives as 10 Νοεμβρίου. His words rebuked and shook her soul. By the grace of God she renounced her profligate life, sold her various ornaments and distributed the money to the poor; and when she had been catechised and baptised she went, eight days afterwards, to Jerusalem, where she passed the rest of her life in hard asceticism. The same day page carries a second woman of her name, [Pelagia the Virgin of Antioch](/saints/pelagia-the-virgin-of-antioch), who is another person and another entry.

The Russian calendar keeps her at the head of its own 8 October, «Прп. Пелагии (457)», and the day's one icon — the plain service, marked in the Typikon with no sign at all — stands immediately before her line, which is the only line of its paragraph. The day's service is hers: «8 октября: Преподобной матери нашей Пелаги». The site reaches two lives for her and they disagree twice. The shorter says she was converted by [Nonnus](/saints/nonnus-the-bishop), bishop of Edessa, and that before her baptism she was at the head of the dancing-women of Antioch in Palestine. The longer says Nonnus was bishop of Heliopolis and that she was born in Antioch in Syria, and that her admirers called her Margarita, the pearl, for the gold and the precious stones she wore.

In the shorter account she rode past the doors of a church, richly dressed, while Nonnus was preaching at them; the faithful turned their faces away from her and the bishop looked after her a long while, and in his cell he prayed for her and grieved that the poverty of his soul's clothing could not match the splendour of a harlot's.

In the longer, the bishops of the neighbouring dioceses had come to Antioch for a council, and in a break between sessions a noisy crowd of young men came by with Pelagia among them, immodestly dressed and bare-shouldered, laughing and joking. The bishops dropped their eyes and Nonnus looked at her steadily, and when the crowd had gone he asked them whether her beauty and her dress had not pleased them. He said he had learned much from her: she had set herself to please men, and how many hours had she spent on it — and that at the dread judgement the Lord would condemn them by her, who have an immortal bridegroom in heaven and neglect the state of their souls.

Both accounts turn on a sermon about the judgement. She came into the church, was shaken, and asked for baptism; the longer has her say that her sins were more numerous than the sand of the sea and that there was not water enough in the sea to wash her filthy deeds away, and the good shepherd comforted her with the hope of God's mercy and baptised her. The shorter adds that the devil came to her in the night and urged her back to her old life, and that she prayed, made the sign of the cross, and he vanished. She gathered her jewels and brought them to Nonnus, who had them given away to the poor with the words, «Пусть будет умно расточено худо собранное» — let what was ill-gathered be wisely scattered.

She put on a hair shirt — or, in the longer account, a man's clothes — left the city and went to Jerusalem, where she was tonsured and taken for a youth. She shut herself in a cell on the Mount of Olives and lived there in repentance, fasting and prayer, and the people of the country round about took her for the monk Pelagius, a eunuch. She reached great spiritual gifts and died about 457, and the longer life says that it was at her burial that the monk was found to be a woman.
