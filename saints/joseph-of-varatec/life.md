# Joseph of Văratec

Joseph was born about 1750 in the village of Valea Jidanului in Transylvania, of a devout Orthodox family. Those were the years of the rising of the Orthodox Romanians of Transylvania under the venerable Sofronie of Cioara against Austrian pressure and the union, and doxologia.ro's life says that many families steadfast in Orthodoxy crossed into Moldavia because of the persecutions, his among them. Drawn to the life of those who struggle for Christ, he went round the monasteries of Moldavia and followed what he saw.

He knew the monastic life from his youth and became a disciple of the venerable Paisius Velichkovsky while Paisius was at Dragomirna, following him afterwards to Secu and to Neamț, and learning there obedience, humility, the guard of the mind, abstinence, fasting, vigils and the prayer of the mind. When the brotherhood moved to Secu and Neamț he came too, and on the elder's recommendation he was tonsured and ordained hieromonk.

About 1779 he is found as spiritual guide at the skete of Pocrov near Neamț and as counsellor of the nuns of Gura Carpenului and Durău, and later, with the elder's blessing, he became a hermit in the mountains of Neamț with two other fathers, Gherman and Gherontie. After 1785, at Paisius's direction and with the bishop's blessing, he was appointed to lay the foundations of the monastery of Văratec together with mother Olimpiada, and afterwards Nazaria, his disciples from Durău.

A hermit and a skilled teacher of the Jesus Prayer, a renowned priest and confessor and a good organiser, he is the first confessor of the settlement at Văratec and one of its founders, and Metropolitan Veniamin Costachi of Moldavia valued him for the holiness of his life. He built the monastery's first two churches, the wooden one of 1785 and the stone one of 1808–1812, both dedicated to the Dormition of the Mother of God, and for thirty years he had the spiritual care of the monasteries of the Neamț mountains.

Among his disciples was the hieromonk Irinarh Rosetti (†1859), who founded the monastery of Horaița and then went to the Holy Land and built the church on Mount Tabor. The nuns of Văratec remembered a hard life of monastic labour and a love of God, of people, of prayer and of books. God gave him the gift of working miracles and of driving out unclean spirits, and many were healed in soul and body at his prayer. He reposed on 28 December 1828 and was buried in the narthex of the Dormition church at Văratec.

The places in the Neamț mountains where he struggled are still called Poiana lui Iosif, Pârâul lui Iosif and Chiliile lui Iosif, and a Typikon of the prayer of the heart survives from him, which he kept together with his disciples.
