# Theonas, with Symeon and Pherbinus

saint.gr's calendar names him and gives no life. Its whole account of the three is one sentence — «Οι Όσιοι Θεωνάς. Συμεών και Φερβίνος απεβίωσαν ειρηνικά», the venerable Theonas, Symeon and Pherbinus died peacefully. The page gives no country, no century, no monastery and no incident for any of the three; it gives them no years, names no relics and prints no hymn.

What it does print is a couplet for the three together: «Ψυχὰς δέχου τρεῖς οὐ κεκηλιδωμένας, / Ψυχῶν ἀκηλίδωτε τῶν θείων τόπε» — receive three souls that are not stained, O unstained place of souls divine. Beyond the fact of a peaceful end, that is the whole of what the source says about them, and what it says is that the three are counted together. [Symeon](/saints/symeon-with-theonas-and-pherbinus) and [Pherbinus](/saints/pherbinus-with-theonas-and-symeon) are the other two.

Theonas stands first of the three in the page's own order. He is not the Theonas whom the same day page lists separately as archbishop of Thessalonica, who has a long life of his own there; this one is one of a company of three about whom the calendar records only that they reposed in peace.

**The Russian church calendar keeps the three of them on the Julian 5 April**, where the Greek calendar keeps them on the 4th, and its line is «Прпп. Феоны , Симеона и Форвина (IV).» — the venerable Theonas, Symeon and Pherbinus, fourth century. Like the Greek page it carries no life for them: three name links, «Феона, прп.», «Симеон, прп.» and «Форвин, прп.», each giving this one day and nothing else, and no narrative behind any of them. What the Russian line adds to the Greek sentence is the century, and it is the first date any source read has given these three folders.

**That this Russian line is these three folders is a reading, and it is made here on four counts.** The three names stand together in one entry, in the same order the Greek page prints them; «Форвин» is the Russian for Φερβίνος letter by letter, Russian writing β as в; all three carry the same rank word, Прп. against Όσιος; and the two calendars are one number apart on this whole day and not on this entry alone — the martyrs Agathopodes and Theodulus, whom the Greek keeps on 4 April, and Plato of Studion, whom the Greek and the Romanian both keep on 4 April, stand on this same Russian 5 April page. No source read states the identification, and the question is put to the author in this batch’s commit message.
