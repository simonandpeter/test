# Paul of Plousias

Paul lived in the iconoclast period — doxologia places him at the end of the eighth century and the beginning of the ninth — and was chosen bishop of the city of Plousias in Bithynia, which before that was called Kieros. The Romanian account sets him among the tireless defenders, the fearless watchmen and the unshaken pillars of Orthodoxy.

Crowds of men who held the same right belief were persecuted, banished, tortured, imprisoned and killed, and others had their noses or their tongues cut off — holy sacrifices of martyrdom for the defence of the icons. But the danger of suffering such torments did not check Paul's devout ardour.

He went about from place to place and encouraged resistance to the imperial orders, teaching Christians that they owed neither respect nor obedience to the iconoclast patriarchs and bishops, and protesting against the lawless change they wanted to impose on the Church. For this he was persecuted, imprisoned and exiled; but he stayed unmoved in his struggle and gave up his spirit, a fighter to the last moment of his life. doxologia prints no hymn for him and gives no year for his death.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and says what he actually did. saint.gr puts him in the years of the iconoclasm, at the end of the eighth century and the beginning of the ninth, and gives his see as Plousias, or Prousias, in Bithynia, the town called Kieros before. He was distinguished, the page says, among the tireless defenders and fearless guardians and advocates of Orthodoxy, at a time when crowds of those who thought as he did were hunted, exiled, tortured, imprisoned and killed, some of them with their noses and tongues cut off — holy victims of the martyrdom for the Icons. The likelihood that he would suffer the same did not check his zeal. He went about from place to place encouraging resistance to the imperial orders; he taught that Christians owe no respect and no obedience to iconoclast patriarchs and bishops, and he protested against the unholy overturning they meant to impose on the Church. For that conduct he was hunted, imprisoned and exiled, and he stayed unshaken in his honourable struggle, giving up his breath fighting to the last moment. The page prints over him one distich: the trumpet of Plousias falls silent, waiting for the last trumpet's sound.
