# Athanasius of Brest

Athanasius of Brest was a Belarusian, born about 1597 into the devout Christian family of the Filipoviches — at Brest, the Greek synaxarion says, into the Orthodox brotherhood of the town, in the years after the Union of 1596 was imposed there with the power of the Polish state. He had a serious education, knowing Russian, Polish, Greek and Latin, theology and history, as his surviving diary shows; in his youth he was for a time a tutor in the houses of Polish nobles, and in 1627 took the tonsure from the abbot Joseph at the monastery of the Holy Spirit at Vilna — on the road to the Kupyatichi monastery, the Greek adds, he carried a cripple on his back who taught him the unceasing prayer of Jesus, and the wonderworking icon of the Mother of God there, from which he once heard a voice sending him to Moscow for the tsar’s help, shaped his life. Ordained hieromonk in 1632 and made superior of the Duboy monastery near Pinsk, he defended Orthodoxy, by the special blessing of the Mother of God, in the old Russian lands seized by the Commonwealth; from 1638 to 1648 he was abbot of the St Simeon monastery at Brest, and in 1647 at Warsaw won from King Vladislav the privileges of the Orthodox communities — to which the authorities of Brest answered, become Uniates, and we shall grant everything free. He bore many wrongs from the Uniates and unlawful punishments from the authorities, was imprisoned three times, sent to Kyiv to stand before a church court and acquitted; for ten years, among ill-wishers, he fought without pause for holy Orthodoxy, and all attempts to break his resolve were in vain. Tried again, he was condemned to death for cursing the Union, and died a martyr in the night of 4–5 September 1648; his relics, found on 20 July 1679, work wonders at Brest.
