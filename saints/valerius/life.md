# Valerius

doxologia.ro's calendar names him and gives no life. Its line for 23 aprilie reads «Sfântul Mucenic Valerie» — the holy martyr Valerius — and the page it links for him carries neither a Viață tab nor a Tropar tab, only the feast line "23 Aprilie" and the readings appointed for the day: the epistle from the second letter to Timothy, chapter 2, verses 1 to 10, and the gospel from John, chapter 15, verses 17 to 27 and chapter 16, verses 1 to 2.

Nothing further about him is printed there. His country is not named, no century is given, and the manner of his martyrdom is not told. The word «Mucenic» in the line is the whole of what the calendar says about how he died.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, and gives one fact more than the Romanian. The whole body of saint.gr's page is a single sentence: «Ο Άγιος Μάρτυς Ουαλέριος τελειώθηκε διά ξίφους» — the holy martyr Valerius was perfected by the sword. Its couplet says the same thing twice over, that an evil head, the executioner, took away the divine head of Valerius by the sword. No year, no country, no city, no judge and no relic are given, and the page prints no hymn. His entry stands among those of the great martyr George's day, but nothing on it ties him to George's passion as the pages of Anatolius, Protoleon and Glycerius tie those three, and nothing of the kind has been supplied here.

The Serbian church keeps the whole company on this day, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović heads the entry with the great martyr alone — „Св. вел. муч. Ђорђе.“ — and names the rest in its body. [George](/saints/george-the-trophy-bearer) was born in Cappadocia, the son of rich and devout parents; his father suffered for Christ and his mother moved to Palestine. Grown, he went into the army, reached the rank of tribune in his twentieth year, and in that rank was in the service of the emperor Diocletian. When the emperor began his terrible persecution of the Christians, George stepped before him and confessed boldly that he was a Christian too.

The emperor threw him into prison, ordered his feet set in the stocks and a heavy stone laid on his chest; then had him bound to a wheel with boards of great nails set under it and turned on it until his whole body was one wound; then buried him in a ditch with only his head above the earth and left him there three days and three nights; and then had a magician give him a deadly poison. Through all of it George prayed without ceasing, and God healed him on the instant and saved him from death, to the great wonder of the people. And when by his prayer he raised a dead man, many received the faith of Christ.

Among them, the entry says, were the emperor's wife [Alexandra](/saints/alexandra-the-empress), the chief priest of the idols [Athanasius](/saints/athanasius-once-a-magician), the farmer [Glycerius](/saints/glycerius-the-farmer), and Valerius, [Donatus](/saints/donatus-23-april) and [Therinus](/saints/therinus). At the last the emperor condemned George and his own wife to be beheaded with the sword; the blessed Alexandra gave up her spirit at the place of execution before the sword came to her, and George was beheaded in 303. The wonders at his grave are without number, the entry says, and so are his appearances, in dreams and waking, to those who have remembered him and asked his help from then to this day.

What the entry adds to this folder is the company it names him in — he is the fourth of the six converts, listed with Donatus and Therinus after the empress, the chief priest and the farmer — and nothing else: no trade, no city, no rank. The entry gives this folder no year of its own. It dates only the great martyr's beheading, „св. Ђорђе би посечен 303. год.“, and the empress's death just before it; of the others it says only that they received the faith, and nothing at all about how or when they died. **No date was added here**, and the folder stands as it was.
