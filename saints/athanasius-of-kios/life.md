# Athanasius of Kios

saint.gr says Athanasius was a rich and devout elder of the community at Kios in Bithynia. Because he stood up for his fellow townsmen against the heavy taxation of the Turks, he was slandered with a charge that he had said he would become a Muslim. He was summoned by the authorities of the reigning city and pressed hard to betray his Christian faith in order to save himself.

Athanasius would not deny Christ, and after harsh tortures he was imprisoned; and because even then he stayed unmoved in his faith, they beheaded him at Constantinople on 24 July 1670. A life of the saint, the page adds, was written by John Karyophyllis. It gives no year of his birth, and no account of his family or of how long he was held.

Above the notice the page sets a distich: «Aθανάσιε δος τι και λήψη μάκαρ, Δος την κεφαλήν καθανασίαν λάβε» — Athanasius, give something and you shall receive, blessed one: give your head and receive deathlessness. The page ranks him both with the saints of Kios and with the new martyrs who suffered after the fall of Constantinople, and prints no hymn for him.
