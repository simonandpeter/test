# Fausta of Cyzicus

Fausta, an undefiled bride of Christ the Master, was born in the days of the pagan emperor Maximian and brought up in the good faith in the city of Cyzicus by well-born, wealthy and believing parents; and when they had died and given up their souls to the Lord, the maiden was left alone at thirteen. She gave herself to fasting and prayer and the reading of the divine books, and passing over all the deceit of this world — riches, the sweetnesses and pleasures of the body — she walked the strait and hard road of life. So many good works could not be hidden, for as the Lord said, a city set on a hill cannot be hid; and word of her came to the emperor Maximian.

He sent at once from his palace the chief servant of the idols, a man named [Evilasius](/saints/evilasius-companion-of-fausta), to Cyzicus, with this command: if she could be persuaded to sacrifice to the gods it would go well with her, and if not he was to throw her into the depth of the water. Evilasius came and found her young in years but old in faith and wisdom, and brought her to his judgement seat and pressed her to sacrifice. "I will not sacrifice to gods that are deaf and blind and have no feeling, being the work of men's hands," she said, "for I have Jesus Christ as my Bridegroom."

She told him to have a painter paint her likeness, and when it was done she bade the soldiers tear the picture to pieces, and said: "As the Lord God lives, as I feel not the least pain for what you have done to this image, which is my form and likeness, so my soul feels nothing at all of the torments you give my body." He had her put in a wooden coffin fastened with wooden pins and ordered it sawn through. The servants laboured from the first hour to the sixth, wore out six saws and could not touch her, for she was hard as a diamond and sang inside the coffin, glorifying God; they set fire round it and it would not burn, while she sang that though she walked through the midst of the fire it would not burn her.

Evilasius was dismayed, and setting her before him said: "Woman, you frighten me with these wonders. I am eighty years old and have never seen the like; I adjure you by the God you believe in to tell me the whole truth." She told him of the immortal and everlasting God who keeps His servants in every place, who came down to earth and was born unspeakably of the most pure Virgin, who worked many wonders and came of His own will to the Cross, was buried and rose the third day, ascended in glory, and will come again at the end of the ages to judge the living and the dead and repay every man according to his works. Hearing it, Evilasius began to know the great power of the living God, was filled with the Holy Spirit, and loosed her from her bonds and let her go free.

One of his servants ran to Maximian and told him that Evilasius had forsaken the emperor's love and wished to become a Christian; and the emperor in great anger sent [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-fausta), his eparch, a harsh and inhuman man, to Cyzicus. Maximus had Evilasius hung on a beam and beaten, and his ribs burned with lighted torches; and under the burning he asked Fausta, who stood by watching him suffer, to pray for him, and she prayed that God would receive him into the fold of His rational flock.

Then the eparch set her before him too, and had her bound and iron nails driven into her feet; and she felt no pain at all. A soldier named Claudius advised that she be given to the beasts if he wished to kill her quickly, and she was led naked to the place; but the lioness they loosed ran and bowed before her, and all the other beasts fell at her feet and worshipped her. The eparch had her tied by the feet and dragged naked along the ground, and she cried to the Lord to cover His creature so that shameless and unclean eyes should not see the nakedness of His bride's body — and a cloud came down from above and covered her like a chosen garment.

Then another soldier, Eusebius, asked to have her, and had a blacksmith make a great number of long iron nails, which he drove into her head, her forehead, her eyes, her breast, her shins and her whole body; and she prayed within herself, giving thanks to Christ who knows the hearts, and asking that Maximus the eparch too might be brought to the holy faith. Seeing that so young a maiden, pierced through and with the blood running from her, took no account of such a torment, Eusebius had a great cauldron filled with pitch and brimstone and boiled hard, and threw Fausta into it together with Evilasius, with a great fire beneath; and the two martyrs stood in that cauldron as in a green and cool place, singing and glorifying God, and at once the fire went out and the cauldron grew cold.

Then Maximus the eparch, seeing the faith and patience of the saints, prayed that God would receive him too and number him with those two, that he, the least, might complete the number of three after the image of the Holy Trinity. The heavens opened and he saw the Son of God with the ranks of the saints and the angels. Fausta gave thanks that God wills that none should perish but that all should be saved and come to the knowledge of the truth, saying that she stood between these two servants of His like a vine bearing clusters, and asking that the three of them be never parted, since the Lord had said that where two or three are gathered in His name He is in the midst of them. As she spoke, a voice was heard from heaven calling those who had laboured and were laden with sufferings for His name to their rest in the Kingdom of Heaven; and the saints, filled with unspeakable joy, gave their souls into God's hands on the sixth day of the month of February.

The Greek calendar keeps the three of them on the same day and gives the year and the order of their conversions. Fausta was of Cyzicus in Asia Minor, daughter of rich and godly parents, and was left an orphan at thirteen with the whole of their great fortune; neither her youth nor the deceit of wealth mastered her mind or her heart. In 299, under the emperor Maximian, the eminent senator [Evilasius](/saints/evilasius-companion-of-fausta) called on her, as the governor's representative, to deny Christ and sacrifice; and when she refused, and bore the tortures with such endurance, and came out of the fire unharmed, the old man's own paganism collapsed in him. He had her brought before him, looked at her with reverence, asked her about the Orthodox faith, and set her free. That news enraged the prefect [Maximus](/saints/maximus-companion-of-fausta), who summoned Evilasius and reproached him; Evilasius told him the whole story unmoved, and the prefect then put both of them to frightful tortures — after which he too confessed Christ and knelt before her asking forgiveness. The thing rang through all Cyzicus and reached Diocletian, by whose order all three were put to death.

The Serbian church keeps the three of them on the Julian 6 February, and Bishop Nikolai's Ohrid Prologue tells the whole of it in four sentences and crosses the other two sources in four places. The reign is Maximian's, «between 305 and 311», where saint.gr dates that emperor 285 to 305 and puts the affair in 299. Evilasius is an eighty-year-old priest of the idols, as in the Romanian life and not a senator as on the Greek page, and Maximus is the eparch; both were her torturers, and both were astonished by her courage and turned Christian by it.

The painting is different too. Threatened with yet more frightful torments, she told the judge to have an icon of her made with all the torments he was threatening painted on it; and when it was finished and shown to her she said, "As this icon feels no torment at all, so neither does my body feel the torments of your punishments, for my soul is established in the Lord." Then the judge threw her into a cauldron of boiling water, and there this holy thirteen-year-old girl made her end with the prayer still on her lips — where the Romanian life has her and Evilasius stand unburnt in a cauldron of pitch and brimstone, and all three of them die at a voice from heaven. Neither reading is preferred here.
