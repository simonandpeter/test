# George of Neapolis

saint.gr's calendar for 3 Νοεμβρίου names «Άγιος Γεώργιος ο νέος Ιερομάρτυρας, ο Νεαπολίτης», and dates him «(; - 1797)». He was a priest, the page says, and came from Neapolis in Asia Minor, which in Turkish is called Nev Şehir; and he was distinguished as the serving priest of the church of the Dormition of the Theotokos there, walking «ἐν ἀληθείᾳ καὶ ὁσιότητι καὶ δικαιοσύνῃ», blameless in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord.

In 1797 the Christians of the village of Malakoti called him to serve for them, in place of their own priest, whom the Turks were hunting. The priest George accepted gladly and set out for the village. But suddenly, near the place called Kompia-Dere, he was seized by enraged Turkish shepherds, who robbed him, tortured him, and at the last beheaded him.

The relic of the new martyr George was recovered «σῶον καί ἀκέραιον καί ἄφθαρτον καί πλῆρες οὐρανίου εὐωδίας καί χάριτος» — whole and unharmed and incorrupt and full of heavenly fragrance and grace — after he appeared in a vision to a widow, devout and fearing God. It was kept first in the house of his fellow priest Neophytos, and after that man's death in the parish church of Neapolis. There, the page says, the metropolitans of Caesarea Paisios, Kleoboulos and John venerated it on their regular visits, and wondered at the grace he had received from God, being shown a wonderworker.

After the Asia Minor catastrophe of 1922 the holy relic was brought to Greece, on the occasion of the exchange of populations in 1924, by the then serving priest of Neapolis, the archimandrite Ignatios, and laid in the church of Saint Eustathios at Perissos in Attica, where it is kept to this day in excellent condition. No hymn is printed for him, and the page carries no asterisk and not the sentence «Δεν είναι διασταυρωμένη η ύπαρξη του Αγίου».
