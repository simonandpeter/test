# Thespesius (20 November)

saint.gr's calendar for 20 Νοεμβρίου names three brothers: «Άγιοι Ευστάθιος, Θεσπέσιος και Ανατόλιος». They lived in the years of the emperor Maximian, in the year 300 by the page's reckoning; their homeland was Gangra in Asia Minor, and their parents were idolaters called Philotheos and Eusebia. Of the three, Eustathius busied himself with letters, and the other two with their father's trade, the selling of clothes.

It was [Lucian](/saints/lucian-of-antioch), the presbyter of Antioch, who brought them to the Christian faith, after meeting Philotheos and Anatolius on a journey into the East. He catechised them; and when they reached Nicomedia they were baptised by [Anthimus](/saints/anthimus-of-nicomedia), the bishop of that city, who ordained Philotheos presbyter and Eustathius deacon.

After the death of their parents the three were denounced to Maximian, and when they had suffered horrible tortures they were beheaded at the last. The couplet set over the notice turns on the manner of it: the deceivers cut off the heads of three who were dead already, showing no pity even to the dead — such is deception.

This folder is Thespesius, named second, one of the two who followed their father's trade, brother to [Eustathius](/saints/eustathius-20-november) and [Anatolius](/saints/anatolius-20-november).

The Сретенский monastery’s calendar keeps the three of them on one line for the Julian 20 November, «Мчч. Евстафия, Феспесия и Анатолия (312).», and its life page is three sentences long and gives four facts the Greek page did not. They were natives of the city of Gangra; they were the children of a rich merchant; they were baptised by the bishop [Anthimus of Nicomedia](/saints/anthimus-of-nicomedia); and they died as martyrs at Nicaea, having borne the cruellest tortures. The Greek page had given the baptism and the bishop; the birthplace and the merchant father are new, and so is Nicaea as the place of death.

**The year does not agree with the year this folder held.** saint.gr put the three of them «στα χρόνια του βασιλιά Μαξιμιανού το έτος 300»; days.pravoslavie.ru closes its line with «(312)» and its life page with «(+ 312)». Twelve years, two sources, and nothing read chooses between them, so the interval is widened to hold both and the display stops being a single year.

He is the second of the three on the line, on the name page and in the life page’s title, and the brother in the middle: the page makes no distinction between the three beyond the order in which it names them.

The Охридски пролог tells the three brothers as one story. They were brothers by birth, of Nicomedia, born to the heathen parents Philotheus and Eusebia, who afterwards received the true faith together with their sons from the bishop of Nicomedia — whom the book names and to whom this folder already carries a `related` row. Philotheus was ordained presbyter; and when he and his wife had reposed, the dreadful persecution broke out under the wicked emperor Maximian and the three sons were brought to trial, judged, tortured and condemned to death.

Angels appeared to them in prison more than once, gave them manna for food and filled their young hearts with strength and courage in endurance. At the place of execution two friends came up and talked with them, and while they were still talking the martyrs gave up their souls to God; the soldiers then cut off their dead heads and carried them to the judge. The book puts the passion about the year 313, which falls outside the «300 or 312» this folder holds from two other calendars, so nothing in `dates` is moved.
