# Nicholas (Georgievsky)

Nicholas was born in 1865 in the village of Kolomenskoye in the Nagatino
district of the Moscow county, the son of the priest Sergius Georgievsky.
Finishing the Moscow seminary in 1886 he was ordained priest and served in
the churches of the Moscow diocese; at the beginning of the twentieth
century he served at the church of Cosmas and Damian in the settlement of
Bolshevo in the Mytishchi district, built at the end of the eighteenth
century, where in 1900 by his efforts and the local benefactors’ the south
chapel of the Kazan icon was finished; raised to archpriest, he was for a
long time, through the persecutions too, dean of the churches of the
Mytishchi district. At the beginning of 1930 the godless managed to get
the church of Cosmas and Damian closed, but the faithful would not accept
it and sent delegates to the authorities with a petition for its opening,
and he too appealed to the faithful to prevent the church’s destruction;
on 15 April 1930 it was opened again and put in order after the wrecking.
In revenge the authorities began collecting material for his arrest, and
the usual witnesses gave the investigators what they needed: that he had
appealed from the ambo to the believing women, telling them the
authorities wanted to close the church and asking them to work on their
husbands, communists though they might be; that greeting the faithful at
Easter 1931 he had said, thank God we have lived to see it — we shall
probably not meet it like this again, because the communists are
squeezing; and one woman, sent by the party cell of the “First of May”
factory to the Bolshevo church in April 1930, reported that in his sermon
on the birth of Christ he had pasted in anti-Soviet agitation — for
instance, that Jesus Christ was baptised and bequeathed it to us all, and
now people had stopped acknowledging it and stopped baptising their
children. He was arrested on 14 July 1931 and put in a cell at the
Mytishchi section of the OGPU, and questioned on 26 July, when to every
question he answered categorically: I do not admit myself guilty of the
charge and refuse to give testimony. On 28 July the OGPU troika sentenced
him to three years’ exile in Kazakhstan, and he died on 10 September 1931
— 28 August of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him — in
the transit prison at Alma-Ata. Learning of his death his daughter applied
for a death certificate so as to inherit the house, and in answer the OGPU
troika, in addition to the sentence of exile, resolved to confiscate the
house he had left too. The life was compiled by the igumen Damascene
(Orlovsky); the Russian calendar keeps him also in the synaxis of the
saints of Moscow.
