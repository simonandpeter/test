# Aetherius of Cherson

Aetherius is one of the bishops of Cherson whom the Romanian calendar keeps together on the seventh of March, and the life doxologia prints for the seven of them gives him a short chapter of its own. Some years after the earlier bishops had been killed there, he was sent to Cherson by the patriarch of Jerusalem, in the days of Constantine the Great, who had then begun to come to the faith.

Seeing the hard unbelief of the people of Cherson, who would not receive him, Aetherius went to the emperor himself and obtained an order for them; and returning, he built a church in the city and set everything there in good order. Then he went back once more to the emperor, to thank him for that kindness; and on the journey home he fell ill, and reaching an island by ship he died there.

When the Christians of Cherson learned of it they wept much for him, and sent to the emperor Constantine to tell him of their bishop's end and to ask for another in his place; and [Capito](/saints/capito-of-cherson) was sent to them. The Romanian calendar counts Aetherius among the hieromartyr bishops of Cherson, although the life it prints has him dying of sickness on his way home; the troparion doxologia gives for those bishops does not name him, so no hymn is recorded here.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and sets him a generation later than the first of the company. saint.gr says the patriarch of Jerusalem sent him to Cherson after the three, and that as soon as he saw the heathen people enraged he took refuge with Constantine the Great and asked that the idolaters be expelled from the city, which was done. Having raised a church there he went to the emperor to thank him, and on his return he was seized and drowned in the river Danube. The page's distich turns on that water: out of the river you go to God, Father, to him who was bathed in a river in the flesh. Because the page sends him to Constantine, the reign of Diocletian it names at the head of the notice cannot be a date for the company, and it gives him no year of his own.

The Serbian church keeps him on this day among the seven bishops of Cherson, and the Ohrid Prologue of Bishop Nikolai Velimirović gives the seven of them one entry. All of them were bishops in Cherson at different times, it says, and all of them suffered at the hands of unbelievers — whether of Jews, or of Greeks, or of Scythians — except Aetherius alone, who died in peace. All of them went out into those wild parts as missionaries, sent by the patriarch of Jerusalem to carry the light of the Gospel; all were tormented, and all suffered for the sake of their Lord.

He is the exception the entry makes for itself. Aetherius lived in the time of [Constantine the Great](/saints/constantine-the-great), and so governed his church in freedom and in peace; he built a great temple in Cherson, and made a peaceful end.

The Russian church keeps all seven of them on this day, on one line — «Священномучеников, в Херсонесе епископствовавших: Василия , Ефрема , Капитона , Евгения , Еферия , Елпидия и Агафодора (IV).» — and its life is the fullest account of the company any of the four calendars read has given. They carried the gospel, it says, into the lands of the northern Black Sea from the Danube to the Dnieper, the Crimea among them, and sealed their apostolate with a martyr's death in the fourth century at Херсонес Таврический. The page sets them in a history: the faith had reached the Crimea, anciently Таврида and subject to the Roman emperors, long before the baptism of Владимир; its beginning there was the preaching of the apostle Андрей Первозванный; and its spread was helped against their will by its own enemies, since the emperors used the place for state criminals, which in the first three centuries meant confessors of Christ — «в правление Траяна (98–117) был сослан на заточение в Инкерманские каменоломни святой Климент, епископ Римский», who went on preaching in the Inkerman quarries and was martyred there. The pagans of the Crimea resisted hard and many preachers died in the struggle.

The episcopal see at Херсонес was founded at the beginning of the fourth century, in a turning time when the city, a base for Roman soldiers, was passing into dependence on Byzantium. In the reign of Diocletian, which the page gives as 284 to 305, and in the year 300 — «то есть еще до начала гонения, которое император начал с 303 года», before the persecution the emperor began in 303 — the patriarch Ермон of Jerusalem, whose years the same sentence gives as 303 to 313, sent many bishops out into various countries for the preaching of the gospel. **The two numbers do not agree and nothing is done about it here**: a patriarch whose reign the page dates from 303 cannot have sent anybody in 300, and both figures are the page's own.

**He is the one of the seven the Russian page does not kill**, and it dates him by a reign instead of a year. Some years after the stoning of the three, already in the reign of Константин Великий — «306–337; память 21 мая», the page says, naming his feast as well as his years — a bishop Еферий came to Херсонес from Jerusalem. He met the same opposition from the pagans at first, but the emperor, who had proclaimed the freedom of the Christian faith throughout the empire, did not permit violence against the preacher and issued an edict allowing the Christians of Херсонес to hold their services unhindered. By his efforts a church was built in the city «где святой мирно правил своей паствой», where he governed his flock in peace. To thank the emperor for his protection of the Christians he travelled to Constantinople; on the way back he fell ill and died «на острове Амосе (в Греции) 7 марта» — on the island of Amos, in Greece, on 7 March. **No year is given for it anywhere on the page**, and the island it names is not a form this corpus can place, so no `locations` is written and the recorded death is the reign.

The preaching of the hieromartyrs, the page ends, established the faith at Херсонес: from the beginning of the fifth century the city was a spiritual centre from which Christianity spread north towards Rus, from the ninth the Russians were settling in it, modern excavation has found more than fifty churches there of the fifth to fourteenth centuries, and in 987 Владимир was baptised in it. «Этот древний город явился колыбелью христианства для Руси» — this ancient city was the cradle of Christianity for Rus.
