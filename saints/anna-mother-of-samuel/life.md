# Anna, mother of Samuel

doxologia.ro's calendar names her and gives no life. The line for 9 decembrie reads «Sfânta Prorociță Ana, mama Prorocului Samuel», and her own page on that site carries the name, the date, an icon and some articles, and no life: no century, no place, no account of her beyond what the line itself says. Two things the line does assert, and they are the two this entry rests on: that she is a prophetess, which is what «Prorociță» says, and that she is the mother of the Prophet [Samuel](/saints/samuel-the-prophet).

What the page prints beside the name are the readings appointed for her commemoration: the epistle 1 Corinthians 14:20-25, in which Paul tells the Corinthians not to be children in understanding but children in malice; and the Gospel Luke 4:22-30, the Lord in the synagogue at Nazareth, where they wondered at the words of grace that came out of his mouth and then drove him out of the town.

Nothing further is set down here because nothing further was read. The Romanian calendar keeps her on the day after the forefeast of Saint Anna and on the same day as the conception of the Theotokos, which is the day's own feast; and the three other churches' rows say which calendars were not consulted.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day, 9 December, and gives the life the Romanian calendar did not. Anna was barren for many years, saint.gr says, and sank into great grief; her soul longed to hold a child of her own, but the time went by and the barrenness stayed. For all that, Anna — who was the wife of Elkanah son of Jeremeel, from Armathaim — had a continual hope in God; and every year she went up to the house of the Lord at Shiloh and prayed with tears and with fastings. Her good husband Elkanah tried to comfort her, but Anna was uncomfortable and kept praying to God to give her a child, and she would give it back to him.

Her fervent prayer was heard and Anna conceived. She bore a son and called him [Samuel](/saints/samuel-the-prophet). When the child was weaned the two parents went up to Shiloh with him and with what the law appointed for such occasions; and there the devout Anna brought her little boy to the priest Eli, to dedicate him to the service of the house of the Lord. Full of joy and eagerness she said that marvellous hymn: my heart is made firm in the Lord, my horn is lifted up in my God, my mouth is opened wide against my enemies, I am glad in your salvation.

After this, the page says, the divine grace blessed Anna's faith and devout dedication still more, and gave her three sons more and two daughters; and so her own prophetic insight was fulfilled, in which she had said that the barren woman has borne seven, and she that was rich in children has grown weak. To this Anna, it ends, Chrysostom devoted many brilliant homilies.
