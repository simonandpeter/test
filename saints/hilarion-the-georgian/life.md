# Hilarion the Georgian

Doxologia's life gives him as the son of an aristocrat of Kakheti, and of all his brothers the only one dedicated to God from his birth. His father built a monastery on his own land, and the child Hilarion grew up in it. At fourteen he left the monastery and his father's guardianship and settled in a small cave in the desert of David-Gareji, where he stayed ten years.

Many had heard of the father Hilarion who was tireless in prayer and fasting at the monastery of Saint David of Gareji, and crowds came to him for counsel and blessing. When the bishop of Rustavi came to visit him he ordained him priest, and soon he was made abbot of that monastery. As the people's reverence grew he decided to leave the country: he chose one of the brothers to take his place as abbot, and set out on pilgrimage to Jerusalem.

On the road a band of robbers attacked him and sought to kill him, and their hands withered on the spot; when they understood that God had punished them for raising their hands against him they fell on their knees and begged forgiveness, and he blessed them. He visited the Holy Places and then settled in a cave in the desert of Jordan — by tradition, the page says, the same cave the Prophet [Elijah](/saints/elijah-the-tishbite) had lived in.

One night he saw the Most Holy Mother of God in the midst of twelve men on the Mount of Olives, at the place of the Ascension, and she said to him: Hilarion, return to thy home and prepare a table for the Lord, my Son. Back in Georgia he learned that his father and his brothers had died, and his mother gave the family inheritance to her one living son. With it he founded a monastery, gave lands to the community, gathered seventy-six worthy ascetics, and shared out what was left to the poor.

When the clergy announced that they meant to make him a bishop he left Georgia a second time, taking two companions, and travelled towards Constantinople. They reached Mount Olympus in Asia Minor and settled in a small abandoned church, where the sacristan of the monastery of Saint Ioannikios found them and saw that in a whole week they had eaten nothing but a little lentil. The abbot of the Great Lavra there, hearing that a service had been held by an unknown priest in a language other than Greek, ordered the strangers off the monastery's land; and that night the Mother of God appeared to him in a dream and rebuked him for driving away men who had left their own country for love of her Son. In the morning he knelt before Hilarion, asked forgiveness and begged him to stay.

He spent five years on Olympus, then went again to Constantinople to venerate the life-giving Cross, and from there towards Rome to venerate the tombs of the Apostles Peter and Paul. On the way he healed by his prayers the son of the prefect of Thessalonica, who witnessed the wonder, sought out the wonderworker and begged him to stay and choose a place for himself in the city. The saint, recognising a true lover of God in him, agreed; the prefect built a church on the place he chose, and before long the whole city knew of him.

He spent the rest of his days in Thessalonica. When the Lord made known to him the day of his departure he called the prefect, thanked him, and counselled him to love monks and suffering and to be just and merciful; and he fell asleep on 19 November 875. The prefect in his grief prepared him a marble tomb, and the sick who came to it in faith were healed. The prefect and the archbishop told the emperor Basil the Macedonian, who brought the relics to Constantinople and, before a shrine could be made, kept them in his own chamber; there, on the third night, a strange fragrance filled the room.

Then Hilarion appeared to the emperor in a vision and said: You have done a good work in preparing a shelter for my remains; but the sweet scent you smell was gained in the desert and not in the city, so if you would receive the divine blessings, take me out to the desert. The emperor told the patriarch and the prefect of the vision, and with their agreement brought the relics to the monastery called Romana. His page carries no Tropar tab, so the calendar gives him no hymn.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day, 19 November, and calls him «ο Ίβηρ, ο εν Θεσσαλονίκη» — the Georgian, the one at Thessalonica. saint.gr says he lived in the ninth century and played a great part in the religious life of his own country, that he travelled to the Holy Land, to Constantinople and to Bithynia, where he came to know other spiritual fathers, and then set out for Rome by way of Thessalonica, working wonders as he went. On the way back he stayed at Thessalonica, built a church there in 870, and his work, which was chiefly teaching, was something remarkable for the people of that city. He fell asleep in 875.
