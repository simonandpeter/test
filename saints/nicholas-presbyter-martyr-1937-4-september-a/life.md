# Nicholas the Presbyter (4 September, the first)

Nicholas Lebedev was born in 1867 in the village of Loshchemli in the Vyshny
Volochyok district of Tver province, the son of the priest Andrew Lebedev,
and after the Tver seminary was ordained. In the thirties he served at the
church of Russkaya Kosheva in the Krasny Kholm district. He had a family —
his wife Praskovya, two sons and two daughters — but by the early thirties
the children were grown and had gone to other towns, and so as not to harm
them he hardly wrote to them and hid their addresses, saying he had no
contact with them; even his wife lived apart from him, at Bezhetsk. In 1937
he turned seventy, and one might have hoped the authorities would spare a
man of that age; but in a persecution there is no age limit for arrest and a
martyr’s death. He was arrested on 8 August 1937 and put in the Bezhetsk
prison, and three days later questioned about the “anti-Soviet gatherings
of the clergy” Bishop Gregory (Kozyrev) held: he had been once, he said, at
the bishop’s name-day, where the priests talked animatedly of many things,
and whether anything anti-Soviet was said he could not say, since he did not
remember. Asked about a conversation at his psalmist Funtikov’s house in
1934, he said that after the parish rounds at Uzunikha he had called in and
passed on as news the murder of Kirov, and had said that with the new open
sale of bread the unthinking would buy it up and it would run short again;
that the faithful often came to him complaining that there was no bread in
the village or the shops and the taxes were heavy, and asking whether there
would be a war and what the papers said — to which he had answered with
sympathy that the bread was short because part had gone to Spain and part to
the Red Army and the preparations for war, and that a war against the USSR
was being prepared but when, the papers did not say; and that no one had
complained to him of the collective farms. Questioned again on 15 August —
how he had taken the closing of the Bezhetsk churches; in his heart he was
unhappy, he said, but had done nothing to stop it — and told that the
investigation had established his membership of a counter-revolutionary
group of clergy, he answered that he had belonged to none, had heard of
none, and had done no agitation. The NKVD men were displeased with the old
priest’s answers and his refusal to sign false testimony, and questioned him
again and again; at the last interrogation, on 17 August, he repeated that he
had nothing to add and admitted no guilt. On 13 September the NKVD troika
sentenced him to be shot, and he was shot on 17 September 1937 — 4 September
of the old style, the day the Russian calendar keeps him, with the bishops
Gregory and Sergius and their company. The Jubilee Council of Bishops of
August 2000 numbered him among the new martyrs and confessors of Russia.
