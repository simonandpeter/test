# Carpus of the Seventy

Carpus was one of the seventy apostles, a follower and servant of the apostle Paul, for he carried Paul's letters to those he sent them to. Having laboured with him a long time in the preaching of the word of Christ and suffered many trials, he was set by him as bishop in Beroea of Thrace. He worked at the preaching in Crete as well, where he received Dionysius the Areopagite into his house.

There, doxologia's life says, he saw the Lord appear to him in a vision while he was praying that two sinners might be punished; and he heard Him say: Strike me instead, for I am ready to be crucified again for the salvation of men. Dionysius witnesses this of him too, that he would not begin to celebrate the most pure and life-giving Mysteries until some divine vision appeared to him from heaven. A note at the foot of the page sends the reader to the story as Dionysius the Areopagite tells it, on the third of October.

While he was bishop in Beroea he turned many Greeks from the worship of idols to Christ God, and reproving the Jews he taught them to believe that the Christ whom they had crucified is the true God and the Maker of all. Afterwards he was killed savagely and without mercy by the other Jews who had not believed, and gave his soul into the hands of the Lord. Faithful and God-fearing men laid his honoured relics in that same city, and many healings are given there to the sick.

The Greek calendar keeps him on the same day and gives him a see and a text. saint.gr puts him in the years of the emperor Nero and counts him among the seventy disciples of the Lord; it makes him a fellow-worker of the Apostle Paul, and says that from Paul's second letter to Timothy — «τὸν φαιλόνην ὃν ἀπέλιπον ἐν Τρωάδι παρὰ Κάρπῳ», 2 Timothy 4,13, which the page cites — we learn that he worked for the spreading of the Gospel at Troas. Afterwards, it says, he became bishop at Varna in Thrace — «ο δε Σ. Ευστρατιάδης, αναφέρει Βέρροια της Θράκης», Eustratiades giving Berroia of Thrace instead — and there, by his holy life and the enlightenment of the Holy Spirit, he became a spiritual star of the first magnitude. In his work he bore many temptations and afflictions with great bravery and patience, not fearing pains or hardships, and the page says the Lord's word had settled in him, «ἐν τῷ κοσμῷ θλίψιν ἔξετε». He died peacefully, giving light by his example to many.

The Ohrid Prologue keeps him on the same day and sides with the Romanian book against the Greek one on how he died: he suffered at last from the unbelieving Jews, it says, and was killed, and passed in soul into the Kingdom of God. Of his life it gives what the other two give — one of the Seventy, a follower and companion of Paul, set by him as bishop of Beroea in Thrace, preaching the Gospel in Crete too, where he received [Dionysius](/saints/dionysius-the-areopagite) the Areopagite into his house.

What it adds is his character and one detail of the vision. Dionysius bears witness of him, it says, that he was a man of unusually pure mind, of meekness and of guilelessness; that the Lord Jesus Himself appeared to him in a vision with His angels; and that he never began the holy liturgy before he had first had a heavenly sight. The vision doxologia tells is a different one, of the Lord alone answering a prayer that two sinners be punished, and the two are left as two. This book gives no year either, so the folder stays undated by the agreement of three calendars.
