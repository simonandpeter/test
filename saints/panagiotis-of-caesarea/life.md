# Panagiotis of Caesarea

saint.gr's calendar for 24 Ιουνίου names him and gives him only a short notice. It says that this new martyr suffered at Constantinople for his Christian faith on 24 June 1765, and that his honoured relic was buried with honours by the Christians in the church of the Life-giving Spring there. The page then sets its own account against itself: according to other reports, it adds, this martyr suffered in the year 1767, and at the age of twenty.

The heading over the notice reads «Αγιος Παναγιωτης ο Καισαρευς (; - 1765)», and that epithet is the whole of what the page says about where he came from. Its apolytikion calls him a divine shoot of Caesarea and a fellow in manner of the prize-winning martyrs, and its megalynarion an ornament of the Caesareans. Above the notice stand three verses of an epigram, the last of them «Χριστοῖο ἐρῶ Παναγιώτης ἀλκίφρων δειρὴν ἐτμήθη» — loving Christ, Panagiotis the stout-hearted had his neck cut through.

That verse is the only word the page gives for the manner of his death. It records that a portion of his relic is kept at the Skete of Saint Panteleimon of the Monastery of Koutloumousiou on the Holy Mountain. Nothing is said of his trade, his family or his trial, and no century is named beyond the year.
