# Paul (7 December)

saint.gr's calendar for 7 December keeps the venerable Paul the Obedient, and begins by saying what is not known of him: where he came from and where he was a monk, the synaxaria do not record. What they do record, the page says, are some of his wonders — among them that he put his hand into boiling pitch and was not burned.

From his monastery, where he had the reputation of a saint and a wonderworker, he set out for Jerusalem; and when he had venerated the Holy Places he crossed to Cyprus, where he lived as an ascetic on a mountain for a good many years. But because those who came running to him disturbed him he left for Constantinople, and having gone up by a divine command to the mountain called Parigoria he died after a short time. The couplet over his name says that Paul, hastening to be hidden from everybody, shone out everywhere more than the light.

The Russian calendar begins where the Greek one does, by saying that the time of his life is not known and that only a short life of him has survived; and then it adds what that short life holds. He was the son of parents of means and left the life of the world when he came of age. The name «Послушливый», the Obedient, he was given for the deep humility that was his own and for a complete renunciation of his own will. Once he stirred boiling pitch with his hand and took not the least burn from it.

Part of the brotherhood treated him as an ascetic who bore God in him and some began to receive him with mistrust, and at the prayers of the monks it was shown them in a vision of its own kind that their brother was a true ascetic: they were all caught up into paradise in the night and talked with the monk there, and he let them carry away a flower or a branch each for a remembrance, and when they woke from sleep they found the flowers and the branches of paradise in their hands. After that he set out for Jerusalem and then for Cyprus; and living a solitary life he departed to God on the mountain Parigori, a voice of God having said to him before his end: “Go up, Paul, onto the mountain, and receive the end of your life.”
