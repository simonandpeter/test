# Ketevan

Ketevan was crucified on a tree in the shape of a cross and tortured with red-hot pincers; a helmet heated red was set on her head, she was beaten with steel rods and her body pierced with nails, and the blessed martyr gave her soul into God’s hands on 13 September 1624. A descendant of the royal Bagration family and great-granddaughter of King Constantine of Kartli, she became the wife of David, heir of Alexander II of Kakheti, and ruled the kingdom herself; her deep piety showed in her care for the needs of the Georgian Church, building churches, shelters and houses for travellers and the homeless, and after her husband’s death she withdrew into solitude. Constantine, called Okayan, her husband’s brother, accepted Islam and on the orders of Shah Abbas I sent assassins to kill his dying father Alexander II and his brother George, then had the bodies put on camels and sent to Queen Ketevan, who, horrified, mourned the innocent sufferers and buried them at the cathedral of Alaverdi; the faithless Constantine-Okayan then asked for her hand, threatening force if she refused. She gathered her people of Kakheti and went against him and defeated the apostate, who died an inglorious death with many of the Persian army, and under her wise rule peace and justice were restored in Kakheti; Shah Abbas gave back her son Teimuraz, who had lived years at the shah’s court as a hostage and kept his Orthodox faith unstained.
