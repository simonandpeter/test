# Eugenia

Under Commodus, who came to the empire of Rome after his father Marcus, in the seventh year of his reign, a renowned man named Filip was chosen eparch over the whole of Egypt, and came from Rome to Alexandria with his wife Claudia and his children. He had two sons, Avit and Serghie, and a daughter, Eugenia, beautiful in face and brave in spirit. Filip ruled Egypt after the Roman order and the customs of his fathers; he hated sorcery and the Jewish nation, but towards the Christians he was gentler, for though he had driven them out of the city by the emperor's order he left them to live in peace and to serve their God freely, honouring them for their perfect life and their love of wisdom.

His own care was for Greek learning, in which he brought his daughter up, teaching her rhetoric in both tongues, Latin and Greek, and hoping to see her advance in philosophy; and she, being very sharp of mind and eager at her lessons, took everything in easily, and whatever she heard or read once she remembered always, having it written in her heart as on tablets of brass.

She was fair of face and well formed in body, but fairer in her wise mind and adorned with the purity of virginity. When one of the first men of the Romans, Acvilin, sought her as a bride for his son Acvelie, and her parents asked whether she wished to marry that well-born young man, she answered that a husband should be chosen good in nature rather than good in family, since one has to live with him and not with his family. Others sought her too, young men of birth and glory and wealth, and she made nothing of them all, for purity was her desire and her care, to live in pure virginity.

The beginning of her turning to God was this: a book came into her hands, by chance or rather by God's providence, containing the epistles of the holy apostle Paul, and reading it attentively she found that there is one true God, who made the whole world. Her mind was enlightened at once, having long been cleansed and made ready to receive the Holy Spirit; so she believed in Christ with an undoubting heart, but did not dare to confess him openly with her mouth, for fear of her parents' anger.

When the imperial order was being carried out in Alexandria and the Christians driven from the city, whose teaching Eugenia greatly wished to hear, she asked her parents' leave to go out of the city to one of their nearby villages for the air; and they, having no suspicion of her thoughts, did not hinder her. As she went out in her carriage with her eunuchs and her slaves, passing by the Christians' village and a monastery, she heard within it the voice of monks singing: all the gods of the nations are the works of men's hands, but the Lord who made the heavens is almighty.

Hearing this, as though ashamed of her father's error, she sighed from the heart and said to her eunuchs, one of whom was called Protan and the other Iachint, that they had studied enough of the writings with her and had understood together the doctrines of the philosophers Socrates, Aristotle and Plato, and had read and known the heresy of the Stoics and the opinion of the Epicureans and of the other sophists and versifiers — and that all of it was nothing. She told them that she would no longer be called their mistress but their sister and fellow servant, since we all have the Lord in common for our God and Father.

The words pleased both the eunuchs, and when night came they did as they had agreed: returning late towards the city they secretly got Eugenia down from the carriage, none of the slaves feeling it, and she put on a man's clothes and a man's name. Falling in with the people who were going and singing, she asked one of them who the old man was who rode on an ass in the midst of the people, and learned that he was the bishop Elen.

Not long before those days a sorcerer named Zarie had appeared in those parts, deceiving the people with many great tricks and charms and saying that the bishop Elen was a liar. The bishop met him before the people with words inspired of God, and a great fire was made ready; the bishop called the sorcerer into the flame, and the sorcerer told him to go in first, since he had ordered it so, and the holy bishop signed himself and went in. Hearing all this, the blessed Eugenia rejoiced in spirit, and marvelled, and sighed.

As they came near the monastery the monks came out to meet the bishop, singing, «We have received, O God, your mercy in the midst of your people»; and the people went in with the bishop, and Eugenia went in with her eunuchs. Eutropie, the man who had been speaking with them, came to the bishop and said that three young brothers had come in after him who had left the worship of idols and wished to join the brotherhood. The blessed bishop Elen looked on her with love and said that she was rightly named Eugenia, for the name fitted her spirit, since she had a soul of courage and showed herself a man in everything; and turning to Protan and Iachint he said that they too, slaves in the body, were free in spirit.

The carriage and the slaves, who had gone ahead and knew nothing, came to the gates of the city and went in, and the household came out before the house with lights; but running to the carriage they found it empty. Her parents wept for her day and night with inconsolable lamentation, often calling her name, beating their faces and sprinkling their heads with ashes, and falling to the ground from weakness and pain. Unable to find her anywhere, Filip questioned the idol-priests, the sorcerers and the enchanters, and offered a multitude of sacrifices to the idols; and believing the fable he was told, his grief eased, and he had a golden idol made in the likeness of his beloved daughter and set it in an honoured place in Alexandria.

Covered with a man's clothes and a man's name, and taken for a man because of her manly ways and the greatness of her soul, the blessed virgin lived in that monastery. Her speech was humble, loving, gentle and sparing of words, full of the fear of God and of profit; nobody was before her at the singing in church, and she was useful to all, comforting the sorrowful. Three years after her turning to God the abbot of that monastery died, and the brethren, gathering, begged her with one voice and one soul to be their abbot, for they did not know her hidden secret.

Fearing on the one hand to be against the law, a woman ruling over men, and ashamed on the other to slight the great request of all those honourable persons, she took it on. She laboured at all the monastery's services and never left the church's accustomed rule, Matins and the hours and, on the marked days, the Liturgy and Vespers, counting as lost in vain the hour she spent otherwise.

There was in Alexandria a woman named Melantia, rich in goods but poor in good works, who fell into a fever and suffered from it above a year. Her house being near that monastery, and hearing of the physician there, she was cured; and a little later, wishing to thank him for her healing, she made three vessels of pure silver, filled them with money and sent them to her physician who took no payment. He would not receive them, but sent them back.

From that time Melantia, knowing Evghenie, visited him often and clung to him with a disorderly love, not knowing the woman's nature that was hidden in the man's face and name. One day, coming to her village near the monastery and thinking of the young monk and burning for him, she feigned sickness and sent to him, begging that he come himself to visit her; and to what Melantia said the venerable Eugenia was ashamed, and answered harshly, telling her to be silent and not try to hurt them with the poison of the old serpent.

Filled with unspeakable shame and with anger, Melantia went into the city, came to the eparch and said that a young man of the Christian faith, said to be a physician, living in a monastery near her village, had attempted her. Hearing it the eparch was filled with anger and sent for the young man, that is for Evghenie, and for all who lived with him, and had them bound in chains and taken to prison. A day was appointed for a great judgement and punishment of the monks, for he meant to give some of them to be eaten by beasts, to burn others with fire and to hang others on wood.

The eparch Filip came with his sons Avit and Serghie, and sitting in the accustomed place of judgement he had the blessed Eugenia brought into the midst of the spectacle with her eunuchs and the other monks, fettered in chains. So she came to judgement before her father and her brothers, guilty of no wrong, dressed in a man's monastic clothes and with her face bent down so as not to be known; and she answered the eparch's hot words gently, that her Lord Jesus Christ, whom she served, teaches purity and promises eternal life to those who keep their virginity untouched.

When Melantia's slave-woman was brought and added lie to lie to please her mistress, saying that she knew this shameless young man to have done lawlessness often with women, Eugenia said that the time of speaking had come, since the time of silence had ended, and that it was a free time to confess the truth, so that the lie brought against them should not boast without measure and be made a reproach to the Christians. Saying this she tore her garment from above, uncovered a part of her holy and most pure virgin body and showed that she was of the woman's sex; and she said to the eparch that he was her father after the body.

Before she had finished her word she was known by her father and her brothers, by her voice and by certain marks she had in her face, for they had turned their eyes on her eagerly. The people who watched marvelled and lifted up their voices, crying that Christ is one, that he is the one true God, the God of the Christians. Melantia, while she was still there in the spectacle, was filled with great shame and fear, and a great fire fell from heaven on her house and burned it to its foundations with all her riches and her treasury, so that not a trace of it was left.

But since envy of the enemy follows holiness, some of the chief idolaters of Alexandria took it very hard that the Christians were honoured, and stirred the emperors to anger against Filip, who wrote to him. Reading that imperial letter, the eparch feigned sickness until he had sold all he had and given part to the churches and part to the poor; for the blessed Filip was a well-spoken orator, and he brought many of the chief Greeks to the faith. After a time another eparch came from Rome in his place, named Terentie, who wanted to kill Filip but feared the people, since all were ready to give their lives for him, and so secretly set men on; and so Filip died.

After the death of Saint Filip, Eugenia gathered Christian virgins to herself and lived with them in virginity, serving God, while her mother Claudia built a great house for strangers and served the strangers and the sick. There was then in Rome a young maiden of the imperial family, an orphan of both parents, named Vasila, who had been entrusted until her age to an uncle of hers named Elen, and had been given by the emperors to a man named Pompie. Eugenia rejoiced greatly at a messenger from her and, taking counsel with her mother and the eunuchs, and knowing that words out of the mouth teach a man more than a letter does, she sent her eunuchs to her; and speaking with her day and night they taught her how to believe in Christ, and she believed with all her heart.

When the pagans Valerian and Galerie came to the throne a greater persecution was ordered against the Christians, for the imperial command went out that all the Christian teachers be killed. Sitting with Vasila and many virgins of Christ, the blessed Eugenia said to them all that the time of the vintage had come, in which the grapes are cut and trodden underfoot; and that the most glorious thing is to die for virginity itself, for what are the empty allurements of this world, which come with a temporary gladness and go with an eternal pain?

That same day a slave-woman of Vasila's ran to Pompie, her mistress's betrothed, and told him; and Pompie in great anger went to Elen, who had brought Vasila up, and demanded to see the maiden. Going away in shame, he begged some of the senators to come with him, and fell at the emperors' feet, complaining against his betrothed and against Eugenia. The emperor pitied him and gave an imperial order that the virgin Vasila should either consent to receive her betrothed or be killed.

When the order came to her, Vasila answered with a loud voice and much boldness that she had the King of kings for her betrothed, for he is Jesus, the Son of God, and that beside him she would have no other corruptible husband. Then they brought Eugenia too and asked her where they had learned the sorcery by which they mastered even the great gods; and she answered that the eparch had said truly, for the Christians do master his gods. Brought to the temple, she stood before the goddess in the attitude of prayer and called on the eternal God who had counted her worthy to be born and to be kept until that day a pure virgin of his Only-begotten Son.

When the emperor learned of it he ordered a great stone tied to her neck and her body thrown into the depths of the Tiber; but as soon as it was done the stone came loose, and she walked on the water, as the apostle Peter once did. Every day the angels brought her heavenly food, bread sweeter than honey and whiter than snow. The greatest thing of all is that the King of the angels himself came to visit her and said: «Eugenia, I am he who received the cross and death for you, as you for love of me endure such cruel torments. Therefore I shall count you worthy of many gifts and of very great glory in my eternal kingdom; and for your assurance that you will receive these things in heaven, let this be your sign: you will depart from this temporal and corruptible world, to come to the life above, on the very day on which I was born a man.»

At those words of our Lord Jesus Christ, Eugenia melted like wax in the fire of the divine longing, and poured herself out in love towards him like a river of waters; and he went from her eyes, leaving in her heart an unspeakable spiritual joy, so that she lived in the dark prison as in heaven. The pagans sent an executioner, and he stabbed her in the prison, in the month of December, on the twenty-fifth day, on the very lordly day of the Nativity of the Lord. Her mother and her household buried her holy relics with honour in a field of theirs outside the city, in a place called the Romans' Way, where she had herself buried the relics of many other saints before.

To her mother, weeping many days at the tomb, she appeared one night in a dream, so bright and adorned that Claudia could not look at her face. Other virgins were with her, and she asked why her mother wept and lamented for them instead of being glad and rejoicing, and told her to know that she was in much and unspeakable gladness with the holy martyrs and with her father Filip, reigning together with Christ, who on the Sunday would take her also into the everlasting gladness. Hearing this and seeing the holy angels travelling with her, Claudia rejoiced greatly and thanked God; and having disposed well of all her goods and given them to the poor, she rested in Jesus Christ our Lord.

doxologia.ro's calendar keeps her on 24 decembrie, while the life it links has her stabbed in prison on 25 December, the day of the Nativity, as the sign she was given; the day's line gives no year, and the page the site keeps for her troparion is an audio recording of the hymn in Greek and prints no text.

The Greek calendar keeps her on the same day and fills in the household. saint.gr has her of Rome, her father [Philip](/saints/philip-father-of-eugenia) and her mother Claudia, and two brothers besides. Her father was made prefect at Alexandria and took them all there, and it was there that a Christian girl put the letters of the Apostle Paul into her hands. When her parents wanted to give her to a Roman official named Aquilinas she dressed as a man one night and fled to another city, where she was catechised, baptised and given the monastic habit at once. Years later she came home; the whole house became Christian, and the idolaters wounded her father mortally. The day keeps [Basilla](/saints/basilla-martyr-24-december) too, and her servants [Protas](/saints/protas-companion-of-eugenia) and [Hyacinth](/saints/hyacinth-companion-of-eugenia).

The Охридски пролог keeps her on 24. децембар ст. ст. and heads the day with her: „Преподобна мученица Евгенија и други с њом.“ — Eugenia and others with her. She was the daughter of Филип, eparch of the whole of Egypt, it says, and was born at Rome. In that time the Christians had been driven out of Alexandria and were living outside the city; the virgin Eugenia visited them and received their faith with her whole heart. She fled from her parents with her two faithful eunuchs, was baptised by the bishop Елије, and in man’s clothing entered a men’s monastery, where she took the monastic habit.

So far did she cleanse her heart by a struggle of her own choosing that she received from God the grace of healing the sick, and healed among others a rich woman named Мелантија. But afterwards that woman tried to draw Eugenia into bodily sin, never suspecting that Eugenia was a woman; and being flatly refused she went in revenge to the eparch and slandered her exactly as Potiphar’s wife once slandered the chaste Joseph — the book’s own comparison. The eparch had all the monks chained and thrown into prison with her.

But when she was brought out to trial, Eugenia showed herself to her own father as his daughter. Филип rejoiced and was baptised with his whole house, and was chosen bishop of Alexandria; and when the Roman emperor heard of it he sent a wicked voivode named Теренције, who came to Alexandria and killed him secretly. Then Eugenia moved to Rome with her mother and her brothers, and there turned the heathen to the right faith fearlessly and zealously, girls above all. So she brought to the faith a beautiful virgin named [Basilla](/saints/basilla-martyr-24-december), who was soon beheaded for Christ, as Eugenia had foretold to her; and then both the eunuchs were beheaded, [Protas](/saints/protas-companion-of-eugenia) and [Hyacinth](/saints/hyacinth-companion-of-eugenia).

At the last her own martyr’s end came. The temple of Diana fell down and was destroyed by her nearness. Her tormentors threw her first into water and then into fire, and God saved her from both. The Lord Jesus himself appeared to her in the prison and told her that she would suffer on the very day of His Nativity; and so it was. «Мачем је посекоше 25. децембра 262. године у Риму» — they beheaded her with the sword on 25 December in the year 262, at Rome, which is the day after the one this book keeps her on and a year that sits inside the half-century this folder already holds. After her death she appeared to her mother in great glory, and comforted her.
