# Sebastian of Karaganda

Sebastian, Stephen Vasilevich Fomin in the world, was born in the year 1884 in the province of Oryol in Russia. In 1906 he entered the monastery of Optina and was tonsured a monk there with the name Sebastian. He was ordained deacon in 1923 and presbyter in 1927, and he served in the Karaganda region of Kazakhstan; and there, saint.gr says, he fell asleep in peace in the year 1966.

That is the whole of the page. It says nothing of the elders under whom he lived at Optina, nothing of how he came from Oryol to Kazakhstan or of what the years between his ordination and his ministry there were like, and nothing of his burial or his relics. It prints no couplet and no hymn, and this folder does not supply what the calendar does not say.

The Russian calendar keeps the finding of his relics on its own 9 October, «Обретение мощей прп. Севастиана Карагандинского исп (1997)», his repose on 6 April and his name in the synaxis of the new martyrs on 26 January, and the life it reaches is where almost everything on this folder now comes from. He was Stepan Vasilievich Fomin in the world, born on 28 October 1884 in the village of Kosmodemyanskoye in Orel province to a poor peasant family. His parents died when he was five and he went to live with his eldest brother's family; the middle brother was tonsured at Optina. He finished the three-class parish school well, read the books the parish priest lent him, and, being too weak in health for field work, herded the cattle. In winter he often went to see his brother at Optina.

On 3 January 1909 he was received into the Optina skete as cell-attendant to the elder [Joseph](/saints/joseph-of-optina), and after Joseph's death in 1911 he passed under the direction of Nectarius and stayed with him as his cell-attendant until 1923. He was tonsured into the mantle with the name Sebastian in 1917, as the persecution of the Church was beginning. Optina was closed on 10 January 1918 and went on existing under the guise of an agricultural artel, with a museum set up on its ground and the brothers' cells let out to summer tenants; many of the younger novices left. In 1923 the services stopped altogether and the monks were evicted, and all that time he remained under Nectarius's care.

He was ordained priest in 1927 by the bishop of Kaluga, and after Nectarius died in 1928 he came to the town of Kozlov and was given the church of the Prophet Elijah, where he served until his arrest. He disliked unprayerful part-singing and worked to set a reverent monastic chant in his parish; he fought the Renovationists in Kozlov and kept up his links with the scattered brotherhood of Optina.

He was arrested in February 1933, and his answer under interrogation is on the page: that he looked on everything the Soviet power did as the wrath of God and that power as a punishment for people; that he had said so to those around him and to anyone else the subject came up with; that he had told them it was necessary to pray and to live in love, and only then would they be delivered of it; and that he had little to thank the Soviet power for in the closing of the churches and monasteries, because the Orthodox faith was being destroyed by it.

He was sentenced to seven years at the timber felling, despite his weak health and an injured left arm, and he kept Sunday in prayer and conversation and spent the night watches praying rather than sleeping. I was in prison, he said afterwards, and I did not break the fasts; if they gave out some slop with meat in it I did not eat it, I traded it for an extra ration of bread.

After his release he stayed in the village of Bolshaya Mikhailovka outside Karaganda and cared for everyone who came to God, going to their houses and performing the offices without any permission from the authorities — the people in Karaganda were faithful, he said, they will not give you away. His spiritual children came to him from every corner of the country and he found each of them somewhere to live; the nuns among them he would bless to live with a family, in the manner of the Optina elders, so that they became, the page says, like guardian angels of a house.

Only in 1955 did the faithful win an official registration for the community at Bolshaya Mikhailovka, and together they built a church, consecrated to the Nativity of the Mother of God on the day of the Ascension. He chose his own priests, and a women's monastic community gathered round him. Archbishop Joseph (Chernov) of Petropavlovsk said of it that the father had planted a vineyard here and then grown it with tears, and that it was a little church, not to be seen from the ground, with a pillar of fire burning from it up to heaven. On 22 December 1957 the same archbishop raised him to archimandrite with a patriarchal charter for zealous service, and in 1964, on his name day, gave him a bishop's staff, which the page calls an award without example.

He kept the church rule without fault and allowed no omission or shortening in the services, which were, the page says, an inseparable condition of his inner life. His favourite figure in conversation was the apostle John the Theologian, and he often called his flock to honour that apostle of love. He honoured the icons and said they are given us as help against the dark powers; that some are particular in the glory of their grace, and that the wonderworking images prayed over for centuries carry grace from the Lord like little streams.

He had a fine humour and liked a joke, always a kindly one, and never grudged the time a conversation took. When the authorities called him in to close his church he disarmed them so completely that they were left without speech and afterwards wondered what sort of old man this was that they could do nothing about. He asked people to live more peaceably, told them to pray for the dead above all, and said: glory be to God for everything. On 3 April 1966 he was tonsured into the great schema by bishop Pitirim (Nechaev), who came to him to perform it.

**The same calendar keeps his repose on the Julian 6 April**, and this folder renders on the Julian 9 October, the finding of his relics, because one row per church is the schema and that is the row the folder was given. The 6 апреля line was read on 6 October 2026 and it is «Прп. Севастиана Карагандинского, исп. (1966).» — venerable Sebastian of Karaganda, confessor, 1966 — which is the year this folder already holds for his death, and the name link behind it, page 9163, gives the fullest form of his name the calendar prints anywhere: «Севастиан (Фомин) Карагандинский, схиархимандрит, исп.», with the surname the folder did not have.

The day page prints a troparion for him in глас 3, headed «Тропарь священноисповедника схиархимандрита Севастиана Карагандинского», and it is **the same text this folder already carries** off the October day, word for word — the servant of the Holy Trinity, the earthly angel and heavenly man, the successor of the spiritual eldership of Optina. No second hymn is added for it; that the two days print one troparion is recorded here instead. The April line gives him the same two words of rank as the October one, «Прп.» and «исп.», and his `types` and `office` are untouched.
